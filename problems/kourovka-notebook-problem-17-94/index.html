Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.94

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be an arbitrary nontrivial group, let $Z=\langle t\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}$ be an infinite cyclic group, and put $H=Z*G$, the free product of these groups. For $r\in H$, define its normal closure in $H$ by \[ \langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle_H =\langle h r h^{-1}:h\in H\rangle, \] the smallest normal subgroup of $H$ containing $r$.

The supplied well-known problem asks whether there exist a nontrivial group $G$ and an element $r\in Z*G$ such that \[ \langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle_{Z*G}=Z*G. \] Its conjectured negative answer, called Kervaire's conjecture in the supplied editors' comments, is the assertion \[ \text{for every group }G\ne\{1\}\text{ and every }r\in Z*G, \qquad (Z*G)/\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle_{Z*G}\ne\{1\}. \] No finiteness, countability, or torsion hypothesis on $G$ is imposed in the general question.

The supplied editors' comments report that the negative answer has been proved when $G$ is torsion-free and when $G$ is residually finite. Here torsion-free means that $g^n=1$, for $g\in G$ and a positive integer $n$, implies $g=1$; residually finite means that every nonidentity element of $G$ has nonidentity image under some homomorphism from $G$ to a finite group. These special-case results are supplied source qualifications and have not been independently verified here.

Short English statement

Can the free product of an infinite cyclic group and a nontrivial group be generated as a normal subgroup by one element? The supplied conjectured answer is no for every nontrivial group. The editors report this negative answer for torsion-free and residually finite groups.
