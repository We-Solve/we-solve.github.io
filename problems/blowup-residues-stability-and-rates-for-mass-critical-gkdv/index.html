Blowup Residues, Stability, and Rates for Mass-Critical gKdV

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $s\geq 0$, let $H^s({\mathbb{R}})$ denote the Sobolev space \[ H^s({\mathbb{R}})=\left\{f\in\mathcal S'({\mathbb{R}}):\widehat f\text{ is a function and }\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(1+|\xi|^2)^s|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\xi<\infty\right\}. \] In particular, $H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ consists of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak derivative. Set \[ I=\left(\frac13,\frac12\right),\qquad J=\left(\frac37,\frac12\right). \] The supplied record concerns the mass-critical generalized Korteweg--de Vries equation on ${\mathbb{R}}$. Its original excerpt refers to equation (1) and Theorem 1, but neither is included in full. Consequently, the equation's normalization and sign, the precise definition of the power-rate exponent $\nu$, and the hypotheses of the constructed family cannot be recovered from the supplied text. The questions below retain those source-dependent terms explicitly; they are not a complete specification of a solution class.

The intended blowup residue is the remainder obtained after subtracting the singular bubble from a solution and approaching its blowup time. The excerpt uses the time orientation $t\downarrow0$. It does not supply the bubble, its modulation parameters, or the topology in which the remainder converges. Thus, saying that the residue belongs to $H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ must be distinguished from asserting convergence in $H^1({\mathbb{R}})$, which is not specified here.

  1. For every solution in the source's intended class that blows up in finite time at a finite spatial point, with power-rate exponent $\nu\in I$, does a blowup residue exist and belong to $H^1({\mathbb{R}})$? The supplied assessment says that this is open even for the particular solution attributed to reference [6] at $\nu=2/5$. That reference is not identified in the supplied excerpt.
  2. For each $\nu\in J$, do the corresponding finite-time, finite-point blowup solutions attributed to Theorem 1 enjoy any form of stability? The source does not specify a perturbation space, a topology, permitted changes in blowup parameters, or a precise stability property.
  3. Determine whether the only $H^1$ power blowup rates are $\nu=2/5$ and $\nu>3/7$. Restricted to the finite-time, finite-point range $I$, the proposed classification can be expressed as \[ \mathcal R_{H^1}\cap I\stackrel{?}{=}\left\{\frac25\right\}\cup\left(\frac37,\frac12\right), \] where $\mathcal R_{H^1}$ denotes the set of power-rate exponents realized by solutions in the source's intended $H^1$ class. This notation is conditional on specifying that solution class and its rate convention. The supplied phrase "$H^1$ power blowup rates" does not explicitly distinguish regularity of the solution from regularity of its residue, and the scope of the classification outside $I$ is not specified. Within $I$, the displayed candidate also excludes the endpoint $\nu=3/7$.
  4. If the proposed classification holds, construct, for every \[ \nu\in\left(\frac13,\frac37\right)\setminus\left\{\frac25\right\}, \] a finite-time, finite-point blowup solution with that power-rate exponent and infinite energy, belonging to a suitable $H^\kappa({\mathbb{R}})$ with $0<\kappa<1$. The record does not specify the energy functional or what "infinite energy" means, nor whether one common $\kappa$ must work for all such $\nu$ or $\kappa$ may depend on $\nu$.

The excerpt additionally attributes an $H^1({\mathbb{R}})$ residue $r_\alpha$ to the construction, with $r_\alpha(x)=0$ for $x<0$ and an asserted local power profile for $0<x<\delta$, near the blowup point placed at the origin. Its displayed power, the coefficient involving $c_\alpha$ and $Q$, and the relation between $\alpha$ and $\nu$ are corrupted by text extraction. The excerpt also ends mid-sentence. These expressions cannot be imposed as precise formulas from the supplied evidence. The corrected statement supplies $\nu=2/5$; the corresponding fraction in the original excerpt is corrupted. These source defects require mathematical review before a fully specified formulation can be accepted.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether finite-time blowup at a finite point leaves an $H^1$ remainder, whether the constructed families are stable, and which power blowup rates are possible in $H^1$. Conditional on the proposed classification, the remaining specified rates are to be constructed at suitable regularity $H^\kappa$, $0<\kappa<1$, with infinite energy. Several essential definitions and formulas are missing or corrupted in the supplied excerpt.
