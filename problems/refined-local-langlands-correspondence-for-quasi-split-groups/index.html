Refined Local Langlands Correspondence for Quasi-Split Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a local field and let $G$ be a quasi-split connected reductive algebraic group over $F$. Write $\widehat G$ for its complex dual group and ${}^{L}G$ for its $L$-group. Let $\varphi$ be a tempered Langlands parameter for $G$: an admissible local Langlands parameter whose Weil-group part has bounded image in $\widehat G$, using the chosen standard convention for parameters. Write $\Pi_\varphi(G)$ for its conjecturally associated tempered $L$-packet, consisting of equivalence classes of irreducible admissible tempered representations of $G(F)$. The existence and meaning of this packet belong to the correspondence being requested; they are not supplied hypotheses establishing that correspondence.

A Whittaker datum $\mathfrak w$ is a $G(F)$-conjugacy class of pairs $(B,\psi)$, where $B$ is an $F$-rational Borel subgroup with unipotent radical $U$, and $\psi:U(F)\to{\mathbb{C}}^\times$ is a nondegenerate unitary character. A representation $\pi$ is $\mathfrak w$-generic if \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{U(F)}(\pi,\psi)\ne 0. \] For an algebraic group $H$, let $\pi_0(H)$ denote its group of connected components. Two component-group conventions relevant to interpreting the record are \[ C_\varphi=\operatorname{Cent}_{\widehat G}(\operatorname{im}\varphi),\qquad A_\varphi^{\mathrm{raw}}=\pi_0(C_\varphi),\qquad \mathcal S_\varphi=\pi_0\!\left(C_\varphi/Z(\widehat G)^{\Gamma_F}\right), \] where $\Gamma_F$ is the absolute Galois group of $F$. The source's phrase "component group of the parameter centralizer" does not specify whether the central quotient is intended. Accordingly, write $A_\varphi$ for the component group still to be fixed from the source, and write $\operatorname{Irr}(A_\varphi)$ for its irreducible complex representations up to equivalence.

The requested assertion, conditional on resolving this convention, is that for every such $F,G,\varphi$ and every Whittaker datum $\mathfrak w$, the packet admits an injective parametrization \[ \iota_{\mathfrak w}:\Pi_\varphi(G)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(A_\varphi), \] which is bijective over the non-Archimedean fields covered by the assertion, and satisfies \[ \exists!\,\pi_{\mathfrak w}\in\Pi_\varphi(G)\text{ that is }\mathfrak w\text{-generic}, \qquad \iota_{\mathfrak w}(\pi)=\mathbf 1 \Longleftrightarrow \pi\text{ is }\mathfrak w\text{-generic}. \] Here $\mathbf 1$ is the trivial representation of $A_\varphi$. The generic constituent is unique relative to the fixed Whittaker datum.

The record additionally requires the expected endoscopic character identities and, in its original formulation, a "perfect pairing." A parametrization permits character values \[ \langle a,\pi\rangle_{\mathfrak w} =\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\iota_{\mathfrak w}(\pi)(a)\right), \qquad a\in A_\varphi, \] but the record does not define the claimed perfectness or specify the endoscopic data, transferred test functions, transfer-factor normalizations, or character identities. This character-value formula therefore does not resolve that additional requirement. The complete endoscopic assertion cannot be made precise from the supplied text alone.

There is also an unresolved field qualification: the original statement requires bijectivity over $p$-adic fields, whereas the rewritten statement requires it over non-Archimedean local fields. These scopes differ if positive-characteristic local fields are included. No restriction settling that discrepancy is supplied.

Short English statement

The supplied problem asks for a Whittaker-dependent parametrization of every tempered packet for a quasi-split connected reductive group, with injectivity, the stated non-Archimedean bijectivity requirement, a unique generic constituent labeled by the trivial representation, and endoscopic compatibility. The component-group convention, positive-characteristic scope, endoscopic identities, and meaning of the original "perfect pairing" remain unresolved. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
