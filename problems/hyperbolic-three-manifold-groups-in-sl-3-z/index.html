Math-4213

Hyperbolic Three-Manifold Groups in $\operatorname{SL}_3(\mathbb Z)$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write \[ \operatorname{SL}_3({\mathbb{Z}})=\{A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3\times3}({\mathbb{Z}}):\det A=1\}. \] Under the usual convention, a hyperbolic $3$-manifold is a connected, complete Riemannian $3$-manifold without boundary whose sectional curvature is constantly $-1$. Finite volume means $\operatorname{Vol}(M)<\infty$. For a base point $m_0\in M$, let $\pi_1(M,m_0)$ denote its fundamental group.

Does there exist a finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold $M$ and a group homomorphism \[ \rho:\pi_1(M,m_0)\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_3({\mathbb{Z}}) \qquad\text{such that}\qquad \ker\rho=\{1\}? \] The existence of such a homomorphism is independent of the choice of base point. The supplied statement imposes no orientability or compactness restriction; both compact and noncompact finite-volume manifolds are within the stated scope. Completeness and absence of boundary are included here as the usual hyperbolic-manifold convention, but are not explicitly specified in the supplied wording and require confirmation against the report.

Short English statement

Does some finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold have a fundamental group that embeds in the group of determinant-one $3\times3$ integer matrices? The supplied record labels this question open; that status is not independently verified here.
