In the intended Riemannian setting, let $(M,g)$ be the manifold under consideration. Interpret $\Lambda$ as its free loop space, with a loop-space model and topology suitable for the length filtration, and put \[ \Lambda_L=\{\gamma\in\Lambda:\operatorname{length}_g(\gamma)\leq L\},\qquad L\geq0. \] The free-loop interpretation and the precise model of this filtration are not specified in the supplied excerpt. For a fixed coefficient field $\mathbb{k}$, write \[ b_i(X;\mathbb{k})=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}H_i(X;\mathbb{k}),\qquad B(N)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}b_i(\Lambda;\mathbb{k}) \quad(N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}). \] The coefficient field is also unspecified by the source.

The excerpt attributes to Gromov, under the reference label $[\mathrm{Gromov2001}]$, the following background assertion: if $M$ is simply connected, there is a constant $C>0$ such that \[ \sum_{i\geq0}b_i(\Lambda_{CN};\mathbb{k}) \ \geq\ \sum_{i=0}^{N}b_i(\Lambda;\mathbb{k}) \qquad\text{for every integer }N\geq1. \] This records the supplied attribution; the excerpt does not provide the complete hypotheses or bibliographic entry for that assertion.

Let $G(T)$ denote the number of nonconstant closed geodesics of length at most $T$, using a fixed counting convention. The source does not say whether this counts geometrically distinct images, prime closed geodesics, or iterates, nor how parametrizations and orientation are identified.

The question attributed to Gromov asks whether exponential growth of the loop-space Betti numbers forces exponential growth of $G(T)$. If the hypothesis means cumulative exponential lower growth, and the conclusion means exponential lower growth for all sufficiently large lengths, its quantitative form is \[ \begin{split} &\Bigl[\exists a,h>0\ \exists N_0\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\ \forall N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\quad N\geq N_0\Longrightarrow B(N)\geq a e^{hN}\Bigr] \\[-2pt] &\hspace{1cm}\Longrightarrow \Bigl[\exists c,\eta>0\ \exists T_0\geq0\ \forall T\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad T\geq T_0\Longrightarrow G(T)\geq c e^{\eta T}\Bigr]. \end{split} \] The source does not specify whether exponential growth is cumulative, degreewise, or expressed through a limiting growth rate. It also does not specify compactness, boundary conditions, metric regularity, or whether simple connectivity is a hypothesis of the question itself or only of the preceding background assertion. These unresolved qualifications prevent treating this schema as a conjecture for an unrestricted class of manifolds.

Short English statement

The question attributed to Gromov asks whether exponentially growing loop-space Betti numbers force exponentially many closed geodesics as the length bound increases. The supplied record labels it open. The manifold hypotheses, homology coefficients, meaning of exponential growth, and convention for counting closed geodesics require clarification.
