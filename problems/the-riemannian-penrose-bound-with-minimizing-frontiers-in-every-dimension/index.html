The Riemannian Penrose bound with minimizing frontiers in every dimension

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For $n\ge3$, let $(E,h)$ be a connected complete exterior with one Euclidean end and compact complement, including its complete possibly singular frontier $\Sigma$. Completeness is measured within each finite-distance class of its intrinsic length metric. The metric extends smoothly through the frontier and satisfies \[ R_h\ge0,\quad R_h\in L^1(E,dV_h),\quad R_h=O(r^{-n-\beta})\ (\beta>0),\qquad h-\delta=O_2(r^{-\tau})\ (\tau>(n-2)/2). \] The full frontier is the perimeter support of a filled side, outer minimizing and locally perimeter minimizing on both sides. Its regular part is smooth, embedded and minimal; its singular part satisfies $\dim_{\mathrm H}\Sigma_{\mathrm{sing}}\le n-8$. With $\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb S^{n-1}|$ and the usual ADM energy normalization $[2(n-1)\omega_{n-1}]^{-1}$, \[ m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(h)\ge\frac12\left(\frac{\mathcal H_h^{n-1}(\Sigma)}{\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{(n-2)/(n-1)}. \] Every frontier component contributes to the area; no spin or frontier-connectedness hypothesis is imposed.

Outline of the argument

A conformal flow is built from smooth obstacles while preserving frontier area. Regular-patch estimates control forcing, and distance-median separation handles the singular region; mass monotonicity and the limiting comparison give the stated numerical inequality.
