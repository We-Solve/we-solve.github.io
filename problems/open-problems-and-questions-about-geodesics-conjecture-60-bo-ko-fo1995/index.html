Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Conjecture 60 ([Bo-Ko-Fo1995])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T^2={\mathbb{R}}^2/{\mathbb{Z}}^2$. Under the usual smooth, globally defined interpretation, let $g$ be a smooth Riemannian metric on $T^2$. A polynomial first integral of degree exactly three in the velocities is a smooth function $F:TT^2\to{\mathbb{R}}$ of the form \[ F(x,v)=A_0(x)+A_1(x)(v)+A_2(x)(v,v)+A_3(x)(v,v,v), \] where $A_k$ is a smooth symmetric covariant $k$-tensor field and $A_3\not\equiv0$, such that, for every affinely parametrized $g$-geodesic $\gamma:I\to T^2$, \[ \frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}F\left(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t)\right)=0 \qquad(t\in I). \] A nonzero Killing vector field is a smooth vector field $X\not\equiv0$ satisfying $\mathcal L_Xg=0$; equivalently, with $\nabla$ the Levi--Civita connection, \[ g(\nabla_YX,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_ZX)=0 \] for all smooth vector fields $Y,Z$.

Conjecture 60, under these conventions. For every such metric $g$, if its geodesic flow admits a polynomial first integral of degree exactly three in the velocities, then $g$ admits a nonzero Killing vector field.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied wording does not specify smoothness, global versus local integrals, or whether the cubic integral must be homogeneous. The formulation above explicitly uses smooth global data and allows lower-degree terms. For the homogeneous reading, require $A_0=A_1=A_2=0$. These conventions require source review. The word "nonzero" is also implicit rather than explicit in the supplied conclusion; without it, the zero vector field would satisfy the conclusion for every metric.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture says that a Riemannian metric on the two-dimensional torus whose geodesic flow has a cubic polynomial conserved quantity must have a nonzero infinitesimal isometry. The precise regularity, globality, and homogeneity conventions require review.
