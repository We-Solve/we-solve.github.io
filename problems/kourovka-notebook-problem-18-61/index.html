Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.61

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A $2$-group is understood here in the usual periodic sense: a group $G$, possibly infinite, such that every element has finite order equal to a power of $2$. No local finiteness assumption is imposed. For a group $H$, define its lower central series by \[ \gamma_1(H)=H,\qquad \gamma_{n+1}(H)=[\gamma_n(H),H]\quad(n\geq 1), \] where $[A,B]$ denotes the subgroup generated by the commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$. Thus $H$ is nilpotent of class at most $c$ if $\gamma_{c+1}(H)=\{1\}$.

Question (D. V. Lytkina, Kourovka Notebook 18.61). Is it true that every $2$-group $G$ satisfying \[ \forall H\leq G,\qquad |H|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \gamma_4(H)=\{1\} \] is nilpotent; equivalently, must there exist an integer $c\geq 1$ such that $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1\}$? The integer $c$ may depend on $G$; the question does not require $G$ itself to have class at most $3$.

The supplied record additionally states that the answer is affirmative when the hypothesis on finite subgroups is replaced by $\gamma_3(H)=\{1\}$, meaning class at most $2$. This is retained as a supplied background assertion; no separate supporting citation is supplied. The record does not explicitly define its convention for an infinite $2$-group.

Short English statement

Must a possibly infinite $2$-group be nilpotent if every finite subgroup is nilpotent of class at most $3$? The source states that the analogous implication for class at most $2$ holds. Its label of open is supplied registry information, not an independently verified assessment.
