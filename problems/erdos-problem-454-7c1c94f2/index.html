FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Let $p_k$ denote the $k$th prime (so $p_1=2,p_2=3,\dots$). For each integer $n\ge 2$ define \[ f(n):=\min_{1\le i<n}\big(p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}\big). \] The question is whether \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\big(f(n)-2p_n\big)=\infty. \]

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

Only what is stated in the problem text is used: Pomerance $1979$ proved the $\limsup$ is at least $2$. No other external results are imported here.

ATTACK PLAN

  • Rewrite $f(n)-2p_n$ in terms of asymmetry of cumulative prime gaps around index $n$.
  • Prove structural lemmas (parity, easy bounds) that constrain possible behavior.
  • FAST REALITY CHECK: compute $f(n)-2p_n$ for $n$ up to a few thousand and record extremes.

WORK

Fast reality check (exact computation). Using exact primes and a direct $O(n^2)$ scan for $n\le N$, I found:

  • For $2\le n\le 200$, the maximum of $f(n)-2p_n$ is $12$ (at $n=189$) and the minimum is $-36$ (at $n=198$).
  • For $2\le n\le 2000$, the maximum is $26$ and the minimum is $-208$.
  • For $2\le n\le 5000$, the maximum is $32$ and the minimum is $-294$.
  • For $2\le n\le 20000$, the maximum is $58$ (achieved already with $i=1$) and the minimum is $-614$.
These computations do not decide the $\limsup$ question but confirm the quantity takes both positive and negative values and grows (slowly) in this range.

Lemma 454.1 (parity for $n\ge 3$). For every $n\ge 3$, $f(n)$ is even. Consequently $f(n)-2p_n$ is an even integer.

Proof. For $n\ge 3$ consider two specific indices: \begin{align*} S_{n-1}&:=p_{n+(n-1)}+p_{n-(n-1)}=p_{2n-1}+p_1=p_{2n-1}+2,\\ S_{n-2}&:=p_{n+(n-2)}+p_{n-(n-2)}=p_{2n-2}+p_2=p_{2n-2}+3. \end{align*} The number $S_{n-1}$ is odd (odd prime $p_{2n-1}$ plus $2$), while $S_{n-2}$ is even (odd prime $p_{2n-2}$ plus $3$). Since consecutive odd primes differ by at least $2$, we have $p_{2n-1}\ge p_{2n-2}+2$, hence \[ S_{n-1}=p_{2n-1}+2\ge (p_{2n-2}+2)+2=p_{2n-2}+4>p_{2n-2}+3=S_{n-2}. \] Therefore, among the candidates in the minimum defining $f(n)$, the odd candidate $S_{n-1}$ is strictly larger than the even candidate $S_{n-2}$. Hence the minimum is attained at some $i\le n-2$, for which both primes $p_{n+i},p_{n-i}$ are odd, so $p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}$ is even. Thus $f(n)$ is even. Since $p_n$ is odd for $n\ge 2$, $2p_n$ is even, so $f(n)-2p_n$ is even. $\square$

Lemma 454.2 (gap-difference upper bound from the $i=1$ term). For every $n\ge 2$, \[ f(n)\le p_{n+1}+p_{n-1}, \] and hence \[ f(n)-2p_n\le (p_{n+1}-p_n)-(p_n-p_{n-1}). \]

Proof. Since $i=1$ is allowed in the defining minimum, we have \[ f(n)=\min_{1\le i<n}(p_{n+i}+p_{n-i})\le p_{n+1}+p_{n-1}. \] Subtracting $2p_n$ gives \[ f(n)-2p_n\le (p_{n+1}+p_{n-1})-2p_n=(p_{n+1}-p_n)-(p_n-p_{n-1}). \] $\square$

VERIFICATION

  • Lemma 454.1 carefully handles the only parity-odd candidate in the minimum (the one involving $p_1=2$) by comparing it to an explicit even candidate.
  • Lemma 454.2 uses only that $i=1$ is included in the minimum; no other assumptions.
  • The computations are consistent with Lemma 454.1: for $n\ge 3$ all observed values of $f(n)-2p_n$ are even.

FINAL. UNRESOLVED

(i) Strongest proved partial result. For $n\ge 3$, $f(n)-2p_n$ is always even (Lemma 454.1). Also $f(n)-2p_n$ is bounded above by the local prime-gap difference $(p_{n+1}-p_n)-(p_n-p_{n-1})$ (Lemma 454.2), and exact computations up to $n=20000$ show the quantity attains values as large as $58$ and as small as $-614$ in that range.

(ii) First gap (crisp). Prove that there exist arbitrarily large $n$ such that for all $1\le i<n$, \[ p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}\ge 2p_n + T \] for a given large threshold $T$ (equivalently $f(n)-2p_n\ge T$), or else construct $n$ showing a uniform upper bound.

(iii) Top 3 next moves.

  1. Try to relate $f(n)$ to a "convexity" property of the prime sequence in index space: show that if cumulative gaps to the right dominate cumulative gaps to the left for all $i$, then $f(n)-2p_n$ is large.
  2. Computation: push the brute force to $n\le 10^5$ (with optimization) and record the growth rate of $\max_{m\le n}(f(m)-2p_m)$.
  3. Attempt a disproof strategy: look for a plausible mechanism forcing some $i$ to make $p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}\le 2p_n+C$ uniformly (e.g. by pairing a large forward gap with a smaller backward cumulative gap).

(iv) Minimal counterexample structure. A minimal counterexample to the conjecture $\limsup(f(n)-2p_n)=\infty$ would be a constant $C$ such that for all large $n$ there exists some $1\le i<n$ with $p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}\le 2p_n+C$; such an $i$ would witness a strong asymmetry between prime gaps to the right and left of index $n$.
