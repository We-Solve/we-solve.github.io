Tropical Curve Counts for Smooth Cubic Log Divisors

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $E\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2$ be a smooth plane cubic, and let $X,M_E$ denote $X={\mathbb{P}}^2$ with the divisorial logarithmic structure determined by $E$: its local sections are regular functions that are invertible away from $E$. This logarithmic pair is denoted by ${\mathbb{P}}^2(\log E)$ in the supplied record. The base field is not specified in that record.

Logarithmic Gromov--Witten invariants of this pair are virtual curve invariants defined using moduli spaces of stable logarithmic maps to $(X,M_E)$, together with specified discrete data and insertions. Such data include the genus, curve class, marked points, and contact orders along $E$. Denote a chosen invariant, once its conventions and admissible data $\mathcal D$ have been fixed, by $\operatorname{GW}^{\log}_{\mathcal D}({\mathbb{P}}^2,E)$.

Construction and correspondence problem. For a smooth cubic plane curve $E$, construct an appropriate tropical space $\mathcal T_E$, a notion of tropical curves in that space, and a counting rule with specified multiplicities and matching constraints, such that \[ N^{\mathrm{trop}}_{\mathcal D}(\mathcal T_E) = \operatorname{GW}^{\log}_{\mathcal D}({\mathbb{P}}^2,E) \] for the intended range of corresponding enumerative data $\mathcal D$.

Unresolved scope. The supplied statement does not specify the structure required of $\mathcal T_E$, the tropical counting rule, the base field, the range of genera and curve classes, the contact conditions, the insertions, or the conventions for the logarithmic invariants. Thus the displayed equality records the requested correspondence, rather than a fully quantified conjecture. These choices and their admissibility conditions must be supplied before a precise theorem can be stated.

The original excerpt motivates changing the logarithmic divisor through a toric correspondence slogan: tropical curves in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ and algebraic curves in a projective toric variety have matching nonnegative counts when constraints, genus, and degree match. It supplies no hypotheses making that slogan a general theorem. In particular, it does not establish nonnegativity, integrality, or an unweighted enumerative interpretation for the logarithmic invariants sought here.

Short English statement

Construct a tropical space and a curve-counting theory corresponding to logarithmic Gromov--Witten invariants of the projective plane with a smooth cubic boundary. The source leaves the precise tropical structure, invariant conventions, and range of curve data unspecified.
