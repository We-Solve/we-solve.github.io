Math-6932

Monotonicity of Hook Counts in Symmetric Partitions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$. A partition $\lambda\vdash n$ is a finite nonincreasing sequence of positive integers with sum $n$, with the empty partition allowed when $n=0$. Its diagram is $D(\lambda)=\{(i,j):1\leq j\leq\lambda_i\}$, its conjugate is $\lambda'_j=\#\{i:\lambda_i\geq j\}$, and the hook length of a cell is \[ h_\lambda(i,j)=\lambda_i-j+\lambda'_j-i+1. \] A $t$-hook means a cell whose hook length is exactly $t$; hooks are counted with multiplicity over cells and partitions. A partition is self-conjugate if $\lambda=\lambda'$.

The supplied excerpt does not define doubled-distinct partitions. To make the statement explicit, use the following working convention, subject to confirmation against the cited source. If $r$ is the number of diagonal cells, write the Frobenius coordinates as $a_i=\lambda_i-i$ and $b_i=\lambda'_i-i$, for $1\leq i\leq r$. A doubled-distinct partition satisfies $a_i=b_i+1$ for every $i$. Include the empty partition. The conjugate convention $b_i=a_i+1$ gives the same total hook counts, since conjugation preserves hook lengths; the source's intended definition still requires confirmation.

For $t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}$, define \[ \operatorname{sc}^{\star}_t(n)= \sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash n\\\lambda=\lambda'}} \#\{(i,j)\in D(\lambda):h_\lambda(i,j)=t\}, \qquad \operatorname{dd}^{\star}_t(n)= \sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash n\\\lambda\text{ doubled-distinct}}} \#\{(i,j)\in D(\lambda):h_\lambda(i,j)=t\}. \] Both counts are zero at $n=0$.

Conjecture 3 as expressed in the original excerpt. Interpreting $n\gg t$ with an implied constant independent of $t$ as a uniform linear threshold, the proposed statement is \[ \exists C>0\ \forall t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\quad n>Ct\ \Longrightarrow\ \begin{cases} \operatorname{sc}^{\star}_t(n)\geq\operatorname{sc}^{\star}_{t+1}(n),\\ \operatorname{dd}^{\star}_t(n)\geq\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_{t+1}(n). \end{cases} \] The excerpt additionally proposes \[ \forall t\in\{8,10,12,\ldots\}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\qquad \operatorname{dd}^{\star}_t(n)\geq\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_{t+1}(n). \] Unresolved threshold discrepancy. The supplied clean statement instead asks for a threshold bounded uniformly in $t$, which literally gives the stronger formulation \[ \exists N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\ \forall t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall n\geq N,\qquad \operatorname{sc}^{\star}_t(n)\geq\operatorname{sc}^{\star}_{t+1}(n) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{dd}^{\star}_t(n)\geq\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_{t+1}(n). \] The record does not resolve whether this stronger formulation was intended; it must not be substituted silently for the original uniform linear threshold.

Supplied partial-result context, not independently verified. The excerpt says that asymptotic results cited there as [6] and [2] establish eventual inequalities separately for each fixed $t$, allowing a threshold depending on $t$. It reports the following as Theorems 4 and 5:

  • For every integer $k\geq1$ and every integer $n>84k$, $\operatorname{sc}^{\star}_{2k-1}(n)\geq\operatorname{sc}^{\star}_{2k}(n)$.
  • For every integer $n\geq0$ other than $6,10$, $\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_1(n)\geq\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_2(n)$.
  • For all integers $k\geq1$ and $n\geq0$ other than the pairs $(k,n)=(1,12),(2,10),(3,14)$, $\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_{2k}(n)\geq\operatorname{dd}^{\star}_{2k+1}(n)$.

Short English statement

Count all cells of hook length $t$ across self-conjugate partitions and across doubled-distinct partitions of $n$. The original conjecture asks whether both totals decrease from $t$ to $t+1$ whenever $n>Ct$, with one constant $C$ independent of $t$. The cleaned record instead suggests an absolute threshold independent of $t$; this stronger interpretation remains unresolved. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports specific proved cases and exceptions.
