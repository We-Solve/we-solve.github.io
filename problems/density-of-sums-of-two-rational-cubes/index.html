Math-914

Density of Sums of Two Rational Cubes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, and define \[ \mathcal S=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\text{there exist }x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ with }n=x^3+y^3\}. \] The rational numbers $x,y$ are unrestricted: the supplied statement imposes no positivity, integrality, distinctness, or nonzero condition on them. For each $M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$, put \[ A(M)=\#\left(\mathcal S\cap\{1,\ldots,M\}\right). \] Determine whether the natural density \[ \delta(\mathcal S)=\lim_{\substack{M\to\infty\\M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}}\frac{A(M)}{M} \] exists and, if it does, determine its value. In particular, the supplied curated formulation asks whether \[ \lim_{\substack{M\to\infty\\M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}}\frac{A(M)}{M}=\frac12. \] Here natural density is the interpretation of the curated phrase "asymptotic proportion." The original quoted question asks only what proportion is representable; the explicit value $1/2$ appears in the curated statement and literature assessment. The supplied assessment labels the problem partially solved and reports positive proportions of representable and nonrepresentable integers, with improved bounds from newer Selmer-group work. It supplies neither numerical bounds nor their precise hypotheses. These are supplied literature qualifications, not independently verified assertions in this draft.

Short English statement

Among all positive integers up to $M$, count those expressible as $x^3+y^3$ with $x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}$. Determine whether their proportion converges and whether its limit is $1/2$. The supplied curation describes this as partially solved; this draft requires human mathematical and source review.
