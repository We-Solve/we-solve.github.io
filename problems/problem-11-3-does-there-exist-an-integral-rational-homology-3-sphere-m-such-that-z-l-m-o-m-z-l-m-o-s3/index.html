Problem 11.3 — Does there exist an integral/rational homology 3-sphere M such that Z L M O(M ) = Z L M O(S3)?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A closed oriented $3$-manifold $M$ is an integral homology sphere if $H_i(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_i(S^3;{\mathbb{Z}})$ for all $i$, and a rational homology sphere if the same holds over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. Let $Z_{\rm LMO}(M)$ denote the Le--Murakami--Ohtsuki invariant, valued in its completed diagram algebra with the source's normalization. Equality of these invariants means equality of the full completed elements, not just a fixed finite-degree truncation.

The literal extracted question asks whether an integral/rational homology $3$-sphere $M$ satisfies \[ Z_{\rm LMO}(M)=Z_{\rm LMO}(S^3). \] It omits a nontriviality requirement and thus literally permits $M=S^3$. The supplied research summary explicitly identifies the intended issue as existence of a nontrivial integral homology sphere with this equality, equivalently detection of $S^3$. Under that intended qualification, the integral version is \[ \exists M:\quad H_*(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_*(S^3;{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad M\not\cong S^3,\quad Z_{\rm LMO}(M)=Z_{\rm LMO}(S^3). \] The rational version replaces the homology condition by its ${\mathbb{Q}}$-coefficient counterpart. The statement's slash does not decide between these versions, and they are not asserted equivalent. The normalization and degree-zero conventions, particularly for rational homology spheres, must be fixed before the equality is used. The trailing heading "11.3 Characterization of the image of the LMO invariant" is surrounding source context, not an additional precise request in this record.

Short English statement

Is there a homology three-sphere other than $S^3$ with exactly the same full LMO invariant as $S^3$? The intended nontriviality comes from the supplied research summary; it is missing from the literal question. Integral and rational versions and normalization must remain distinct.
