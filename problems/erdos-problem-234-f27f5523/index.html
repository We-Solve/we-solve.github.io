Statement

Let $p_n$ denote the $n$th prime ($p_1=2,p_2=3,\dots$) and let \[d_n := p_{n+1}-p_n \qquad (n\ge 1)\] be the $n$th prime gap. Throughout, $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm.

For $c\ge 0$, define the set \[A_c := \left\{n\in\mathbb{N} : n\ge 2\ \text{and}\ \frac{d_n}{\log n} < c\right\}. \] (We restrict to $n\ge 2$ so that $\log n$ is defined.)

Define the natural density of $A_c$ (if it exists) by \[ f(c) := \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N}\,\left|A_c\cap\{1,2,\dots,N\}\right|. \]

Claim/conjecture. For every $c\ge 0$, the limit defining $f(c)$ exists, and the resulting function $f:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ is continuous.

Edge cases. For $c=0$, $A_0=\varnothing$ (since $d_n>0$), so $f(0)=0$ if the above definition is used.

Restatement from Math-723-A2 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.

Erdős Problem #234 (Round 2)

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): obstruction/gap-closure. Round 1 left two distinct gaps: existence of the densities \[ F_N(c):=\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{2\le n\le N:\ \frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log n}<c\right\} \] for fixed $c$, and continuity of the limiting function if the limit exists. The most promising strict advance is to remove one of those obstacles unconditionally: I will prove a quantitative upper bound \[ F_N(c)\ll c \] for fixed $c>0$. This shows that any putative limiting distribution is automatically continuous at $0$, so the only genuinely open part that remains is existence of the densities and continuity away from the origin.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 results without re-proving them.

  • Round-1 Lemma 234.1: the normalized gaps \[ a_n:=\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log n} \] have mean value $1+o(1)$, i.e. \[ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=2}^{N} a_n \to 1. \]
  • Round-1 Corollary 234.2: for every fixed $c>0$, \[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\{2\le n\le N: a_n\ge c\}\le \frac{1}{c}, \] so any limiting law would be tight on the right.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

The new tool is a pair-counting upper bound averaged over short gap lengths. The key quantitative input is a standard Selberg-sieve prime-pair estimate together with the elementary average-order identity \[ \sum_{h\le H}\frac{h}{\phi(h)}\ll H. \] This converts upper bounds for prime pairs into an upper bound for the frequency of small consecutive prime gaps.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

After Round 1, the exact unresolved issue was: even if one believes the densities should exist, no rigorous argument had been given for continuity of the limiting function. I now target the specific sub-gap at $c=0$.

The precise claim to prove is:

Claim 234-R2. There exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that for every fixed $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$, \[ F_N(c)\le Cc+O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right). \] In particular, \[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}F_N(c)\le Cc. \]

This overcomes one concrete obstacle from Round 1: continuity at the origin is no longer part of the open problem.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Let \[ d_n:=p_{n+1}-p_n \qquad (n\ge 1). \]

I will use the following standard external theorem.

Prime-pair upper bound (Selberg sieve). There exists an absolute constant $A>0$ such that for all integers $h\ge 2$ and all $X\ge h+2$, \[ \Pi(X;h):=\#\{p\le X:\ p\text{ and }p+h\text{ are both prime}\} \le A\,\frac{h}{\phi(h)}\,\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}. \] For odd $h>1$, the left-hand side is actually $0$.

The only other ingredient needed is the following elementary mean-value lemma.

Lemma 234.2.1. For all $H\ge 1$, \[ \sum_{h\le H}\frac{h}{\phi(h)}\ll H. \]

Proof. Use the divisor identity \[ \frac{n}{\phi(n)}=\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\mu^2(d)}{\phi(d)}. \] Indeed, both sides are multiplicative, and on prime powers $p^k$ they equal \[ \frac{p}{p-1}=1+\frac{1}{p-1}. \] Therefore \[ \sum_{h\le H}\frac{h}{\phi(h)} = \sum_{h\le H}\sum_{d\mid h}\frac{\mu^2(d)}{\phi(d)} = \sum_{d\le H}\frac{\mu^2(d)}{\phi(d)}\lfloor\frac{H}{d}\rfloor. \] Hence \[ \sum_{h\le H}\frac{h}{\phi(h)} \le H\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^2(d)}{d\phi(d)}. \] The Euler product for the last series is \[ \prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\right), \] which converges absolutely. So the whole sum is $O(H)$. $\square$

Now prove the announced bound on small normalized gaps.

Theorem 234.2.2. There exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that for every fixed $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$, \[ F_N(c)\le Cc+O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right). \] Consequently, \[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}F_N(c)\le Cc. \]

Proof. Set \[ X:=p_{N+1}. \] If $2\le n\le N$ and $d_n/\log n<c$, then certainly $d_n<c\log N$ because $\log n\le \log N$. Therefore \[ N F_N(c) \le \#\{2\le n\le N:\ d_n<c\log N\}. \] Now all prime gaps are even except the unique gap $p_2-p_1=1$. Hence \[ \#\{2\le n\le N:\ d_n<c\log N\} \le 1+\sum_{2\le h<c\log N}\#\{2\le n\le N:\ d_n=h\}. \] If $d_n=h$, then $(p_n,p_n+h)$ is a prime pair with $p_n\le p_N<X$. Discarding the consecutiveness condition can only enlarge the count, so \[ \#\{2\le n\le N:\ d_n=h\}\le \Pi(X;h). \] Hence \[ N F_N(c) \le 1+\sum_{2\le h<c\log N}\Pi(X;h) \le 1+A\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}\sum_{2\le h<c\log N}\frac{h}{\phi(h)}. \] By Lemma 234.2.1 with $H=\lfloor c\log N\rfloor$, \[ \sum_{2\le h<c\log N}\frac{h}{\phi(h)}\ll c\log N+1. \] Also, by the prime number theorem, \[ X=p_{N+1}\asymp N\log N, \qquad \log X\asymp \log N. \] Therefore \[ N F_N(c) \ll 1+\frac{N\log N}{(\log N)^2}(c\log N+1) \ll 1+cN. \] Dividing by $N$ yields \[ F_N(c)\le Cc+O\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\right) \] for some absolute $C$. $\square$

Corollary 234.2.3. If the densities \[ f(c)=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_N(c) \] exist for all $c\ge 0$, then automatically \[ f(c)\ll c \qquad (c\downarrow 0), \] so $f$ is continuous at $0$.

Proof. Since $f(0)=0$ and Theorem 234.2.2 gives $f(c)\le Cc$ for every fixed $c>0$, one has $f(c)\to 0$ as $c\downarrow 0$. $\square$

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Did I silently use consecutiveness where only prime pairs were counted? No. I only used the inequality \[ \#\{n:d_n=h\}\le \Pi(X;h), \] which is valid because every consecutive prime pair is a prime pair, but not conversely.

  • What about odd gaps? For $n\ge 2$, prime gaps are even. The exceptional gap $1$ between $2$ and $3$ contributes the harmless additive term $1$.

  • Are the sieve hypotheses satisfied? Yes. Here $h<c\log N$ and $X=p_{N+1}\asymp N\log N$, so $h\le X$ for all large $N$.

  • Could the bound depend on $c$ in a hidden way? The theorem is uniform in fixed $c$ because the only $c$-dependence enters through the upper limit $H\asymp c\log N$ in Lemma 234.2.1.

  • Did this prove too much? No. The argument only bounds the upper density of small gaps. It does not control oscillation of $F_N(c)$ in $N$, so it does not prove the existence of the density.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

The new rigorous advance over Round 1 is:

  • the continuity issue at $c=0$ is now settled conditionally on existence of the densities, because any putative limit must satisfy $f(c)\ll c$ as $c\downarrow 0$;
  • equivalently, the only genuinely open part remaining is existence of the densities and continuity for positive $c$.

The exact unresolved gap is still the same hard one identified in Round 1: proving that $F_N(c)$ has a limit for each fixed $c$, or producing a value of $c$ for which the densities fail to exist.

Erdős Problem #234: independent continuation

Date: 23 September 2026. Source versions read in full: 234.tex in gpt_pro_5.4.

1) OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE AUDIT

For $a_n=(p_{n+1}-p_n)/\log n$, the problem asks that the empirical distribution converge to a continuous distribution. The source supplies a sieve-based small-gap upper bound and correctly leaves convergence open. Its assertion that there are no prime pairs of odd difference is false: $2$ and $2+h$ can both be prime. There is at most one such pair for each odd $h$. Consecutive gaps for $n\ge2$ are even, so restricting that argument to even $h$ removes the error without changing the intended estimate.

2) WORK: WHAT COMPACTNESS DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE

Assume the mean estimate already used in the source, \[ \frac1{N-1}\sum_{n=2}^N a_n\longrightarrow1. \] Define probability measures $\mu_N=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{n=2}^N\delta_{a_n}$. For every $R>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mu_N([R,\infty))\le(1+o(1))/R$. These measures are tight. One can prove subsequential compactness directly: choose successively subsequences on which $\mu_N([0,q])$ converges for each nonnegative rational $q$, use a diagonal subsequence, then take right limits to obtain a nondecreasing right-continuous distribution function. Tightness gives total mass one; squeezing between rational endpoints gives convergence at every continuity point of the limiting function.

Every resulting probability measure $\mu$ satisfies \[ \int_0^\infty t\,d\mu(t)\le1. \] For proof, apply weak convergence to $\min(t,R)$, whose average is at most $1+o(1)$, and let $R$ increase. Equality of the limiting mean is not justified without uniform integrability: a small amount of mass can escape to large values while preserving total first moment.

If one additionally retains the source's small-gap estimate $\limsup_N\mu_N([0,c))\le Cc$, then the open-set inequality for weak convergence gives $\mu([0,c))\le Cc$. Letting $c$ decrease to zero shows $\mu(\{0\})=0$. This last conclusion uses the stated sieve input; the compactness and moment arguments above do not require it.

3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

Neither tightness nor the absence of an atom at zero implies uniqueness of the subsequential measure. They also do not exclude an atom at a positive point. For example, $\delta_1$ and $(\delta_{1/2}+\delta_{3/2})/2$ both have mean one and no atom at zero. This is an illustration of the logical gap, not a model of the actual prime gaps.

7) FINAL AND REFERENCE

UNRESOLVED. The precise remaining tasks are uniqueness of all subsequential limits and absence of positive atoms. The supplied elementary bounds do not supply either. No new prime-gap theorem is claimed. Exact target checked at https://www.erdosproblems.com/234 through its indexed primary record.

8) COMPLETION ESTIMATE

COMPLETION: 5% This is a subjective estimate of progress toward the entire stated problem, not a probability that the conjecture or an unverified proof is correct.

Erdős Problem #234

  1. FORMAL RESTATEMENT.

    Let $p_n$ denote the $n$th prime ($p_1=2,p_2=3,\dots$) and let \[d_n := p_{n+1}-p_n \qquad (n\ge 1)\] be the $n$th prime gap. Throughout, $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm.

    For $c\ge 0$, define the set \[A_c := \left\{n\in\mathbb{N} : n\ge 2\ \text{and}\ \frac{d_n}{\log n} < c\right\}. \] (We restrict to $n\ge 2$ so that $\log n$ is defined.)

    Define the natural density of $A_c$ (if it exists) by \[ f(c) := \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N}\,\left|A_c\cap\{1,2,\dots,N\}\right|. \]

    Claim/conjecture. For every $c\ge 0$, the limit defining $f(c)$ exists, and the resulting function $f:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ is continuous.

    Edge cases. For $c=0$, $A_0=\varnothing$ (since $d_n>0$), so $f(0)=0$ if the above definition is used.

  2. QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK.

    This is an "existence of limiting distribution" problem for the normalized gaps $a_n:=d_n/\log n$. Heuristically, in Cramér/Hardy--Littlewood models one expects a limiting distribution (often Poisson/exponential for suitably normalized gaps), which would in particular imply the existence of $f(c)$ and its continuity.

    Unconditionally, one has very strong information on extremes of prime gaps (liminf $=0$ in the $d_n/\log p_n$ normalization, and arbitrarily large multiples of the average gap), but no unconditional theorem gives the full limiting distribution of the normalized gaps.

  3. ATTACK PLAN.

    Proof strategies.

    • $P1$ Try to show convergence of the empirical distribution functions $F_N(c):=\frac1N|\{2\le n\le N: d_n/\log n<c\}|$ to a limit using deep distributional results on primes in short intervals / correlations of primes.
    • $P2$ Prove existence of the density for all $c$ by deriving asymptotics for counts of gaps $\le c\log n$ from (a strong form of) the Hardy--Littlewood prime tuples conjecture.

    Disproof/construction strategies.

    • $D1$ Attempt to use known irregularity phenomena for primes in short intervals (à la Maier) to construct a $c$ for which $F_N(c)$ has distinct limit points.
    • $D2$ Attempt to show $f$ cannot be continuous by forcing a nontrivial point mass in any putative limiting distribution.

    Best path chosen here. In this write-up, the proof track $P1$--$P2$ and the disproof track $D1$--$D2$ both stall at the point where one would need substantially finer distributional control of prime gaps than is established here. Accordingly, I record rigorous partial results that any full solution must be consistent with: (i) the normalized gaps have mean $1+o(1)$; (ii) this yields quantitative bounds on upper/lower densities for large $c$.

  4. WORK.

    Lemma 234.1 (Mean of normalized gaps).
    Let $a_n:=\dfrac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log n}$ for $n\ge 2$. Assuming the Prime Number Theorem, one has \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=2}^{N} a_n = 1. \]
    Proof: Write $d_n:=p_{n+1}-p_n$. For $N\ge 2$ consider \[ S(N):=\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{d_n}{\log n}. \] For each $n\ge 2$ we have \[ \frac{d_n}{\log n}=\int_{p_n}^{p_{n+1}} \frac{dt}{\log n}. \] On the interval $[p_n,p_{n+1})$ the prime-counting function satisfies $\pi(t)=n$, hence $\log n = \log \pi(t)$ there. Summing over $n=2,\dots,N$ and concatenating the integrals gives the identity \[ S(N)=\int_{p_2}^{p_{N+1}}\frac{dt}{\log\pi(t)}. \] By the Prime Number Theorem, $\pi(t)\sim t/\log t$, so for sufficiently large $t$ we have \[ \log\pi(t)=\log t + O(\log\log t). \] For large $t$, the error term satisfies $|O(\log\log t)|\le \tfrac12\log t$, so the function $u\mapsto 1/(\log t+u)$ can be expanded to first order, yielding \[ \frac{1}{\log\pi(t)} = \frac{1}{\log t} + O\!\left(\frac{\log\log t}{(\log t)^2}\right). \] Therefore, with $X:=p_{N+1}$, \[ S(N)=\int_{p_2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t} \;+ O\!\left(\int_{p_2}^{X}\frac{\log\log t}{(\log t)^2}\,dt\right). \] The main integral is the logarithmic integral $\operatorname{li}(X)$ up to an additive constant. By integration by parts, \[ \int_{2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t}=\frac{X}{\log X}+O\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}\right). \] For the error integral, since the integrand is eventually increasing in $t$ and bounded above by its value at $X$, one has \[ \int_{2}^{X}\frac{\log\log t}{(\log t)^2}\,dt \ll \frac{X\log\log X}{(\log X)^2}. \] Combining these estimates gives \[ S(N)=\frac{X}{\log X}+O\!\left(\frac{X\log\log X}{(\log X)^2}\right). \] Finally, by the Prime Number Theorem in the equivalent form $p_{N+1}\sim (N+1)\log(N+1)$, we have $X\sim N\log N$ and $\log X = \log N + O(\log\log N)$. Hence \[ \frac{X}{\log X}=N\,(1+o(1)), \qquad \frac{X\log\log X}{(\log X)^2}=N\,o(1). \] Therefore $S(N)=N+o(N)$, and dividing by $N$ yields \[ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=2}^{N}a_n = \frac{S(N)}{N} \to 1. \] ∎
    Corollary 234.2 (A Markov-type bound on large normalized gaps).
    Assume Lemma 234.1. Fix $c>0$. Then \[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left|\{2\le n\le N: a_n\ge c\}\right|\le \frac{1}{c}. \] In particular, for $c>1$, \[ \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left|\{2\le n\le N: a_n< c\}\right|\ge 1-\frac{1}{c}. \]
    Proof: For each $N$ and $c>0$, \[ \sum_{n=2}^{N}a_n \ge \sum_{\substack{2\le n\le N\\ a_n\ge c}} a_n \ge c\,\left|\{2\le n\le N: a_n\ge c\}\right|. \] Divide by $N$ and take $\limsup$; Lemma 234.1 gives $\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=2}^N a_n =1$, hence the displayed bound. The second inequality is the complement. ∎

    What this does not give. These arguments control only the first moment of $a_n$ and hence give only coarse bounds on the distribution of $a_n$; they do not address existence of the density $f(c)$ for a fixed $c$, nor continuity of $f$.

  5. VERIFICATION.

    • Quantifiers. Lemma 234.1 uses only $N\to\infty$ and PNT; the conversion $S(N)=\int dt/\log\pi(t)$ is exact.
    • Edge cases. The definition starts at $n\ge 2$ so $\log n$ is defined. Any finite number of initial terms does not affect densities.
    • Error control. The step $1/\log\pi(t)=1/\log t + O(\log\log t/(\log t)^2)$ requires $\log\pi(t)=\log t + O(\log\log t)$ and that $\log t$ dominates $\log\log t$; this holds for all sufficiently large $t$.

  6. FINAL.

    UNRESOLVED.

    • (i) Strongest fully proved partial result obtained here. Assuming PNT, the mean of the normalized gaps $a_n=(p_{n+1}-p_n)/\log n$ tends to $1$, and hence for every fixed $c>0$, $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N|\{n\le N: a_n\ge c\}|\le 1/c$.
    • (ii) First gap. No unconditional (or even PNT-level) method in this write-up proves that the limit defining $f(c)$ exists for an arbitrary fixed $c$, nor continuity of the putative limit function.
    • (iii) Top 3 next moves.
      • Target an "asymptotic for the count of gaps $\le c\log n$" from a strong Hardy--Littlewood prime tuples input.
      • Try to convert known irregularity results for primes in short intervals into oscillations of $F_N(c)$ to attack existence of the density.
      • Study whether any currently known moment information on prime gaps (even conditional) implies continuity/no-atom properties of a limiting law.
    • (iv) What a minimal counterexample would likely look like. A value $c_0$ and two sequences $N_j\to\infty$, $M_j\to\infty$ such that $F_{N_j}(c_0)$ and $F_{M_j}(c_0)$ converge to different limits, i.e. a persistent oscillation in the frequency of "$d_n< c_0\log n$".
