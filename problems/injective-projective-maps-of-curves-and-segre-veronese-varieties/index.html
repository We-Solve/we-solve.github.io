Injective Projective Maps of Curves and Segre–Veronese Varieties

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The record asks three existence questions concerning injective projective maps. It does not explicitly define "injected." The formulations below use an algebraic morphism that is injective on complex points; they do not require a closed immersion. This interpretation requires confirmation from the source. We use the convention that a variety is integral, so that a smooth projective curve is connected; the record does not explicitly state this convention.

For a complex projective variety $X$, an injective morphism to ${\mathbb{P}}^m_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ means a morphism $f:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^m_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ such that \[ \forall x,y\in X({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad f(x)=f(y)\Longrightarrow x=y. \] Under this interpretation, the curve question is: \[ \text{For every smooth complex projective curve }C,\quad \text{does there exist an injective morphism }C\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^2_{{\mathbb{C}}}? \] The supplied corrected statement also asks the general question: \[ \text{For every integer }d\geq 1\text{ and every smooth complex projective variety }X \text{ with }\dim X=d,\quad \text{does there exist an injective morphism }X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2d}_{{\mathbb{C}}}? \] Here the complex ground field is carried over from the curve question; the general question does not explicitly specify its ground field. The displayed formulation concerns positive dimensions; the source does not specify the range of $d$.

For the Segre--Veronese question, the standard family is defined as follows. Let $r\geq 1$, let $n_1,\ldots,n_r\geq 1$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_r\geq 1$ be integers, and put \[ D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_i,\qquad N+1=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{n_i+a_i}{a_i}. \] The complete linear system of $\mathcal O(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ gives the Segre--Veronese embedding \[ \nu_{\boldsymbol a}:\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{P}}^{n_i}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^N_{{\mathbb{C}}}; \qquad S=\operatorname{im}(\nu_{\boldsymbol a}),\quad \dim S=D. \] The source does not specify restrictions on these parameters or explicitly state the ground field for this question.

When $N>2D$, a linear projection to ${\mathbb{P}}^{2D}_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ is induced by a surjective linear map \[ q:{\mathbb{C}}^{N+1}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2D+1}. \] Its center is $\Lambda={\mathbb{P}}(\ker q)$, of dimension $N-2D-1$. If $\Lambda\cap S=\varnothing$, its restriction to $S$ is a morphism. Thus the question, in the range where a projection to the stated target exists, is whether for every such $S$ there is a $q$ with \[ {\mathbb{P}}(\ker q)\cap S=\varnothing, \qquad \forall x,y\in S({\mathbb{C}}),\quad {\mathbb{P}}(q)(x)={\mathbb{P}}(q)(y)\Longrightarrow x=y. \] For $N=2D$, an invertible linear map supplies the target identification. For $N<2D$, a projection onto ${\mathbb{P}}^{2D}_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ in this sense does not exist. The source does not explain whether these parameter choices are excluded, whether a linear inclusion into the target is allowed, or whether the intended target has dimension at most $2D$. This qualification remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether every smooth complex projective curve admits an injective algebraic morphism to the projective plane, whether every smooth projective variety of dimension $d$ admits one to ${\mathbb{P}}^{2d}$, and whether every Segre--Veronese embedding admits an injective linear projection to a projective space of twice its dimension. The meaning of injection, the ground field in the latter two questions, connectedness conventions, and projection terminology when the ambient dimension is too small require confirmation. These are statement-only questions; the supplied open status is not independently verified.
