Question 4. For a 2 × 2 table of probabilities

$ p00 p01 p10 p11 $: does it makes sense to fix values of two of the odds ratios α = p00 p11

p01 p10

and α∗ = p00 p01

p11 p10

and look at the locus of possible values for the third odds-ratio α∗∗ = p00 p10

p01 p11?Describe the curve (in the probability simplex) obtained by intersecting two of these quadric surfaces.

Precise interpretation

Write $(a,b,c,d)=(p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11})$ with nonnegative entries summing to one. The PDF identifies the ratios $\alpha=ad/(bc)$, $\beta=ab/(dc)$, and $\gamma=ac/(bd)$. Here a finite ratio is defined only when its denominator is nonzero. We describe both that rational locus and the cleared equations. We do not assign arbitrary values to $0/0$, or introduce unspecified extended-infinity conventions. This qualification matters because the source does not fix boundary conventions.
Every positive pair and the remaining ratio.
Fix $A,B>0$, set $r=\sqrt{AB}$ and $s=\sqrt{A/B}$. If $\alpha=A,\beta=B$ on the rational domain, all four entries are positive. Multiplication and division of the two equations give $a/c=r$ and $d/b=s$. Conversely these two identities imply both prescribed ratios. Setting $t=b/c>0$ and normalizing gives exactly \[ (a,b,c,d)=\frac{(r,t,1,st)}{1+r+(1+s)t},\qquad t>0, \quad\gamma=B/t^2. \] Thus every positive value of $\gamma$ occurs exactly once. The locus is the open affine segment from $u=(r,0,1,0)/(r+1)$ to $v=(0,1,0,s)/(1+s)$: its convex coefficient at $v$ is $\lambda=(1+s)t/[1+r+(1+s)t]$, which increases bijectively from zero to one. Neither endpoint belongs to the rational domain of both fixed ratios.
The cleared intersection is larger.
Consider $ad=Abc$ and $ab=Bdc$ throughout the closed simplex. When all entries are positive the preceding proof applies. If an entry is zero and $A,B>0$, the two equations force either $b=d=0$ or $a=c=0$: for example $a=0$ forces $bc=dc=0$, hence either $c=0$ or $b=d=0$; the cases $b,c,d=0$ follow in the same way by substitution. Conversely every point on either of these coordinate edges satisfies both equations. Hence the nonnegative cleared intersection consists exactly of the closed connector $[u,v]$ together with the edges $\{b=d=0\}$ and $\{a=c=0\}$. The edges must not be treated as tables with the specified finite ratios: their denominators vanish.
Finite zero parameters.
On the rational domain $b,c,d>0$. Therefore $\alpha=0$ if and only if $a=0$, which also forces $\beta=0$. A pair with exactly one zero and the other positive has no rational solution. If $A=B=0$, the rational locus is $a=0$, $b,c,d>0$, $b+c+d=1$, and $\gamma=0$. For completeness, substitution into the cleared equations gives these additional closed-simplex loci. If $A=0<B$, they are the three coordinate edges $\{a=c=0\}$, $\{a=d=0\}$, $\{b=d=0\}$: either $d>0$ forces $a=c=0$, or $d=0$ forces $ab=0$. If $B=0<A$, they are $\{a=c=0\}$, $\{a=b=0\}$, $\{b=d=0\}$, by the same argument with $b$ and $d$ exchanged. If both are zero, $ad=ab=0$ yields the face $a=0$ together with the edge $b=d=0$. Negative finite ratio values are inadmissible for nonnegative tables. Other choices of which two of the three ratios are fixed reduce to this case by permuting $b,c,d$.
