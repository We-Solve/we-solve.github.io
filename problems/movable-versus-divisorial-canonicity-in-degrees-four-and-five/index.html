Math-1230

Movable versus divisorial canonicity in degrees four and five

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Cheltsov asks, for a smooth Fano variety $X$ with $\operatorname{Pic}X\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}$, whether birational superrigidity is equivalent to \[ \forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\ \forall\text{ movable }\mathcal M\subset|-mK_X|: \quad(X,\tfrac1m\mathcal M)\text{ is canonical}.\tag{1} \] Movable means having no fixed divisorial components. Birational superrigidity is understood in the Mori-fiber-space category, requiring birational maps from the rank-one Fano variety to other Mori fiber spaces to be biregular in the appropriate sense. The original attributes the superrigidity-to-(1) implication to MMP.

It also introduces the two distinct readings \[ \forall m>0\ \forall D\in|-mK_X|:\quad (X,\tfrac1mD)\text{ is canonical},\tag{2a} \] or the same condition with "purely log terminal" in place of "canonical," denoted (2b). These are singularity conditions on all individual divisors, stronger in scope than testing movable systems; the canonical and plt alternatives must not be conflated. Canonicity is tested by nonnegative exceptional discrepancies, with the usual boundary conventions. The source reports that sufficiently general degree-$N$ hypersurfaces $X_N\subset{\mathbb{P}}^N$ satisfy (1) and (2) for $N\ge6$, attributing this to Pukhlikov [8], and asks what happens for $N=4,5$.

The attached unreviewed aid reports that the Noether--Fano criterion answers the equivalence and that de Fernex's theorem with its erratum yields (1) for every smooth quartic threefold and quintic fourfold. It leaves the general quartic/quintic (2a) and (2b) questions unresolved in its literature assessment. For each $N\ge4$ it proposes a special smooth degree-$N$ hypersurface satisfying (1) but having an anticanonical divisor $D$ with local canonical and log-canonical thresholds at a point $v$ equal to $(N-2)/N$ and $(N-1)/N$, respectively. This is a claimed special-example obstruction to (1) implying (2), not a statement about sufficiently general hypersurfaces.

The original additionally claims that under (1),(2), $X\times{\mathbb{P}}^1$ has only its two projection Mori-fiber structures, and then says every birational map to a Mori fiber space is an isomorphism. The aid flags the latter literal assertion as false: $\operatorname{Bir}_X(X\times{\mathbb{P}}^1)=\operatorname{PGL}_2({\mathbb{C}}(X))$ in the complex interpretation. Classifying structures up to an appropriate square equivalence differs from prohibiting these fiberwise birational maps. The aid only records the projections as the two regular elementary contractions; it does not establish the full birational-structure classification.

Short English statement

Compare movable-system canonicity with birational superrigidity and all-divisor singularity conditions, especially for quartic threefolds and quintic fourfolds. The product claim also needs an equivalence convention because fiberwise birational maps need not be regular.
