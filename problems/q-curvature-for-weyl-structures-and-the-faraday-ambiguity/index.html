Q-curvature for Weyl structures and the Faraday ambiguity

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M^n,[g])$ be an oriented Riemannian conformal manifold of even dimension. A Weyl structure is a torsion-free connection $D$ preserving the conformal class. In a representative metric write $Dg=-2\alpha\otimes g$; under $\widehat g=e^{2\omega}g$, $\widehat\alpha=\alpha-{\,\mathrm{d}}\omega$. Its Faraday form is $F_D={\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha$, independent of the representative. Call $D$ closed if $F_D=0$, and exact if $D$ is the Levi-Civita connection of a global representative.

Problem 22, whose antecedent is in companion 20002042, asks for a natural top form $\mathcal Q([g],D)$, equivalently a scalar density of weight $-n$, independent of representative and reducing to $Q_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g$ when $D=\nabla^g$. The Riemannian $Q$-curvature normalization is part of the choice. The source suggests that existence might restrict that choice. The supplied aid attributes a general natural cotractor/paired-density construction for smooth Weyl structures in all even $n\geq4$ to Hirachi, Luebbe and Matsumoto, with dimension two handled elementarily; full references and construction conventions are not supplied.

For a closed Weyl structure, the aid gives a patching statement. Locally $\alpha={\,\mathrm{d}}\varphi_i$, so $g_i=e^{2\varphi_i}g$ is $D$-parallel. On connected overlaps the $g_i$ differ by constant homotheties. Since the chosen critical metric $Q$-form is unchanged by constant homothety, the forms $Q_{g_i}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_{g_i}$ patch canonically for that choice of metric $Q$, including nonexact closed structures. This does not require a single globally $D$-parallel metric.

The displayed four-dimensional answer/context attributed to Eastwood--Slovák is \[ \mathcal Q_4=\left(2\mathbf P^2-2\mathbf P^{ab}\mathbf P_{ba}-D_aD^a\mathbf P\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g. \] Here $\mathbf P_{ab}$ is the Weyl Rho tensor, potentially nonsymmetric, $\mathbf P=g^{ab}\mathbf P_{ab}$, and the Weyl derivatives act on the appropriate conformal densities. The extract suppresses those weights and much tensor typography; it is not an unweighted scalar formula independent of volume.

The aid reports the convention-sensitive identity \[ \mathbf P^{ab}\mathbf P_{ba} =\mathbf P^{ab}\mathbf P_{ab}-\tfrac12F_D^{ab}(F_D)_{ab}. \] The reversed contraction is therefore material. Away from the closed locus, $\mathcal Q+cF_D^{\wedge n/2}$ is another natural top-form extension with the same reduction on metric structures. In dimension four, $c|F_D|_g^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g$ is also conformally invariant and can change total integrals on closed manifolds when $F_D\ne0$. Wedge-power ambiguities alone need not change those totals, since a global representative makes $F_D$ exact. The aid says the Eastwood--Slovák and Hirachi--Luebbe--Matsumoto extensions can have different total integrals; no full comparison formula is supplied. Existence therefore does not imply uniqueness off the closed locus, even though all such Faraday additions vanish when $D$ is locally metric.

Short English statement

Extend metric $Q$-curvature naturally to Weyl connections in every even dimension. The supplied aid reports a general construction and canonical local-metric patching for closed Weyl structures. Faraday terms show that agreement with metric $Q$-curvature does not ensure uniqueness for nonclosed structures.
