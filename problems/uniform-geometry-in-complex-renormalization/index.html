Math-1927

Uniform geometry in complex renormalization

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal P$ be a finite collection of critically periodic quadratic polynomials $f(z)=z^2+c$. For a sequence of choices $f_i\in\mathcal P$, write $m_i$ for the exact period of its critical point $0$, so $f_i^{m_i}(0)=0$ and no smaller positive iterate returns to $0$. Retain the source's tuning operation and write \[ g_n=f_1\vdash\cdots\vdash f_n,\qquad n_k=\prod_{j=1}^k m_j. \] Here a superscript on $g_n$ denotes iteration, not pointwise exponentiation. For $k<n$ and the source's indices $i\leq n_k$, consider \[ S_{n,k,i}=\left\{g_n^{\,n_k\ell+i}(0):\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ 0\leq\ell<m_{k+1}\right\}. \] The assertion is that these sets have geometry bounded uniformly as $n$, $k<n$, and $i\leq n_k$ vary, with the finite collection fixed. A bound may depend on that fixed collection, but should not deteriorate with tuning depth or the displayed indices. Whether it must also be uniform over all sequences drawn from the collection is not explicit.

The record does not define bounded geometry or its scale-invariant numerical criterion. Possible separation/diameter ratios, annular-modulus bounds, and other a priori geometry conditions cannot be identified without the source. It also does not define or delimit the tuning operation, give a lower bound for $i$, or specify whether $k=0$ is included. Nonnegative iterates require an appropriate nonnegative index range; the customary alternatives $1\leq i\leq n_k$ and $0\leq i\leq n_k$ are not silently equated. Degenerate critical periods and singleton or repeated-point sets require conventions for any geometry criterion. Thus the orbit sets and intended uniformity are specified, but the exact bounded-geometry theorem is missing a defining predicate.

Short English statement

Iterated tunings from a fixed finite list of critically periodic quadratics should produce certain small critical-orbit configurations with uniformly controlled geometry at every tuning depth. The source does not give the geometry criterion or complete index and tuning conventions.
