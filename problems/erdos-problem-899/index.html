Erdős Problem 899: two substantial cases of difference growth

Statement and scope.
Let $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ be infinite, with $A(N)=|A\cap[1,N]|=o(N)$, and put $D(N)=|(A-A)\cap[1,N]|$. The general theorem, due to Ruzsa, asserts $\limsup D(N)/A(N)=\infty$. The general proof remains unresolved in this write-up. We prove the assertion for sets of positive upper Banach density and for every lacunary sequence. The first case includes very sparse unions of long, densely occupied intervals; the second includes sets with large multiplicative gaps.
Positive upper Banach density forces many differences.
Suppose there are intervals $I_j\subset\mathbb Z$, with lengths tending to infinity, such that $|A\cap I_j|/|I_j|\to\delta>0$. If $A+t_1,\ldots,A+t_k$ are pairwise disjoint translates, then on the same intervals \[ |(A+t_i)\cap I_j|=|A\cap I_j|+O(|t_i|). \] Dividing their total by $|I_j|$ gives $k\delta\leq1$. Consequently a greedy family of pairwise disjoint translates terminates after finitely many steps, at shifts $t_1,\ldots,t_k$. Its maximality says that every translate $A+t$ meets some $A+t_i$, so \[ \mathbb Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^k((A-A)+t_i). \tag{1} \] The sign is harmless because $A-A$ is symmetric.

Let $C=\max_i|t_i|$. Covering the integers $C+1,\ldots,N-C$ by $1$ uses differences in $[1,N]$, and therefore \[ N-2C\leq kD(N). \] Since $A(N)=o(N)$, this proves the stronger conclusion $D(N)/A(N)\to\infty$ in this case. In particular, if $A$ contains intervals of arbitrarily large length, then in fact $A-A=\mathbb Z$. Positive upper Banach density does not imply positive ordinary density, so the case is compatible with the hypothesis of the problem.

Lacunary sets have quadratically many distinct differences.
First suppose $b_1<b_2<\cdots$ and $b_{i+1}\geq3b_i$. For fixed $j\geq2$, the $j-1$ differences $b_j-b_i$, $i<j$, are distinct and lie in \[ [2b_j/3,b_j). \] The intervals for different $j$ are disjoint: the next one starts at least at $2b_j>b_j$. Thus the first $s$ terms have at least $\binom s2$ distinct positive differences, all at most $b_s$.

Now let $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ satisfy $a_{i+1}\geq(1+\epsilon)a_i$, for fixed $\epsilon>0$. Choose $h$ with $(1+\epsilon)^h\geq3$. Among $a_1,\ldots,a_k$, one residue class of indices modulo $h$ has at least $\lceil k/h\rceil$ members. They form a 3-lacunary subsequence, so \[ D(a_k)\geq\binom{\lceil k/h\rceil}{2}, \qquad A(a_k)=k. \] Hence \[ \frac{D(a_k)}{A(a_k)} \geq\frac1k\binom{\lceil k/h\rceil}{2} \longrightarrow\infty. \] Every such sequence has $A(N)=O_\epsilon(\log N)$, so it satisfies the required zero-density hypothesis.

A useful exact model.
For $A=\{3^j:j\geq0\}$, the differences of its first $k$ terms are all distinct by the preceding intervals. Thus the quadratic lower bound is attained by the finite initial segment's difference count. For the full infinite set, later terms can only add differences; they cannot invalidate the lower bound. This distinction is important because $D(N)$ counts differences from all of $A$, not only $A\cap[1,N]$.
Attribution and remaining gap.
The full resolution is recorded at https://www.erdosproblems.com/899, checked 24 September 2026. A historical discussion is C. L. Stewart, On difference sets of sets of integers, https://www.numdam.org/item/SDPP_1977-1978__19_1_A4_0/. The arguments above are elementary special-case proofs. They do not handle general sets of zero upper Banach density which have no fixed lacunarity ratio. No claim is made to have proved Ruzsa's theorem in that remaining regime, or to have run Lean verification.
