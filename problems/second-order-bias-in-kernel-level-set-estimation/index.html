Second-Order Bias in Kernel Level-Set Estimation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f$, $K$, and $c$ denote the underlying function, kernel, and level appearing in the supplied record, and let $\widehat f_h$ denote its kernel estimator with bandwidth $h>0$. In the ambient space ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, define \[ L=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:f(x)\geq c\},\qquad \widehat L_h=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\widehat f_h(x)\geq c\}. \] The source writes the mean distance-in-measure risk as \[ \operatorname{MDM}(h)=I_1(h)+I_2(h). \] Here the dependence of the estimator and risk terms on the sample size $n$ is suppressed in the source notation.

The proposed question is whether there exists a constant $B=B(f,K,c)$ such that, writing $h=h_n$ for the bandwidth at sample size $n$, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}h_n^{-2}I_2(h_n)=B(f,K,c), \] without assuming \[ nh_n^{d+4}\longrightarrow0. \] Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not define $I_1$ or $I_2$, specify the measure and expectation defining $\operatorname{MDM}$, or give the sampling model and kernel-estimator formula. It also omits the admissible classes of $f$, $K$, and $c$, boundary regularity assumptions, and the remaining conditions and quantifiers on bandwidth sequences. Consequently, the displayed decomposition does not determine $I_2$, and this is a transcription of the intended limit question rather than a fully specified mathematical conjecture. Removing the displayed bandwidth condition leaves the other intended hypotheses unresolved.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the term called $I_2$ in a kernel level-set estimation risk has an $h^2$-scaled limit depending only on $f$, $K$, and $c$, without the condition $nh^{d+4}\to0$. The risk decomposition and remaining hypotheses are missing, so a precise conjecture requires further source clarification.
