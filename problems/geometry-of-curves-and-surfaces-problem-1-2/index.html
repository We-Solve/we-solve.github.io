Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.2

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In a standard compact-surface interpretation, let $f:M^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be a smooth immersion of a closed connected surface. One common tightness convention requires every generic height function $h_v(x)=\langle f(x),v\rangle$, $v\in S^2$, to be a perfect Morse function over ${\mathbb{Z}}/2$: the number of critical points of each index equals the corresponding Betti number. The source does not give its exact tightness definition or state whether surfaces are embedded, immersed, or necessarily compact.

Under a deformation-rigidity interpretation, the question would ask whether every smooth isometric family $f_t$ with $f_0=f$ satisfies \[ f_t^*g_{\mathrm{Euc}}=f^*g_{\mathrm{Euc}}quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_t=Q_t f+a_t, \qquad Q_t\in O(3),\ a_t\in{\mathbb{R}}^3. \] Here the conclusion is that every deformation is induced by ambient rigid motions. Global rigidity, asserting congruence of every isometric realization, and infinitesimal rigidity, concerning first-order variations, are different statements. The source's single word rigid does not distinguish them or say whether compared realizations must themselves remain tight.

The literal question is whether all smooth tight surfaces in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ are rigid in the intended sense. No convexity, orientability, genus or embedding assumption is added. The supplied summary reports classical convex rigidity and describes the nonconvex smooth question as unresolved. Its vague pinched/nonrigid examples and projective-plane language do not identify an admissible smooth embedded counterexample; those category distinctions need checking.

Short English statement

Are smooth tight surfaces in three-space rigid under isometric comparison or deformation? The source must specify tightness, immersion versus embedding, compactness and the exact notion of rigidity.
