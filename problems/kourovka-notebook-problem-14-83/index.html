Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.83

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group is called a Chernikov group if it has a normal subgroup of finite index isomorphic to a direct product of finitely many quasicyclic groups $C_{p^\infty}$, where the primes may vary and the empty product is allowed. Here $C_{p^\infty}$ is the group of all complex roots of unity whose orders are powers of the prime $p$. In accordance with the source, for a group property $\sigma$, a group is quasi-$\sigma$ if it does not have property $\sigma$, but every proper subgroup has property $\sigma$. Thus a quasi-Chernikov group is not Chernikov and every proper subgroup is Chernikov; in particular, it is infinite.

A group is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. An involution is an element of order exactly $2$. Write $b^g=g^{-1}bg$, and let $\langle a,b^g\rangle$ denote the subgroup generated by $a$ and $b^g$. An infinite simple group $G$ is a monster of the third kind if, for every $a,b\in G\setminus\{1\}$ with $a^2\ne1$ or $b^2\ne1$, the set \[ \{g\in G:\langle a,b^g\rangle=G\} \] is infinite. The infinitude here concerns the conjugating elements $g$, not necessarily distinct conjugates of $b$.

Question (A. I. Sozutov, Kourovka Notebook 14.83). Is every simple quasi-Chernikov group a monster of the third kind? Equivalently, does every simple group $G$ that is not Chernikov and whose proper subgroups are all Chernikov satisfy \[ \forall a,b\in G\setminus\{1\},\qquad (a^2\ne1\ \lor\ b^2\ne1) \ \Longrightarrow\ \left|\{g\in G:\langle a,g^{-1}bg\rangle=G\}\right|\text{ is infinite}? \] The supplied source reports an affirmative answer for simple quasi-finite groups, meaning infinite simple groups all of whose proper subgroups are finite, and attributes this special case to Sozutov's 1997 paper. This is a supplied literature assertion, not an independently verified result here. The source also requests comparison with V. P. Shunkov's definitions in Archive problems 6.63 and 6.64; those definitions are not supplied.

Short English statement

Must every infinite simple group that is not Chernikov, but has only Chernikov proper subgroups, have the following property: for any two nonidentity elements, at least one of which is not an involution, infinitely many conjugating elements make the first element and a conjugate of the second generate the whole group? The supplied record labels this problem open and reports the quasi-finite case as known.
