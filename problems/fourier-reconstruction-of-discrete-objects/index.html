Research attempt: Exact sparse reconstruction and the missing source target

On $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, set $\zeta=e^{-2\pi i/N}$ and define the unnormalized Fourier data $m_k=\sum_{x=0}^{N-1}f(x)\zeta^{kx}$. Without a structural restriction, missing a frequency $k_0$ precludes uniqueness: the nonzero vector $f(x)=e^{2\pi i k_0x/N}$ has $m_k=0$ for every $k\ne k_0$. This follows by summing the finite geometric series whose ratio is an $N$th root of unity different from one. Hence it is indistinguishable from zero on any observed set omitting $k_0$.

Now assume exactly $s\geq1$ nonzero entries and observe $m_0,\ldots,m_{2s-1}$, with $2s\leq N$. Write their distinct locations as $x_1,\ldots,x_s$, their nonzero values as $c_1,\ldots,c_s$, and $z_j=\zeta^{x_j}$. The $s$ by $s$ Hankel matrix $H=(m_{a+b})_{a,b=0}^{s-1}$ factors as \[ H=V\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_s)V^T, \qquad V_{a,j}=z_j^a. \] The Vandermonde determinant $\det V=\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)$ is nonzero: the determinant is alternating, vanishes at each equality of two variables, has the product's degree and leading coefficient, proving the formula. Thus $H$ is invertible even though it need not be Hermitian or positive definite.

Solve the linear system \[ \sum_{b=0}^{s-1}m_{a+b}q_b=-m_{a+s},\qquad 0\leq a<s. \] The polynomial $Q(z)=z^s+\sum_{b=0}^{s-1}q_bz^b$ is exactly $\prod_j(z-z_j)$. Indeed the latter polynomial satisfies the displayed system after substituting $m_k=\sum_jc_jz_j^k$, and invertibility proves uniqueness. Evaluate $Q$ at all $N$ roots $\zeta^x$ to recover the support, and then solve the Vandermonde system for $c_j$ from the first $s$ moments. This is an explicit exact-arithmetic reconstruction using $O(s^3+Ns)$ scalar arithmetic operations, with conventional elimination and direct evaluation. It is not a finite-precision stability theorem or a bit-complexity bound for arbitrary complex inputs.

Uniqueness under an at-most-$s$ promise can also be proved without first knowing the exact support size. The difference of two such vectors has at most $2s$ nonzero entries. If their observed moments agree, the first $t$ equations, where $t\leq2s$ is the difference's actual support size, form a square Vandermonde system and force all entries to vanish.

The consecutive-frequency hypothesis is substantive. With even $N$ and only even frequencies, the point masses at $0$ and $N/2$ have identical data, since $\zeta^{kN/2}=1$ for every even $k$. Even one-sparse objects need not be identifiable from an arbitrary set of the same number of frequencies.
