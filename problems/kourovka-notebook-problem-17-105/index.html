Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.105

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. A pro-$p$ group is a topological group isomorphic to an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. Free pro-$p$ groups and free pro-$p$ products are understood in the category of pro-$p$ groups with continuous homomorphisms.

For a pro-$p$ group $G$ and a positive integer $n$, let $F_p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be the free pro-$p$ group on the indicated finite basis, and put \[ G\amalg_p F_p(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \] for its free pro-$p$ product with $G$. An equation over $G$ is an expression $v=1$ with $v$ in this free pro-$p$ product. For $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in G^n$, let $v(a)$ be the image of $v$ under the unique continuous homomorphism to $G$ that restricts to the identity on $G$ and sends each $x_i$ to $a_i$. For any set $S$ of such elements, define \[ V_G(S)=\{a\in G^n: v(a)=1\text{ for every }v\in S\}. \] Thus solutions are sought in the Cartesian power $G^n$, called the affine space in the source.

The group $G$ is equationally Noetherian, in the sense specified by this problem, if for every positive integer $n$ and every set $S\subseteq G\amalg_p F_p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, there exists a finite subset $S_0\subseteq S$ such that \[ V_G(S)=V_G(S_0). \] Here $V_G(\varnothing)=G^n$, and equivalence of systems means equality of their solution sets in $G^n$.

Problem (Kourovka Notebook 17.105). Is every free pro-$p$ group equationally Noetherian in this sense? The prime $p$ is arbitrary. The supplied statement gives no restriction on the rank of the free pro-$p$ group; whether a finite-rank restriction was intended remains unresolved. The equations above are elements of the free pro-$p$ product, as explicitly required by the source.

Short English statement

Must every system of equations in finitely many variables over a free pro-$p$ group have exactly the same solutions as some finite subsystem? Equations are interpreted in the free pro-$p$ product, and solutions lie in the given group. The supplied source does not specify a rank restriction.
