Erdős Problem #149

Status Open · erdosproblems.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph with maximum degree $\Delta=\Delta(G)$. A set of edges $M\subseteq E$ is strongly independent if the induced subgraph on the endpoints of $M$ is a disjoint union of edges (equivalently, $M$ is an induced matching).

Let $\chi'_s(G)$ denote the minimum number of strongly independent edge-sets whose union is $E$ (equivalently, the minimum number of colors in a strong edge-coloring). The question is whether \[ \chi'_s(G)\ \le\ \frac54\,\Delta^2 \] for all graphs $G$.

Ambiguity note. For odd $\Delta$, the right-hand side is not an integer, and the sharp Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture is usually stated with a slightly smaller (still $\sim \frac54\Delta^2$) expression. Interpreting the prompt literally, one should read it as $\chi'_s(G)\le \left\lceil \frac54\Delta^2\right\rceil$.

Erdős Problem #149 (Round 2): corrected formulation and a broad solved class

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Here I pursue path (C): the literal Round-1 formulation \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \] is not the standard exact Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture when $\Delta$ is odd. The minimal corrected target is \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is even}, \] and \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is odd}. \] My Round-2 goal is then to prove this corrected statement for a nontrivial infinite class of graphs, thereby excluding that entire class from any potential counterexample.

The class I will handle is: all $2$-degenerate graphs.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 points.
  1. The line-graph reformulation \[ \chi'_s(G)=\chi(L(G)^2). \]
  2. The Round-1 greedy upper bound \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1. \]
  3. The Round-1 $C_5$-blow-up example showing that the conjectured constant is tight in the even-$\Delta$ regime.
  4. The Round-1 ambiguity note that the odd-$\Delta$ case requires a piecewise correction.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND 2)

The new ingredient is to combine two known theorems that were not used in Round 1.
  1. Andersen's theorem. Every graph with maximum degree at most $3$ has strong chromatic index at most $10$.
  2. Yu's theorem for $k$-degenerate graphs. Every $k$-degenerate graph of maximum degree $\Delta$ satisfies \[ \chi'_s(G)\le (4k-2)\Delta-k(2k-1)+1. \] For $k=2$, this becomes \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \]

These two statements together imply that the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil bound holds for every $2$-degenerate graph.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND 2)

Round 1 left a huge gap between the general greedy bound and the conjectured piecewise bound. I will not close that general gap, but I will prove a broad solved class.

The exact claims to prove are:

  1. If $G$ is $2$-degenerate and $\Delta\ge 4$, then Yu's theorem gives a linear bound $6\Delta-5$, and this linear bound is already at most the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil target.
  2. If $G$ is $2$-degenerate and $\Delta\le 3$, then the corrected target also holds: for $\Delta\le 2$ this follows from the Round-1 greedy bound, and for $\Delta=3$ it follows from Andersen's theorem.

This will prove the conjectured bound for all $2$-degenerate graphs and hence show that any counterexample must have degeneracy at least $3$ (and in particular maximum degree at least $4$).

5) WORK (ROUND 2)

External theorem 1 (Andersen).
If $G$ is a graph with maximum degree at most $3$, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 10. \]
External theorem 2 (Yu).
If $G$ is a $k$-degenerate graph with maximum degree $\Delta$, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le (4k-2)\Delta-k(2k-1)+1. \] In particular, if $G$ is $2$-degenerate, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \]
Claim.
Every $2$-degenerate graph satisfies the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil bound \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is even}, \] and \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is odd}. \]
Proof.
Let $G$ be $2$-degenerate, and let $\Delta=\Delta(G)$.

Case 1: $\Delta\le 2$. By the Round-1 greedy bound, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1. \] For $\Delta=1$, this is $1$, which equals the corrected target. For $\Delta=2$, this is $5$, which again equals the corrected target \[ \frac54\cdot 2^2=5. \]

Case 2: $\Delta=3$. By Andersen's theorem, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 10. \] The corrected odd-$\Delta$ target equals \[ \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} = \frac{45-6+1}{4} =10. \] So the target holds exactly.

Case 3: $\Delta\ge 4$ and even. By Yu's theorem, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \] So it is enough to check \[ 6\Delta-5\le \frac54\,\Delta^2. \] This is equivalent to \[ 5\Delta^2-24\Delta+20\ge 0. \] The quadratic function on the left has derivative $10\Delta-24>0$ for every $\Delta\ge 3$, so it is increasing on $[4,\infty)$. At $\Delta=4$ it equals \[ 5\cdot 16-24\cdot 4+20=4>0. \] Hence it is positive for every even $\Delta\ge 4$, proving the desired inequality.

Case 4: $\Delta\ge 5$ and odd. Again Yu gives \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \] Now it is enough to check \[ 6\Delta-5\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4}. \] This is equivalent to \[ 5\Delta^2-26\Delta+21\ge 0. \] Its derivative is $10\Delta-26>0$ for every $\Delta\ge 3$, so it is increasing on $[5,\infty)$. At $\Delta=5$ it equals \[ 5\cdot 25-26\cdot 5+21=16>0. \] Hence it is positive for every odd $\Delta\ge 5$.

All cases are proved.

Corollary.
Any counterexample to the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture must have degeneracy at least $3$ and maximum degree at least $4$.
Proof.
If the graph had degeneracy at most $2$, the claim above would dispose of it. If it had maximum degree at most $3$, Cases 1 and 2 would dispose of it. Therefore every counterexample lies outside both ranges.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

I check the delicate points.
  1. Odd-$\Delta$ correction. The corrected target is the standard piecewise Erdős--Nešetřil form, not simply $\lceil 5\Delta^2/4\rceil$. This matters already at $\Delta=3$, where the exact target is $10$.
  2. Small degrees. Yu's linear estimate is not enough for $\Delta=3$, since it gives $13$. That is why Andersen's theorem is required in Case 2.
  3. Use of Round-1 results. The Round-1 greedy bound is only used for $\Delta\le 2$, where it is exact enough. There is no hidden dependence on unproved new claims.
  4. Hypotheses of external theorems. Yu requires only $k$-degeneracy and maximum degree; Andersen requires only $\Delta\le 3$. These hypotheses are exactly what is used here.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 2 does not solve the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture in full, but it gives a real structural advance over Round 1:

  1. it replaces the vague odd-$\Delta$ formulation by the standard exact piecewise conjecture;
  2. it proves that the corrected conjecture holds for every $2$-degenerate graph;
  3. consequently, it rules out all $2$-degenerate graphs from the search for a counterexample.

Erdős Problem #149: Strong chromatic index

FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph with maximum degree $\Delta=\Delta(G)$. A set of edges $M\subseteq E$ is strongly independent if the induced subgraph on the endpoints of $M$ is a disjoint union of edges (equivalently, $M$ is an induced matching).

Let $\chi'_s(G)$ denote the minimum number of strongly independent edge-sets whose union is $E$ (equivalently, the minimum number of colors in a strong edge-coloring). The question is whether \[ \chi'_s(G)\ \le\ \frac54\,\Delta^2 \] for all graphs $G$.

Ambiguity note. For odd $\Delta$, the right-hand side is not an integer, and the sharp Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture is usually stated with a slightly smaller (still $\sim \frac54\Delta^2$) expression. Interpreting the prompt literally, one should read it as $\chi'_s(G)\le \left\lceil \frac54\Delta^2\right\rceil$.

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

This is the classical Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture $1985$. It is known that the constant $5/4$ would be best possible via a blow-up construction of $C_5$. The best general asymptotic upper bound currently recorded in the Erdős Problems database is $\chi'_s(G)\le 1.772\,\Delta^2$ for sufficiently large $\Delta$ (Hurley--de Joannis de Verclos--Kang), improving earlier constants $1.835,1.93,1.998$.

It also remains open in full generality whether the clique number of $L(G)^2$ is at most $\frac54\Delta^2$, although it is known under additional forbidden-cycle hypotheses.

DEFINITIONS / SETUP

  • Strong edge-coloring. A map $\varphi:E\to [t]$ is a strong edge-coloring if whenever $e\neq f$ have $\varphi(e)=\varphi(f)$, then $e$ and $f$ are strongly independent (i.e. form an induced matching).
  • Strong chromatic index. $\chi'_s(G)$ is the minimum $t$ for which a strong edge-coloring exists.
  • Line graph formulation. Let $L(G)$ be the line graph of $G$ and $L(G)^2$ its square. Then $\chi'_s(G)=\chi(L(G)^2)$: edges of $G$ are vertices of $L(G)$, and "strong conflict" corresponds to distance at most $2$ in $L(G)$.

KNOWN RESULTS I WILL USE

I only use two elementary facts, each proved below: a greedy upper bound and the blow-up lower bound.

MAIN ATTEMPT

1) Elementary greedy upper bound $\chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1$

Proposition (Greedy bound): For every graph $G$ of maximum degree $\Delta$, \[ \chi'_s(G)\ \le\ 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1. \]
Proof: We color edges one-by-one in an arbitrary order, always choosing the smallest available color. Fix an edge $e=uv$ being colored. A previously colored edge $f$ is forbidden to share color with $e$ if $f$ is adjacent to $e$ (shares an endpoint) or if $f$ is adjacent to some edge adjacent to $e$ (i.e. in the square of the line graph).

Count the number of edges at strong distance at most $1$ from $e$: there are at most $(\deg(u)-1)+(\deg(v)-1)\le 2(\Delta-1)$ edges incident to $u$ or $v$ other than $e$.

Now consider edges at strong distance exactly $2$ from $e$. Any such edge must be incident to a neighbor of $u$ or of $v$ (other than $u,v$ themselves). The vertex $u$ has at most $\Delta-1$ neighbors besides $v$, and each such neighbor $w$ is incident to at most $\Delta-1$ edges other than $uw$. Thus the number of edges incident to the neighbors of $u$ (excluding those already counted as incident to $u$) is at most $(\Delta-1)(\Delta-1)=(\Delta-1)^2$. Similarly for $v$, giving at most $2(\Delta-1)^2$ additional forbidden edges.

Hence, at the time we color $e$, there are at most \[ 2(\Delta-1) + 2(\Delta-1)^2 = 2\Delta^2-2\Delta \] previously colored edges whose colors cannot be used. Therefore at most $2\Delta^2-2\Delta$ colors are forbidden, so with $2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1$ colors available, a greedy choice always succeeds. ∎

2) The blow-up of $C_5$ yields the lower bound $5\Delta^2/4$

Proposition (Tightness example for even $\Delta$): Let $t\in\mathbb{N}$ and let $G$ be the $t$-blow-up of the cycle $C_5$: replace each vertex of $C_5$ by an independent set $A_0,\dots,A_4$ of size $t$, and replace each edge of $C_5$ by all edges of the complete bipartite graph between the corresponding parts (indices modulo $5$). Then $\Delta(G)=2t$ and \[ \chi'_s(G)=|E(G)| = 5t^2 = \frac54\,\Delta(G)^2. \]
Proof: Each vertex lies in exactly two bipartite blocks, so $\Delta(G)=2t$. Also $E(G)$ is the disjoint union of the five complete bipartite graphs between consecutive parts, so $|E(G)|=5\cdot t^2$.

It remains to show that in $G$ no two distinct edges are strongly independent, i.e. every color class in a strong edge-coloring has size at most $1$. Let $e\neq f$ be edges of $G$. Project each edge to the corresponding base edge of $C_5$ (i.e. remember only which pair of parts it lies between). In $C_5$, any two distinct edges are at distance at most $2$ in the line graph (indeed $L(C_5)\cong C_5$ and $L(C_5)^2\cong K_5$). Translating back to $G$, this implies that for any two edges $e,f$ there is either

  • a shared endpoint (if the corresponding base edges share a vertex), or
  • an adjacency between an endpoint of $e$ and an endpoint of $f$ coming from a base edge connecting the relevant parts (if the base edges are at line-graph distance $2$).
In either case, the induced subgraph on the four endpoints contains an extra edge besides $e$ and $f$, so $\{e,f\}$ is not a strongly independent set. Hence every strongly independent set has size at most $1$, and $\chi'_s(G)\ge |E(G)|$. The reverse inequality $\chi'_s(G)\le |E(G)|$ is trivial, so equality holds. ∎

ADVERSARIAL CHECK

  • Proposition Proposition (Greedy bound) only uses an upper bound on the number of conflicting edges; overcounting is harmless. The conclusion is therefore safe.
  • Proposition prop:C5blowup hinges on the claim that any two distinct edges in the blow-up are at distance at most $2$ in the line graph. This is true because the base edges of $C_5$ already have this property, and the blow-up only adds adjacencies, never removes them.
  • These arguments do not approach the conjectured constant $5/4$ from above; they only show $1$ a coarse universal bound $2\Delta^2+O(\Delta)$ and $2$ that $5/4$ would be best possible.

FINAL

UNRESOLVED --- PARTIAL PROGRESS.
  • Farthest point reached. I gave complete proofs of (i) the classical greedy bound $\chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1$ and (ii) the $C_5$ blow-up construction showing $\chi'_s(G)$ can equal $\frac54\Delta^2$ (so the conjectured constant is tight for even $\Delta$).
  • Remaining gap. Proving $\chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\Delta^2$ (or giving a counterexample) for general graphs remains open; existing best results replace $5/4$ by a larger constant (currently $\approx 1.772$).
  • Promising next steps. Any improvement seems to require either (a) improved control on sparse neighborhood structure in $L(G)^2$ together with probabilistic coloring methods, or (b) structural decomposition approaches for extremal configurations resembling the $C_5$ blow-up.

COMPLETION: 30%
