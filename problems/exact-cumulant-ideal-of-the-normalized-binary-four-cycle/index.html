Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 15. What is the cumulant ideal of the binary four-cycle model?

Russell Steele

Russell Steele spoke about mixture models, model selection, and the Bayesian Infor-mation Criterion (BIC).

Specified convention and attempted route

Take four binary variables, normalized cells $p_x$ for $x\in\{0,1\}^4$, and the positive graphical model with edge potentials on $12,23,34,41$. The ideal under investigation in this attempt is the polynomial vanishing ideal of its complex Zariski closure inside the normalization hyperplane, transported to square-free cumulants. These conventions are explicit additions needed to calculate; the source asks for an ideal without spelling them out. No complete generating list is claimed below.
An explicit cumulant equation.
Set $m_\varnothing=1$ and $m_A=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(A)}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_B$ for nonempty $A$. This formula follows by expanding the exponential of $\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}\kappa_A t_A$ in the ring with $t_i^2=0$: ordered disjoint partitions occur $k!$ times and cancel $1/k!$. Let \[ T=1-m_2-m_4+m_{24},\quad U=m_1-m_{12}-m_{14}+m_{124}, \quad V=m_3-m_{23}-m_{34}+m_{234}, \] \[ W=m_{13}-m_{123}-m_{134}+m_{1234}. \] These are respectively the total probability of $X_2=X_4=0$, and its portions with $X_1=1$, $X_3=1$, and both equal to one. For the conditional $2$ by $2$ table of $(X_1,X_3)$ on this event, the four unnormalized entries are $T-U-V+W,U-W,V-W,W$. Their determinant is $TW-UV$. Graphical factorization, with $X_2,X_4$ fixed, separates the factors containing $X_1$ from those containing $X_3$, and therefore proves the cumulant polynomial equation \[ TW-UV=0. \] Permuting conditioned binary values, and then exchanging the two nonedges, gives eight necessary determinant equations. The formula is valid even on the closure, since it is polynomial and holds everywhere in the positive factorization.
Why these eight equations do not generate the desired ideal.
A complete exact search over four-cell supports suggested the following example, whose verification is independent of the search. Give mass $1/4$ to each of \[ A=\{0000,0011,1101,1110\} \] and zero to all other cells. In $A$, all four values of the pair $(X_2,X_4)$ occur exactly once, as do all four values of $(X_1,X_3)$. Conditioning on either pair leaves a point mass on the opposite pair. Every one of the eight conditional determinants is therefore zero. However the graphical factorization also implies \[ Q(p)=p_{0000}p_{0011}p_{1101}p_{1110} -p_{0001}p_{0010}p_{1100}p_{1111}=0. \] For a direct proof, compare the two four-element multisets in this formula. On edge $12$, both have pair counts $(2,0,0,2)$ in the order $00,01,10,11$. On each of the other three edges, both have counts $(1,1,1,1)$. In a product of four edge-factorized probabilities, each edge potential therefore has exactly the same exponent on both sides, and the two normalizing factors are both $Z^{-4}$. This proves $Q=0$ on the graphical model and hence on its algebraic closure. At our law supported on $A$, instead, $Q=4^{-4}\ne0$. Consequently the eight determinants cannot generate the vanishing ideal: if they did, every simultaneous zero of them would annihilate $Q$. This is a boundary obstruction; no assertion is made that this law is a positive four-cycle factorization.

The coordinate change does not remove the obstruction. Probability reconstruction is $p_S=\sum_{B\supseteq S}(-1)^{|B\setminus S|}m_B$, by the binomial inclusion--exclusion identity. The cumulant change is invertible by the finite logarithm, whose ordered-partition expansion inverts the exponential above. Thus substituting the reconstruction in $Q$ gives an explicit additional cumulant relation, still violated by the cumulants of our example. Merely transporting the eight familiar quadrics cannot be the complete calculation requested by the source.

Audit and gap

Its displayed witness is proved fully above; the finite search is not claimed exhaustive over ideals or all degrees.
