Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied Question 22 asks two questions under the hypotheses that $f$ is quasi-hyperbolic and dissipative and that $J$ is connected. The excerpt does not define the ambient class of maps, $J$, quasi-hyperbolicity, external rays, or a finite quotient of the real solenoid. Consequently, the following mathematical interpretation is conditional and requires comparison with the cited source.

In the customary complex Hénon setting, one considers a polynomial automorphism $f:{\mathbb{C}}^2\to{\mathbb{C}}^2$ of Hénon type, of dynamical degree $d\geq 2$. In this setting, define \[ K^{\pm}(f)=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^2:\{f^{\pm n}(z):n\geq 0\}\text{ is bounded}\right\},\qquad J^{\pm}(f)=\partial K^{\pm}(f),\qquad J(f)=J^+(f)\cap J^-(f). \] Dissipativity means $0<|\det Df|<1$, where the Jacobian determinant is constant. These conventions are not specified in the supplied excerpt. The hypothesis of quasi-hyperbolicity must retain its meaning in the source; no definition of that property is supplied here.

Under the source's intended conventions, ask whether every such $f$ with connected $J(f)$ has each of the following properties:

  1. Every external ray lands at a point of $J(f)$. More explicitly, if an external ray is parametrized as $\gamma:(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}}^2$, with $t\downarrow 0$ corresponding to its end toward $J(f)$, the requested landing property is \[ \exists p_\gamma\in J(f)\quad \lim_{t\downarrow 0}\gamma(t)=p_\gamma. \] This formula describes landing; it does not define which curves are the external rays in the source.
  2. The space $J(f)$ is a finite quotient of the real solenoid, in the sense intended by the source.
For clarity about the second unresolved terminology, a common degree-$d$ solenoid is the inverse limit \[ \mathcal S_d=\left\{(\zeta_0,\zeta_1,\ldots)\in(S^1)^{{\mathbb{N}}_0}:\zeta_{n+1}^{\,d}=\zeta_n\text{ for every }n\geq 0\right\},\qquad S^1=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\zeta|=1\}, \] with the subspace topology from the product, where ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. If the intended solenoid is $\mathcal S_d$ and a finite quotient means a finite-to-one continuous image, the second question asks for a continuous surjection \[ q:\mathcal S_d\longrightarrow J(f),\qquad \#q^{-1}(p)<\infty\quad\text{for every }p\in J(f). \] The excerpt does not establish this interpretation, identify the solenoid's degree, or specify whether uniformly bounded fibers, a group quotient, or compatibility with the dynamics is required. These qualifications remain unresolved.

Short English statement

Question 22 asks whether quasi-hyperbolicity, dissipativity, and connectedness of $J$ force every external ray to land on $J$, and whether $J$ is a finite quotient of the real solenoid. The ambient setting and several essential definitions are absent from the excerpt; the displayed complex Hénon interpretation is provisional.
