Exotic Four-Manifolds and the Smooth Poincaré Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^n$ denote the sphere with its standard smooth structure, and give $S^1\times S^3$ the product smooth structure. An exotic smooth structure on a specified smooth manifold $M$ means a smooth manifold $X$ homeomorphic to $M$ but not diffeomorphic to $M$. The supplied record does not specify whether the homeomorphisms and diffeomorphisms must preserve orientation.

The source groups together the following questions and conjectural assertions.

  1. Does there exist a smooth four-manifold $X$ such that \[ X\cong_{\mathrm{homeo}}S^4, \qquad X\not\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}S^4? \] The assertion that no such $X$ exists is the formulation of the smooth four-dimensional Poincaré conjecture used in the supplied record.
  2. Does there exist a smooth four-manifold $X$ such that \[ X\cong_{\mathrm{homeo}}S^1\times S^3, \qquad X\not\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}S^1\times S^3? \]
  3. Do exotic definite four-manifolds exist? For a closed, connected, oriented smooth four-manifold $M$, its intersection form on real cohomology is \[ Q_M(a,b)=\langle a\smile b,[M]\rangle, \qquad a,b\in H^2(M;{\mathbb{R}}). \] It is definite if it is positive definite or negative definite. This explains the conventional closed, oriented setting; the supplied record does not specify closedness, compactness, boundary conditions, orientability, simple connectivity, or restrictions on the intersection form. Moreover, it supplies no reference smooth structure relative to which a definite manifold is called exotic. If the intended meaning is the existence of homeomorphic but nondiffeomorphic definite smooth four-manifolds, the question concerns a pair $M,N$ with \[ M\cong_{\mathrm{homeo}}N, \qquad M\not\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}N. \] The missing class of allowed manifolds must be fixed before this becomes a fully quantified assertion.
  4. The source calls the following the unknotting conjecture: for every $g\in{\mathbb{N}}$, there is a unique smooth embedded surface $\Sigma\subset S^4$ of genus $g$ such that \[ \pi_1(S^4\setminus\Sigma)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Here the fundamental group is explicitly that of the complement, following the supplied reconstructed statement. To make the usual closed, connected, orientable surface interpretation explicit, let $F_g$ be a closed, connected, orientable surface of genus $g$, and consider \[ \mathcal E_g= \left\{\Sigma=e(F_g): e:F_g\hookrightarrow S^4 \text{ is a smooth embedding and } \pi_1(S^4\setminus\Sigma)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}. \] Under that interpretation, the intended uniqueness assertion would have the form \[ \forall g\in{\mathbb{N}},\qquad |\mathcal E_g/\!\sim|=1. \] The source does not specify the equivalence relation $\sim$, such as smooth ambient isotopy or equivalence under an ambient diffeomorphism. It also does not explicitly specify the surface conventions or whether $0\in{\mathbb{N}}$. These qualifications remain unresolved; the displayed quotient is a template requiring those choices, not a completed precise conjecture.
The report describes the unknotting conjecture as governing the discussion, but the supplied record states no precise implication between these questions.

Short English statement

The record asks whether the standard $S^4$ and $S^1\times S^3$ admit exotic smooth structures, whether exotic definite four-manifolds exist, and whether a smooth surface in $S^4$ with infinite cyclic complement group is unique for each genus. The definite-manifold domain and the equivalence relation for surface uniqueness are unspecified. The supplied open-status labels and source-verification claims are curation assertions, not independent verification.
