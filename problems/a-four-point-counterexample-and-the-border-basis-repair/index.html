Question 47. [Pistone] Take a design of N distinct rational vectors D = {v1,..., v N } ⊆ Qd

and xα1,..., x αN are monomials in Q[x1,..., x n] which are linearly independent modulo the design ideal I(D). Further suppose that these monomials are equal to the set of standard monomials of some monomial ideal M.Suppose that M = 〈m1,..., m r〉. Since the monomials xα1,..., x αN are a basis for a quotient space, there is a unique representation of the mi as linear combinations of the xαj.This produces r polynomials f1,..., f r which belong to I(D). Question: are the only points which vanish on these polynomials the design points; that is, does V (f1,..., f r) = D? [S2]

Research attempt: A complete witness for the already recorded negative answer

Work in $\mathbb Q[x,y]$, and take \[ D=\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(0,1)\},\qquad \mathcal O=\{1,x,y,xy\},\qquad M=(x^2,y^2). \] These are four distinct rational points, and $\mathcal O$ is exactly the set of standard monomials of $M$: a monomial avoids $M$ precisely when both exponents are at most one. To prove independence modulo $I(D)$, suppose $a+bx+cy+dxy$ vanishes on $D$. On the first three points the polynomial $a+(b+c)t+dt^2$ vanishes at $0,1,2$. Substitution gives $a=0$, $b+c+d=0$, and $2(b+c)+4d=0$, whence $d=0$ and $b+c=0$. Substitution at $(0,1)$ gives $c=0$, hence $b=0$. Thus the evaluation map from the four-dimensional span of $\mathcal O$ to $\mathbb Q^D$ is bijective. Every polynomial has a unique interpolating representative in that span, because its four values do.

The two minimal generators interpolate as \[ x^2\equiv xy,\qquad y^2\equiv xy-x+y\pmod{I(D)}. \] Indeed these identities hold on the diagonal and at $(0,1)$. The source construction therefore gives \[ f_1=x^2-xy=x(x-y),\qquad f_2=y^2-xy+x-y=(y-x)(y-1). \] Their zero set over any characteristic-zero field is the entire diagonal together with $(0,1)$. To prove equality, points with $x=y$ satisfy both equations. At a common zero with $x\ne y$, the first equation forces $x=0$ and the second forces $y=1$. In particular $(3,3)$ is an extra rational point. Every hypothesis of the precise historical question holds, so its proposed affirmative statement fails.

The attempted inference that independence of the proposed standard monomials implies that the interpolated corner relations generate $I(D)$ fails here. All corner equations share $x-y$ as a factor, even though their evaluations impose four independent interpolation conditions. An explicit additional relation removes the excess component: \[ h=xy^2-3xy+2x=x(y-1)(y-2). \] It vanishes on $D$. On the excess diagonal it restricts to $x(x-1)(x-2)$, and it also vanishes at $(0,1)$. Consequently $V(f_1,f_2,h)=D$. This is an instance-specific repair, not a proof that arbitrary corner relations suffice.
