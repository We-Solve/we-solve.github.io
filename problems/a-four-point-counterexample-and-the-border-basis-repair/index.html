Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For a finite rational design $D$, its design ideal is $I(D)=\{f:f(v)=0\text{ for all }v\in D\}$. Monomials are independent modulo $I(D)$ when their residue classes are linearly independent in the quotient ring. Standard monomials of a monomial ideal are those not in that ideal. Interpolating a generator $m_i$ in a quotient basis yields $f_i=m_i-h_i\in I(D)$; the question asks whether their common zero set introduces extra points.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Question 47. [Pistone] Take a design of N distinct rational vectors D = {v1,..., v N } ⊆ Qd

and xα1,..., x αN are monomials in Q[x1,..., x n] which are linearly independent modulo the design ideal I(D). Further suppose that these monomials are equal to the set of standard monomials of some monomial ideal M.Suppose that M = 〈m1,..., m r〉. Since the monomials xα1,..., x αN are a basis for a quotient space, there is a unique representation of the mi as linear combinations of the xαj.This produces r polynomials f1,..., f r which belong to I(D). Question: are the only points which vanish on these polynomials the design points; that is, does V (f1,..., f r) = D? [S2]
