Math-1329

Grading and resolution-dependence obstructions to higher-dimensional Nakajima operators

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Over ${\mathbb{C}}$, let $H_d$ be the smoothable component of $\operatorname{Hilb}^d(\mathbb A^n)$ and choose resolutions $\pi_d:\widetilde H_d\to H_d$. The source asks whether analogues of Nakajima operators can be defined on their cohomology, naturally involving $\bigoplus_dH^*(\widetilde H_d,{\mathbb{Q}})$. It does not specify the resolutions, cohomology theory, correspondences, degree shifts or desired relations.

The attached aid considers literal fundamental-class Borel--Moore correspondences on fixed resolutions. Suppose a length-$r$ punctual incidence family is pure of complex dimension $\delta$, so the associated incidence over a length-$d$ configuration and its support point has expected dimension $nd+n+\delta$. Assume pullbacks and pushforwards with the chosen supports are defined. For labels of cohomological degrees $p,q$, its stated creation and transpose-annihilation shifts are \[ \deg(\mathsf P_{-r})=p+2[n(r-1)-\delta],\qquad \deg(\mathsf P_r)=q-2n-2\delta. \] For paired labels with $p+q=2n$, a nonzero scalar commutator of degree zero therefore requires \[ 2\delta=n(r-1). \] The aid's obstruction concerns this necessary grading condition for an unshifted Heisenberg relation, not the existence of every conceivable operator construction.

For the curvilinear punctual family, $\delta=(n-1)(r-1)$ and the commutator degree is \[ -2(n-2)(r-1). \] Thus for $n>2$ and $r>1$ its ordinary fundamental class cannot yield the literal degree-zero scalar Nakajima commutator. The cases $n=2$ or $r=1$ have no such grading defect; no sufficiency claim follows from that observation.

Independently, the aid reports resolution dependence of full ordinary cohomology. In $\operatorname{Hilb}^4(\mathbb A^3)$, the point $[\mathfrak m^2]$, with $\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)$, is a smoothable singular point. Starting with a strong projective resolution, successive point blow-ups above it remain projective resolutions and isomorphisms over the smooth locus, while their Betti numbers grow without bound. Consequently full ordinary cohomology cannot be a resolution-independent target without additional choices or replacements. The aid does not exclude constructions using chosen resolutions, altered gradings, different cycles or another cohomology theory.

Short English statement

Seek higher-dimensional Nakajima operators on resolutions of smoothable Hilbert components. The supplied aid identifies a grading obstruction for the simplest fundamental-class correspondence and shows that the full cohomology of an unspecified resolution is not canonical.
