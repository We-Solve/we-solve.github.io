Math-5092

Solved intended local pencil problem and dyadic rank-seven annuli

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The AIM item concerns smooth complete intersections $X=V(f_1,f_2)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n$ of two quadrics. Its global context takes a number field, with $k={\mathbb{Q}}$ for simplicity, and states that for $n\geq8$, $X({\mathbb{R}})\ne\varnothing$ implies $X({\mathbb{Q}})\ne\varnothing$, attributed to Sansuc, Swinnerton-Dyer and Colliot-Thélène. It records a circle-method approach for $n\geq9$ (ten variables), attributed to Heath-Brown.

For $n=7$, the stated local question takes two quadratic forms in eight variables over a $p$-adic field $K$ with smooth common zero locus, hence separable determinant polynomial $\det(\lambda f_1+\mu f_2)$. Must some $[\lambda:\mu]\in{\mathbb{P}}^1(K)$ yield a form containing three hyperbolic planes? A hyperbolic plane is a split nonsingular binary quadratic form, and containing three means Witt index at least three. Smoothness alone is the printed hypothesis; the supplied report emphasizes that the intended known local theorem requires $X(K)\ne\varnothing$ as an additional essential assumption.

The report attributes to Heath-Brown the global Hasse principle and weak approximation for smooth intersections in ${\mathbb{P}}^7$, and to Colliot-Thélène's Théorème 7.3 the intended local statement for every $p$-adic field, including dyadic fields, under that nonemptiness hypothesis. Hasse principle means that points over all completions imply a rational point; weak approximation means density in the product over any finite set of completions. Full field and theorem hypotheses are not supplied, and these attributions do not prove the literal smoothness-only local version.

Its additional local proposition uses an affine pencil $q(t)$ with a $K$-rational simple determinant root $t_0$. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer. It claims a threshold $M$ and parity $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$ such that \[ \text{for all }m\geq M\text{ with }m\equiv\epsilon\pmod2, \quad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal O_K^\times, \quad i_W(q(t_0+u\pi^m))=3, \] and every indicated form is nonsingular. This is exact index three, rather than merely at least three. The root has rank seven in the separable-pencil setting. A simple root gives determinant valuation $v_K(\det'(q)(t_0))+m$ sufficiently near it; the report does not supply its explicit threshold or parity formula, so the proposition is retained without guessing those constants. It covers this rational-root sector, not every pencil.

The original remarks additionally report an unwritten odd-characteristic odd-degree-extension argument by Heath-Brown, a rank-at-most-seven pencil-member case, and lower-dimensional conditions such as two conjugate lines or a ground-field conic for $n\geq5$. Their exact residue-characteristic, point and dimension hypotheses are incomplete. They remain contextual claims requiring verification.

Short English statement

Ask for a three-hyperbolic-plane member in an eight-variable $p$-adic quadratic pencil. The report identifies the essential local-solubility hypothesis in the known theorem, including dyadic fields, and cites the global Hasse-principle result. Its additional statement gives whole parameter annuli of exact Witt index three near a rational simple determinant root, with one valuation parity.
