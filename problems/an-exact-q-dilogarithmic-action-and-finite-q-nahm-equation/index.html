An exact q-dilogarithmic action and finite-q Nahm equation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The original asks how $q$-analogues of the dilogarithm enter the theory, in the workshop's Nahm-sum context. It does not specify which quantum dilogarithm or what relationship is sought. The attached aid uses the compact version. For $0<q<1$ define \[ (z;q)_\infty=\prod_{m\ge0}(1-zq^m),\qquad \mathcal L_q(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{z^m}{m(1-q^m)}=-\log(z;q)_\infty\quad(|z|<1). \] Let $A\in M_r({\mathbb{Q}})$ be symmetric positive definite, $B\in{\mathbb{Q}}^r$, $C\in{\mathbb{Q}}$, and consider the Nahm summand \[ w_q(\mathbf n)=\frac{q^{\frac12\mathbf n^tA\mathbf n+B^t\mathbf n+C}}{\prod_i(q)_{n_i}},\qquad \mathbf n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}^r. \] Writing $X_i=q^{n_i}$ gives the exact action identity \[ \log w_q(\mathbf n)=\left(\tfrac12\mathbf n^tA\mathbf n+B^t\mathbf n+C\right)\log q -\sum_i\mathcal L_q(qX_i)-r\log(q;q)_\infty. \] The aid states that the continuous real extension of this log-weight to $\mathbf n\in[0,\infty)^r$ is strictly concave. Its adjacent-lattice ratio is \[ \frac{w_q(\mathbf n+e_i)}{w_q(\mathbf n)} =\frac{q^{A_{ii}/2+B_i}\prod_jX_j^{A_{ij}}}{1-qX_i}. \] Thus adjacent-weight balance gives the exact finite-$q$ system \[ 1-qX_i=q^{A_{ii}/2+B_i}\prod_jX_j^{A_{ij}}. \] This balance equation is not asserted to be identical to the continuous derivative critical-point equation at finite $q$.

Put $q=e^{-\epsilon}$ and $u_i=-\log X_i=\epsilon n_i$. The scaled action has classical limit \[ \Phi(u)=\sum_i\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-u_i})\right)-\tfrac12u^tAu, \qquad \operatorname{Li}_2(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{z^m}{m^2}. \] Its unique critical point $u^*\in(0,\infty)^r$ solves \[ 1-X_i^*=\prod_j(X_j^*)^{A_{ij}},\qquad X_i^*=e^{-u_i^*}, \] the classical Nahm balance equations used in the Bloch-group setting. The aid further states that the real-analytic local branch of the finite-$q$ balance system through $u^*$ has \[ u'(0)=-(A+H)^{-1}(h+d),\quad h_i=\frac{X_i^*}{1-X_i^*},\quad H=\operatorname{diag}(h_i),\quad d_i=A_{ii}/2+B_i. \] The derivative $u'(0)$ is with respect to $\epsilon$. The parameter $A$ controls the leading classical point, $B$ enters this first displacement, and $C$ contributes only an overall normalization of the summand. These statements give a local finite-$q$/classical dictionary, not a modularity criterion for all Nahm sums.

Short English statement

The compact quantum dilogarithm expresses each Nahm summand exactly. Its finite lattice balance deforms the classical dilogarithmic critical equations, and the supplied aid gives the first displacement of that balance point as $q$ tends to one.
