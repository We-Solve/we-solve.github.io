Attempt: selecting a small suite from declared coverage sets

For the source's one-line suite directive, I tested the restricted optimization in which $m\ge1$ required capabilities must be covered, each tool covers a known subset, all tools have unit cost and every combination is compatible. Assume a covering suite exists. The greedy algorithm repeatedly picks a tool covering the most still-uncovered capabilities.
Proposition: If an optimal suite has $k\ge1$ tools, greedy uses at most $k(1+\log m)$ tools. This familiar set-cover guarantee need not hold after changing the declared selection problem to include compatibility or workflow constraints.
Proof: If $r$ capabilities remain, the $k$ sets in an optimal cover still jointly cover them. At least one therefore covers at least $r/k$ of those capabilities, or their union could not cover all $r$. Greedy covers at least as many, leaving at most $r(1-1/k)$. After $t$ selections the uncovered count is at most $m(1-1/k)^t\le m e^{-t/k}$; for $k=1$, greedy finishes immediately. Taking $t=\lfloor k\log m\rfloor+1$ makes the bound strictly less than one, forcing the integer uncovered count to zero. Finally $t\le k\log m+1\le k(1+\log m)$. ∎

Verification and failed extension

For capabilities $\{1,\ldots,6\}$ and tools $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, $B=\{1,2,5\}$ and $C=\{3,4,6\}$, greedy selects $A$ first and then needs both $B,C$, while $B,C$ alone form an optimal two-tool cover. This checks that greedy is not always exact. If $A$ conflicts with both $B$ and $C$, committing to the largest set leaves no feasible extension although $B,C$ remain feasible; the original proof no longer applies because an optimal-cover set may be unavailable after a greedy choice.
