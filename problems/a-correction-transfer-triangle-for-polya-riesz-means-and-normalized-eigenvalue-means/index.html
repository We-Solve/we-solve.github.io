Math-248

A correction-transfer triangle for Polya, Riesz means, and normalized eigenvalue means

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This source record concatenates a collection of spectral questions and historical remarks, rather than one isolated problem. The formulas below distinguish its reconstructed notation, source-time literature reports, and the separate correction-transfer proposition.

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, $n\geq2$, be a nonempty bounded open set. Let $0<E_1\leq E_2\leq\cdots$ be the Dirichlet eigenvalues of $-\Delta$, defined through $H^1_0(\Omega)$, with multiplicity. Put $\omega_n=|S^{n-1}|/n$, \[ W_\Omega=(2\pi)^2(\omega_n|\Omega|)^{-2/n},\quad R_\sigma(\Lambda)=\sum_j(\Lambda-E_j)_+^\sigma,\quad L_{\sigma,n}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{\Gamma(\sigma+1)}{(4\pi)^{n/2}\Gamma(\sigma+1+n/2)}. \] For $\sigma=0$, use the strict counting convention $(\Lambda-E_j)_+^0=1_{E_j<\Lambda}$.

The first section contains the following questions and background.

  1. Michael Loss and Timo Weidl: Pólya's conjecture is $E_j\geq W_\Omega j^{2/n}$. The source says it remains open even at $j=3$; this is historical wording. It recalls Li--Yau, \[ \sum_{j=1}^JE_j\geq\frac n{n+2}W_\Omega J^{1+2/n}, \] and Berezin's 1972 bound $R_\sigma(\Lambda)\leq L_{\sigma,n}^{\mathrm{cl}}|\Omega|\Lambda^{\sigma+n/2}$ for $\sigma\geq1$. It asks for $0\leq\sigma<1$, with $\sigma=0$ equivalent to Pólya, and recalls that order one yields Li--Yau by Legendre duality.
  2. Timo Weidl: seek lower-order corrections to Li--Yau, especially involving boundary area. The source reports discrete-domain corrections, Melas's moment-of-inertia correction, a February 2009 two-dimensional boundary correction by Kovarik--Vugalter--Weidl, and Weidl's earlier effective-boundary improvement for Riesz order at least $3/2$. It describes further improvements below $3/2$ as desirable; no universal boundary-profile formula or complete domain hypotheses are supplied.
  3. Timo Weidl: seek corrections to the reversed Neumann Li--Yau analogue of Kröger. The source describes Neumann Pólya as open for $j\geq2$, apart from a then-recent two-dimensional $j=2$ result of Girouard and others. Neumann indexing, regularity and compact-resolvent assumptions are not specified in the excerpt, so no unverified indexing formula is imposed.
  4. Timo Weidl: analogous questions in magnetic fields, referring to an unavailable later item (10).
  5. Evans Harrell and Joachim Stubbe: for $\sigma\geq2$, the source reports that \[ r_\sigma(\Lambda)=\Lambda^{-\sigma-n/2} \left(L_{\sigma,n}^{\mathrm{cl}}|\Omega|\Lambda^{\sigma+n/2}-R_\sigma(\Lambda)\right) \] is nonincreasing and tends to zero at infinity. It asks for boundary corrections from the trace-identity method. For periodic boundary conditions it reports a related monotonicity and a connection with the Gauss circle or lattice-point problem. The extracted dimension $d$ is reconstructed as $n$, and the phase-space integral uses a positive part.
  6. Harrell and Stubbe: seek analogous monotonicity and Berezin--Li--Yau results for higher-order operators, such as the clamped plate, and fractional Laplacians; a then-accepted Klein--Gordon paper is mentioned for $\sqrt{-\Delta}$. Precise realizations and boundary conditions beyond the example are not supplied.
  7. Harrell and Stubbe: define \[ M_p(J)=\left(\frac{n+2p}{nJ}\sum_{j=1}^JE_j^p\right)^{1/p}\ (p>0),\qquad M_0(J)=e^{2/n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^JE_j\right)^{1/J}. \] All have Weyl leading term $W_\Omega J^{2/n}$. The supplied verification explicitly reconstructs the damaged dispersion as $\Delta_J=M_1(J)^2-M_2(J)^2$, not the dimensionally inconsistent printed $M_1^2-M_2$. The reported inequalities are \[ \Delta_J\geq\tfrac14(E_{J+1}-E_J)^2,\qquad M_1(J)-\sqrt{\Delta_J}\leq E_J\leq E_{J+1}\leq M_1(J)+\sqrt{\Delta_J}. \] For $p>0$, seek a bound \[ M_p(J)^{2p}-M_{2p}(J)^{2p} \leq C(p,\Omega)E_1^{2p}J^{2p\kappa},\qquad\kappa<2/n, \] with the left side also an explicit reconstruction. The permitted dependence of $\kappa$ is not stated.
  8. Harrell and Stubbe: ask whether $E_J\leq M_p(J)$ for every $J\geq1$, $p\geq0$, and whether some $\Omega,J$ attain equality in the gap-dispersion inequality.

The supplied aid addresses a correction-transfer part of these questions. Let $q=1+n/2$, $A=L_{1,n}^{\mathrm{cl}}|\Omega|$, and \[ \Lambda_J=\left(\frac{J}{qA}\right)^{1/(q-1)}=W_\Omega J^{2/n}. \] For any domain-dependent profile $\Phi_\Omega$, if the single-cutoff bound \[ R_1(\Lambda_J)\leq A\Lambda_J^q-\Phi_\Omega(\Lambda_J) \] holds, then the aid asserts the exact implication \[ M_1(J)\geq\Lambda_J+\frac{n+2}{nJ}\Phi_\Omega(\Lambda_J). \] No global-in-cutoff estimate is required for this implication at a fixed $J$. In contrast, propagation to higher orders uses an order-one bound throughout the integration interval: for $\sigma>1$, \[ R_\sigma(\Lambda)=\sigma(\sigma-1)\int_0^\Lambda(\Lambda-t)^{\sigma-2}R_1(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t, \] and the correction becomes $\Phi_{\Omega,\sigma}(\Lambda)=\sigma(\sigma-1)\int_0^\Lambda(\Lambda-t)^{\sigma-2}\Phi_\Omega(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t$. Where a valid order-one profile has boundary coefficient $\tfrac14|\partial\Omega|L_{1,n-1}^{\mathrm{cl}}$, this integration carries it to $\tfrac14|\partial\Omega|L_{\sigma,n-1}^{\mathrm{cl}}$. This is conditional on the profile's validity, not a universal boundary correction for every domain and cutoff. Applied to the reported proved two-term Dirichlet Riesz asymptotic for $\Omega=(0,1)^2$, the aid obtains \[ M_1(J)=4\pi J+\frac{32\sqrt\pi}{3}J^{1/2}+o(J^{1/2})\qquad(J\to\infty). \]

The record also includes a second section and extended remarks on Lieb--Thirring problems. For $n\geq1$, $V\geq0$, and the negative eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ of $-\Delta-V$ on $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$, use \[ \sum_j|\lambda_j|^\gamma\leq L_{n,\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n}V^{\gamma+n/2},\qquad C_{n,\gamma}=L_{n,\gamma}/L_{\gamma,n}^{\mathrm{cl}}. \] The source lists the ranges $\gamma\geq1/2$ for $n=1$, $\gamma>0$ for $n=2$, and $\gamma\geq0$ for $n\geq3$, with the last zero-order case being CLR. It reports $C_{n,\gamma}\geq1$ and Aizenman--Lieb monotonicity in $\gamma$. Its historical table records: $C_{1,1/2}=2$; for $1/2<\gamma<3/2$, upper bound 2 with improvements for some exponents and conjectured optimum $2((\gamma-1/2)/(\gamma+1/2))^{\gamma-1/2}$; and $C_{n,\gamma}=1$ for $\gamma\geq3/2$. The February 2009 entries give 3.64 for $1/2\leq\gamma<1$, $n\geq2$, and 1.82 for $1\leq\gamma<3/2$, $n\geq2$, with optimum 1 conjectured in the latter range for $n\geq3$. For $0\leq\gamma<1/2$, the table gives 6.87 for $n=3$, conjectured $8/\sqrt3$, and 10.34 for $n\geq4$; for $n=2$, $0<\gamma<1/2$, it prints a question mark. Its Eden--Foias note reads numerically about 1.82 at $(n,\gamma)=(1,1)$, consistent with $\pi/\sqrt3$; the extracted fraction is damaged. These are source-time bounds and conjectures, not verified present-day best constants.

The appended questions are: Richard Laugesen asks whether an optimal potential exists, particularly for $n=1$, $1/2<\gamma<3/2$, including the fixed-number-of-bound-states variant, and whether an optimizer can have a single bound state. The source relates this to Keller's one-eigenvalue optimizer and the 1976 Lieb--Thirring conjecture, and mentions endpoint delta and reflectionless potentials; admissible function versus measure classes need clarification. Eric Carlen asks for $\sum_j|\lambda_j|^\gamma\leq C|\lambda_1|^\gamma$, allowing dependence on dimension, exponent, and unspecified integrability data. Rafael Benguria asks for Lieb--Thirring analogues for the Benjamin--Ono Lax operator, noting workshop progress by Ekholm, Frank and Hundertmark on monotonicity. Rupert Frank asks for a direct proof and corrections to the Eden--Foias bound; the February 2009 note reports an operator-valued extension and dimension lifting. Timo Weidl asks for direct eigenvalue-sum estimates without passing through Birman--Schwinger counting.

Almut Burchard's appended Ovals Problem concerns a smooth closed curve $\gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ of length $2\pi$, curvature $\kappa(s)$, and \[ H_\gamma=-\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}^2}{{\,\mathrm{d}} s^2}+\kappa(s)^2 \] on $2\pi$-periodic functions. It asks whether the principal eigenvalue $e(\gamma)\geq1$. The source says this is open even for convex planar loops, reports equality for a family with $\kappa(s)=(a^2\cos^2s+a^{-2}\sin^2s)^{-1}$, $a>0$, degenerating to two length-$\pi$ segments as $a\to0$, and records a then-best lower bound 0.6085, nearby-family results, and results under extra geometric constraints. It records discussions by Benguria, Loss, Burchard, Thomas and Linde; claims by Linde and Burchard about possibly cornered minimizers; a possible affine symmetry; proposed flows by Carlen, Mazzeo and Benguria, including affine curvature flow; ODE methods proposed by Rapti and Lee; and Laugesen's suggested Birman--Schwinger operator $T=\kappa({\,\mathrm{d}}^2/{\,\mathrm{d}} s^2+c)^{-1}\kappa$, $0<c<1$, whose largest eigenvalue is proposed to exceed one. The suggested endpoint $c=1$ must address the kernel spanned by $\sin s,\cos s$ and the stated restriction $\kappa\psi\perp\sin s,\cos s$; strict inequality at equality curves is unresolved in the excerpt. The cornered-minimizer claims also require a relaxed admissible class. These ancillary questions and proposed approaches are preserved as source reports, not supplied solutions.

Short English statement

The record combines Pólya, eigenvalue-sum, Lieb--Thirring, and Ovals questions with historical updates. The supplied partial theorem transfers an order-one Riesz deficit at one Weyl cutoff into a normalized eigenvalue-mean correction and propagates valid profiles to higher orders. It gives the explicit second term on the unit square; it does not solve the full collection.
