Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.97

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $H$, define its derived series by $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(k+1)}=[H^{(k)},H^{(k)}]$. The group has derived length $n$ if $H^{(n)}=1$ and $H^{(n-1)}\neq 1$. Let $L_r$ be the free group on $r$ generators. The free soluble group of rank $r$ and derived length $n$ is \[ F=L_r/L_r^{(n)}. \] A group is $m$-generated if it has a generating set of at most $m$ elements; minimality of this generating set is not assumed. Write $\operatorname{Th}(H)$ for the set of all first-order sentences true in $H$, in the language of groups with multiplication, inversion, and identity.

For every pair of integers $n\geq 3$ and $r\geq 2$, every positive integer $m$, and every $m$-generated group $G$, does \[ \operatorname{Th}(G)=\operatorname{Th}(F) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\cong F \] hold?

The supplied record states that the answer is affirmative when $m\leq r$. It also states that the analogous question has an affirmative answer for $n=2$, a case outside the main question's stated range $n\geq 3$. These affirmative-case assertions and the record's open-status label are supplied source qualifications, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

Must every finitely generated group with the same first-order theory as a finite-rank free soluble group of derived length at least three be isomorphic to that free soluble group? The source reports affirmative answers when $m\leq r$, and for the analogous derived-length-two case.
