Problem 3.6 — Study the double coset HφH inM, using new finite or infinite quotients of M.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal M$ be the source's mapping class group, $H\le\mathcal M$ its handlebody subgroup and $\phi\in\mathcal M$ the Heegaard gluing parameter, with all markings and reference gluing still to be recovered. Problem 3.6 asks to study \[ H\phi H=\{h_1\phi h_2:h_1,h_2\in H\} \] using new finite or infinite quotients of $\mathcal M$, emphasizing finite quotients. For a quotient homomorphism $q:\mathcal M\to Q$, the induced double coset is $q(H)q(\phi)q(H)$. If $Q$ is finite and explicitly computable, images of generators of $H$ generate its image, avoiding the source's earlier difficulty of recognizing that image in infinite quotients. No quotient family or completeness criterion is supplied, and equality in a quotient is not itself asserted to classify the original double coset.

The proposed applications are:

  1. Determine how many stabilizations are needed before two inequivalent Heegaard splittings become equivalent.
  2. Detect when a Heegaard splitting is not of minimal genus.
  3. Detect when a Heegaard splitting is stabilized.
  4. Decide whether any of the earlier, unprovided representations help answer the preceding three questions.
Conventionally stabilization is connected sum with the standard genus-one splitting of $S^3$. A splitting of a manifold $X$ is nonminimal when its genus exceeds the least Heegaard genus of $X$; stabilized means it comes from a lower-genus splitting by stabilization. These are distinct tests, not interchangeable conditions. In (1), the intended common underlying manifold, splitting equivalence, and counting stabilizations on the two sides must be fixed.

The source cites a presentation of $H$ by Waynryb [51], preserving the supplied spelling, as input for quotient-image computation. It also reminds the reader that, for homology spheres and after changing the reference gluing or "base point," the gluing parameter may range over the Torelli group $I_g$, and by Morita [36] even over $K_g$. The subgroup definitions and homology conventions are missing. This contextual reduction is not a claim that raw identity-based gluing by $I_g$ alone produces homology spheres. The final sentence introduces another question not supplied in the extract.

Short English statement

Use manageable quotient images of the handlebody double coset to investigate stabilization, minimal genus and detection of stabilized Heegaard splittings. Keep finite and infinite quotient options, with the source's practical emphasis on finite quotients. The representations and original gluing conventions are missing.
