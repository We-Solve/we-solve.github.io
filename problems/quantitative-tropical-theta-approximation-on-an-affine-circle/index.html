Math-1494

Quantitative tropical-theta approximation on an affine circle

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's Section A.8 contains a program in tropical affine geometry. It calls a real $m$-manifold $B$, with dense integral-affine locus $B_0$ and discriminant $\Delta=B\setminus B_0$, a "tropical Calabi--Yau manifold" when affine transitions lie in ${\mathbb{R}}^m\rtimes\operatorname{GL}_m({\mathbb{Z}})$ and $\Delta$ is a locally finite union of locally closed submanifolds. It proposes integral-slope piecewise-linear functions on $B_0$, extending continuously to $B$, as a structure sheaf. Sturmfels asks whether these singularities define a natural tropical Calabi--Yau variety, in relation to Zharkov's question about cutting tentacles. The bare definition supplies no balancing, polarization, local singularity model or convexity data.

Let $\operatorname{Aff}(B,{\mathbb{R}})$ be the additive sheaf of continuous functions restricting to integral-slope affine functions on $B_0$. The source defines a tropical line bundle $\mathcal L$ by $H^1(B,\operatorname{Aff}(B,{\mathbb{R}}))$. For an additive Čech cocycle $\alpha_{ij}$, a section is a compatible collection of tropical functions $s_i$ with $s_i-s_j=\alpha_{ij}$, using ordinary subtraction. It mentions theta functions on tori. Eisenbud asks for tropical Riemann--Roch, also referring to the page-4 Riemann--Roch question, and the section asks for ampleness and meaningful embeddings into tropical projective space, including for more general tropical varieties.

Its concrete exercise uses the homogeneous max-plus cubic whose ten terms are \[ -6+3x,\ -4+2x+y,\ -3+x+2y,\ -6+3y,\ -4+2y+z, \] \[ -3+y+2z,\ x+y+z,\ -3+2x+z,\ -1+x+2z,\ -3+3z, \] where the first four homogeneous terms also include the appropriate zero powers of $z$. Equivalently the first term is $-6+3x$ and all displayed terms have total tropical degree three. The tropical curve is the locus where the maximum is attained at least twice. After removing unbounded rays, an edge with $v-w=\ell d$, $d$ primitive integral, has affine length $|\ell|$. The source asks to check perimeter $13$, embed the resulting circle ${\mathbb{R}}/13{\mathbb{Z}}$ in $\mathbb{TP}^2$ using three degree-five line-bundle sections, and interpret the observed contraction of segments with degree-three sections as a possible failure of very ampleness. The aid instead reports perimeter $15$ for the exact printed coefficients under the standard projective lattice convention. This conflict is unresolved; neither the length-$13$ assertion nor the requested degree-five embedding is adopted as a verified result. Failure of one chosen triple of sections would also need distinguishing from failure of every complete linear-system embedding.

For the SYZ-related part, the integral-affine lattice $\Lambda\subset TB_0$ is locally spanned by $\partial/\partial y^j$, and its dual $\check\Lambda\subset T^*B_0$ by ${\,\mathrm{d}} y^j$. The source constructs the complex torus bundle $X(B_0)=TB_0/\Lambda$ and canonical symplectic bundle $\check X(B_0)=T^*B_0/\check\Lambda$. It seeks smooth local sections $u_i-u_j=\alpha_{ij}$ with positive Hessian satisfying \[ \det\left(\frac{\partial^2u_i}{\partial y^j\partial y^k}\right) =\text{positive constant}, \] so that their pullbacks give semi-flat Ricci-flat Kähler potentials under the necessary compatibility hypotheses. Gross, Siebert and Zharkov ask for a tropical Monge--Ampère equation and sections $s_n\in\Gamma(B,\mathcal L^{\otimes n})$ with $n^{-1}s_n$ converging to such a solution, together with numerical construction and visualization. This sequence notation explicitly repairs the source's $s_i/s_n$ mismatch using $\hbar=1/n$. Singular extension, boundary conditions, convergence topology and an actual numerical algorithm are not specified.

The aid's quantitative special case has no singularities. Let $B={\mathbb{R}}/L{\mathbb{Z}}$, $L>0$, with positive degree $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$ and automorphy $u(y+L)-u(y)=dy+dL/2$. Normalize \[ u(y)=\frac{dy^2}{2L},\qquad T_n(y)=\max_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(jy-\frac{Lj^2}{2nd}\right)\quad(n\geq1). \] Then $T_n(y+L)-T_n(y)=ndy+ndL/2$, so $T_n$ is a max-plus section of $\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$, and \[ u(y)-n^{-1}T_n(y) =\frac{L}{2n^2d}\operatorname{dist}(ndy/L,{\mathbb{Z}})^2 \leq\frac{L}{8n^2d}. \] The constant-Hessian potential with the fixed automorphy and $u(0)=0$ is unique. Writing distributional Hessians as periodic measures on the circle, for every Lipschitz test function $\phi$, \[ \left|\int_B\phi\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\left(n^{-1}T_n''-(d/L){\,\mathrm{d}} y\right)\right| \leq\frac{L}{4n}\operatorname{Lip}(\phi). \] The notation on the left denotes integration against the signed difference measure, not an additional derivative. This proves an $O(n^{-2})$ potential and $O(n^{-1})$ weak-measure approximation in that circle class, not the broad singular higher-dimensional problem.

The original remark also interprets phase/translation patching classes in $H^1(B_0,(\Lambda\otimes{\mathbb{R}})/\Lambda)$ as B-field data under mirror symmetry. The aid reports established tropical Riemann--Roch/curve linear-system theories and more structured Gross--Siebert singular-affine settings, while qualifying general singular Monge--Ampère approximation as known only in structured cases; no complete modern status theorem is supplied.

Short English statement

The section asks for singular tropical Calabi--Yau structures, line bundles, Riemann--Roch, embeddings and Monge--Ampère approximation. The aid gives exact theta-section convergence on an affine circle. It also flags that the printed cubic's reported perimeter is $15$, conflicting with the exercise's $13$; the general singular program is not resolved.
