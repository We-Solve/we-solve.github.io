Math-14714

Universal Permuton Convergence and Algebraic Functional Equations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A permutation of size $n$ is a bijection $\sigma:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A pattern of $\sigma$ is the permutation obtained by replacing the entries of a subsequence by their relative ranks. A permutation class $C$ is a set of permutations closed under taking patterns. Write $C_n=C\cap S_n$, and, whenever $C_n\ne\varnothing$, let $\sigma_{n,C}$ be uniformly distributed on the finite set $C_n$. An asymptotic question as $n\to\infty$ requires $C$ to contain permutations of arbitrarily large size; a finite class does not provide such a sequence.

A permuton is a Borel probability measure on $[0,1]^2$ whose two coordinate marginals are uniform. Associate to $\sigma\in S_n$ the permuton $\mu_\sigma$ with density $n$ on the union of the squares \[ \bigcup_{i=1}^n \left[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}\right] \times \left[\frac{\sigma(i)-1}{n},\frac{\sigma(i)}{n}\right], \] and density zero elsewhere, with square boundaries irrelevant to the measure. Equip the space of permutons with the weak topology.

Permuton convergence question. For every permutation class $C$ containing permutations of arbitrarily large size, does $\mu_{\sigma_{n,C}}$ converge as $n\to\infty$? The supplied record does not specify whether the intended conclusion is convergence in distribution to a possibly random permuton, \[ \mu_{\sigma_{n,C}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm d}M_C, \] or convergence in probability to a deterministic permuton $\mu_C$. These are distinct formulations; neither is selected here. Almost-sure convergence would additionally require a coupling across sizes, which is not supplied.

Graphon direction. A graphon, in the usual simple undirected graph setting, is a symmetric measurable function $W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]$; graph limits are commonly considered up to zero cut distance. The source asks what happens in contexts such as graphons. It does not specify the graph families, their closure properties, the sampling law, the limiting topology, or whether the limit is deterministic or random. Thus this is an open direction in the supplied record, rather than a fully quantified graphon conjecture. The preceding definition describes a conventional setting and does not establish that this is the intended setting.

Algebraicity questions. The coefficient field, its characteristic, and the admissible formal-series rings are not specified. To express the requested implication, let $K$ denote a field that must be specified as part of a precise formulation. In the possible formal interpretation $F\in K[[t,u]]$, the series $F(t,0)$ and $(\partial_uF)(t,0)$ are well defined in $K[[t]]$. Here algebraicity over $K(t,u)$ means that there exists a nonzero polynomial $Q\in K[t,u,Y]$ such that \[ Q(t,u,F(t,u))=0. \] The first requested task is to identify explicit hypotheses on the coefficient setting, a polynomial $P$, and its admissible solutions under which \[ P\left(F(t,u),F(t,0),(\partial_uF)(t,0),t,u\right)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(t,u)\text{ is algebraic over }K(t,u). \] For this formal interpretation one may take $P\in K[X,A,B,t,u]$. This ambient ring is an explicit interpretation, not a coefficient specification supplied by the source. The original excerpt includes "etc." after the listed arguments, so the displayed equation need not exhaust the intended types of auxiliary terms. No unconditional implication for arbitrary $P$ or arbitrary solutions is asserted.

For the multivariate question, a corresponding possible interpretation uses finite lists \[ F\in K[[t,u,v]],\qquad G_1,\ldots,G_r\in K[[t,u]],\qquad H_1,\ldots,H_s\in K[[t]], \] and a polynomial relation \[ P\left(F(t,u,v),G_1(t,u),\ldots,G_r(t,u), H_1(t),\ldots,H_s(t),t,u,v\right)=0. \] Identify explicit hypotheses under which every admissible solution $F$ is algebraic over $K(t,u,v)$, meaning that some nonzero $Q\in K[t,u,v,Y]$ satisfies $Q(t,u,v,F)=0$. The source does not specify relations determining the auxiliary series, initial conditions, uniqueness requirements, or nondegeneracy assumptions. Its phrase "natural assumptions" remains an unresolved part of the requested characterization, not an established hypothesis or theorem.

Short English statement

The supplied entry asks whether uniform permutations from every class of unbounded size have a permuton limit, seeks an analogous graphon question, and asks which polynomial functional equations guarantee algebraicity of their formal-series solutions. The convergence mode, graphon model, coefficient setting, and algebraicity hypotheses remain unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical and source review; the supplied open-status assessment is dated curation.
