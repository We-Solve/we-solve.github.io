A two-vertex counterexample to color-number monotonicity

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
For a finite graph $G$, a proper $q$-coloring assigns colors to vertices with adjacent colors distinct. Single-site Glauber dynamics selects a vertex and color uniformly, accepting the recoloring only when proper. Its mixing time is $t(q)=\min\{t:\max_\sigma\|P_q^t(\sigma,\cdot)-\pi_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le1/e\}$, where $\pi_q$ is uniform on proper colorings. Random $k$-SAT has $n$ variables and $m=rn$ clauses; a clustering transition concerns the geometry of its solution set.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
The record includes source questions (5)--(16), not only the coloring question.
  1. For a finite graph of maximum degree $\Delta$, does $t(q)$ decrease as $q$ increases, in the ergodic range $q\ge\Delta+2$? For a square lattice with $n$ sites, determine mixing behavior for $q=4,5$, in the source’s boundary convention; it records historical polynomial behavior for $q=3$ and $O(n\log n)$ for $q\ge6$.
  2. Is some $k$-SAT variant, possibly on a lattice, a suitable Boolean model for the scaling of jamming?
  3. Construct a constrained phase-transition model, soften its constraints and determine sensitivity to that change.
  4. Identify useful response or correlation functions for constraint-satisfaction problems such as $k$-SAT and determine their transition singularities or power laws.
  5. Explain the physical meaning, if any, of the finite external field used in the refined second-moment satisfiability bound, including whether it minimizes suitable zero-temperature free-energy fluctuations.
  6. Find algorithms that empirically solve in roughly linear time at values of $k$ and clause density where clustering has been rigorously established.
  7. Prove clustering below the satisfiability transition for $k=3,4,5$, in the source’s setting.
  8. Prove an order/disorder transition for equilibrium hard spheres or hard disks.
  9. Determine how to incorporate friction into a jamming model.
  10. For Ising Glauber dynamics on the $d$-dimensional lattice at fixed $\beta$ (and possibly fixed external field), determine whether sharp total-variation cutoff in the large-volume limit occurs exactly when the infinite-volume Gibbs state is unique.
  11. Define a mathematically useful thermodynamic limit of random $k$-SAT with $n\to\infty$ and $m=rn$ at constant $r$.
  12. For an Ising model in a finite $\mathbb Z^d$ box with $\beta>\beta_c$, zero field and plus boundary, determine whether Glauber mixing from the source’s warm starts (all plus or random) is $O(n\log n)$, with $n$ sites.
