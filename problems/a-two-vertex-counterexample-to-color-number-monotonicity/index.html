Definitions and notation.
For a finite graph $G$, a proper $q$-coloring assigns colors to vertices with adjacent colors distinct. Single-site Glauber dynamics selects a vertex and color uniformly, accepting the recoloring only when proper. Its mixing time is $t(q)=\min\{t:\max_\sigma\|P_q^t(\sigma,\cdot)-\pi_q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le1/e\}$, where $\pi_q$ is uniform on proper colorings. Random $k$-SAT has $n$ variables and $m=rn$ clauses; a clustering transition concerns the geometry of its solution set.

An exact two-vertex coloring-chain calculation.
Take a graph consisting of two isolated vertices, so $\Delta=0$ and every $q\ge2$ is in the stated ergodic range. At each discrete step, one vertex is chosen uniformly and its color is replaced by a uniform color; every proposal is accepted. Start at $(1,1)$. After $t\ge1$, the probability that one particular vertex has never been selected is $2^{-t}$, and both cannot be unselected. Conditional on both having been selected, the final colors are independent uniform; conditional on only one being selected, the other remains one. Therefore \[P^t-\pi=2^{-t}(\delta_1\otimes u+u\otimes\delta_1-2u\otimes u), \] where $u$ is the uniform measure on $q$ colors. Summing absolute values over the cases where zero, one or two coordinates equal one gives \[\|P^t-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2^{-t}\,2(q-1)^2/q^2. \] All starting states have the same distance by color symmetry. At $q=2$, the distance at one step is $1/4<1/e$, while at time zero it is $3/4$, so $t(2)=1$. At $q=3$, the one-step distance is $4/9>1/e$ and the two-step distance is $2/9<1/e$, so $t(3)=2$. Thus monotonic decrease fails for this explicit finite graph under the source's threshold and update rule.
