Extremal Invariants of Bounded-Volume Hyperbolic Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the conventional interpretation of a hyperbolic metric as a complete Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$, fix an integer $n\geq 2$ and a real number $v>0$. Let \[ \mathcal H_n(v)=\{(M,g): M\text{ is a connected closed hyperbolic }n\text{-manifold},\ \operatorname{Vol}_g(M)\leq v\}/\text{isometry}. \] Here closed means compact without boundary. No orientability restriction is stated in the source. The curvature normalization and dimension range above are conventional interpretations that require confirmation against the cited report.

For $(M,g)\in\mathcal H_n(v)$, define its systole by \[ \operatorname{sys}(M,g)=\min\{\ell_g(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a nonconstant closed geodesic in }M\}. \] Provisionally interpret its kissing number as \[ K(M,g)=\#\{\gamma:\gamma\text{ is an unoriented primitive closed geodesic},\ \ell_g(\gamma)=\operatorname{sys}(M,g)\}, \] where a primitive geodesic is not an iterate of a shorter closed geodesic, and geodesics differing only by parametrization or reversal are counted once. The source does not specify this counting convention, so this definition requires confirmation.

Let $\Delta_g=-\operatorname{div}_g\nabla_g$ be the nonnegative Laplace--Beltrami operator on real-valued functions. Write its eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, as \[ 0=\lambda_0(M,g)<\lambda_1(M,g)\leq\lambda_2(M,g)\leq\cdots, \qquad m_1(M,g)=\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}\ker(\Delta_g-\lambda_1(M,g)I). \] The choice of the scalar Laplace--Beltrami operator is the conventional reading of the supplied wording.

The problem is to determine the extremal values of these four invariants over $\mathcal H_n(v)$. More precisely, when this class is nonempty, the separate optimization interpretation asks for \[ \sup_{(M,g)\in\mathcal H_n(v)}\operatorname{sys}(M,g),\qquad \sup_{(M,g)\in\mathcal H_n(v)}K(M,g),\qquad \sup_{(M,g)\in\mathcal H_n(v)}\lambda_1(M,g),\qquad \sup_{(M,g)\in\mathcal H_n(v)}m_1(M,g), \] and for the manifolds attaining each supremum, when it is attained. No existence of maximizers is asserted. The source's phrase "determine or maximize" does not specify whether separate extrema, simultaneous optimization, or a broader classification is intended; the displayed formulation records the separate optimization interpretation.

For an orientable closed hyperbolic surface of genus $g\geq2$, \[ \operatorname{Area}(M)=4\pi(g-1). \] Thus prescribing its area is equivalent to prescribing its genus, whereas imposing an area upper bound only bounds the possible genera. For a nonorientable closed hyperbolic surface of nonorientable genus $h\geq3$, the corresponding formula is $\operatorname{Area}(M)=2\pi(h-2)$. Consequently, the source's assertion that volume is equivalent to fixing genus in dimension two needs both an orientability convention and a distinction between prescribed volume and a volume upper bound.

Short English statement

Determine the largest possible systole, number of shortest closed geodesics, first positive scalar Laplace eigenvalue, and its multiplicity among connected closed hyperbolic manifolds with volume at most $v$. The draft interprets these as four separate optimization problems. Counting conventions, orientability, and the intended relation between bounded area and fixed genus require review.
