Math-6650

Potentials for Quivers from Twice-Punctured Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns quivers associated with triangulations of closed surfaces of positive genus having exactly two punctures. Under the conventional interpretation, let $\Sigma$ be a compact connected oriented surface without boundary, of genus $g\geq 1$, let $M\subset\Sigma$ consist of exactly two distinct marked points, and let $\tau$ be a triangulation of $(\Sigma,M)$. Write $Q(\tau)$ for its signed adjacency quiver. The record does not explicitly specify orientability, connectedness, the kind of triangulation, or the conventions for self-folded triangles; this interpretation therefore requires source confirmation.

Fix a coefficient field $k$, whose required hypotheses are not supplied. For a finite quiver $Q$ without loops or oriented two-cycles, let $k\langle\!\langle Q\rangle\!\rangle$ denote its completed path algebra, completed with respect to the ideal generated by the arrows. A potential on $Q$ is a possibly infinite $k$-linear combination of cyclic paths, considered up to cyclic equivalence. Here cyclic equivalence identifies a cyclic path with its cyclic rotations and takes the closure of the resulting relations in the arrow-ideal topology.

Two potentials $S,S'$ on $Q$ are weakly right equivalent, written $S\sim_{\mathrm{wr}}S'$, if there are a scalar $\lambda\in k^{\times}$ and a continuous $k$-algebra automorphism $\varphi$ of $k\langle\!\langle Q\rangle\!\rangle$, fixing every vertex idempotent, such that $\varphi(S)$ is cyclically equivalent to $\lambda S'$. A potential is non-degenerate in the quiver-with-potential mutation sense if every finite sequence of vertex mutations can be performed successively and, after reduction at each step, the resulting quiver has no oriented two-cycles. Let $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{nd}}(Q;k)$ denote the collection of such potentials.

Question, with unresolved source conventions. For every surface and triangulation in the intended class, is there exactly one weak right equivalence class of non-degenerate potentials on its associated quiver? With the coefficient field and triangulation conventions fixed, the question is \[ \left|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{nd}}\left(Q(\tau);k\right)/\!\sim_{\mathrm{wr}}\right|=1 \qquad\text{for every admissible }(\Sigma,M,\tau),\quad g(\Sigma)\geq 1,\quad |M|=2. \] This asks both for existence and for equivalence of any two non-degenerate potentials on each fixed quiver. It does not assert equivalence between potentials on different quivers. The coefficient-field hypotheses and the precise class of admissible triangulations are unresolved in the supplied record; the display is a formulation schema, not an assertion universally quantified over all fields.

Short English statement

For every quiver obtained from a triangulation of a closed positive-genus surface with exactly two punctures, ask whether a non-degenerate potential exists and whether any two such potentials differ only by a right equivalence and multiplication by a nonzero scalar. The coefficient field and triangulation conventions require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
