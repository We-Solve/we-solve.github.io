Math-1713

A rectangle-data obstruction and a precise k-Schur boundary test

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The AIM workshop question asks for a good definition of level-restricted $q,t$-Kostka numbers, asks whether such a definition makes sense for root systems other than type $A$, and asks for its relationship with $k$-$q,t$-Kostka numbers. Here $q,t$ denote deformation parameters. A level restriction is additional representation-theoretic data; the source does not specify its normalization, the indexing set, or axioms characterizing a "good" definition. The parameter called $k$ in the last question must not be identified with the level without an explicit convention.

The supplied research aid concerns the generalized version with a sequence $R=(R_1,\ldots,R_m)$ of rectangular partitions. Write $s_\nu$ for a Schur function and $[s_\lambda]f$ for its coefficient in $f$. At stable level, the ungraded tensor-product multiplicity associated with this sequence is \[ c_{\lambda,R}=[s_\lambda]\prod_{j=1}^m s_{R_j}. \] The aid gives the following obstruction to forgetting the block structure. In type $A$, with at least two available rows, take \[ R_{\mathrm{col}}=((1,1)),\qquad R_{\mathrm{split}}=((1),(1)),\qquad \lambda=(2). \] The first sequence consists of one two-box column, whereas the second consists of two one-box rectangles. Forgetting the rectangle separators and retaining only their row lengths gives $\mu=(1,1)$ in both cases. Nevertheless, \[ [s_{(2)}]s_{(1,1)}=0,\qquad s_{(1)}^2=s_{(2)}+s_{(1,1)},\qquad [s_{(2)}]s_{(1)}^2=1. \] Consequently, no family depending only on $(\lambda,\mu,\ell)$, where $\ell$ is the level and $\mu$ is precisely this separator-forgetting encoding, can specialize to all generalized level-restricted multiplicities: choose a common stable level for these two sequences, and its value at $q=t=1$ would have to be both $0$ and $1$. A proposed two-parameter refinement must retain $R$, or restrict to a specified canonical choice $R=R(\mu)$. This does not obstruct ordinary Kostka families with a fixed interpretation of their content index.

This obstruction leaves the original construction problem open in the supplied record, including its non-type-$A$ part. The aid proposes comparing modern positive $k$-$q,t$ coefficients with crystal or fusion coefficients only after the rectangle subfamily, index correspondence, and normalization are specified. It singles out an explicitly normalized $Q=0$ boundary identity as the remaining comparison test. No formula for that identity, definition of $Q$, or relation between $Q$ and the original parameters $q,t$ is supplied, so an exact boundary equality cannot be asserted here.

Short English statement

Define a level-restricted two-parameter Kostka theory, determine its scope beyond type $A$, and compare it with $k$-$q,t$-Kostka numbers. The supplied partial result shows that a universal generalized theory cannot discard how its rectangles are grouped: one column and two separate boxes have different stable multiplicities despite identical ungrouped row data.
