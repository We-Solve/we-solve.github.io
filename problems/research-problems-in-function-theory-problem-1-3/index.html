Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 1.3

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f$ be a nonconstant meromorphic function on ${\mathbb{C}}$. Write $n(r,a)$ for the number of $a$-points in $|z|\le r$, counted with multiplicity, using poles for $a=\infty$, and $\overline n(r,a)$ for the corresponding count without multiplicity. Let $N(r,a)$ be the integrated counting function and \[ T(r,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|f(re^{it})|\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t+N(r,\infty), \quad \delta(a,f)=1-\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{N(r,a)}{T(r,f)}. \] The order is $\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log T(r,f)/\log r$; finite order means $\rho<\infty$. The deficiency sum runs over all $a\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}$, with nonnegative sums interpreted as suprema of finite subsums.

The conjecture recorded as Problem 1.3 is \[ \rho<\infty,\quad \sum_{a\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\delta(a,f)=2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho=\frac n2\text{ for an integer }n\ge2, \qquad \delta(a,f)\in{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ for every }a. \] The source credits F. Nevanlinna with this conclusion under the extra hypothesis of no multiple values, stated there as $n(r,a)=\overline n(r,a)$ for every value $a$, and also refers to R. Nevanlinna.

The supplied research summary reports that Drasin (1987) resolves the conjecture, even assuming only finite lower order $\liminf_{r\to\infty}\log T(r,f)/\log r<\infty$. It also reports that every deficient value is asymptotic, that the reduced denominators of the rational deficiencies are at most $2\rho$, and a growth representation $T(r,f)=r^\rho\ell(r)$. Here an asymptotic value means a limit of $f$ along a curve tending to infinity, in the spherical topology for $\infty$. The excerpt does not define the auxiliary function $\ell$, so no additional regularity for it is asserted. The summary mentions a shorter proof and two further properties due to Eremenko without specifying those properties. These are supplied literature claims, not independent verification.

Short English statement

A finite-order meromorphic function with maximal deficiency sum two should have half-integer order at least one and rational deficiencies. The supplied summary reports Drasin's affirmative solution and stronger conclusions.
