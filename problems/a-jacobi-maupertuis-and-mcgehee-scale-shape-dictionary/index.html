A Jacobi-Maupertuis and McGehee scale-shape dictionary

Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. At fixed energy $E$, the Jacobi--Maupertuis metric on the Hill region is conformal to the mass metric by the factor $2(E+U)$. Its geodesics, with appropriate reparametrization away from degeneracies, describe mechanical trajectories. McGehee coordinates rescale position and time to analyze collision or asymptotic boundaries. The two frameworks encode different geometric features.

Assume $E=0$ and angular momentum zero in the source’s celestial-mechanics setting. Can Jacobi’s metric give insights beyond those obtained from McGehee coordinates?
