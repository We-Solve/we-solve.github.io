Rigidity of Symmetric-Gradient Differential Inclusions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the purpose of explaining the notation, let $n\geq 1$, let $\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be open, and let $a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$. These parameters and their hypotheses are not specified in the supplied statement. Define the symmetric tensor product by \[ a\mathbin{\odot}b:=\frac{a\otimes b+b\otimes a}{2}, \qquad (a\otimes b)_{ij}:=a_i b_j, \] and the line, possibly degenerate, of symmetric matrices \[ L_{a,b}:=\{\lambda(a\mathbin{\odot}b):\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\}. \] For a differentiable map $u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^n$, its symmetric gradient is \[ Eu:=\frac{Du+(Du)^{\mathsf T}}{2}. \] The same expression defines a distribution for locally integrable $u$. A map belongs to $\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)$, the space of functions of bounded deformation, when $u\in L^1(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^n)$ and $Eu$ is a finite matrix-valued Radon measure.

The supplied problem asks: what structure can be proved for sufficiently regular $u$ satisfying \[ Eu\in L_{a,b} =\{\lambda(a\mathbin{\odot}b):\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\}? \] Under a function-valued interpretation with fixed $a,b$, the displayed constraint means that $Eu(x)\in L_{a,b}$ at the relevant points $x\in\Omega$; the scalar multiple may depend on $x$. The source does not specify whether the constraint holds everywhere, almost everywhere, or in a distributional or measure-valued sense. In particular, the reference to BD theory in the background does not specify how this inclusion is to be read when $Eu$ has a singular measure part.

This is an unresolved formulation of a structure question, rather than a specified theorem to prove. The dimension, domain, regularity class, assumptions and quantifiers on $a,b$, boundary conditions, and desired structural conclusion are absent. The notation above explains conventional meanings without selecting these missing hypotheses. A precise classification target requires their clarification.

Short English statement

The problem asks what structural restrictions follow when the symmetric gradient of a sufficiently regular vector field lies in the line generated by $a\mathbin{\odot}b$. The source leaves the regularity, domain, vector hypotheses, meaning of the inclusion, and intended structure unspecified. Its supplied classification as partially solved is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
