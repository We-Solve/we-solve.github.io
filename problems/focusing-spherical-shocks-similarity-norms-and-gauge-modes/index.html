Focusing spherical shocks: similarity norms and gauge modes

Definitions and mathematical statement

Recovered open problem. Are nonstationary spherical waves, for example focusing inviscid shocks, stable? The supplied boundary recovery attributes this first-page problem to S. Benzoni-Gavage, H. K. Jenssen and M. Williams. It specifies no governing gas/system, equation of state, dimension, shock profile, perturbation class or stability norm. The next-presenter kinetic equation is separate material.

Supplied self-similar kinematic reduction. Assume a translation-invariant whole-space problem has a focusing spherical front \[ R(t)=A(T-t)^\alpha,\qquad A>0,\quad\alpha>0,\quad t<T, \qquad\tau=-\log(T-t) \] in normalized time units. A linearized relative radial displacement with spherical harmonic $Y_\ell$ has the form \[ \frac{\delta R(t,\vartheta)}{R(t)} =\varepsilon e^{s\tau}Y_\ell(\vartheta),\qquad s\in{\mathbb{C}}. \] Its absolute displacement is proportional to $(T-t)^{\alpha-s}$. Therefore relative shape decays for $\operatorname{Re}s<0$, whereas absolute displacement decays for $\operatorname{Re}s<\alpha$. In particular $0<\operatorname{Re}s<\alpha$ is relative growth with absolute shrinking. Equalities are threshold cases; Jordan/logarithmic corrections are not classified. These are linearized magnitude statements, not a nonlinear stability conclusion after a growing relative perturbation ceases to be small.

Supplied gauge diagnostics. Varying the focusing time gives $\delta R/R=\alpha\,\delta T/(T-t)$, hence the radial branch $(\ell,s)=(0,1)$. Moving the center by a constant vector gives first-order absolute displacement $\delta a\cdot\vartheta$, hence the translation branch $(\ell,s)=(1,\alpha)$. These modes must appear in an unprojected similarity formulation when its admissible spectral domain includes the corresponding parameter derivatives. Fixing a boundary/center/time or choosing weighted spaces may exclude them, so an arbitrary reduced spectrum is not required to retain them. Stability modulo center and focusing-time changes must modulate or project these branches rather than count them automatically as physical shape instabilities.

The aid reports rigorous ideal-gas Guderley existence results and radial linear/nonspherical modal analyses, but no general nonlinear nonspherical stability theorem in the literature it checked. No specific equation, admissible-parameter theorem or proving citation is supplied. The source problem remains broader than the similarity norm and gauge-mode consistency test.

Separate kinetic paragraph. The appended P. Szmolyan "Kinetic and Boltzmann Equations" setup has damaged typography. Its standard reading would be $f_t+v\cdot\nabla_x f=Q(f,f)$; for a planar profile in direction $e$ the corresponding equation is $(v\cdot e-c)F'=Q(F,F)$. The printed $(v-c)f'$ can be read in one space dimension. The collision operator and kinetic hypotheses are not defined, and no completed kinetic question appears here. This paragraph and the running ARCC/page header are not part of the spherical-shock problem.

Short English statement

The source asks broadly about stability of focusing spherical shocks. The aid supplies a norm-sensitive diagnostic: relative and absolute perturbations have different decay thresholds, and time/center changes produce exact gauge branches. This does not prove nonlinear nonspherical stability. The appended Boltzmann setup belongs to the next presenter.
