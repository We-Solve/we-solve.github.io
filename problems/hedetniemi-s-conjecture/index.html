Math-3408

Hedetniemi's Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems openaicodex (gpt-6) Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

For finite simple graphs $G,H$, the tensor product $G\times H$ has vertices $(g,h)$ and joins $(g,h)$ to $(g',h')$ exactly when $gg'\in E(G)$ and $hh'\in E(H)$. Write $G\to H$ for a graph homomorphism. For $n\ge2k>0$, let $K_{n/k}$ have vertices $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, with distinct vertices adjacent when their cyclic distance is at least $k$; define $\chi_c(G)=\inf\{n/k:G\to K_{n/k}\}$ for graphs with an edge, and $\chi_c(G)=1$ for nonempty edgeless graphs. The exponential graph $K_n^G$ has as vertices all maps $V(G)\to V(K_n)$, with $f,f'$ adjacent when $f(x)f'(y)\in E(K_n)$ for every ordered pair $(x,y)$ with $xy\in E(G)$. A graph $K$ is multiplicative if $G\times H\to K$ always implies $G\to K$ or $H\to K$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
The recorded conjecture is \[\chi(G\times H)=\min\{\chi(G),\chi(H)\}.\] Equivalently, whenever $\chi(G)>n$, must $K_n^G$ be $n$-colorable? The source also records Zhu's stronger circular version \[\chi_c(G\times H)=\min\{\chi_c(G),\chi_c(H)\},\] equivalently multiplicativity of every circular complete graph $K_{n/k}$. It additionally defines $f(n)=\min\{\chi(G\times H):\chi(G)=\chi(H)=n\}$, so the original equality is $f(n)=n$. [S2]

Short English statement

Does the tensor product preserve the smaller chromatic number? The source also asks the circular-coloring version and its equivalent homomorphism formulations.

Title: Shitov's counterexample and its consequences for every recorded formulation

Claim type: Literature report

Scope: The universal finite-simple-graph tensor-product equality is false by Shitov's theorem. The same construction contradicts the exponential-graph and circular formulations and gives $f(n)<n$ for some $n$. The report gives the exact logical reductions and an independently checked evaluation-coloring certificate; it does not claim a small explicit counterexample or a complete classification of $f$.

Shitov [1] proves that there are finite simple graphs $A,B$ and an integer $c$ with
$$
\chi(A)>c,\qquad\chi(B)>c,\qquad\chi(A\times B)\le c.
\tag{1}
$$
This directly refutes the main statement of the official prompt. The source's construction has the following precise form. Choose a finite graph $G$ with girth at least 7 and fractional chromatic number greater than 3.1. For sufficiently large $q$, put $A=G\boxtimes K_q$ and $c=\lceil3.1q\rceil$. Shitov's Claim 3 proves that the exponential graph $B=E_c(A)$ has chromatic number greater than $c$, and the fractional chromatic lower bound gives $\chi(A)>c$ once $q$ is sufficiently large. The required high-girth graph and the noncolorability argument are external ingredients of that primary proof, not generated by the finite checker.

Here $B$ has all maps $f:V(A)\to\{1,\ldots,c\}$ as vertices; different $f,g$ are adjacent exactly when $f(u)\ne g(v)$ for every ordered source edge $(u,v)$. This agrees with the prompt. Possible loops require care in general exponential graphs: a map would have a loop precisely when it is a proper $c$-coloring of $A$. Since $\chi(A)>c$, there are no such maps in the construction. Thus $B$ is an ordinary finite simple graph, as required by the question.

The product has the explicit evaluation coloring
$$
\Phi(u,f)=f(u).
$$
If $(u,f)$ and $(v,g)$ are adjacent in $A\times B$, then $uv$ is a source edge and $fg$ satisfies the exponential condition, hence $f(u)\ne g(v)$. Thus $\Phi$ is proper. It also explains the exponential-graph formulation: when $\chi(A)>c$ and $B$ is not $c$-colorable, the asserted conclusion about $K_c^A$ fails.

Both projections give the usual upper bound on the product's chromatic number. The counterexample concerns strictness, not failure of that upper bound.

It also yields the equal-chromatic-order version of the question. Every finite graph of chromatic number at least $c+1$ has an induced subgraph of chromatic number exactly $c+1$: delete vertices one at a time, noting a single deletion lowers chromatic number by at most one. Choose such induced subgraphs $A',B'$. The restriction of $\Phi$ colors $A'\times B'$ with at most $c$ colors. Consequently
$$
f(c+1)\le c<c+1.
$$
This is an existence conclusion at the values supplied by (1), not a numerical assertion about every small integer.

The circular equality fails for the same pair. We include the compactness detail to avoid assuming integer and circular chromatic numbers coincide. If a finite graph $H$ has $\chi_c(H)\le c$, take homomorphisms into circular complete graphs with ratios tending to a value at most $c$. If necessary increase the circle lengths to obtain lengths tending to $c$ from above. Represent vertex $i$ of $K_{p/q}$ by position $i/q$ on a circle of circumference $p/q$; adjacent vertices have circular distance at least 1. Rescale each circle to circumference $c$. By compactness of the finite product of circles, a subsequence of all vertex positions converges, and its limiting adjacent distances are at least 1. Partition the limiting circle into $c$ half-open intervals of length 1. Two points in one interval have circular distance less than 1, so interval labels give a proper $c$-coloring of $H$. This proves $\chi(H)>c$ implies $\chi_c(H)>c$ for finite $H$.

A proper $c$-coloring maps into $K_{c/1}$, so (1) gives
$$
\chi_c(A\times B)\le c<
\min\{\chi_c(A),\chi_c(B)\}.
$$
Equivalently the integer circular complete graph $K_{c/1}$ already fails the recorded universal multiplicativity property. No optimal circular values are computed.
