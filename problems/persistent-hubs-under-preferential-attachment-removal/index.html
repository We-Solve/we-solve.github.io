Persistent Hubs under Preferential-Attachment Removal

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks about persistence of a maximum-degree vertex in preferential-attachment models with vertex removal. It does not specify an attachment rule, a removal rule, a parameter range, an initial graph, or a convention for ties. Thus it supplies a family of questions rather than a fully specified probabilistic assertion.

For a specified discrete-time model, let $(G_n)_{n\geq 0}$ be its random graph process, with vertex identities retained across time. Write $V_n=V(G_n)$, let $d_n(v)$ denote the degree of a present vertex under the model's degree convention, and, whenever $V_n\neq\varnothing$, define \[ M_n=\left\{v\in V_n:d_n(v)=\max_{u\in V_n}d_n(u)\right\}. \] One possible precise meaning of persistence is the event \[ \mathcal P=\left\{\exists N\geq0\ \exists v\in V_N\ \forall n\geq N:\ v\in M_n\right\}. \] This event requires that the same vertex remain present and have maximum degree at every sufficiently late time, while allowing ties. If instead the intended object is a single leader, the model must specify a rule selecting $L_n\in M_n$. Provided this leader is defined at every sufficiently late time, eventual persistence and infinitely many identity changes are respectively \[ \mathcal P_L=\left\{\exists N\geq0\ \exists v\ \forall n\geq N:\ L_n=v\right\}, \qquad \mathcal C_L=\left\{L_{n+1}\neq L_n\text{ for infinitely many }n\right\}. \] The source question is to determine, for the intended removal models and parameter values, whether the maximum-degree vertex eventually persists or its identity changes infinitely often. Whether this asks for an almost-sure dichotomy, event probabilities, or a classification across models is unspecified. The displayed events are candidate formalizations requiring these choices; the source does not establish their intended equivalence to its wording. Empty graphs, extinction, and any continuous-time interpretation also require explicit conventions.

The original excerpt additionally lists a question about the relation between local limits of preferential-attachment models and a "Bernoulli model." Neither that model nor the desired relation or mode of convergence is specified, so no precise secondary assertion can be extracted.

The adjacent graph-distance passage presents, as background, a theorem for $\mathrm{PA}_n(m,\delta)$ with $m\geq2$ and fixed $\delta>0$: for vertices $o_1,o_2$ chosen independently and uniformly from $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, there exist $0<c_1\leq c_2<\infty$ such that \[ \Pr\!\left(c_1\log n\leq \operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{PA}_n(m,\delta)}(o_1,o_2)\leq c_2\log n\right)\longrightarrow1. \] Here graph distance is the shortest-path distance; the excerpt also introduces $\operatorname{diam}(G)$, the graph diameter. The excerpt does not define this preferential-attachment model or specify disconnected-graph conventions. This supplied background statement does not furnish the missing hypotheses for the removal question.

Short English statement

Determine whether a maximum-degree vertex eventually remains the leader in preferential-attachment graphs with vertex removal, or whether the leader changes infinitely often. A precise statement requires the attachment and removal mechanisms, parameter ranges, probability quantifiers, and conventions for ties and extinction. The accompanying Bernoulli-model question is also underspecified.
