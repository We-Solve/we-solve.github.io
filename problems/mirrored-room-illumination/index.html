Math-1891

Mirrored-room illumination

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a bounded polygonal room and $s\in\Omega$ a point source. A point $q\in\Omega$ is illuminated from $s$ if it is reached by a finite polygonal light path starting at $s$, lying inside the room between reflections, and obeying equal angles of incidence and reflection at each side it hits. Direct paths with no reflection are allowed. Under the usual billiard convention, paths through vertices are excluded because no unique reflection rule is defined there. Let $I(\Omega,s)$ be the set of illuminated points and put \[ U(\Omega,s)=\Omega\setminus I(\Omega,s). \] Characterize the pairs $(\Omega,s)$ for which $U(\Omega,s)=\varnothing$. In particular, can $U(\Omega,s)$ be nonempty for a polygonal mirrored room and a chosen source?

The source also phrases the last question as the existence of a "dark region." If this means a nonempty open subset of $U(\Omega,s)$, or positive-area $U(\Omega,s)$, it is stronger than the existence of a single unilluminated point. The supplied summary attributes to Tokarsky (1995) rational-angle polygonal examples containing points not illuminated from a chosen source. That report gives a negative answer to universal illumination but does not itself supply a general characterization or establish an open dark region. Boundary targets, holes in the room, and corner trajectories are not specified in the source; the definitions above use interior targets and the usual nonvertex reflection rule.

Short English statement

Determine which polygonal mirrored rooms are fully illuminated from a specified point source. Distinguish the existence of an unilluminated point from the stronger possibility of an open or positive-area dark region.
