Math-6165

Covering by Random Translates

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a prime $p$, write $G_p={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$. For a nonempty set $A\subseteq G_p$, define its translate-covering number by \[ \tau_p(A)=\min\left\{|B|:B\subseteq G_p,\ A+B=G_p\right\}, \qquad A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}. \] Thus $\tau_p(A)\leq k$ means that at most $k$ translates $A+b$ cover $G_p$; overlap is permitted.

The supplied question asks whether a random set of size $\sqrt p$ almost surely has covering number at most $100\sqrt p$. Since $\sqrt p\notin{\mathbb{Z}}$ for prime $p$, that cardinality is not literal. To display the precise missing conventions, fix a choice \[ m_p\in\{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor,\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\}, \] and let $A_p$ be uniform among the $m_p$-element subsets of $G_p$. If "almost surely" is intended to mean with probability tending to one as $p$ tends to infinity through primes, the resulting candidate assertion is \[ \lim_{\substack{p\to\infty\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}} \Pr\left(\tau_p(A_p)\leq\lfloor100\sqrt p\rfloor\right)=1. \] The floor in the bound expresses an integer covering number being at most the real number $100\sqrt p$. The two choices of $m_p$, and the asymptotic meaning of the probability phrase, are explicit possible conventions, not decisions supplied by the original statement; no equivalence between variants is asserted.

The supplied triage dated 2026-08-17 identifies this as Green's Problem 39, labels it open, and reports that Green did not know the analogous assertion even with $100$ replaced by $1.01$. It reports no verified high-probability $O(\sqrt p)$-translate covering theorem at the stated density. These are supplied dated assessments rather than a new literature conclusion.

Short English statement

Does a uniformly random subset of roughly $\sqrt p$ elements in ${\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$ admit a cover of the whole group by at most $100\sqrt p$ of its translates, with probability tending to one? The original wording needs an integer size convention and a precise probability interpretation before this candidate formulation is fixed.
