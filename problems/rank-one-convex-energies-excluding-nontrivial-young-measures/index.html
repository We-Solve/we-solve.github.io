Rank-One Convex Energies Excluding Nontrivial Young Measures

Definitions and mathematical statement

For positive integers $m,n$, a function $f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ is rank-one convex if \[ f((1-t)A+tB)\leq (1-t)f(A)+tf(B) \] for all $A,B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(A-B)\leq1$ and all $t\in[0,1]$. This gives the standard full-matrix-space definition; the supplied record does not specify the dimensions, the domain of the integrand, or its regularity.

To explain outer variations conditionally, suppose that $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ is open, $f$ is continuously differentiable, and $u\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^m$ has sufficient regularity and integrability for the following expressions and differentiation to be valid. For the energy \[ I_f(u)=\int_\Omega f(Du(x))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] outer stationarity means \[ \left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}I_f(u+t\varphi)\right|_{t=0} =\int_\Omega Df(Du):D\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x=0 \qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^m). \] Here $A:B=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}B_{ij}$; equivalently, $\operatorname{div}Df(Du)=0$ in distributions. These are variations of the dependent variable. No stationarity under changes of the independent variable is imposed. This is a conditional explanation of the terminology, not a specification of the missing hypotheses in the source problem.

A parametrized Young measure on a finite-dimensional phase space $E$ is a weakly measurable family $(\nu_x)_{x\in\Omega}$ of Borel probability measures on $E$. Call it trivial if $\nu_x$ is a Dirac probability measure for almost every $x\in\Omega$. For a homogeneous Young measure, triviality means that its single probability measure is a Dirac mass. The supplied record does not identify the phase space, whether the measures are homogeneous, or which generating sequences and differential constraints are admissible.

Requested mathematical target, with unresolved qualifications. Find rank-one convex integrands $f$ for which every admissible Young measure associated with the differential inclusion for critical points under outer variations only is trivial. If $\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{out}}(f)$ denotes the intended admissible class, the desired property is \[ \forall\nu\in\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{out}}(f),\qquad \nu_x\text{ is a Dirac mass for almost every }x. \] The notation $\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{out}}(f)$ is a placeholder for information absent from the supplied record. In particular, the actual differential inclusion, the admissibility and generation conditions, growth assumptions, boundary conditions, and dimensions remain unspecified. Consequently, the record does not determine a unique fully quantified problem. The quantifier over Young measures must not be interpreted as ranging over all probability measures supported on an unspecified inclusion set.
