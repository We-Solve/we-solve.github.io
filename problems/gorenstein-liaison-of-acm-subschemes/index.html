Math-1177

Gorenstein Liaison of ACM Subschemes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question. Is every arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay (ACM) subscheme of ${\mathbb{P}}^n$ in the Gorenstein liaison class of a complete intersection, that is, glicci? In a standard homogeneous formulation over a fixed field $k$, write $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ and let $I_X\subset S$ be the saturated homogeneous ideal of a closed subscheme $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$. ACM means that its homogeneous coordinate ring $S/I_X$ is Cohen--Macaulay, equivalently $\operatorname{depth}(S/I_X)=\dim(S/I_X)$.

For the intended pure codimension $c$, two subschemes $X,Y$ are directly Gorenstein linked if there is an arithmetically Gorenstein subscheme $Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$ of the same codimension containing both, with the residual ideal equalities\[I_Y=I_Z:I_X,\qquad I_X=I_Z:I_Y.\] Here $S/I_Z$ is Gorenstein, and $I:J=\{s\in S:sJ\subseteq I\}$. The usual unmixed and saturated hypotheses belong to this projective liaison convention. A complete intersection of codimension $c$ has homogeneous ideal generated by a regular sequence of length $c$. The question asks whether for every ACM $X$ there is a finite chain $X=X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_r\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$, each consecutive pair directly Gorenstein linked, with $X_r$ a complete intersection. This is Gorenstein liaison, not a requirement that every link itself be a complete intersection, and no even-link restriction is imposed. The ambient projective space is kept fixed; passage to a larger projective embedding would be an additional variant.

The source does not specify the base field, characteristic, or detailed liaison conventions, so the standard formulation above requires source checking. Its original status commentary names good determinantal schemes as known examples. The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the problem partially solved and mentions codimension-two, determinantal, monomial, arithmetically Gorenstein, and other cases, while calling the general ACM-to-glicci assertion open. Exact hypotheses for these named classes are not given, so no unrestricted theorem about every member of a broadly named class is inferred. This dated assessment requires independent verification.

Short English statement

Can every ACM projective subscheme be connected to a complete intersection by finitely many Gorenstein links in the same projective space? The source reports many special cases but leaves the general assertion open in its dated triage. Gorenstein links must be distinguished from complete-intersection links.
