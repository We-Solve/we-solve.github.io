Extremal Fat-Point Initial Degrees and Star Configurations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source poses two questions about initial degrees of fat points. It does not specify the ground field, its characteristic, or the precise meaning of initial degree in the multiprojective question. The formulations below explain standard interpretations and identify the choices still requiring review.

For a field $k$, a positive integer $n$, and a finite set of distinct $k$-rational points $Z\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$, let $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ and let $I(P)\subseteq S$ be the homogeneous ideal of a point $P$. For an integer $m\geq1$, define the fat point scheme $mZ$ by \[ I(mZ)=\bigcap_{P\in Z}I(P)^m, \qquad \alpha(mZ)=\min\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}:I(mZ)_t\neq0\}. \] Thus $\alpha(Z)=\alpha(1Z)$, and membership in $I(mZ)$ means vanishing to order at least $m$ at every point of $Z$. These definitions use rational points over a chosen field; the source does not settle the intended field or whether points should instead be taken over its algebraic closure.

For hyperplanes $H_1,\ldots,H_d\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$ in general position, their point star configuration is \[ \operatorname{Star}_n(H_1,\ldots,H_d) =\bigcup_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\\|J|=n}} \bigcap_{j\in J}H_j. \] Here the usual general-position interpretation requires every collection of at most $n$ hyperplanes to meet in the expected codimension and no $n+1$ hyperplanes to have a common point. Whether the source's word "general" requires a stronger genericity condition is unresolved.

Projective question. Suppose \[ d=\alpha(nZ)=\alpha(Z)+n-1. \] Must either $Z$ be contained in a hyperplane, or there exist $d$ general hyperplanes $H_i=V(\ell_i)$ such that \[ Z=\operatorname{Star}_n(H_1,\ldots,H_d)? \] The source additionally asks for splitting of every degree-$d$ hypersurface vanishing to order at least $n$ on $Z$. If "those hyperplanes" means the same fixed arrangement, the assertion in the star-configuration alternative is \[ \forall\,0\neq F\in I(nZ)_d,\qquad F=c\prod_{i=1}^{d}\ell_i \quad\text{for some }c\in k^\times. \] The record does not unambiguously determine whether splitting is required only in this alternative, whether the factors must be this fixed arrangement, or whether a separate splitting assertion is also intended for the hyperplane alternative.

Multiprojective question. Let $X=({\mathbb{P}}^1_k)^n$, and let $Z\subseteq X$ be a finite set of points. Define $mZ$ locally by $\mathcal I_{mZ}=\bigcap_{P\in Z}\mathcal I_P^m$. The source asks whether \[ d=\alpha(nZ)=\alpha(Z) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z=A_1\times\cdots\times A_n, \qquad A_i\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^1(k),\quad |A_i|=d. \] Such a Cartesian product is a $d$ by $\cdots$ by $d$ grid. The symbol $\alpha$ on $X$ is not defined in the supplied record. One possible interpretation is the diagonal initial degree \[ \alpha_{\mathrm{diag}}(mZ) =\min\left\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}: H^0\!\left(X,\mathcal I_{mZ}\otimes\mathcal O_X(t,\ldots,t)\right)\neq0\right\}. \] Replacing both occurrences of $\alpha$ by $\alpha_{\mathrm{diag}}$ gives a precise candidate question after the ground-field and boundary conventions are fixed. This interpretation is not established by the supplied source record; total degree or other multigraded conventions must not be substituted silently. The record also does not explicitly exclude the empty set or specify the intended lower bound on $n$.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether an extremal equality between initial degrees of simple and fat points forces containment in a hyperplane or a star configuration, together with a splitting property for minimal-degree hypersurfaces. The second asks whether a corresponding equality on $({\mathbb{P}}^1)^n$ forces a Cartesian grid. The ground field, the multiprojective degree convention, boundary cases, and the scope of the splitting assertion require clarification.
