Pfaffian decomposition of global conformal invariants

Definitions and mathematical statement

The original field of Problem 32 is truncated at "Is it possible to write any S, as in". The supplied corrected statement asks whether a natural critical density with conformally invariant integral has a universal pointwise decomposition into an Euler/Pfaffian term, a local conformal invariant and an exact natural divergence. The verification reports recovering that statement from the source continuation; this draft retains the extraction limitation.

In the intended even-dimensional polynomial $O(n)$-natural category, let $n\geq2$ be even and $\mathbf S_g=P(g)\,dv_g$, where $P$ is a universal curvature/derivative scalar of weight $-n$. Assume $\int_M\mathbf S_g$ is invariant under conformal changes on closed $n$-manifolds. The aid reports that Alexakis's completed Deser--Schwimmer theorem gives \[ \mathbf S_g=c\,\mathbf{Pf}_g+W(g)\,dv_g+d(\iota_{T_g}dv_g), \] where $\int_M\mathbf{Pf}_g=\chi(M)$, $W$ is a pointwise conformal invariant of weight $-n$, and $T_g$ is natural. Closedness is used for the global integral premise and vanishing of the integrated divergence; the pointwise formula retains its divergence term.

The aid's coefficient functional is \[ \lambda(P)=\tfrac12\int_{S^n}P(g_{\mathrm{round}})\,dv_{\mathrm{round}}. \] Since the local conformal term vanishes on the conformally flat sphere, the divergence integrates to zero and $\chi(S^n)=2$, the Euler coefficient is $c=\lambda(P)$. If $\mathcal G_n$ denotes this space of global conformal invariant densities, $\mathcal I_n$ its local conformal invariants and $\mathcal D_n$ exact natural divergences, the reported conclusion is \[ \mathcal G_n/(\mathcal I_n+\mathcal D_n)\cong\mathbb R, \] with $\lambda$ inducing the isomorphism and Euler density generating the line. The numerator here has the global conformal-integral condition; it is not the unrestricted space of all critical-weight local invariants.

The aid also gives a separate oriented four-dimensional coefficient test. Let \[ \mathbf S=(x|\operatorname{Riem}|^2+y|\operatorname{Ric}|^2 +zR^2+t\Delta R)\,dv_g+p\,p_1(g), \] where coefficients are real constants and $p_1(g)$ is the first Pontrjagin form. In this displayed $SO(4)$-natural family, universal conformal invariance of the integral is reported equivalent to \[ x+y+3z=0. \] With $\Delta R=\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{grad}R)$ and $E_4=|\operatorname{Riem}|^2-4|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+R^2$, the exact decomposition is \[ \begin{split} \mathbf S={}&-\frac{2x+y}{2}E_4\,dv_g +(2x+y/2)|W|^2\,dv_g+p\,p_1(g)\\ &+t\,d(\iota_{\operatorname{grad}R}dv_g). \end{split} \] The Pontrjagin direction is a local conformal-invariant term in this four-dimensional oriented setting; it is not silently included in the initial parity-insensitive $O(n)$ category. The displayed $E_4$ is unnormalized, with $\int E_4dv=32\pi^2\chi$, so its coefficient differs from the Euler-characteristic-normalized $c$. These are reported theorem and corollary statements, not independently proved or newly classified here.

Short English statement

The recovered question is the global conformal-invariant decomposition theorem, which the supplied aid attributes to Alexakis. In its even-dimensional polynomial natural category, the quotient by local conformal terms and divergences is the Euler line, with coefficient read from the round sphere. The separate four-dimensional oriented example explicitly retains the Pontrjagin direction and normalization.
