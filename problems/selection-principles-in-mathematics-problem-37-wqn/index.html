Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 37 ([wqn])

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a topological space in the class intended by the source. The supplied record does not specify this class or its separation assumptions; in particular, it does not say whether $X$ must be a set of reals or may be an arbitrary Tychonoff space.

Write $C_p(X)=C(X,{\mathbb{R}})$ for the set of continuous real-valued functions on $X$, with the topology inherited from the product ${\mathbb{R}}^X$, and let $\mathbf{0}$ be the constant zero function. A basic neighborhood of $\mathbf{0}$ is \[ U(F,\varepsilon)=\{f\in C(X,{\mathbb{R}}): |f(x)|<\varepsilon\text{ for every }x\in F\}, \] where $F\subseteq X$ is finite and $\varepsilon>0$.

Using the usual countable-cover convention, let $\Gamma(X)$ consist of the countably infinite families $\mathcal U$ of open proper subsets of $X$ such that \[ \text{for every }x\in X,\qquad \left|\{U\in\mathcal U:x\notin U\}\right|<\infty. \] Let $\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}(C_p(X))$ consist of the countably infinite sets $A\subseteq C_p(X)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$ that converge to $\mathbf{0}$, meaning that \[ \text{for every finite }F\subseteq X\text{ and every }\varepsilon>0,\qquad |A\setminus U(F,\varepsilon)|<\infty. \] These are standard conventions for interpreting the notation; the supplied excerpt does not provide its own definitions or conventions concerning trivial covers and inclusion of the distinguished point.

For families $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ of sets, the selection principle $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ means that for every sequence $(A_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ with $A_n\in\mathcal A$, there are choices $a_n\in A_n$ such that $\{a_n:n\geq1\}\in\mathcal B$.

The source states, with the reference label $[\mathrm{wqn}]$, the implication \[ X\models\mathsf S_1(\Gamma(X),\Gamma(X)) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_p(X)\models\mathsf S_1\left(\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}(C_p(X)),\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}(C_p(X))\right). \] This implication is retained as a supplied assertion, without independent verification. The question is whether, for every $X$ in the intended class, \[ C_p(X)\models\mathsf S_1\left(\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}(C_p(X)),\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}(C_p(X))\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\models\mathsf S_1(\Gamma(X),\Gamma(X)). \] Thus, subject to the asserted forward implication, the requested direction is from the selection property of $C_p(X)$ to that of $X$. The unspecified class of spaces remains an unresolved qualification of this statement.

Short English statement

Does the property of selecting one function from each of countably many sets converging pointwise to zero, so that the selected set still converges to zero, characterize $\mathsf S_1(\Gamma,\Gamma)$ for $X$? The source supplies the implication from $X$ to $C_p(X)$ and asks for equivalence. Its intended class of spaces is unspecified.
