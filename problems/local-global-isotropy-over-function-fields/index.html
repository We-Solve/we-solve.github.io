Math-3545

Local–Global Isotropy over Function Fields

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be an integral scheme such that $2$ is invertible in $\mathcal O_X$, and let $K=K(X)$ be its function field. In particular, $\operatorname{char}(K)\ne 2$. Let $\Omega(K)$ denote the set of equivalence classes of all nontrivial rank-one discrete valuations of $K$. A representative may be normalized as a surjective valuation $v:K^\times\to{\mathbb{Z}}$; write $K_v$ for the corresponding completion. As stated in the supplied record, this set comprises all such valuations, with no restriction to valuations having a center on $X$.

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space of positive dimension, and let $q:V\to K$ be a quadratic form. The form is nondegenerate if its polar form $b_q(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)$ has zero radical. For a field extension $L/K$, write $q_L$ for the scalar extension to $V\otimes_K L$. The form $q_L$ is isotropic if there exists $0\ne x\in V\otimes_K L$ with $q_L(x)=0$.

The general question in the supplied record asks whether the following implication holds for every nondegenerate quadratic form $q$ over $K$: \[ \left(\forall v\in\Omega(K),\ \exists\,0\ne x_v\in V\otimes_K K_v\text{ such that }q_{K_v}(x_v)=0\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\exists\,0\ne x\in V\text{ such that }q(x)=0\right). \] The supplied background reports counterexamples in some classes of fields. Thus this broad question is retained as a question about the scope of the principle, without asserting that the unrestricted implication is an unresolved conjecture.

The more specific question is the following. Let $k$ be a totally imaginary number field, meaning a finite extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with no embedding into ${\mathbb{R}}$, let $C$ be an integral algebraic curve over $k$, and put $K=k(C)$. For every nondegenerate quadratic form $q$ over $K$ of dimension $n\ge 5$, does the displayed implication hold with $\Omega(K)$ consisting of all rank-one discrete valuations of $K$? The supplied record does not specify whether the curve must be geometrically integral, smooth, or proper, or whether additional conditions on its constant field are intended. These qualifications remain unresolved; none is imposed here.

Short English statement

The question asks when a quadratic form that has a nonzero zero over every discrete valuation completion also has a nonzero zero over the original field. Its specific target concerns forms of dimension at least $5$ over function fields of curves over totally imaginary number fields. The precise intended hypotheses on the curve require review.
