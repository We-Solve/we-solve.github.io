Directionality in rank-one transfer

Definitions and mathematical statement

The record contains three distinct source questions: develop relative endoscopy with the relative trace formula; interpret transfer from $\mathrm{GL}_2$ to an elliptic torus, opposite the direction in MR3117742; and relate intertwining operators to character relations, with Labesse--Langlands as a model. The trailing "Beyond Endoscopy" is a following heading. No local field, relative spaces, transfer normalization, or packet hypotheses are specified.

The aid's directionality obstruction fixes a local field $F$, a quadratic field extension $E/F$, $T=\operatorname{Res}_{E/F}\mathbb G_m$, and $G=\mathrm{GL}_2$. Their connected dual groups are $\widehat G=\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb{C}})$, $\widehat T=({\mathbb{C}}^\times)^2$, with Weil action on $\widehat T$ swapping coordinates outside $W_E$. For an L-homomorphism ${}^LG\to{}^LT$ respecting the Weil projection and algebraic on connected dual groups, the connected restriction must be \[ g\longmapsto((\det g)^m,(\det g)^m),\qquad m\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Characters of $\mathrm{GL}_2$ factor through determinant, and Weil equivariance forces the two exponents equal. Therefore it cannot retract a standard torus-to-group L-embedding, whose connected restriction is $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(z_1,z_2)$, or distinguish torus parameters with equal product. This excludes that reverse L-homomorphism interpretation, not every opposite test-function operator.

A different conditional construction is transpose transfer. After choosing test-function/distribution spaces and Haar or spectral pairings, a forward kernel operator $A:\mathcal H_T\to\mathcal H_G$ can have a transpose characterized by \[ \langle Af,h\rangle_G=\langle f,A^{\mathsf t}h\rangle_T. \] If represented by an integrable kernel $K(g,t)$, then $(Af)(g)=\int K(g,t)f(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t$ and $(A^{\mathsf t}h)(t)=\int K(g,t)h(g)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} g$, for bilinear pairings. A Hermitian adjoint instead conjugates the kernel. Such a reverse arrow depends on measures, domains, convergence, and the normalization of the forward stable-character kernel. The record supplies none of these analytic data, so it does not establish an unconditional reverse-transfer theorem or functorial inverse.

For the character relation, assume a finite abelian group $A_0$ acts by an honest normalized family of self-intertwiners $R(a)$, with $R(a)R(b)=R(ab)$, commuting with a representation $\pi$. Where all test-operator traces exist, define \[ D_a(f)=\operatorname{Tr}(\pi(f)R(a)),\qquad e_\chi=|A_0|^{-1}\sum_a\chi(a)^{-1}R(a),\qquad \Theta_\chi(f)=\operatorname{Tr}(\pi(f)e_\chi). \] Finite Fourier inversion gives \[ D_a=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A_0}\chi(a)\Theta_\chi,\qquad \Theta_\chi=|A_0|^{-1}\sum_a\chi(a)^{-1}D_a. \] For $|A_0|=2$, this is $D_e=\Theta_++\Theta_-$, $D_a=\Theta_+-\Theta_-$, the signed rank-one pattern invoked by the aid. Calling the $\Theta_\chi$ packet characters requires the relevant constituent and multiplicity identification. A projective intertwiner action needs cocycle modifications and is not covered by ordinary Fourier inversion. These conditional formulas do not construct general relative endoscopy or identify all Labesse--Langlands transfer normalizations.

Short English statement

The aid separates three source questions. A reverse L-map from $\mathrm{GL}_2$ to the elliptic torus collapses connected parameters to determinant data, while a transpose kernel transfer is a different conditional notion. Honest finite abelian intertwiner actions yield character relations by Fourier inversion, with packet normalization still requiring input.
