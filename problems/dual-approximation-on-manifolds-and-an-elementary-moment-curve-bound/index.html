Math-1595

Dual approximation on manifolds and an elementary moment-curve bound

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $n\ge1$ and $v>0$, let \[ W_v=\left\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:|q\cdot y-p|<\|q\|_\infty^{-v} \text{ for infinitely many }(q,p)\in({\mathbb{Z}}^n\setminus\{0\})\times{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}. \] This is dual approximation by integer linear forms, using the max norm, as defined in companion permanent ID $20003299$. A vector is very well approximable if it belongs to $W_{n+\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$. For a smooth $d$-dimensional nondegenerate submanifold $\mathcal M\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, D. Kleinbock's Question 26 asks for estimates of \[ \dim_H(\mathcal M\cap W_v) \] using dynamics. In the source's chart formulation, nondegeneracy means that positive-order partial derivatives of a local parametrization span ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ at the points under consideration. The exact differentiability/order and exceptional-point conventions should be checked in the source references. The question covers $v>0$; the nontrivial very-well-approximable regime is $v>n$.

The attached aid reports that the expected value \[ d-1+\frac{n+1}{v+1}\qquad(v>n) \] has been established in substantial classes, including the Veronese-curve value $(n+1)/(v+1)$, but not for every nondegenerate manifold. It supplies no complete primary citations or class hypotheses, so no general exact-dimension theorem is asserted here.

Its separate elementary model is: for every compact interval $I\subset{\mathbb{R}}$ with nonempty interior and every $v>0$, \[ \dim_H\{t\in I:(t,t^2,\ldots,t^n)\in W_v\} \le\min\left\{1,\frac{n(n+1)}{v+1}\right\}. \] The same dimension describes the corresponding subset of the moment curve, since its parametrization is bi-Lipschitz on $I$. The reported covering argument considers each exact shell $\|q\|_\infty=Q$: there are $O(Q^{n-1})$ choices of $q$, $O(Q)$ relevant integers $p$, and each resonance sublevel \[ \left\{t\in I:\left|\sum_{j=1}^nq_jt^j-p\right|<Q^{-v}\right\} \] is covered by at most $2n+1$ intervals of diameter $O(Q^{-(v+1)/n})$. The Hausdorff--Cantelli sum is bounded by a constant times $\sum_Q Q^{n-s(v+1)/n}$, yielding the displayed coarse bound. The aid explicitly identifies this as a Remez-type elementary argument, not the requested dynamics proof or a sharp general estimate.

The text beginning "5.3" belongs to the next question's multiplicative-approximation setup. With $\langle x\rangle=\operatorname{dist}(x,{\mathbb{Z}})$, it reports almost-everywhere positivity of \[ \liminf_{q\to\infty}q(\log q)^{2+\varepsilon} \langle q\alpha\rangle\langle q\beta\rangle \] for every $\varepsilon>0$, and attributes the stronger limit $+\infty$ to [184]. These are separate contextual measure claims, not hypotheses or conclusions of Question 26.

Short English statement

Estimate the Hausdorff dimension of dual $v$-approximable points on a nondegenerate manifold, preferably using dynamics. The supplied moment-curve bound is explicit but coarse and elementary; it does not establish the proposed exact formula for all manifolds.
