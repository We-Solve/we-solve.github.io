Average-Case Complexity of Clique on Sparse Graphs

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

An Erdos-Renyi graph $G(n,p)$ has independent edges, each present with probability $p$. A $k$-clique is a $k$-vertex set with every possible joining edge. Average-case complexity is measured over this graph distribution, separately from worst-case clique hardness.

Record scope and qualifications.
The catalogue title is retained as metadata. Named certificates, contracts, bounds or frameworks in that title are not asserted as results of the original source extract. The exact threshold regime, success probability, parameter scaling, and machine/access model are not fully quantified in the supplied record.

Source-record limitation: The cleaned modest variant switches from k-cliques to n^epsilon-cliques while retaining a runtime parameter k, without defining their relation or the distributional success criterion. The relationship between these parameters is absent from the supplied extraction.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For Erdős–Rényi graphs with edge probability near the threshold for containing a $k$-clique, does deciding $k$-Clique require time $n^{\Omega(k)}$? More modestly, is there an $n^{o(k)}$-time algorithm for deciding whether such a graph contains a clique of size $n^\varepsilon$? [S2]

Short English statement

Determine the average-case cost of finding or deciding cliques near their random-graph threshold.
