Effective Oppenheim heights and an exact four-square transfer family

Definitions and mathematical statement

The aid recovers Question 35 as an effective Oppenheim problem: let $Q$ be a nondegenerate indefinite quadratic form on ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, $d\geq3$, irrational in the sense of not being a real scalar multiple of a rational form. For every $\varepsilon>0$, seek an effective bound $T=T_Q(\varepsilon)$ and $x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ with \[ 0<|Q(x)|<\varepsilon,\qquad\|x\|<T. \] The hypotheses come from preceding source context as reported by the aid, not the locally extracted question alone. The coefficient/Diophantine dependence of $T$ is part of the request; writing $T(\varepsilon)$ does not make it uniform in $Q$. The source's purported easier version omits $0<|Q(x)|$ and any $x\ne0$ condition, so it is literally satisfied by $x=0$; the intended nontrivial version requires a repair. Its reference to upcoming Margulis--Götze work for $d\geq5$ is historical.

The aid reports modern effective theorems answering the broad strict question, but gives no full references, explicit general bound or coefficient hypotheses. Its explicit special case is the eight-variable form, for irrational $\alpha>0$, \[ Q_\alpha(u,v)=\sum_{i=1}^4u_i^2-\alpha\sum_{i=1}^4v_i^2, \qquad u,v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^4. \] Define \[ N_\alpha(\varepsilon)=\min\{n\geq1:\|n\alpha\|<\varepsilon\}, \qquad H_\alpha(\varepsilon)=\min\{\|(u,v)\|_\infty: 0<|Q_\alpha(u,v)|<\varepsilon\}, \] where $\|n\alpha\|$ is nearest-integer distance. For $0<\varepsilon<\min\{\alpha,1/2\}$, the reported four-square transfer theorem is \[ \frac12\sqrt{N_\alpha(\varepsilon)} \leq H_\alpha(\varepsilon) \leq\sqrt{(\alpha+1)N_\alpha(\varepsilon)}. \] Irrationality ensures an approximation from positive integer square sums is nonzero, and the small-$\varepsilon$ conditions exclude a spurious zero block. For badly approximable $\alpha$, meaning $\inf_{n\geq1}n\|n\alpha\|>0$, the aid obtains \[ H_\alpha(\varepsilon)=\Theta_\alpha(\varepsilon^{-1/2}) \quad(\varepsilon\to0). \] This is a coefficient-dependent asymptotic for the stated family, not a sharp bound for all forms. A separate reported irrational perturbation of $x^2+y^2-3z^2$ shows why coefficient-free uniform bounds fail; the perturbation formula is not supplied.

The extraction then begins separate Section 5.6. For a nondegenerate indefinite rational binary form $Q(x_1,x_2)=ax_1^2+bx_1x_2+cx_2^2$ that represents no nonzero rational zero, it defines \[ m(Q,x)=\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2}|Q(x+z)|,\qquad m(Q)=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2}m(Q,x), \] and, if the supremum is isolated, \[ m_2(Q)=\sup\{m(Q,x):m(Q,x)<m(Q)\}. \] It motivates $m(Q)$ via norm forms of real quadratic fields in an integral basis and states an equivalence with a Euclidean algorithm when $m(Q)<1$. The precise intended assertion concerns the norm-Euclidean condition, not every possible Euclidean function; normalization and any attainment/boundary qualifications need its actual reference. It then introduces a conjecture communicated by A. Pollington but does not give it. This binary inhomogeneous material and the page header are not clauses of the effective higher-dimensional Question 35.

Short English statement

The strict small-value question needs coefficient-dependent effective bounds and excludes the zero vector automatically. The aid gives a precise eight-variable height estimate and badly-approximable asymptotic, while general effective references are missing. The binary inhomogeneous minima and norm-Euclidean discussion belong to the next section.
