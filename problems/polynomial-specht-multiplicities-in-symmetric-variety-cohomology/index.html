Polynomial Specht Multiplicities in Symmetric-Variety Cohomology

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S_n$ denote the symmetric group on $n$ letters. For a partition $\mu\vdash n$, let $S^{\mu}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ denote the corresponding rational Specht module. For a finite-dimensional rational $S_n$-representation $W$, define its Specht multiplicity by \[ [W:S^{\mu}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}] :=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[S_n]}(S^{\mu}_{{\mathbb{Q}}},W). \] The length $\ell(\lambda)$ of a partition $\lambda$ is its number of positive parts.

The supplied record describes an ideal $I$ in an inverse-limit ring $\Lambda$ of symmetric polynomials, a sequence of associated symmetric varieties $V_n$, and a partition $\{\lambda\}_n$ indexing an $S_n$-representation for sufficiently large $n$. It does not specify the coefficient ring, the inverse-limit convention and transition maps, the construction or ambient field of $V_n$, or the rule defining $\{\lambda\}_n$. These are unresolved parts of the formulation. In particular, no partition-padding convention or real-versus-complex interpretation of the varieties is imposed here. The cohomology theory and the hypotheses ensuring finite-dimensional rational cohomology with the required $S_n$-action also need confirmation.

Subject to those missing definitions and hypotheses, the intended conjecture is the following: for every ideal $I$ allowed by the intended construction, every fixed partition $\lambda$, and every fixed integer $p\geq 0$, there exist an integer $N$ and a polynomial $P_{I,\lambda,p}(t)$ such that, for every integer $n\geq N$, the partition $\{\lambda\}_n$ is defined and \[ m_{I,\lambda,p}(n) :=\left[H^p(V_n;{\mathbb{Q}}):S^{\{\lambda\}_n}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\right] =P_{I,\lambda,p}(n). \] The threshold and polynomial may depend on $I$, $\lambda$, and $p$. This is a conditional statement of the supplied mathematical target; the record does not determine a complete, unambiguous class of instances.

The supplied excerpt also reports a special case, described as joint work with D. Perrucci. It concerns a principal ideal $I=(f)$, where $f$ is a finite linear combination of elementary symmetric functions. The extracted sum suggests \[ f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_i\sigma_i\in\Lambda, \] where $\sigma_i$ denotes the elementary symmetric function of degree $i$, with the usual convention $\sigma_0=1$. However, the accompanying extracted range is literally $0\leq i\geq d$; the finite range $0\leq i\leq d$ is therefore a plausible reading requiring source confirmation. The coefficient domain and any further hypotheses on $f$ are not supplied. In the intended setting, the excerpt reports that for every fixed partition $\lambda$, for all sufficiently large $n$, \[ m_{(f),\lambda,0}(n)= \begin{cases} 0,&\ell(\lambda)>1,\\ c_{f,\lambda},&\ell(\lambda)=1, \end{cases} \qquad c_{f,\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}. \] It presents this as verifying the conjecture for these principal ideals when $p=0$. This is a supplied special-case claim, not an independently verified theorem here. If the empty partition is allowed, the displayed cases do not specify its treatment.

Short English statement

For each fixed ideal, partition, and cohomological degree, the question asks whether the corresponding Specht multiplicity eventually equals a polynomial in the number of variables. The supplied record reports a special case in degree zero, but essential definitions of the varieties and partition indexing remain unspecified. This statement-only draft requires human mathematical review.
