Distinguishing intrinsic and boundary order-three conformal operators

Definitions and mathematical statement

The paired aid identifies this canonical record as a splice rather than a separate problem: its opening sentence finishes Problem 11, while the paragraph about order-three operators introduces Problem 12. The absent antecedent of "this would provide a unique specification of $Q$" is not reconstructed. The text mentions Rod Gover, Alice Chang and Jie Qing and an order-three $P_3$ on a three-manifold occurring as a boundary or embedded hypersurface, with an associated $Q_3$. It does not specify the domain, locality or normalization of the operator.

The aid supplies a locality diagnostic. On a manifold of dimension at least two, a scalar formally self-adjoint differential operator of odd order cannot be elliptic. In the usual scalar Hermitian symbol convention, its principal symbol is real and odd in the cotangent variable; connectedness of the unit cotangent sphere forces a zero. Thus an intrinsic scalar self-adjoint elliptic order-three operator cannot be a finite-order local differential operator. A pseudodifferential realization is a possible nonlocal alternative, not an identification forced by the source wording alone.

The aid distinguishes the local Chang--Qing boundary $P_3$: its input is an ambient function near a hypersurface $\Sigma^3\subset X^4$, and it depends on third normal jets. It does not factor through the boundary trace. Writing $B_3$ for this type of operator to distinguish its domain, the diagnostic is that no intrinsic trace operator $\overline P_3$ satisfies \[ B_3u=\overline P_3(u|_\Sigma)\qquad\text{for every ambient }u. \] The reported third-normal-jet dependence permits functions with zero trace but nonzero output. This boundary operator is not a counterexample to the intrinsic scalar lemma; its self-adjoint boundary theory is not automatically the same as an $L^2(\Sigma)$ operator on arbitrary boundary traces.

For a separately specified intrinsic pair on a closed three-manifold, suppose \[ e^{3\omega}P_{3,\widehat g}f=P_{3,g}f,\qquad e^{3\omega}Q_{3,\widehat g}=Q_{3,g}+P_{3,g}\omega, \qquad\widehat g=e^{2\omega}g, \] with $P_{3,g}$ formally self-adjoint and $P_{3,g}1=0$. The aid reports that the affine law is consistent under composition of conformal changes and that \[ \int_\Sigma Q_{3,\widehat g}\,dv_{\widehat g} =\int_\Sigma Q_{3,g}\,dv_g. \] For a fixed $P_3$, two $Q_3$ assignments obeying this same law differ exactly by a scalar conformal invariant of weight $-3$, meaning $I_{\widehat g}=e^{-3\omega}I_g$. Conversely adding any such invariant preserves the law. This is uniqueness modulo that ambiguity, not an absolute uniqueness statement or an existence theorem for the unspecified source pair.
