Math-6722

Ordinary and Symbolic Powers of Point-Set Ideals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source poses two questions concerning ordinary and symbolic powers. It does not specify the ground field, the admissible ambient rings for the second question, or the symbolic-power convention for nonradical ideals. The formulations below therefore retain these qualifications explicitly.

For a proper ideal $I$ of a commutative Noetherian ring $R$, its ordinary power $I^m$ is the ideal generated by products of $m$ elements of $I$. Under the associated-prime convention, set \[ I^{(m)}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Ass}_R(R/I)}\left(I^mR_{\mathfrak p}\cap R\right), \qquad \operatorname{bight}(I)=\max_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Ass}_R(R/I)}\operatorname{ht}_R(\mathfrak p), \quad m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}, \] where contraction to $R$ is understood in the displayed intersections. A minimal-prime convention instead uses $\operatorname{Min}_R(R/I)$; these choices agree for radical ideals in this setting but need not agree for general ideals. The source does not resolve which convention or ideal class is intended in its second question.

For the first question, the usual finite reduced point-set interpretation uses an unspecified field $k$, the bihomogeneous coordinate ring \[ S=k[x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1],\qquad \deg(x_i)=(1,0),\quad \deg(y_i)=(0,1), \] and the bihomogeneous vanishing ideal $I(X)\subseteq S$ of a finite reduced set $X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^1_k\times{\mathbb{P}}^1_k$. Here arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay means that $S/I(X)$ is Cohen--Macaulay; equivalently, its depth at the homogeneous maximal ideal equals its Krull dimension. This interpretation, including finiteness and reducedness, requires confirmation from the original report: the supplied statement says only "Cohen--Macaulay point sets."

Geometric characterization question. Under this interpretation, characterize exactly those arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay point sets $X$ for which \[ I(X)^3=I(X)^{(3)}. \] The source also describes this as a question about equivalent line unions in ${\mathbb{P}}^3$. For a set of $k$-rational points, one possible precise correspondence is obtained by giving all four variables of $S$ degree one: the ideal of a point $(a,b)$ is generated by one linear form in the $x$-variables and one in the $y$-variables, and hence defines a line in $\operatorname{Proj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}^3_k$. The same intersection of point ideals then defines the corresponding union of lines. The source does not specify this correspondence, rationality assumptions, or the intended class of line unions; its equivalence must be understood subject to that unresolved qualification.

Delayed failure question. Does the intended class of ideals contain, for every $t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}$, an ideal $I_t\subseteq R_t$, with $h_t=\operatorname{bight}(I_t)$, such that \[ \left(\forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\ m\leq h_t+t-1 \Longrightarrow I_t^m=I_t^{(m)}\right) \qquad\text{and}\qquad I_t^{h_t+t}\ne I_t^{(h_t+t)}? \] The admissible rings $R_t$, whether they may vary with $t$, and any requirements that the ideals be radical, homogeneous, or geometrically defined are not supplied. This is consequently a quantified question schema pending specification of that class, rather than a fully determined existence assertion.

Short English statement

Characterize the Cohen--Macaulay point sets in ${\mathbb{P}}^1\times{\mathbb{P}}^1$ whose third ordinary and symbolic ideal powers coincide. Also ask whether, for every positive integer $t$, an ideal can have equality through exponent $\operatorname{bight}(I)+t-1$ and first failure at the next exponent. The field, point-set interpretation, line-union correspondence, and admissible ideal class require clarification.
