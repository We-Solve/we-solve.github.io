Branko Grunbaum in Many Dimensions — Question 5

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An affine hyperplane in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ has the form \[ H_i=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^4:\langle a_i,x\rangle=b_i\},\qquad a_i\in{\mathbb{R}}^4\setminus\{0\},\quad b_i\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] For four such hyperplanes and a sign vector $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^4$, define the corresponding open region by \[ C_\sigma=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^4:\sigma_i(\langle a_i,x\rangle-b_i)>0\text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,4\}. \] These regions are pairwise disjoint and cover ${\mathbb{R}}^4\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^4H_i$.

For the discrete formulation below, use the convention that a finite set is in general position if every subset of at most five points is affinely independent. This convention is explicit here because the supplied record does not define general position. Interpret separation as requiring every point to lie outside the four hyperplanes.

Under these conventions, the discrete question is: for every integer $n\geq1$ and every set $P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^4$ of $16n$ points in general position, do there exist $a_1,\ldots,a_4\in{\mathbb{R}}^4\setminus\{0\}$ and $b_1,\ldots,b_4\in{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ P\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^4H_i=\varnothing, \qquad \lvert P\cap C_\sigma\rvert=n \quad\text{for every }\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^4? \] Thus the sixteen subsets must arise from the sixteen sign regions of the same four hyperplanes.

The source also asks whether every "object" in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ can be partitioned into sixteen "equal parts" by four hyperplanes. The supplied record specifies neither the class of objects nor the meaning of equality. It also does not prescribe how portions lying on a cutting hyperplane are handled. Consequently, this broader question cannot be given a unique precise mathematical statement from the supplied evidence; no measure, regularity assumption, or notion of congruence is imposed here.

Short English statement

The discrete question asks whether four hyperplanes can place any $16n$ points in general position in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ into sixteen sign regions containing exactly $n$ points each. The draft explicitly uses affine general position and requires the cutting hyperplanes to avoid the points. The broader question about dividing every object into equal parts remains underspecified.
