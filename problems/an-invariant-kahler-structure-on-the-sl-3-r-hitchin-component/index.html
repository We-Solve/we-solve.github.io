Math-1752

An invariant Kähler structure on the SL(3,R) Hitchin component

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a closed oriented surface of genus $g\geq2$, let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Map}(S)$, and let $\operatorname{Hit}_3(S)$ be the Hitchin component of the $SL(3,{\mathbb{R}})$ character variety, containing the principal Fuchsian representations. Question 2.1 of John Loftin asks whether it admits a $\Gamma$-invariant Kähler structure: an integrable complex structure $J$ and a closed real $(1,1)$-form $\Omega$ with $\Omega(v,Jv)>0$ for every nonzero tangent vector, both preserved by $\Gamma$.

Richard Wentworth's Comment 2.2 cites Theorem 1.0.2 of [Lab06] for the parametrization by cubic differentials over Teichmüller space, with fiber $H^0(X,K_X^3)$, and notes an invariant complex structure. Loftin's Comment 2.3 points to Weil--Petersson geometry transverse to the fibers and Kähler geometry on the fibers as evidence. Fiberwise and transverse positivity alone is motivation, not a proof of a globally closed positive form.

The supplied assessment credits Kim--Zhang with an affirmative solution for every such $S$: a mapping-class-invariant positive-definite Kähler structure whose Fuchsian/Teichmüller zero locus carries the Weil--Petersson metric and is totally geodesic. The complex structure and identifications used in that construction must be specified; the mere smooth cubic-bundle parametrization does not identify every possible complex structure. The aid distinguishes this positive form from a later compatible but indefinite Goldman pseudo-Kähler form, which is not substituted for a positive metric.

Its additional observation is on the negative holomorphic bundle realization used to induce the positive form. Let $\pi:E\to\mathcal T(S)$ be that realization with its invariant Hermitian metric, let $\rho(v)=\|v\|_{L^2}^2$, and use the normalized construction \[ \Omega_E=\pi^*\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}+i\partial\bar\partial\rho. \] The requisite negativity and equivariant identification are hypotheses of this realization, not assertions that the natural cubic bundle and its dual have identical holomorphic structures. With this form and circle generator $X$ of period $2\pi$, fiber rotation $v\mapsto e^{i\theta}v$ is a holomorphic isometric $S^1$ action commuting with $\Gamma$. Its moment map in the convention \[ \iota_X\Omega_E=-d\mu \] is $\mu=\rho$, up to an additive constant. The factor is tied to the displayed $i\partial\bar\partial$ convention. The zero section is exactly the fixed minimum set $\mu^{-1}(0)$ and, being a fixed set of isometries, is totally geodesic. Transporting the structure through the stated realization gives the corresponding observation on the Hitchin component. The record does not provide the full negative-bundle identification or normalization from the original construction, so those require checking before treating this formula as canonical.

Short English statement

John Loftin asks for a positive mapping-class-invariant Kähler structure on the $SL(3,{\mathbb{R}})$ Hitchin component. The supplied literature assessment credits Kim--Zhang with such a structure, extending Weil--Petersson geometry on the Fuchsian locus. In its specified negative-bundle model, fiber rotation has squared norm as moment map; the required bundle identification and normalization remain to be checked.
