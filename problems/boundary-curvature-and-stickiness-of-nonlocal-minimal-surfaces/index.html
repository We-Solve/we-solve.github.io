Boundary Curvature and Stickiness of Nonlocal Minimal Surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks about boundary behavior of nonlocal minimal surfaces, with particular emphasis in its reconstructed statement on surfaces beyond planar graphs. It does not specify a kernel, dimension, admissible geometry, or notion of boundary equation. The following standard fractional-perimeter framework provides terminology only; its identification with the intended setting requires review.

Let $n\geq 1$, $s\in(0,1)$, and let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be open. For disjoint measurable sets $A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, write \[ L_s(A,B)=\int_A\int_B\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] For a measurable set $E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, put $E^c={\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus E$ and define its relative fractional perimeter by \[ P_s(E;\Omega)=L_s(E\cap\Omega,E^c)+L_s(E\setminus\Omega,E^c\cap\Omega). \] In the finite-perimeter version of this framework, $E$ is a minimizer with prescribed exterior data $E_0$ if $P_s(E;\Omega)<\infty$, $E\setminus\Omega=E_0\setminus\Omega$ up to null sets, and \[ P_s(E;\Omega)\leq P_s(F;\Omega) \] for every measurable $F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ having the same exterior data. These are explicit conventions for this explanatory framework, not hypotheses recovered from the source.

At a point $x$ of a suitably chosen representative of $\partial E$, define the fractional mean curvature, when the principal value exists, by \[ H_s[E](x)=\operatorname{PV}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n} \frac{\mathbf{1}_{E^c}(y)-\mathbf{1}_E(y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y =\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus B_\varepsilon(x)} \frac{\mathbf{1}_{E^c}(y)-\mathbf{1}_E(y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y. \] The corresponding classical nonlocal mean-curvature equation is $H_s[E](x)=0$.

Requested problem, with unresolved qualifications. Determine under which hypotheses the nonlocal mean-curvature equation for a nonlocal minimal surface continues to hold up to the boundary of its minimizing domain, including the setting described in the supplied reconstruction as beyond planar graphs. In the framework above, potential boundary contact points lie in $\partial E\cap\partial\Omega$; the source does not specify which such points are intended, whether the equation means a pointwise principal-value identity there, an interior limiting identity, or a weak or viscosity formulation, or what boundary regularity is assumed.

Also characterize how generally boundary stickiness occurs. Here "stickiness" is only a descriptive term for attachment of a minimizing surface to the domain boundary or failure to attain prescribed exterior data continuously; the source supplies no precise predicate. It also gives no topology, measure, probability law, or parameter family defining "typical." Thus a precise quantified assertion about typicality, and a universal boundary identity, cannot be extracted without additional hypotheses. The source presents research questions rather than a specified theorem to prove.

Short English statement

Determine when nonlocal minimal surfaces satisfy their mean-curvature equation up to the domain boundary, especially beyond planar graphs, and characterize the prevalence of boundary stickiness. The supplied source leaves the admissible surfaces, boundary formulation, definition of stickiness, and meaning of typicality unspecified. The fractional-perimeter definitions above provide a conditional framework for review.
