Additive indecomposability of finite modifications of the primes

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 013; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that a set that agrees with the primes apart from finitely many elements cannot be a sumset of two nonnegative-integer sets each having at least two members.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of primes, and let $A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ with $|A|,|B|\ge2$. For their ordinary sumset $A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}$, the manuscript claims \[ |(A+B)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathcal P|=\infty. \] Consequently even a finite modification of the prime set cannot be expressed as a sum of two nontrivial sets of nonnegative integers; the symmetric difference condition allows finitely many additions or deletions of primes.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 013; 2026-09-24), finite summands are eliminated by shifted-prime sieving. For two infinite summands, complementary residue supports produce character restrictions at separated prime scales; a transfer comparison contracts their possible coverage until it conflicts with coverage of all sufficiently large primes.
