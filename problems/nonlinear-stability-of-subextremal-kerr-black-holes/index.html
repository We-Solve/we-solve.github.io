Definitions and conventions.
The vacuum Einstein equations are $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$ for a Lorentzian spacetime metric $g$. Kerr solutions are the stationary rotating black-hole family with mass $M>0$ and rotation parameter $a$; subextremality means $|a|<M$. Initial data consist of a Riemannian metric and second fundamental form satisfying the vacuum constraint equations. Nonlinear stability concerns the maximal evolution of perturbed data, including its exterior geometry and late-time convergence, with final Kerr parameters allowed to differ from the initial parameters.

Stationary parameter modes must be removed before decay.
Let $g(M,a)$ be the Kerr metric in a smooth common coordinate region, with $|a|<M$. Since $\operatorname{Ric}(g(M,a))=0$, differentiating the identity gives \[ D\operatorname{Ric}_{g}(\partial_Mg)=0,\qquad D\operatorname{Ric}_{g}(\partial_ag)=0. \] These are solutions of the linearized vacuum equation and are stationary in stationary coordinates. They cannot decay to zero in any norm that detects the changed parameters. Already at $a=0$, the asymptotic component $g_{tt}=-1+2M/r$ has $\partial_Mg_{tt}=2/r$, which is time independent and nonzero.
Gauge modes add another kernel.
For a differentiable family of diffeomorphisms $\Phi_s$ with generator $X$, covariance gives $\operatorname{Ric}(\Phi_s^*g)=0$. Differentiation yields $D\operatorname{Ric}_g(\mathcal L_Xg)=0$. Thus a naive coercive estimate on every metric perturbation is obstructed both by genuine parameter changes and by pure coordinate changes. The two should not be identified: the mass change has an asymptotic charge interpretation, whereas the allowed gauge changes depend on boundary conventions.
What a nonlinear proof still needs.
One must choose gauge and final parameters and estimate the remaining radiative part; the source explicitly permits convergence to a nearby Kerr member. The calculation does not construct those modulation parameters dynamically or prove global existence, exterior control or decay for the full subextremal range. The first gap is a nonlinear estimate after accounting for these kernels, in a precisely specified perturbation and convergence topology. No small-rotation result is substituted for all $|a|<M$.
