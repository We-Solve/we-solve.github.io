Math-4866

Local-Global Density of Galois Cover Specializations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T$ be transcendental over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and let $E/{\mathbb{Q}}(T)$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$. The extension is ${\mathbb{Q}}$-regular if $E\cap\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}={\mathbb{Q}}$, inside a common algebraic closure. Its genus is the genus of the smooth projective geometrically integral curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with function field $E$. Assume this genus is $g\geq 2$. For a finite set $A$, write $|A|$ for its cardinality.

The supplied record asks whether, for every such extension, \[ \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{\left|\operatorname{Sp}(E)\cap S(G,x)\right|} {\left|\operatorname{Sp}(E)^{\mathrm{loc}}\cap S(G,x)\right|} =0. \] Here $\operatorname{Sp}(E)$, $\operatorname{Sp}(E)^{\mathrm{loc}}$, and $S(G,x)$ are retained exactly as source notation. The supplied record does not define these sets. In particular, it does not specify the specialization parameters and exclusions, whether specializations must have full Galois group $G$, the equivalence relation used to count extensions, the local conditions and places involved, or the ordering invariant bounded by $x$. These choices must be recovered from the cited source before the displayed question becomes a fully specified mathematical statement.

The quotient additionally requires finite numerator and denominator and a positive denominator for the values of $x$ under consideration. The record provides neither these guarantees nor a convention for zero denominators. No convention is imposed here.

The supplied dated assessment describes a conditional result for abelian groups with sufficiently many branch points and labels the general genus-at-least-two question as remaining open. It does not supply the conditional hypotheses or the required branch-point bound; consequently, that assessment is recorded as a source qualification rather than formulated as a theorem.

Short English statement

For every ${\mathbb{Q}}$-regular Galois extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}(T)$ of genus at least two, the source asks whether the displayed ratio of global specialization counts to locally admissible counts tends to zero. The counting sets, local conditions, ordering, and denominator conventions are unspecified in the supplied record, so a precise interpretation still requires source review.
