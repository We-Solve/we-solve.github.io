Math-3912

Monochromatic Empty Triangles

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each integer $n\geq 1$, let $\mathcal C_n$ be the collection of pairs $(R,B)$ of disjoint finite subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ such that $|R|=|B|=n$ and no three points of $P=R\cup B$ are collinear. Points of $R$ are red and points of $B$ are blue; here general position is interpreted as the stated noncollinearity condition.

A triangle determined by $P$ is the convex hull $\operatorname{conv}\{x,y,z\}$ of an unordered three-element subset $\{x,y,z\}\subseteq P$. It is monochromatic if all three vertices belong to $R$, or all three belong to $B$. It is empty if \[ \operatorname{int}\left(\operatorname{conv}\{x,y,z\}\right)\cap P=\varnothing. \] Thus emptiness excludes interior points of either color. The interior is the ordinary planar interior; the general-position assumption also excludes additional points on the edges.

Let $T(R,B)$ denote the number of monochromatic empty triangles, counting each unordered vertex triple once, and define \[ f(n)=\min_{(R,B)\in\mathcal C_n}T(R,B). \] Equivalently, $f(n)$ is the largest nonnegative integer such that every configuration in $\mathcal C_n$ contains at least $f(n)$ monochromatic empty triangles.

Problem. Prove or disprove that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^2}=0. \] Explicitly, the proposed assertion is \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\geq1\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\quad n\geq N\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists(R,B)\in\mathcal C_n:\ T(R,B)<\varepsilon n^2. \]

Short English statement

Among all general-position configurations with exactly $n$ red and $n$ blue points, let $f(n)$ be the smallest number of monochromatic triangles whose interiors contain no point of either color. Determine whether $f(n)/n^2$ tends to zero. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft preserves that label without independently verifying its current status.
