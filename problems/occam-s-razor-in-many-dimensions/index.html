Research attempt

The source leaves the sensing model, error, signal class and entropy constraints unspecified. This attempt tests a precision-free measurement-count lower bound. It first proves a finite-alphabet lower bound allowing adaptive sensing and algorithmic randomness, then gives a single real measurement that defeats the same count when precision is unrestricted. This identifies a necessary modeling distinction, not a new universal sensing theorem.

Let $\mathcal X$ be $M\ge1$ signals in a metric space, with pairwise distances strictly greater than $2\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon\ge0$. An algorithm makes at most $m\ge0$ adaptive queries. Each answer has at most $B\ge2$ possible symbols; subsequent queries may depend on previous answers and private random bits independent of the signal. There is no external measurement noise in this model. Reconstruction succeeds when its distance from the input is at most $\varepsilon$.

Proposition: For a uniformly random input in $\mathcal X$, every such algorithm has success probability at most $B^m/M$. Consequently success at least $1-\delta$, for $0\le\delta<1$, requires \[ m\log_2B\ge\log_2 M+\log_2(1-\delta). \tag{3} \]
Proof: Fix all internal randomness. Pad runs ending early with dummy answers, so every transcript has length $m$. There are at most $B^m$ transcripts. Once a transcript is fixed its reconstruction is fixed. That reconstruction cannot be within $\varepsilon$ of two distinct signals, since the triangle inequality would put their distance at most $2\varepsilon$. Hence at most $B^m$ input signals can be reconstructed successfully for this fixed seed. Uniform input yields success at most $B^m/M$. Averaging over the independent random seed preserves the bound, including any random reconstruction choices. Taking logarithms of $1-\delta\le B^m/M$ proves $3$. ∎

For the class of binary vectors with exactly $k$ ones among $n$ coordinates, $1\le k\le n$, exact recovery has $M=\binom nk$. If $B=2^b$, $b\ge1$, zero-error recovery therefore requires $mb\ge\log_2\binom nk$. Also \[ \binom nk=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n-j}{k-j}\ge(n/k)^k, \] because each factor is at least $n/k$. Thus $mb\ge k\log_2(n/k)$, with the expected trivial bound when $k=n$; the $k=0$ singleton is trivial as well. This is a standard counting argument, not a claimed new sensing lower bound.

Why the unqualified extension fails.
On all binary vectors, the single exact real linear observation \[ y=\sum_{i=1}^n2^{i-1}x_i \] is injective. If two binary strings differ, let $j$ be their largest differing index. The difference contributed by coordinate $j$ has absolute value $2^{j-1}$, whereas all earlier coordinates together have absolute value at most $2^{j-1}-1$, so the observations differ. Reading binary digits recovers the signal. This observation carries $n$ bits and uses exponentially large dynamic range; it has no uniform $b$-bit alphabet. Consequently removing the answer-capacity assumption from $3$ is invalid, even though the map is linear and all signals are discrete and sparse when restricted to a fixed weight.
Entropy maximization does not specify a sensor.
For any distribution $p$ on an $M$-point set, concavity of $\log$ gives \[ H_2(p)=\sum_{p_i>0}p_i\log_2(1/p_i) \le\log_2\left(\sum_{p_i>0}p_i/p_i\right) \le\log_2M. \] Equality for $M>1$ requires full support and equal probabilities, so the uniform law attains the maximum. In $3$, this uniform law supplies a hard average case through counting. Maximizing entropy alone does not constrain the number of symbols an exact real-valued sensor can transmit and cannot repair the failed precision-free conclusion.
