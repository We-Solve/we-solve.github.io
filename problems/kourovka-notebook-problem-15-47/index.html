Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.47

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For positive integers $n,k$, define $\mathcal E(n,k)$ to mean that there exist a finite set $\Omega$ of cardinality $n$, permutation groups $M<G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$, and a partition $\mathcal P$ with $k$ nonempty parts of \[ \Omega^{(2)}=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega\times\Omega:\alpha\ne\beta\} \] satisfying the following conditions. The group $M$ is transitive on $\Omega$. Under the diagonal action $g\cdot(\alpha,\beta)=(g\alpha,g\beta)$, the group $G$ permutes the parts of $\mathcal P$ transitively, and every element of $M$ fixes every part setwise. Finally, the image of the induced homomorphism \[ \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal P),\qquad \rho(g)(P)=gP, \] is a noncyclic group. Equivalently, the parts describe a decomposition of the complete directed graph on $\Omega$, without loops, into edge-disjoint isomorphic directed graphs, with the stated group actions.

For a prime $r$ and a positive integer $n$, define the $r$-part of $n$ by \[ n_r=r^{v_r(n)},\qquad v_r(n)=\max\{a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:r^a\mid n\}. \] Write $C(n,k)$ for the condition \[ n_r\equiv1\pmod{k}\qquad\text{for every prime }r. \] In particular, $n_r=1$ when $r\nmid n$. The supplied source reports that, under the same hypotheses on $M,G,\Omega,\mathcal P$, cyclicity of $\rho(G)$ implies $C(n,k)$, and attributes this result to the cited 2003 paper.

The problem asks for examples with noncyclic induced group for values $n,k$ failing $C(n,k)$. The existential reading is \[ \exists n,k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\quad \left(\mathcal E(n,k)\ \text{and}\ \exists\text{ a prime }r:\ n_r\not\equiv1\pmod{k}\right). \] Unresolved quantifier qualification. The source asks for examples "for any $n,k$" not satisfying the congruence condition. It does not explicitly distinguish the existential reading above from a request for examples for every prescribed pair of parameters violating the condition. Under the latter reading, the intended range of admissible pairs also requires clarification; the source supplies no such range. Both readings are retained for review.

Short English statement

Seek a transitive permutation group $M$ properly contained in $G$, together with a partition of all ordered pairs of distinct points that $M$ fixes part by part and $G$ permutes transitively. Can the induced group on the parts be noncyclic while some prime-power part $n_r$ of the number of points fails $n_r\equiv1\pmod{k}$, where $k$ is the number of parts? The source's phrase "for any $n,k$" leaves unresolved whether one example or a family covering prescribed parameters is requested.
