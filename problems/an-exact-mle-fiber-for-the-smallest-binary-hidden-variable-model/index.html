Research attempt

Literature and scope

Question 59 is the broad agenda of using algebra for hidden-model MLEs. I tested whether solving interior algebraic score equations and checking positive probabilities suffices to identify maxima. A binary latent-spin model gives an exact saddle that survives both tests, along with a nonisolated maximum set.
Proposition: Let $H$ be a uniform spin $\pm1$ and $X=HU$, $Y=HV$, where independent noise spins have means $a,b\in(-1,1)$. For data of size $N>0$ with empirical product mean $c\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}$, the interior stationary set is \[ \{(a,b):ab=c\}\ \cup\ \{(0,0)\}. \] The first part consists of global maxima, and the origin is a saddle with strictly positive observable probabilities.
Proof: Summing over $H$ gives $p(x,y)=(1+abxy)/4$ for $x,y\in\{\pm1\}$. With $t=ab$, the log likelihood up to constants is \[ \ell(t)=\frac N2\left((1+c)\log(1+t)+(1-c)\log(1-t)\right), \qquad \ell'(t)=\frac{N(c-t)}{1-t^2}. \] Its derivative changes from positive to negative at $t=c$, so that is its unique observable maximum. The value $c$ is attainable in the parameter domain, for example using $a=\sqrt{|c|}$ and $b=\operatorname{sign}(c)\sqrt{|c|}$.

The parameter scores are $b\ell'(ab)$ and $a\ell'(ab)$. Since $|ab|<1$, denominators never vanish. Either $ab=c$, or both scores force $a=b=0$, proving completeness of the stationary set without adding denominator roots. At the origin the Hessian is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Nc\\Nc&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose eigenvalues are $\pm N|c|$. Along the corresponding directions the leading nonzero likelihood changes have opposite signs, so the origin is a saddle. Its four probabilities are all $1/4$. Every point with $ab=c$ has the maximum observable probability law and is a global parameter maximum along a continuum. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At $N=100,c=1/2$, the Hessian eigenvalues are $-50,50$. If $c=0$, the origin instead lies on the maximum set $ab=0$, so the stated exclusion of zero is essential. This elementary singular-parameter example shows precisely why positivity and score equations are insufficient for classifying MLEs. It does not provide algorithms for arbitrary hidden graphs or a complete boundary analysis. No novelty is claimed.
