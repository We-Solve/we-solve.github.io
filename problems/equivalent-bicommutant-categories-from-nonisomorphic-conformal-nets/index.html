Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A conformal net assigns local von Neumann algebras to intervals, with locality and conformal covariance. An associated bicommutant category is the tensor category constructed from the net by the source's operator-algebraic analogue of taking a double commutant. Tensor equivalence is equivalence respecting products and unit objects. Nonisomorphic nets can differ as local theories even when a derived category fails to distinguish them.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Do there exist large families of pairwise nonisomorphic conformal nets whose associated bicommutant categories are equivalent as tensor categories?
Source scope and qualification.
The size and parameterization of large families and the precise associated category must be the report's definitions. The question concerns equivalence of that category, not merely equality of numerical invariants or representation-ring dimensions.

Why matching fusion rules is insufficient.
A candidate construction of many nets might first compare only fusion rings. Test that shortcut on categories of finite-dimensional $\mathbb Z/2$-graded vector spaces. On homogeneous degrees $a,b,c\in\{0,1\}$, twist the usual associator by $\omega(a,b,c)=(-1)^{abc}$. The pentagon is the cocycle identity \[ \omega(b,c,d)\omega(a,b+c,d)\omega(a,b,c) =\omega(a+b,c,d)\omega(a,b,c+d), \] with addition modulo two. Both exponents expand to $abc+abd+acd+bcd$, so it holds. Unit constraints hold because any zero argument gives one.
A genuine tensor distinction.
This category has the same two simple objects and multiplication as the untwisted one. But a normalized scalar change of tensor identifications by $\eta(a,b)$ has coboundary at $(1,1,1)$ equal to $\eta(1,1)\eta(1,0)/(\eta(0,1)\eta(1,1))=1$. It cannot remove $\omega(1,1,1)=-1$. A tensor equivalence must fix the sole nontrivial simple object, so this rules out such an equivalence despite identical fusion rules.
