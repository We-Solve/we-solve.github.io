Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 3.29

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a bounded full-dimensional body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ with uniform unit density, its Newtonian potential, up to an irrelevant nonzero normalization factor, is \[ U_K(x)=\int_K\|x-y\|^{2-n}\,dy\quad(n\geq3). \] In dimension $2$ the corresponding potential uses the logarithmic kernel, for example $U_K(x)=-\int_K\log\|x-y\|\,dy$. A quadratic interior potential means that there is one polynomial $Q\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ of degree at most $2$ with $U_K(x)=Q(x)$ throughout the interior of $K$ (with boundary equality interpreted according to the body convention). It is not merely a different quadratic formula near each point.

The source states that ellipsoids have this property and that Nikliborc and Dive independently proved the converse in dimensions $2$ and $3$. H. S. Shapiro asks for the converse in every integer dimension $n>3$: \[ U_K\text{ is quadratic inside }K \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\text{ is an ellipsoid}. \] An ellipsoid is an affine image of a Euclidean ball, equivalently a set of the form $\{x:(x-c)^{\mathsf T}A(x-c)\leq1\}$ with $A$ symmetric positive definite, or its interior if open bodies are used. The source prefers a new method because the earlier proofs involve highly nontrivial calculations; this is a methodological preference rather than an extra hypothesis on $K$.

The supplied word "body" does not specify bounded-domain, connectedness, boundary-regularity, or regular-closure assumptions. These matter to exact set equality: the potential only depends on the body up to Lebesgue-null changes, so a conclusion as an exact set cannot be posed for completely arbitrary measurable sets without a convention. No missing body hypotheses are silently supplied.

The research summary claims a solution in all dimensions by Shapiro and later results, describing a "rat-trap"/harmonic-extension theorem and mentioning Reznick and algebraic approaches. It supplies no precise theorem or reference establishing the required scope. This claimed resolution conflicts with the registry's open status and OPEN-TRIAGE classification; both are preserved as supplied assessments rather than reconciled by an invented literature verification.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a uniform body whose interior Newtonian potential is quadratic must be an ellipsoid in dimensions above $3$, preferably with a less computational proof. The supplied summary claims an all-dimensional solution, but gives no precise citation and conflicts with the open registry label. The body's regularity convention also needs clarification.
