Unique Equilibria for Subcritical Reinforcement on Infinite Graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an infinite graph of bounded degree, so that $\sup_{v\in V}\deg_G(v)<\infty$. Write $p(v)$ for the firing rate at vertex $v$, and assume that these rates are bounded above. Let $\alpha<1$ be the reinforcement parameter, and write $X_e(t)$ for the source's normalized reinforcement variable associated with edge $e\in E$ at time $t$.

The supplied record does not define the reinforcement process, its normalization, its initial conditions, or the equations and admissible state space defining an equilibrium. Consequently, the following is a formulation of the intended conjecture conditional on those missing definitions, rather than a complete standalone specification of a stochastic model. No additional lower bound on $\alpha$, positivity or uniform lower bound for $p(v)$, or graph assumptions beyond those stated above are imposed here.

Subcritical conjecture, as supplied. For every graph, firing-rate assignment, and parameter satisfying the preceding hypotheses, does the intended reinforcement model have exactly one admissible equilibrium $x=(x_e)_{e\in E}$, with \[ \text{for every }e\in E,\qquad \mathbb P\!\left(\lim_{t\to\infty}X_e(t)=x_e\right)=1? \] The probability law and the quantification over initial conditions remain unspecified in the supplied record. If $E$ is countable, the displayed assertion also gives convergence for all edges on a single event of probability one. Countability is not explicitly stated in the record.

Supplied subcritical context. The excerpt asserts that, for finite graphs, concavity of a function $L$ gives convergence to a unique equilibrium maximizing $L$ on a nonnegative simplex. Neither $L$ nor a reliably typeset normalization constraint is supplied. It attributes partial results to Y. Couzinié and C. Hirsch, reference [2, Theorem 2.2], under the assumption $p(v)\equiv1$: convergence for $\alpha<\tfrac12$, and convergence for $\alpha<1$ when $G=\mathbb Z$. The supplied dated assessment describes the first result as applying to bounded-degree graphs. These are reported source claims, not independently verified results here.

Separate critical context retained from the excerpt. The source also discusses $\alpha=1$, which is outside the subcritical conjecture. It describes $L$ as non-strictly concave and reports nonuniqueness of equilibrium on even cycles, citing reference [8]. For uniform firing rates, it gives the equilibria on a cycle of length $2k$ in the form \[ \{(a,b,a,b,\ldots,a,b):a+b=2\}, \] without explicitly specifying the admissible domains of $a,b$. It reports uniqueness on odd cycles and claims that, as $k\to\infty$, the law of $(a,b)$ tends to the Dirac measure at $(1,1)$; the law selecting these equilibria is not defined in the record. This motivates a separate critical conjecture for $G=\mathbb Z$ and $p(v)\equiv1$, rendered in the excerpt as almost surely $x_v(t)\to1$ for every $v$. The relation of that notation to the edge variables $X_e(t)$ is unspecified. This critical conjecture is retained as source context and is not incorporated into the subcritical target.

Short English statement

The supplied subcritical conjecture asks whether every infinite bounded-degree graph with bounded firing rates has a unique equilibrium and almost-sure convergence of every normalized edge variable to that equilibrium when $\alpha<1$. The process, equilibrium equations, and initial-condition assumptions are missing, so a complete standalone mathematical statement requires further source information. The supplied partial results and separate critical-case discussion are retained as attributed context.
