Math-7120

Abelianizations of Finitely Presented Thompson $F$ Subgroups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ denote Thompson's group in its standard interval realization: the group, under composition, of increasing piecewise linear homeomorphisms $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ with finitely many breakpoints, all in ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]\cap(0,1)$, and with every linear slope in $\{2^k:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}$. Here ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]=\{m/2^j:m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\}$.

A subgroup $H\leq F$ is finitely presented if, as an abstract group, it admits a presentation with finitely many generators and finitely many defining relators. Its commutator subgroup is \[ [H,H]=\langle xyx^{-1}y^{-1}:x,y\in H\rangle, \] and its abelianization is $H_{\mathrm{ab}}=H/[H,H]$. An abelian group $A$, written additively, is torsion-free if $na=0$ implies $a=0$ for every $a\in A$ and every integer $n\geq1$.

The question is whether every finitely presented subgroup $H\leq F$ has torsion-free abelianization. Equivalently, is it true that, for every finitely presented $H\leq F$, every $h\in H$, and every integer $n\geq1$, \[ h^n\in[H,H]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h\in[H,H]? \]

The supplied record labels this question open. Its supplied literature assessment attributes to Rover an example disproving the analogous assertion for finitely generated subgroups, while stating that the report leaves the finitely presented case open. These are source qualifications, not independently verified conclusions. The report's bibliographic year is supplied as 2019, whereas its report designation and proposed year refer to 2018; this draft does not resolve that dating distinction.

Short English statement

Must the abelianization of every finitely presented subgroup of Thompson's group $F$ contain no nonzero element of finite order? The supplied record distinguishes this question from the finitely generated version, which it reports is disproved by Rover's example. Its open-status assessment requires independent review.
