Statistical Optimality and Validation in Learning-and-Optimization Models

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Learning-and-optimization (LEO), also termed learning enabled optimization in the supplied references, combines statistical learning and optimization. The supplied problem is a request for a conceptual and mathematical framework, rather than a conjecture about a specified class of models.

To explain the proposed probability criterion schematically, suppose a minimization model has a nonempty feasible set $X$, an objective $J:X\to{\mathbb{R}}$ with finite infimum $J_*:=\inf_{x\in X}J(x)$, and a measurable random feasible output $\widehat x$ on a probability space with law $\mathbb P$. For a specified tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$, an output is $\varepsilon$-optimal when $J(\widehat x)\leq J_*+\varepsilon$; its probability of being $\varepsilon$-optimal is \[ \mathbb P\!\left(J(\widehat x)\leq J_*+\varepsilon\right). \] An exact deterministic optimum would satisfy $J(x)=J_*$. This notation is illustrative only: the source does not specify a minimization formulation, feasible set, objective, probability law, measurability assumptions, or finite optimal value. It also does not specify whether randomness comes from training data, an algorithm, future observations, or some combination, or whether optimality is measured for an empirical or population objective.

Research problem. For LEO models, determine whether deterministic optimality is the appropriate goal or whether an appropriate notion of statistical optimality should quantify the probability of obtaining an $\varepsilon$-optimal solution. Formulate a suitable notion of generalizability and determine whether hypothesis tests can validate such models. The source supplies no model class, generalization target, null and alternative hypotheses, sampling assumptions, confidence requirement, or error criterion. Consequently, these requests cannot yet be expressed as a uniquely specified quantified theorem or testing problem; choosing those ingredients is part of the requested framework.

Short English statement

Develop an appropriate notion of optimality for LEO models, possibly using the probability of obtaining an $\varepsilon$-optimal solution, and define generalizability and statistical model validation. The source poses a research program without enough assumptions to specify a unique theorem.
