Math-1910

Expansive components

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\alpha:\mathbb Z^2\to\operatorname{Homeo}(X)$ be an action on a compact metric space $(X,d)$. Assume that for some nonzero $\boldsymbol n\in\mathbb Z^2$, the homeomorphism $\alpha^{\boldsymbol n}$ is topologically conjugate to a two-sided shift of finite type, that is, a shift on sequences over a finite alphabet determined by finitely many forbidden words. No mixing assumption is stated.

A line $L\subset\mathbb R^2$ through the origin is expansive for $\alpha$ if there exist $r>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that, whenever $x,y\in X$ satisfy \[ d(\alpha^{\boldsymbol m}x,\alpha^{\boldsymbol m}y)\leq\varepsilon \quad\text{for all }\boldsymbol m\in\mathbb Z^2 \text{ with }\operatorname{dist}(\boldsymbol m,L)\leq r, \] then $x=y$. Let \[ \mathcal E(\alpha)=\{v\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}:\mathbb Rv\text{ is expansive}\}. \] Its connected components are the expansive components in the cone convention. Equivalently, one may describe their directional intervals; identifying opposite directions does not change either existence question below. A line has irrational slope precisely when it contains no nonzero vector of $\mathbb Z^2$.

Under the stated shift-of-finite-type-element hypothesis:

  1. Can $\mathcal E(\alpha)$ have infinitely many connected components?
  2. Can an expansive component have a nonzero boundary ray contained in a line of irrational slope?
The supplied research summary describes both questions as unresolved through its 2026 assessment. Its phrase "$\mathbb Z^2$ SFTs" is not used to strengthen the actual hypothesis, which says that one element of the action is a one-dimensional SFT.

Short English statement

For a $\mathbb Z^2$ action with a shift-of-finite-type element, can the set of expansive directions have infinitely many components? Can one such component end at an irrational direction?
