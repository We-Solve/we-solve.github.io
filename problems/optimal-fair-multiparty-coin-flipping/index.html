Optimal Fair Multiparty Coin Flipping

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

In multiparty coin flipping, participants aim to produce a common random bit despite adversarial participants. Fairness restricts the adversary's ability to bias output or obtain an outcome while preventing honest participants from doing so.

Record scope and qualifications.
The catalogue title is retained as metadata. Named certificates, contracts, bounds or frameworks in that title are not asserted as results of the original source extract. The meaning of $m$, overhead measure, communication model, and precise fairness notion are absent from the supplied question.

Source-record limitation: The variable m, overhead measure, corruption model, and precise fairness requirement are not specified.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does there exist a fair three-party coin-flipping protocol without the $O(\log^2 m)$ factor? More generally, does there exist a fair protocol for any number of parties against any number of corrupted parties? [S2]

The label is preserved for provenance and is not an independent verification of present open status or mathematical correctness. The catalogue literature assessment is dated 2026-08-21.

Short English statement

Remove the stated overhead in fair three-party coin flipping and determine what fairness is achievable with more parties and corruptions.
