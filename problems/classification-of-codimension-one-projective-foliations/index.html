Math-1001

Classification of Codimension-One Projective Foliations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a complex projective manifold of dimension $n$. Under the usual convention allowing singularities, a codimension-one holomorphic foliation $\mathcal F$ is a saturated coherent subsheaf $T_{\mathcal F}\subseteq T_X$ of rank $n-1$, closed under the Lie bracket. Saturation means that $T_X/T_{\mathcal F}$ is torsion-free. On the locus where this subsheaf is a subbundle, its integral leaves have complex codimension one.

In the usual meromorphic interpretation, a transverse projective structure is represented by a ${\mathbb{P}}^1$-bundle over $X$, a flat meromorphic connection on that bundle, and a meromorphic section generically transverse to the connection's horizontal foliation, whose pullback induces $\mathcal F$. Away from singularities and poles, this gives local first integrals with values in ${\mathbb{P}}^1$, related on overlaps by locally constant elements of $\operatorname{PGL}_2({\mathbb{C}})$. The supplied record does not specify whether this meromorphic convention, including the allowance of poles, is intended.

For a projective surface $Y$, a one-dimensional foliation $\mathcal G$ has tangent sheaf of generic rank one. For a rational map $\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y$, the equality $\mathcal F=\pi^*\mathcal G$ means that, on a dense open subset where the map and both foliations are regular, the tangent distribution of $\mathcal F$ is \[ T_{\mathcal F}=({\,\mathrm{d}}\pi)^{-1}(T_{\mathcal G}), \] and that its saturated extension to $X$ is the given foliation. This inverse image must have generic rank $n-1$. The record does not explicitly require $\pi$ to be dominant or $Y$ to be smooth; these conditions must be checked against the intended source formulation.

Supplied conjecture (Cerveau--Lins Neto). Is it true that, for every complex projective manifold $X$ and every codimension-one holomorphic foliation $\mathcal F$ on $X$, at least one of the following holds?

  1. $\mathcal F$ is transversely projective.
  2. There exist a projective surface $Y$, a one-dimensional foliation $\mathcal G$ on $Y$, and a rational map $\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y$ such that \[ \mathcal F=\pi^*\mathcal G. \]
The alternatives are not asserted to be mutually exclusive. The definitions above express a conventional reading; the singularity, meromorphic-structure, and pullback conventions remain subject to source verification and human mathematical review.

Short English statement

The supplied Cerveau--Lins Neto conjecture asks whether every codimension-one holomorphic foliation on a complex projective manifold either has a transverse projective structure or comes by rational pullback from a one-dimensional foliation on a projective surface. The record labels the conjecture open; this status and the precise geometric conventions require independent review.
