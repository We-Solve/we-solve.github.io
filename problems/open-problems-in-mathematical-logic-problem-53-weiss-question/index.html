Open Problems in Mathematical Logic — Problem 53 (Weiss' question)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Interpret the question in the usual setting of Borel actions on standard Borel spaces. A standard Borel space is a measurable space isomorphic to the Borel measurable space of a Polish space. This ambient-space hypothesis is conventional but is not explicit in the supplied statement.

Let $G$ be a countable group, equipped with the discrete Borel structure. Say that $G$ is amenable if, for every finite set $K\subseteq G$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a nonempty finite set $F\subseteq G$ such that \[ |kF\mathbin{\triangle}F|<\varepsilon |F|\qquad\text{for every }k\in K, \] where $\triangle$ denotes symmetric difference. A Borel action of $G$ on a standard Borel space $X$ is a Borel map $G\times X\to X$, denoted $(g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x$, satisfying $e\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$. Its orbit equivalence relation is \[ E_G^X=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\exists g\in G\ (y=g\cdot x)\}. \] A Borel equivalence relation $E$ on $X$ is hyperfinite if there are Borel equivalence relations $E_n\subseteq X\times X$, indexed by integers $n\geq 0$, such that every $E_n$-class is finite and \[ E_n\subseteq E_{n+1}\quad(n\geq0),\qquad E=\bigcup_{n\geq0}E_n. \] The finite classes need not have a common bound on their sizes.

Question (attributed in the supplied record to Weiss). For every countable amenable group $G$, every standard Borel space $X$, and every Borel action $G\curvearrowright X$, is $E_G^X$ hyperfinite?

No freeness assumption or probability measure is imposed. Hyperfiniteness here is required on all of $X$, without discarding an exceptional set. These specifications express the conventional Borel interpretation; the supplied one-sentence statement does not explicitly specify the ambient measurable space or distinguish global hyperfiniteness from measure-dependent variants.

Short English statement

Weiss' question asks whether every Borel action of a countable amenable group on a standard Borel space has an orbit relation that is an increasing union of Borel equivalence relations with finite classes. The standard Borel setting is a conventional interpretation of the supplied wording and requires review. The registry labels the problem open; that status is supplied metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
