Math-1598

A finite-superlevel certificate for inhomogeneous minima

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The phrase "as above" refers to section 5.6, recovered from companion permanent ID $20003321$. Let \[ Q(x_1,x_2)=ax_1^2+bx_1x_2+cx_2^2,\qquad a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Q}}, \] be nondegenerate, indefinite, and anisotropic over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, meaning $Q(x)=0$ for $x\in{\mathbb{Q}}^2$ only when $x=0$. Its discriminant $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ is positive and nonsquare in ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and its splitting field is $K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt\Delta)$. On $\mathbb T^2={\mathbb{R}}^2/{\mathbb{Z}}^2$, define \[ m(Q,x)=\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2}|Q(x+z)|,\qquad M=m(Q)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb T^2}m(Q,x). \] Isolation of $M$ means that some $\delta>0$ satisfies \[ \{m(Q,x):x\in\mathbb T^2\}\cap(M-\delta,M)=\varnothing. \] After this isolation is established, define \[ m_2(Q)=\sup\{m(Q,x):x\in\mathbb T^2,\ m(Q,x)<M\}. \] Conjecture 36, attributed to E. Barnes and H. Swinnerton-Dyer and communicated by A. Pollington, states that $M\in{\mathbb{Q}}$ is isolated and that there exist torus classes with representatives $x,y\in K^2$ such that \[ m(Q,x)=M,\qquad m(Q,y)=m_2(Q). \] The supplied verification explicitly warns, citing Godwin, that $m_2(Q)$ need not itself be isolated. It also records the duplicated word "the" as a source typographical duplication, not OCR damage.

The attached aid reports a conditional finite certificate. First take $Q$ primitive integral of the above type and a hyperbolic integral automorphism $T\in\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})$ with $Q(Tz)=Q(z)$. If for some $0<c<M$ the torus superlevel \[ X_c=\{x\in\mathbb T^2:m(Q,x)\ge c\} \] is finite, then $T$ permutes $X_c$. Every member is therefore periodic under $T$ and has a rational representative; its inhomogeneous minimum is a discrete rational value. The aid reports that this gives rational attainment and isolation of $M$. If the largest value below $M$ represented in $X_c$ exists and is strictly larger than $c$, it is $m_2(Q)$ and is attained at a rational representative. The positive gap between threshold and top is explicit here; finiteness of a set containing only points at a threshold equal to $M$ would not by itself certify isolation.

The reported normalization laws are \[ m(rQ,x)=|r|m(Q,x)\quad(r\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}),\qquad m(Q\circ U,x)=m(Q,Ux)\quad(U\in\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})), \] with the corresponding scaling and invariance of $m(Q)$ and, when defined, $m_2(Q)$. They transfer the certificate to rational forms. The aid gives the exact check \[ m(X^2-2Y^2)=\tfrac12,\qquad m(X^2-2Y^2,(0,\tfrac12))=\tfrac12. \] No finite superlevel certificate is supplied for every admissible form, so this conditional result does not establish the full conjecture.

Short English statement

For an anisotropic indefinite rational binary quadratic form, is its top inhomogeneous minimum rational and isolated, with both first and second minima attained over its splitting field? The supplied finite-superlevel certificate proves these conclusions only when its stated finite-set and threshold conditions hold; it does not require the second minimum to be isolated.
