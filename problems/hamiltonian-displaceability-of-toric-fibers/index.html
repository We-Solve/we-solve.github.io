Math-3451

Hamiltonian Displaceability of Toric Fibers

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,\omega)$ be a toric symplectic manifold of real dimension $2n$, equipped with an effective Hamiltonian action of $\mathbb T^n={\mathbb{R}}^n/{\mathbb{Z}}^n$ and moment map $\mu:M\to({\mathbb{R}}^n)^*$. Write $\Delta=\mu(M)$, and restrict attention to the values \[ U=\{u\in\Delta:\mu^{-1}(u)\text{ is a Lagrangian torus of dimension }n\}. \] Here a Lagrangian torus is an embedded submanifold diffeomorphic to $\mathbb T^n$ on which $\omega$ restricts to zero. For $u\in U$, set $T_u=\mu^{-1}(u)$.

A Hamiltonian isotopy is a smooth family of diffeomorphisms $\phi_H^t$, $0\leq t\leq1$, starting at the identity and generated by a smooth time-dependent Hamiltonian $H_t$, with $\iota_{X_{H_t}}\omega=-{\,\mathrm{d}} H_t$. Its flow must exist on all of $M$ throughout this interval. Write $\operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega)$ for the group of time-one maps of the admissible Hamiltonian isotopies. The source does not specify whether $M$ is compact or which support restrictions apply to Hamiltonians when $M$ is noncompact; the admissible class, and thus this group in the noncompact case, remains a qualification requiring clarification.

Classification problem. For each manifold and moment map in the intended class, determine the set \[ \mathcal D(M,\omega,\mu) =\left\{u\in U:\exists\phi\in\operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega),\quad \phi(T_u)\cap T_u=\varnothing\right\}. \] Equivalently, determine exactly which Lagrangian torus fibers are Hamiltonian-displaceable in the ambient manifold $M$. The isotopy is not required to preserve the torus action or the moment map.

The restriction to $U$ expresses the source's requirement that the fibers under consideration be Lagrangian tori. Fibers that are not Lagrangian tori, including lower-dimensional boundary fibers in the compact toric setting, are not included in this formulation. The source does not state a separate displacement problem for such fibers. No compactness, properness, or additional geometric hypothesis is supplied, so the intended class of toric manifolds remains unresolved.

Short English statement

Determine, for each toric symplectic manifold in the intended class, exactly which Lagrangian torus fibers can be moved completely off themselves by a Hamiltonian isotopy. The source leaves compactness and the Hamiltonian support convention for noncompact manifolds unspecified. Its partially solved label is supplied dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
