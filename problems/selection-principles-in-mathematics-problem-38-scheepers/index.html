Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 38 (Scheepers)

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a topological space $X$, let $C_p(X)=C(X,{\mathbb{R}})$ denote the space of continuous real-valued functions with the topology of pointwise convergence, and let $\mathbf 0$ be the constant zero function. A basic neighborhood of $\mathbf 0$ is \[ N(F,\varepsilon)=\{f\in C(X,{\mathbb{R}}): |f(x)|<\varepsilon\text{ for every }x\in F\}, \] where $F\subseteq X$ is finite and $\varepsilon>0$.

Under the conventional definitions used for this draft, let \[ \Omega_{\mathbf 0}=\{A\subseteq C_p(X)\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}:\mathbf 0\in\overline A\}, \] and let $\Gamma_{\mathbf 0}$ consist of infinite sets $A\subseteq C_p(X)\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}$ such that every neighborhood of $\mathbf 0$ contains all but finitely many elements of $A$.

An open $\omega$-cover of $X$ is a family $\mathcal U$ of open subsets of $X$ such that $X\notin\mathcal U$ and every finite subset of $X$ is contained in some member of $\mathcal U$. An open $\gamma$-cover is an infinite open cover $\mathcal U$ with $X\notin\mathcal U$ such that each point of $X$ belongs to all but finitely many members of $\mathcal U$. Write $\Omega(X)$ and $\Gamma(X)$ for these respective families of covers.

For families $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ of sets, the principle $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ means that for every sequence $(A_n)_{n\geq 1}$ with $A_n\in\mathcal A$, there exist choices $a_n\in A_n$ for all $n\geq 1$ such that $\{a_n:n\geq 1\}\in\mathcal B$.

Question (Scheepers, as attributed in the supplied record). Is \[ \mathsf S_1\left(\Gamma_{\mathbf 0},\Omega_{\mathbf 0}\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathsf S_1\left(\Gamma(X),\Omega(X)\right) \] for $C_p(X)$ and $X$, respectively?

Short English statement

The question asks whether selecting one function from each of a sequence of sets converging to the zero function, so that the selected functions accumulate at zero, is equivalent to selecting one member from each of a sequence of open $\gamma$-covers of $X$ so that the selected members form an $\omega$-cover.
