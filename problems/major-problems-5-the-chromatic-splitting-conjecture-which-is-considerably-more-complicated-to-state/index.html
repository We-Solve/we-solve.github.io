Major problems 5 — The chromatic splitting conjecture, which is considerably more complicated to state.

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every chromatic height $n$ and prime $p$, does the chromatic splitting conjecture hold for the $p$-local sphere? Write $L_n$ for localization at $E(n)$ and $L_{K(n)}$ for localization at Morava $K(n)$. The natural map $L_{n-1}S\to L_{n-1}L_{K(n)}S$ is predicted to exhibit $L_{n-1}S$ as a wedge summand, with the complementary spectrum given by the prescribed wedge of lower chromatic localizations and suspensions. This asks for the full splitting prediction, including its prescribed complementary summands.

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

Major problems 5 — The chromatic splitting conjecture, which is considerably more complicated to state.

OpenAI's claim

Manuscript 1 (family 318, 2026-09-25): At primes at least five, the K$3$-local sphere is claimed to retain a nonzero degree-minus-three class under rationalized K$2$-localization, obstructing unmarked strong-splitting wedge equivalences; Manuscript 2 (family 318, 2026-09-27): The canonical weak-splitting unit is claimed to have no retraction at height p for p at least five and height p+1 for p at least seven, witnessed by $\beta_1^{(p-1)^2}$. Manuscript 3 (family 318, 2026-09-25): At prime three and height three, the overlap is claimed to lie outside the ordinary thick subcategory generated by lower local spheres, even allowing arbitrary finite cofibers and retracts of derived-complete sphere modules; Manuscript 4 (family 318, 2026-09-25): For n at least one and p greater than n+1, lower-height localization of the K(n)-local sphere is claimed to have an ordered $2^n$-stage filtration with the classical chromatic-splitting cofibers and canonical initial unit. Manuscript 5 (family 318, 2026-09-27): At height three and every prime at least five, the overlap is claimed to have an explicit eight-stage sphere-layer filtration with identified signed attachments; its first height-one attachment is nonzero.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For a prime $p\ge5$, let $S=S_p^\wedge$ be the completed sphere, $L_j$ localization at Johnson--Wilson $E(j)$, $L_{K(j)}$ localization at Morava $K(j)$, and $L_0$ rationalization. OpenAI claims that the canonical map \[ L_0L_{K(3)}S\longrightarrow L_0L_{K(2)}L_{K(3)}S \] is nonzero on $\pi_{-3}$. Consequently it claims that $L_2L_{K(3)}S$ is not equivalent, even without compatibility with the unit summand, to \[ L_2(S\vee\Sigma^{-1}S)\vee L_1(\Sigma^{-3}S\vee\Sigma^{-4}S) \vee L_0(\Sigma^{-5}S\vee\Sigma^{-6}S\vee\Sigma^{-8}S\vee\Sigma^{-9}S). \] This is an obstruction to the proposed strong height-three wedge formula at every prime in the stated range.
Mathematical claim 2.
Let $p\ge5$, $S=S_p^\wedge$, $L_j=L_{E(j)}$ and $L_{K(j)}$ be chromatic localizations. Put \[ d_p=2p(p-1)-2,\qquad k_p=(p-1)^2,\qquad z_p=\beta_1^{k_p}\in\pi_{d_pk_p}S, \] where $\beta_1$ is the first classical beta element. OpenAI claims that the image of $z_p$ in $\pi_{d_pk_p}L_{p-1}S$ is nonzero but is killed by the unit-induced map \[ i_{p,S}:L_{p-1}S\longrightarrow L_{p-1}L_{K(p)}S. \] Hence this map has no homotopy retraction. The finite input is the sphere and the height equals the prime; this does not assert such failure at every height.
Mathematical claim 3.
At prime $3$, with completed sphere $S=S_3^\wedge$, OpenAI claims \[ L_2L_{K(3)}S\notin\operatorname{Thick}\{L_0S,L_1S,L_2S\} \] in the category of $E(2)$-local $S$-modules. Here $L_j$ is $E(j)$-localization, $L_{K(3)}$ is Morava-$K(3)$ localization, and the thick subcategory allows finite cofibers, suspensions and retracts. Thus arbitrary finite attachments, including Moore corrections, cannot assemble this chromatic overlap from the indicated lower-height localized spheres.
Mathematical claim 4.
For $n\ge1$ and prime $p>n+1$, put $S=S_p^\wedge$, $D=L_{K(n)}S$ and $X_{n,p}=L_{n-1}D$, where $L_j=L_{E(j)}$. OpenAI claims a filtration in $E(n-1)$-local $S$-modules with $2^n$ cofibers indexed by subsets $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$: \[ C_\varnothing=L_{n-1}S,\qquad C_I=\Sigma^{-d(I)}L_{n-\max I}S\ (I\ne\varnothing),\qquad d(I)=\sum_{i\in I}(2i-1). \] The empty subset is first, the remaining maxima are nondecreasing, and the first-layer map is the canonical unit. It claims a single family $y_i\in\pi_{1-2i}D$ whose ordered products $y_I$ give, for every $1\le t<n$, equivalences \[ L_{K(t)}\left(\bigvee_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-t\}}\Sigma^{-d(I)}S\xrightarrow{(y_I)}D\right). \] A filtered extension is asserted, not a split wedge decomposition before localization.
Mathematical claim 5.
For every prime $p\ge5$, put $S=S_p^\wedge$ and $X=L_2L_{K(3)}S$. OpenAI claims an eight-layer filtration $0=F_0\to\cdots\to F_8\simeq X$ of $E(2)$-local $S$-modules whose ordered cofibers are \[ L_2S,\ \Sigma^{-1}L_2S,\ \Sigma^{-3}L_1S,\ \Sigma^{-4}L_1S,\ \Sigma^{-5}H\mathbb Q_p,\ \Sigma^{-6}H\mathbb Q_p,\ \Sigma^{-8}H\mathbb Q_p,\ \Sigma^{-9}H\mathbb Q_p. \] Here $L_j=L_{E(j)}$, $L_{K(3)}$ is Morava-$K$ localization and $H\mathbb Q_p$ is the Eilenberg--Mac Lane spectrum. The inclusion $F_1\simeq L_2S\to X$ is the unit localization map. The displayed layers do not assert that the attaching maps vanish.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 318, 2026-09-25): Marked connected deformations produce a genuine K$2$-local detector receiving the height-three sphere, forcing factorization through the localization unit. Division-algebra and linear trace classes are compared on a determinant-lattice modification space, retaining integral cohomology before rationalization. Their nonzero rank-two restriction conflicts with the vanishing natural map that the proposed wedge would have in degree minus three. Manuscript 2 (family 318, 2026-09-27): The named beta power survives under the height-$p-1$ unit by an ordinary homotopy-fixed-point detection calculation. At each upper height, a separated multiplicative filtration puts beta_1 in degree at least two and vanishes beyond a dimension-controlled bound, killing that same power. Completion and naturality identify a nonzero lower-localized class in the unit's kernel, obstructing a retraction.

Manuscript 3 (family 318, 2026-09-25): Integral connected and etale markings retain an exceptional norm-character line on the height-two stratum. Full principal-unit action separates that line from every periodicity power carried by the permitted inputs. Comparisons through ordinary finite-stage coefficients and actual homotopy preserve the character across the whole deformation, giving a representation obstruction to any finite assembly. Manuscript 4 (family 318, 2026-09-25): Finite marking and transfer calculations first establish coefficient finiteness. A separately constructed integral family of sphere-cochain classes has normalized exterior products acting as bases under lower-height tests. Removing blocks in decreasing chromatic height yields the ordered cofibers; independent rational dimension counts supply the final block while retaining the extensions between stages.

Manuscript 5 (family 318, 2026-09-27): Integral frame comparisons construct actual bases at heights two and one, while a trace-class calculation fixes their nonzero rational degree-three comparison. A chosen compatibility homotopy feeds two fracture squares, giving the filtration and the signs of its connecting maps. The separate canonical-map obstruction then detects a nontrivial first middle attachment for these same choices.
