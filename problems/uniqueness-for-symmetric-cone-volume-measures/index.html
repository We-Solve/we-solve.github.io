Math-805

Uniqueness for Symmetric Cone-Volume Measures

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the usual Euclidean interpretation, let $n\geq 1$ be an integer. A convex body in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. It is origin-symmetric if $K=-K$. Write $\operatorname{vol}_n$ for Lebesgue volume and $S^{n-1}$ for the unit sphere. For a convex body $K$, its support function is \[ h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle,\qquad u\in S^{n-1}. \] Its surface area measure $S_K$ assigns to a Borel set of directions the boundary area of points whose outer unit normal lies in that set; the normal is defined at almost every boundary point. Use the unnormalized cone-volume measure \[ V_K(\omega)=\frac1n\int_{\omega}h_K(u)\,\mathrm dS_K(u) \qquad(\omega\subseteq S^{n-1}\text{ Borel}). \] For nonzero linear subspaces $E_1,\ldots,E_m$ with ${\mathbb{R}}^n=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_m$, and sets $A_i\subseteq E_i$, write \[ A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_m =\{a_1+\cdots+a_m:a_i\in A_i\}. \] Thus every vector in this sum has a unique decomposition into its subspace components; the subspaces are not required to be orthogonal.

Question under these conventions. For every integer $n\geq1$ and every pair of origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{vol}_n(K)=\operatorname{vol}_n(C),\qquad V_K(\omega)=V_C(\omega)\quad\text{for every Borel }\omega\subseteq S^{n-1}, \] must there exist an integer $1\leq m\leq n$, nonzero linear subspaces $E_1,\ldots,E_m$ with ${\mathbb{R}}^n=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_m$, origin-symmetric convex bodies $K_i,C_i$ in $E_i$ relative to its topology, and scalars $\lambda_i>0$, such that \[ K=K_1\oplus\cdots\oplus K_m,\qquad C=C_1\oplus\cdots\oplus C_m,\qquad K_i=\lambda_i C_i\quad(1\leq i\leq m)? \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify the ambient dimension range, the normalization of cone-volume measure, or the precise direct-sum convention. The formulation above makes the standard Euclidean conventions explicit; their correspondence with the report requires review.

Short English statement

If two origin-symmetric convex bodies have equal volume and identical cone-volume measures, must they split along common complementary linear subspaces into corresponding factors that differ only by positive dilations? The supplied record labels this question open; that status and the explicit conventions in this draft require human review.
