Open Problems on Central Simple Algebras — Problem 5

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a field. Under the finite-dimensional central interpretation described below, let $D$ be a division algebra with center $F$ and $\dim_F D=n^2<\infty$; its degree is $\deg D=n$. A field extension $L/F$ splits $D$ if \[ D\otimes_F L\cong M_n(L) \] as $L$-algebras. Interpret "split by an abelian group" as the existence of a finite Galois splitting extension $L/F$ whose Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$ is abelian.

With these explicitly stated interpretations, the qualitative question is whether \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every field }F\text{ and every finite-dimensional central division }F\text{-algebra }D,\\ \text{there exists a finite Galois extension }L/F\text{ such that}\\ \operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\text{ is abelian and }D\otimes_F L\cong M_{\deg D}(L). \end{gathered} \] The supplied record also asks for degree bounds over specific fields. It does not identify those fields, specify which degree is to be bounded, or give the parameters on which such a bound may depend. Consequently this quantitative request cannot yet be expressed as a definite mathematical assertion.

The finite-dimensional central setting and the interpretation using an abelian Galois splitting extension are explicit provisional interpretations of the supplied wording, not hypotheses confirmed from the cited source. No characteristic restriction is supplied. The record does not say that $L$ must embed in $D$, be a maximal subfield, or satisfy $[L:F]=\deg D$; none of these additional conditions is imposed here.

Short English statement

Under an explicit provisional interpretation, ask whether every finite-dimensional division algebra over its center becomes a matrix algebra after a finite abelian Galois extension. The accompanying request for degree bounds remains unspecified because the record names neither the fields nor the degree to be bounded. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
