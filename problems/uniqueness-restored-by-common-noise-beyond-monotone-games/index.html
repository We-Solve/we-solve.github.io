Uniqueness restored by common noise beyond monotone games

GT-083 | Mean-field games | R: formal research agenda

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix $T>0$, an initial law $m_0\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)$, and smooth bounded couplings $F,G:\mathbb T^d\times\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)\to\mathbb R$, which need not be monotone. Here $d\geq1$ is fixed, $\mathbb T^d=\R^d/\mathbb Z^d$, and $\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)$ denotes Borel probability measures with the weak topology. The couplings are jointly Borel measurable and bounded in value. Since the coupling class is an output of this agenda, each proposed class $\mathcal C$ must explicitly define its use of "smooth": the spatial and measure regularity, the definition and orders of any measure derivatives, and all continuity and derivative bounds, including whether bounds are uniform over the class. No particular measure differentiability convention or derivative bound is implicit in that word. An admissible ordinary common-noise model is a sequence of vector fields $\Sigma=(\sigma_k)_{k\geq1}$ on $\mathbb T^d$ satisfying $\sum_k\|\sigma_k\|_{C^3}^2<\infty$, acting through independent common Brownian motions $(W^k)$: \[ dX_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t+\sum_{k\geq1}\sigma_k(X_t)dW_t^k, \qquad X_0\sim m_0. \] Work on a complete probability space carrying mutually independent inputs $X_0$, a standard $d$-dimensional Brownian motion $B$, and standard real Brownian motions $(W^k)_{k\geq1}$. Let $(\mathcal F_t)$ be the usual augmentation of the filtration generated by $X_0$, $(B_s)_{s\leq t}$ and $(W_s^k)_{s\leq t,k\geq1}$; the driving processes are Brownian relative to this filtration. The Itô convention is used, with the common-noise series interpreted as its square-integrable stochastic-integral limit. Admissible controls are $\R^d$-valued, $(\mathcal F_t)$-progressively measurable processes satisfying $\mathbb E\int_0^T|\alpha_t|^2dt<\infty$; $X^\alpha$ denotes the strong solution driven by these inputs, interpreted modulo $\mathbb Z^d$.

A strong equilibrium is a common-noise-adapted random element $(\mu_t)$ of $C([0,T];\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d))$, with its Borel sigma field for uniform weak convergence, and an admissible control $\alpha_*$ such that \[ \mu_t=\mathcal L(X_t^{\alpha_*}\mid\mathcal F_t^0),\qquad J(\alpha_*;\mu)\leq J(\alpha;\mu)\quad\text{for every admissible }\alpha, \] where $(\mathcal F_t^0)$ is the usual augmentation of $\sigma(W_s^k:s\leq t,k\geq1)$. Conditional-law consistency uses a weakly continuous version of the conditional-law process and holds for every $t\in[0,T]$ outside one null event. The cost is \[ J(\alpha;\mu)=\mathbb E\!\left[\int_0^T \left(\tfrac12|\alpha_t|^2+F(X_t^\alpha,\mu_t)\right)dt +G(X_T^\alpha,\mu_T)\right]. \] In a deviation, the prescribed process $\mu$ and the driving inputs are unchanged; the deviating control remains nonanticipating in $(\mathcal F_t)$. Call equilibria unique in the strong sense if, on the same input probability space with the same initial random variable $X_0$ and Brownian drivers $B,(W^k)$, all equilibrium flows are indistinguishable and their optimal controls agree $dt\otimes d\mathbb P$-almost everywhere. Existence and this uniqueness are required on every stochastic basis of the specified form.

The agenda is to identify verifiable hypotheses on a declared coupling class $\mathcal C$ and noise class $\mathcal N$ for which \[ \begin{gathered} \forall (F,G)\in\mathcal C\quad\forall T>0\quad \forall m_0\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d),\\ \text{the chosen noise model has exactly one strong equilibrium}, \end{gathered} \] including a specified member of $\mathcal C$ with multiple equilibria when the common noise is removed. Each proposed theorem must explicitly quantify $\Sigma\in\mathcal N$ and its selection relative to $(F,G),T,m_0$, including whether one noise works uniformly or noise is selected for each instance. Any dependence of the noise class itself on these data must also be declared. Thus the display is a schema whose noise quantifiers must be supplied, not an implicit existential or universal quantifier over $\mathcal N$. The noise-free witness must specify $(F_*,G_*)\in\mathcal C$, $T_*>0$ and $m_{0,*}$, and exhibit at least two equilibrium flows that are not indistinguishable under the same equilibrium convention. Removing common noise means setting every $\sigma_k=0$ and omitting the common Brownian inputs and their filtration, while retaining the witness's couplings, horizon, initial law and individual diffusion. The formula specifies a target theorem family, not a conjecture that every admissible $\Sigma$ has this property.

Short English statement

Which common noises and coupling classes make previously nonunique MFG equilibria unique?

Variants and scope boundaries

The ordinary conditional-law model above is one editorial instantiation. Other population noises, such as Wright--Fisher or Fleming--Viot noise, require their own consistent representative-player model; they cannot be inserted as unexplained additive noise on a measure. Finite-dimensional additive Brownian noise is not a universal uniqueness mechanism. Strong uniqueness and uniqueness of a weak equilibrium law differ.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.
