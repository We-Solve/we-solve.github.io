Econ-109

Uniqueness restored by common noise beyond monotone games

LLM status Open unverified

C73 — Stochastic and Dynamic Games - Evolutionary Games - Repeated Games Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra ultra 15% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

GT-083 | Mean-field games | R: formal research agenda

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix $T>0$, an initial law $m_0\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)$, and smooth bounded couplings $F,G:\mathbb T^d\times\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)\to\mathbb R$, which need not be monotone. An admissible ordinary common-noise model is a sequence of vector fields $\Sigma=(\sigma_k)_{k\geq1}$ on $\mathbb T^d$ satisfying $\sum_k\|\sigma_k\|_{C^3}^2<\infty$, acting through independent common Brownian motions $(W^k)$: \[ dX_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t+\sum_{k\geq1}\sigma_k(X_t)dW_t^k, \qquad X_0\sim m_0. \] The individual Brownian motion $B$ and $X_0$ are independent of the common noises. The Itô convention is used. Controls are progressively measurable with respect to the noises and initial state, with finite expected quadratic energy.

A strong equilibrium is a common-noise-adapted probability flow $(\mu_t)$ and a control $\alpha_*$ such that \[ \mu_t=\mathcal L(X_t^{\alpha_*}\mid\mathcal F_t^0),\qquad J(\alpha_*;\mu)\leq J(\alpha;\mu)\quad\text{for every admissible }\alpha, \] where $\mathcal F_t^0=\sigma(W_s^k:s\leq t,k\geq1)$ and \[ J(\alpha;\mu)=\mathbb E\!\left[\int_0^T \left(\tfrac12|\alpha_t|^2+F(X_t^\alpha,\mu_t)\right)dt +G(X_T^\alpha,\mu_T)\right]. \] In a deviation, $\mu$ and the common Brownian path remain fixed. Call equilibria unique in the strong sense if, on the same input probability space, all equilibrium flows are indistinguishable and their optimal controls agree $dt\otimes d\mathbb P$-almost everywhere.

The agenda is to identify verifiable hypotheses on a declared coupling class $\mathcal C$ and noise class $\mathcal N$ for which \[ \begin{gathered} \forall (F,G)\in\mathcal C\quad\forall T>0\quad \forall m_0\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d),\\ \text{the chosen noise model has exactly one strong equilibrium}, \end{gathered} \] including a specified member of $\mathcal C$ with multiple equilibria when the common noise is removed. Dependence of $\Sigma$ on $(F,G)$ or $T$ must be stated in the hypothesis; it is not silently uniform. The formula specifies a target theorem family, not a conjecture that every admissible $\Sigma$ has this property.

Short English statement

Which common noises and coupling classes make previously nonunique MFG equilibria unique?

Variants and scope boundaries

The ordinary conditional-law model above is one editorial instantiation. Other population noises, such as Wright--Fisher or Fleming--Viot noise, require their own consistent representative-player model; they cannot be inserted as unexplained additive noise on a measure. Finite-dimensional additive Brownian noise is not a universal uniqueness mechanism. Strong uniqueness and uniqueness of a weak equilibrium law differ.

Source relationship and status

The source discusses regularization beyond known parametric cases. The model and theorem family here are editorial; no universal positive result or noise threshold is attributed to the source.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.

A smooth coordination game immune to additive common noise

Problem ID: C73-107. Status: Unresolved. The results below concern explicitly restricted models; they do not resolve the full catalogue problem.

Question and scope

The catalogue asks for coupling and common-noise classes that restore strong uniqueness in mean-field games previously admitting several equilibria. Here we give a quantitative obstruction: a smooth bounded, translation-invariant terminal coupling on the circle has several strong equilibria for every intensity of ordinary additive common Brownian noise. This specifies a class that a positive regularization theorem must exclude. It does not exclude state-dependent vector fields or other population-noise models.

The source survey [CD] explains the dimensional difficulty with ordinary common noise. The construction and calculations below are supplied here, without a novelty claim. We work on $\mathbb T={\mathbb{R}}/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$; rescaling gives the unit torus. Write $\ell(dx)=dx/(2\pi)$ and let $P_t$ be the heat semigroup with generator $\partial_{xx}$. In particular $P_t\cos x=e^{-t}\cos x$. Fix $T>0$, $\kappa>0$, and \[ F=0,\qquad G(x,\mu)=-\kappa\int\cos(x-y)\,\mu(dy),\qquad m_0=\ell. \] This coupling is smooth in $x$ and in its two Fourier moments. It is bounded by $\kappa$. It is nonmonotone: for a signed difference $\eta=\mu-\nu$, \[ \int[G(x,\mu)-G(x,\nu)]\eta(dx) =-\kappa\left[\left(\int\cos x\,d\eta\right)^2+ \left(\int\sin x\,d\eta\right)^2\right]. \] The player minimizes ${\mathbb{E}}[\int_0^T\alpha_t^2/2\,dt+G(X_T,\mu_T)]$.

An explicit deterministic fixed point

For $q\in[0,1]$, put $a=\kappa q/2$, $H_a(x)=e^{a\cos x}$, and \[ h_q(t,x)=P_{T-t}H_a(x),\qquad u_q(t,x)=-2\log h_q(t,x). \] These functions are smooth and $h_q$ is bounded away from zero. Direct differentiation gives \[ \partial_tu_q+\partial_{xx}u_q-\tfrac12(\partial_xu_q)^2=0, \qquad u_q(T,x)=-\kappa q\cos x. \] Let $X^q$ start uniformly and solve \[ dX_t^q=-\partial_xu_q(t,X_t^q)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB_t. \] Its drift is bounded and Lipschitz, so the equation has a unique strong solution. Denote its deterministic marginal law by $m_t^q$.
Lemma (Verification and terminal density): For every progressively measurable control with finite expected quadratic energy, including controls adapted to additional independent Brownian motions, \[ {\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_0^T\tfrac12\alpha_t^2dt-\kappa q\cos X_T\right] =\int u_q(0,x)\ell(dx) +\tfrac12{\mathbb{E}}\int_0^T|\alpha_t+\partial_xu_q(t,X_t)|^2dt. \] The terminal density of $m_T^q$ with respect to $\ell$ is \[ f_q(y)=H_a(y)P_T\left[(P_TH_a)^{-1}\right](y). \]
Proof: Apply Itô's formula to $u_q(t,X_t)$, use its displayed equation, take expectations, and complete the square. Bounded derivatives and finite energy justify localization followed by passage to the limit; the stochastic integral has mean zero.

For the density, let $p_t(x,y)$ be the heat kernel with respect to $\ell$. For fixed initial point $x$, define the transition density \[ p_{t-s}(x,y)\frac{h_q(t,y)}{h_q(s,x)},\qquad 0\le s<t\le T. \] The heat equation and semigroup identity show it integrates to one and obeys composition. Differentiating its action on a smooth test function at $t=s$ gives generator $\partial_{xx}+2(\partial_x\log h_q)\partial_x$, which is the displayed drift. Uniqueness of the diffusion therefore identifies this transition law. Set $s=0,t=T$ and average $x$ over $\ell$. Symmetry of the heat kernel gives the formula for $f_q$. ∎

Define $\Phi(q)=\int\cos y\,f_q(y)\ell(dy)$. Evenness implies $\int\sin y\,f_q(y)\ell(dy)=0$.

Proposition (Explicit bifurcation condition): If \[ \kappa>\frac{4}{1-e^{-2T}}, \] then there is a $q_*\in(0,1)$ satisfying $\Phi(q_*)=q_*$. Both $m^0$ and $m^{q_*}$ are deterministic mean-field equilibria, and their terminal laws differ.
Proof: The density formula and positivity make $\Phi$ continuously differentiable. At $a=0$, $H_a=1+a\cos+O(a^2)$ uniformly, hence \[ P_TH_a=1+ae^{-T}\cos+O(a^2),\quad P_T[(P_TH_a)^{-1}]=1-ae^{-2T}\cos+O(a^2). \] Consequently $f_q=1+(\kappa q/2)(1-e^{-2T})\cos+O(q^2)$, so \[ \Phi(0)=0,\qquad \Phi'(0)=\frac\kappa4(1-e^{-2T})>1. \] Thus $\Phi(q)-q>0$ for all sufficiently small positive $q$. Since $f_1$ is a strictly positive density and $\cos y<1$ on a set of positive measure, $\Phi(1)<1$. The intermediate value theorem supplies $q_*$. At this fixed point the actual coupling is $G(x,m_T^{q_*})=-\kappa q_*\cos x$, so the verification lemma proves optimality and the diffusion supplies consistency. At $q=0$, the drift is zero and every marginal is uniform; its coupling is zero. Its cosine moment is zero, unlike that of $m_T^{q_*}$. ∎

This is a construction of actual equilibria, not merely nonzero solutions of a linearized equation. For example $T=1$ and $\kappa=5$ satisfy the strict inequality since $5(1-e^{-2})>4$.

Transporting the equilibria by common noise

Fix any $\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and an independent common Brownian motion $W$. Write $\tau_z\mu$ for translation of a law by $z$ modulo $2\pi$.
Theorem (No additive-noise threshold in this family): Under the strict condition in Proposition 2.2, the game \[ dX_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t+\sigma dW_t \] with the above $F,G,m_0$ has at least two distinct strong equilibria, for every finite $\sigma$. More generally the same conclusion holds for finitely or countably many constant vector fields with square-summable coefficients. In particular, arbitrarily large additive common-noise variance does not restore the strong uniqueness requested in the catalogue.
Proof: For either deterministic equilibrium $m^q$, set \[ \mu_t^q=\tau_{\sigma W_t}m_t^q,\quad X_t=X_t^q+\sigma W_t,\quad \alpha_t=-\partial_xu_q(t,X_t-\sigma W_t). \] The control has finite energy and the conditional law of $X_t$ given the common noise is $\mu_t^q$, because $X^q$ depends only on $X_0,B$.

For a deviation $\alpha$, put $Z_t=X_t-\sigma W_t$. Then $dZ_t=\alpha_tdt+\sqrt2\,dB_t$. The same subtraction is valid for controls depending on $W$; no conditional independence of a deviating state is presumed. Translation invariance gives, path by path, \[ G(X_T,\mu_T^q)=G(Z_T,m_T^q)=-\kappa q\cos Z_T. \] The verification lemma in the enlarged filtration proves the candidate optimal against every allowed deviation. For $q=0$ the terminal measure is uniform; for $q=q_*$ the absolute value of its first Fourier coefficient is $q_*>0$, invariant under translation. Thus the two flows on the same probability space differ. For countably many constants, their square-summable stochastic sum is a continuous Brownian translation with the summed variance, and the argument is identical. ∎

What this does and does not establish

The noise moves the center of the coordination pattern but never its magnitude. The nonuniqueness is therefore not removed by observing or conditioning on the common path. The example obeys bounded smooth couplings and ordinary conditional-law consistency; it does not insert an unexplained noise directly into the space of measures.

A positive theorem could still use state-dependent vector fields that change shape, or a coupling class excluding translation-invariant coordination. Establishing such a theorem uniformly over initial laws and horizons, with a game exhibiting deterministic multiplicity, remains open here. The calculation does not identify the exact deterministic bifurcation threshold below the sufficient condition, nor does it classify all equilibria.
