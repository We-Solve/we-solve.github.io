Math-1470

Stationary theta graphs on every circle-symmetric Riemannian two-sphere

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A unit-weight stationary geodesic net consists of geodesic edges with the balancing condition \[ \sum_{e\ni v}u_{e,v}=0 \] at each vertex $v$, where $u_{e,v}$ is the outgoing unit tangent of an incident edge (each incident half-edge is counted). An embedded $\Theta$-graph has two distinct vertices joined by three nonconstant geodesic arcs whose interiors are pairwise disjoint. For three unit-weight edges on a surface, balancing means that the three outgoing tangent directions meet at angles $2\pi/3$ at each vertex.

Question 11.1.1, attributed to [Has-Mor1996], asks: does every smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $S^2$ admit a stationary $\Theta$-graph? The source excerpt omits its preceding definition of stationary net, so the embedded unit-weight convention used here follows the supplied aid; any broader immersed or weighted convention requires checking against the original definition.

The original source reports the weaker positive-curvature alternative: every positively curved Riemannian two-sphere has a stationary $\Theta$-net, a stationary figure-eight net, or a stationary eyeglasses net. This alternative does not select the $\Theta$ topology. The appended Section 11.2 calls a stationary net nontrivial when it has a vertex of valency at least three, equivalently in its convention when it is not a disjoint union of simple closed geodesics; this introduces a subsequent topic rather than an added hypothesis of Question 11.1.1.

The supplied special-case claim assumes an effective isometric $S^1$-action on $(S^2,g)$, exactly two fixed poles, and free action elsewhere. In meridian coordinates, write \[ g={\,\mathrm{d}} r^2+a(r)^2{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta^2,\qquad 0<r<L,\quad \theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}), \] with the circles at $r=0,L$ collapsed smoothly to the poles. For every phase $\theta_0$, the three meridians at \[ \theta_0,\quad\theta_0+2\pi/3,\quad\theta_0+4\pi/3 \] are reported to form an embedded unit-weight stationary $\Theta$-graph: all three edges have length $L$, and the outgoing angles at both poles are exactly $120$ degrees. The distinct graphs in this construction are parametrized by $S^1/C_3$, where $C_3$ acts by phase addition of $2\pi/3$. No curvature-sign assumption is used in this claim.

The aid also supplies the family \[ g_\epsilon={\,\mathrm{d}} r^2+(\sin r+\epsilon\sin^3r)^2{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta^2, \qquad 0\le r\le\pi, \] with smooth pole collapse. It singles out $\epsilon=1/4$ as a smooth example with sign-changing Gaussian curvature; every graph above then has total length $3\pi$. The record does not specify the full parameter range for its family or for curvature sign change, and no such range is asserted here. These symmetry-based examples do not settle the question for arbitrary metrics.

Short English statement

The general question asks for a stationary theta-shaped geodesic network on every Riemannian two-sphere. The supplied aid constructs a continuous family using three equally spaced meridians when the metric has the stated circle symmetry, including a particular metric with curvature of both signs. Its reported open status and special-case proof require independent review.
