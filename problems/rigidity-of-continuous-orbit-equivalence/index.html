Rigidity of Continuous Orbit Equivalence

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ and $H$ be countable groups acting by homeomorphisms on compact topological spaces $X$ and $Y$, respectively. For the following formulation, give the groups the discrete topology. Call an action topologically free if \[ \operatorname{int}_X\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\}=\varnothing \qquad(g\in G\setminus\{e_G\}), \] and impose the analogous condition on $H\curvearrowright Y$. The source does not specify separation or metrizability assumptions on the spaces, or its precise convention for topological freeness; these remain qualifications requiring review.

Using the continuous-transfer-map convention, the actions are continuously orbit equivalent if there exist a homeomorphism $h:X\to Y$ and continuous maps \[ a:G\times X\to H, \qquad b:H\times Y\to G \] such that \[ h(g\cdot x)=a(g,x)\cdot h(x), \qquad h^{-1}(s\cdot y)=b(s,y)\cdot h^{-1}(y) \] for every $g\in G$, $x\in X$, $s\in H$, and $y\in Y$. In particular, $h(G\cdot x)=H\cdot h(x)$ for every $x\in X$.

One possible meaning of conjugacy for actions of possibly different groups is the existence of a group isomorphism $\theta:G\to H$ and a homeomorphism $k:X\to Y$ satisfying \[ k(g\cdot x)=\theta(g)\cdot k(x) \qquad(g\in G,\ x\in X). \] The source does not resolve whether this meaning is intended, whether the groups must instead coincide and $\theta$ must be the identity, or whether the given orbit-equivalence homeomorphism itself must implement conjugacy.

Problem, subject to these unresolved conventions. Characterize the hypotheses on pairs of topologically free actions of countable groups on compact spaces under which continuous orbit equivalence implies conjugacy. With conjugacy interpreted through a group isomorphism as above, the implication to be characterized is \[ \left(\exists\,h,a,b\text{ satisfying the continuous orbit equivalence conditions}\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ \left(\exists\,\theta,k\text{ satisfying the conjugacy conditions}\right). \] In particular, the supplied statement asks for hypotheses beyond a described known duality-group-versus-solvable-group criterion that exclude the described counterexamples for higher-rank free abelian groups and nonabelian free groups. The record supplies neither the exact criterion nor the counterexample constructions or their action hypotheses. Consequently, this refinement cannot be given a precise theorem-level formulation from the supplied evidence alone. The original wording refers to the relevant actions without specifying that class; the displayed formulation records a classification question, not an assertion that the implication holds for all such actions.

Short English statement

Determine which hypotheses force continuously orbit equivalent topologically free actions of countable groups on compact spaces to be conjugate. The supplied record particularly asks for conditions beyond a cited duality-group-versus-solvable-group criterion that exclude described free abelian and nonabelian free group counterexamples. The intended action class, precise meaning of conjugacy, and hypotheses of that criterion require clarification from the cited source.
