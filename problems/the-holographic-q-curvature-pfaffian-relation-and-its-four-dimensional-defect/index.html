The holographic Q-curvature–Pfaffian relation and its four-dimensional defect

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks for an explicit relation between Q-curvature and the curvature Pfaffian in the conformally flat case. Dimension, Laplacian sign and Pfaffian normalization are not stated. The aid reports the Graham--Juhl holographic formula as a solution in every even dimension; the following fixes the conventions needed for that relation.

For $n=2m\geq4$, use $\Delta=\nabla^i\nabla_i$, \[ J=\frac{R}{2(n-1)},\qquad P=\frac1{n-2}(\operatorname{Ric}-Jg),\qquad c_m=\frac{(-1)^m}{2^{2m}m!(m-1)!}. \] Here $P$ is the Schouten tensor and $\sigma_j(P)$ is the elementary symmetric function of its endomorphism eigenvalues. In the locally conformally flat case use the formal Poincaré metric \[ g_+=r^{-2}({\,\mathrm{d}} r^2+g_r),\qquad g_r(\cdot,\cdot)=g((I-r^2P/2)\cdot,(I-r^2P/2)\cdot). \] Write its volume ratio as \[ v(r)=\left(\frac{\det g_r}{\det g}\right)^{1/2} =\sum_{j=0}^m v_{2j}r^{2j},\qquad v_{2j}=(-1)^j2^{-j}\sigma_j(P). \] Define the harmonic-extension operators $p_{2k}$, $1\leq k<m$, by the formal expansion solving $\Delta_{g_+}u=O(r^n)$ to these orders, \[ u=f+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{2k}p_{2k}f+\text{higher terms}, \] and let $p_{2k}^*$ denote their formal adjoints for ${\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g$. In these conventions the reported holographic identity is \[ 2n c_m Q_n=n v_n+ \sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(n-2k)p_{2k}^*(v_{n-2k}). \] Normalize the scalar Pfaffian in this conformally flat setting by $\operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}}=m!\sigma_m(P)$. Substitution gives the explicit relation \[ Q_n=2^{m-1}(m-1)!\operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}} +\frac1{2n c_m}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1} (n-2k)p_{2k}^*(v_{n-2k}). \] The sum is a natural divergence: the extension of a constant is constant, so $p_{2k}1=0$, and formal adjoints express these terms as divergences. Its integral vanishes on a closed manifold; boundary terms must be retained otherwise. In dimension two the corresponding normalization is simply $Q_2=\operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}}=K$.

The aid also gives a four-dimensional normalization test valid without conformal flatness. Set \[ E_4=|\operatorname{Rm}|^2-4|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+R^2, \qquad Q_4=-\tfrac16\Delta R-\tfrac12|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+\tfrac16R^2. \] Then \[ E_4-4Q_4-\tfrac23\Delta R=|W|^2. \] On a locally conformally flat four-manifold this yields \[ E_4=4Q_4+\tfrac23\Delta R, \qquad \operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}}=E_4/8, \qquad Q_4=2\operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}}-\tfrac16\Delta R. \] The aid's workshop normalization is $E_{\mathrm{AIM}}=4E_4=32\operatorname{Pff}_{\mathrm{GJ}}$. These are scalar normalizations before multiplying by volume density, not interchangeable normalized Euler forms. Since $\int E_4\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}=32\pi^2\chi(M)$, a closed locally conformally flat four-manifold satisfies \[ \int_MQ_4\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}=8\pi^2\chi(M). \] Vanishing of the defect at a point says only $W=0$ at that point; vanishing throughout an open set is equivalent there to local conformal flatness. The relation is modulo an explicit divergence, not generally pointwise proportionality of Q-curvature and the Pfaffian.

Short English statement

The reported holographic formula writes Q-curvature as a fixed multiple of the Pfaffian plus an explicit divergence in even-dimensional conformally flat geometry. In dimension four the remaining defect is exactly $|W|^2$, and the integral identity on a closed conformally flat manifold follows with the stated normalization.
