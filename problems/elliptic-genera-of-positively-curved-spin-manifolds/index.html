Math-766

Elliptic Genera of Positively Curved Spin Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A closed manifold is a compact smooth manifold without boundary. Let $(M,g)$ be a Riemannian manifold equipped with a spin structure. The condition $\operatorname{sec}(M,g)>0$ means that every two-dimensional tangent plane at every point of $M$ has strictly positive sectional curvature.

For an oriented closed manifold of dimension $4k$, its signature $\operatorname{sign}(M)$ is the signature of the symmetric intersection pairing \[ H^{2k}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\times H^{2k}(M;{\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}, \qquad (a,b)\longmapsto\langle a\smile b,[M]\rangle. \] The spin structure supplies an orientation.

Write $\phi(M)$ for the elliptic genus intended by the source. The supplied record does not specify its construction, normalization, or modular group, so this notation retains an unresolved source-dependent meaning.

Source question. For every closed Riemannian spin manifold $(M,g)$ satisfying $\operatorname{sec}(M,g)>0$, is its elliptic genus constant as a modular function, with constant value equal to its signature; that is, \[ \phi(M)\equiv\operatorname{sign}(M)? \] Here the equality asks for an identity of modular functions, not merely equality at a particular modular parameter.

Unresolved qualifications. The source imposes no dimension restriction. The signature definition above applies in dimensions divisible by four; a convention for other dimensions, or an intended restriction to dimensions $4k$, must be confirmed. No such restriction is silently imposed. The precise elliptic genus and its normalization must also be confirmed before this question has a fully specified mathematical interpretation.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every closed spin manifold with strictly positive sectional curvature has an elliptic genus that is constant and equal to its signature. The intended genus, its normalization, and the treatment of dimensions not divisible by four remain unspecified. The supplied curation labels the problem partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
