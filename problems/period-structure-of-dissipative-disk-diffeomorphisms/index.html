Period Structure of Dissipative Disk Diffeomorphisms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D$ denote the disk appearing in the source, and let $f\colon D\to D$ be a map in the source's class of dissipative disk diffeomorphisms. The precise meaning of this class remains unresolved: the record specifies neither the regularity, the open or closed disk convention, boundary conditions, whether the map is onto or is an embedding into the disk, nor the meaning of dissipativity.

Write ${\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$ and ${\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. Define the set of least periods by \[ \operatorname{Per}(f)=\left\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\exists x\in D,\ f^p(x)=x\ \text{and}\ f^j(x)\ne x\ \text{for every }1\leq j<p\right\}, \] where $f^j$ is the $j$-fold iterate of $f$.

The supplied question asks whether every such $f$ satisfies both \[ \exists n_0\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\qquad \operatorname{Per}(f)\subseteq \{n2^k:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\ n\leq n_0,\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\}, \] and \[ \operatorname{Per}(f)\text{ is infinite} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\qquad \{n2^k:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Per}(f). \] Here $n_0$ and $n$ may depend on $f$. The exponent convention is made explicit as $k\geq0$; the source uses $\mathbb N$ without defining it. Allowing or excluding zero does not change these existential assertions after adjusting the positive multipliers or their bound.

Unresolved scope. No entropy hypothesis is included in either supplied formulation. The cited literature is labeled "zero entropy," so the relationship between its hypotheses and the displayed question requires source review. No zero-entropy or mild-dissipativity assumption has been added to the question. Until the intended class of maps is specified, this is a conditional formalization of the supplied wording.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the least periods of every dissipative disk diffeomorphism come from finitely many sequences obtained by repeated doubling, and whether infinitely many distinct periods force one complete doubling sequence. The intended class of diffeomorphisms and any missing entropy hypothesis require clarification from the sources.
