Definitions and mathematical statement

Definitions and notation.
A hyper-Kähler metric has three parallel complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations. In four dimensions a gravitational instanton is a complete noncompact Ricci-flat geometry in the source’s self-dual setting. ALE and ALF describe different asymptotic geometries; ALF has an asymptotic circle direction over a three-dimensional base. A toric hyper-Kähler quotient is obtained by hyper-Kähler reduction of a flat quaternionic vector space by a torus, using specified moment-map levels. Asymptotics describe the metric, curvature, volume and degeneration of ends as distance tends to infinity. Gibbons--Hawking metrics form a special four-dimensional class, not the whole family of such quotients.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
As a first step toward systematic understanding of the Hodge cohomology of toric hyper-Kähler quotients, understand their asymptotic geometry, beginning with the Bielawski--Dancer and Gibbons--Rychenkova works named by the source.
Scope and source qualification.
