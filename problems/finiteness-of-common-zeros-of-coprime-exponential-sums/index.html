Math-3657

Finiteness of Common Zeros of Coprime Exponential Sums

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks whether two coprime one-variable exponential sums have finitely many common zeros. It does not specify the coefficient field, the domain of the variable, or the ring in which divisibility is taken. The following is an explicitly provisional complex-analytic formulation requiring mathematical review.

Let \[ \mathcal E=\left\{z\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j e^{\lambda_j z}:m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\ a_j,\lambda_j\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}, \] where the empty sum is zero and the functions are defined on ${\mathbb{C}}$. Regard $\mathcal E$ as a ring under pointwise operations. A function $d\in\mathcal E$ divides $f\in\mathcal E$ if $f=dh$ for some $h\in\mathcal E$. A unit is a function $d\in\mathcal E$ for which $dv=1$ for some $v\in\mathcal E$.

Under these provisional conventions, the proposed statement is \[ \forall f,g\in\mathcal E,\qquad \left[\text{every common divisor of }f\text{ and }g\text{ in }\mathcal E\text{ is a unit}\right] \Longrightarrow \#\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:f(z)=g(z)=0\}<\infty. \] The supplied clean statement says "no nonconstant common divisor," whereas the original statement says "no nontrivial common divisor." These expressions require reconciliation: in $\mathcal E$, every $e^{\lambda z}$ is a unit, including nonconstant ones, and divides every element. Thus the literal exclusion of nonconstant common divisors cannot express coprimality in this ring. The formulation above interprets "nontrivial" as "nonunit"; that interpretation and the definition of exponential sum remain unverified.

The original record also mentions finiteness of $(A\setminus A')\cap G$ under unspecified finite-rank and anomalous-subvariety hypotheses. Its supplied statement-verification note assigns that separate higher-dimensional Mordell--Lang generalization to OWR-12985-004; it is therefore not included in the proposed statement for this record.

Short English statement

Under an explicitly provisional interpretation using finite sums of complex exponentials on ${\mathbb{C}}$, the conjecture asks whether two functions with no common nonunit exponential-sum divisor have only finitely many common zeros. The domain and divisor convention need review. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
