Math-835

Tame Fontaine–Mazur Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual number-field formulation, let $K$ be a number field, $p$ a prime and $S$ a finite set of finite places of $K$ with \[ S\cap\{v:v\mid p\}=\varnothing. \] Let $K_S$ be the maximal algebraic extension unramified at finite places outside $S$, and put $G_{K,S}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_S/K)$ with its profinite topology. Archimedean-place conventions and whether the source instead uses a maximal pro-$p$ extension are not explicit; the group must be matched to the report rather than silently switching the two definitions. Finiteness of $S$, omitted in the short formula, is supported by the supplied finite-tame-place assessment and is essential to the usual conjecture's scope.

The tame Fontaine--Mazur assertion is \[ \forall n\geq1\ \forall\text{ continuous homomorphisms } \rho:G_{K,S}\to\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{Z}}_p):\qquad |\rho(G_{K,S})|<\infty. \] Continuity uses the $p$-adic topology on the target. The finite image may depend on the representation; no uniform bound on its order or a fixed dimension is asserted. The question ranges over the intended number fields, primes and finite allowed sets, not just known special classes.

The equivalent source formulation excludes an infinite $p$-adic analytic quotient: no continuous quotient of $G_{K,S}$ is an infinite compact $p$-adic Lie group. Compactness is relevant because the domain is profinite. The usual equivalence uses the closed-subgroup/linearity facts for compact $p$-adic analytic groups; it is not a statement about arbitrary noncompact Lie-group quotients. If $G_{K,S}$ denotes the maximal pro-$p$ Galois group instead, the representations and quotients are correspondingly in that source setting, and identification with the full-group formulation requires its own conventions.

The literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports many special cases and criteria for finite sets of tame places, but no proof for all number fields and representations. The recent 2026 reference is retained as supplied; no independent current-status or source verification is claimed.

Short English statement

If ramification is allowed at only finitely many places away from $p$, must every continuous integral $p$-adic representation of the specified Galois group have finite image? Equivalently, must it lack infinite compact $p$-adic analytic quotients? The source's full-versus-pro-$p$ group convention needs confirmation.
