Kourovka Notebook Problem 5.38

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is finitely generated if $G=\langle S\rangle$ for some finite subset $S\subseteq G$. Define its derived series by $G^{(0)}=G$ and \[ G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]\qquad(n\geq 0), \] where $[H,H]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in H$. The group $G$ is soluble if $G^{(d)}=\{1_G\}$ for some integer $d\geq 0$. It is Hopfian if every surjective group homomorphism $G\to G$ is injective, and hence an automorphism.

The direct product $A\times B$ has multiplication $(a,b)(a',b')=(aa',bb')$.

Question (P. M. Neumann, 1976; Kourovka Notebook Problem 5.38). For every pair of finitely generated soluble Hopfian groups $A$ and $B$, is $A\times B$ Hopfian? Equivalently, must every surjective group homomorphism \[ \varphi:A\times B\longrightarrow A\times B \] be injective?

The supplied registry labels this problem open. Its accompanying discussion reports an affirmative answer only as an unverified AI solution claim attributed to Achyuth Jayadevan. That claim is not treated here as an established resolution.

Short English statement

If two groups are each finitely generated, soluble, and Hopfian, must their direct product also be Hopfian? The supplied source reports an unverified affirmative solution claim while retaining an open registry status.
