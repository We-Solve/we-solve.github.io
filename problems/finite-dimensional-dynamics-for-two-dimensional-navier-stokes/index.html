Math-387

Finite-dimensional dynamics for two-dimensional Navier–Stokes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The usual two-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes model is \[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+f,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad \nu>0, \] with velocity $u$, pressure $p$, an appropriate phase space, and specified spatial domain and boundary conditions. The source says "periodically forced" but does not state whether this refers to time-periodic forcing, spatial periodicity, or both, and supplies no domain, regularity, normalization, or boundary data.

For a specified autonomous semiflow $S(t)$ on a phase space $H$, a global attractor $\mathcal A$ is a compact invariant set attracting bounded subsets of $H$. A concrete finite-dimensional smooth-dynamics version of the first question asks whether there are a finite $N$, a smooth vector field $V$ on a neighborhood of a compact invariant set $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^N$, its flow $\Phi_t$ on $K$, and a homeomorphism $h:\mathcal A\to K$ such that \[ h(S(t)u)=\Phi_t(h(u))\qquad(u\in\mathcal A,\ t\ge0). \] This preserves the time parameter. The source asks for conjugacy to a smooth finite-dimensional system; it does not explicitly demand that $h$ itself be smooth. Its research summary says "smoothly conjugate", which is a potentially stronger assertion and requires a specified smooth structure and regularity convention. Neither version is silently substituted for the other.

If $f(x,t+T)=f(x,t)$ is the intended forcing, the evolution is nonautonomous before a phase is included. One may study the autonomous extension with $\dot\theta=1$ on ${\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}$ and forcing $f(x,\theta)$, or the time-$T$ return map. These have different attractor and conjugacy formulations; the source does not select one. The displayed autonomous conjugacy is consequently a template conditional on this choice, not an assumption that a nonautonomous equation already defines $S(t)$ on velocity space alone.

The second question asks whether transient dynamics can be described or controlled in finite-dimensional terms. It does not specify exact versus approximate reduction, the initial-data class, a tracking or error estimate, time horizon, admissible controls, or the required notion of finite-dimensional state. A statement about dynamics on $\mathcal A$ alone does not automatically address trajectories away from it. No inertial-manifold, determining-coordinate, or control theorem is inserted as an equivalent question without a source specification.

The supplied research summary labels both finite-dimensional conjugacy and transient reducibility open. This is an attributed triage assessment, not independent verification of a precise formulation.

Short English statement

Can the attractor dynamics of the stated two-dimensional Navier--Stokes system be represented exactly by a smooth finite-dimensional system, and can finite-dimensional methods also capture or control transients? The forcing convention, attractor model, regularity of conjugacy, and meaning of transient reduction are missing. The supplied summary labels the program open.
