Math-1619

Evans diagnostics for primary and secondary cubic-autocatalysis front instability

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The intended question is S. Malham's cubic-autocatalysis front problem. Its setup appears in the preceding companion record: \[ u_t=\delta\Delta u-uv^2,\qquad v_t=\Delta v+uv^2,\qquad\delta>0. \] Let $U(\xi)=(u(\xi),v(\xi))$, $\xi=x-ct$, be a planar front joining prescribed reaction equilibria. The source asks for its stability properties and an Evans-function description of the secondary instability of the cellular front produced after the primary planar instability. The subsequent M. Haragus KP-I material belongs to a separate problem. The companion source describes complex fronts around $\delta\sim8$ and the suggested occurrence of spatiotemporal chaos with two length scales, called biscale chaos. These are descriptions and questions, not rigorous stability conclusions.

The aid reports a numerical primary threshold near $\delta=2.300$ and subsequent numerical multidimensional Evans calculations for secondary cellular-front instability, while leaving rigorous biscale-chaos stability open. No numerical values here are promoted to exact thresholds or a nonlinear theorem.

For a conditional analytic diagnostic put $D=\operatorname{diag}(\delta,1)$ and $F(u,v)=(-uv^2,uv^2)$. The longitudinal linearization in the traveling frame is \[ L_0=D\partial_\xi^2+c\partial_\xi+DF(U(\xi)), \qquad L(k)=L_0-k^2D, \] where $DF$ is the reaction Jacobian and $k$ a real transverse frequency. Assume a specified closed Fredholm realization of $L_0$ has an isolated algebraically simple eigenvalue zero, with right eigenvector $U'$ and an admissible dual eigenvector $\psi$ satisfying $p=\langle\psi,U'\rangle\ne0$. These assumptions are additional and may fail in an unweighted space. Define \[ P w=U'\frac{\langle\psi,w\rangle}{p},\quad Q=I-P, \quad S=(L_0|_{\operatorname{Ran}Q})^{-1} \text{ on }\operatorname{Ran}Q. \] The reported translation eigenvalue expansion is \[ \lambda(k)=-d_{\mathrm{eff}}k^2+\lambda_4k^4+O(k^6), \quad d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\langle\psi,DU'\rangle}{p}, \quad\lambda_4=-\frac{\langle\psi,DSQDU'\rangle}{p}. \] If an analytic Evans function $E(\lambda,\rho)$ represents this simple branch with $\rho=k^2$, $E(0,0)=0$ and $E_\lambda(0,0)\ne0$, the corresponding derivatives at $(0,0)$ give \[ d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{E_\rho}{E_\lambda},\qquad \lambda_4=-\frac{E_{\rho\rho}-2d_{\mathrm{eff}}E_{\lambda\rho} +d_{\mathrm{eff}}^2E_{\lambda\lambda}} {2E_\lambda}. \] For equal diffusion $\delta=1$ the exact shift is $L(k)=L_0-k^2I$ and $\lambda(k)=-k^2$, so $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ and $\lambda_4=0$.

The reaction conserves $u+v$, so the constant vector $(1,1)$ solves the formal adjoint zero equation. For matching total end states, however, \[ \langle(1,1),U'\rangle =[u+v]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}=0. \] It therefore cannot serve as the normalized dual translation vector in the preceding formulas; its membership in an actual adjoint domain also requires checking.

For a front periodic in $y$ with period $L$, a Bloch--Evans analysis must examine all exponents $\theta\in[-\pi/L,\pi/L]$, using perturbations $e^{i\theta y}w(\xi,y)$ with periodic $w$, and account separately for essential spectrum and neutral symmetries. A small-$\theta$ spectral scale is not by itself a proof of nonlinear mode mixing or biscale chaos. The aid supplies this conditional framework, not the required full rigorous stability analysis.

Short English statement

Study primary and secondary stability of cubic-autocatalysis fronts and construct the appropriate multidimensional Evans function. The aid gives conditional translation-branch formulas, an equal-diffusion check and an adjoint-pairing warning; it does not prove nonlinear biscale chaos.
