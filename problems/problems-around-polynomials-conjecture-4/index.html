Problems Around Polynomials — Conjecture 4

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

For positive integers $k,\ell$, let $\widetilde\sharp(2k,\ell)$ be the largest possible number of isolated real zeros of a polynomial $p\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_\ell]$ of total degree exactly $2k$ admitting a representation \[p=\sum_{j=1}^m q_j^2,\qquad m\geq1,\quad\deg q_j\leq k.\] Such a polynomial is nonnegative on $\mathbb R^\ell$. A zero $x$ is isolated if some neighborhood of $x$ contains no other zero; distinct points are counted once.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Is it true for every $k,\ell\geq1$ that \[\widetilde\sharp(2k,\ell)=k^\ell?\] [S2]

Short English statement

Can a degree-2k sum-of-squares polynomial in any number of variables have more than k to that number of isolated real zeros?
