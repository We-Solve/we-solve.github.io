Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A convex body is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. An SOS density $q$ has nonnegative values and is normalized by $\int_Kq\,d\mu=1$; its objective is $\int_Kp q\,d\mu$, minimizing over the degree bound.

A polynomial is a sum of squares (SOS) if it is $\sum_j q_j^2$ for polynomials $q_j$. Moment-SOS hierarchies impose positivity through increasingly large truncated moment matrices and corresponding degree-bounded SOS certificates.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $p_{\min}$ be the minimum of a polynomial $p$ on a convex body $K$, and let $p(r)$ be the degree-$2r$ sum-of-squares-density upper bound obtained from a reference measure on $K$. What is the sharp asymptotic rate of $p(r)-p_{\min}$ for general convex bodies? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The reference measure, polynomial class, body regularity, and uniformity of the asymptotic rate require the source.

Literature and context check

The report's Laurent contribution, pp. 2991--2993, distinguishes Lebesgue and Chebyshev reference measures and describes the eigenvalue route. The following computations rederive an interval instance and test a simple concentration ansatz; they do not establish the sharp rate for general convex bodies.

Research attempt: A density family that stalls and an exact Chebyshev calculation

First take $K=[-1,1]$, Lebesgue reference measure, and objective $p(x)=x+1$ with minimum zero. The single-square density proportional to $(1-x)^{2r}$ is admissible at degree $2r$. With $t=(1-x)/2$, its expectation of $p$ is \[ \frac{\int_0^1 2(1-t)t^{2r}\,dt}{\int_0^1t^{2r}\,dt} =2\left(1-\frac{2r+1}{2r+2}\right)=\frac1{r+1}. \] Thus this particular family gives exactly order $1/r$; analyzing it more tightly cannot prove a faster rate for that same family. This is not a lower bound on the optimum over all admissible densities.

Now change the reference measure explicitly to the Chebyshev probability measure $dx/(\pi\sqrt{1-x^2})$. With $x=\cos\theta$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$, an orthonormal polynomial basis is $b_0=1$, $b_j=\sqrt2\cos(j\theta)$ for $j\ge1$. Orthogonality follows by integrating the identity $2\cos(j\theta)\cos(k\theta)=\cos((j-k)\theta)+\cos((j+k)\theta)$; all nonconstant integer-frequency cosines integrate to zero.

For degree-at-most-$r$ polynomials, multiplication by $x$ followed by orthogonal projection has a symmetric matrix $J_r$ with zero diagonal, first off-diagonal entry $1/\sqrt2$, and subsequent off-diagonal entries $1/2$. This follows from $\cos\theta\cos(j\theta)=(\cos((j+1)\theta)+\cos((j-1)\theta))/2$ and the normalization of $b_0$. For $r\ge1$, at each \[ \theta_k=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2(r+1)},\quad 0\le k\le r, \] the vector $(1,\sqrt2\cos\theta_k,\ldots,\sqrt2\cos(r\theta_k))$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\cos\theta_k$. The first row gives $\cos\theta_k$, the second row uses $1+\cos(2\theta)=2\cos^2\theta$, the interior rows use the recurrence, and the last row uses $\cos((r+1)\theta_k)=0$. The eigenvalues are distinct; symmetry makes the corresponding eigenvectors orthogonal, since $\lambda u^Tv=(J_ru)^Tv=u^TJ_rv=\mu u^Tv$. There are $r+1$ nonzero orthogonal vectors, so they form a basis. Expanding any coefficient vector in that basis shows its Rayleigh quotient is a weighted average of these eigenvalues.

For any sum-of-squares density $h=\sum_\ell q_\ell^2$ of degree at most $2r$, each $q_\ell$ may be taken of degree at most $r$: any higher leading degree would have a strictly positive sum of squared leading coefficients and could not cancel. Its normalized objective integral is a weighted average of the Rayleigh quotients for $I+J_r$. The minimum is therefore achieved by a single eigenvector square and equals \[ p(r)=1-\cos\frac{\pi}{2(r+1)}. \] For $r=0$, the only densities are constant and the same formula gives one. Finally $1-\cos t=2\sin^2(t/2)$ is between $2t^2/\pi^2$ and $t^2/2$ for $0\le t\le\pi/2$, using the chord lower bound and tangent upper bound for sine. Hence this chosen measure gives an exact $\Theta(r^{-2})$ rate.

Verification and adversarial audit

Both measure normalizations and the degree of the density are explicit. The tridiagonal eigenvector calculation includes the exceptional first off-diagonal normalization and checks $r=0$ separately. The order-$1/r$ value is only the performance of one Lebesgue-density ansatz; it is not misused as a universal lower bound.
Audit corrections.
Changing the reference measure is announced explicitly. An ansatz's slow convergence is distinguished from slow convergence of the optimized hierarchy.

Scope and remaining gaps

The exact one-dimensional Chebyshev calculation is known material rederived from first principles. Neither it nor the failed Lebesgue ansatz determines the sharp rate for general convex bodies with their specified reference measures and uniformity requirements.
