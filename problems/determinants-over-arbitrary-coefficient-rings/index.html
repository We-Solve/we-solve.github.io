Math-1683

Determinants over arbitrary coefficient rings

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source problem and recovered antecedent. In Section 8, "Kisin: Pseudo-representations", recast dimension-$d$ trace pseudocharacter theory without assuming $(d!)^{-1}\in A$. The source warns that this is not merely a question about divided powers. The preceding setup, supplied by the verification, has a group $G$, commutative coefficient ring $A$, or more generally a unital $A$-algebra $R$. The trailing "8.2" is a neighboring section marker, not an additional question.

Reported replacement by determinant laws. For arbitrary commutative unital $A$, a degree-$d$ determinant is a multiplicative homogeneous polynomial law $D:R\to A$. Concretely it is a family of maps \[ D_B:R\otimes_A B\to B \] natural in every commutative $A$-algebra $B$, with $D_B(1)=1$, $D_B(xy)=D_B(x)D_B(y)$, and $D_B(bx)=b^dD_B(x)$, in the polynomial-law sense. The characteristic coefficients are specified by \[ D_{A[t]}(t1-r)=\sum_{i=0}^d(-1)^i\Lambda_i(r)t^{d-i}, \qquad\Lambda_0=1,\quad\Lambda_d=D. \] The trace is $\Lambda_1$, and the associated universal Cayley--Hamilton quotient enforces these characteristic equations. Naturality under scalar extension and multiplicativity, not merely a trace with formal divided powers, are essential.

The aid attributes this arbitrary-ring framework to Chenevier and exact agreement with Taylor trace pseudocharacters when $d!$ is invertible to Ophir's theorem. The full references and hypotheses are not supplied. The framework is not a claim that every determinant over every ring is realized by an actual matrix representation over that same ring without further hypotheses. For group problems one applies it to $R=A[G]$; continuity data require additional specification if $G,A$ are topological.

Reported degree-two trace fiber. Fix a degree-two determinant $D$ with trace $T$. The aid reports that another determinant with the same trace is exactly $D+q$, where $q$ is a normalized additive homogeneous quadratic polynomial law with $q(1)=0$, $2q=0$, and \[ q(xy)=D(x)q(y)+D(y)q(x)+q(x)q(y). \] All these identities are required naturally after every commutative base change $B$, for all $x,y\in R\otimes_A B$; pointwise identities only on $R$ are not substituted for polynomial-law identities. Conversely such a $q$ gives a determinant with unchanged trace. This describes a possible nontrivial trace fiber in small characteristic rather than identifying trace and determinant data universally.

Explicit reported-type characteristic-two obstruction. Let $A={\mathbb{F}}_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ and $C_4=\langle g:g^4=1\rangle$. One concrete realization uses \[ \rho_0(g)=I_2,\qquad \rho_1(g)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\\epsilon&1\end{pmatrix}. \] The second matrix squares to $(1+\epsilon)I_2$, hence has fourth power $I_2$. Both representations have trace zero on every group element, and therefore identical trace laws on the group algebra, but \[ \det\rho_0(g)=1,\qquad\det\rho_1(g)=1+\epsilon. \] Their determinant polynomial laws are different. Thus even full trace data can lose infinitesimal deformation information when $2!$ is not invertible. The example supports the need for the determinant-law replacement, not a failure of that replacement. All supplied framework, equivalence and fiber assertions require independent verification.

Short English statement

The aid reports determinant polynomial laws as the arbitrary-ring replacement for factorial-dependent trace pseudocharacters. In degree two a normalized quadratic torsion law can change the determinant without changing the trace. Two dual-number representations of $C_4$ make that loss of deformation data explicit.
