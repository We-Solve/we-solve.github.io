Math-1310

A Bailey-Chebyshev-Bloch dictionary and terminal-choice blindness

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks for a dictionary between Bailey-pair methods, Bloch groups and orthogonal polynomials, possibly useful for torsion computations. In a standard convention a Bailey pair relative to $a$ satisfies \[ \beta_n=\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{\alpha_j}{(q;q)_{n-j}(aq;q)_{n+j}}, \qquad (b;q)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-bq^j). \] The source does not choose a Bailey family, field or Bloch-group convention. For a field $K$, take the ordinary Bloch group $\mathcal B(K)$ as the kernel of the map $[x]\mapsto x\wedge(1-x)$ from the pre-Bloch group (symbols modulo five-term relations) to $K^*\wedge K^*$. Torsion means a class annihilated by a nonzero integer; this does not specify its exact order.

The supplied partial dictionary concerns the standard rank-$r$ Andrews--Gordon Bailey-chain family, $r\ge1$, whose Nahm quadratic matrix is \[ A_{ij}=2\min(i,j)\qquad(1\le i,j\le r). \] The corresponding sums have the form $\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}^r}q^{n^{\mathsf T}An/2+B^{\mathsf T}n+C}/\prod_i(q;q)_{n_i}$, with terminal choices affecting the lower terms. The report gives the unique positive saddle in $(0,1)^r$, satisfying $1-x_i=\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}$, as \[ c=\cos\frac\pi{2r+3},\qquad x_i=\frac{U_{i-1}(c)U_{i+1}(c)}{U_i(c)^2}. \] Here $U_0(t)=1$, $U_1(t)=2t$, $U_{j+1}(t)=2tU_j(t)-U_{j-1}(t)$ are Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind. The reported distinguished class \[ \Xi_r=\sum_{i=1}^r[x_i]\in\mathcal B({\mathbb{Q}}(c)) \] is torsion over the totally real field ${\mathbb{Q}}(c)$. No order or explicit annihilating integer is supplied.

For fixed $r$, the report asserts that the $r+1$ Andrews--Gordon terminal companions have distinct linear terms and product residue classes but the same saddle and same ordinary Bloch class $\Xi_r$. Thus a dictionary whose only output is this ordinary class necessarily loses terminal-choice information. It does not identify the companions as the same function or rule out a richer dictionary retaining linear terms or additional data. Their individual linear vectors, product formulas and a general orthogonal-polynomial/Bloch correspondence are absent. The explicit Chebyshev coordinates provide one special-family link, rather than the full dictionary sought by the source.

Short English statement

Develop a Bailey-pair, orthogonal-polynomial and Bloch-group dictionary. The supplied report gives Chebyshev coordinates for a torsion Bloch class in an Andrews--Gordon family and shows that this ordinary class forgets which terminal companion was chosen. It does not compute all torsion orders or provide a general dictionary.
