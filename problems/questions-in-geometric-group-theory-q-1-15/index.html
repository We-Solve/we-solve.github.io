Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Choose a finite symmetric generating set $S$ for a group $G$. Give its Cayley graph unit-length edges and the induced path metric. The group is word-hyperbolic if there is a number $\delta\geq0$ such that each side of every geodesic triangle lies within distance $\delta$ of the other two sides. Changing the finite generating set does not change whether this property holds.

A group is residually finite if each nonidentity element survives in some finite quotient: \[ \forall g\in G\setminus\{1\}\ \exists Q\text{ finite}\ \exists\phi:G\to Q\text{ a homomorphism},\quad\phi(g)\ne1. \] The target may be replaced by the image of $\phi$, making the homomorphism surjective. Equivalently, the intersection of all finite-index normal subgroups of $G$ is trivial.

Is every word-hyperbolic group residually finite? The finite quotient and its cardinality may depend on the element being separated; the question requests neither a uniform bound nor an efficient procedure for finding such a quotient. It includes groups with torsion as well as torsion-free groups. Hyperbolicity alone is the hypothesis: linearity, a cubical action, or membership in a particular manifold class is not assumed. A negative answer requires a hyperbolic group containing a nonidentity element killed by every homomorphism to every finite group, rather than merely a group with few small quotients.

Short English statement

Can every nonidentity element of every hyperbolic group be detected in a finite quotient?

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
There is claimed to be a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ whose finite residual is nontrivial: \[ R_f(G)=\bigcap_{\substack{Q\text{ finite}\\\phi:G\to Q}}\ker\phi\ne\{1\}. \] For this finitely generated example, the linear-obstruction corollary states \[ \forall K\text{ commutative field},\ \forall n\ge1,\ \forall\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}_n(K),\qquad R_f(G)\subseteq\ker\rho. \] Thus a single nonidentity element is annihilated by every such finite-dimensional representation.

Outline of the argument

Punctured-line blocks with few short cycles give triangular relations meeting a geometric filling criterion. Real and prime-field rank comparisons produce a persistent finite-quotient obstruction, while local factorization certificates and planar metric bounds establish hyperbolicity and exclude torsion.

Formal verification
