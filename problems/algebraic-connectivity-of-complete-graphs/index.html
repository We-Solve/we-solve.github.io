Algebraic Connectivity of Complete Graphs

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d,n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ satisfy $d\geq 3$ and $n\geq d+1$. Let $K_n$ be the complete simple graph on $n$ vertices, and put $m=\binom{d+1}{2}$. For a real symmetric matrix $A$, write $\lambda_i(A)$ for its $i$-th smallest eigenvalue, counting multiplicity.

For a graph $G=(V,E)$ and a placement $p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^d$, the supplied excerpt uses a rigidity matrix $R(G,p)$ with $d|V|$ rows and defines its stiffness matrix by \[ L(G,p)=R(G,p)R(G,p)^{\mathsf T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d|V|\times d|V|}. \] The excerpt states the rank bound \[ \operatorname{rank}R(G,p)\leq d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2} \] and calls a framework infinitesimally rigid when equality holds. It calls a graph $d$-rigid when some placement yields an infinitesimally rigid framework, and asserts equivalence with infinitesimal rigidity at generic placements. In the present range $|V|=n\geq d+1$, the displayed bound makes $\lambda_{m+1}(L(G,p))$ the eigenvalue immediately following the guaranteed $m$ zero eigenvalues.

The source attributes to Jordán and Tanigawa the definition \[ a_d(G)=\sup_{p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^d}\lambda_{m+1}\left(L(G,p)\right). \] Unresolved definition. The supplied excerpt does not give the entries or normalization of $R(G,p)$. Its displayed supremum ranges over all maps, but it does not explain whether coincident vertex positions are admissible or how the rigidity matrix is defined there. The separate observation about dimension one assumes an injective placement and identifies the rigidity matrix with an incidence matrix; this does not supply the missing higher-dimensional definition. Thus the formula above retains the source's notation and quantifier without supplying an unsupported normalization or placement restriction.

Requested conjecture, conditional on recovering those conventions. With $a_d$ interpreted exactly as intended in the source, establish \[ \forall d\in{\mathbb{N}}\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\qquad \left(d\geq3\ \text{and}\ n\geq d+1\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ a_d(K_n)\leq\frac nd. \] This is the explicit target in the supplied curated statement. The original excerpt instead says that a displayed lower bound is conjectured to be essentially tight; that qualitative assertion alone does not specify the exact upper bound above. Moreover, the extracted lower bound contains the corrupted expression $a_d(K_n)\geq\text{``21 nd''}$, so its coefficient and fraction structure cannot be recovered reliably from this record alone.

Short English statement

For every integer dimension $d\geq3$ and every $n\geq d+1$, the supplied curated problem asks whether the $d$-dimensional algebraic connectivity of $K_n$ is at most $n/d$. A complete formulation requires confirmation of the rigidity matrix's normalization and its admissible placements. The supplied original excerpt expresses only conjectured essential sharpness of a lower bound.
