Math-752

Combinatorial Description of Plethysm Coefficients

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Lambda_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ be the ring of symmetric functions over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, with Schur basis $\{s_\rho\}$ indexed by partitions. For a partition $\rho$, write $|\rho|$ for its size, and let $p_r$ denote the power-sum symmetric function of degree $r$. For each integer $r\geq 1$, let $\psi_r$ be the ${\mathbb{Q}}$-algebra endomorphism of $\Lambda_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ determined by $\psi_r(p_j)=p_{rj}$. Plethysm $f[g]$ is obtained by expressing $f$ as a polynomial in the power sums and substituting $p_r\mapsto\psi_r(g)$.

For positive integers $m,n$, partitions $\lambda\vdash m$, $\mu\vdash n$, and $\nu\vdash mn$, use the convention \[ s_\lambda[s_\mu] =\sum_{\nu\vdash mn}a^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}s_\nu. \] These Schur expansion coefficients are the plethysm coefficients under this convention.

Problem. Give a general combinatorial description of $a^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}$, uniformly for all positive integers $m,n$ and all partitions $\lambda\vdash m$, $\mu\vdash n$, and $\nu\vdash mn$. A customary interpretation of this request is to specify explicit finite combinatorial objects, with rules depending on $(\lambda,\mu,\nu)$, whose number equals $a^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}$.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied excerpt does not define the coefficient notation or specify the order of the two plethysm arguments; the displayed expansion makes the convention used in this draft explicit. It also does not define what qualifies as a combinatorial description, so the counting interpretation is an interpretation of the intended target. The statement above treats positive-degree Schur functions; the source does not specify whether empty partitions or degree-zero cases are intended.

Short English statement

Find a uniform combinatorial rule for the coefficients obtained when the plethysm of two Schur functions is expanded in the Schur basis. The draft makes the argument order and positive-degree domain explicit, but these conventions and the precise meaning of a combinatorial description require confirmation from the source.
