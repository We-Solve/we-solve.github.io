Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.93

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $n\geq 2$, and let $F_n$ be a free group of rank $n$. Write $A_n=\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$. For $g\in F_n$, define the inner automorphism $c_g$ by $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$ for every $x\in F_n$, and put \[ I_n=\operatorname{Inn}(F_n)=\{c_g:g\in F_n\}\leq A_n. \] Work in the first-order language of groups $\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$. A subset $D\subseteq A_n$ is definable with parameters if there exist a nonnegative integer $m$, a tuple $\bar a\in A_n^m$, and a first-order formula $\varphi(x,\bar y)$ such that \[ \forall\alpha\in A_n,\qquad \alpha\in D\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A_n\models\varphi(\alpha,\bar a). \] Parameter-free definability requires $m=0$.

The question attributed to V. Tolstykh is: for every finite rank $n\geq 2$, is $I_n$ a first-order definable subgroup of $A_n$? The source does not specify whether parameters are permitted, so the parameter-free and parameter-allowed readings remain unresolved qualifications of this draft. No requirement that one formula work uniformly for all ranks is stated.

An element $p\in F_n$ is primitive if it belongs to some free basis of $F_n$. The source additionally states that the set \[ P_n=\{c_{p^k}:p\in F_n\text{ is primitive},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\} \] is definable in $A_n$. Here powers are interpreted as integer powers, including the zeroth power. This auxiliary definability assertion is supplied background; its parameter convention and a supporting proof or separate citation are not supplied.

Short English statement

For each finite rank $n\geq 2$, V. Tolstykh asks whether the inner automorphisms can be defined by a first-order formula inside the group $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$. The source leaves the use of parameters unspecified and reports that inner automorphisms induced by powers of primitive elements form a definable set.
