Statement

For an integer $n\ge 1$, define \[ f(n) := \left|\{k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}: 2^k < n\ \text{and}\ n-2^k\ \text{is prime}\}\right|. \] (Equivalently, $f(n)$ is the number of representations $n=p+2^k$ with $p$ prime and $k\ge 0$.)

Claim/conjecture. As $n\to\infty$, \[ f(n) = o(\log n), \] meaning: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $n_0(\varepsilon)$ such that $f(n)\le \varepsilon\log n$ for all $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)$.

Erdős Problem #236 (Round 2)

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): obstruction/gap-closure. Round 1 proved only a first-moment statement, \[ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\le N} f(n)\to \frac{1}{\log 2}, \] which implies $f(n)=o(\log n)$ for a density-$1$ set of integers but says nothing about the size of rare exceptional values. The strict next step is to control correlations among the events "$n-2^k$ is prime". I will prove a second-moment bound \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N\log\log N, \] which is substantially stronger than Round 1 and yields a quadratic tail bound for large values of $f(n)$.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 results without re-proving them.

  • Round-1 Lemma 236.1: the trivial pointwise bound $f(n)\ll \log n$.
  • Round-1 Lemma 236.2: assuming the prime number theorem, \[ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\le N} f(n)\to \frac{1}{\log 2}. \]
  • Round-1 Corollary 236.3: for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, \[ \frac{1}{N}\#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge \varepsilon \log N\}\to 0. \]

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

The new structural observation is that the differences between two powers of $2$ factor as \[ 2^j-2^k = 2^k(2^{j-k}-1). \] Thus pair correlations of the translate set $\{n-2^k\}$ reduce to prime-pair counts with differences of the very special form $2^k(2^m-1)$. Combined with the Selberg-sieve prime-pair upper bound and the classical estimate \[ \frac{m}{\phi(m)}\ll \log\log(3m), \] this yields a workable second moment.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

After Round 1, the exact gap was the lack of any control on exceptional $n$ where many of the numbers $n-2^k$ are prime simultaneously. The claim now to prove is:

Claim 236-R2. Assuming the prime number theorem and the standard Selberg-sieve upper bound for prime pairs, \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N\log\log N. \] Hence for every $\lambda\ge 1$, \[ \#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge \lambda\}\ll \frac{N\log\log N}{\lambda^2}. \]

This overcomes the Round-1 obstacle of having only first-moment information.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Let \[ f(n)=\#\{k\ge 0:2^k<n,\ n-2^k\text{ is prime}\}. \] As in Problem #234, I use the standard prime-pair upper bound \[ \Pi(X;h)\ll \frac{h}{\phi(h)}\frac{X}{(\log X)^2} \qquad (h\ge 2), \] uniformly for $X\ge h+2$.

The only extra elementary input is the standard estimate \[ \frac{m}{\phi(m)}\ll \log\log(3m) \qquad (m\ge 1). \]

Theorem 236.2.1. Assume the prime number theorem and the prime-pair upper bound above. Then \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N\log\log N. \]

Proof. Let \[ K:=\left\lfloor \log_2\!\left(\frac{N}{4}\right)\right\rfloor. \] There are at most two powers of $2$ in the interval $(N/4,N]$, so for every $n\le N$, \[ f(n)\le g(n)+2, \] where \[ g(n):=\#\{0\le k\le K: n-2^k\text{ is prime}\}. \] Hence \[ f(n)^2\le 2g(n)^2+8, \] and therefore \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N + \sum_{n\le N} g(n)^2. \] So it remains to bound \[ M(N):=\sum_{n\le N} g(n)^2. \]

Expanding the square gives \[ M(N)=\sum_{0\le j,k\le K} S_{j,k}(N), \] where \[ S_{j,k}(N):=\#\{n\le N: n-2^j\text{ and }n-2^k\text{ are both prime}\}. \]

Diagonal terms.
For $j=k$ we have \[ S_{j,j}(N)=\#\{n\le N:n-2^j\text{ prime}\}=\pi(N-2^j)\le \pi(N)\ll \frac{N}{\log N}. \] Summing over $0\le j\le K$ gives \[ \sum_{0\le j\le K} S_{j,j}(N)\ll \frac{KN}{\log N}\ll N. \]
Off-diagonal terms with one exponent equal to $0$.
Suppose $j>0$ and $k=0$. Then the two primes differ by $2^j-1$, which is odd. Two odd primes cannot differ by an odd number, so one of the two primes must be $2$. Hence there is at most one such $n$, namely possibly $n=2^j+2$. Therefore \[ S_{j,0}(N)\ll 1, \qquad S_{0,j}(N)\ll 1. \] Summing over $1\le j\le K$ contributes only $O(K)=O(\log N)$.
Remaining off-diagonal terms.
Now assume $1\le k<j\le K$. Put \[ m:=j-k, \qquad d:=2^j-2^k = 2^k(2^m-1). \] Because $j\le K$, we have $2^j\le N/4$, so the larger prime in the pair is at most $N-2^k$ and the smaller is at most $N-2^j\ge 3N/4$. Thus the prime-pair upper bound applies with $X\asymp N$ and gives \[ S_{j,k}(N)\ll \frac{d}{\phi(d)}\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}. \] Since $2^m-1$ is odd, \[ \frac{d}{\phi(d)}= \frac{2^k(2^m-1)}{\phi(2^k)\phi(2^m-1)} \le 2\,\frac{2^m-1}{\phi(2^m-1)}. \] Hence \[ S_{j,k}(N)\ll \frac{N}{(\log N)^2}\,\frac{2^m-1}{\phi(2^m-1)}. \] For a fixed $m\ge 1$, the number of pairs $(j,k)$ with $1\le k<j\le K$ and $j-k=m$ is $K-m$. Therefore \[ \sum_{1\le k<j\le K} S_{j,k}(N) \ll \frac{N}{(\log N)^2} \sum_{m=1}^{K-1}(K-m)\frac{2^m-1}{\phi(2^m-1)}. \] Now use the standard bound $u/\phi(u)\ll \log\log(3u)$ with $u=2^m-1$: \[ \frac{2^m-1}{\phi(2^m-1)}\ll \log\log(2^{m+1})\ll \log(m+1). \] Thus \[ \sum_{1\le k<j\le K} S_{j,k}(N) \ll \frac{N}{(\log N)^2} \sum_{m=1}^{K-1}(K-m)\log(m+1). \] Since \[ \sum_{m=1}^{K-1}(K-m)\log(m+1)\ll K^2\log K, \] and $K\asymp \log N$, we obtain \[ \sum_{1\le k<j\le K} S_{j,k}(N)\ll N\log\log N. \] The same bound holds for the symmetric sum with $j<k$. Combining the diagonal, $k=0$, and off-diagonal contributions gives \[ M(N)\ll N\log\log N. \] Therefore \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N + M(N)\ll N\log\log N, \] as claimed. $\square$

Corollary 236.2.2 (quadratic tail bound). For every $\lambda\ge 1$, \[ \#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge \lambda\}\ll \frac{N\log\log N}{\lambda^2}. \] In particular, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, \[ \frac{1}{N}\#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge (\log\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\}\to 0. \]

Proof. Apply Chebyshev's inequality to Theorem 236.2.1: \[ \lambda^2\#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge \lambda\} \le \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N\log\log N. \] Taking $\lambda=(\log\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ yields the second statement. $\square$

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Did I lose control of large exponents $2^k>N/4$? No. There are at most two such exponents, so their total contribution is absorbed in the inequality $f(n)^2\le 2g(n)^2+8$.

  • Was the prime-pair theorem used only where $X\asymp N$? Yes. I applied it only after restricting to $j,k\le K$, which ensures $N-2^j\ge 3N/4$.

  • What about odd differences? Those occur only when one exponent is $0$. I handled them separately and used the parity obstruction rather than the sieve.

  • Could the sum over $m$ be underestimated? No. The combinatorial count of pairs with $j-k=m$ is exactly $K-m$ for $1\le m\le K-1$.

  • Does this prove the original pointwise conjecture? No. The second moment still allows a sparse exceptional set of integers with much larger $f(n)$, so the uniform statement $f(n)=o(\log n)$ remains open.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

The strict new progress beyond Round 1 is the second-moment theorem \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)^2 \ll N\log\log N, \] which implies the tail bound \[ \#\{n\le N:f(n)\ge \lambda\}\ll \frac{N\log\log N}{\lambda^2}. \] This is substantially stronger than the previous first-moment argument and shows that values as large as $(\log\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ already occur on a density-$0$ set.

The remaining gap is still the genuinely difficult one: excluding a sparse exceptional sequence $n_j$ with $f(n_j)$ as large as a positive proportion of $\log n_j$.

8) COMPLETION ESTIMATE (MANDATORY)

Erdős Problem #236: independent continuation

Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.

1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT

For $f(n)=\#\{k\ge0:n-2^k\text{ is prime}\}$, is $f(n)=o(\log n)$ uniformly as $n\to\infty$?

All supplied versions considered: 236.tex. The source obtains a second-moment bound, which permits sparse exceptions. I instead examine the pointwise congruence obstruction and its exact scope.

2) WORK

If $n$ is even, then for $k\ge1$, $n-2^k$ is even and can be prime only when it equals two. There is at most one such exponent. The case $k=0$ contributes at most one more, so $f(n)\le2$ for even $n$.

Now let $\ell$ be any odd prime and let $r$ be the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $\ell$. If $n\bmod\ell$ belongs to the subgroup generated by $2$, there is exactly one exponent class $k\equiv k_0\pmod r$ for which $\ell\mid n-2^k$. Among these exponents at most one can contribute a prime, namely the exponent (if any) satisfying $2^k=n-\ell$. Writing $K=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$, we obtain \[ f(n)\le (1-1/r)(K+1)+2. \] In particular for odd $n$ not divisible by three, $r=2$ gives $f(n)\le\tfrac12\log_2 n+O(1)$.

For several primes these forbidden exponent classes can be combined exactly modulo the least common multiple of their orders. If their union covers a proportion $\theta$ of exponent classes, then $f(n)\le(1-\theta)(K+1)+O(\operatorname{lcm}(r_1,\ldots,r_s)+s)$. This follows by counting complete periods and allowing one exceptional prime equality for each modulus.

3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

If $\ell\mid n$, then $n$ is outside the nonzero subgroup and this prime removes no exponent. In particular an odd $n$ divisible by many small primes defeats any fixed list of these tests. The modulus-dependent remainder prevents unrestricted growth of the list without further work.

4) FINAL

UNRESOLVED. These pointwise bounds settle even $n$ and constrain many odd residue classes, but leave odd integers with strong divisibility by small primes. Neither the congruence argument nor the supplied moment bound excludes every exceptional sequence.

5) SOURCES CHECKED

No external theorem is used in the new argument. The supplied source was read in full; the saved Tao status snapshot is dated 2026-09-22.

6) COMPLETION ESTIMATE

This is a conservative subjective measure of progress toward the original question, not a probability of correctness or a claim that the remaining work is routine. COMPLETION: 10%

Erdős Problem #236

  1. FORMAL RESTATEMENT.

    For an integer $n\ge 1$, define \[ f(n) := \left|\{k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}: 2^k < n\ \text{and}\ n-2^k\ \text{is prime}\}\right|. \] (Equivalently, $f(n)$ is the number of representations $n=p+2^k$ with $p$ prime and $k\ge 0$.)

    Claim/conjecture. As $n\to\infty$, \[ f(n) = o(\log n), \] meaning: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $n_0(\varepsilon)$ such that $f(n)\le \varepsilon\log n$ for all $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)$.

  2. QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK.

    This is a "prime + power of two" representation-count problem. Heuristics suggest $f(n)$ is typically $\asymp 1$, since there are $\asymp \log n$ possible exponents $k$ and each $n-2^k$ has "probability" $\asymp 1/\log n$ of being prime.

    Known results discussed in the problem literature include (i) lower bounds showing $f(n)$ can be large infinitely often (Erdős obtained $f(n)\gg \log\log n$ infinitely often), and (ii) results of Vaughan bounding the rarity of integers $n$ for which $n-2^k$ is prime for all $2^k<n$.

  3. ATTACK PLAN.

    Proof strategies.

    • $P1$ Seek a uniform sieve/large-sieve upper bound for the number of $k$ such that $n-2^k$ is prime; any bound $f(n)\ll \log n/\omega(n)$ with $\omega(n)\to\infty$ would imply the conjecture.
    • $P2$ Control correlations of the set $\{n-2^k\}$ modulo many small primes to show that too many of them must be composite.

    Disproof/construction strategies.

    • $D1$ Try to construct an explicit sequence $n_j$ for which $n_j-2^k$ is prime for $\gg \log n_j$ values of $k$ (this would contradict the conjecture). Any such construction would be extremely strong, akin to producing very long "prime constellations" along the translate of the power-of-two set.

    Best path chosen here. In this write-up, the proof track $P1$--$P2$ stalls at producing a pointwise $o(\log n)$ upper bound, and the disproof track $D1$ would require constructing an explicit $n$ with $\gg \log n$ prime values among the translate $\{n-2^k\}$. Accordingly, I establish rigorous "first-moment" information: the average size of $f(n)$ is bounded (indeed tends to a constant), which implies $f(n)=o(\log n)$ for almost all $n$ but leaves open the required uniform bound for all $n$.

  4. WORK.

    Lemma 236.1 (Trivial pointwise bound).
    For all $n\ge 1$, \[ f(n) \le \lfloor \log_2(n-1)\rfloor + 1 \ll \log n. \]
    Proof: If $2^k<n$ then $k\le \lfloor\log_2(n-1)\rfloor$. For each admissible $k$ there is at most one representation $n=(n-2^k)+2^k$, so $f(n)$ is at most the number of such $k$. ∎
    Lemma 236.2 (Average order of $f(n)$).
    Assume the Prime Number Theorem. Then, as $N\to\infty$, \[ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n\le N} f(n) \to \frac{1}{\log 2}. \]
    Proof: By definition, \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n) =\sum_{n\le N}\ \sum_{\substack{k\ge 0\\2^k<n}} \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n-2^k), \] where $\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(m)$ is $1$ if $m$ is prime and $0$ otherwise. Interchanging the order of summation (all terms are nonnegative and the range is finite) gives \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n) =\sum_{k\ge 0}\ \sum_{\substack{n\le N\\2^k<n}} \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n-2^k). \] For fixed $k$, make the substitution $p=n-2^k$. Then $p$ runs over primes with $p\le N-2^k$, and the condition $2^k<n$ is equivalent to $p\ge 1$ which is automatic for primes. Thus the inner sum equals $\pi(N-2^k)$, and we obtain the exact identity \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)=\sum_{0\le k\le \lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor} \pi(N-2^k). \] Let $K:=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$. For each fixed $k\le K$, we have $N-2^k\asymp N$, and by the Prime Number Theorem, \[ \pi(N-2^k)=\frac{N-2^k}{\log N}+o\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right) \qquad (N\to\infty), \] uniformly for $0\le k\le K$ because $\log(N-2^k)=\log N+O(1)$ on this range. Summing over $k$ yields \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n) =\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(N-2^k) +o\!\left(\frac{KN}{\log N}\right). \] Compute the finite geometric sum: \[ \sum_{k=0}^{K}(N-2^k)=(K+1)N-(2^{K+1}-1)=(K+1)N+O(N), \] since $2^{K}\le N<2^{K+1}$ implies $2^{K+1}=O(N)$. Also, $K=(\log N)/(\log 2)+O(1)$, hence \[ \frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(N-2^k)=\frac{(K+1)N}{\log N}+O\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)=\frac{N}{\log 2}+o(N). \] Finally, the error term satisfies \[ \frac{KN}{\log N}=\left(\frac{\log N}{\log 2}+O(1)\right)\frac{N}{\log N}=\frac{N}{\log 2}+O\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)=O(N), \] so multiplying by the little-$o(1)$ gives $o(N)$. Altogether, \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n)=\frac{N}{\log 2}+o(N). \] Dividing by $N$ gives the claimed limit. ∎
    Corollary 236.3 (A density-one version of the conjecture).
    Assume Lemma 236.2. For any fixed $A>0$, \[ \left|\{n\le N: f(n)\ge A\log N\}\right| \ll \frac{N}{A\log N}. \] In particular, the set of $n$ for which $f(n)\ge A\log n$ has natural density $0$.
    Proof: For each $N$, \[ \sum_{n\le N} f(n) \ge \sum_{\substack{n\le N\\ f(n)\ge A\log N}} f(n) \ge A\log N\cdot \left|\{n\le N: f(n)\ge A\log N\}\right|. \] Rearrange and insert Lemma 236.2, which gives $\sum_{n\le N} f(n)\ll N$. ∎

    What this does not give. Corollary 236.3 is an "almost all $n$" statement. The conjecture asks for the stronger pointwise bound $f(n)=o(\log n)$ holding for every sufficiently large $n$, and the above averaging argument does not rule out a sparse exceptional sequence $n_j$ with $f(n_j)$ comparable to $\log n_j$.

  5. VERIFICATION.

    • Quantifiers. Lemma 236.2 proves a limit for the average as $N\to\infty$ (not a pointwise estimate). All steps are finite sums; the only analytic input is PNT.
    • Edge cases. The definition allows $k=0$ (so $2^k=1$); also $p=2$ is allowed.
    • Uniformity in $k$. For $0\le k\le \log_2 N$, we indeed have $\log(N-2^k)=\log N+O(1)$, so PNT can be applied uniformly at the scale needed for an $o(N)$ aggregate error.

  6. FINAL.

    UNRESOLVED.

    • (i) Strongest fully proved partial result obtained here. Assuming PNT, $\frac1N\sum_{n\le N} f(n)\to 1/\log 2$, hence $f(n)=o(\log n)$ for a density-one subset of integers $n$.
    • (ii) First gap. No argument here gives a uniform-in-$n$ improvement over the trivial bound $f(n)\ll \log n$; in particular it does not preclude a sparse exceptional sequence $n_j$ with $f(n_j)\asymp \log n_j$.
    • (iii) Top 3 next moves.
      • Develop a large-sieve/Selberg-sieve upper bound tailored to the set $\{n-2^k\}$ that holds uniformly in $n$.
      • Try to bound higher moments $\sum_{n\le N} f(n)^m$ sharply enough to force a global maximum bound on $f(n)$.
      • Explore whether results on primes in arithmetic progressions to moduli related to orders of $2$ can force many of the values $n-2^k$ to be composite.
    • (iv) What a minimal counterexample would likely look like. An explicit sequence $n_j\to\infty$ and a fixed $\delta>0$ with $f(n_j)\ge \delta\log n_j$ for all $j$, i.e. unusually many primes among the translate $\{n_j-2^k\}$.

  7. COMPLETION ESTIMATE (MANDATORY).
