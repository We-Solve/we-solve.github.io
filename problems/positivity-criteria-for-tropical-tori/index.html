Positivity Criteria for Tropical Tori

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X={\mathbb{R}}^n/\Lambda$ be a tropical torus as in the supplied record. Here $\Lambda$ denotes the period lattice. The record does not specify its compatibility with an integral affine structure, the permitted dimensions, or the cohomology and positivity conventions to be used.

Once these structures and conventions have been fixed, write $H^{1,1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})$ for the group of integral classes of bidegree $(1,1)$. In the usual codimension-one interpretation, an effective tropical cycle is a balanced weighted polyhedral cycle of pure codimension one with nonnegative integral weights. Provided the chosen theory supplies a cycle-class map, define \[ \mathcal E_X=\left\{\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}):\alpha=[Z]\text{ for an effective tropical cycle }Z\right\}. \] This notation is conditional on those choices; it does not resolve the missing conventions in the source.

The supplied problem asks for the following three investigations.

  1. Determine whether the stated positivity criterion for membership in $\mathcal E_X$ admits a converse. If $\mathcal P_X$ denotes the integral classes satisfying the intended positivity condition, the proposed characterization can be recorded schematically as \[ \mathcal E_X\stackrel{?}{=}\mathcal P_X. \] The record does not define $\mathcal P_X$, state the original implication, or specify whether positivity is strict, weak, or semidefinite.   2. Determine whether tropical analogues of the Nakai--Moishezon and Kleiman criteria exist. A precise statement requires the relevant class of tropical spaces, the notion of ampleness or positivity, the intersection pairing, the test cycles, and the numerical cones and their topology. These data, including whether the proposed criteria concern only tropical tori, are absent from the record.
  3. Determine which combinatorial types of tropical hypersurfaces permit large or small ranges of Picard ranks. A precise formulation requires a specified family of hypersurfaces, a definition of combinatorial type, a convention for Picard rank, and a quantified meaning of a large or small range. The record also does not specify whether these hypersurfaces lie in $X$ or in another ambient tropical space.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether positivity characterizes effective integral $(1,1)$-classes on tropical tori, whether tropical versions of the Nakai--Moishezon and Kleiman criteria exist, and how hypersurface combinatorics constrain Picard ranks. The positivity condition, numerical framework, hypersurface families, and rank conventions are unspecified, so a fully precise conjecture requires further source clarification.
