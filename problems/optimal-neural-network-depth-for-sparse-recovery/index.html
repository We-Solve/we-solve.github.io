Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For exact recovery of $s$-sparse vectors by ReLU networks, is the known depth bound $\lceil\log_2s+2\rceil$ optimal?

The precise source distinction

The primary report, printed pages3053--3055, distinguishes exact recovery of all sparse vectors from arbitrary-precision relative approximation on that unbounded set. Its exact theorem for $s\ge2$ uses $\lceil\log_2s\rceil+2$ hidden layers and leaves optimality open. We rederive its known one-hidden-layer lower bound from the odd part at infinity, then test whether that mechanism can give the requested logarithmic depth lower bound. It cannot be extended in the same way even to two hidden layers.

Complete lower bound for one hidden layer

Let $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ with $m\le n$, and let \[ f(y)=W\sigma(Vy+b)+c, \] where $\sigma$ is coordinatewise ReLU and the hidden width is arbitrary but finite. Set \[ E=\sup_{x\in\Sigma_1\setminus\{0\}} \frac{\|x-f(Ax)\|_2}{\|x\|_2}. \] If $E$ is infinite the claimed bound is immediate. Otherwise, for every fixed $y$ the positive homogeneity and continuity of ReLU imply \[ f_\infty(y):=\lim_{t\to\infty}f(ty)/t=W\sigma(Vy). \] The coordinate identity $\sigma(z)-\sigma(-z)=z$ gives $f_\infty(y)-f_\infty(-y)=WVy$. Apply the relative-error promise to $x=te_i$ and $x=-te_i$, divide by $t$ and pass to the limit. Taking the average of the two resulting error vectors yields \[ \left\|e_i-\tfrac12WVAe_i\right\|_2\le E \quad(1\le i\le n). \] Put $B=\tfrac12WVA$, whose rank is at most $m$. Then $\|I-B\|_{\rm F}^2\le nE^2$. On the other hand choose an orthonormal basis beginning with a basis of $\ker B$. The Frobenius squared norm is the sum of $\|(I-B)v\|^2$ on any orthonormal basis, by expansion of the trace. Each basis vector in $\ker B$ contributes one, so \[ nE^2\ge\|I-B\|_{\rm F}^2\ge n-\operatorname{rank}B\ge n-m. \] Thus $E\ge\sqrt{1-m/n}$, for biased as well as unbiased one-hidden-layer networks. When $m<n$, exact recovery is impossible even for sparsity one. This proof is a rederivation of the source's known lower bound, not a new depth theorem.

The attempted extension fails at the odd-part step

A two-hidden-layer homogeneous ReLU network already need not have a linear odd part. On $\mathbb R^2$ define \[ h(y)=\max(0,y_1,y_2). \] Its first layer computes $a=\sigma(y_1)$ and $b=\sigma(y_2)$; its second computes $\sigma(a-b)$ and $\sigma(b)$; summing these outputs gives $h$, so it is a network with two hidden layers and no biases. For $q(y)=h(y)-h(-y)$, \[ q(1,0)=1,\qquad q(0,1)=1,\qquad q(1,1)=1. \] The last value is not the sum of the first two, so $q$ is not linear. Consequently no rank-$m$ matrix $B$ follows from the one-layer argument for this architecture. This explicit failure does not prove two layers suffice for exact sparse recovery; it identifies the first invalid step in the attempted higher-depth lower bound.

Injectivity and stability do not settle representation depth

The measurement promise $\ker A\cap\Sigma_{2s}=\{0\}$ is equivalent to injectivity on $\Sigma_s$. One direction follows because the difference of two $s$-sparse vectors is $2s$-sparse. For the converse, partition the support of a nonzero $2s$-sparse kernel vector into two sets of size at most $s$, and write it as the difference of two $s$-sparse vectors, yielding a collision. Under this promise define \[ \alpha=\min_{|T|\le\min(2s,n),\,T\ne\varnothing} \min_{\|z\|_2=1}\|A_Tz\|_2>0. \] Each submatrix has trivial kernel, so its continuous norm on the compact unit sphere has positive minimum; finitely many supports preserve positivity. Therefore the inverse on the union of sparse measurement images is Lipschitz: \[ \|x-x'\|_2\le\alpha^{-1}\|Ax-Ax'\|_2 \quad(x,x'\in\Sigma_s). \] This proves stability of the abstract inverse, but gives no specified ReLU depth for an extension to the ambient space. Likewise, an approximation theorem with a different network for every accuracy does not imply that any one finite network realizes the limiting inverse exactly.

An additional shortcut based only on counting linear regions cannot give a depth lower bound when width is unbounded. With $w$ hyperplanes in $m$ dimensions the number of regions is at most $\sum_{j=0}^m\binom wj$: adding one hyperplane splits at most the number of regions cut out on that hyperplane by its predecessors, proving the bound inductively using Pascal's identity. The bound grows without limit with $w$ for $m\ge1$. Hence comparing a fixed finite number of required pieces to that count cannot force additional depth. This does not assert that any prescribed piecewise-linear map is realizable by one hidden layer; the odd-part obstruction shows otherwise.
