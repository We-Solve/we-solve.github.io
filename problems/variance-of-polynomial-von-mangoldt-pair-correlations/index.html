Math-1259

Variance of polynomial von Mangoldt pair correlations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Conrey asks for the variance, as a function of the shift $h$, of a polynomial von Mangoldt correlation $\sum_f\Lambda(f)\Lambda(f+h)$. For monic $f\in{\mathbb{F}}_q[T]$, the polynomial von Mangoldt function is $\Lambda(f)=\deg P$ if $f=P^m$ for a monic irreducible $P$ and $m\ge1$, and zero otherwise. The source gives only $f$ as the summation subscript: it specifies neither degree nor monicity, the shift set and probability measure, normalization, or limiting regime. Thus no unique variance is defined by the original sentence.

The supplied report explicitly labels the following as a reconstruction. Fix a prime power $q$ and integer $n\ge1$, let $G_n=\{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_q[T]:\deg h<n\}$, including zero, and let $Q=q^n$. Set \[ C_n(h)=\sum_{\substack{f\text{ monic}\\\deg f=n}}\Lambda(f)\Lambda(f+h),\quad \mu=Q^{-1}\sum_{h\in G_n}C_n(h),\quad V=Q^{-1}\sum_{h\in G_n}(C_n(h)-\mu)^2. \] All shifts are uniform in this definition. The reported mean is $\mu=Q$. To specify the normalization of its Fourier identity, put $a(u)=\Lambda(T^n+u)$ for $u\in G_n$, and for an additive character $\chi:G_n\to{\mathbb{C}}^*$ put $\widehat a(\chi)=\sum_{u\in G_n}a(u)\overline{\chi(u)}$. The report states \[ V=Q^{-2}\sum_{\chi\ne1}|\widehat a(\chi)|^4. \] For the uniform nonzero-shift mean and variance \[ \mu_*=(Q-1)^{-1}\sum_{h\ne0}C_n(h),\qquad V_*=(Q-1)^{-1}\sum_{h\ne0}(C_n(h)-\mu_*)^2, \] the reported ANOVA decomposition has the explicitly centered form \[ V=\frac{Q-1}{Q}V_*+ \frac{Q-1}{Q^2}(C_n(0)-\mu_*)^2. \] These formulas concern this reconstructed model, not an unspecified distribution of shifts in the source.

For $n=2$ over every finite field, the candidate theorem states \[ C_2(h)=q^2+q(r_q(h)-1),\qquad r_q(h)=\#\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_q:h(x)=0\}\quad(h\in G_2). \] In particular $r_q(0)=q$. The three classes of shifts are zero, nonzero constants, and nonconstant polynomials of degree one; their respective sizes are $1,q-1,q(q-1)$, and their correlation values are $2q^2-q,q^2-q,q^2$. The report gives \[ V=q(q-1),\qquad V_*=\frac{q^3}{(q+1)^2}. \] The second variance is centered at its own nonzero-shift mean $\mu_*$. The higher-degree question remains associated in the report with the nontrivial additive-Fourier fourth moment, rather than a supplied general asymptotic evaluation.

Short English statement

The source asks for the variance of a polynomial prime-pair correlation without specifying how to sum or average. Under the report's explicit fixed-degree monic reconstruction, the mean and Fourier formula are exact, and degree two has a complete all-characteristic distribution. Including zero among shifts and excluding it produce different, separately centered variances.
