Definitions and notation.
Dedekind’s eta function is $\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)$, $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, on the upper half-plane. An eta-product is a finite product $\prod_\delta\eta(\delta\tau)^{r_\delta}$, with specified exponents. A mock modular form is a holomorphic part of a nonholomorphic modular completion. Differential equations can involve derivatives with respect to moduli or $q$, but the variable and coefficient class must be stated.

An explicit eta-product differential equation.
Let $F(\tau)=\prod_{\delta\in D}\eta(\delta\tau)^{r_\delta}$, with finite $D\subset\mathbb N$ and integers $r_\delta$, and $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$. The product definition converges locally uniformly for $|q|<1$, is nonzero on the upper half-plane (equivalently on the punctured disk after choosing the prefactor branch), and can be logarithmically differentiated termwise. With $D_q=q\,d/dq$, \[D_q\log\eta(\delta\tau)=\frac\delta{24}-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\delta n q^{\delta n}}{1-q^{\delta n}}=\frac\delta{24}E_2(\delta\tau), \] where this equation defines $E_2(\tau)=1-24\sum_{n\ge1}nq^n/(1-q^n)$. Hence \[D_qF=\frac1{24}\left(\sum_{\delta\in D}\delta r_\delta E_2(\delta\tau)\right)F. \] Local uniform convergence of the derivative series follows by comparison with $\sum n r^n/(1-r^n)$ on $|q|\le r<1$. Negative exponents cause no poles in the upper half-plane because eta has no zeros there, as follows from convergence of the logarithm of its product.
Example and limitation.
For $F=\eta^{24}$, the equation reduces to $D_qF=E_2F$. It is a concrete first-order equation with a specified coefficient function, rather than the tautology $F'=(F'/F)F$ with an unknown coefficient. However this is a differential identity of eta-products and does not classify the unspecified mock-modular or root-system objects of the source. Eta-products are not automatically mock modular; the supplied terminology repair is retained. Identifying the intended function class and a structurally meaningful coefficient ring, then relating these equations to anomaly equations when appropriate, remains the first gap.
