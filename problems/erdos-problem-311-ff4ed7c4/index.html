Statement

For each $N\in\mathbb N$, define \[ \delta(N):=\min\left\{\left|1-\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\right|:\; A\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,N\},\; \forall S\subseteq A,\ \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n\neq 1 \right\}. \] Equivalently, we look for a subset $A$ whose reciprocal sum is as close as possible to $1$, subject to the constraint that no subsubset $S\subseteq A$ has reciprocal sum exactly $1$.

The question asks for the asymptotic behavior of $\delta(N)$ as $N\to\infty$; in particular, whether \[ \delta(N)=e^{-(c+o(1))N} \quad\text{for some constant }c\in(0,1). \]

Stress points / ambiguity checks.

  • The constraint is on all subsubsets $S\subseteq A$, including $S=A$; thus any $A$ with $\sum_{n\in A}1/n=1$ is forbidden.
  • Without the constraint, the minimal nonzero distance to $1$ is at least $1/\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,N)$ by a denominator argument (proved below).
  • The statement "$\delta(N)=e^{-(c+o(1))N}$" should be read as a conjectural asymptotic, not as already-established.

Restatement from Math-16156-A3 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.
