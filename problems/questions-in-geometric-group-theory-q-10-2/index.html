Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 10.2

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a connected CW complex with no cells of dimension greater than three, and put $\pi=\pi_1(X)$. A local coefficient system of abelian groups on $X$ consists of groups transported isomorphically along paths, with transport depending only on endpoint-preserving homotopy and respecting composition. After choosing a basepoint, such a system is equivalent to a $\mathbb Z\pi$-module, with monodromy given by the fundamental-group action.

Its cohomology $H^3(X;\mathcal M)$ can be computed from the cellular chains of the universal cover using the corresponding module-valued cochains. Assume \[ H^3(X;\mathcal M)=0 \qquad\text{for every local coefficient system }\mathcal M. \] Does there exist an $X$ satisfying this condition which is not homotopy equivalent to any two-dimensional CW complex?

The target two-complex is allowed to have infinitely many cells, and the source wording does not require $X$ to be finite. The finite-complex version is an important additional restriction, but it is not silently substituted here. No asphericity assumption is made; higher homotopy in degree two can be present. Vanishing only for constant integer coefficients would be much weaker than the universal condition displayed above. Likewise, a particular three-cell presentation that cannot be collapsed directly does not constitute a counterexample, because an unrelated two-complex could still have the same homotopy type. The problem concerns homotopy dimension rather than a chosen cell decomposition.

Short English statement

Can a three-dimensional complex have vanishing third cohomology for every local system yet fail to have the homotopy type of a two-complex?

OpenAI's claim

Wall's finite D$2$ question is claimed to have a negative example: a finite three-dimensional complex with infinite fundamental group satisfies D$2$ but has no finite two-dimensional homotopy model. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
OpenAI claims a finite connected CW complex $X$ of dimension three such that, for its universal cover $\widetilde X$, \[ H_i(\widetilde X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i>2),\qquad H^3(X;M)=0\quad\text{for every }\mathbb Z[\pi_1X]\text{-module }M, \] yet $X$ is without a homotopy equivalent finite CW model in dimension two or below. The cohomology condition ranges over all local coefficient modules, and no asphericity assumption is made.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 321, 2026-10-06): A rank-one twisted-homology obstruction is proved by repeatedly lifting a fixed finite source through cyclic-cover image towers, whose simplex growth cannot continue. A five-generator presentation has a finitely normally generated perfect subgroup and a quotient involution detected by a complex character. Its explicit boundary matrix gives vanishing twisted second homology; finite plus construction preserves this while contradicting the necessary two-dimensional character condition.
