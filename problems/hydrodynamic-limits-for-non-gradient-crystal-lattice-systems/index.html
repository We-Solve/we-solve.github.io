Hydrodynamic Limits for Non-Gradient Crystal-Lattice Systems

Definitions and mathematical statement

Terminology and unresolved scope. A crystal lattice commonly means a connected graph equipped with a free action of a group $\Gamma\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^d$, for some positive integer $d$, by graph automorphisms, with finite quotient graph. This is a conventional interpretation of the terminology, not a definition specified in the supplied record. The record does not specify a lattice, its dimension, an embedding, or a macroscopic spatial domain.

An interacting particle system is a stochastic process whose configurations assign particle states to lattice sites and whose transition rules describe their interactions. A hydrodynamic limit describes convergence, after spatial and temporal rescaling, of suitable empirical conserved quantities to deterministic macroscopic evolution. For conservative dynamics, the gradient condition usually expresses microscopic conserved currents as discrete gradients of local functions; the precise condition depends on the model. The term non-gradient indicates failure of the relevant gradient condition, which the supplied record does not define.

Source question. Can the hydrodynamic limit be derived for a non-gradient interacting particle system on a crystal lattice?

Qualification. This is a research question rather than a fully specified mathematical proposition. The record supplies no configuration space, transition generator, interaction assumptions, conserved quantities, initial distributions, scaling, boundary conditions, topology of convergence, or candidate macroscopic equation. It also does not specify whether the requested result concerns one particular system, existence of an example, or every system in an intended class. Consequently, no uniquely quantified hydrodynamic-limit assertion can be extracted without additional source information. The terminology above explains the question and does not add hypotheses to it.

Short English statement

The question asks for a rigorous macroscopic evolution law for a non-gradient particle system on a periodic crystal lattice. The supplied record leaves the system and the limiting assertion unspecified, so a precise theorem statement requires additional hypotheses. Its open status is supplied dated curation, not independently verified here.
