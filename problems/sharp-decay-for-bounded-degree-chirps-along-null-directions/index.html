Math-1289

Sharp decay for bounded-degree chirps along null directions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied reconstruction uses the Euclidean unit ball $B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$, the quadratic phase $x_3^2$, and distinct lines $V_j$ spanned by nonzero null vectors for $v_1^2+v_2^2-v_3^2=0$. For $v_j\in V_j\setminus\{0\}$, define \[ I_\lambda(f_1,\ldots,f_N)=\int_B e^{i\lambda x_3^2} \prod_{j=1}^N f_j(x\cdot v_j)\,dx. \] Christ's intended question is whether, for arbitrary bounded measurable $f_j:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$, an estimate \[ |I_\lambda(f_1,\ldots,f_N)|\le C|\lambda|^{-\epsilon} \prod_{j=1}^N\|f_j\|_\infty\qquad(|\lambda|\ge1) \] holds for some $\epsilon>0$, and what the optimal exponent is. The precise allowable dependence of $C,\epsilon$ on $N$ and the directions is not supplied. The extraction instead says "different light cones" and corrupts the phase, estimate symbol and epsilon; the verification stops after announcing the phase repair. Thus the displayed null-line/$x_3^2$ formulation is the supplied research interpretation, not independently verified recovered text.

The reported partial theorem restricts the input class. For every fixed integer degree bound $D\ge0$, every finite collection of nonzero null directions, and arbitrary real polynomials $P_j$ of degree at most $D$, it asserts \[ \left|\int_B e^{i\lambda x_3^2}\prod_{j=1}^N e^{iP_j(x\cdot v_j)}\,dx\right| \le C_D|\lambda|^{-1/2}\qquad(|\lambda|\ge1), \] uniformly in $N$, placement of directions and all polynomial coefficients. Repeated directions are not excluded in this partial theorem. For finite complex measures $\mu_j$ on the real degree-$D$ polynomial coefficient space and mixtures $f_j(t)=\int e^{iP(t)}\,d\mu_j(P)$, the claimed bound becomes \[ |I_\lambda(f_1,\ldots,f_N)|\le C_D|\lambda|^{-1/2} \prod_j\|\mu_j\|_{\mathrm{TV}}. \] This is a product total-variation bound, not the original product $L^\infty$-norm estimate for arbitrary inputs. The report gives exponent $1/2$ as sharp in this class because taking all inputs equal to one leaves a stationary-phase term of order $|\lambda|^{-1/2}$.

The report also states an exact tensor criterion for quadratic cancellation. With $e_3=(0,0,1)$, cancellation by quadratic ridge phases $a_j(x\cdot v_j)^2$ is the matrix identity \[ \lambda e_3e_3^{\mathsf T}+\sum_j a_jv_jv_j^{\mathsf T}=0. \] This describes cancellation of the quadratic part, not a decay theorem for all bounded functions. The source's statement that estimate (0.2) had only been proved for $N\le5$, with reference [4], is a historical qualification; no current verification or full bibliographic reference is supplied.

Short English statement

Seek oscillatory decay with arbitrary bounded ridge inputs along light-cone directions. The supplied reconstructed partial result proves sharp half-power decay for bounded-degree polynomial chirps and their finite-measure mixtures, a restricted class whose total-variation estimate does not resolve the original $L^\infty$ question.
