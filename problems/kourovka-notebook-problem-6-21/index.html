Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.21

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. For a finite group $G$, write $1_G$ for its identity and $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ for its automorphism group. An automorphism $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is fixed-point-free if \[ \{g\in G:\alpha(g)=g\}=\{1_G\}. \] Define the lower central series by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_i(G),G]$, where $[H,G]$ is the subgroup generated by $h^{-1}g^{-1}hg$ with $h\in H$ and $g\in G$. A group is nilpotent of class at most $c$ if $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1_G\}$.

The supplied source attributes to G. Higman the assertion that, for every prime $p$, there is a positive integer bounding the nilpotency classes of all finite groups admitting a fixed-point-free automorphism of order exactly $p$. Accordingly, define the optimal bound by \[ \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(p)=\min\left\{c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:\ \forall\text{ finite groups }G,\ \forall\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G),\quad \left(\operatorname{ord}(\alpha)=p\ \text{and}\ \{g\in G:\alpha(g)=g\}=\{1_G\}\right) \Longrightarrow\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1_G\}\right\}. \] A Higman's function is any function $\chi$ from the primes to the positive integers satisfying this uniform bound. The source also attributes to Higman the lower bound \[ \chi(p)\geq\frac{p^2-1}{4} \qquad\text{for every prime }p \] for every such function.

Problem. Determine $\chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(p)$ for every prime $p$. In particular, is \[ \chi_{\mathrm{opt}}(p)= \begin{cases} 1,&p=2,\\ \dfrac{p^2-1}{4},&p>2 \end{cases} \] for every prime $p$? The supplied record states that these equalities are known for $p\leq7$; this historical qualification and the attributed background results are reproduced as supplied, without independent verification.

Short English statement

Determine the smallest uniform bound on the nilpotency class of a finite group admitting a fixed-point-free automorphism of a given prime order $p$. The proposed optimal bound is $1$ for $p=2$ and $(p^2-1)/4$ for odd primes. The supplied source states that this holds for $p\leq7$.
