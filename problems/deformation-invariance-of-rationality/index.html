Math-4163

Deformation Invariance of Rationality

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A smooth complex projective variety $V$ of dimension $n$ is called rational if it is birational over ${\mathbb{C}}$ to ${\mathbb{P}}^n_{{\mathbb{C}}}$. Here birational means that dense Zariski open subsets of the two varieties are isomorphic. It is called irrational if it is not rational.

In the algebraic interpretation of the supplied question, let $B$ be a nonempty irreducible complex algebraic variety and let $f\colon X\to B$ be a smooth projective morphism whose fibers $X_b=f^{-1}(b)$, for $b\in B({\mathbb{C}})$, are nonempty integral varieties of a common dimension $n\geq 0$. Is the following assertion true for every such family? \[ \forall b,c\in B({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad X_b\text{ is rational}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ X_c\text{ is rational}. \] Equivalently, does there exist a family satisfying these hypotheses and points $b,c\in B({\mathbb{C}})$ such that $X_b$ is rational and $X_c$ is irrational? Existence of such a family would give a negative answer to deformation invariance. The supplied background also singles out the restriction $n=3$.

The source does not explicitly specify whether the base is algebraic or analytic, or state an integrality or connectedness convention for fibers. The displayed formulation uses algebraic bases and the usual integral meaning of variety; these conventions require confirmation against the original problem. No smoothness assumption on $B$ is supplied.

Short English statement

Must rationality be constant among the fibers of every smooth projective family over an irreducible complex base? Equivalently, can such a family contain both a rational fiber and an irrational fiber? The supplied background highlights threefolds. The algebraic-base and integral-fiber conventions need source confirmation.
