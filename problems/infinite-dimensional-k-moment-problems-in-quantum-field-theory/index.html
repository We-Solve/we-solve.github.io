Definitions and conventions.
A moment functional assigns values to polynomial observables on an infinite-dimensional configuration space. For a specified subset $K$, a representing measure is a positive measure $\mu$ supported in $K$ such that $L(p)=\int_K p\,d\mu$ for the allowed polynomials $p$. Cylinder positivity versus support.
The qualification in [S2] leaves the configuration topology open. I test the concrete choice $K=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R)$, with coordinate polynomials, rather than claim a criterion for all field spaces. For a polynomial $p$ depending on its first $N$ coordinates put \[ L(p)=2^{-N}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^N}p(\epsilon). \] Adding unused coordinates duplicates summands and leaves this value unchanged, so this defines one normalized functional on all cylinder polynomials. It is positive on squares and indeed on every polynomial pointwise nonnegative on $K$: every finite sign vector extends to a point of $K$ by adding zeros. Thus even positivity on all such nonnegative cylinder polynomials holds.
Obstruction.
Suppose a countably additive probability measure $\mu$ on $K$, with measurable coordinates, represented $L$. For each $n$, \[ \int_K(x_n^2-1)^2\,d\mu=L((x_n^2-1)^2)=0. \] Nonnegativity forces $x_n^2=1$ almost surely. The intersection of these countably many probability-one sets still has measure one, but is empty in $\ell^2$, since then $\sum_nx_n^2=\infty$. This contradiction needs neither a moment-determinacy theorem nor an infinite product-measure construction.
