Capacity-Preserving Conversion to Unsplittable Flows

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A splittable flow may route one demand along several paths; an unsplittable flow routes its entire demand on one path. $d_{\max}=\max_i d_i$, $x_e,y_e$ are edge loads, and $c(x)=\sum_e c_e x_e$.

The catalogue title is retained as metadata. Named certificates, contracts, bounds or frameworks in that title are not asserted as results of the original source extract. The cost-preserving x+dmax target is refuted.

The historical source snapshot still labels this record open. A July 2026 formally verified counterexample refutes its cost-preserving rounding assertion; this record preserves the source wording and is not presented as an unresolved conjecture.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Given a single-source network with capacities $u$, costs $c$, source $s$, sinks $t_i$, and demands $d_i$ satisfying the no-bottleneck condition $d_{\max}\leq u_e$, does every splittable flow $x$ satisfying the demands admit an unsplittable flow $y$ satisfying the same demands such that $y_e\leq x_e+d_{\max}$ for every edge $e$ and $c(y)\leq c(x)$? [S2]
Source status.
The supplied catalogue labels this source record open; that metadata was checked against the supplied snapshot on 2026-10-11. The label is preserved for provenance and is not an independent verification of present open status or mathematical correctness. The catalogue literature assessment is dated 2026-08-21.

Short English statement

Round a single-source flow to one path per demand while preserving cost and allowing only the stated additive edge-load increase.
