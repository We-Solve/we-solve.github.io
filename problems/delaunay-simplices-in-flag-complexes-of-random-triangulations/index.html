Delaunay Simplices in Flag Complexes of Random Triangulations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\geq 1$ be an integer, and let $X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be the set of points of the Poisson point process mentioned in the source. The source does not specify its intensity measure, homogeneity, or nondegeneracy assumptions. Work on configurations for which the stated Delaunay triangulation $T$ is defined, and regard $T$ as an abstract simplicial complex with vertex set $X$.

The flag complex of the $1$-skeleton of $T$ is \[ F(T)=\left\{\sigma\subset X: \sigma\text{ is finite and }\{x,y\}\in T\text{ for all distinct }x,y\in\sigma\right\}. \] For each integer $0\leq k\leq d$, write \[ F_k(T)=\{\sigma\in F(T):|\sigma|=k+1\},\qquad D_k(T)=\{\sigma\in F_k(T):\sigma\in T\}. \] Thus a flag simplex is called Delaunay precisely when it is a simplex of the given triangulation $T$. Flag simplices are understood abstractly; their definition alone imposes no affine independence requirement.

Source question. For a Poisson point process in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, determine how many members of $F_d(T)$, and more generally of $F_k(T)$ for each integer $0\leq k\leq d$, belong to $D_k(T)$.

Unresolved specification. The source does not determine a unique numerical or probabilistic target. It does not specify whether "how many" means a random count, an expected count, a spatial intensity, or a proportion; the supplied literature assessment describes a fraction question. Counts over all of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ need not be finite. A finite-window formulation would require a window and a rule assigning simplices to it, while a proportion would require a sampling or limiting convention. None of these choices is imposed here. The probability law and the treatment of configurations with nonunique Delaunay triangulations also require clarification before a fully specified quantitative problem can be stated.

Short English statement

Start with the Delaunay triangulation of a Poisson point set in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$. Add an abstract simplex whenever all pairs of its vertices are Delaunay edges. The question asks how many of these flag simplices are themselves Delaunay simplices, especially in dimension $d$ and more generally for $0\leq k\leq d$. The process law and the intended counting or fraction convention remain unspecified.
