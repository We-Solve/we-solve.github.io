Math-4369

Decidability of Finite Field Extensions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether every finite extension of a decidable field is decidable. It does not specify a logical language. Under the usual interpretation using the pure first-order language of rings $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ring}}=\{0,1,+,-,\cdot\}$, define \[ \operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(F)=\{\varphi:\varphi\text{ is an }\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ring}}\text{-sentence and }F\models\varphi\}. \] A field $F$ is called decidable in this interpretation if an algorithm, given any $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ring}}$-sentence, determines whether it belongs to $\operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(F)$. This concerns the complete theory, without requiring a computable presentation of $F$. For an extension of fields $K\subseteq L$, its degree is $[L:K]=\dim_K L$, and it is finite when this dimension is finite.

With this language qualification, the original question asks whether \[ \forall\,K\subseteq L\text{ fields},\qquad \left(\operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(K)\text{ is decidable}\ \land\ [L:K]<\infty\right) \Longrightarrow \operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(L)\text{ is decidable}. \] There is no restriction on characteristic in the supplied statement. The assertion concerns decidability for each individual extension; it does not specify a uniform algorithm for all extensions.

Unresolved source qualification. The registry labels the record open, but its supplied statement-verification note says that the official report answers this question negatively by constructing a decidable field with an undecidable finite extension. Under the interpretation above, that reported negative answer would have the form \[ \exists\,K\subseteq L\text{ fields}:\quad [L:K]<\infty,\qquad \operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(K)\text{ is decidable},\qquad \operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{ring}}(L)\text{ is undecidable}. \] This is a transcription of the supplied verification claim, not an independently verified result. The record marks its statement status as unrecoverable and supplies no clean replacement statement. Consequently, the displayed universal implication is preserved as the original question, with conflicting status information explicitly retained; it is not presented as a verified open problem.

Short English statement

The original question asks whether a finite field extension always preserves decidability of the complete first-order theory, provisionally read in the pure language of rings. The supplied registry contains conflicting status information: its open label and dated literature assessment coexist with a verification note reporting a negative answer. This statement-only draft requires human mathematical and source review.
