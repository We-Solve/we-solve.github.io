Common twists, sparse families, and the marked-torsor bottleneck

Definitions and mathematical statement

The original asks whether Bhargava's arithmetic-statistical methods can yield Hasse-principle results for the following special surfaces and extend Swinnerton-Dyer's diagonal analysis. A Hasse-principle statement relates rational points to points over every completion of ${\mathbb{Q}}$, with any further hypotheses explicitly required; no universal unconditional local-to-global assertion is stated here.

For homogeneous binary cubics $F,G\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]$, consider \[ S=\{F(x,y)=G(z,w)\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3_{{\mathbb{Q}}},\qquad u^3=tF(x,y),\quad v^3=tG(z,w). \] The latter are common cubic twists, with $t\in{\mathbb{Q}}^\times$ considered up to cubes when using twist classes. Smoothness/separability hypotheses making these plane curves genus one are not given. The source calls the descriptions equivalent; the report stresses that zero-value boundary cases must be treated separately rather than dividing blindly by $u,v$ or the cubic values.

For separable quartics $f,g$, the second model is \[ z^2=f(x)g(y),\qquad u^2=tf(x),\quad v^2=tg(y), \] with common quadratic twist class $t\in{\mathbb{Q}}^\times/{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times2}$. The source calls the surface Kummer K3. A proper smooth model, affine boundary cases, and the distinction between affine quartic points and points on projective genus-one torsors must be specified before making rational-point equivalences. Projective points at infinity alone do not automatically supply the required affine solution.

The stated goal is to remove the finiteness assumption on the $2$-primary Tate--Shafarevich group of the relevant quadratic twists. Here $\operatorname{Sha}(E/{\mathbb{Q}})$ is the kernel of $H^1({\mathbb{Q}},E)\to\prod_vH^1({\mathbb{Q}}_v,E)$ for the relevant Jacobian elliptic curve; the extracted symbol "X" is interpreted in that sense by the research report. Which twists and auxiliary descent hypotheses occur in Swinnerton-Dyer's argument are not supplied.

The report attributes positive-proportion rational-point results for split cubic surfaces, and analogous Kummer families, to Browning, while explicitly leaving general fixed-surface and finiteness-removal questions open. It proposes exact common-twist/value-class criteria, including cubic zero cases and the affine/projective distinction, but does not include the complete criteria.

Its relative selector requires exceptional estimates within the same admissible common twist family. For a specified height ordering and admissible sets $\mathcal A(T)$, a sufficient proposed condition is that the sum of the two marked-class bad counts is $o(|\mathcal A(T)|)$, so some admissible twist is good for both coverings. Density-zero exceptions in the ambient unrestricted family do not imply this relative estimate for a sparse $\mathcal A$. The admissible conditions, marked classes, and ordering are not defined. These are unreviewed reductions and family-average reports, not a solution for every fixed surface.

Short English statement

Use arithmetic statistics to find one common twist with the required rational points and improve local-to-global arguments for the special cubic and Kummer surfaces. The report distinguishes family-average progress from fixed-surface results and insists on relative exceptional estimates inside the common admissible twist family. The original finiteness-removal objective remains open in its assessment.
