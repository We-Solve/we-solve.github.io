Physical Realizability of Quantum Computation

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The standard circuit model uses finitely many qubits, preparation in a known input state, a universal set of local unitary gates, and final measurements. A uniform polynomial-size circuit family represents efficient quantum computation. Physical feasible realization requires scalable devices that preserve the necessary quantum coherence, control, and measurement accuracy under a specified noise model; it is stronger than a formal circuit construction.
Source scope and qualifications.
This is the workshop introduction's foundational physical question. No universal engineering-resource budget, device class, or noise law is supplied. Evidence for hardness of classical simulation is not a proof of scalable physical realizability, and fault-tolerance assumptions cannot be silently treated as established experimental facts.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Is the standard model of quantum computation feasibly realizable?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Fault-tolerant quantum-computation theorems establish in-principle computation below an error threshold, but scalable physical realization of the standard model remains an engineering and physics problem. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Determine whether scalable physical systems can feasibly implement the standard quantum-computation model.
