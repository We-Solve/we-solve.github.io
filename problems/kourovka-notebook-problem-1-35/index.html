Kourovka Notebook Problem 1.35

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group. A partial order on $G$ is a reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive relation on $G$; it is a linear order if any two elements are comparable. For $C\in\{L,R,B\}$, call a partial order $C$-invariant if it satisfies the corresponding condition: \[ \begin{aligned} L:&\quad x\leq y\Longrightarrow gx\leq gy,\\ R:&\quad x\leq y\Longrightarrow xg\leq yg,\\ B:&\quad x\leq y\Longrightarrow gxh\leq gyh, \end{aligned} \qquad(x,y,g,h\in G). \] For a fixed convention $C$, say that $G$ is pro-orderable under convention $C$ if every $C$-invariant partial order $\leq$ on $G$ admits a $C$-invariant linear order $\preccurlyeq$ on $G$ extending it, meaning \[ \forall x,y\in G,\qquad x\leq y\Longrightarrow x\preccurlyeq y. \] A group $G$ is simple if $G\neq\{1\}$ and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $G$.

Part (c), attributed to A. I. Mal'cev and L. Fuchs. Does there exist a simple pro-orderable group? For each fixed convention $C$, the corresponding precise existence question is \[ \exists G\;\left(G\text{ is simple and pro-orderable under convention }C\right). \] The supplied source does not specify whether its group orderings are left invariant, right invariant, or invariant on both sides. Thus the displayed family makes this unresolved qualification explicit; it does not identify which member is the intended source question. Reading "ordering" as an arbitrary order on the underlying set would omit a potentially essential group compatibility requirement.

Archived parts retained from the source. Under the same unresolved ordering convention, part (a) asks whether the wreath product of every pair of pro-orderable groups is pro-orderable, and part (b), attributed to A. I. Mal'cev, asks whether every subgroup of every pro-orderable group is pro-orderable. The supplied excerpt does not specify whether the wreath product in (a) is restricted or unrestricted. Its archive reports a negative answer to each of (a) and (b), attributed to V. M. Kopytov. This is a supplied historical report, not an independently verified assertion here; it does not supply an answer to (c).

Short English statement

Part (c) asks whether a nontrivial simple group can have the property that every compatible partial order extends to a compatible linear order. The required compatibility convention is absent from the supplied excerpt and needs clarification. The record labels the overall problem partially solved and reports negative answers to archived parts (a) and (b), while retaining (c) in the main section.
