Statement

A (simple) graph $G$ has chromatic number $\chi(G)$ equal to the least cardinal $\kappa$ such that $V(G)$ can be properly colored with $\kappa$ colors.

Given graphs $G_1,G_2$ with $\chi(G_1)=\chi(G_2)=\aleph_1$, question: must there exist a graph $H$ with $\chi(H)=4$ (or alternatively with $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$) such that $H$ is a subgraph of both $G_1$ and $G_2$? Here "$H$ is a subgraph of $G_i$" means there exists an injective map $f_i:V(H)\to V(G_i)$ such that if $xy\in E(H)$ then $f_i(x)f_i(y)\in E(G_i)$.

Restatement from Math-660-A3 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.

Erdős Problem #62 (Round 3): Common $4$-chromatic subgraphs of $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs

1) ROUND-3 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): obstruction / prerequisite extraction.

Round 2 reduced Problem #62 to the intersection problem for the sets of finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs appearing in two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs, and ruled out all proof strategies based on a fixed or finite unavoidable $4$-chromatic template family. The most promising next step is therefore to convert the pairwise-common-subgraph question into a single-graph necessary condition.

The new goal of Round 3 is to prove that if the answer to Problem #62 were positive, then every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph would have to contain finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs with arbitrarily large odd girth and also with arbitrarily large order. This gives a concrete obstruction criterion for any future counterexample search.

2) ROUND-2 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following specific Round 2 results without re-proving them.
  • Round 2 Corollary 4: a common $4$-chromatic subgraph exists for $G_1,G_2$ if and only if there exists a common finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraph.
  • Round 2 Lemma 5: every fixed non-bipartite graph can be omitted by some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.
  • Round 2 Corollary 7: every finite family of graphs of chromatic number at least $3$ can be simultaneously omitted by some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.
  • Round 2 Proposition 8: a counterexample pair would follow from sufficiently flexible omission of prescribed families of finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-3)

The genuinely new ingredient is the following three-step strengthening.
  1. Round 2 only formalized omission for fixed graphs and finite families. Round 3 proves an explicit bounded-odd-girth family avoidance theorem: any family of non-bipartite graphs whose odd girths are uniformly bounded can be simultaneously omitted by some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.
  2. Assuming a positive answer to Problem #62, this immediately forces every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ to have finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of unbounded odd girth.
  3. Combining the positive answer with Round 2 Corollary 7 also forces every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ to have finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of unbounded order.
In contrapositive form, a single $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph with bounded $4$-critical odd-girth spectrum, or bounded $4$-critical order spectrum, would already disprove Problem #62.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-3)

Gap left after Round 2. Round 2 showed that no fixed graph and no finite family of $4$-chromatic graphs can be unavoidable. But this still left open the possibility that every pair of $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs might intersect in some infinite, highly nonuniform family of finite $4$-critical graphs.

Precise Round 3 claims. Let $\mathcal C_4$ be the set of isomorphism types of finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs, and let \[ \mathcal C_4(G):=\{F\in \mathcal C_4 : F\text{ embeds into }G\}. \] I will prove:

  1. if $S\subseteq \mathcal C_4$ has bounded odd girth, then some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph omits every member of $S$;
  2. if Problem #62 has a positive answer, then for every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$, the odd girths of graphs in $\mathcal C_4(G)$ are unbounded;
  3. if Problem #62 has a positive answer, then for every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$, the orders of graphs in $\mathcal C_4(G)$ are unbounded.
This plan overcomes the Round 2 obstacle by turning the unresolved pairwise-intersection problem into a concrete single-graph prerequisite. Any future disproof need only construct one $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph violating either prerequisite.

5) WORK (ROUND-3)

5.1. A family-version of Round 2 odd-girth avoidance

Proposition 9 (bounded-odd-girth family avoidance).
Let $\mathcal H$ be any family of non-bipartite graphs such that \[ M:=\sup\{\mathrm{og}(H):H\in \mathcal H\}<\infty. \] Then for every infinite cardinal $\kappa$ there exists a graph $G$ with $|V(G)|=\kappa$ and $\chi(G)=\kappa$ such that no member of $\mathcal H$ embeds into $G$.

Proof. Choose an integer $s>M$. By the external input already used in Round 2 (recorded in Hajnal--Komjáth), there exists a graph $G$ of size and chromatic number $\kappa$ with odd girth $\mathrm{og}(G)>s$. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that some $H\in \mathcal H$ embeds into $G$. Because $H$ is non-bipartite, it contains an odd cycle of length $\mathrm{og}(H)\le M < s < \mathrm{og}(G)$. Its image in $G$ would be an odd cycle of the same length, contradicting the definition of odd girth. Hence no $H\in \mathcal H$ embeds into $G$. $\square$

Corollary 10.
If $S\subseteq \mathcal C_4$ is any family of finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs with bounded odd girth, then there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ such that \[ \mathcal C_4(G)\cap S=\varnothing. \]

Proof. Every member of $S$ is non-bipartite, so Proposition 9 applies with $\kappa=\aleph_1$. $\square$

Round 3 gain over Round 2. Round 2 only made this omission principle explicit for finite families. Proposition 9 shows that the same mechanism works for arbitrary families, provided their odd girths stay uniformly bounded.

5.2. A positive answer would force unbounded odd girth in every $\mathcal C_4(G)$

Theorem 11 (necessary odd-girth richness under a positive answer).
Assume Problem #62 has a positive answer; that is, whenever $G_1,G_2$ have chromatic number $\aleph_1$, they have a common subgraph of chromatic number $4$. Then for every graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ and every integer $r\ge 3$, there exists a finite $4$-vertex-critical graph $F\in \mathcal C_4(G)$ with \[ \mathrm{og}(F)>r. \]

Proof. Fix $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ and an integer $r\ge 3$. By the large-odd-girth existence theorem used in Round 2, there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $H_r$ with \[ \mathrm{og}(H_r)>r. \] By the assumed positive answer to Problem #62, the graphs $G$ and $H_r$ have a common subgraph of chromatic number $4$. Applying Round 2 Corollary 4, there is a finite $4$-vertex-critical graph $F$ that is a subgraph of both $G$ and $H_r$. Because $F\subseteq H_r$, every odd cycle of $F$ is also an odd cycle of $H_r$. Therefore \[ \mathrm{og}(F)\ge \mathrm{og}(H_r)>r. \] Thus $F\in \mathcal C_4(G)$ and $\mathrm{og}(F)>r$, as required. $\square$

Corollary 12.
Under the positive answer to Problem #62, for every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ the set \[ \{\mathrm{og}(F):F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\} \] is unbounded in $\mathbb N$.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 11. $\square$

Interpretation. A positive solution to the pair problem would already imply a surprisingly strong single-graph consequence: every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph would contain finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of arbitrarily large odd girth. This is much stronger than anything obtained in Round 2 and sharply narrows the obstruction.

5.3. A positive answer would force unbounded order in every $\mathcal C_4(G)$

Theorem 13 (necessary size richness under a positive answer).
Assume Problem #62 has a positive answer. Then for every graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ and every integer $N\ge 1$, there exists a finite $4$-vertex-critical graph $F\in \mathcal C_4(G)$ with \[ |V(F)|>N. \]

Proof. Fix $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ and $N\ge 1$. Let \[ S_N:=\{F\in \mathcal C_4: |V(F)|\le N\}. \] This set is finite, because there are only finitely many isomorphism types of graphs on at most $N$ vertices. By Round 2 Corollary 7, there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $H_N$ that contains none of the graphs in $S_N$. By the assumed positive answer to Problem #62, $G$ and $H_N$ have a common $4$-chromatic subgraph; by Round 2 Corollary 4, they therefore have a common finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraph $F$. Now $F\in \mathcal C_4(G)$ and $F\notin S_N$ because $H_N$ omits every member of $S_N$. Hence $|V(F)|>N$. $\square$

Corollary 14.
Under the positive answer to Problem #62, for every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ the set \[ \{|V(F)|:F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\} \] is unbounded in $\mathbb N$. In particular, $\mathcal C_4(G)$ is infinite.

Proof. Unboundedness follows from Theorem 13. Infinitude is immediate. $\square$

5.4. A one-graph obstruction criterion for disproving Problem #62

Corollary 15 (single-graph obstruction criterion).
Any one of the following statements would already imply a negative answer to Problem #62.
  1. There exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ such that \[ \sup\{\mathrm{og}(F):F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\}<\infty. \]
  2. There exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ such that \[ \sup\{|V(F)|:F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\}<\infty. \]

Proof. If $1$ holds, let \[ R:=\sup\{\mathrm{og}(F):F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\}. \] By Proposition 9, there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $H$ with odd girth $>R$. Then no finite $4$-vertex-critical graph embedded in $G$ can embed in $H$. By Round 2 Corollary 4, $G$ and $H$ cannot have a common $4$-chromatic subgraph, so Problem #62 fails.

If $2$ holds, let \[ N:=\sup\{|V(F)|:F\in \mathcal C_4(G)\}. \] By Round 2 Corollary 7, there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $H$ omitting every finite $4$-vertex-critical graph on at most $N$ vertices. Again Round 2 Corollary 4 implies that $G$ and $H$ have no common $4$-chromatic subgraph, so Problem #62 fails. $\square$

Remark 16.
Corollary 15 is the sharpest new reduction obtained in Round 3. It shows that the search for a counterexample pair can be replaced by a search for a single $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph whose finite $4$-vertex-critical spectrum is bounded in some globally avoidable invariant. The most natural remaining candidate invariant is odd girth. Hence the new bottleneck is:
Does every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph contain finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of arbitrarily large odd girth?
A positive answer to Problem #62 would force this statement by Theorem 11. Conversely, a single counterexample to this one-graph statement would disprove Problem #62 by Corollary 15.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Does Proposition 9 really extend Round 2? Yes. Round 2 handled fixed graphs and finite families; Proposition 9 handles arbitrary families with uniformly bounded odd girth. No cardinality restriction on $\mathcal H$ is needed.

  • Does Theorem 11 improperly assume finiteness of the common subgraph? No. The positive answer only yields a common $4$-chromatic subgraph a priori, but Round 2 Corollary 4 upgrades this to a common finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraph, which is exactly what is used.

  • Is odd girth monotone in the right direction? Yes. If $F\subseteq H_r$, then every odd cycle of $F$ is an odd cycle of $H_r$, so $\mathrm{og}(F)\ge \mathrm{og}(H_r)$.

  • Is $S_N$ finite in Theorem 13? Yes. There are only finitely many graphs on at most $N$ labeled vertices, hence finitely many isomorphism types, and therefore finitely many finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs of order at most $N$.

  • Any hidden converse claim? None is made. Unbounded odd girth or unbounded order in every $\mathcal C_4(G)$ is proved only as a necessary condition for a positive answer, not as a sufficient one.

  • Compatibility with Round 2 Proposition 8. There is no conflict. Proposition 8 gave a sufficient condition for a counterexample pair via arbitrary family omission. Round 3 instead gives a one-graph obstruction criterion via boundedness in odd girth or size.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 3 advances beyond Round 2 in a mathematically substantive way.

  • It upgrades finite-family omission to bounded-odd-girth family omission (Proposition 9).
  • It proves that any positive solution to Problem #62 would force every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph to contain finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of arbitrarily large odd girth (Theorem 11).
  • It proves that any positive solution to Problem #62 would force every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph to contain finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraphs of arbitrarily large order (Theorem 13).
  • It reduces any future disproof to a single-graph construction: it would suffice to build one $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph whose finite $4$-vertex-critical spectrum is bounded in odd girth or in order (Corollary 15).
The main remaining gap is therefore sharply formulated: determine whether every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph must already have $4$-critical finite subgraphs of unbounded odd girth.

Erdős #62; independent attempt, 2026-09-23. Must two graphs of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ share a four-chromatic subgraph, or even one of chromatic number $\aleph_0$? I read the entire 62.tex. WORK: A COUNTABLE SPECTRUM MODEL

For any graph $G$ of infinite chromatic number, choose one finite graph $F_i$ from each isomorphism type of finite subgraph of $G$, and form their disjoint union $H=\bigsqcup_i F_i$. There are countably many finite graphs, so $H$ is countable. It has chromatic number exactly $\aleph_0$: if all $F_i$ were $r$-colourable for some fixed finite $r$, compactness would give an $r$-colouring of $G$, contrary to hypothesis. Compactness here is the finite intersection property in the product $\{1,\ldots,r\}^{V(G)}$, with the closed edge constraints. Every finite connected graph $F$ embeds in $H$ if and only if it embeds in $G$. The forward direction holds because the image of a connected graph lies in one component $F_i$; the reverse direction holds because a representative of $F$ is one of the components. In particular every finite vertex-critical graph is connected: otherwise a component of maximum chromatic number survives deletion of a vertex from another component. Thus \[ \mathcal C_4(H)=\mathcal C_4(G). \] The same construction works for every finite critical chromatic level.

ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

Disconnected finite spectra need not be identical, since the union may create new disjoint configurations; connectedness is why the critical-spectrum assertion is correct. A countable graph can never have chromatic number $\aleph_1$. Therefore realizing a desired finite spectrum by a disjoint union, even one with unbounded finite chromatic number, cannot supply the uncountably chromatic counterexample required by the problem. Conversely this observation does not prove that any prescribed countable spectrum has an uncountably chromatic realization. That realization obstacle remains untouched. No general common four-chromatic graph is found, and the stronger countably chromatic variant also remains unresolved.

Erdős Problem #62 (Round 2): Common $4$-chromatic subgraphs of $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (B): counterexample-driven gap-closure. Round 1 reduced the question to whether two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs must share some finite $4$-chromatic subgraph type, but left open how one could either force an intersection or construct two graphs with disjoint $4$-chromatic finite spectra.

In Round 2 I push the counterexample direction by sharpening the finite reduction (from "finite $4$-chromatic" to "finite $4$-critical"), and by proving that no fixed non-bipartite graph (hence no fixed $4$-chromatic graph) can be unavoidable in the class of uncountably chromatic graphs. This rules out an entire family of "single-template" proof strategies and isolates the precise combinatorial difficulty: simultaneous avoidance of unbounded-odd-girth $4$-critical graphs.

2) ROUND-1 FOUNDATION USED

I rely on the following Round 1 results (and do not re-prove them):
  • Lemma 1 (compactness for finite $k$-colorability): if every finite induced subgraph of $G$ is $k$-colorable then $G$ is $k$-colorable.
  • Lemma 2 (finite $(k+1)$-chromatic induced subgraph): if $\chi(G)>k$ then $G$ contains a finite induced subgraph $H$ with $\chi(H)=k+1$.
  • Round 1 gap formulation: for $\chi(G_i)=\aleph_1$, the problem reduces to whether the sets of isomorphism types of finite $4$-chromatic graphs appearing in $G_1$ and $G_2$ must intersect.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

  1. Critical reduction: it suffices to look for a finite $4$-vertex-critical common subgraph (a strictly smaller target family than all finite $4$-chromatic graphs).
  2. Odd-girth avoidance principle: using the existence of uncountably chromatic graphs with arbitrarily large odd girth, any fixed non-bipartite graph $H$ (in particular any fixed $4$-chromatic $H$) can be avoided by some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.
  3. Finite-family non-compactness: consequently, no finite list of $4$-chromatic graphs can serve as a universal "menu" from which every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph must contain at least one.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

  • Gap after Round 1: Round 1 showed each $G_i$ contains some finite $4$-chromatic subgraph, but did not control which. The missing step is either:
    1. prove an intersection theorem forcing a shared finite $4$-chromatic type, or
    2. build two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs with disjoint finite $4$-chromatic spectra.
  • Round 2 approach: I sharpen the reduction and then show that "single-template" and even "finite-template" strategies are doomed: for any predetermined (finite) candidate set, one can build an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph avoiding all of them. This isolates that any counterexample (or any positive proof) must engage with infinitely many $4$-critical graphs of unbounded odd girth.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

5.1. Reduction to finite $4$-critical graphs

Definition.
A finite graph $F$ is $4$-vertex-critical if $\chi(F)=4$ and for every vertex $v\in V(F)$ we have $\chi(F-v)\le 3$. (Equivalently: every proper induced subgraph of $F$ is $3$-colorable.)
Lemma 3 (every $\chi\ge 4$ graph contains a finite $4$-vertex-critical induced subgraph).
If $\chi(G)\ge 4$, then $G$ contains a finite induced subgraph $F$ that is $4$-vertex-critical.

Proof (using Round 1 Lemma 2). Apply Round 1 Lemma 2 with $k=3$ to obtain a finite induced subgraph $H\subseteq G$ with $\chi(H)=4$. Among all induced subgraphs of $H$ with chromatic number $4$, choose one $F$ with the minimum number of vertices. Then $\chi(F)=4$. If $F'$ is a proper induced subgraph of $F$, then by minimality $\chi(F')\le 3$. Thus $F$ is $4$-vertex-critical. $\square$

Corollary 4 (pairwise common $4$-chromatic $\Leftrightarrow$ common finite $4$-critical).
Let $G_1,G_2$ be graphs. The following are equivalent:
  1. There exists a graph $H$ with $\chi(H)=4$ that is a subgraph of both $G_1$ and $G_2$.
  2. There exists a finite $4$-vertex-critical graph $F$ that is a subgraph of both $G_1$ and $G_2$.
The same equivalence holds with "$\chi(H)=\aleph_0$" in place of "$\chi(H)=4$".

Proof. $2$$\Rightarrow$$1$ is trivial since $F$ itself has chromatic number $4$. For $1$$\Rightarrow$$2$, let $H$ be a common $4$-chromatic subgraph. By Lemma 3 applied to $H$, there is a finite induced subgraph $F\subseteq H$ that is $4$-vertex-critical. Since subgraph-containment is transitive, $F$ is a subgraph of both $G_1$ and $G_2$.

If $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$, then in particular $\chi(H)\ge 4$, so Lemma 3 again gives a finite $4$-vertex-critical induced subgraph $F\subseteq H$, hence common to $G_1,G_2$. $\square$

Consequence. Problem #62 is equivalent to:

Do any two graphs $G_1,G_2$ with $\chi(G_i)=\aleph_1$ necessarily contain a common finite $4$-vertex-critical subgraph?
This is a strictly sharper "finite target" than Round 1 (finite $4$-chromatic).

5.2. Odd-girth avoidance: no fixed $4$-chromatic template can be obligatory

Definition.
For a graph $G$, its odd girth $\mathrm{og}(G)$ is the minimum length of an odd cycle in $G$ if $G$ is non-bipartite, and $\infty$ if $G$ is bipartite.
External input (Erdős--Hajnal; Lovász).
For every integer $s\ge 3$ and every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, there exists a graph $G$ of cardinality $\kappa$ and chromatic number $\kappa$ containing no odd circuit of length $\le s$ (equivalently: $\mathrm{og}(G)>s$). This is stated in Hajnal--Komjáth $1984$ as known background, with pointer to Erdős--Hajnal $1966$ and Lovász $1969$ for constructive proofs.
Lemma 5 (avoidance of a fixed non-bipartite graph).
Let $H$ be any (finite or infinite) non-bipartite graph, and let $\kappa$ be any infinite cardinal. Then there exists a graph $G$ with $|V(G)|=\kappa$ and $\chi(G)=\kappa$ such that $H$ is not a subgraph of $G$. In particular, for $\kappa=\aleph_1$ there is an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph omitting $H$.

Proof. Since $H$ is non-bipartite, it contains at least one odd cycle; thus $\mathrm{og}(H)=:t<\infty$. Apply the external input with parameter $s=t$ to obtain a graph $G$ of size and chromatic number $\kappa$ with odd girth $\mathrm{og}(G)>t$. If there were an embedding of $H$ into $G$, the image of an odd cycle of length $t$ in $H$ would be an odd cycle of length $t$ in $G$, contradicting $\mathrm{og}(G)>t$. Hence $H$ does not embed into $G$. $\square$

Corollary 6 (no universal $4$-chromatic graph).
There is no fixed graph $H$ with $\chi(H)\ge 3$ (in particular, $\chi(H)=4$ or $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$) that is a subgraph of every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.

Reason. Apply Lemma 5 with $\kappa=\aleph_1$. $\square$

Corollary 7 (finite-family non-compactness for $4$-chromatic candidates).
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a finite set of graphs, each of chromatic number at least $3$. Then there exists an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ that contains none of the graphs in $\mathcal{H}$ as subgraphs. In particular, for any finite list of finite $4$-chromatic graphs $H_1,\dots,H_m$, there is an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph avoiding all of them.

Proof. Let $t:=\max\{\mathrm{og}(H):H\in\mathcal{H}\}$; this maximum is finite since $\mathcal{H}$ is finite and each $\mathrm{og}(H)<\infty$. Choose an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph $G$ with odd girth $>t$. Then no $H\in\mathcal{H}$ embeds into $G$ by the same odd-cycle argument as in Lemma 5. $\square$

Interpretation for Problem #62. Any proof of a positive answer cannot proceed by:

  • exhibiting a single $4$-chromatic "unavoidable" template, nor
  • exhibiting a finite list of $4$-chromatic templates, one of which must appear in every $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph.
Both strategies are refuted by Corollary 6 and Corollary 7.

5.3. Baseline common-subgraph facts (chromatic $2$ and $3$)

These do not solve the $4$-chromatic question, but they calibrate what is already forced.
Known fact A (finite obligatory graphs are exactly finite bipartite graphs).
Hajnal--Komjáth $1984$ recalls that a graph of uncountable chromatic number must contain a $4$-cycle and in fact must contain every finite bipartite graph. Consequently any two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs share, for each fixed finite bipartite graph $B$, a common copy of $B$ (hence a common $2$-chromatic subgraph).
Known fact B (a specific countable bipartite graph is obligatory).
Hajnal--Komjáth (1984, Theorem 1) proves that every graph $G$ with $\chi(G)\ge\aleph_1$ contains a specific countable bipartite graph $F$ (the "semicomplete bipartite" graph). Hence any two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs have a common countable subgraph of chromatic number $2$.
Known fact C (common $3$-chromatic subgraph).
Erdős--Hajnal--Shelah (as also recorded in the problem statement) implies that any two $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs have a common odd cycle $C_{2k+1}$ for some sufficiently large $k$, hence a common $3$-chromatic subgraph.

5.4. A precise reduction for a counterexample strategy

Let $\mathcal{C}_4$ denote the (countable) set of isomorphism types of finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs. For a graph $G$, let \[ \mathcal{C}_4(G):=\{F\in\mathcal{C}_4 : F\text{ embeds into }G\}. \] By Corollary 4, Problem #62 asks whether for all $\aleph_1$-chromatic $G_1,G_2$ we must have \[ \mathcal{C}_4(G_1)\cap\mathcal{C}_4(G_2)\ne\varnothing. \]
Proposition 8 (conditional counterexample reduction).
Assume the following
(*) For every subset $S\subseteq\mathcal{C}_4$ there exists a graph $G_S$ with $\chi(G_S)=\aleph_1$ such that $\mathcal{C}_4(G_S)\cap S=\varnothing$.
Then the answer to Problem #62 is negative.

Proof. Let $S\subseteq\mathcal{C}_4$ be arbitrary and put $T:=\mathcal{C}_4\setminus S$. Let $G_1:=G_S$ and $G_2:=G_T$ given by (*). Then $\mathcal{C}_4(G_1)\subseteq T$ and $\mathcal{C}_4(G_2)\subseteq S$, hence $\mathcal{C}_4(G_1)\cap\mathcal{C}_4(G_2)=\varnothing$. By Corollary 4, $G_1$ and $G_2$ have no common $4$-chromatic (nor $\aleph_0$-chromatic) subgraph. $\square$

What Round 2 adds: Proposition 8 formalizes the exact missing ingredient for a disproof: the ability to build $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs simultaneously omitting arbitrary specified families of finite $4$-critical graphs. Corollary 7 shows this is possible for any finite family (indeed any family with bounded odd girth), but it remains open for families with unbounded odd girth, which is precisely where $4$-critical graphs naturally live.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Transitivity check (Corollary 4): If $F$ is an induced subgraph of $H$ and $H$ is a subgraph of $G_i$, then $F$ is a subgraph of $G_i$ (edges of $F$ are edges of $H$ and hence map to edges of $G_i$). No induced-ness is needed for this transitivity.

  • Criticality definition: Lemma 3 produces a finite induced subgraph $F$ minimal among induced subgraphs of chromatic number $4$. This ensures vertex-criticality (every proper induced subgraph is $3$-colorable). No edge-critical property is claimed or used.

  • Odd-girth argument (Lemma 5): Any non-bipartite graph contains a finite odd cycle, so $\mathrm{og}(H)$ is a finite odd integer. Embedding preserves cycles, so forbidding cycles up to that length suffices.

  • External input scope: The avoidance Lemma 5 hinges on the existence of $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs with arbitrarily large odd girth. If this external fact were weakened (e.g., only large girth but not odd girth), the avoidance implication would fail for graphs whose shortest odd cycle is small.

  • No overreach: Proposition 8 is explicitly conditional; it does not assert (*) in ZFC. It only isolates (*) as a sufficient principle for a counterexample.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 2 advances beyond Round 1 by:

  • reducing Problem #62 to the intersection problem for finite $4$-vertex-critical graphs (Corollary 4);
  • proving that no fixed non-bipartite graph (hence no fixed $4$-chromatic or $\aleph_0$-chromatic graph) can be obligatory for $\aleph_1$-chromatic graphs (Lemma 5 and Corollary 6);
  • showing a non-compactness barrier: any finite list of $4$-chromatic candidates can be simultaneously avoided by some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph (Corollary 7);
  • isolating a concrete sufficient condition (*) that would yield a counterexample pair (Proposition 8).
