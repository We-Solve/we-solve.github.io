Math-1277

Cohomological dimension one is not a rational-point principle

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Colliot-Thélène's AIM question asks whether every smooth projective rationally connected variety $X/k$ has a $k$-point when $\operatorname{cd}(k)\leq1$. Rational connectedness is interpreted geometrically. For the characteristic-zero examples below, cohomological dimension at most one means vanishing of Galois cohomology above degree one for torsion discrete modules. The index is \[ \operatorname{ind}(X)=\gcd\{[k(x):k]:x\in X\text{ a closed point}\}. \] Index one is weaker than existence of a rational point.

The supplied report attributes two negative answers to different constructions over characteristic-zero fields of cohomological dimension one: Ax's smooth quintic hypersurface in ${\mathbb{P}}^9$, of index one and without rational points; and a Colliot-Thélène--Madore smooth cubic surface of index three and without rational points over their own field. These are not asserted to lie over a common field. The hypersurfaces are in the geometrically rationally connected class in the report's setting.

Its additional fixed-field corollary chooses the single Colliot-Thélène--Madore field $F$ and its cubic surface $S$. It claims that every smooth projective geometrically rationally connected $F$-curve has an $F$-point, while for every integer $n\geq2$ there exists a smooth projective geometrically rationally connected $n$-fold over the same $F$ with index three and no $F$-point. The reported construction may be expressed as \[ X_n=S\times_F{\mathbb{P}}_F^{n-2},\qquad \dim X_n=n,\quad\operatorname{ind}(X_n)=3,\quad X_n(F)=\varnothing. \] The curve assertion uses the genus-zero/conic interpretation and the vanishing of the Brauer group in cohomological dimension one. This is a dimension dichotomy over one field, rather than a new independent construction of that field.

The source remarks mention a false proof for an intersection of two quadrics in ${\mathbb{P}}^4$, a positive finite-field result attributed to Esnault, and a positive curve-function-field result attributed to Harris, Graber and Starr. The latter lacks its algebraically closed constant-field and characteristic hypotheses in the excerpt, so it is not a claim for arbitrary function fields. Further remarks describe hopes that universal torsors over surfaces might be birational to suitable homogeneous spaces, and an earlier cubic-surface point failure with $(\operatorname{Br}k)[3]=0$, attributed to Madore and Colliot-Thélène. These hopes are not established implications. The final "With hindsight" remark is truncated and gives no additional recoverable assertion.

Short English statement

Ask whether cohomological dimension at most one forces rational points on rationally connected varieties. The supplied literature claims refute this, including a cubic surface of index three. The report propagates that one surface to every dimension at least two over the same field, while genus-zero curves over that field still have rational points.
