Rational injectivity of maximal coarse assembly

OpenAI's claim

A coarse union of finite connected graphs with uniformly bounded degrees is claimed to have an infinite-order kernel class for maximal coarse assembly, refuting rational injectivity in that graph setting. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
OpenAI claims finite connected graphs $X_j$ of uniformly bounded vertex degree and their uniformly discrete coarse disjoint union $X=\bigsqcup_jX_j$, with an infinite-order class $\alpha\in KX_1(X)$ such that \[ \mu_X^{\max}(\alpha)=0\in K_1(C^*_{\max}(X)). \] Here $KX$ is coarse topological $K$-homology and $C^*_{\max}$ the maximal Roe completion of finite-propagation locally compact operators, without uniform rank bounds on compact matrix entries. Coarse disjoint union means only finitely many component pairs lie within any fixed distance. Thus maximal coarse assembly fails rational injectivity.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 307, 2026-10-05): Two torus-graph ends are linked by a long collar carrying finite congruence fibers. Growing roots of pulled-back line bundles retain an integral K-homology difference at every fixed Rips scale. Horizontal transport followed by Kazhdan averaging splits the collar's maximal Roe-algebra K-theory map, annihilating the assembly image while leaving the topological class persistent.
