Math-1911

One-sided full-shift automorphisms

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a finite nonempty alphabet, $X=A^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}}$ with the product topology, and let $\sigma_+:X\to X$ be the left shift. An automorphism of this one-sided full shift is a homeomorphism $T:X\to X$ satisfying $T\sigma_+=\sigma_+T$. Although $\sigma_+$ is generally not invertible, the automorphism $T$ is invertible.

For a compatible metric $d$ on $X$, $T$ is expansive if \[ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x,y\in X,\qquad \left[d(T^n x,T^n y)<\delta\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right]\Longrightarrow x=y. \] The source asks whether, for every such expansive $T$, there are a finite nonempty alphabet $B$ and a homeomorphism $h:X\to B^{{\mathbb{Z}}}$ such that \[ h\circ T=\sigma_B\circ h, \] where $\sigma_B$ is the two-sided full shift. The size of $B$ is not specified. This is conjugacy of the dynamical system $(X,T)$, not of the noninvertible map $\sigma_+$.

The supplied research summary attributes to Nasu the weaker conclusion that an expansive automorphism of a one-sided shift of finite type is conjugate to a two-sided shift of finite type. A shift of finite type is defined by finitely many forbidden finite blocks; it need not be a full shift. Therefore that quoted theorem does not by itself establish the source's full-shift conclusion. The summary alternates between full shift and shift of finite type, so its solved label cannot settle that distinction without a precise theorem and any additional hypotheses.

Short English statement

Is every expansive invertible map commuting with a one-sided full shift conjugate to a two-sided full shift? The supplied solved-status summary quotes a shift-of-finite-type conclusion, which is weaker than the full-shift target.
