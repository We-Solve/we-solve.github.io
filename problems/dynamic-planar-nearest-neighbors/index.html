Dynamic Planar Nearest Neighbors

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Maintain a finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ under insertions of new sites and deletions of existing sites. A query supplies $q\in\mathbb R^2$ and must return a site $p\in P$ minimizing $\|q-p\|_2$. Any minimizer is acceptable in a tie; when $P$ is empty, the structure reports this fact. Distances are exact, not approximate.

Is there a data structure supporting insertions, deletions, and nearest-neighbor queries all in $O(\log(n+2))$ time, where $n$ is the current number of sites? Use the usual real-arithmetic model for planar geometric data structures, with polynomial space and preprocessing. The strongest reading requests a worst-case bound for every operation. An amortized or randomized solution must state its guarantee explicitly; these are distinct performance formulations of the source's general target.

Updates may occur in arbitrary order, and their locations are not assumed to follow a random distribution. A structure allowing only insertions does not meet the fully dynamic requirement. Nor does logarithmic query time alone suffice when one update operation is slower. For an amortized formulation, the running time is assessed over whole update sequences, rather than charging every deletion its own worst-case cost. The source's related convex-hull reduction can guide constructions but does not itself supply the requested simultaneous bounds.

Short English statement

Can a fully dynamic planar point set support exact nearest-neighbor queries, insertions, and deletions in logarithmic time per operation?
