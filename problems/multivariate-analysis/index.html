Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let the $n$ columns $M_j\in\mathbb R^p$ be independent with law $N_p(\mu,\Sigma)$, where $\Sigma$ is positive definite with eigenvalues $\eta_1\ge\cdots\ge\eta_k>1$ and $\eta_{k+1}=\cdots=\eta_p=1$, with $k$ fixed as $p,n\to\infty$. Put $\bar M=n^{-1}\sum_j M_j$, $\widehat M_j=M_j-\bar M$, $S=n^{-1}\widehat M\widehat M^T$, and write $l_1\ge\cdots\ge l_p\ge0$ for the sample eigenvalues. Assume $p/n\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Determine the asymptotic distributions of the leading sample eigenvalues $l_1,l_2,\ldots$ for these real spiked populations, identifying their appropriate centerings, scalings and joint laws throughout the different spike regimes, including the transition at $\eta=1+\sqrt\gamma$. [S2]

Short English statement

Find the limiting eigenvalue statistics for high-dimensional real Gaussian covariance matrices with finitely many population spikes, including phase transitions.

Removing the empirical mean exactly.
This attempt checks a normalization issue before any spike asymptotics. Let $Z$ be a $p\times n$ matrix of independent standard normal entries, and write $M=\mu\mathbf1^T+\Sigma^{1/2}Z$. With $P=I_n-n^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$, the centered matrix is $\widehat M=\Sigma^{1/2}ZP$. Choose an orthogonal matrix whose last column is $n^{-1/2}\mathbf1$. Orthogonal invariance of each Gaussian row shows that $ZO$ again has independent standard normal entries. Consequently \[ S\ \stackrel{d}=\ \frac1n\Sigma^{1/2}XX^T\Sigma^{1/2} =\frac{n-1}{n}\widetilde S, \qquad \widetilde S=\frac1{n-1}\Sigma^{1/2}XX^T\Sigma^{1/2},\] where $X$ is $p\times(n-1)$. This identity is exact, including when $p\ge n$; in that case the rank is at most $n-1$ and there are the corresponding zero sample eigenvalues.
What this transfers and what it does not.
Suppose a theorem for the uncentered model supplies a deterministic centering $c_{p,n-1}$, positive scale $a_{p,n-1}$, and a joint limit for a finite list of eigenvalues. Multiplying both center and scale by $(n-1)/n$ transfers that theorem exactly to the stated centered convention. One must use aspect ratio $p/(n-1)$ in the uncentered theorem. For example, an $O(n^{-1})$ centering correction is negligible at scale $n^{-2/3}$ or $n^{-1/2}$, provided its coefficient is uniformly bounded; this follows from the respective ratios $n^{-1/3}$ and $n^{-1/2}$ tending to zero. It need not be discarded before checking a critical spike tuning.
First gap.
The calculation removes neither the spectral interaction among spikes nor the critical transition. It does not derive any joint edge or outlier law, and it assumes no theorem about those laws. Deriving the required limiting distribution for $\widetilde S$, uniformly in the relevant critical tuning, remains the first missing step. The elementary Gaussian reduction is known in substance and is not counted as new progress on the complete distributional problem.
