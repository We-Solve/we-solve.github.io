A split status sentence for Weiss's divergent-orbit conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This is the first part of a literature-status sentence following B. Weiss's Conjecture 48, not a standalone conjecture. It says that [200] formulated the conjecture and checked special cases, then stops before saying which ones. The following companion record names one-dimensional $D$ [38] and the full maximal real split torus case $D=A$ [188].

The antecedent setup, in companion permanent ID $20003342$, has a semisimple real algebraic group $G$ with arithmetic structure, a noncocompact arithmetic lattice $\Gamma$, and a closed subgroup $D$ of a maximal real split torus $A$. For $x=g\Gamma$, divergence means properness of $D\to G/\Gamma$, $d\mapsto dx$: every sequence leaving compact subsets of $D$ has image leaving compact subsets of $G/\Gamma$. The source calls divergence "obvious" when finitely many open subsemigroups covering $D$ have representation-vector contraction certificates. For the usual rational certificate, the representations are defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ and the witness vectors must be nonzero rational vectors, with the arithmetic lattice structure ensuring discreteness; the terse antecedent does not explicitly state all those witness conditions. Allowing a zero witness would make the criterion vacuous.

Conjecture 48 compares $\dim D$ with $\operatorname{rank}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}G$: no divergent orbits above the rational rank, only obvious ones at equality, and nonobvious ones below it. This is a historical formulation. Companion research material warns that the last clause needs almost-${\mathbb{Q}}$-simple or appropriate factorwise scope and fails literally for arbitrary semisimple products, so a blanket modern resolution of all three clauses is not asserted.

The present aid supplies a precise active-weight criterion for a single sector. Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional real exponent space, $C\subset W$ a closed, pointed, full-dimensional convex cone, and $S=\operatorname{int}C$. Let a real split torus representation have weight decomposition $V=\bigoplus_\chi V_\chi$, and let $0\ne w=\sum_\chi w_\chi$. A weight is active if $w_\chi\ne0$. Write \[ C^*=\{\ell\in W^*: \ell(H)\ge0\text{ for every }H\in C\}. \] The reported equivalence is \[ \rho(\exp H_j)w\longrightarrow0 \text{ for every }H_j\in S\text{ with }\|H_j\|\to\infty \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -\chi\in\operatorname{int}(C^*)\text{ for every active }\chi. \] Equivalently there are $K,\varepsilon>0$ such that \[ \|\rho(\exp H)w\|\le Ke^{-\varepsilon\|H\|}\quad(H\in S). \] Thus strict contraction on every unbounded sector sequence requires a uniform margin including directions approaching its boundary. For an irredundantly faceted polyhedral cone $C=\{H:\ell_j(H)\ge0,\ 1\le j\le m\}$, the finite certificate is \[ -\chi=\sum_{j=1}^m c_{\chi j}\ell_j,\qquad c_{\chi j}>0 \quad\text{for every active }\chi. \] Interiors here are taken in the displayed exponent space; the full-dimensional convention avoids silently using an ambient interior for a lower-dimensional sector.

For the modular lattice model $a_t=\operatorname{diag}(e^t,e^{-t})$, a frame $g$ whose two inverse coordinate lines are rational has nonzero $v_+,v_-\in{\mathbb{Q}}^2$ with $gv_+\in{\mathbb{R}} e_2$, $gv_-\in{\mathbb{R}} e_1$. These are explicit standard-representation shrinking witnesses on the forward and backward sectors, respectively; this includes $g\in\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})$. The criterion concerns rational certificates for such models, not existence of a certificate for every divergent orbit. All auxiliary results are supplied machine-generated aid requiring independent verification.

Short English statement

This fragment reports special cases of Weiss's divergent-orbit conjecture. Its supplied mathematical content is a sector test: every active weight must be strictly negative with a uniform margin on the closed cone, giving a finite positive-normal certificate for polyhedral sectors.
