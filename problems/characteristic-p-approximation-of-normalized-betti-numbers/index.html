Characteristic-p Approximation of Normalized Betti Numbers

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, where $p$ is prime; the record does not assume that $k$ is finite. Let $\Gamma$ be a residually finite group, meaning that every nonidentity element survives in some finite quotient of $\Gamma$. Consider a descending sequence \[ \Gamma=\Gamma_0\geq\Gamma_1\geq\Gamma_2\geq\cdots, \qquad [\Gamma:\Gamma_i]<\infty, \qquad \bigcap_{i\geq0}\Gamma_i=\{1\}. \] The supplied statement calls this a residual chain but does not explicitly specify whether every $\Gamma_i$ must be normal in $\Gamma$. Thus the admissible class of chains, and consequently the scope of the independence question below, requires source confirmation.

Let $X$ be a finite free $\Gamma$-CW complex. Under the usual equivariant interpretation of this terminology, the action is free and there are finitely many $\Gamma$-orbits of cells, so that $\Gamma\backslash X$ is a finite CW complex. This interpretation of "finite" requires confirmation from the source; finiteness of the underlying CW complex is a different condition. No contractibility or connectedness hypothesis on $X$ is supplied.

For each integer $n\geq0$, put \[ b_n(\Gamma_i\backslash X;k) :=\dim_k H_n(\Gamma_i\backslash X;k), \] where $H_n(-;k)$ denotes ordinary singular homology with coefficients in $k$, equivalently cellular homology for these CW complexes. The index $[\Gamma:\Gamma_i]$ is the number of cosets of $\Gamma_i$ in $\Gamma$.

The supplied question asks, for every such $k$, $\Gamma$, and $X$, and every integer $n\geq0$:

  1. Does the finite real limit \[ L_n(\Gamma,X;k;(\Gamma_i)) :=\lim_{i\to\infty} \frac{b_n(\Gamma_i\backslash X;k)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_i]} \] exist for every admissible residual chain?
  2. Whenever these limits exist, are they independent of the residual chain? More explicitly, for two admissible residual chains $(\Gamma_i)$ and $(\Lambda_j)$ in the same group, must \[ \lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{b_n(\Gamma_i\backslash X;k)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_i]} = \lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{b_n(\Lambda_j\backslash X;k)}{[\Gamma:\Lambda_j]}? \]
  3. If a chain-independent limit exists, what invariant of the data $(\Gamma,X,k,n)$ equals it?
The record supplies no proposed invariant or precise candidate equality. Its original wording asks about "every group," whereas its normalized statement restricts to residually finite groups equipped with a residual chain. No formulation for groups without such a chain is supplied, so an extension to those groups remains unspecified.

Short English statement

For a field of positive characteristic and a free group action with finite CW quotient, ask whether the Betti numbers of the finite covers, divided by their covering degrees, converge along every residual chain. Ask also whether the limit is independent of the chain and which invariant describes it. The intended normality condition on the chain and the finiteness convention require confirmation. The supplied open label is dated curation, not an independently verified status.
