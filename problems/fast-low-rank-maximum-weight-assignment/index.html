Fast Low-Rank Maximum-Weight Assignment

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A perfect matching of a complete bipartite graph selects one incident edge at every vertex, maximizing the sum of selected entries of $W$. Rank $r$ is the dimension of the weight matrix's column space.

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. Matrix completion reconstructs a low-rank matrix from a subset of its entries; matrix sensing reconstructs it from specified linear measurements. Tensor decomposition represents a multiway array as a sum of simple outer products.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For the maximum-weight assignment problem on a complete bipartite graph with weight matrix $W\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ of rank $r$, can the optimum be computed in time $O(nr^2\log n)$? [S2]

Literature and context check

The original report, p. 3012, explicitly gives the rank-one sorting observation and asks about rank two and beyond. The representation of the real weight matrix is not fixed. The attempt separates an entry-query obstruction from a concrete failure of extending one-coordinate sorting to factored rank two.

Research attempt: A rank-one access obstruction and a rank-two sorting failure

In a deterministic entry-query model, an unknown rank-one matrix need not reveal its factors quickly. Consider nonnegative matrices with exactly one positive entry and all others zero; every such matrix has rank exactly one. Suppose an algorithm has received zero on $q<n^2-n$ queried locations and now outputs a perfect matching $P$. More than $n$ locations are unqueried, while $P$ contains exactly $n$ locations. Choose an unqueried location outside $P$ and put a one there, zero elsewhere. This matrix is compatible with every query response, has rank one, gives the reported matching weight zero, and has optimum one because the special edge can be extended to a perfect matching. Hence no exact algorithm can always terminate with a correct matching after fewer than $n^2-n$ queries on this adversarial transcript. In particular the requested near-linear rank-one bound cannot hold for this unrestricted entry-query representation.

This is not an obstruction when factors are supplied. If $W=uv^T$, sort both vectors in increasing order and match corresponding entries. Any assignment with $u_i\le u_j$ but assigned $v_a>v_b$ improves or stays equal after swapping, since the gain is $(u_j-u_i)(v_a-v_b)\ge0$. Repeated removal of inversions yields the sorted matching, proving optimality even with negative entries and ties. Factor access permits the report's $O(n\log n)$ comparison bound.

A straightforward rank-two extension, sorting points by their first coordinate, fails. Use row factors $u_1=(0,M)$, $u_2=(1,0)$ and column factors $v_1=(0,1)$, $v_2=(1,M)$, with $M\ge2$. Both first-coordinate orders are $1,2$, so that rule picks the diagonal of \[ W=\begin{pmatrix}M&M^2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \] Its value is $M+1$, while the other matching has value $M^2>M+1$ for $M\ge2$. The determinant is $M\ne0$, so this is genuinely rank two. The difference in values can grow quadratically in $M$. This refutes that fixed-coordinate rule, not all geometric rank-two algorithms or all possible projections chosen adaptively from the data.
