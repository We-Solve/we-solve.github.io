Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A unitary braided fusion category is a semisimple rigid tensor category with finitely many simple objects, a unitary structure and compatible braiding. A strong tensor generator $X$ produces all objects in the stated generating sense. The local algebra on a finite region $I$ is $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal B}(X^{\otimes I})$. Use the source's canonical state formed from tensor powers of its map $\mathbf1\to X$ and the resulting represented net. Superselection sectors encode inequivalent localized charge representations of that net.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a unitary braided fusion category with strong tensor generator $X$, and let its braided fusion spin system be the associated two-dimensional net with local algebras $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{B}}(X^{\otimes I})$. Prove that the superselection-sector category of this net is equivalent to the $W^*$-categorical completion of $\mathcal{B}$.
Scope and source qualification.
The primary report already proves equivalence as a $W^*$ category. Its Conjecture 1 asks for equivalence as a $W^*$ braided tensor category, preserving tensor product and braiding. This additional structure is missing from the condensed catalogue wording below and constitutes the remaining conjecture.

Short English statement

Prove that the charge-sector equivalence with the input category's completion also preserves tensor product and braiding.

Why an equivalence of underlying categories cannot decide braiding.
Consider finite-dimensional $\mathbb Z_2$-graded Hilbert spaces, with degree-preserving maps and the usual graded tensor product. There are two unitary braidings on homogeneous tensors of degrees $a,b\in\{0,1\}$: \[ c^+_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=w\otimes v, \qquad c^-_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{ab}w\otimes v. \] Naturality is immediate because maps preserve degrees. The hexagon signs agree because $(-1)^{a(b+c)}=(-1)^{ab}(-1)^{ac}$, with the analogous identity in the other variable. Both therefore give braided tensor structures on the same underlying finite semisimple $W^*$ category.
An invariant that separates them.
Let $s$ be the one-dimensional odd object. Then $s\otimes s$ is the tensor unit and $c^+_{s,s}=+I$, whereas $c^-_{s,s}=-I$. A monoidal equivalence must fix the unit and hence carry the only other simple object to itself. Its tensor comparison on $s\otimes s$ is an invertible scalar; conjugation by that scalar cannot change the sign of the self-braiding. Thus no braided equivalence identifies these two structures.
