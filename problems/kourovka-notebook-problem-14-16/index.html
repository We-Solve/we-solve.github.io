Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.16

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group and let $V$ be any set of group words, each involving finitely many variables. Define the set of word values and their inverses by \[ W_V(G)=\{w(g_1,\ldots,g_k)^{\varepsilon}: w\in V,\ w\text{ involves }k\text{ variables},\ g_1,\ldots,g_k\in G,\ \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\}. \] The verbal subgroup associated with $V$ is $V(G)=\langle W_V(G)\rangle$. Following the attribution to Yu. I. Merzlyakov in the supplied source, its width with respect to $V$ is finite if there exists a nonnegative integer $m$ such that every $h\in V(G)$ admits an expression \[ h=x_1\cdots x_r,\qquad 0\le r\le m,\qquad x_i\in W_V(G). \] The empty product is the identity. The width is the least such bound, subject to the convention for whether zero is included among natural numbers, and is $\infty$ if no finite bound exists. The value $\infty$ indicates unbounded finite product lengths, not infinite products.

For a finitely generated group $G$, choose a finite symmetric generating set $S$, and let \[ \gamma_{G,S}(n)=\left|\{g\in G: |g|_S\le n\}\right|,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, \] where $|g|_S$ is the least number of generators from $S$ whose product is $g$. The group has polynomial growth if $\gamma_{G,S}(n)\le C(n+1)^d$ for all $n$, for some $C>0$ and nonnegative integer $d$. It has intermediate growth if its growth is subexponential, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{G,S}(n)^{1/n}=1, \] but it has no polynomial growth bound. These growth classifications do not depend on the chosen finite generating set.

Problem (V. G. Bardakov, 1999; catalogue entry). Is it true that for every finitely generated group $G$ of intermediate growth and every set $V$ of group words, the verbal subgroup $V(G)$ has finite width with respect to $V$? The bound may depend on both $G$ and $V$.

The supplied source states as background that the analogous finite-width assertion holds for finitely generated groups of polynomial growth. This background assertion and the supplied open status are retained as source claims; neither has been independently verified here.

Short English statement

Does every set of group words have a finite product-length bound in every finitely generated group of intermediate growth? More precisely, must every element of the associated verbal subgroup be a product of a uniformly bounded number of word values or their inverses, with the bound allowed to depend on the group and the set of words?
