A topological $3$-manifold is a Hausdorff, second-countable space locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^3$ (or a half-space at a boundary when boundaries are allowed). Literature and context check

The original 2015 problem list, Problem 3.10, was consulted. "Practical" has no quantitative threshold or benchmark there. The following exact finite algorithm isolates one useful positive certificate and explicitly tests why that certificate alone is not a homeomorphism decision procedure.

Research attempt: A complete simplicial certificate stops short of homeomorphism recognition

Restrict temporarily to two connected triangulated $3$-manifolds encoded by $n$ tetrahedra each, with affine face-pairing permutations and any unglued boundary faces listed. This is a chosen finite input model, not a restriction silently imposed on the original agenda. Fix a source tetrahedron. For each target tetrahedron and each of its $24$ vertex correspondences, propagate that correspondence across adjacent faces. Once the image and vertex permutation of a tetrahedron are known, its neighboring tetrahedron's image and permutation are forced by the two face pairings; the fourth vertex maps to the fourth vertex. Reject a seed if a previously assigned image or permutation conflicts, if injectivity fails, or if a boundary face would map to a glued face.

If the propagation succeeds, connectedness reaches all tetrahedra. The map is a bijection and respects every face identification. On each tetrahedron use the affine map on its vertices. These maps agree on identified faces and so descend to a continuous map of the quotient manifolds; the inverse affine maps also agree, yielding a continuous inverse. This is a verified homeomorphism. Conversely every combinatorial isomorphism supplies one of the $24n$ seeds, and its forced propagation cannot fail. Thus this algorithm decides combinatorial isomorphism, using $O(n)$ operations per seed and $O(n^2)$ total operations in the finite permutation/word model, with $O(n)$ auxiliary storage. Different tetrahedron counts reject combinatorial isomorphism immediately.

The attempted extension treated a rejection as a topological negative answer. It fails on an explicit elementary pair. A single tetrahedron is a triangulated $3$-ball. Insert a point in its interior and cone it to its four boundary faces; the resulting four tetrahedra triangulate the very same ball. The identity map of that ball is a homeomorphism, but one and four tetrahedra cannot be in bijection. The obstruction persists for closed examples by performing the same subdivision inside one tetrahedron of a closed triangulation, increasing the tetrahedron count by three without changing the underlying manifold.

Thus the certificate can safely accept some pairs and can provide exact output, but its negative answer concerns the encodings only. Enlarging it to a search over subdivisions or local moves needs a terminating stopping criterion and an analysis of resource use. Neither is obtained from seed propagation. In particular, counting $24n$ seeds does not bound the number of alternative triangulations that might need consideration.

Verification and adversarial audit

The propagation proof includes boundary faces, repeated visits and injectivity, and checks both soundness and completeness for combinatorial isomorphism. The one-to-four interior subdivision falsifies the proposed topological use of rejection. No software benchmark or complete homeomorphism recognizer was implemented or run.
