Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 3.10

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F_n$ be the free group of finite rank $n\geq2$, considered in the pure first-order language of groups. A subset $H\subseteq F_n$ is definable with parameters if there are a group-language formula $\varphi(x;y_1,\ldots,y_m)$, $m\geq0$, and parameters $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in F_n$ such that \[ H=\{g\in F_n:F_n\models\varphi(g;a_1,\ldots,a_m)\}. \] The conjecture asks whether every such $H$ that is a subgroup satisfies \[ H=F_n\quad\text{or}\quad \exists h\in F_n:\ H=\langle h\rangle. \] The trivial subgroup is included as cyclic by taking $h=1$. No parameter-free, finite-index or finite-generation restriction on $H$ is imposed. The rank-one case is already cyclic and adds no nonabelian case.

The supplied nested research summary reports an affirmative theorem, first obtained from Kharlampovich--Myasnikov's definable-set results (November 2011 preprint; 2013 publication) and independently reproved by Perin--Pillay--Sklinos--Tent, Theorem 4.3 (2014), in the stronger torsion-free hyperbolic setting. It connects the result to Malcev's 1965 question. These are supplied literature claims, not a new proof.

The registry labels the item solved and SOLVED-IN-LITERATURE. The older source was manually transcribed from the 2004 list, accessed 2026-07-29, and its original status/rights evidence remains NEEDS_REVIEW. This draft preserves those provenance qualifications and does not claim an independent source or current-status check.

Short English statement

Every first-order definable subgroup of a nonabelian finite-rank free group, allowing parameters, should be either cyclic or the whole group. The supplied later literature summary reports this theorem proved, including a stronger torsion-free hyperbolic version.
