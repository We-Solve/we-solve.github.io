Math-7471

Uniqueness of Fixed-Bounce Geodesic Loops in Billiards

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be a billiard domain and $x\in\partial\Omega$. The supplied record does not specify the ambient geometry, dimension, boundary regularity, or convexity assumptions on $\Omega$. For a positive integer $q$, write formally \[ \mathcal L_q(\Omega,x)=\left\{\gamma:\ \gamma\text{ is a billiard geodesic loop based at }x,\ \operatorname{wind}(\gamma)=1,\ N_{\mathrm{refl}}(\gamma)=q\right\}. \] Here a loop based at $x$ begins and ends at $x$; $N_{\mathrm{refl}}$ denotes the number of reflections under the intended counting convention, and $\operatorname{wind}$ denotes the intended winding number. These last two conventions are not defined in the supplied record. In the usual Euclidean interpretation, a billiard path consists of straight segments joined at boundary contacts satisfying the equal-angle reflection law wherever that law is defined. This interpretation alone does not resolve the missing hypotheses or conventions.

Question, conditional on specifying the admissible class and conventions. For every domain $\Omega$, boundary point $x$, and reflection count $q$ in the intended ranges, does there exist exactly one such loop, that is, \[ \#\mathcal L_q(\Omega,x)=1? \] If its unique member is denoted by $\gamma_{q,x}$, the proposed loop-length function is \[ \psi_q(x)=\operatorname{Length}(\gamma_{q,x}),\qquad x\in\partial\Omega. \] The assertion is an existence-and-uniqueness question, not a theorem asserted here. The source leaves unspecified the allowable values of $q$, the meaning of winding number, whether the base point counts as a reflection, whether the reflection law is imposed at the closing contact, and when two loops are identified, including under reparametrization or reversal. Consequently, the displayed formulation is a conditional formalization of the supplied target; it is not a fully specified conjecture for arbitrary billiard domains. Uniqueness of a loop would make its length unambiguous, but a common length for several loops would be a weaker property.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether each boundary point of a billiard domain supports exactly one loop with winding number $1$ and $q$ reflections, thereby defining its length $\psi_q(x)$. A precise conjecture requires additional domain hypotheses and conventions for winding number, reflection counting, and equality of loops. The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved; that status is not independently verified here.
