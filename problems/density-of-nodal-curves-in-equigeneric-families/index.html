Density of Nodal Curves in Equigeneric Families

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a smooth projective surface, let $\xi$ be a curve class on $S$, and let $g$ be the prescribed genus. The supplied record denotes by $V_g^\xi$ a parameter space of reduced curves on $S$ of class $\xi$ and genus $g$. It does not specify the base field, the equivalence relation defining the class, the construction of this parameter space, or the genus convention for reducible curves. In particular, reducedness must not silently be strengthened to irreducibility or connectedness.

Once those choices are fixed, put \[ V_{g,\mathrm{nod}}^\xi =\{[C]\in V_g^\xi:\text{every singularity of }C\text{ is an ordinary node}\}. \] For the geometric interpretation over a field $k$, an ordinary node at a singular closed point $p$ of $C_{\bar k}$ means \[ \widehat{\mathcal O}_{C_{\bar k},p} \cong \bar k[[u,v]]/(uv), \] where $\bar k$ is an algebraic closure of $k$. Whether this geometric interpretation is intended requires confirmation. Under the convention that every singularity must be a node, smooth curves are included.

The density question, if Zariski density is intended, is whether \[ \overline{V_{g,\mathrm{nod}}^\xi}^{\,V_g^\xi}=V_g^\xi. \] Equivalently, every nonempty relatively Zariski open subset of $V_g^\xi$ should contain a curve whose singularities are all ordinary nodes. This formulation also covers the empty parameter space, for which the equality is vacuous. The record gives no further restrictions on $S$, $\xi$, or $g$; whether the intended question is universal or restricted to particular families, including $K3$ surfaces, remains unresolved.

The record also asks whether density can be proved using a proposed local criterion involving an equisingular ideal $I$ and an object $A$, expressed in the cleaned statement as \[ I\subsetneq A \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the singularity is worse than an ordinary node}. \] Neither $A$, the precise equisingular ideal $I$, nor the meaning of "worse than an ordinary node" is defined in the supplied record. Its original wording uses $I\subset A$, whereas the cleaned wording explicitly requires proper containment. Accordingly, this criterion is retained as a source claim requiring clarification, not asserted as a theorem. The local-to-global deformation hypotheses needed to connect such a criterion to density are also unspecified.

Short English statement

The question asks whether reduced curves of a fixed class and genus on a smooth projective surface can be approximated within their parameter space by curves having only ordinary nodes. It also proposes an equisingular-ideal criterion as a possible route to density. The field, parameter space, genus convention, scope, and local ideal notation need clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
