Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.74

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The principal target is part (b) of Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.74, proposed by V. V. Nekrashevich in 2006.

A group $G$ is amenable if there exists a finitely additive probability measure $\mu\colon\mathcal P(G)\to[0,1]$ such that $\mu(gA)=\mu(A)$ for every $g\in G$ and every $A\subseteq G$.

Part (b) asks whether every group generated by automata of polynomial growth, in the sense of S. Sidki's cited 2004 paper, is amenable. The source does not reproduce Sidki's definition. A conventional polynomial-activity interpretation can be made precise as follows. Let $X$ be a finite nonempty alphabet, and let $X^*$ be the rooted tree of finite words over $X$. For a rooted-tree automorphism $g$ and a word $v$, its section $g|_v$ is characterized by \[ g(vw)=g(v)\,g|_v(w)\qquad(w\in X^*). \] An automorphism is finite-state if its set of sections is finite. Define \[ a_g(n)=\left|\{v\in X^n:g|_v\ne\mathrm{id}\}\right|. \] It has polynomial activity if there exist $C>0$ and $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}$ such that \[ a_g(n)\le C(n+1)^d\qquad\text{for every }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}. \] Under this interpretation, the question is whether, for every such alphabet $X$ and every set $S\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(X^*)$ of finite-state automorphisms of polynomial activity, the group $\langle S\rangle$ is amenable. The constants and degree may depend on the generator. This is an explicit interpretation requiring comparison with Sidki's precise convention; the supplied source does not specify whether the generating automata must form a finite family or share a uniform polynomial bound.

The record also retains archived part (a). It describes a purported group $G=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle$ of permutations of ${\mathbb{Z}}$, with formulas \[ \alpha(n)=n+1,\qquad \beta(0)=0,\qquad \beta(2^k m)=2^k(m+2), \] and asks whether $G$ is amenable. The supplied qualification is that $m$ is odd and $n$ is a positive integer. No range for $k$ is supplied, and the positive-integer restriction on $n$ does not specify $\alpha$ on all of ${\mathbb{Z}}$. Consequently, these formulas are preserved without treating them as a complete definition of the claimed permutation group. The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer to part (a), attributed to G. Amir, O. Angel and B. Virág; that report is not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Part (b) asks whether every group generated by polynomial-growth automata in Sidki's sense is amenable. A conventional polynomial-activity formulation is provided, with its correspondence to the cited definition left for review. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports an affirmative solution to archived part (a), whose displayed generator definitions contain unresolved domain qualifications.
