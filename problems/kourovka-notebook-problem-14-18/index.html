Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.18

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Lambda$ be a totally ordered abelian group. A $\Lambda$-tree is a $\Lambda$-valued metric space in which any two points are joined by a unique segment isometric to an interval in $\Lambda$, intersections of segments with a common endpoint are segments, and two segments meeting only at a common endpoint have a segment as their union. Group actions on such trees are understood to be by isometries.

A family $\mathcal D$ of groups discriminates a group $G$ if, for every finite subset $F\subseteq G\setminus\{1_G\}$, there exist $D\in\mathcal D$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to D$ such that \[ \varphi(a)\ne 1_D\qquad\text{for every }a\in F. \] The group $D$ and the homomorphism $\varphi$ may depend on $F$. A group is torsion-free if its identity is its only element of finite order. A hyperbolic group means a finitely generated group whose Cayley graph for a finite generating set has uniformly thin geodesic triangles.

Question. Is every finitely generated group $G$ admitting a free action on some $\Lambda$-tree discriminated by the family of torsion-free hyperbolic groups? Explicitly, does every such $G$ satisfy \[ \forall F\subseteq G\setminus\{1_G\},\quad |F|<\infty \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists D\ \exists\varphi:G\to D \quad \begin{cases} D\text{ is torsion-free and hyperbolic},\\ \varphi\text{ is a group homomorphism},\\ \varphi(a)\ne 1_D\text{ for all }a\in F? \end{cases} \] Here $\Lambda$ and the tree may depend on $G$; the supplied statement places no further restriction on $\Lambda$. Freeness requires that no nonidentity element fix a point. For general ordered abelian groups, the supplied record does not specify whether freeness additionally excludes inversions of segments, whose midpoints need not exist in the tree. This convention remains unresolved, and the question retains the source's term "free action" subject to that qualification.

Short English statement

Must every finitely generated group acting freely on some $\Lambda$-tree admit, for each finite set of nonidentity elements, a homomorphism to a torsion-free hyperbolic group that keeps every element of that set nonidentity? The target may vary with the finite set. The source leaves the convention concerning inversions in a free action unspecified.
