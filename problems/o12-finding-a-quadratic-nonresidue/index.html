O12 — Finding a quadratic nonresidue

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be an odd prime, given in binary, and write $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. A quadratic nonresidue is an element $a\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ for which $x^2=a$ has no solution in $\mathbb F_p$. Equivalently, its Legendre symbol is $\left(\frac ap\right)=-1$, or $a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\pmod p$.

Does a deterministic classical algorithm, on every odd prime input $p$, produce an integer $a$ with $1\le a<p$ having this property in time polynomial in $\ell(p)=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor+1$? The exponent and coefficients of the time bound must be independent of $p$. The prime $2$ is excluded because its unit group contains no quadratic nonresidue; this is the necessary nonempty-domain convention for the catalogue question.

The output may be any nonresidue. It need not be the least positive one, nor belong to a prescribed small interval. Primality of the input is a promise; no factorization of $p-1$ or other auxiliary data are supplied. Testing a proposed answer by the displayed modular exponentiation is efficient, but the question concerns finding a successful proposal without random sampling. An exhaustive search through candidates would answer the question only if its worst-case cost is proved polynomial in binary input length, with no unproved hypothesis on character sums or zeroes of zeta functions.

Short English statement

Can one find a quadratic nonresidue modulo every odd prime deterministically in polynomial bit complexity?

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For every finite-order Hecke character of $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, every Dirichlet character and the zeta function, the principal claim is \[ \operatorname{Re}s>7/8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(s)\ne0, \] where a principal character's pole at $s=1$ is allowed. The weaker displayed $11/12$ theorem follows within this scope. The cited arithmetic consequence gives absolute $A,C>0$ such that every odd prime $p$ has least positive quadratic nonresidue \[ n(p)\le C(\log p)^A. \] Thus $n(p)\ll_{\delta} p^{\delta}$ for each fixed $\delta>0$. Given binary $p$ and $a\in\mathbb F_p$, a deterministic algorithm of time polynomial in $\log p$ is further claimed to return a square root of $a$ or correctly declare it nonexistent. The half-plane assertion leaves zeros between the critical line and $7/8$ untreated.
Manuscript 2.
For any binary-encoded prime $p$ and nonzero densely encoded $f\in\mathbb F_p[x]$ of degree $n$, a uniform deterministic algorithm is claimed to output \[ f=c\prod_i g_i^{e_i} \] with $c$ the leading coefficient, distinct monic irreducible $g_i$ and positive integer multiplicities, in $O(((n+1)\lceil\log_2p\rceil)^{10^{12}})$ bit operations. Constants have an empty factor list. The computation is unconditional and deterministic; it receives neither an integer-factorization service nor a primitive-root oracle.

The separate algebraic reduction sets $L=\lceil\log_2p\rceil$, $B=20+(n+1)(L+1)$. If $p>B^{200000}$ and $n\ge2$, supply for every prime $q\le n$ a prime $\ell_q\notin\{2,3,p,q\}$ with \[ \ell_q\equiv1\pmod{12q},\qquad p^{(\ell_q-1)/q}\not\equiv1\pmod{\ell_q}. \] That reduction has bit cost $O((B+E)^C)$ for absolute $C$, where $E=\max(2,\max_q\ell_q)$, and $E=2$ when no such primes are required. This bound depends on the numerical size of the auxiliary primes; the full theorem additionally constructs sufficiently small primes.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-09-30), smoothed character sums are estimated in two ways: directly by large-sieve and recursive moment bounds, and through a Mellin transform involving the reciprocal of an $L$-function. Poisson summation and sextic residue characters align the two expressions so that their savings force continuation of the reciprocal.

For manuscript 2 (family 142; 2026-10-04), primary generators and norm equations reduce splitting to an odd-degree separator. Lifted ramification classes are filtered to keep extension degrees polynomial, and divisors at infinity distinguish roots; the analytic input bounds the auxiliary primes required by the finite algebraic algorithm.
