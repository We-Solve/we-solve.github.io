Orthogonal Symmetry Conjecture for Rigid Higher Categories

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

An $(\infty,n)$-category has morphisms at all levels, with those above level $n$ invertible. Rigidity supplies the duals or adjoints in the report's enriched setting. Taking the opposite at each of $n$ tiers gives commuting reflections, hence an action of $O(1)^n$. An extension to $O(n)$ must restrict to that action and retain the required homotopy-coherent compatibility.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let $\operatorname{Cat}^{\mathrm{rigid}}_{(\infty,n)}(\mathcal{V})$ be the $\infty$-category of rigid $(\infty,n)$-categories enriched over a presentably symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category $\mathcal{V}$. Taking opposites in each categorical tier gives an action of $O(1)^n$. Prove that this action extends to an action of the orthogonal group $O(n)$.

Short English statement

Extend the independent categorical opposite symmetries to the full orthogonal group action on rigid enriched higher categories.

A necessary connected-group compatibility test.
In $O(2)$, let $r_1=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$ and $r_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. They are conjugate by a rotation through $\pi/2$, which is joined to the identity by a continuous rotation path. Consequently any homotopy-coherent $O(2)$ action must relate the two reflection functors by compatible equivalences; merely having two commuting involutions does not supply this path data.
An elementary nonextension example.
Let the discrete set $S=\{0,1\}^2$ carry the action in which $r_1$ flips the first bit and $r_2$ flips the second. It cannot extend to a continuous $O(2)$ action. Indeed, for any $s\in S$, a continuous map from the connected group $SO(2)$ to the discrete set $S$ is constant, so rotations act trivially. Conjugacy would then force $r_1$ and $r_2$ to act identically, contrary to their values on $(0,0)$.
Application limit.
This set is not the category of rigid higher categories, so it is no counterexample to the conjecture. It shows that extending the given $O(1)^n$ action is a genuine additional problem. For $n=1$ the source and target groups agree; for $n\geq2$, rigidity must supply equivalences relating tier opposites and all higher coherence along rotations and their loops. The first gap is construction of that coherent continuous action for the stipulated enrichment $\mathcal V$, not a verification that opposite operations square to the identity.
