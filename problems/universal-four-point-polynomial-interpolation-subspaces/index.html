Universal Four-Point Polynomial Interpolation Subspaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a field. The supplied record does not specify the field or explicitly identify the domain of the planar points. Under the interpretation that the points belong to $K^2$, the interpolation question can be stated as follows.

For a four-element subset $X\subseteq K^2$, let $K^X$ denote the vector space of all functions from $X$ to $K$. For a four-dimensional $K$-linear subspace $V\subseteq K[x,y]$, define the evaluation map \[ \operatorname{ev}_{X,V}:V\longrightarrow K^X, \qquad p\longmapsto \left(a\mapsto p(a)\right). \] Say that $V$ interpolates arbitrary data on $X$ when this map is surjective. Since both spaces have dimension four, this is equivalent to the evaluation map being an isomorphism.

Determine the minimum size of a family $\mathcal F$ of four-dimensional $K$-linear subspaces of $K[x,y]$ satisfying \[ \forall X\subseteq K^2,\quad |X|=4 \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists V\in\mathcal F\ \forall f:X\to K\ \exists p\in V\ \forall a\in X,\quad p(a)=f(a). \] Here $\mathcal F$ is chosen independently of $X$, while $V$ may depend on $X$ and must interpolate every function on that same set. The points are distinct because $X$ is a set of cardinality four. No degree bound on the members of $V$ is supplied. A numerical target requires clarification of $K$, the intended point domain, and whether the minimum is sought among finite families or as a cardinality allowing infinite families. This formulation is conditional on the stated interpretation of the point domain.

The original extract begins with the word "corresponding", whose antecedent is absent. It then appears to run into a separate problem headed "Dimensions of Spline spaces", attributed to Schenck. That material is retained here as a distinct source qualification, not as an additional hypothesis on the interpolation family. Let $\Delta$ be a simplicial subdivision (triangulation) of a simply connected bounded domain in ${\mathbb{R}}^2$. For positive integers $r,m$, let \[ S_m^r(\Delta)=\{s:s\text{ is }C^r\text{ on the domain and }s|_T \text{ is a polynomial of total degree at most }m \text{ for every triangle }T\in\Delta\}. \] This is a real vector space. The adjacent question asks for its dimension in terms of the combinatorics, local geometry, and global geometry of $\Delta$. The extract describes this question as open even in a case printed as $\texttt{S31}$, apparently $S_3^1(\Delta)$; the indices require confirmation. It also describes a long history and the algebraic smoothness condition: for adjacent triangles with polynomial pieces $f,g$, and an affine linear polynomial $\ell$ whose zero line contains their common edge, matching derivatives through order $r$ across the edge is expressed by \[ \ell^{r+1}\mid(f-g),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad f-g\in\langle\ell^{r+1}\rangle. \] These spline assertions and their open-status description are supplied source content, not independently verified claims.

Short English statement

Find the smallest fixed family of four-dimensional polynomial spaces such that, for every set of four distinct planar points, one space in the family interpolates all prescribed values on those points. The field and point domain remain unspecified; the displayed formulation assumes points in $K^2$. The original extract also contains an apparently separate spline-dimension problem attributed to Schenck.
