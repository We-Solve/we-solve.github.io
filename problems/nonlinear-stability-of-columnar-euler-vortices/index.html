Nonlinear Stability of Columnar Euler Vortices

Definitions and mathematical statement

The incompressible three-dimensional Euler equations for velocity $u(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$ and pressure $p(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}$ are \[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0. \] Here the spatial domain $D$, boundary conditions, behavior at infinity, and solution regularity must be specified; the supplied record does not specify them. A steady solution $U$ satisfies \[ (U\cdot\nabla)U=-\nabla P,\qquad \nabla\cdot U=0 \] for a steady pressure $P$. Invariance along a column axis with unit direction $e$ means $(e\cdot\nabla)U=0$, wherever this expression is defined. The source does not specify the intended class of columnar vortex profiles, including whether axial flow is permitted.

Source question. What conditions, if any, ensure nonlinear stability of columnar vortices for the three-dimensional Euler equations?

A conditional mathematical template makes the missing stability choices explicit. Suppose an admissible metric space $(X,d_X)$ of divergence-free velocity fields, boundary conditions, and a solution class have been fixed, and suppose a global Euler evolution map $\Phi_t\colon X\to X$ is defined for every $t\geq0$. For a steady columnar vortex $U\in X$, nonlinear Lyapunov stability in $X$ would mean \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall u_0\in X:\quad d_X(u_0,U)<\delta \ \Longrightarrow\ \forall t\geq0,\quad d_X(\Phi_t(u_0),U)<\varepsilon. \] The intended problem could then ask for hypotheses on $U$, $X$, and the admissible perturbations ensuring this property. Short English statement

Determine conditions under which columnar steady vortices of the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations remain close to their initial equilibrium under small nonlinear perturbations. The domain, vortex profiles, perturbation class, notion of closeness, treatment of symmetries, and solution existence requirements remain unspecified.
