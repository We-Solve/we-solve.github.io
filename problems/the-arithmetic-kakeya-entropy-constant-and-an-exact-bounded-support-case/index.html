Math-259

The arithmetic Kakeya entropy constant and an exact bounded-support case

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finitely supported real random variable $Z$, write $H(Z)=-\sum_z\Pr(Z=z)\log\Pr(Z=z)$, using one fixed logarithm base and $0\log0=0$. The random variables $X,Y$ may have an arbitrary joint distribution; independence is not assumed.

Problem 4.10, attributed to T. Tao, asks for the best entropy exponent in a finite family of linear projections. The printed text introduces $r_1,\ldots,r_n$ but imposes $H(X+r_jY)<\log N$ for $j=1,\ldots,N$, then asks for $H(X-Y)<(1+\varepsilon)\log N$. Its remark gives the then best known $\varepsilon=0.67512\ldots$. The supplied verification identifies genuine printing defects: the slope count and entropy threshold are conflated, and the restriction $r_j\ne-1$ is missing. Allowing $-1$ would make the target projection one of the hypotheses and trivialize the question.

A precise reconstruction consistent with the research aid is to determine \[ \varepsilon_* =\inf\left\{\varepsilon\ge0:\ \exists n\ge1\ \exists r_1,\ldots,r_n\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{-1\}\ \forall(X,Y),\quad H(X-Y)\le(1+\varepsilon)\max_{1\le j\le n}H(X+r_jY)\right\}. \] Here the slopes are chosen before the joint law and do not depend on its support. Equivalently, for these slopes and every real threshold $N>1$, the strict projection bounds $H(X+r_jY)<\log N$ imply $H(X-Y)<(1+\varepsilon)\log N$. This separates $n$ from $N$; it is a reconstruction, not an unambiguous literal transcription. Restricting $N$ to integers, allowing slope choices to depend on $N$, or asking for attainment of the infimum would require additional source clarification.

The supplied aid reports the global upper bound $\varepsilon_*\le0.675130870566646\ldots$ and describes $\varepsilon=0$ as conjectural. No citation for this updated bound or exact formulation of the zero-exponent conjecture is supplied. In particular, $\varepsilon_*=0$ and the existence of one finite slope family attaining exponent $1$ are distinct assertions.

The aid proves a bounded-support special case. If the joint support $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ has at most $m\ge1$ points, every collection of $\binom m2+1$ distinct real slopes avoiding $-1$ contains a slope $r$ for which $(x,y)\mapsto x+ry$ is injective on $S$. Consequently, \[ H(X-Y)\le H(X,Y)=H(X+rY)\le\max_{s\in R}H(X+sY). \] In particular $R=\{0,1,\ldots,\binom m2\}$ works simultaneously for all joint laws supported on at most $m$ atoms. The dependence of the slope family on $m$ prevents this from settling the unrestricted problem.

More explicitly, the exceptional slopes are contained in \[ \left\{-\frac{x-x'}{y-y'}:(x,y),(x',y')\in S,\ (x,y)\ne(x',y'),\ y\ne y'\right\}, \] which has at most $\binom m2$ elements. For $S=\{(2^{2i},2^i):1\le i\le m\}$, its pair-collision slopes $-(2^i+2^k)$, $i<k$, are distinct, so this collision-count bound is attained. This does not establish optimality of the number of slopes for every $m$. For $m=2$, two slopes are necessary in the stated universal entropy comparison: for any single admissible slope one may place a nondegenerate two-point law in its kernel, while $X-Y$ remains nonconstant.

Short English statement

Tao asks how closely finitely many admissible projection entropies can control the entropy of $X-Y$. The printed slope count and threshold are inconsistent, so the draft labels its repaired quantifiers explicitly. The supplied bounded-support result gives exponent $1$ using a number of slopes depending on the support size; the global zero-exponent question remains a conjectural target in the aid.
