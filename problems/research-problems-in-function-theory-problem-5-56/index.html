Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$ and let $\mathcal O(\mathbb D)$ be the ring of all complex-valued holomorphic functions on $\mathbb D$, with pointwise addition and multiplication. In the usual pure-ring reading, a first-order ring property of an element is defined by a formula $\varphi(x)$ in the language $(0,1,+,-,\cdot)$ with one free variable, whose quantifiers range only over $\mathcal O(\mathbb D)$. Analytic notions, evaluation maps, and the constant field are not automatically named predicates. Whether additional parameters are permitted is not stated in the source; parameter-free definability is an explicit possible reading, not an unnoticed convention.

Rubel asks, separately for each of the following subsets, whether such a defining formula exists:

  1. The constant functions: $f(z)=c$ for some $c\in{\mathbb{C}}$ and every $z\in\mathbb D$.
  2. The bounded functions: $\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}|f(z)|<\infty$.
  3. The admissible functions, defined in the source by \[ T(r,f)\ne O\!\left(\log\frac1{1-r}\right)\qquad(r\uparrow1). \]
Here $T$ is the intended disk Nevanlinna characteristic. For a holomorphic function the usual convention is, up to harmless normalization terms, \[ T(r,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|f(re^{i\theta})|\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta, \quad 0<r<1, \] where $\log^+s=\max(\log s,0)$ and $\log^+0=0$. In this convention admissibility means \[ \forall C>0\ \forall r_0\in(0,1)\ \exists r\in(r_0,1),\qquad T(r,f)>C\log\frac1{1-r}. \] It is failure of an eventual big-$O$ bound, not the stronger assertion that the quotient tends to infinity. These analytic descriptions specify the target subsets; they are not claimed to be formulas in the ring language.

The final source question asks what happens for more general domains, replacing $\mathcal O(\mathbb D)$ by the corresponding holomorphic-function ring. It does not specify the domains, a replacement for the disk growth condition, or whether a single formula must work uniformly for all domains. Constantness and boundedness still have their ordinary analytic meanings on any chosen domain; admissibility requires a stated characteristic and boundary-growth convention.

The supplied triage says the questions were open in Hayman's 2018 edition and reports no definitive later resolution. This is a source-attributed assessment, not independent verification of definability or nondefinability.

Short English statement

Within the ring of all holomorphic disk functions, can first-order ring formulas recognize constant functions, bounded functions, or functions whose characteristic escapes the stated logarithmic bound? The three definability questions are separate. The permitted parameters and the general-domain version are unspecified, and the source reports no verified resolution.
