Existence of infinite finitely presented torsion groups

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: An infinite ordinarily finitely presented periodic group and an infinite-dimensional finitely presented nil algebra over $\mathbb F_2$ are claimed to exist; The periodic group of the companion is claimed residually finite, with an infinite finite-index finitely presented subgroup whose element orders are finite powers of two and unbounded. Together they claim an affirmative answer to the existence question and a counterexample to the nonexistence conjecture.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For a constructed unital associative algebra $R$ over $\mathbb F_2$, the Steinberg group $G=\operatorname{St}_{12}(R)$ is claimed to satisfy \[ |G|=\infty,\qquad G\text{ finitely presented},\qquad \forall g\in G\ \exists n\ge1:\ g^n=1. \] The companion corollary also assigns $G$ Kazhdan property $(T)$, hence nonamenability. Periodicity here permits element orders to vary.
Result 2.
The source claims existence of a group $G$ with an ordinary finite presentation such that \[ |G|=\infty,\qquad G\text{ residually finite},\qquad \forall g\in G\ \exists k\ge0:\ g^{2^k}=1. \] Thus all elements have finite order, and each order is a power of two; no common bounded exponent is asserted.

Outline of the argument

Finite local rules encode a hierarchy and a faithful computation tableau in admissible words. The resulting graded algebra has nil behavior without global nilpotence; a Steinberg-type group construction then transports these finite relations into an infinite group while forcing each element to have finite order.

Phases of long graded words make finite-degree truncations detect group elements. Faithful Steinberg strips, cyclic-shift comparisons, and long-entry commutator calculations treat the central kernel as well, so residual finiteness survives passage to the periodic two-group subgroup.
