Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.71

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be an infinite set and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ be a permutation group. The action is 2-transitive if, for any $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\Omega$ with $\alpha\neq\beta$ and $\gamma\neq\delta$, there exists $g\in G$ such that $g(\alpha)=\gamma$ and $g(\beta)=\delta$. For $\alpha\in\Omega$, write \[ G_\alpha=\{g\in G:g(\alpha)=\alpha\}. \] A group is locally soluble if every finitely generated subgroup is soluble. Here a group $H$ is soluble if its derived series, defined by $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(j+1)}=[H^{(j)},H^{(j)}]$, reaches the identity subgroup after finitely many steps.

Question. Suppose that $G$ is 2-transitive and that $G_\alpha$ is locally soluble for some $\alpha\in\Omega$. Must there exist a field $K$, an integer $n\geq1$, and a homomorphism \[ \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(K) \] such that $\rho(g)\neq I_n$ for at least one $g\in G$, and the only $\rho(G)$-invariant $K$-linear subspaces of $K^n$ are $\{0\}$ and $K^n$?

Point stabilizers in a transitive action are conjugate, so the local solubility hypothesis then holds at every point. We use the usual faithful meaning of permutation group; under this convention, an infinite permutation group cannot have finite degree. The field and dimension may depend on $G$. Non-triviality does not require faithfulness of $\rho$, and irreducibility is over $K$, without an additional requirement of absolute irreducibility.

Short English statement

Does every infinite 2-transitive permutation group with locally soluble point stabilizers admit a non-trivial irreducible representation of finite positive dimension over some field? This is the question attributed to A. N. Fomin in Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.71.
