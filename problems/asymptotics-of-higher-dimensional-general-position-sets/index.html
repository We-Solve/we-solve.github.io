Math-3904

Asymptotics of Higher-Dimensional General-Position Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ for a positive integer $n$. Under the usual interpretation of the integer grid $[n]^d$ as a subset of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, for integers $d\geq 1$ and $0\leq k\leq d$, define \[ \alpha_{d,k}(n)=\max\left\{|A|: A\subseteq[n]^d,\quad |A\cap L|\leq k+1\text{ for every affine subspace }L\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ with }\dim L=k\right\}. \] Thus the forbidden configuration consists of $k+2$ distinct points contained in a common $k$-dimensional affine subspace. Configurations whose affine span has dimension less than $k$ are also forbidden, since they are contained in a $k$-dimensional affine subspace.

Problem. For each fixed pair $(d,k)$ in the intended parameter range, determine the asymptotic order of $\alpha_{d,k}(n)$ as $n\to\infty$. In particular, determine the asymptotic order of \[ \alpha_{4,2}(n)=\max\left\{|A|:A\subseteq[n]^4,\quad |A\cap L|\leq 3\text{ for every affine plane }L\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^4\right\}. \] Determining the asymptotic order means finding a positive function $f_{d,k}(n)$ and constants $c_{d,k},C_{d,k}>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large positive integers $n$, \[ c_{d,k}f_{d,k}(n)\leq\alpha_{d,k}(n)\leq C_{d,k}f_{d,k}(n). \] The parameters $d,k$ are held fixed; the constants and the threshold for $n$ may depend on them.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify the admissible integer range for $d,k$ or the ambient scalar field. The definition above makes the conventional real-affine interpretation explicit and is meaningful for $0\leq k\leq d$; whether the source intends the endpoint cases or only a narrower range requires confirmation. The supplied original excerpt also discusses a separate random finite-field problem and contains corrupted displays of background bounds. Those passages do not supply an additional target or a reliably recoverable bound for this statement.

Short English statement

For a fixed dimension $d$ and parameter $k$, find how the largest subset of the integer grid $\{1,\ldots,n\}^d$ with no $k+2$ distinct points in a common affine $k$-dimensional subspace grows as $n\to\infty$. The highlighted case asks for the largest subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}^4$ with no four points in an affine plane. The real-affine interpretation and the intended parameter range require source confirmation.
