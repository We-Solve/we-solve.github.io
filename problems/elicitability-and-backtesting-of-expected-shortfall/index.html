Elicitability and Backtesting of Expected Shortfall

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source poses a methodological research question rather than a specified conjecture. The following notation provides a framework for expressing it; the distribution class, tail convention, and backtesting criterion are not specified by the source.

For an upper-tail loss convention, let $\alpha\in(0,1)$, and let $\mathcal F$ be a class of probability distributions on ${\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying $\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|x|\,{\,\mathrm{d}} F(x)<\infty$. Define \[ q_u(F)=\inf\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:F(x)\geq u\},\qquad \operatorname{VaR}_{\alpha}(F)=q_{\alpha}(F),\qquad \operatorname{ES}_{\alpha}(F)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{1}q_u(F)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} u. \] These conventions specify illustrative notation; the source does not select a confidence level or resolve loss versus return conventions.

For an action domain $A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^k$, a single-valued functional $T:\mathcal F\to A$ is elicitable on $\mathcal F$ if there exists a measurable scoring function $S:A\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$, with finite expected scores, such that \[ \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{a\in A}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(a,x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} F(x)=\{T(F)\} \quad\text{for every }F\in\mathcal F. \] Scalar elicitability concerns $k=1$; joint elicitability of Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall concerns the vector functional \[ T_{\alpha}(F)=\left(\operatorname{VaR}_{\alpha}(F),\operatorname{ES}_{\alpha}(F)\right). \] The admissible distribution and action classes, including any quantile-uniqueness conditions needed for a particular strict scoring formulation, must be specified before applying these definitions to the supplied literature claims.

Source question. What role does elicitability play in backtesting risk measures, particularly Expected Shortfall, in light of the supplied literature assessment that Expected Shortfall is not singly elicitable but is jointly elicitable with Value at Risk on appropriate classes? Here backtesting refers broadly to assessing risk forecasts using subsequently observed losses. The source does not specify whether the intended assessment is forecast comparison, calibration, hypothesis testing, or another criterion, nor does it specify a sampling model or a mathematical property to prove. Consequently, no unique theorem or conjecture can be extracted without additional hypotheses and a precise backtesting objective. The non-elicitability and joint-elicitability claims are retained as supplied background with unresolved domain qualifications, not asserted universally over $\mathcal F$.

Short English statement

The question asks how elicitability informs the assessment of risk forecasts, especially forecasts of Expected Shortfall and of its pair with Value at Risk. The supplied record labels the question open, but leaves the distribution class, statistical assumptions, and meaning of backtesting unspecified. This is therefore a statement-only research-question draft requiring human mathematical review.
