Isoperimetric dimension and connective constants

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a graph $G=(V,E)$ and finite $A\subset V$, write $\partial_E A=\{\{x,y\}\in E:x\in A,\ y\notin A\}$. One usual meaning of isoperimetric dimension greater than $1$ is the existence of $d>1$ and $c>0$ such that \[ |\partial_E A|\ge c|A|^{(d-1)/d}\qquad(\varnothing\ne A\subset V\text{ finite}). \] For a vertex $o$, let $c_n(G,o)$ count length-$n$ walks $(o=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ with adjacent consecutive vertices and all vertices distinct. Define the upper rooted exponential growth rate by \[ \overline\mu(G,o)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}c_n(G,o)^{1/n}. \] When the limit exists, the rooted connective constant is $\mu(G,o)=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_n(G,o)^{1/n}$. Conjecture 4.34 in the supplied record asks to prove that every graph $G$ of isoperimetric dimension greater than $1$ satisfies $\mu(G)>1$. To make this exact, the source's graph class, boundary convention, and definition of $\mu(G)$ must be supplied. In particular, the record does not say that $G$ is connected, locally finite, of bounded degree, or transitive, does not specify a root, and does not ensure that a root-independent limit exists. Under the displayed edge-boundary interpretation and a rooted upper-growth interpretation, the corresponding candidate assertion is $\overline\mu(G,o)>1$ for every vertex $o$ of every admissible $G$. This candidate is conditional on those conventions; it does not resolve the missing conventions of the source statement.

Short English statement

The conjecture links an isoperimetric inequality of dimension strictly greater than one to exponential growth of self-avoiding walks. The supplied statement leaves the graph class and connective-constant convention implicit. Its supplied open status is a triage assessment, with source verification dated 2026-07-29; no resolution is asserted here.
