Axisymmetry of Spherical Near-Horizon Geometries

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A degenerate black-hole horizon has zero surface gravity. Its near-horizon limit gives equations for a Riemannian cross-section metric and a one-form on the horizon section. Axisymmetry means there is a nontrivial circle action preserving those data. A spherical section is topologically $S^2$; classification up to diffeomorphism must respect the field equation's cosmological constant and matter assumptions. A Kerr near-horizon geometry is the vacuum spherical example singled out by the question. In the primary vacuum convention, the unknowns are a smooth metric $g_{AB}$ and one-form $\omega_A$ on $S^2$, satisfying $R_{AB}-(D_A\omega_B+D_B\omega_A+2\omega_A\omega_B)=0$, where $D$ and $R_{AB}$ are the metric covariant derivative and Ricci tensor.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Is every solution of the near-horizon-geometry equations on $S^2$ axisymmetric and therefore induced by a degenerate Kerr metric?
Source scope and qualification.
The actual vacuum equation was recovered from PDF page 18. The report establishes local rigidity near Kerr and states the global removal-of-axisymmetry conjecture; modern status is not freshly established here. The problem is not enlarged to matter or nonzero cosmological constant.

Short English statement

Must every spherical solution of the indicated near-horizon equations have rotational symmetry and come from extremal Kerr?
