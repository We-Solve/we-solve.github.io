Math-3360

Bicategorical Limits of Nonproper Product Systems

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A conventional framework for interpreting the record is the following. Regard a monoid $M$, with identity $e$, as a category with one object. Let $\mathsf{Corr}$ denote the bicategory whose objects are $C^*$-algebras, whose arrows $A\to B$ are right Hilbert $B$-modules $E$ equipped with a nondegenerate $*$-homomorphism $\varphi_E:A\to\mathcal L_B(E)$, and whose $2$-arrows are unitary bimodule isomorphisms. Here $\mathcal L_B(E)$ is the algebra of adjointable operators. Composition is the interior tensor product. The compact operators $\mathcal K_B(E)$ are the closed linear span of the operators $\theta_{\xi,\eta}(\zeta)=\xi\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle_B$. A correspondence is called proper when $\varphi_E(A)\subseteq\mathcal K_B(E)$.

Under this convention, a homomorphism $F:M\to\mathsf{Corr}$, interpreted as a pseudofunctor, is a product system consisting of a $C^*$-algebra $A$, correspondences $E_m:A\to A$ for every $m\in M$, and unitary multiplication maps \[ \mu_{m,n}:E_m\otimes_A E_n\xrightarrow{\cong}E_{mn},\qquad E_e\cong A, \] with the unit identities and the associativity identities \[ \mu_{mn,p}\circ(\mu_{m,n}\otimes 1) =\mu_{m,np}\circ(1\otimes\mu_{n,p}) \qquad(m,n,p\in M), \] after the canonical tensor-product associativity identifications. This fixes a multiplication-order convention for the monoid category. In this framework, a nonproper product system means that $\varphi_{E_m}(A)\nsubseteq\mathcal K_A(E_m)$ for at least one $m\in M$. The supplied record does not specify these conventions or explicitly define nonproperness.

Recorded question, conditional on these conventions. For every monoid $M$ and every nonproper product system $F:M\to\mathsf{Corr}$, does a bicategorical limit exist, and, whenever it exists, how can it be described? No cancellativity, Ore condition, countability, separability, fullness, or faithfulness hypothesis is supplied.

More precisely, if "limit" means a conical pseudolimit, seek an object $L$ and a pseudocone $\lambda:\Delta L\Rightarrow F$ such that, for every $C^*$-algebra $D$, composition with $\lambda$ induces an equivalence \[ \mathsf{Corr}(D,L)\simeq\operatorname{PsCone}(\Delta D,F), \] pseudonatural in $D$. Here $\Delta D$ is the constant diagram, and the right-hand side is the category of pseudocones and modifications. In the convention above, a pseudocone has a correspondence $H:D\to A$ and coherent unitary identifications $H\otimes_A E_m\cong H$ for all $m\in M$.

Unresolved target. The original wording asks what a homomorphism into the correspondence bicategory is and what its "limit" would look like. The supplied literature assessment instead discusses colimits. A conical pseudocolimit would have the different universal property \[ \mathsf{Corr}(L,D)\simeq\operatorname{PsCone}(F,\Delta D), \] with cocones given by correspondences $H:A\to D$ and coherent unitary identifications $E_m\otimes_A H\cong H$. The supplied evidence does not resolve whether a limit, a colimit, or a lax variant is intended. Consequently, the displayed limit question is a conditional formalization of the clean statement, not a verified reconstruction of the report's precise target.

Short English statement

The record asks whether an arbitrary nonproper product system over a monoid has a universal bicategorical limit and how to describe it. A conventional correspondence framework is given above, but the intended universal construction remains uncertain because the supplied literature discussion concerns colimits. The supplied status is partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
