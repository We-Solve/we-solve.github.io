Math-842

A BMO Approach to the Lindelöf Hypothesis

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\zeta(s)$ denote the Riemann zeta function, defined for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ by \[ \zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s} \] and elsewhere by its meromorphic continuation to ${\mathbb{C}}$, with its sole pole at $s=1$. The Lindelöf hypothesis asserts that \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists C_{\varepsilon}>0\;\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}, \qquad \left|\zeta\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\right| \leq C_{\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon}. \] Here $C_{\varepsilon}$ may depend on $\varepsilon$, but not on $t$.

BMO means bounded mean oscillation. For context, the standard space $\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}})$ consists of locally integrable functions $f$ for which \[ \sup_I\frac{1}{|I|}\int_I|f(x)-f_I|\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x<\infty, \qquad f_I=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_I f(x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] where the supremum is over bounded intervals $I\subset{\mathbb{R}}$ of positive length. This definition supplies terminology only: the source does not identify the domain, measure, or particular BMO space intended for the proposed approach.

Research question. Can one prove the Lindelöf hypothesis by developing or applying a suitable BMO theory, or by another functional-analytic method?

The displayed zeta bound specifies the mathematical target precisely. The requested method remains a research direction: the supplied record gives no particular BMO assertion, endpoint estimate, operator, or criterion whose proof would establish that bound. Its dated literature assessment reports that BMO-type spaces discussed in the report were shown not to be the appropriate endpoint space. The spaces and endpoint results are not specified in this record, so that qualification cannot be rendered here as a precise obstruction theorem and does not assert that every BMO approach is impossible.

Short English statement

The question asks whether BMO theory or another functional-analytic method can prove that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, the Riemann zeta function on the critical line is bounded by a constant times $(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon}$. The intended BMO framework is unspecified. The supplied dated assessment describes limitations of BMO spaces discussed in the report; it does not exclude every possible BMO approach.
