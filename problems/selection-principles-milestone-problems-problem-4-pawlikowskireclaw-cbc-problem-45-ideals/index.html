Selection Principles Milestone Problems — Problem 4 ([pawlikowskireclaw], [CBC, Problem 45], [ideals])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and give every $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ its usual subspace topology. For this draft, a cover of $X$ means a family $\mathcal U$ of subsets of $X$ with $\bigcup\mathcal U=X$ and $X\notin\mathcal U$. Let $\mathcal B(X)$ be the family of countable Borel covers of $X$, where Borel means Borel in the subspace $X$. Let $\mathcal B_\Gamma(X)$ consist of those $\mathcal U\in\mathcal B(X)$ that are infinite and satisfy \[ \forall x\in X,\qquad \left|\{U\in\mathcal U:x\notin U\}\right|<\aleph_0. \] Thus each point belongs to all but finitely many members of a Borel $\gamma$-cover. These are explicit cover conventions adopted for this draft; the supplied record does not state the original conventions, so they require source verification.

For families $\mathscr A$ and $\mathscr D$ of covers of $X$, the selection principle $\mathsf S_1(\mathscr A,\mathscr D)$ means \[ \forall (\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\mathscr A^{{\mathbb{N}}}\; \exists (U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\quad \left[(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;U_n\in\mathcal U_n) \ \land\ \{U_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\in\mathscr D\right]. \] The selected family is regarded as a set, so repetitions are discarded; it need not be infinite when $\mathscr D=\mathcal B(X)$.

Question (Tsaban's 2003 source list, Problem 4). Does ZFC prove the existence of a set $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ |X|>\aleph_0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathsf S_1\left(\mathcal B_\Gamma(X),\mathcal B(X)\right)? \] Equivalently, can one establish in ZFC that there is an uncountable $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ such that, for every sequence $(\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of countable Borel $\gamma$-covers of $X$, there are choices $U_n\in\mathcal U_n$ satisfying $X=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}U_n$? The phrase "in ZFC" is interpreted here as asking for provable existence, rather than merely existence in some model of ZFC.

Supplied context and qualifications. A set $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ is a $\sigma$-set if every relative $G_\delta$ subset of $X$ is a relative $F_\sigma$ subset of $X$. The record attributes to [CBC] the assertion that every set satisfying $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal B_\Gamma,\mathcal B_\Gamma)$ is a $\sigma$-set. It attributes to Miller [Mil79Len] the consistency of every $\sigma$-set of reals being countable, and consequently states that there are no uncountable ZFC examples satisfying that stronger selection principle. These are supplied contextual assertions, not independently verified results in this draft.

Writing $\mathcal O(X)$ for the countable open covers under the same cover convention, the record also states that Borel's Conjecture, whose consistency it attributes to Laver, implies countability of every set satisfying $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal O,\mathcal O)$, and of the unspecified "classes below it." Its ensuing phrase "there do not exist ZFC examples" must be qualified by uncountability to express the intended obstruction; the literal wording does not include that qualification. The referenced lower classes and their ordering are not supplied. The registry labels the present question open; that label and the source's historical assessment are supplied information, not a verified current literature assessment.

Short English statement

Does ZFC prove that there is an uncountable set of reals for which one can select one member from each of any sequence of countable Borel $\gamma$-covers so that the selected sets cover the space? Each point belongs to all but finitely many members of each $\gamma$-cover. The original cover conventions and abbreviated references require verification; the supplied open status is not independently verified.
