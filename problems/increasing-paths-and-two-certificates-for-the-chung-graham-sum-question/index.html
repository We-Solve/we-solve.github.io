Math-1347

Increasing paths and two certificates for the Chung-Graham sum question

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple undirected graph with a bijective edge labeling $\lambda:E(G)\to\{1,\ldots,|E(G)|\}$. An increasing path starting at $v$ is a sequence of distinct vertices $v=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_\ell$ with consecutive vertices adjacent and $\lambda(v_0v_1)<\cdots<\lambda(v_{\ell-1}v_\ell)$. Its length is the number $\ell$ of edges, and the length-zero path is allowed. Put \[ t_\lambda(v)=\max\{\ell:\text{there is such a path from }v\},\qquad S_\lambda(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}t_\lambda(v). \] Ron Graham's two questions in the source are \[ \exists c>0\ \forall n\geq2\ \forall\lambda\text{ on }K_n:\quad \max_v t_\lambda(v)\geq cn, \] and the stronger arbitrary-graph assertion \[ \forall G\ \forall\lambda:\qquad S_\lambda(G)\geq |E(G)|. \] The restriction $n\geq2$ excludes the one-vertex graph, which has no positive-length path.

The source reports a Calderbank--Chung--Sturtevant (1984) labeling with maximum length at most $n/2$, a Graham--Kleitman (1973) universal lower bound $(1+o(1))\sqrt n$, and a then-recent claimed improvement to $(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt n$ by unnamed graduate students. The upper-bound statement lacks its range of $n$ and rounding conventions; it is preserved as a source report rather than asserted literally for every small $n$.

Writing $f(K_n)=\min_\lambda\max_v t_\lambda(v)$, the attached aid reports the published lower bound \[ f(K_n)\geq n/2^{O(\sqrt{\log n\log\log n})}=n^{1-o(1)}, \] and says both the constant-linear question and the sum question remain open. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently checked current claims.

Its two partial certificates use $n_+=|\{v:\deg_G(v)>0\}|$ and $a_\lambda(G)$, the number of edges lying in no increasing closed trail. Here a closed trail starts and ends at the same vertex, may revisit other vertices, uses no edge twice, and has strictly increasing labels in its chosen traversal. The aid states \[ S_\lambda(G)\geq2a_\lambda(G),\qquad S_\lambda(G)\geq n_++\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}\binom{\ell(P)}2 \] for every vertex-disjoint family $\mathcal P$ of increasing paths, with $\ell(P)$ again counting edges. Consequently the sum assertion holds when $a_\lambda(G)\geq|E(G)|/2$, or when the path-packing credit is at least $|E(G)|-n_+$. For forests the aid records the sharp stronger bound $S_\lambda(G)\geq2|E(G)|$. These conditional results are not a proof for arbitrary edge-ordered graphs.

Short English statement

Must every edge ordering of a complete graph contain a linearly long increasing simple path? More strongly, must the longest increasing lengths summed over starting vertices exceed the number of edges? The supplied aid gives partial trail and path-packing certificates, including a stronger result for forests.
