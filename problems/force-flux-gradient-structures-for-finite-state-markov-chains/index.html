Force-Flux Gradient Structures for Finite-State Markov Chains

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a nonempty finite state space and let \[ \mathcal P(S)=\left\{\rho\in[0,1]^S:\sum_{i\in S}\rho_i=1\right\} \] be its probability simplex. A finite-state Markov chain has probability laws in $\mathcal P(S)$. The source does not specify its time parameter. In the discrete-time interpretation, a transition matrix $P$ satisfies $P_{ij}\geq0$ and $\sum_jP_{ij}=1$, and row-vector laws evolve by $\rho_{n+1}=\rho_nP$. In the continuous-time interpretation, a generator $Q$ satisfies $Q_{ij}\geq0$ for $i\ne j$ and $\sum_jQ_{ij}=0$, and laws evolve by $\dot\rho_t=\rho_tQ$. These are alternative interpretations, not additional hypotheses supplied by the record.

For a specified stationary distribution $\pi$, detailed balance means $\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}$ in discrete time, or $\pi_iQ_{ij}=\pi_jQ_{ji}$ in continuous time, for every $i,j\in S$. This describes the usual stationary notion of reversibility; the source does not specify the reference distribution or precisely define its term "irreversible."

Source question. Do analogous force--flux, large-deviation, and gradient-flow structures exist for finite-state Markov chains, including in the irreversible case?
