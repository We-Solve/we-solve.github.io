Math-5344

Diophantine additive polynomials and the failure of finite Artin--Schreier tests

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix a prime $p$ and write $R={\mathbb{F}}_p[Z]$. An additive polynomial is an element \[ F(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^n\alpha_i Z^{p^i},\qquad \alpha_i\in{\mathbb{F}}_p, \] including the zero polynomial. Equivalently $F(A+B)=F(A)+F(B)$ for all $A,B\in R$. Question 5 asks whether the subset $\operatorname{Add}_p\subseteq R$ of these polynomials is Diophantine. In the language with named coordinate $Z$, this means a fixed finite existential system of polynomial equations over $R$ in $F$ and finitely many auxiliary variables, defining precisely $\operatorname{Add}_p$. The aid reports a positive answer from Demeyer's 2007 theorem that recursively enumerable subsets of this polynomial ring are Diophantine with the coordinate named: membership in $\operatorname{Add}_p$ is recursive by inspecting the finitely many nonzero exponents. In the pure ring language without parameters the subset is not even first-order definable: the automorphism $Z\mapsto Z+1$ sends the additive polynomial $Z$ to the nonadditive polynomial $Z+1$. These language conventions cannot be interchanged.

Preserve also Demeyer's objection to the proposed finite tests. For $m\ge1$ put \[ V_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} Z^j\{A^p-A:A\in R\} +\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}\{Z,Z^2,\ldots,Z^m\}. \] The proposal tests $F^m\in V_m$ for successive $m$. For example the first three tests have respectively one, two and three Artin--Schreier terms, multiplied by $1$, then $1,Z$, then $1,Z,Z^2$, together with the indicated scalar multiples of $Z,\ldots,Z^m$. The source reports that every additive polynomial passes, but so does the single nonadditive polynomial \[ H(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\left(Z^{2p}-Z^{p+1}\right)^{p^i} \] at every level $m\ge1$. Thus increasing the finite number of these tests does not give the desired definition.

The aid further reports an exact normal form. For $1\le m<p$, reduce an exponent $e=j+pn$ to $j+n$ whenever $0\le j<m$ and $n\ge1$, repeating until a terminal exponent is reached. The allowable $j$ is unique in this range, and each step strictly decreases the exponent. For $G=\sum_e c_eZ^e$, membership $G\in V_m$ is equivalent to vanishing, in ${\mathbb{F}}_p$, of the coefficient sum over every terminal class whose terminal exponent is outside $\{1,\ldots,m\}$; terminal classes ending in those $m$ free monomials are unrestricted. For $m\ge p$, $V_m$ is the entire subspace of polynomials with zero constant coefficient. These are supplied research refinements, not independently reviewed assertions.

The extract also contains the subsequent Fact 6, attributed to Cornelissen. For a field $L$ of characteristic $p$, let \[ A_L=\left\{\textstyle\sum_i a_i Z^{p^i}:a_i\in L\right\} \] with ordinary addition and multiplication given by composition. Put $\tau(Z)=Z^p$; the multiplicative identity is the polynomial $Z$. The equation \[ f\circ\tau=\tau\circ f \] defines the centralizer $A_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_p[T]$ inside this noncommutative ring, where $T$ corresponds to $\tau$. The source states an analogous fact in its quotient skew field $Q_L$, and concludes undecidability of the indicated Diophantine theories from results attributed to Denef and Pheidas for ${\mathbb{F}}_q[Z]$ and ${\mathbb{F}}_q(Z)$. It proposes transferring undecidability if $A_L$ or $Q_L$ can be Diophantinely defined in $L[Z]$ or $L(Z)$. For this transfer the interpretation must include the noncommutative composition operation; an ordinary subring with inherited commutative multiplication cannot supply it. The element named in the ring language must likewise be specified: the source's notation "a symbol for $Z$" does not distinguish the identity polynomial from the Frobenius generator $\tau$. The quotient construction, parameter convention and interpretation details are not supplied. Fact 6 is separate from Question 5, as the source-verification field expressly notes.

Short English statement

Ask whether additive polynomials over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ form a Diophantine subset of ${\mathbb{F}}_p[Z]$. The supplied assessment answers yes with the coordinate named and explains why the proposed Artin--Schreier tests fail, while the pure ring language gives a different answer. The appended composition-ring observation needs its own interpretation and parameter conventions.
