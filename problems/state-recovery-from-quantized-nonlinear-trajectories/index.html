Research attempt: Exact feasible-state projection and quantitative special regimes

The exact information content of both recursions
Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of all finite sequences $(x_0,\ldots,x_T)$ with $x_{t+1}=f(x_t)$ and $x_t\in P(t)$ for every $t$. No regularity or injectivity of $f$ is needed in this paragraph. Induction shows $Q^+(t)$ is precisely the set of endpoints of compatible prefixes: the assertion is true at zero, and taking $f$ of the preceding endpoint set followed by intersection with $P(t)$ is exactly the existential predecessor condition. Backward induction likewise shows $Q^-(t)$ is the set of starting points of compatible suffixes, because its inverse image uses all predecessors.

If $z\in Q^+(t)\cap Q^-(t)$, choose a prefix ending at $z$ and a suffix starting there. Their common state permits concatenation into a sequence in $\mathcal T$. Conversely any sequence in $\mathcal T$ supplies both witnesses. Therefore \[ Q(t)=\{x_t:(x_0,\ldots,x_T)\in\mathcal T\}. \tag{1} \] In particular $Q(t)$ is the smallest guaranteed state enclosure based solely on these exact observations and the known deterministic map: excluding any point of it would exclude one realizable full trajectory. This is a precise set-theoretic meaning of finest information, not a claim about efficient representation, probabilities, or approximate computations. Empty feasible sets mean inconsistent data; a realized unknown trajectory makes all of them nonempty.

Two sufficient diameter estimates
Use Euclidean diameter. If $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz on the states being compared, with $0\leq L<1$, then for two feasible trajectories $\lVert x_t-y_t\rVert\leq L^t\lVert x_0-y_0\rVert$ by repeated application. Equation $1$ thus gives \[ \operatorname{diam}Q(t)\leq L^t\operatorname{diam}P(0). \] Separately suppose $\lVert f(x)-f(y)\rVert\geq\lambda\lVert x-y\rVert$ for all relevant pairs, with $\lambda>1$. Applying this inequality to the suffix of two feasible trajectories gives \[ \operatorname{diam}Q(t)\leq\lambda^{-(T-t)}\operatorname{diam}P(T). \] This expansion assumption implies injectivity on the compared set; local derivative expansion alone is not a substitute for it when folding is possible. The source only requires the observed trajectory to stay in its compact observation region, not that every state be mapped back into that region.
An exact mixed stable/unstable example
For $f(x,y)=(a x,b y)$ with $0<a<1<b$ and all observations $P(t)=[-1,1]^2$, a state $(x,y)$ at time $t$ has a compatible prefix precisely when $|x|\leq a^t$ and $|y|\leq1$. It has a compatible suffix precisely when $|x|\leq1$ and $|y|\leq b^{-(T-t)}$. These statements follow directly by iterating $a$ and $b$ in the respective time directions. Consequently \[ Q(t)=[-a^t,a^t]\times[-b^{-(T-t)},b^{-(T-t)}],\qquad \operatorname{diam}Q(t)=2\sqrt{a^{2t}+b^{-2(T-t)}}. \] Both sides shrink exponentially for times separated linearly from the two ends. This realizes the source's hyperbolic intuition without asserting a general stable-manifold theorem. By contrast the identity map with a repeated nontrivial cell has $Q(t)=P(t)$, so arbitrarily many observations need not shrink the set at all.

Verification and adversarial audit

The concatenation proof does not invert $f$ or assume a unique predecessor. Both endpoints $t=0,T$, inconsistent observations, constant maps, and the identity map are covered. Diameter estimates compare two complete feasible trajectories, so no independently chosen time slices are mistaken for a trajectory. The hyperbolic map need not preserve the whole compact square; only the admissible finite trajectories remain in it, as allowed in the primary source.
