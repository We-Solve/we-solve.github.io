Open Problems in Wavelet Theory — Problem 11

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$, let $\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}^n$, and call a real matrix $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{R}})$ expansive if every eigenvalue of $A$ has modulus greater than $1$. Use the Fourier transform convention \[ \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x \] for integrable functions, extended to $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ by continuity.

For a specified exponent $N>0$, say that $\psi\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ has the required decay of order $N$ if there exist constants $C,D,R>0$ such that \[ |\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{-N}\quad\text{for almost every }|x|\geq R, \qquad |\widehat\psi(\xi)|\leq D|\xi|^{-N}\quad\text{for almost every }|\xi|\geq R. \] Here $|\cdot|$ denotes the Euclidean norm. These almost-everywhere bounds express the supplied decay conditions for representatives of $L^2$ functions; they impose no additional bounds near the origin.

Under the usual finite-generator interpretation, an orthonormal wavelet family for $(A,{\mathbb{Z}}^n)$ consists of functions $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_r\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$, where $r\geq1$ is an integer, such that \[ \left\{|\det A|^{j/2}\psi_\ell(A^j x-k): j\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n,\ 1\leq\ell\leq r\right\} \] is an orthonormal basis of $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$.

Requested characterization. Characterize the expansive dilations $A$ for which such an orthonormal wavelet family exists with every generator well-localized: both the generator and its Fourier transform have polynomial decay of some large order $N>0$, in the sense above. For finitely many generators, their exponents may be replaced by a common minimum exponent.

Unresolved qualifications. The source says "some large $N>0$" but supplies no numerical threshold, dependence on dimension, or quantifier specifying how large $N$ must be. Thus the decay condition above is precise for each fixed $N$, while the intended range of $N$ in the requested characterization remains unspecified. The finite-generator interpretation is also explicit here as a convention: the supplied phrase "possibly with multiple generators" does not state whether infinitely many generators are allowed. The source describes this question as a variant of Problem 3.1, whose statement is not supplied.

Short English statement

With translation lattice ${\mathbb{Z}}^n$, determine which expansive matrices admit an orthonormal wavelet basis whose generators and Fourier transforms decay polynomially at infinity. Multiple generators are allowed. The meaning of a "large" decay exponent and the permitted number of generators require clarification.
