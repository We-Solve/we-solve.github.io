Statement and source

If every large integer is a sum of at most $h$ elements of $A$, must the sums of at most $h$ distinct elements have bounded gaps? The answer is yes for $h=2$ and no for every $h\ge3$. We reconstruct the block counterexample of Hegyvári, Hennecart and Plagne, Answer to a question of Burr and Erdős on restricted addition and related results, https://hegyvari.web.elte.hu/artEB4.pdf, including the role of earlier blocks.

Order two

Every sufficiently large odd integer represented by at most two elements of $A$ is either itself in $A$, or a sum of two distinct elements: a repeated pair has even sum. Thus the restricted sumset contains every sufficiently large odd integer. Consecutive gaps are at most two. The bound is valid with the "at most" convention in the question.

An explicit construction for every higher order

Fix $h\ge3$, put $P=2^{h-2}$, $C=3P-1$, and define \[ x_0=1,\quad x_{j+1}=Cx_j^2+hx_j,\quad E_j=[0,x_j^2]\cup\{2x_j^2,4x_j^2,\ldots,Px_j^2\}, \qquad A=\bigcup_{j\ge0}(x_j+E_j). \] We allow zero as padding when discussing sums of at most $h$ terms; zero need not belong to $A$. The blocks are disjoint and increasing, since their largest element $x_j+Px_j^2$ is below $x_{j+1}$.

For any positive integer $M$, the set $E=[0,M]\cup\{2M,4M,\ldots,2^{h-2}M\}$ satisfies \[ (h-1)E\supseteq[0,(2^{h-1}-1)M],\qquad hE\supseteq[0,(3\cdot2^{h-2}-1)M].\tag{1} \] For the first assertion induct on $h$. The case $h=2$ is $E=[0,M]$. From the preceding order, adding either $[0,M]$ or the new largest point $2^{h-2}M$ gives the adjoining intervals $[0,2^{h-2}M]$ and $[2^{h-2}M,(2^{h-1}-1)M]$. For the second assertion, add $[0,M]$ and then the largest point to the first interval; the two resulting intervals overlap and cover the stated range.

Taking $M=x_j^2$, $1$ shows that $h(x_j+E_j)$ contains $[hx_j,x_{j+1}]$. The single-term interval $[x_j,x_j+x_j^2]$ overlaps it for all large $j$. Thus every sufficiently large integer in $[x_j,x_{j+1}]$ is a sum of at most $h$ terms of $A$. These intervals cover a tail, proving the basis property.

Let $R$ consist of sums of at most $h$ distinct elements of $A$. Any such sum below $x_{j+1}$ uses only blocks through $j$. Its largest possible value is no larger than the sum of the $h$ largest elements of that entire initial part. For large $j$ all these elements are in the current block, since its ordinary interval contains more than $h$ elements and every older element is below $x_j$. There are $h-2$ spike elements; bounding the remaining two by $x_j+x_j^2$ gives \[ \sum\text{(the $h$ largest elements)} \le hx_j+\left(2+4+\cdots+P+2\right)x_j^2 =hx_j+2P x_j^2. \] All future elements are at least $x_{j+1}$. Hence the interval \[ (hx_j+2P x_j^2,\quad x_{j+1}) \] contains no element of $R$. Its length is $(P-1)x_j^2\to\infty$. Since $x_{j+1}\in R$ and $R$ is unbounded, these empty intervals imply unbounded consecutive gaps.

If "order" means the least possible order

The same construction has least order $h$, not merely order at most $h$. Put $M=x_j^2$ and $q=3P-2$, and take an integer $n_j=qM+\lfloor M/2\rfloor$. It lies below $x_{j+1}$, so a representation with at most $h-1$ terms uses no future block. Relax all earlier and ordinary current elements to the interval $[0,M+x_j]$. If $r$ terms are from this interval and the remaining terms are spikes, their sum lies in \[ [sM,\ (s+r)M+(h-1)x_j], \] where $s$ is a sum of at most $h-1-r$ powers $2,4,\ldots,P$. For large $j$, $(h-1)x_j<M/3$. To contain $n_j$, this interval must have $s\le q$ and $s+r\ge q+1$.

Let $w(s)$ be the minimum number of coins of values $1,2,4,\ldots,P$ needed for $s$. Binary carrying proves $w(s)=\lfloor s/P\rfloor+\operatorname{popcount}(s\bmod P)$: whenever two equal nonmaximal coins occur, replace them by the next coin. In particular $w(q)=h-1$. Also $w(s+1)\le w(s)+1$. Writing $d=q-s\ge0$, we obtain $w(s)\ge h-1-d$, whereas $r\ge d+1$. Thus the required number of summands is at least $w(s)+r\ge h$, a contradiction. The sequence $n_j\to\infty$ proves that order $h-1$ does not suffice.

Assessment

Both the affirmative order-two case and every higher-order counterexample are proved completely, including exact order and the exclusion of sums mixing different blocks. No external theorem is needed beyond the attributed construction idea.
