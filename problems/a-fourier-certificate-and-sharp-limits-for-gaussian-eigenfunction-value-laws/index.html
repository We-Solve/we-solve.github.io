Math-336

A Fourier certificate and sharp limits for Gaussian eigenfunction value laws

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Conjecture 41, attributed to J. Marklof, is introduced in the neighboring source paragraph as a prediction for generic negatively curved compact Riemannian manifolds, in the Berry random-wave discussion. It is not an assertion for every compact or finite-volume manifold in the broader spectral setup. The meaning of generic, admissible boundary cases and eigenfunction choices must be fixed. Let $dV$ be normalized volume, $dV(X)=1$, and let $\varphi_i$ be real Laplace eigenfunctions with $\|\varphi_i\|_{L^2(dV)}=1$ and eigenvalues tending to infinity. The conjectured value-distribution limit, for every fixed finite $a<b$, is \[ dV\{x:a\leq\varphi_i(x)\leq b\} \longrightarrow\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_a^b e^{-t^2/2}\,dt. \] Real-valuedness is required for these ordered intervals. On a closed manifold positive-eigenvalue functions also have mean zero by orthogonality to constants; that fact is not imposed on unspecified Dirichlet problems.

Let $\mu_i=(\varphi_i)_*dV$ be the probability law of values, and let $\gamma$ be standard Gaussian measure. The statement is exactly $\mu_i\Rightarrow\gamma$. Its characteristic-function target is \[ \varepsilon_i(T)=\sup_{|t|\leq T} \left|\int_X e^{it\varphi_i(x)}dV(x)-e^{-t^2/2}\right| \longrightarrow0\quad\text{for every fixed }T>0. \] The aid states this compact-uniform convergence is equivalent to weak convergence. It is a scalar value-law criterion, not a condition on spatial quantum limits.

The supplied quantitative smoothing lemma asserts that for any probability law $\mu$, $I=[a,b]$ of length $L$, and $\delta,T>0$, with $\varepsilon_\mu(T)=\sup_{|t|\leq T}|\widehat\mu(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|$, \[ |\mu(I)-\gamma(I)|\leq \frac{2\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}} +\frac{T(L+\delta)}\pi\varepsilon_\mu(T) +\frac8{\pi\delta T}. \] The terms are Gaussian boundary smoothing, compact-frequency error and high-frequency remainder. The order of choices matters: choose a small smoothing width, then a sufficiently large finite cutoff, then control that cutoff's characteristic-function error. The lemma does not establish this error estimate for the eigenfunctions.

The aid also demonstrates the limit of moment conclusions. For $k\geq2$ put $\epsilon_k=k^{-3}$, $\sigma_k^2=(1-k^{-1})/(1-k^{-3})$, and \[ \nu_k=(1-\epsilon_k)N(0,\sigma_k^2) +\frac{\epsilon_k}{2}(\delta_k+\delta_{-k}). \] These laws have exactly mean zero and variance one, converge weakly to $\gamma$, but have fourth moment $3(1-\epsilon_k)\sigma_k^4+\epsilon_kk^4\to\infty$. They are abstract probability laws, not asserted to arise from eigenfunctions. They show that Gaussian weak convergence plus exact $L^2$ normalization alone cannot yield fourth-moment convergence; extra uniform tail control is required.

Finally the one-point value law is distinct from QUE, which tests the spatial measures $|\varphi_i|^2dV$, and from multi-point random-wave limits, which require joint distributions and correlations. The supplied aid gives these reductions and limitations, not a proof of the conjecture for the intended generic geometric class.

Short English statement

Marklof's conjecture predicts standard Gaussian value distributions for normalized real high-energy eigenfunctions in the intended generic negatively curved compact setting. The supplied aid gives an equivalent characteristic-function target and an explicit smoothing estimate. It also shows why weak Gaussian laws and $L^2$ normalization do not by themselves control fourth moments or establish QUE or multi-point random-wave behavior.
