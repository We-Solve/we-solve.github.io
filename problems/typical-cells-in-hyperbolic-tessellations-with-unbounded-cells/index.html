Typical Cells in Hyperbolic Tessellations with Unbounded Cells

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb H^d$ denote real hyperbolic space, with hyperbolic distance $d_{\mathbb H}$. The supplied record does not specify the permitted dimensions $d$. A nonempty cell $C\subseteq\mathbb H^d$ is unbounded if \[ \operatorname{diam}_{\mathbb H}(C):=\sup\{d_{\mathbb H}(x,y):x,y\in C\}=\infty. \] Let $T$ denote the hyperbolic tessellation considered in the source. Its precise admissible class and probabilistic assumptions are not provided in the supplied excerpt.

Construction problem. Find a natural extension of the notions of cell intensity and typical cell to hyperbolic tessellations $T$ containing unbounded cells. The extended notions should recover the existing notions on their original domain of definition. The intended target is a general extension of these notions, rather than a construction restricted to a particular example of a tessellation.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not define what makes an extension natural, state the original domain of definition, or specify the required invariance, local finiteness, convexity, integrability, or normalization assumptions. It also does not specify whether the typical cell is positioned, rooted, or considered up to isometry. Thus the construction problem cannot be sharpened into a fully quantified existence assertion from this record alone. The original excerpt contains a disrupted formula for the existing typical-cell distribution, referring to $\gamma_d(T)$, a cell center $z(C)$, a set $B$, and an isometry kernel $\kappa$; their definitions and the necessary hypotheses are missing, so that formula is not reconstructed here.

Short English statement

The problem asks for natural definitions of cell intensity and a typical cell when a hyperbolic tessellation has unbounded cells, compatible with the existing definitions where those apply. The supplied excerpt leaves the admissible tessellations and the meaning of naturality unspecified. This is a statement-only construction problem requiring mathematical review.
