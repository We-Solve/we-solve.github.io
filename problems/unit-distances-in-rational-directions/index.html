Unit Distances in Rational Directions

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n$ be a positive integer and let $P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a set of $n$ distinct points in the Euclidean plane with its fixed coordinate axes. For distinct $p,q\in P$, let $\theta(p,q)\in[0,\pi)$ denote the angle of the unoriented line through $p$ and $q$, measured from the positive $x$-axis modulo $\pi$. Define the number of unordered unit-distance pairs in rational directions by \[ U_{\mathrm{rat}}(P)=\#\left\{\{p,q\}\subseteq P:\ p\ne q,\ \lVert p-q\rVert_2=1,\ \frac{\theta(p,q)}{\pi}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\right\}. \] The literal question in the supplied statement is whether \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}\quad\forall P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2\text{ with }|P|=n,\qquad U_{\mathrm{rat}}(P)<n^{1+\varepsilon}. \] Here the positive-integer range for $n$ is an explicit convention; the record does not specify whether the empty configuration is included. The displayed question retains the strict inequality and coefficient $1$. No threshold $n_0(\varepsilon)$ or multiplicative constant is supplied, so an asymptotic interpretation cannot be substituted without clarification.

The supplied original statement presents this bound declaratively, whereas the cleaned statement presents it as a question. Moreover, the supplied dated literature assessment describes the extraction as a truncated theorem and conjecture fragment, reports that a rational-angle unit-distance bound is proved, and says that no complete underlying conjecture is stated for verification. Consequently, the display formalizes the literal supplied question only; the intended complete conjecture and its relation to the reported proved bound remain unresolved.

Short English statement

Count unordered pairs of distinct planar points at distance $1$ whose joining line has angle in $\pi{\mathbb{Q}}$ modulo $\pi$. The supplied question asks whether this count is strictly less than $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every positive integer $n$, every configuration, and every $\varepsilon>0$. The intended asymptotic qualifications and the complete underlying conjecture remain unclear.
