Caldero–Chapoton Maps for General Hereditary Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An algebra is hereditary if, in the chosen convention for its modules, every submodule of a projective module is projective. The source does not specify left versus right modules, the base field or division algebra, or finiteness assumptions on the algebra and its modules.

For an algebra $A$, an $A$-module $M$, and a suitable dimension datum $\mathbf e$, the submodule quiver Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}^{A}_{\mathbf e}(M)$ parametrizes submodules $U\subseteq M$ of dimension datum $\mathbf e$. This notation is conditional on specifying the base, the dimension convention, and the geometric meaning of the parameter space; the supplied record does not provide those choices for general hereditary algebras.

Problem (source formulation). For a general hereditary algebra $A$, determine an appropriate analogue of the Euler characteristic of $\operatorname{Gr}^{A}_{\mathbf e}(M)$ that can be used to define the Caldero--Chapoton map in all relevant cases. The supplied assessment explicitly includes real and quaternionic cases among the unresolved extensions and reports that a polynomial Euler characteristic analogue is available over finite fields.

Unresolved scope. The phrase "all relevant cases" is not defined. In particular, the source supplies neither a precise class of algebras and modules nor a target ring, normalization, compatibility axioms, or formula for the desired Caldero--Chapoton map. Consequently, this is a request to construct a suitable invariant and map, rather than a fully specified existence conjecture with fixed hypotheses. The reported finite-field result and unresolved extensions are supplied literature qualifications, not claims independently verified in this draft.

Short English statement

Find a replacement for the Euler characteristic of spaces of submodules that allows the Caldero--Chapoton map to be defined for general hereditary algebras. The supplied record reports a finite-field polynomial analogue and leaves extensions, including real and quaternionic cases, unresolved. The intended class of algebras, geometric conventions, and required properties of the replacement remain unspecified.
