Smoothability of Group Actions on One-Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a connected, oriented one-dimensional manifold, and let $\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(M)$ denote its group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms. Let $G<\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(M)$, and denote the given faithful action by $\iota:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(M)$. The source does not specify whether manifolds with boundary are allowed.

For a specified differentiability class $C^r$ and a compatible differentiable structure on $M$, write $\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)$ for the group of bijections of $M$ whose maps and inverses are of class $C^r$. The source leaves the range of $r$ and the differentiable structure unspecified; the following questions are conditional on making these choices.

  1. Abstract smoothability. For the given $G$ and $M$, does there exist an injective homomorphism \[ \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M)? \] Here $G$ is considered as an abstract group, so this question imposes no continuity requirement on $\rho$ with respect to a topology inherited from $\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(M)$. The source does not explicitly require this new action to preserve orientation.
  2. Smoothability of the given action. Does there exist a homeomorphism $h:M\to M$ such that \[ h\,\iota(g)\,h^{-1}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r}(M) \qquad\text{for every }g\in G? \] This asks for one homeomorphism simultaneously conjugating the entire given action to a $C^r$ action.
  3. Obstructions. What properties of $G$, of the given action, of $M$, or of the regularity $r$ obstruct the actions or conjugacies above? This is a request for obstruction criteria, with no specific necessary or sufficient condition asserted by the source.
The record also asks whether an infinite Kazhdan group can act faithfully by homeomorphisms on the circle $S^1={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$. A Kazhdan group is a group with property $(T)$. In the discrete-group interpretation, property $(T)$ means that every unitary representation $\pi:K\to\mathcal U(H)$ on a complex Hilbert space having almost invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector. Here almost invariant unit vectors means \[ \forall E\subseteq K\text{ finite}\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists v\in H: \quad \|v\|=1,\qquad \|\pi(k)v-v\|<\varepsilon\quad(k\in E). \] Under an existential reading, the circle question is \[ \exists K\ \left[\,|K|=\infty,\ K\text{ has property }(T),\ \exists\text{ an injective homomorphism } K\to\operatorname{Homeo}(S^1)\,\right]? \] The wording does not explicitly distinguish this reading from asking whether every infinite Kazhdan group admits such an action. Moreover, the statement does not impose countability, whereas the supplied literature assessment specifically mentions faithful countable property-$(T)$ circle actions. These quantifier and domain qualifications remain unresolved. The circle question uses all homeomorphisms; orientation preservation is not expressly required.

Short English statement

For a group already acting faithfully on a connected one-dimensional manifold, ask whether its abstract group has a faithful $C^r$ action on the same manifold, whether the given action becomes $C^r$ after a topological change of coordinates, and what obstructs these possibilities. A further question concerns faithful circle actions of infinite groups with Kazhdan's property $(T)$. Regularity, boundary conventions, and the precise quantifiers and countability restriction in the Kazhdan question require clarification.
