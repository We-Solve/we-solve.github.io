Statement

For a finite set $P\subset\mathbb{R}^2$, write \[ \Delta(P):=\{\|p-q\|:p,q\in P,\ p\ne q\} \] for the set of (nonzero) Euclidean distances determined by pairs of points. Let $\mathcal{G}_n$ be the family of all $n$-point sets $P\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ in general position in the sense:

  • no three points of $P$ are collinear;
  • no four points of $P$ are concyclic.
Define \[ h(n):=\min\{\,|\Delta(P)|: P\in\mathcal{G}_n\,\}. \]

Question $\#98$. Does $h(n)/n\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$?

Restatement from Math-672-A3 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.

Erdős Problem #98

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

I pursue path (A): continue the proof attempt by determining the next exact small case after Round-1. Round-1 established $h(3)=1$ and $h(4)=2$, but did not determine $h(5)$. The Round-2 target is to prove the exact value of $h(5)$.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 results without re-proving them.

  • The exact values $h(3)=1$ and $h(4)=2$.
  • The problem remains globally unresolved: Round-1 only gave the general bounds \[ c\,\frac{n}{\log n}\le h(n)\le n\exp(c\sqrt{\log n}). \]

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

The new ingredient is a two-sided exact argument for $n=5$.

  • The lower bound $h(5)\ge 3$ comes from a classification theorem: a $5$-point planar set with only two distances must be a regular pentagon, which is excluded by the "no four concyclic" hypothesis.
  • The upper bound $h(5)\le 3$ comes from an explicit $5$-point configuration in general position that determines exactly three distances.

Together these give the exact value $h(5)=3$.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

The gap left by Round-1 was the complete lack of exact information beyond $n=4$.

To close the next case, I need two claims:

  • Lower bound: a $5$-point set in general position cannot determine only $2$ distances.
  • Upper bound: there exists a $5$-point set in general position determining exactly $3$ distances.

The first claim uses a precise external classification theorem; the second is fully explicit and verified by direct computation.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Theorem.
\[ h(5)=3. \]
Proof of the lower bound $h(5)\ge 3$.
I use the following theorem.

External theorem used. Among planar $5$-point sets that determine exactly two distances, the regular pentagon is the unique possibility up to similarity.

Assume for contradiction that $h(5)=2$. Then there exists a $5$-point set $P\subset \mathbf{R}^2$ in general position such that $|\Delta(P)|=2$. By the external theorem, $P$ must be similar to a regular pentagon. But all five vertices of a regular pentagon are concyclic, so $P$ violates the condition "no four points are concyclic". This contradiction proves \[ h(5)\ge 3. \]

Proof of the upper bound $h(5)\le 3$.
Consider the five points \[ A=(0,0),\qquad B=(1,0),\qquad C=\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\frac12\right),\qquad D=\left(\frac12,1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),\qquad E=(0,1). \] Let \[ P:=\{A,B,C,D,E\}. \]

I first compute all squared pairwise distances. A direct calculation gives \[ AB^2=AC^2=AE^2=CE^2=DE^2=1, \] \[ AD^2=BC^2=BD^2=CD^2=2-\sqrt3, \] and \[ BE^2=2. \] These are all ten unordered pairs, so \[ \Delta(P)=\left\{1,\sqrt{2-\sqrt3},\sqrt2\right\}, \] hence \[ |\Delta(P)|=3. \]

It remains to check that $P$ is in general position.

No three points are collinear.
For three points $X,Y,Z$, let $\Delta(X,Y,Z)$ denote twice the signed area of triangle $XYZ$. A direct calculation yields \[ \begin{array}{lll} \Delta(A,B,C)=\frac12, & \Delta(A,B,D)=1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, & \Delta(A,B,E)=1, \\[6pt] \Delta(A,C,D)=-1+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, & \Delta(A,C,E)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, & \Delta(A,D,E)=\frac12, \\[6pt] \Delta(B,C,D)=-\frac32+\sqrt3, & \Delta(B,C,E)=-\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, & \Delta(B,D,E)=\frac12-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}, \\[6pt] \Delta(C,D,E)=-\frac12. \end{array} \] All ten values are nonzero because $\sqrt3\neq 1$ and $\sqrt3\neq 2$. Therefore no three points of $P$ are collinear.
No four points are concyclic.
For four points $X_1,\dots,X_4$ with coordinates $(x_i,y_i)$, define \[ \Gamma(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4):= \det\!\begin{pmatrix} x_1^2+y_1^2 & x_1 & y_1 & 1\\ x_2^2+y_2^2 & x_2 & y_2 & 1\\ x_3^2+y_3^2 & x_3 & y_3 & 1\\ x_4^2+y_4^2 & x_4 & y_4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \] The four points are concyclic if and only if $\Gamma=0$. For the five $4$-subsets of $P$, direct computation gives \[ \begin{array}{ll} \Gamma(A,B,C,D)=\dfrac{2-\sqrt3}{2}, & \Gamma(A,B,C,E)=\dfrac{1-\sqrt3}{2}, \\[10pt] \Gamma(A,B,D,E)=\dfrac{1-\sqrt3}{2}, & \Gamma(A,C,D,E)=\dfrac{\sqrt3-2}{2}, \\[10pt] \Gamma(B,C,D,E)=\sqrt3-2. \end{array} \] Again every value is nonzero. So no four points of $P$ are concyclic.

Thus $P$ is in general position and determines exactly $3$ distances, which proves \[ h(5)\le 3. \]

Combining the lower and upper bounds, we conclude \[ h(5)=3.\qquad \square \]

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Lower bound dependence on literature. The only external input is the precise classification of planar $5$-point $2$-distance sets. Its hypothesis matches exactly the hypothetical case $h(5)=2$.
  • Have all ten distances been checked? Yes. The partition \[ 5+4+1=10 \] of the unordered pairs matches the three displayed distance classes.
  • Have all collinearity obstructions been checked? Yes. There are exactly $\binom53=10$ triples, and all ten signed areas were written down explicitly.
  • Have all cocircularity obstructions been checked? Yes. There are exactly $\binom54=5$ four-point subsets, and all five determinants were computed explicitly.
  • Interaction with Round-1. This strictly extends the exact information from $h(4)=2$ to $h(5)=3$.

7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round-2 proves the exact value \[ h(5)=3. \] This is a genuine new theorem beyond Round-1, though the original asymptotic question $h(n)/n\to\infty$ remains open.

Problem 98: a uniform linear bound and exact verification of the example

1) OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE REVIEW

Attempt dated 2026-09-23. Let $h(n)$ be the least number of distinct distances in an $n$-point planar set with no three collinear and no four concyclic. The target is whether $h(n)/n\to\infty$. I read the entire supplied gpt_pro_5.4/98.tex, which discusses $h(5)$ via a classification and explicit coordinates. Here I verify its coordinates exactly and derive an elementary bound for every $n$.

2) WORK

Fix a vertex $v$. At most three other vertices can be at any one distance from $v$: four would lie on the circle with that center and radius. If $d$ distances occur in the whole configuration, it follows that \[ n-1\le3d,\qquad h(n)\ge\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rceil. \] The argument uses only the no-four-concyclic condition. Equivalently, each fixed-distance graph has maximum degree at most three, so its at most $3n/2$ edges cannot cover more than that many of the $\binom n2$ pairs. This improves the elementary scale quoted in the supplied discussion but does not reach the conjectured superlinear scale.

I also rechecked its five coordinates \[ (0,0),(1,0),(\sqrt3/2,1/2),(1/2,1-\sqrt3/2),(0,1) \] using rational arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. The ten squared distances have multiplicities $5,4,1$ at $1,2-\sqrt3,2$. All ten signed-area determinants and all five circle determinants are nonzero. Thus the concrete upper bound $h(5)\le3$ is certified. The independently executed code and exact determinant values are in verification/batch_1_checks.py and its JSON output. This verifies an existing example rather than claiming a new one.

3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

The distance circle need not contain its center; four neighbors alone already violate the hypothesis. The linear lower bound gives only two at $n=5$ and does not replace the supplied classification argument. The source's arXiv citation has an incorrect author list: the checked record lists Zoltán Kovács alone. Its abstract concerns six-point isosceles configurations, so I do not treat the abstract alone as verification of the precise five-point classification.

4) FINAL

UNRESOLVED. The bound remains linear. To prove the original limit one needs a restriction coupling many distinct distance graphs; maximum degree three separately in each graph cannot supply that restriction. No verified superlinear improvement was found.

Complete supplied file and exact local computation on 2026-09-23. Kovács, A note on Erdős's mysterious remark, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05190, checked for citation accuracy; no unverified classification is used in the new proof.

Erdős Problem #98

  1. Formal Restatement.

    For a finite set $P\subset\mathbb{R}^2$, write \[ \Delta(P):=\{\|p-q\|:p,q\in P,\ p\ne q\} \] for the set of (nonzero) Euclidean distances determined by pairs of points. Let $\mathcal{G}_n$ be the family of all $n$-point sets $P\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ in general position in the sense:

    • no three points of $P$ are collinear;
    • no four points of $P$ are concyclic.
    Define \[ h(n):=\min\{\,|\Delta(P)|: P\in\mathcal{G}_n\,\}. \]

    Question $\#98$. Does $h(n)/n\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$?

  2. Quick literature/context check.

    The Erdős Problems page lists this as open (last edited 15 Oct 2025).[ErdosProblems98] It records that Erdős could not even prove $h(n)\ge n$, and that Pach proved an upper bound $h(n)<n^{\log_2 3}$.[ErdosProblems98] It also records a stronger upper bound by Erdős--Füredi--Pach--Ruzsa $1993$: \[ h(n) < n\exp\left(c\sqrt{\log n}\right)\quad\text{for some constant }c>0. \] [ErdosProblems98,EFPR93]

    Independently, for arbitrary $n$-point sets (with no general-position hypotheses), Guth and Katz proved that the number of distinct distances is at least on the order of $n/\log n$.[GK15] Since $\mathcal{G}_n$ is a subset of all $n$-point sets, this lower bound applies to $h(n)$ as well.

  3. Attack plan.

    Proof track (toward $h(n)/n\to\infty$). Seek a superlinear lower bound on $|\Delta(P)|$ under the combined restrictions "no three collinear" and "no four concyclic." Natural approaches:

    • strengthen incidence-based distinct distance arguments by ruling out rich lines and rich circles;
    • "pinned distance" methods: show some point determines $\omega(n)$ distinct distances, then $|\Delta(P)|\ge \omega(n)$.

    Disproof track (construct $P\in\mathcal{G}_n$ with $|\Delta(P)|=O(n)$). Try to build a highly structured configuration with many repeated distances while avoiding collinear triples and concyclic quadruples. A key obstacle is that small perturbations typically destroy distance multiplicities.

    Phase 1 small cases. Compute/construct $h(n)$ for the smallest $n$ to sanity-check the definition and the general-position constraints.

  4. Work.

    (A) Tiny cases (exact).

    Claim 1: $h(3)=1$. Take an equilateral triangle; then $|\Delta(P)|=1$, and clearly no three collinear. Hence $h(3)\le 1$. On the other hand, any three non-collinear points determine at least one distance, so $h(3)=1$.

    Claim 2: $h(4)=2$. Upper bound. Let $A,B,C$ be an equilateral triangle of side length $1$, and let $O$ be its circumcenter (which coincides with centroid). Then \[ \|A-B\|=\|B-C\|=\|C-A\|=1,\qquad \|O-A\|=\|O-B\|=\|O-C\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}. \] Thus $\Delta(\{A,B,C,O\})=\{1,1/\sqrt{3}\}$ has size $2$. The four points are in general position: no three are collinear, and they are not concyclic because the unique circle through $A,B,C$ is the circumcircle centered at $O$, which does not contain $O$. So $h(4)\le 2$.

    Lower bound. A set of four planar points cannot have all six pairwise distances equal (the largest equidistant set in $\mathbb{R}^2$ has size $3$), so $h(4)\ne 1$. Therefore $h(4)=2$.

    (B) General bounds from the literature.

    Lower bound (all $n$). Guth--Katz proved that for any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, \[ |\Delta(P)| \ge c\,\frac{n}{\log n} \] for some absolute constant $c>0$.[GK15] Since $\mathcal{G}_n$ is a subset of all $n$-point sets, \[ h(n)=\min_{P\in\mathcal{G}_n}|\Delta(P)| \ge c\,\frac{n}{\log n}. \] This is far from the desired superlinear growth, but it is a rigorous baseline.

    Upper bound (general position). Erdős--Füredi--Pach--Ruzsa constructed (for all sufficiently large $n$) $n$-point sets in general position with \[ |\Delta(P)| < n\exp\left(c\sqrt{\log n}\right) \] for some constant $c>0$.[EFPR93,ErdosProblems98] Hence \[ h(n) \le n\exp\left(c\sqrt{\log n}\right). \]

    Current gap. To answer "$h(n)/n\to\infty$?" one needs either:

    • a lower bound $h(n)\ge n\cdot\omega(1)$ (even $h(n)\ge n\log\log n$ would suffice), or
    • a construction showing $h(n)=O(n)$.
    Neither is currently achieved by the bounds above.

  5. Verification.

    The computations for $h(3)$ and $h(4)$ are explicit and verify the general-position constraints. For the general bounds:

    • Guth--Katz applies to all point sets, so it applies to $\mathcal{G}_n$ without extra hypotheses.
    • EFPR93 is explicitly a construction in general position as quoted on the Erdős Problems page, and thus gives an upper bound on $h(n)$.
    No claim here asserts the limit $h(n)/n\to\infty$; rather, these bounds delineate what is known.

  6. FINAL: UNRESOLVED.

    • (i) Strongest fully proved partial result: For all $n$, $h(n)\ge c\,n/\log n$ (Guth--Katz), and there exist general-position sets with $h(n)\le n\exp(c\sqrt{\log n})$ (EFPR93). Also $h(3)=1$ and $h(4)=2$ exactly.
    • (ii) Most plausible route forward: Either (a) prove a substantially improved lower bound in general position by combining incidence geometry with the prohibition of 4-point cocircularities, or (b) construct a genuinely linear-distance construction $|\Delta(P)|=O(n)$ while certifying no 3 collinear and no 4 concyclic.
    • (iii) First place the proof attempt breaks: Known distinct-distance lower bounds (even with modern polynomial/incidence methods) are essentially linear up to polylog factors; current techniques do not yield superlinear lower bounds from these general-position constraints.
    • (iv) What a counterexample would have to look like: An infinite family $P_n\in\mathcal{G}_n$ with $|\Delta(P_n)|\le Cn$ for a constant $C$ independent of $n$.

  7. Completion estimate.

    COMPLETION: 15%.
