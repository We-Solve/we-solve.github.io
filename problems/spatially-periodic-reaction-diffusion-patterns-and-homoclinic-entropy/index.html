Spatially Periodic Reaction-Diffusion Patterns and Homoclinic Entropy

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $m\in{\mathbb{N}}$ with $m>1$, and let $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^m$ denote the spatial variable. A solution $u$ is stationary in time if there is a spatial profile $v$ such that $u(t,x)=v(x)$ for every time and spatial point in its domain.
  1. Can the one-dimensional complexity result referred to in the supplied report passage be extended to stationary, asymptotically spatially periodic solutions of reaction--diffusion systems on ${\mathbb{R}}^m$, with localized defects as specified in the supplied clean statement?
  2. Do symmetric homoclinic tangles involving the multi-parameter families of periodic orbits referred to in the supplied clean statement always yield positive topological entropy?

These are qualified problem statements rather than fully specified mathematical propositions. The supplied excerpt calls the first target a "similar result" but does not state the one-dimensional theorem, its hypotheses, or its complexity conclusion. It does not specify the reaction--diffusion equations, the solution class, the meaning of asymptotic spatial periodicity, or the localization condition for defects. For the second question, the excerpt refers to "such families" without supplying their defining context. The relevant dynamical system, symmetry, homoclinic-tangle hypotheses, parameter space, and entropy convention are also unspecified. Consequently, no particular periodicity lattice, asymptotic condition, transversality assumption, or entropy-bearing invariant set is imposed here.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether a one-dimensional complexity result extends to stationary spatially periodic reaction--diffusion patterns with localized defects in higher dimensions, and whether the associated symmetric homoclinic tangles always have positive topological entropy.
