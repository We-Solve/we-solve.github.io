A curriculum-order route and its obstruction

The report discusses architecture-specific reasoning and clean-label effects, not a universal easy-first theorem. We test whether correctly labeled examples can be ordered by a simple feature-norm notion of ease without regard to optimization interactions. Our explicit surrogate is linear regression with two sequential stochastic-gradient updates, not a transformer or state-space model. Let the true coefficient be $\beta=(1,1)$, and take examples \[ a=(1,0),\ y_a=1;\qquad b=(1,1),\ y_b=2. \] Both labels equal the true linear response. Start $\theta=0$ and use step size $\eta=1/2$ on each individual loss $\tfrac12(\theta^Tx-y)^2$, so the update is $T_x(\theta)=\theta+\tfrac12(y-\theta^Tx)x$.
Proposition: The order $a,b$ ends at $(5/4,3/4)$; the order $b,a$ ends at $(1,1)$. Under a test law with $\mathbb E[XX^T]=I$, their squared prediction risks are respectively $1/8$ and zero.
Proof: The first $a$ update gives $(1/2,0)$. Its prediction on $b$ is $1/2$, hence the $b$ update adds $(3/4,3/4)$, yielding $(5/4,3/4)$. In the other order, the first $b$ update is $(1,1)$, already the true coefficient, so the $a$ update is zero. For either output, risk is $(\theta-\beta)^T\mathbb E[XX^T](\theta-\beta)=\|\theta-\beta\|^2$, giving $2(1/4)^2=1/8$ and zero. ∎
Thus the smaller-norm example first is worse for this declared one-pass objective even though all labels are correct. The lesson is the exact failure of a universal ordering inference, not a recommendation to reverse practical curricula. Multiple passes, other step sizes and other test laws can change the comparison.

Verification and remaining gaps

The four rational updates and both risks were computed. Architecture-specific algorithmic reasoning, long-sequence extrapolation and scalable clean-example selection remain outside this model. No criterion in the source is declared met by this auxiliary example.
