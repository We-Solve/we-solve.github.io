Five Open Problems — Compensated compactness for symmetric matrices

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A symmetric matrix field has values in $\operatorname{Sym}_N(\mathbb R)=\{A\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}:A^{\mathsf T}=A\}$. Compensated compactness studies nonlinear information retained under weak convergence because of additional differential constraints. In the requested setting that information may consist of inequalities rather than exact weak-continuity identities. The source does not specify the matrix size, integrability, positivity assumptions, differential constraints, or nonlinear quantities in those inequalities.

Problem (Denis Serre, 2012). Develop a compensated-compactness calculus for such symmetric-matrix situations where only inequalities are available. As a first application, prove complete continuity of the solution semigroup for the multidimensional scalar-conservation-law Cauchy problem.

For context, the usual Euclidean Cauchy problem has the form \[ \partial_tu+\operatorname{div}_xF(u)=0\quad(t>0,\ x\in\mathbb R^d), \qquad u(0,x)=u_0(x), \] and its entropy-solution semigroup, when defined, is written $S_tu_0=u(t,\cdot)$. No flux $F$, spatial dimension $d$, initial-data space, entropy notion, or flux nondegeneracy is given in the supplied record. The equation above is contextual notation, not a theorem asserted for every possible flux.

Complete continuity must also be specified: a weak-to-strong statement such as $u_{0,j}\rightharpoonup u_0$ implying $S_tu_{0,j}\to S_tu_0$ in a chosen strong topology is different from merely mapping bounded sets to relatively compact sets. The source supplies neither the topologies nor time, localization, or boundedness conditions. The task therefore preserves the two linked goals without inventing a universal compactness theorem. The research summary reports limited forms of symmetric-matrix compensated compactness and leaves the proposed calculus and application unresolved.

Short English statement

Build an inequality-based compensated-compactness theory for symmetric matrix fields, then use it to establish the intended compactness/continuity property of multidimensional scalar-conservation-law evolution. The hypotheses and topologies are missing.
