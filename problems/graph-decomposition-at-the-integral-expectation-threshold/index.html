Graph decomposition at the integral expectation threshold

OpenAI's claim

Graph pieces at the expectation threshold: The paper claims a partition of any graph’s edge set into a universally bounded number of pieces whose containment thresholds are controlled by the graph’s integral expectation threshold. The pieces are fixed before host sampling, and their separate embeddings need not be compatible.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For a finite simple graph $J$ on at most $n$ vertices, let $p_c(J;n)=\min\{p:\mathbb P(G(n,p)\supset J)\ge1/2\}$. Let $q(H;n)$ be the largest $p$ for which the family of edge sets containing $H$ has a containment cover $\mathcal C$ with $\sum_{S\in\mathcal C}p^{|S|}\le1/2$. Both parameters are set to zero for edgeless graphs. The claim gives absolute $k\ge2,L\ge1$ such that every $n\ge2$ and $H$ on at most $n$ vertices admit \[E(H)=E(H_1)\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}E(H_k),\qquad p_c(H_i;n)\le Lq(H;n).\] Pieces may be empty or share vertices; their choice precedes the random-host experiment. The separate copies are not required to identify any vertices common to the pieces.

Outline of the argument

Graph pieces at the expectation threshold: A covering input is converted into uniform neighborhood estimates for random hosts. Direct embedding lemmas handle the resulting structured graph pieces. The edge partition is assembled using those estimates so that a single universal number of pieces suffices, without requiring the piece embeddings to agree on their common vertices.
