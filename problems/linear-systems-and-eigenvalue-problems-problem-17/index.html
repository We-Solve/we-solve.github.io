Linear Systems and Eigenvalue Problems — Problem 17

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the usual real interpretation, a symmetric positive definite (SPD) system consists of $A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}$ with $A^{\mathsf T}=A$ and $v^{\mathsf T}Av>0$ for every nonzero $v\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$, together with $b\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$. Here $n\geq1$, and CG denotes the conjugate-gradient method for solving $Ax=b$. The source does not explicitly specify the scalar field; this real interpretation does not assert an extension to complex Hermitian systems.

The phrase "normwise backward error" requires a norm and a perturbation convention. For example, given a vector norm and its induced matrix norm, one possible definition for an approximate solution $\widehat x$ is \[ \eta_{A,b}(\widehat x)=\inf\left\{\delta\geq0:\begin{array}{l} \exists\,\Delta A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n},\ \Delta b\in{\mathbb{R}}^n,\\ (A+\Delta A)\widehat x=b+\Delta b,\\ \|\Delta A\|\leq\delta\|A\|,\quad \|\Delta b\|\leq\delta\|b\| \end{array}\right\}. \] This example allows perturbations to both data and imposes no symmetry or positive-definiteness requirement on the perturbed matrix. The supplied source does not select this definition, a particular norm, or these perturbation rules; they must not be treated as source hypotheses.

Problem (source formulation). Determine how many bits of precision are necessary to guarantee that CG applied to an $n\times n$ SPD system $Ax=b$ produces, in at most $n$ steps, an approximate solution whose normwise backward error is at most the prescribed tolerance $\epsilon$.

More formally, once an arithmetic model, a CG implementation, initialization, an admissible input class, and a backward-error functional $\eta$ have been fixed, the requested guarantee has the form \[ \text{for every admissible }(A,b),\qquad \exists\,k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:\quad \eta(\widehat x_k)\leq\epsilon, \] where $\widehat x_k$ is an iterate computed at the proposed precision. The source does not specify these conventions, the admissible range of $\epsilon$, or whether the precision must depend only on $n$ and $\epsilon$ or may also depend on conditioning and other input parameters. Thus it supplies a precision-complexity question, but not a uniquely specified quantitative bound to be established.

Short English statement

Determine the precision needed for conjugate gradients to achieve normwise backward error at most $\epsilon$ within $n$ steps on an $n\times n$ SPD system. The arithmetic model, backward-error convention, initialization, and permitted dependence on the input remain unspecified.
