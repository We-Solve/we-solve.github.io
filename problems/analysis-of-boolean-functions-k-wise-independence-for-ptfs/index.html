Analysis of Boolean Functions — $k$-wise Independence for PTFs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix $d\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. For a positive integer $n$, let $\gamma_n$ denote the distribution of a vector $\mathbf{G}=(G_1,\ldots,G_n)$ of independent standard normal random variables. Thus $\mathbf{G}$ is the random vector denoted by $\mathbb{Z}$ in the supplied statement.

For a positive integer $k$, call an ${\mathbb{R}}^n$-valued random vector $\mathbf{X}=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ $k$-wise independent with standard Gaussian marginals if, for every subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfying $1\leq |S|\leq\min\{k,n\}$, the joint distribution of $(X_i)_{i\in S}$ is the product of $|S|$ standard normal distributions. In particular, this definition requires full independence when $k\geq n$.

Under this conventional meaning of $k$-wise independence, and interpreting "degree-$d$" as total degree at most $d$, the requested quantity is \[ \begin{split} k_*(d,\varepsilon)=\min\bigl\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:{}&\ \text{for every }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\ \text{every }p\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n] \text{ of total degree at most }d,\\ &\ \text{and every }\mathbf{X}\text{ with the stated }k\text{-wise Gaussian property},\\ &\ \bigl|\Pr[p(\mathbf{X})\geq0]-\Pr[p(\mathbf{G})\geq0]\bigr|\leq\varepsilon \bigr\}. \end{split} \] Here the zero polynomial is included, and the minimum is assigned the value $+\infty$ if the set is empty. The problem is to determine $k_*(d,\varepsilon)$, uniformly over the dimension $n$. The threshold convention is $p\geq0$, including points where $p=0$.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The supplied statement explicitly mentions independence of collections of exactly $k$ coordinates. The definition above uses the conventional interpretation involving all collections of at most $k$ distinct coordinates; for $n<k$, the literal exactly-$k$ condition would instead be vacuous apart from the separately required Gaussian marginals. The source also does not explicitly distinguish total degree exactly $d$ from total degree at most $d$; the display uses the latter interpretation. If exactly $d$ is intended, the polynomial quantifier must be restricted accordingly. The convention for whether $0\in{\mathbb{N}}$ is unspecified; if degree zero is admitted, the same formulation includes constant polynomials.

Supplied background, not independently verified. The record attributes to Diakonikolas, Gopalan, Jaiswal, Servedio, and Viola [DGJ+09] the sufficiency of $k=O(\varepsilon^{-2})$ for $d=1$; to Diakonikolas, Kane, and Nelson [DKN10] the sufficiency of $k=O(\varepsilon^{-8})$ for $d=2$; and to Kane [Kan11b] the general sufficient bound $O_d(1)\varepsilon^{-2^{O(d)}}$ and necessary bound $\Omega(d^2/\varepsilon^2)$. These are recorded contextual claims; their precise parameter ranges, constants, and applicability to the displayed Gaussian formulation require checking the cited works.

Short English statement

Determine the smallest $k$, depending only on $d$ and $\varepsilon$, for which every $k$-wise independent vector with standard Gaussian coordinates approximates every degree-$d$ polynomial threshold probability within $\varepsilon$, in every dimension. The draft explicitly records unresolved degree and small-dimension conventions. The registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
