Optimal-order distinct distances in every dimension at least three

OpenAI's claim

Higher-dimensional distinct distances: For fixed $d\ge3$, the claimed distance lower bound for $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb R^d$ is $c_d n^{2/d}$. The constant depends only on dimension, with no vanishing subpower factor.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
In every fixed integer dimension $d\ge3$, a constant $c_d>0$ is claimed to satisfy \[\left|\{|x-y|:x,y\in P,\ x\ne y\}\right|\ge c_d|P|^{2/d}\] for every finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^d$ of at least two distinct points. The constant depends only on the dimension. This is a global distinct-distance count, not a bound on how often one distance occurs.

Outline of the argument

Higher-dimensional distinct distances: Polynomial interpolation and Hilbert-function estimates track concentration at several degree scales. Geometry of skew-form evaluation flats gives sparse-cone and rich-motion bounds, with exceptional algebraic families treated explicitly. These incidence estimates are combined with the distance reduction in a way that retains a fixed multiplicative constant rather than only the exponent up to a small loss.
