Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 4. Identify the purported limits of the Cohn--Miller sequences, explain the observed rational Taylor coefficients, and determine whether rational coefficients occur beyond the quadratic terms. The extract does not define the sequences, dimensions, normalization, root modification, or convergence topology. Conditional formulation of the supplied aid. For $d\in\{8,24\}$, write $f_{d,m}$ for the intended normalized modified-root Cohn--Miller functions and $M_d$ for the corresponding normalized magic function, leaving their omitted construction explicit. The radial Schwartz space $\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rad}}({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ consists of smooth radial functions with all seminorms \[ p_{A,\alpha}(f)=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d}(1+|x|)^A|\partial^\alpha f(x)| \] finite, for every nonnegative integer $A$ and multi-index $\alpha$. A cluster point means a limit of a subsequence with indices tending to infinity in this topology. The aid asserts \[ f_{d,m_j}\longrightarrow f\text{ in }\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rad}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f=M_d. \] If the entire sequence is relatively compact in this space, this uniqueness of cluster points implies $f_{d,m}\to M_d$. The construction of $M_d$ independently of the sequence does not establish relative compactness or convergence of the sequence. Nor is a unique cluster-point statement an existence statement for a cluster point.

To make the aid's Taylor--moment language precise under an explicit convention, put \[ \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] For radial Schwartz $f$, the coefficient of the finite Taylor jet of $r\mapsto\widehat f(re_1)$ of order $2j$ is \[ \frac1{(2j)!}\left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}^{2j}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} r^{2j}} \widehat f(re_1)\right|_{r=0} =\frac{(-1)^j\pi^{2j}}{j!(d/2)_j} \int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}|x|^{2j}f(x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] where $(a)_0=1$ and $(a)_j=a(a+1)\cdots(a+j-1)$. In particular the quadratic coefficient is $-2\pi^2/d$ times the second moment. The analogous identity for $f$ uses $\widehat f$, since radial functions are even. This is an explicitly chosen Fourier normalization, not a recovery of an omitted source convention. Schwartz convergence transfers each finite Taylor jet and each polynomially weighted moment; it need not imply convergence of an infinite Taylor series to a Schwartz function. The aid reports that all four conjectured quadratic coefficients for the dimension-eight and dimension-twenty-four functions and their transforms are proved, but supplies neither their values nor their proving citations here. It reports no verified theorem for additional rational coefficients or for irrationality of higher coefficients. No such values or theorem are invented.

Adjacent Epstein-zeta context. For a full-rank covolume-one lattice $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, put \[ \theta_L(t)=\sum_{v\in L}e^{-\pi t|v|^2},\qquad E(L,s)=\sum_{v\in L\setminus\{0\}}|v|^{-2s} \quad(\operatorname{Re}s>n/2). \] Then the usual Mellin relation is \[ \pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)E(L,s) =\int_0^\infty(\theta_L(t)-1)t^{s-1}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t \quad(\operatorname{Re}s>n/2). \] These objects depend on the vector-length spectrum with multiplicities. The source attributes continuation to Epstein; "for all $s$" must be understood meromorphically, with a pole at $s=n/2$, not as a finite value there. This paragraph supplies context, not a definition of the Cohn--Miller sequences or an additional zeta-minimization question. Short English statement

The source asks what the Cohn--Miller approximations converge to and why some Taylor coefficients are rational. The aid identifies possible Schwartz limits in dimensions eight and twenty-four and reduces convergence to relative compactness.
