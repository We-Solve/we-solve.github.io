Math-1331

Linear-span lifting and exact plane-curve component dimensions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix integers $d\ge1$, $g$, and $n\ge2$. Write $\operatorname{Hilb}_{d,g}({\mathbb{P}}^n)$ for the Hilbert scheme with polynomial $dt+1-g$, so $d$ is degree and $g$ arithmetic genus. The source asks for a good, preferably sharp, lower bound $L(d,g,n)$ such that every component in its intended class has dimension at least $L(d,g,n)$. The source does not specify whether all subschemes with this polynomial are allowed or only components whose general member is a smooth connected curve, nor a ground field. For the usual smooth-curve deformation interpretation, work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field and keep this interpretation explicit.

For a smooth connected curve $C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n$, the normal Euler characteristic is \[ E_n(d,g)=(n+1)d+(n-3)(1-g). \] This is the expected dimension benchmark and deformation-theoretic lower bound for components through such curves. It specializes to the source's $4d$ in ${\mathbb{P}}^3$ and $5d+1-g$ in ${\mathbb{P}}^4$; the source says the latter is inadequate for fixed $d$ and sufficiently large $g$. Its original remark cites a better bound in the range $d^3\le\lambda(n)g^2$, but does not define $\lambda(n)$ or state that bound.

The supplied aid reports this component-construction criterion. Let $2\le r\le n$, and let $C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^r$ be smooth, nondegenerate, and linearly normal, of degree $d$ and genus $g$, with \[ H^1(C,N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^r})=0. \] Linearly normal means that the restriction $H^0({\mathbb{P}}^r,\mathcal O(1))\to H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(1))$ is an isomorphism. Moving the curve in its component and moving its linear span in ${\mathbb{P}}^n$ is reported to produce an actual ambient Hilbert component of dimension \[ h^0(C,N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^r})+(r+1)(n-r). \] When the original component has expected dimension, the ambient component's excess over $E_n(d,g)$ is exactly \[ (n-r)(g+r-d). \] This is a family of exact benchmarks, not a universal improved lower bound.

For smooth degree-$d$ plane curves with $d\ge2$, $g=(d-1)(d-2)/2$, the reported component in ${\mathbb{P}}^n$ has dimension \[ \binom{d+2}{2}-1+3(n-2). \] Its excess over $E_n(d,g)$ and its normal obstruction-space dimension both equal \[ (n-2)\binom{d-2}{2}=h^1(C,N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^n}). \] Here a nonzero potential obstruction space is not a claim that actual deformations are obstructed; the aid calls it ineffective in this family. The restriction $d\ge2$ retains the plane as the minimal span, excluding lines. Finally rational normal curves of degree $r$ and genus zero in their span ${\mathbb{P}}^r$, including their ambient lifts, are reported to give an infinite family where expected dimension is sharp. The aid does not determine the requested sharp lower bound for all $(d,g,n)$.

Short English statement

The source asks for sharp lower bounds on dimensions of Hilbert-scheme components of curves. The supplied aid constructs exact component dimensions by varying a curve and its linear span, including plane-curve examples with excess dimension and rational normal examples where expected dimension is sharp.
