10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Stability conditions for tightness of k-median LP and k-means SDP

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Given finite data and an integer $1\leq k\leq N$, the source asks for stability-type hypotheses ensuring exactness of a $k$-median linear-programming relaxation or a $k$-means semidefinite-programming relaxation. "Well-explained by $k$ clusters" is motivation, not a specified mathematical hypothesis.

In a common discrete $k$-median model, data points have dissimilarities $d_{ij}$ and one selects at most $k$ data-point centers. A standard LP uses assignment variables $x_{ij}$ and center variables $y_j$: \[ \min\sum_{i,j}d_{ij}x_{ij},\quad \sum_jx_{ij}=1,\quad0\leq x_{ij}\leq y_j\leq1,\quad \sum_jy_j\leq k. \] Integrality would mean an optimum is attained by $0$--$1$ assignments and center selections.

For Euclidean $k$-means, the discrete objective for a partition into nonempty clusters is \[ \sum_{C}\sum_{i\in C}\|v_i-\bar v_C\|_2^2, \qquad \bar v_C=|C|^{-1}\sum_{i\in C}v_i. \] In a common partition-matrix SDP, a genuine partition is encoded by \[ Z=\sum_C|C|^{-1}\mathbf1_C\mathbf1_C^{\mathsf T}. \] Tightness means that an SDP optimum has such a partition encoding; it does not mean every entry of $Z$ is $0$ or $1$. The source does not supply a particular LP/SDP, so these examples explain typical meanings rather than select verified source formulations.

The requested task is to identify precise stability properties and parameter regimes that guarantee the relevant relaxation is integral/exact. For example, a common perturbation-stability notion asks that a unique optimal partition survive every admissible multiplicative perturbation $d_{ij}\leq d'_{ij}\leq\alpha d_{ij}$, but the source fixes neither this notion, the perturbation class, nor $\alpha$. It also does not specify whether exactness means existence of an integral optimum, integrality of all optima, or unique recovery of a distinguished partition.

The supplied summary reports exactness under strong separation in well-separated mixture/community settings, and near-optimal algorithms under more permissive stability hypotheses. It expressly distinguishes those algorithmic results from a general proof of exact LP/SDP integrality under stability, which it calls still partially open. No quantitative separation or stability theorem is supplied.

Short English statement

The problem seeks stability assumptions that force clustering relaxations to return genuine cluster solutions. The exact relaxations and stability model are unspecified. The supplied summary reports separation-based results and stability-based algorithms, while distinguishing both from general exact-integrality guarantees.
