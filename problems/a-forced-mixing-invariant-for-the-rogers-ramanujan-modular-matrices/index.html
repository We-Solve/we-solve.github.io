The core of source problem 1.4 asks whether diagonalizing transformation matrices of vector-valued modular forms is mathematically informative. Its Rogers--Ramanujan example has diagonal $T$ for $\tau\mapsto\tau+1$ and nondiagonal $S$ for $\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$. The attached aid gives a precise weight-zero, two-dimensional formulation. Let $\rho:\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{C}})$ be irreducible, with matrices $S,T$ satisfying $S^2=(ST)^3=I$. In a $T$-eigenbasis write $T=\operatorname{diag}(a,b)$ with $a\ne b$. It states \[ S_{12}S_{21}=\frac{a^2-ab+b^2}{(a-b)^2},\qquad \det(ST-TS)=a^2-ab+b^2. \] The determinant is conjugacy invariant; a nonzero value certifies failure of simultaneous diagonalization. For the Rogers--Ramanujan exponents $-1/60$ and $11/60$, so $a=e^{-2\pi i/60}$ and $b=e^{22\pi i/60}$, it reports \[ S_{12}S_{21}=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{10}>0. \] In a symmetric normalization, put $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. One compatible golden-ratio matrix and its Lee--Yang fusion diagonalization are \[ S=\frac1{\sqrt{\varphi+2}}\begin{pmatrix}-\varphi&1\\1&\varphi\end{pmatrix},\qquad S^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}S =\operatorname{diag}(-\varphi^{-1},\varphi). \] Basis order and normalization affect individual entries, whereas the determinant certificate does not. These specific results do not classify transformation matrices in arbitrary dimensions or weights.

Short English statement

The Rogers--Ramanujan example has an intrinsic mixing certificate determined by its two $T$-eigenvalues. A symmetric $S$ normalization also diagonalizes a fusion matrix.
