Question 3.11 — (Isomorphism problem for subgroups).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix the source's genus $g$, and let $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ be the corresponding mapping class group. The question is whether the isomorphism problem for its finitely presented subgroups is solvable: given effective descriptions of two such subgroups $H,K\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$, decide whether $H\cong K$ as abstract groups. The decision is not whether they are conjugate inside $\operatorname{Mod}_g$, and no embedding-preserving isomorphism is required.

One precise presentation-input version uses \[\mathcal P_g=\{P:\ P\text{ is a finite group presentation and }G(P)\text{ embeds in }\operatorname{Mod}_g\}.\] It asks for a single terminating algorithm on inputs $P,Q\in\mathcal P_g$ returning yes exactly when $G(P)\cong G(Q)$, under the promise that both inputs lie in this class. Another possible version gives finite tuples of mapping-class words generating $H,K$, with a promise that the generated subgroups are finitely presented, or also supplies presentations and embedding maps. The source does not specify which input model is intended; it cannot be assumed that these data can be converted effectively with no additional work. Behavior on inputs outside the promised class need not be prescribed unless a total-recognition problem is intended.

The source contrasts this with reported unsolvability for all finitely generated linear groups, and even for finitely generated subgroups of $\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})$. Those are stated for finitely generated classes, not the narrower finitely presented class in the main question; the exact $n$, encodings and supporting theorems are not provided. No transfer of those negative results to the requested mapping-class subgroup problem is asserted. The ending introduces another algorithmic question but does not supply it.

Short English statement

Is there an algorithm deciding abstract isomorphism for finitely presented subgroups of a mapping class group? The subgroup encoding and fixed-versus-variable genus must be specified; the source's negative linear-group comparisons concern broader finitely generated classes.
