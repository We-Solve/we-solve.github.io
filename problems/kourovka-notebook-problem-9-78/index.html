Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.78

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is periodic if every element has finite order. It is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$, there are a finite group $Q$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to Q$ with $\varphi(g)\ne 1$. For a prime $p$, a $p$-subgroup is a subgroup all of whose elements have order a power of $p$; a Sylow $p$-subgroup means a subgroup maximal under inclusion among the $p$-subgroups of $G$.

The supplied question asks whether there exists a group $G$ satisfying all of the following conditions:

  1. $G$ is periodic and residually finite.
  2. $G$ is an $F^{*}$-group, in the sense referenced by the source as Archive, Problem 7.42.
  3. For every prime $p$, every Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$ is finite.
  4. $G$ is not binary finite.
The supplied record does not include the referenced definition of an $F^{*}$-group or a definition of binary finite. These two conditions retain the source terminology and remain unresolved predicates in this draft; a complete formal statement requires their intended definitions. No uniform bound on the orders of the finite Sylow subgroups is stated.

Short English statement

Does there exist a periodic, residually finite $F^{*}$-group with every Sylow subgroup finite, but which is not binary finite? The meanings of $F^{*}$-group and binary finite require source clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is not independently verified here.
