Math-1690

Affine zero obstructions for powers that are squared norms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq1$ and let $R(z,\bar z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[z_1,\ldots,z_n,\bar z_1,\ldots,\bar z_n]$ be real-valued for all $z\in{\mathbb{C}}^n$, equivalently Hermitian under conjugating coefficients and interchanging $z,\bar z$. D'Angelo's problem asks for necessary and sufficient conditions for \[ \exists N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \exists k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \exists p_1,\ldots,p_k\in{\mathbb{C}}[z_1,\ldots,z_n],\qquad R(z,\bar z)^N=\sum_{j=1}^k|p_j(z)|^2 \] as a polynomial identity. The exponent is positive; allowing $N=0$ would trivialize the question. The zero polynomial is permitted in this general question. Negative real constants and negative multiples of squared norms can qualify using even powers, so the problem must not silently impose $R\geq0$ or an odd exponent.

The supplied partial results are as follows. If $R$ has holomorphic and antiholomorphic degrees at most $d$, set \[ \widetilde R(z_0,z;\bar z_0,\bar z) =z_0^d\bar z_0^dR(z/z_0,\bar z/\bar z_0), \] interpreted as its polynomial expansion, bihomogeneous of bidegree $(d,d)$. For each fixed $N\geq1$, $R^N$ is a squared norm of holomorphic polynomials if and only if $\widetilde R^N$ is such a squared norm; the homogeneous summands on the latter side have degree $dN$. The leading bidegree corner $R_{d,d}$ also has a squared-norm $N$th power whenever $R$ does, with the zero corner allowed in this statement.

Polarize $R$ to the polynomial $\mathcal R(z,\zeta)$ in independent complex variables. If $R(a,\bar a)=0$ and $R$ has a squared-norm power, the aid asserts \[ \mathcal R\in\mathfrak m_a(z)\,\mathfrak m_{\bar a}(\zeta), \] where $\mathfrak m_a(z)=(z_1-a_1,\ldots,z_n-a_n)$ and the second ideal is defined analogously. In particular $\mathcal R(a,\zeta)\equiv0$ and $\mathcal R(z,\bar a)\equiv0$.

For the one-variable consequence assume explicitly $R\not\equiv0$ and bidegree exactly $(d,d)$. Each distinct diagonal zero $a\in{\mathbb{C}}$, meaning $R(a,\bar a)=0$, forces the factor $(z-a)(\zeta-\bar a)$ in the polarization. Consequently there are at most $d$ distinct diagonal zeros. Within this class, equality holds precisely when \[ R(z,\bar z)=c\prod_{\nu=1}^d|z-a_\nu|^2, \qquad c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\},\quad a_1,\ldots,a_d\text{ pairwise distinct}. \] For $d=0$ the product is empty and the statement concerns a nonzero real constant. Distinctness is essential to the equality count: a general degree-$d$ polynomial with repeated roots does not give $d$ distinct zeros. The supplied zero-count criterion and homogenization reductions do not give necessary and sufficient conditions for all Hermitian polynomials, and they do not provide an exponent bound.

Short English statement

The question asks which real-valued complex polynomials have some positive power equal to a finite sum of squared moduli of holomorphic polynomials. The supplied progress preserves this property under bihomogenization and forces strong factors at diagonal zeros. In one variable a nonzero bidegree-$(d,d)$ example has at most $d$ distinct zeros, with equality only in the stated distinct-root squared-modulus form. A full classification is not supplied.
