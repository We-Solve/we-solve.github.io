Stable Commutator Length, Growth, and Subgroup Automorphisms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record combines three families of questions. Several hypotheses and the conjectured asymptotic formula are missing, so the following definitions identify the mathematical objects without claiming a uniquely determined formulation.

For a group $G$, let $[G,G]$ be its commutator subgroup. For $g\in[G,G]$, define its commutator length and stable commutator length by \[ \operatorname{cl}_G(g)=\min\left\{k\geq 0:g=\prod_{i=1}^{k}[a_i,b_i],\ a_i,b_i\in G\right\},\qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}, \] \[ \operatorname{scl}_G(g)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cl}_G(g^m)}{m}. \] The first question asks for the leading constant and error term in a conjectured asymptotic law for stable commutator length of random elements or random-walk positions. The record supplies neither that law nor the group, probability model, length parameter, or mode of convergence. It also does not specify how the random elements are required to lie in the domain of stable commutator length. Consequently, no definite asymptotic equation, constant, or error estimate can be stated from this record alone.

Under the interpretation that "growth" means ordinary word growth, let $G$ be a finitely generated group and $S=S^{-1}$ a finite generating set. Write \[ \gamma_{G,S}(n)=\#\{g\in G:|g|_S\leq n\},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}. \] Say that $G$ has intermediate word growth if \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{G,S}(n)^{1/n}=1 \quad\text{and}\quad \nexists C>0\ \exists d\geq 0\ \forall n\geq 1:\ \gamma_{G,S}(n)\leq Cn^d. \] This property is independent of the finite generating set. With this interpretation, the growth clause asks separately whether there exists a finitely generated group of intermediate word growth and whether there exists a finitely presented group of intermediate word growth. These are distinct existence questions. The record does not specify an additional class of groups, a different growth invariant, or how these questions relate to its stable commutator length clause; its intended target therefore requires source clarification.

For the automorphism clause, let $F$ be a free group and $H\leq F$ a finitely generated subgroup. Define \[ \operatorname{Out}(F)=\operatorname{Aut}(F)/\operatorname{Inn}(F), \qquad [H]_F=\{xHx^{-1}:x\in F\}. \] A natural interpretation of the group in the record is the stabilizer of the conjugacy class of $H$: \[ \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Out}(F)}([H]_F) =\{[\phi]\in\operatorname{Out}(F):\exists x\in F,\ \phi(H)=xHx^{-1}\}. \] The displayed condition is independent of the representative $\phi$. Under this interpretation, the question is whether this stabilizer is finitely generated and whether it is finitely presented, for the intended choices of $F$ and $H$. The record does not specify the rank of $F$, the full quantifiers, or whether preservation means this conjugacy-class stabilizer, a pointwise condition, or an induced automorphism group. Thus the displayed stabilizer is an explicit candidate interpretation, not a verified identification of the intended group.

The supplied label $\text{partially solved}$ applies to the compound record. It does not identify which individual assertions have been established.

Short English statement

The record asks about constants and error terms for random stable commutator length, intermediate group growth, and finiteness properties of outer automorphisms preserving a finitely generated free-group subgroup. Essential probability data, the asymptotic law, and some group-theoretic hypotheses are absent. The definitions above make possible interpretations explicit; the intended formulations and the supplied partial-solution assessment require human mathematical review.
