The talk proposes a $T=20\,\mathrm{ms}$ clock and a signal code whose firing probability and normalized phase offset are both $\sqrt P$. For a probabilistic interpretation, require $0\le P\le1$ and set $u=\sqrt P$. The supplied aid analyzes the explicit local, unwrapped model \[ B_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(u),\qquad Z_i=B_i(u+\delta+\xi_i),\qquad \widehat P_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i, \] where the $B_i$ are independent across cycles, $\xi_i$ are independent centered phase errors with variance $\sigma^2$, independent of the firing indicators, and $\delta$ is a fixed common normalized reference offset. These independence and centering conditions, and unwrapping of phase, are model assumptions added by the aid rather than specified in the talk. The reported formulas are \[ {\mathbb{E}}\widehat P_n=P+u\delta,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat P_n) =\frac{u(1-u)(u+\delta)^2+u\sigma^2}{n}, \] \[ {\mathbb{E}}(\widehat P_n-P)^2 =P\delta^2+\frac{u(1-u)(u+\delta)^2+u\sigma^2}{n}. \] An unknown or unremoved fixed reference error therefore leaves the reported asymptotic floor $P\delta^2$; a known offset can be subtracted from each firing's phase, while pilot estimation requires its own error analysis. At $P=0$ no firing phase is observed. A circular phase at an endpoint of the normalized interval must not silently be treated as an unwrapped linear value.

The aid also reports a clock-reference identifiability obstruction. Phase-only observations of $\phi+\delta$ modulo a cycle cannot distinguish an unknown transmitted phase from an unknown reference shift without additional reference information. This phase-only obstruction does not make the whole scalar $P$ unidentifiable under the stipulated square-root code: repeated firing counts still estimate $u$. The exact scope of the aid's phrase "phase coordinate nonidentifiable" needs this distinction.

For timing multiplexing, let $K\ge2$ distinct centers lie on the circle of circumference $T$, use circular distance, and let $d_{\min}$ be their minimum pairwise distance. If each transmitted timing center may be displaced by at most $J>0$, the aid reports uniform unique nearest-center decoding exactly when \[ d_{\min}>2J, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad K<\frac{T}{2J}. \] Equality allows a midpoint tie. For $J=0$, distinct centers are sufficient and this jitter model gives no finite quotient bound on $K$. This bounded-displacement result is separate from the variance-only jitter model above.

The proposed goals are fast reliable inter-cortical communication, multiplexing, and reproducing cell responses; image reconstruction is the stated task. The claimed reproduction of peristimulus time histograms requires stimulus synchronization to the internal clock, justified in the discussion by an assumed synchronized retinal input. Receptive fields depend on chosen synaptic weights. Learning and prediction costs are not separated, and the feedback rule is said to be motivated by EM, not established as an EM algorithm. Feedback is described as "shutup"; Steve raises excitatory-feedback experiments and disputes an explanation of end stopping by V2 feedback, while Alessandra mentions excitatory center and inhibitory surround feedback. These are attributed discussion statements, not reconciled experimental conclusions. Phase is used for both coding and multiplexing; clock width is related informally to thread count.
