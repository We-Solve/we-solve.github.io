Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Determine whether the constant-free Valiant classes satisfy $VP_0\neq VNP_0$.

Attempt and results

We use integer constant-free circuits with inputs variables and $0,1,-1$, and binary addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Formal degree is one at every input, maximum at addition/subtraction, and sum at multiplication. These conventions are explicit because assigning degree zero to constants would change the following bound. We attempt to separate the source's classes by coefficient growth, then examine why this and monomial counting do not work.
A complete coefficient-height estimate.
For an integer polynomial $P$, let $\|P\|_1$ be the sum of absolute values of its coefficients and put $L(P)=\log_2\max(1,\|P\|_1)$. Coefficientwise triangle inequality and convolution give $\|P\pm Q\|_1\le\|P\|_1+\|Q\|_1$ and $\|PQ\|_1\le\|P\|_1\|Q\|_1$. Order the internal gates topologically as $1,\ldots,s$. At a gate $i$ of formal degree $d_i$, induction proves $L(P_i)\le i d_i$. Inputs have $L=0$. At an addition or subtraction gate, $L(P_i)\le1+\max L(P_j)\le1+(i-1)d_i\le i d_i$, since $d_i\ge1$. At a multiplication gate, the two earlier formal degrees add to $d_i$, so $L(P_i)\le(i-1)(d_j+d_k)=(i-1)d_i$. Thus a circuit of size $s$ and output formal degree $d$ has every coefficient of absolute value at most $2^{sd}$. It follows that polynomial size and polynomial formal degree imply polynomial coefficient bit length.
Why the proposed huge-constant witness leaves both classes.
The integer $2^{2^n}$ is produced from $1+1$ by $n$ repeated squarings in linear size, but its formal degree is $2^n$ under the stated convention. Its bit length is $2^n+1$, so the proved bound rules out computing it with both polynomial size and polynomial formal degree. This does not separate $VP_0$ from $VNP_0$. Indeed suppose \[ f_n(x)=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{p(n)}}g_n(x,y), \] where $g_n$ has constant-free circuit size $S(n)$ and formal degree $D(n)$, all polynomially bounded, and $p(n)$ is polynomial. Substituting zeros or ones cannot increase coefficient $\ell_1$ norm: each resulting coefficient is a sum of some original coefficients, and the triangle inequality bounds the sum of their absolute values by the original total. Hence \[ \|f_n\|_1\le2^{p(n)}\max_y\|g_n(x,y)\|_1 \le2^{p(n)+S(n)D(n)}. \] Every such $VNP_0$ family also has polynomial coefficient bit length. Therefore the enormous integer family fails the defining upper bound for $VNP_0$ as well; placing it outside $VP_0$ cannot witness their separation. Cancellation only reduces these norm bounds and does not alter the conclusion.
Monomial count gives a second false lower-bound route.
The polynomial $\prod_{i=1}^n(1+x_i)$ has a distinct monomial $\prod_{i\in A}x_i$ for every subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, all with coefficient one, by distributivity and uniqueness of the exponent support. It therefore has $2^n$ nonzero monomials. Nevertheless it has a constant-free circuit with $n$ additions and $n-1$ multiplications and formal degree $n$. Thus an argument inferring circuit size from the expanded number of monomials would falsely reject this explicit $VP_0$ family. The remaining route would need a measure respecting sharing and cancellation while distinguishing some specifically defined Boolean-sum family; neither height nor support cardinality supplies it.
