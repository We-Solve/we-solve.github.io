Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.57

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. A pro-$p$ group is a topological group that is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. For a pro-$p$ group $G$, define its topological lower central series by \[ \gamma_1(G)=G,\qquad \gamma_{i+1}(G)=\overline{\langle [x,y]:x\in\gamma_i(G),\ y\in G\rangle}\quad(i\geq 1), \] where $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ and the bar denotes closure in $G$. Say that $G$ has finite width if there exists an integer $d\geq 0$ such that \[ \lvert\gamma_i(G)/\gamma_{i+1}(G)\rvert\leq p^d \qquad\text{for every integer }i\geq 1. \] The source calls this having finite width $p^d$; its inequality does not require $p^d$ to be the least possible bound.

Under the conventional definition, $G$ is hereditarily just infinite if $G$ is infinite and \[ \text{for every open subgroup }H\leq G\text{ and every closed normal subgroup }N\trianglelefteq H, \qquad N\neq\{1\}\Longrightarrow [H:N]<\infty. \] Classification problem, conditional on this interpretation. Describe the pro-$p$ groups $G$ that are hereditarily just infinite and have finite width, with $p$ ranging over primes.

Unresolved source qualification. The supplied definition says that each open subgroup has no closed normal subgroup of infinite index, without excluding the trivial subgroup. The trivial subgroup is closed and normal and has infinite index in every infinite open subgroup of an infinite pro-$p$ group. Consequently, the supplied wording is incompatible with the conventional definition unless the nontriviality qualification is inserted. The displayed classification problem uses that conventional interpretation explicitly and requires source confirmation.

Short English statement

Describe the infinite pro-$p$ groups whose lower central factors have uniformly bounded orders and whose open subgroups have only finite-index nontrivial closed normal subgroups. The source omits the word "nontrivial" in the latter condition; this interpretation therefore requires confirmation. The supplied registry labels the problem open.
