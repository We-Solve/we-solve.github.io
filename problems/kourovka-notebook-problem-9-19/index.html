Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.19

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a nontrivial finite group $X$, define its minimum proper-subgroup index by \[ n(X)=\min\{[X:H]:H<X\}, \] where $H<X$ means that $H$ is a proper subgroup of $X$. This minimum exists because $X$ is finite and nontrivial. The source does not specify a value for $n(\{1\})$; the definitions below consequently use $n$ only for nontrivial groups.

For a nontrivial finite group $G$, let \[ \mathcal S(G)=\{X<G:X\ne\{1\},\ n(X)=n(G)\}. \] A subgroup $A$ is wide in $G$ if it is maximal under inclusion in $\mathcal S(G)$. Explicitly, this means \[ A\in\mathcal S(G) \quad\text{and}\quad \forall X\in\mathcal S(G),\quad A\le X\Longrightarrow A=X. \] Maximality here is within $\mathcal S(G)$; the definition does not require $A$ to be a maximal proper subgroup of $G$.

Part (a), retained in the main section according to the supplied record. Determine all wide subgroups in finite projective special linear, symplectic, orthogonal, and unitary groups. These are the family names given in the source. The supplied record does not explicitly specify ranks, field sizes, simplicity restrictions, exceptional small parameters, or the precise projective and special forms intended for every family. Those scope questions remain unresolved; no additional exclusions or choices of forms are imposed here.

Part (b), archived as solved according to the supplied record. Prove, without using the classification of finite simple groups, that \[ n(F_1)=[F_1:2F_2], \] where the source calls $F_1$ and $F_2$ the Fischer simple groups and describes $2F_2$ as "an extension of a group of order $2$ by $F_2$." The index requires $2F_2$ to designate a subgroup of $F_1$. The supplied record does not explicitly identify the Fischer groups in modern notation, specify that subgroup embedding, or disambiguate the extension description by an exact sequence. The displayed statement therefore retains the source notation and these unresolved qualifications. The supplied archive reports that this part was proved; this draft does not independently verify that report.

Short English statement

A wide subgroup is maximal among proper subgroups having the same minimum proper-subgroup index as the ambient group. Part (a) asks for their classification in the listed finite classical groups. Part (b) asks for a proof, without the classification of finite simple groups, of the stated minimum-index formula for Fischer groups. The supplied registry labels the combined problem partially solved and archives part (b) as solved. Family conventions and the notation in part (b) require review.
