Erdős Problem #909: dimension preserved by squaring

Statement and scope

For every integer $n\geq2$, does there exist a space $S$ with $\dim S=\dim(S\times S)=n$? We use covering dimension for separable metric spaces. Anderson and Keisler gave the known affirmative answer. This attempt reconstructs the one-dimensional model carefully, explains why taking its finite powers cannot solve higher dimensions, and leaves the higher-dimensional construction as a specific gap.

An explicit self-product homeomorphism

Let \[ E=\{(x_i)_{i\geq1}\in\ell^2:x_i\in\mathbb Q\text{ for every }i\}, \qquad P=\{(1/m_i)_{i\geq1}\in\ell^2:m_i\in\mathbb N\}. \] The topology is the norm topology, not the topology of coordinatewise convergence. Interleaving coordinates gives an isometry \[ \Phi:E\times E\longrightarrow E,\quad \Phi(x,y)=(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2,\ldots), \] when the product has distance $\sqrt{\|x-x'\|^2+\|y-y'\|^2}$. Its inverse separates odd and even coordinates; square summability is preserved in both directions. Thus $E^r\cong E$ for every positive finite $r$.

Although $E$ is totally disconnected, this does not imply it has dimension zero. Indeed, distinct $x,y\in E$ differ in some coordinate $j$; an irrational number strictly between $x_j$ and $y_j$ gives a clopen coordinate cut separating them. The positive dimension requires a different argument.

The positive-dimension argument

We show that every open subset $U$ of $\ell^2$ of diameter less than $1/2$ which meets $P$ has boundary meeting $P$. Start with $v=(1/n_i)\in U\cap P$. Fix an initial segment so that $n_i\geq3$ for all remaining indices. Process these remaining coordinates successively. Having chosen $m_i$ in earlier coordinates, choose the smallest positive integer $m_j$ for which \[ v_j=(1/m_1,\ldots,1/m_j,1/n_{j+1},\ldots)\in U, \] with the fixed initial coordinates understood. Such a choice exists, since the previous point corresponds to $m_j=n_j$. The value $m_j=1$ is impossible: its $j$-th coordinate differs from the original point $v\in U$ by at least $2/3$. Therefore $m_j\geq2$, and the point $u_j$ obtained from $v_j$ by replacing $1/m_j$ with $1/(m_j-1)$ lies outside $U$.

Since $U$ is bounded, the partial sums $\sum_{i\leq j}m_i^{-2}$ are bounded. Consequently the coordinatewise limit \[ w=(1/m_i)_{i\geq1} \] belongs to $P$, and $v_j\to w$ in norm: both the tail of $w$ and the unchanged tail of $v$ have norm tending to zero. Also $m_j\to\infty$, whence \[ \|u_j-v_j\|=\frac1{m_j(m_j-1)}\longrightarrow0. \] Thus $w\in\overline U\cap(\ell^2\setminus U)=\partial U$.

If $P$ had dimension zero, a point would have arbitrarily small relatively clopen neighbourhoods. A relatively clopen neighbourhood $W\subset P$ can be represented by an ambient open set $U$ whose boundary avoids $P$: for example use \[ U_0=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,W)<\operatorname{dist}(z,P\setminus W)\}, \] choose $W\subset B(p,1/16)$, and set $U=U_0\cap B(p,1/8)$. The boundary of $U_0$ misses $P$, while the new sphere misses $W=U_0\cap P$. Thus $\partial U\cap P=\varnothing$ and $\operatorname{diam}U<1/2$, contradicting the preceding argument. Therefore $\dim P\geq1$, and by monotonicity $\dim E\geq1$.

The upper dimension bound

Let $S=\{x\in E:\|x\|=1\}$ and fix $p=(r_i)\in S$. Given a positive irrational $\delta$, choose $N$ with $\sum_{i>N}r_i^2<\delta$, and put \[ V=\left\{x\in S:\sum_{i=1}^N r_ix_i>1-\delta\right\}. \] This is clopen in $S$: the finite sum is rational on $E$, so it never equals $1-\delta$. Also $p\in V$. For $x\in V$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives \[ \|p-x\|^2 =2-2\langle p,x\rangle <2\delta+2\sqrt\delta. \] These sets form a clopen neighbourhood base on $S$, proving $\dim S=0$.

Balls in $E$ with centres in $E$ and positive rational radii form a base. Each boundary is a rational translate and dilation of $S$, hence has dimension zero. The standard equivalence of covering and small inductive dimension for separable metric spaces now gives $\dim E\leq1$. Together with the preceding lower bound, \[ \dim E=\dim(E\times E)=1. \]

What this proves and what is still missing

Interleaving proves that replacing $E$ by any finite power $E^r$ still gives dimension $1$; it cannot provide the requested dimension $n\geq2$. More generally, every nonempty metric $X$ satisfies $\dim X\leq\dim(X\times X)$, since $X\times\{x_0\}$ is a closed copy of $X$. Thus the target is the smallest possible product dimension, and the missing step is an explicit higher-dimensional space together with both dimension bounds.

The one-dimensional proof follows Erdős, The dimension of the rational points in Hilbert space, Annals of Mathematics 41 $1940$, 734--736, with the initial-coordinate issue in the reciprocal construction made explicit. Standard dimension-theory facts used above are monotonicity and the equivalence with small inductive dimension in separable metric spaces. Sources: https://www.erdosproblems.com/909; https://combinatorica.hu/~p_erdos/1940-11.pdf. The higher-dimensional resolution is Anderson--Keisler $1967$, DOI $10.1090/S0002-9939-1967-0215288-0$; its construction is not reconstructed here. No novelty is claimed. Attempt status: unresolved for the stated range $n\geq2$.
