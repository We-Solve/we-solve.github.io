Bounds on Cohomological Exponents of Finite $p$-Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $P$ be a finite $p$-group. Write $Z(P)$ for its center and set \[ C(P)=\{z\in Z(P):z^p=1\},\qquad c(P)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}C(P). \] Here an elementary abelian $p$-group is an abelian group in which every nonidentity element has order $p$, viewed as a vector space over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$. Thus $C(P)$ is the largest central elementary abelian $p$-subgroup of $P$, and $c(P)$ is its rank. Define \[ n(P)=\min\{\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}V: V\text{ is a finite-dimensional complex vector space and there exists an injective homomorphism }P\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)\}. \] The supplied question asks whether, for every prime $p$ and every finite $p$-group $P$, \[ e(P)\leq 2n(P)-c(P). \] Unresolved qualification. The supplied record never defines $e(P)$. Its title refers to cohomological exponents, but does not specify a cohomology theory, coefficients, grading, or the construction of this invariant. Consequently the displayed inequality records the supplied question but is not yet a fully specified mathematical proposition. No definition of $e(P)$ is inferred here. The record states no restriction excluding the trivial group or any prime; the missing definition must also determine the meaning of $e(P)$ at these boundary cases.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether an undefined invariant $e(P)$ of every finite $p$-group is bounded above by twice its smallest faithful complex representation dimension minus the rank of its largest central elementary abelian subgroup. Defining $e(P)$ is necessary before the question can be stated precisely. The supplied open status is a curation label, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.
