Math-3393

Exact Transversals for Families with the (p,q)-Property

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p,q$ be positive integers with $q\le p$. Under the usual common-intersection interpretation, a family $\mathcal F$ of sets has the $(p,q)$-property if \[ \forall\,\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F,\quad |\mathcal G|=p\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists\,\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G:\quad |\mathcal H|=q\quad\text{and}\quad \bigcap_{A\in\mathcal H}A\ne\varnothing. \] For a family of subsets of an ambient set $X$, a point transversal is a set $T\subseteq X$ such that $T\cap A\ne\varnothing$ for every $A\in\mathcal F$. Interpret a $k$-transversal as such a set with $|T|\le k$. These interpretations of the terminology require confirmation against the source report, since the supplied record does not define them.

The geometric question is the following proposed implication. Given a family $\mathcal F$ of hyperplanes in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, and integers satisfying $d+1\le q\le p$, does \[ \mathcal F\text{ has the }(p,q)\text{-property} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\,T\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d:\quad |T|\le p-q+1\quad\text{and}\quad \forall H\in\mathcal F,\ T\cap H\ne\varnothing \] always hold? Here a hyperplane is provisionally interpreted as an affine hyperplane \[ H=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\langle a,x\rangle=b\}, \qquad a\in{\mathbb{R}}^d\setminus\{0\},\quad b\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] The supplied record does not explicitly distinguish affine from linear hyperplanes.

The set-theoretic question asks for the analogous implication. Given an ambient set $X$, a family $\mathcal A\subseteq\{A\subseteq X:|A|=r\}$, and integers satisfying $r+1\le q\le p$, does \[ \mathcal A\text{ has the }(p,q)\text{-property} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\,T\subseteq X:\quad |T|\le p-q+1\quad\text{and}\quad \forall A\in\mathcal A,\ T\cap A\ne\varnothing \] always hold?

Unresolved scope qualifications. The record does not specify whether either family must be finite, whether it must have at least $p$ members, or whether families are indexed and may contain repeated members. With the displayed definition, the $(p,q)$-property is vacuous when a family has fewer than $p$ members; any intended size hypothesis must therefore be recovered from the report. No such hypothesis has been silently added. The displays state the proposed bounds for a given family; the intended universal scope over families remains subject to these qualifications. The record also does not explicitly state the lower boundary conventions for $d$ and $r$, or whether a $(p-q+1)$-transversal means at most or exactly that many points. This is a statement-only draft, not an assertion that either implication is true.

Short English statement

The supplied problem asks whether a family in which every $p$ members contain $q$ members with a common point can always be pierced by at most $p-q+1$ points: for hyperplanes when $d+1\le q\le p$, and for $r$-element sets when $r+1\le q\le p$. The intended family-size assumptions and terminology need confirmation. The supplied open label is not independently verified.
