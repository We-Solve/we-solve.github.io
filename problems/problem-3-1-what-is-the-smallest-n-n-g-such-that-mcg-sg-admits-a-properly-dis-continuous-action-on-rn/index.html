Problem 3.1 — What is the smallest n = n(g) such that MCG (Sg) admits a properly dis- continuous action on Rn?

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S_g$ be a closed orientable surface of genus $g$ and let $\Gamma_g=\operatorname{MCG}(S_g)$, in the usual orientation-preserving convention. An action of a discrete group $\Gamma$ on a manifold $Y$ is proper when, for every compact $K\subset Y$, the set $\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma K\cap K\ne\varnothing\}$ is finite. This permits finite stabilizers, which matters because mapping class groups have torsion. No cocompactness is required in the following minima: \[ n_{{\mathbb{R}}}(g)=\min\{n:\Gamma_g\text{ acts properly discontinuously on }{\mathbb{R}}^n\}, \] \[ \operatorname{actdim}(\Gamma_g)=\min\{n:\Gamma_g\text{ acts properly discontinuously on a contractible }n\text{-manifold}\}. \] Thus $\operatorname{actdim}(\Gamma_g)\le n_{{\mathbb{R}}}(g)$. The manifold and action regularity conventions should be checked against the source; the excerpt does not specify them.

Problem 3.1. Determine both minima. The source expects them to agree and proposes \[ n_{{\mathbb{R}}}(g)=\operatorname{actdim}(\Gamma_g)=6g-6\qquad(g\ge2). \] It states $n(1)=2$. Its upper-bound motivation is the properly discontinuous action on Teichmüller space $\mathcal T_g\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{6g-6}$. The proposed equality is a question, not a theorem asserted here.

Suggested lower-bound method. The source invokes the obstructor-complex technique of [BKK02]. A finite simplicial complex $L$ is used as an $m$-obstructor when it carries the required homological obstruction to embedding in ${\mathbb{R}}^m$; the full technical definition is not reproduced in the excerpt. Write $S^k_+=S^k\sqcup\{*\}$ for a pointed sphere in the source's disjoint-point sense. Its examples have \[ L=S^{k_1}_+*\cdots*S^{k_p}_+,\qquad m=k_1+\cdots+k_p+2p-2. \] In particular the join of two pointed $0$-spheres is the utilities graph, a $2$-obstructor. Seek a proper expanding map \[ F:L\times[0,\infty)\longrightarrow X, \] where $X$ is a proper metric space with a proper cocompact isometric $\Gamma$-action. Expansion means that for every pair of disjoint simplices $\sigma,\tau\subset L$, \[ \lim_{t\to\infty}d_X\left(F(\sigma\times[t,\infty)),F(\tau\times[t,\infty))\right)=\infty. \] This is separation of whole image tails, not only of points at equal times. The cited method then gives $\operatorname{actdim}(\Gamma)\ge m+2$. For $\Gamma=\Gamma_g$, the proposed choice is the thick part \[ X=\mathcal T_g^{\ge\varepsilon}=\{Z\in\mathcal T_g:\text{every closed geodesic of }Z\text{ has length at least }\varepsilon\}, \] for a sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$.

The source also reports action-dimension examples: $n(B_k)=2k-3$ for the $k$-strand braid group with $k\ge2$; $n(\Gamma)=\dim(G/K)$ for a lattice in a connected semisimple Lie group $G$, with $K$ maximal compact, citing [BF02a]; and a garbled free-group expression rendered "$n(Fg\ 2)=2g$." The last is plausibly the direct-product example $n((F_2)^g)=2g$, but its exact notation must be checked. These are attributed contextual reports, not independently verified unrestricted theorems. The source says the pointed-sphere joins cover the obstructor applications in [BKK02] and [BF02b].

Short English statement

Find the least dimension of Euclidean space, and of any contractible manifold, admitting a proper mapping-class-group action. The expected answer is $6g-6$ for $g\ge2$; the source proposes a thick-Teichmüller-space obstructor construction for the lower bound.
