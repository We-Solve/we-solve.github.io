Math-1174

Tits’ Center Conjecture for Spherical Buildings

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Tits' Center Conjecture, as supplied. Let $X$ be a spherical building and $Y$ a nonempty convex contractible subcomplex. Must there exist a point $y\in|Y|$ such that\[g(y)=y\quad\text{for every }g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\text{ with }g(Y)=Y?\] Here $|Y|$ is the geometric realization, $g(Y)=Y$ means setwise stabilization, and the quantifier asks for one common fixed point of the entire stabilizer. Separate fixed points chosen for individual automorphisms would be a weaker conclusion. The fixed point is required to lie in $Y$, not merely somewhere in $X$.

In the usual simplicial convention, a spherical building is a building whose apartments are spherical Coxeter complexes, with its standard piecewise spherical metric. Convexity of $Y$ means that each geodesic segment of length less than $\pi$ between points of $|Y|$ lies in $|Y|$. Contractibility refers to the topology of that realization. Automorphisms are the intended simplicial building automorphisms; the source does not state a restriction to type-preserving ones, so that restriction is not inserted silently. It asks for a point, not necessarily a vertex. The excerpt supplies no thickness, local-finiteness, rank, or building-type restrictions, and any such hypotheses in the intended source version must be checked.

Short English statement

A convex contractible subcomplex should contain one point fixed by every building automorphism preserving it. The stabilizer acts setwise, and a common fixed point is required. The supplied open-status rationale describes an incomplete fragment rather than assessing the full conjecture now displayed.
