Math-5169

Exact counting with prescribed phylogenetic splits

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The concrete counting question in the discussion is: given $k$ candidate edges, interpreted as leaf splits $S$, determine for every $J\subseteq S$ the number of leaf-labeled binary trees containing all splits in $J$ and none in $S\setminus J$. The workshop does not fix rooting or planar conventions. The attached aid treats the following explicit version.

Let $X$ be a fixed label set of size $n\geq3$, and let $\mathcal T_X$ consist of finite unrooted, nonplanar trees whose leaves are bijectively labeled by $X$ and whose internal vertices have degree three, up to label-preserving graph isomorphism. An edge induces an unordered split $A\mid(X\setminus A)$; it is nontrivial when both sides have size at least two. Write $\Sigma(T)$ for the nontrivial splits of $T$. For a finite set $S$ of distinct nontrivial splits define \[ N_S(J)=\#\{T\in\mathcal T_X:\Sigma(T)\cap S=J\}\qquad(J\subseteq S). \] This all-pattern counting function is the definite target, not merely the count of trees containing at least $J$.

For $F\subseteq S$ write $R(F)=\#\{T\in\mathcal T_X:F\subseteq\Sigma(T)\}$. Two splits $A\mid A^c$ and $B\mid B^c$ are compatible if at least one of $A\cap B,A\cap B^c,A^c\cap B,A^c\cap B^c$ is empty. The aid records $R(F)=0$ for incompatible $F$; for a compatible family, let $T_F$ be the least-resolved $X$-tree with precisely the nontrivial splits $F$, with no degree-two internal vertices. Its recorded formulas are \[ R(F)=\prod_{v\in V_{\mathrm{int}}(T_F)}(2\deg(v)-5)!!,\qquad N_S(J)=\sum_{J\subseteq K\subseteq S}(-1)^{|K|-|J|}R(K), \] where $(2m-1)!!=1\cdot3\cdots(2m-1)$ for $m\geq1$ and $1!!=1$. In particular $F=\varnothing$ uses the unresolved star. The polynomial $\sum_{J\subseteq S}N_S(J)\prod_{s\in J}z_s$ records all exact patterns. Repeated copies of the same candidate split must have identical presence bits; inconsistent requests have count zero. Trivial leaf splits occur in every tree and therefore cannot be forbidden. These rules precede application of the distinct-nontrivial-split formula. Rooted clade constraints can instead be translated by adjoining a distinguished outgroup leaf and replacing each clade $A$ by $A\mid((X\setminus A)\cup\{o\})$; they are not counted under an unchanged unrooted convention.

Other questions in the same source remain separate: count trees at a prescribed distance from a specified family (nearest-neighbor interchange is suggested); find efficient tree codes; and find notation whose small changes correspond to nearby trees during a random walk. The discussion additionally asks about discrete or vector edge data, character states in the Klein four-group with edge differences, $k$-ary trees, and graph structures with cycles to represent uncertainty and distances faithfully. Neither a metric and neighborhood on codes nor a criterion of efficiency or biological fidelity is specified, so these broader requests are not resolved by the split-counting formulas.

Short English statement

Count every exact pattern of required and forbidden phylogenetic splits. The supplied aid gives compatible-split refinement counts and inclusion--exclusion under an explicit unrooted nonplanar binary convention. The workshop also asks about tree distances, efficient local encodings, and richer biological structures; those questions require further specifications.
