Primitive Generators of Preprojective Cohomological Hall Algebras

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $Q$ be the quiver in the cited source, let $Q_0$ denote its vertex set, and let $\Pi_Q$ denote its preprojective algebra. A dimension vector is a tuple $d=(d_i)_{i\in Q_0}\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}$, recording the dimensions at the vertices. The base field, hypotheses on $Q$, and convention for $\mathbb N$ are not specified in the supplied excerpt and must be recovered from the source.

Write $A_{\Pi_Q}$ for the preprojective cohomological Hall algebra mentioned in the record, and $\mathfrak g_{\Pi_Q,d}$ for the degree-$d$ generator object occurring in its quoted Theorem 3. Let $I_d$ denote the object written $\operatorname{IC}(X_d)$ in the clean statement. Here $\operatorname{IC}$ denotes an intersection complex; the space $X_d$, its coefficients, normalization, and the passage from this complex to a space of generators are not defined in the supplied record.

Conjectural completeness target. For a dimension vector $d$ such that a simple $\Pi_Q$-module of dimension vector $d$ exists, the supplied account of Theorem 3 describes a canonical inclusion of the intersection-complex contribution into the degree-$d$ primitive-generator object. Subject to the source's precise identification of these objects, write this inclusion as \[ \iota_d:I_d\hookrightarrow\mathfrak g_{\Pi_Q,d}. \] The intended completeness assertion is that this canonical inclusion exhausts the primitive degree-$d$ generators: \[ \operatorname{im}(\iota_d)=\mathfrak g_{\Pi_Q,d}, \qquad\text{equivalently, }\iota_d\text{ is an isomorphism}. \] This is a conditional reconstruction of the target, not a fully specified conjecture: the supplied text does not define the generator object or the meaning of primitive, and does not establish that $\mathfrak g_{\Pi_Q,d}$ is literally a primitive subspace of a specified coalgebra. The original excerpt also contains a corrupted canonical direct-sum decomposition with an intersection-complex term involving $\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{vir}}$; its exact coefficients, shifts, and complementary summand cannot be recovered. The simple-module existence hypothesis is explicitly attached to Theorem 3; whether the conjecture is intended to include other dimension vectors remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether the canonical contribution denoted $\operatorname{IC}(X_d)$ accounts for every primitive generator of degree $d$ in the preprojective cohomological Hall algebra. The supplied theorem discussion assumes that a simple module of dimension vector $d$ exists. Definitions of $X_d$, the generator object, and the relevant conventions are missing, so the displayed formulation requires source consultation and human mathematical review.
