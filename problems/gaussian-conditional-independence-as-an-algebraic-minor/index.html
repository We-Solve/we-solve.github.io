Literature and context check

AIM Question 50 mentions Gaussian assumptions as a possibility, without restricting all continuous models to that class. The calculation below reproves a Gaussian criterion and tests what fails without Gaussianity or nonsingularity. It contains no microarray-data analysis or novelty claim.

Research attempt: A Gaussian algebraic criterion and its two boundary failures

Let $(X_i,X_j,Z)$ be a centered jointly Gaussian vector with positive definite covariance, and write $W=(X_i,X_j)^T$, $C=\operatorname{Cov}(Z)$, $D=\operatorname{Cov}(W,Z)$ and $B=\operatorname{Cov}(W)$. Put $R=W-DC^{-1}Z$. Its covariance with $Z$ is zero and its covariance is $S=B-DC^{-1}D^T$. This matrix is positive definite: for nonzero $u$, the variance of $u^TW-u^TDC^{-1}Z$ is positive by positive definiteness of the original covariance.

The Gaussian vector $(R,Z)$ has block diagonal covariance. Its density factors because the inverse covariance is block diagonal and its determinant is $\det S\det C$; the usual Gaussian density formula therefore gives a product of its two marginal densities. Thus $R$ and $Z$ are independent. Conditional on $Z=z$, $W$ is a translate of the Gaussian $R$, so conditional independence of its two scalar coordinates is equivalent to independence of the coordinates of $R$. Those are independent when $S_{12}=0$, by the same density factorization; conversely independence makes their covariance zero. Consequently \[ X_i\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_j\mid Z \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Sigma_{ij}\det C-\Sigma_{iZ}\operatorname{adj}(C)\Sigma_{Zj}=0. \] Here $C^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(C)/\det C$ follows from the cofactor identity $C\operatorname{adj}(C)=(\det C)I$, and $\det C>0$. This proves the selected polynomial criterion, including its domain.

The first attempted extension is to general continuous variables. Take $X$ uniform on $[-1,1]$, $E$ an independent centered Gaussian with positive variance, and $Y=X^2+E$. Their joint distribution has a density, and \[ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=E X^3-E X\,E X^2=0. \] They are dependent: $E[Y\mid X]=X^2$ is nonconstant, whereas independence and integrability would force this conditional expectation to equal the constant $E Y$. Thus a vanishing covariance polynomial does not identify independence for arbitrary continuous distributions.

The second attempted extension clears denominators and includes singular boundary covariances without reanalysis. Take $Z=0$ deterministically and $X_i=X_j=G$ for a nondegenerate centered Gaussian $G$ of variance one. The displayed polynomial is zero: $\det C=0$ and both cross-covariances with $Z$ vanish. But $X_i$ and $X_j$ are not conditionally independent given the constant $Z$; their covariance is one. The polynomial equation alone has acquired spurious boundary cases.

Verification and adversarial audit

Gaussian sufficiency uses an explicit density factorization, and necessity uses covariance of independent integrable variables. The non-Gaussian example has a joint density despite its nonlinear conditional mean. The singular example is deliberately outside the positive-definite hypothesis; it refutes only the proposed hypothesis-free extension.
