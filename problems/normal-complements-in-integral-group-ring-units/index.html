Math-946

Normal Complements in Integral Group-Ring Units

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite nilpotent group. Here nilpotent means that there is a finite central series $1=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_c=G$, with each $G_i$ normal in $G$ and $G_{i+1}/G_i\subseteq Z(G/G_i)$. The integral group ring ${\mathbb{Z}}[G]$ consists of sums $\sum_{g\in G}a_g g$, with $a_g\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, with multiplication induced by the group law. Define its augmentation and normalized unit group by \[ \varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}[G]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad \varepsilon\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)=\sum_{g\in G}a_g, \qquad U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])=\{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]^\times:\varepsilon(u)=1\}. \] Identify $G$ with its canonical subgroup of $U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])$.

A normal complement to $G$ in $U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])$ is a subgroup $N\trianglelefteq U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])$ such that \[ N\cap G=\{1\},\qquad NG=U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G]). \] Equivalently, $U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])=N\rtimes G$ as an internal semidirect product. Such a complement is torsion-free if, for every $n\in N$ and every integer $m\geq1$, the equality $n^m=1$ implies $n=1$.

  1. For every finite nilpotent group $G$, does $G$ have a normal complement in $U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])$?
  2. For every finite nilpotent group $G$ for which a normal complement exists, does there exist a torsion-free normal complement in $U_1({\mathbb{Z}}[G])$? The torsion-free complement need not be the initially given complement.

Short English statement

For each finite nilpotent group $G$, ask whether its canonical copy inside the augmentation-one units of ${\mathbb{Z}}[G]$ has a normal complement. Whenever such a complement exists, ask whether one can choose a torsion-free complement. The source's parenthetical use of "nilpotent" leaves the scope of its original wording uncertain.
