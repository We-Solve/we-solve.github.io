5.11 (Futer) — A combinatorial model with explicit bilipschitz constants

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A map $\phi:(X,d_X)\to(M,d_M)$ is $L$-bilipschitz if it is a bijection, $1\leq L<\infty$, and \[ L^{-1}d_X(x,y)\leq d_M(\phi(x),\phi(y))\leq Ld_X(x,y) \quad\text{for all }x,y\in X. \] Here $d_M$ is the hyperbolic metric on a $3$-manifold $M$, and $X$ is a metric realization of the proposed combinatorial model.

Construction problem (Futer). Build a combinatorial model for hyperbolic $3$-manifolds together with explicit bilipschitz constants. A precise construction should state its combinatorial data and metric realization $X(M)$, give a bilipschitz comparison $\phi_M:X(M)\to M$, and supply an explicit finite bound $L(M)$ in terms of specified available data. The source does not identify the input, permitted geometric data, model type, or dependence of the constant. In particular, "explicit" is not the same as "universal": the statement does not demand one constant independent of $M$, whereas the supplied research summary describes a search for fully explicit universal constants. That strengthening is not silently imposed.

The excerpt also does not fix completeness, volume, compactness, cusps, boundary, or whether finite combinatorial data suffice. Related triangulation, polyhedral, drilling, cone-manifold, and bounded-geometry constructions mentioned in the summary are context; their additional hypotheses are not adopted as restrictions on the original request.

Short English statement

Construct a combinatorial metric model of hyperbolic $3$-manifolds with a proved, explicitly specified bilipschitz bound. The source does not require the constant to be universal or specify the class of model and allowable input data.
