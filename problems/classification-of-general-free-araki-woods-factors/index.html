Math-970

Classification of General Free Araki–Woods Factors

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $M(\mu,m)=\Gamma(\mu,m)''$ for the free Araki--Woods factors referred to in the supplied record. In the usual spectral description, $\mu$ describes the spectral measure class of the underlying orthogonal representation of ${\mathbb{R}}$, and $m$ describes its spectral multiplicity. The record does not specify the admissible measures, multiplicity functions, dimension restrictions, or separability assumptions; these parameter conventions require confirmation before a fully specified parameterwise classification can be stated.

For terminology, let $U:{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal O(H_{{\mathbb{R}}})$ be a strongly continuous orthogonal representation on a real Hilbert space, and let $U^{{\mathbb{C}}}$ be its complexification on $H_{{\mathbb{C}}}$. The representation is almost periodic if \[ H_{{\mathbb{C}}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{\xi\in H_{{\mathbb{C}}}:\text{ there exists }\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\text{ such that }U_t^{{\mathbb{C}}}\xi=e^{it\lambda}\xi\text{ for every }t\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}. \] Here almost periodicity refers to the generating representation. Its failure allows both continuous spectral data and mixtures of atomic and continuous spectral data.

Classification problem. Classify the free Araki--Woods factors $M(\mu,m)$ beyond the almost-periodic case. Under the conventional interpretation of classification as classification of von Neumann algebras up to normal unital $*$-isomorphism, seek an explicitly described invariant $I$ such that, for every two admissible parameter pairs $(\mu,m)$ and $(\nu,n)$ in the intended classification domain, \[ M(\mu,m)\cong M(\nu,n) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I(\mu,m)=I(\nu,n). \] The intended domain must include generating representations that are not almost periodic. The record does not specify whether the requested formulation must also describe every comparison between almost-periodic and non-almost-periodic generating data. It also does not explicitly specify the isomorphism convention; the convention above does not require preservation of a distinguished state and requires source confirmation.

No candidate complete invariant or conjectural equivalence criterion is supplied. The original formulation explicitly emphasizes the absence of even a conjectural classification statement. Thus the displayed equivalence specifies the requested form of a classification, rather than asserting a proposed theorem.

Short English statement

Find a complete classification of free Araki--Woods factors extending beyond those generated by almost-periodic representations. The goal is an explicit invariant that agrees exactly when the factors are isomorphic. The supplied record gives neither a candidate invariant nor a complete specification of the parameter domain and equivalence convention. Its partially solved status and claimed absence of a general classification are supplied dated assessments requiring independent review.
