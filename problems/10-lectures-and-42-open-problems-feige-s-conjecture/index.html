10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Feige's conjecture

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge1$ be an integer, and let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent random variables on a probability space with \[ X_i\ge0\text{ almost surely},\qquad \mathbb E X_i=1\quad(1\le i\le n). \] No identical-distribution assumption is imposed. Feige's conjecture as recorded here asserts \[ \Pr\left\{\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\ge n+1\right\}\le1-e^{-1} \] for every such $n$ and collection. The threshold includes equality.

The supplied research summary reports a July 2026 resolution with the sharper, finite-$n$ bound \[ \Pr\left\{\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\ge n+1\right\} \le1-\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^n\le1-e^{-1}, \] which it calls sharp. It describes Fu--Han--Wang--Yan--Zhang--Zhou as covering a general-shift regime $\delta\ge1$, and Nie--Wei as covering $\delta\le1$, with both relevant at the stated unit shift. No further general-shift formula is supplied or asserted here.

This is explicitly a supplied preprint assessment: the summary calls the papers about one week old at the time of its assessment, not peer-reviewed, and AI-assisted with disclosure. It reports two independent proofs and a Lean formalization as grounds for its high confidence. None of those proof, independence, formalization, or review claims has been independently checked in this draft.

Short English statement

Independent nonnegative mean-one random variables should exceed their total mean by at least one with probability at most $1-e^{-1}$. The supplied summary reports a sharper finite-dimensional resolution in recent, unreviewed July 2026 preprints.
