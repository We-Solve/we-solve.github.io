Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A sampling number is the smallest worst-case reconstruction error achievable from $m$ point evaluations, with the admissible linear or nonlinear reconstruction convention fixed. A Barron space controls a neural-integral representation norm; Fourier-analytic spaces instead constrain weighted Fourier content.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Determine the asymptotic nonlinear sampling numbers for variational Barron spaces. For the Fourier-analytic spaces $U_d^\alpha$, determine the pre-asymptotic dependence of $\sigma_m(U_d^\alpha)_{L^p}$ on $d$ and $m$, and determine the optimal reconstruction error when the sample points are uniform random points. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The norms defining the variational Barron space and $U_d^\alpha$, the sampling domain, and the error/probability convention are source-specific; pre-asymptotic dimension dependence is part of the target.

A dimension-explicit nullspace obstruction for the actual Fourier class

The primary report defines its Barron unit ball using an integrable Fourier density, not an arbitrary discrete Fourier measure. Bare sine waves therefore cannot simply be inserted as admissible functions. We repair that attempted construction by a Gaussian envelope. Work on $\Omega_d=[-1/2,1/2]^d$, $d\ge1$, with smoothness $\alpha=1$ and Euclidean frequency norm. The unit ball consists of real restrictions admitting a density $F$ with $\int(1+|\xi|)|F(\xi)|\,d\xi\le1$.
Proposition: Every deterministic algorithm using $m<d$ point values, even adaptively, has worst-case $L^2(\Omega_d)$ error at least \[ \frac{e^{-\pi/4}}{3\sqrt{2d}} \] on this unit ball.
Proof: Put $\phi_d(x)=e^{-\pi|x|^2/d}$. Its Fourier density under the $2\pi$ convention is $G_d(\xi)=d^{d/2}e^{-\pi d|\xi|^2}$, with integral one and second moment $\int|\xi|^2G_d=1/(2\pi)$. The one-dimensional transform identity follows by differentiating the Fourier integral, integrating by parts to obtain its first-order Gaussian differential equation, and using the Gaussian integral at zero; tensor products give the stated $d$-dimensional formula and moments. For $a\in\mathbb R^d$ with $\|a\|_1=1$, define \[ f_a(x)=\frac{\phi_d(x)}3\sum_{j=1}^d a_j\sin(2\pi x_j). \] A Fourier density is $\frac1{6i}\sum_j a_j[G_d(\xi-e_j)-G_d(\xi+e_j)]$. For either shift, $1+|\xi\pm e_j|\le2+|\xi|$, so its weighted integral is at most $2+1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz and the second moment. Hence the displayed density has weighted norm at most $(2+1/\sqrt{2\pi})/3<1$. Run the deterministic algorithm on the all-zero function and record its at most $m$ query points $x_i$. The $m\times d$ matrix $[\sin(2\pi(x_i)_j)]$ has a nonzero null vector; normalize it to $\|a\|_1=1$. Then $f_a$ and $-f_a$ both return zero at every query. Inductively their entire adaptive transcripts, and thus output $g$, equal the zero transcript. On the cube, $\phi_d\ge e^{-\pi/4}$. The coordinate sine functions are orthogonal and each has squared integral $1/2$. Since $1=\|a\|_1\le\sqrt d\|a\|_2$, \[ \|f_a\|_2\ge\frac{e^{-\pi/4}}{3\sqrt2}\|a\|_2 \ge\frac{e^{-\pi/4}}{3\sqrt{2d}}. \] Finally $2\|f_a\|_2\le\|f_a-g\|_2+\|-f_a-g\|_2$, so one of the indistinguishable inputs has at least the claimed error. ∎
This supplies a limited pre-asymptotic obstruction using the source's actual Fourier-density definition. The adversarial function depends on the deterministic zero transcript. It does not directly give a lower bound on expected error of randomized sampling, because one cannot choose a different fixed input after seeing each random seed.
