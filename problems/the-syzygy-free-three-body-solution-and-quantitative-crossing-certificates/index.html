Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For the planar three-body problem, a shape is a configuration modulo translation, rotation and positive scaling. Its normalized shape space is a sphere, with collisions marked and collinear configurations on an equator. A syzygy is a collinear configuration; its symbol records which body is in the middle. A central configuration has $\nabla_{q_i}U=-\lambda m_iq_i$ in center-of-mass coordinates for a common scalar $\lambda$. After fixing $I=1$, it is a critical point of $U$ on the collision-free inertia ellipsoid. Congruent configurations and scales are factored out for a finiteness count. A relative equilibrium is a rigidly rotating solution with time-independent shape. A homothetic Lagrange solution has $q_i(t)=r(t)a_i$ with the equilateral central configuration $(a_i)$ and no changing shape. In the planar center-of-mass frame the angular momentum is $C=\sum_i m_iq_i\wedge\dot q_i$. A syzygy-free orbit has no collinear configuration during its maximal collision-free time interval.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
In the three-body problem, are the Lagrange homothety solutions the only solutions with $E<0$, zero angular momentum and no syzygies (eclipses)?

Verifying the exceptional family and its endpoints.
Let $a_i$ be an equilateral central configuration with center of mass zero, $I_0=\sum m_i|a_i|^2$, $U_0=U(a)$, and $\nabla_iU(a)=-\lambda m_i a_i$. Euler homogeneity gives $\lambda I_0=U_0$. For $q_i(t)=r(t)a_i$, $r>0$, Newton's equations reduce to $r''=-\lambda/r^2$, and \[E=\tfrac12I_0\dot r^2-U_0/r,\qquad C=0. \] The zero angular momentum follows because each velocity is parallel to its position. The triangle remains equilateral and has nonzero area whenever $r>0$, so it has no syzygy before a collision endpoint.
Negative energy and finite maximal interval.
For $E<0$, the maximum radius is $r_{\max}=U_0/|E|$. The time from radius zero to this turning point is \[\sqrt{I_0/2}\int_0^{r_{\max}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{E+U_0/r}}, \] which is finite: near zero the integrand is $O(\sqrt r)$, and near the turning point it has an integrable inverse-square-root singularity. Reflection in time gives the collapse half. Thus the syzygy-free example is defined on a finite maximal collision-free interval and ends in total collisions; requiring eternal collision-free motion would exclude the very family named in the source.
