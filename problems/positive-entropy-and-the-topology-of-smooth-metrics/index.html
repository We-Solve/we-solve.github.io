Math-282

Positive entropy and the topology of smooth metrics

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed closed smooth manifold $M$ of dimension at least two, let $\mathcal G^\infty(M)$ be its smooth Riemannian metrics. Interpret the source's entropy as topological entropy $h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_g)$ of the unit-speed geodesic flow, and put \[ \mathcal P=\{g\in\mathcal G^\infty(M):h_{\mathrm{top}}(\varphi_g)>0\}. \] Question $8.4.1$ asks whether $\mathcal P$ is dense in the $C^k$ topology, for each specified $k$, including $k=\infty$. The excerpt does not itself define $G$ or entropy; these choices are explicit interpretations. Dimension one must be excluded from the aid's all-dimensions assertion.

For $S\subset\mathcal G^\infty(M)$, taking all closures within this smooth metric space, the aid states \[ \overline S^{\,C^\infty}=\bigcap_{r\geq0}\overline S^{\,C^r}. \] The smooth topology is generated by the finite derivative seminorms on the compact manifold. Consequently density in every finite $C^r$ topology is equivalent to $C^\infty$ density, but density in $C^2$ alone implies neither $C^3$ nor smooth density. The aid reports an explicit oscillatory metric-space example demonstrating this failure; its precise set is not reproduced in the supplied summary. Such a topology example makes no assertion that entropy behaves that way.

The proposed dynamical strategy is to create a nontrivial hyperbolic basic set, or horseshoe, by perturbations small in any prescribed finite $C^r$ norm. Let $\mathcal H$ be the smooth metrics whose geodesic flows contain such a positive-entropy hyperbolic basic set. Then $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal P$; hyperbolic persistence makes this a robust certificate under sufficiently small $C^2$ metric perturbations. In particular, if for every $r\geq2$, every smooth $g$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, one can find smooth $\widetilde g\in\mathcal H$ with \[ \|\widetilde g-g\|_{C^r}<\varepsilon, \] then $\mathcal P$ is smoothly dense. Different finite orders may use different perturbations. The missing step is control of arbitrarily prescribed finite metric jets, not an abstract promotion of a $C^2$ construction. This is a sufficient horseshoe-creation route; equivalence with all positive-entropy density mechanisms is not established here.

The source reports a positive $C^2$ answer from [Contreras2010], also referring to [Con-Pat2002], and a positive $C^\infty$ answer in dimension two from [Kni-Wei2002]. The aid qualifies the general $C^2$ statement to dimensions at least two, describes the smooth surface cases as largely known, and reports finding no general higher-dimensional $C^r$ theorem for $r\geq3$. These are supplied status statements with incomplete theorem hypotheses and references, rather than a new literature determination or a proof of higher-topology density.

Short English statement

Positive-entropy density depends on the metric topology. Density in every finite derivative topology gives smooth density, but a $C^2$ theorem alone does not. The aid proposes horseshoe creation with arbitrarily high finite derivative control as a sufficient route beyond the cited $C^2$ and surface results.
