Open Problems in Integrable Systems — Around the Birkhoff conjecture

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the smooth strictly convex planar interpretation used by the supplied research summary, let $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a compact strictly convex table with nonempty interior. For $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2\setminus K$, choose one of the two consistently oriented supporting tangents from $x$ to $K$, let $p(x)$ be its tangency point, and define the outer billiard map \[ T(x)=2p(x)-x. \] Choosing the opposite tangent reverses the map. An invariant curve $C$ obeys $T(C)=C$.

The rigidity question asks: if the entire exterior ${\mathbb{R}}^2\setminus K$ is foliated by invariant curves, must $K$ be an ellipse? In the standard integrable-foliation interpretation these are smooth simple closed curves surrounding $K$, forming the leaves of a foliation of its exterior. The source sentence itself does not state their smoothness, closedness, or the regularity of $\partial K$; the research summary explicitly uses smooth invariant curves and a strictly convex planar table. These regularity and foliation conventions require checking in the exact theorem.

The requested conclusion is that there are $c\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$ and a symmetric positive-definite $2\times2$ matrix $Q$ such that \[ K=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:(x-c)^{\mathsf T}Q(x-c)\leq1\}. \] The hypothesis is a global exterior foliation, not merely invariant curves near the table, far from it, or on a subset of positive measure. The supplied summary attributes an affirmative resolution in the smooth strictly convex setting to Bialy--Mironov (2023); no proof or independently checked regularity threshold is supplied here.

Short English statement

The source asks whether a planar outer billiard whose whole exterior is foliated by invariant curves must have an elliptical table. The supplied research summary reports an affirmative theorem for strictly convex tables and smooth invariant curves, with exact regularity hypotheses still to be checked.
