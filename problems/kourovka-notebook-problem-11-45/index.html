Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.45

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a finite set of size $v$, and write $\binom{X}{k}$ for its set of $k$-element subsets. A $6$-$(v,k,\lambda)$ design is a pair $\mathcal D=(X,\mathcal B)$, where $\mathcal B\subseteq\binom{X}{k}$ is a set of blocks, without repetitions, and \[ \left|\{B\in\mathcal B:T\subseteq B\}\right|=\lambda \qquad\text{for every }T\in\binom{X}{6}. \] Its automorphism group consists of the permutations of $X$ preserving $\mathcal B$ setwise. A subgroup $G\leq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal D)$ is block-transitive if, for every $B_1,B_2\in\mathcal B$, some $g\in G$ satisfies $g(B_1)=B_2$. A flag is an incident pair $(x,B)$ with $x\in B\in\mathcal B$; flag-transitivity means transitivity on these pairs.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied source does not define "nontrivial." An explicit provisional interpretation is \[ 6<k<v,\qquad \mathcal B\neq\binom{X}{k}. \] The exclusion of complete designs and the boundary conditions in this interpretation require confirmation against the source's intended convention.

Requested statement, under that provisional interpretation. For every choice of integers $v,k,\lambda$ with $6<k<v$ and $\lambda\geq1$, there is no $6$-$(v,k,\lambda)$ design $\mathcal D=(X,\mathcal B)$ with $\mathcal B\neq\binom{X}{k}$ admitting a block-transitive subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal D)$. This is the proposed nonexistence assertion, not a theorem established here.

Supplied historical qualifications. The original statement reports that the proposers had shown the corresponding nonexistence for nontrivial block-transitive $8$-designs, whereas some block-transitive, even flag-transitive, $5$-designs exist. These reports inherit the unresolved meaning of "nontrivial."

The comment labelled 2009 reports that a block-transitive group $G$ on a nontrivial $6$-design must either be an affine group $\operatorname{AGL}(d,2)$ or satisfy \[ \operatorname{PSL}(2,q)\leq G\leq\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(2,q). \] It further attributes to M. Huber's 2010 paper the restriction that, when $\lambda=1$, the only remaining possibility is $G=\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(2,p^e)$, where $p\in\{2,3\}$ and $e$ is an odd prime power. Here an odd prime power means $r^a$ for an odd prime $r$ and an integer $a\geq1$. The supplied excerpt does not specify further parameter or permutation-action qualifications.

The comment labelled 2013 reports that there are no block-transitive $7$-designs in the case $\lambda=1$. Its sentence does not explicitly repeat a nontriviality hypothesis, so its intended scope requires checking.

The comment labelled 2021 reports that there are no nontrivial $6$-designs with $k\leq10^4$ when the automorphism group is almost simple. A finite group $H$ is almost simple if $S\leq H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ for some nonabelian finite simple group $S$. This comment does not explicitly repeat block-transitivity or specify whether it concerns the full automorphism group or a chosen block-transitive subgroup; no resolution of those scope issues is asserted here.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether every nontrivial finite $6$-design fails to admit a group transitive on its blocks. The source leaves "nontrivial" undefined; the explicit formulation above provisionally excludes $k\leq6$, $k=v$, and complete designs. The registry labels the problem open, and supplies several dated reports of partial results. Neither that status nor those reports have been independently verified.
