Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In the six-vertex model, arrows on edges of a square lattice obey the ice rule: two arrows enter and two leave each vertex. Periodic boundary conditions place the lattice on a torus. The report discusses a continuum geometry emerging from finite-size spectral data, described by a complex modular parameter $\tau=\tau_0+i\tau_1$ with $\tau_1>0$. Geometric collapse refers to degeneration of that emergent geometry at a thermal phase transition, rather than removal of the combinatorial periodic lattice.
Source scope and qualifications.
The comparison comes from a dimer model where the torus angle collapses as the imaginary part tends to zero. The six-vertex conjecture deliberately predicts different behavior with nonzero critical imaginary part; it cannot be replaced by the usual cusp degeneration. The exact geometric-collapse observable remains part of the source construction.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Does the six-vertex model on a torus undergo a phase transition at which the emergent torus geometry collapses, with critical modular parameter $\tau$ satisfying $\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\neq0$?

What a modulus can and cannot encode.
Give the coordinate torus $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ the flat metric \[ g_{s,\tau}=s^2|dx+\tau dy|^2,\qquad s>0,\quad\tau=u+iv,\ v>0. \] Its matrix is $s^2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u\\u&u^2+v^2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with determinant $s^4v^2$ and area $s^2v$. A lattice loop indexed by integers $(m,n)$ has length $s|m+n\tau|$.
Two different collapse mechanisms.
Fixing $\tau=i$ and letting $s\downarrow0$ shrinks every length and the area while the imaginary part remains one. Conversely, if $s$ stays bounded below and $\tau$ converges to a finite $u_*+iv_*$ with $v_*>0$, the positive-definite metric matrices stay uniformly bounded below. Then nonzero lattice loops have a uniform positive minimum length, so this flat family does not collapse. This follows from continuity of the smallest eigenvalue and $m^2+n^2\geq1$.
Source-level implication.
Therefore nonzero $\operatorname{Im}\tau$ alone neither forbids nor explains collapse: an independent scale factor or another geometric observable matters. This is not the usual $v\to0$ degeneration and does not replace the source's conjecture with it. The first missing step is to extract both scale and shape from the six-vertex finite-size spectrum and identify which source-defined observable degenerates at the transition. No transfer-matrix calculation or phase-transition theorem is supplied, so the proposed model-specific collapse remains unresolved.
