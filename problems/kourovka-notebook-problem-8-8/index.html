Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.8

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group is cyclic if it is generated by a single element, finitely generated if it has a finite generating set, and finitely presented if it admits a presentation with finitely many generators and finitely many defining relations. For a group $G$ and an element $a\in G$, the condition that every element is conjugate to a power of $a$ means \[ \forall g\in G\;\exists h\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}} \quad g=h a^n h^{-1}. \] Here powers include zero and negative integer powers, and both $h$ and $n$ may depend on $g$.

Part (b), the remaining question in the supplied record (D. V. Anosov). Does there exist a noncyclic finitely presented group $G$ and an element $a\in G$ such that \[ G=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\{h a^n h^{-1}:h\in G\}? \]

Archived part (a) (D. V. Anosov). Does there exist a noncyclic finitely generated group $G$ containing an element $a$ such that every element of $G$ is conjugate to an integer power of $a$? The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer to this part, attributed to V. S. Guba. The registry labels the combined record partially_solved and retains part (b) in the main section. These are supplied source and curation assessments, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

The remaining question asks whether a noncyclic group with a finite presentation can have every element conjugate to a power of one fixed element. The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer when finite presentation is replaced by finite generation.
