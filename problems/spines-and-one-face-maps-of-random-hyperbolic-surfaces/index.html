Spines and One-Face Maps of Random Hyperbolic Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A hyperbolic surface is a surface equipped with a Riemannian metric of constant curvature $-1$. The supplied record does not specify orientability, compactness, completeness, boundary components, or cusps. For a compact connected closed surface $X$, a one-face map means a finite connected graph cellularly embedded in $X$, with exactly one face: its complement is homeomorphic to an open disk. This definition states the usual closed-surface convention; the record does not specify whether a different convention for surfaces with boundary or cusps is intended.

The term spine is not defined in the supplied record. Its geometric construction, any auxiliary choices, and whether it retains edge lengths or other decorations must be specified before its law can be determined. In particular, interpreting a spine as a graph onto which the entire surface deformation retracts would not apply to a compact closed surface.

The source poses the following questions.

  1. Determine the asymptotic law, and in particular any limiting law, of the spine of a random hyperbolic surface of genus $g$ as $g\to\infty$. To make this precise, one must specify a probability measure $\mu_g$ on a specified space of genus-$g$ hyperbolic surfaces, sample $X_g\sim\mu_g$, define its spine $S(X_g)$, and specify a measurable encoding and any normalization $T_g$ into a common topological state space $\mathcal E$. A possible precise limiting-law target would then be to identify a probability measure $\nu$ such that \[ \mathcal L\left(T_g(S(X_g))\right)\Longrightarrow\nu \qquad (g\to\infty), \] where $\mathcal L$ denotes probability law and $\Longrightarrow$ denotes weak convergence on the chosen state space. None of $\mu_g$, $S$, $T_g$, $\mathcal E$, or the intended topology is specified in the record, so this display is a formulation template, not a supplied conjecture asserting that a limit exists.
  2. Find other natural geometric constructions of one-face maps on hyperbolic surfaces. The source does not define the admissible surfaces, the construction against which these are to be considered "other," or a mathematical criterion for naturalness.
  3. Find a geometric adaptation of Chapuy's bijection that constructs random surfaces. This question appears explicitly in the original statement although it is omitted from the shortened statement. The intended bijection, decorations, geometric data, and probability distribution on the resulting surfaces are not specified.

The supplied surrounding exposition describes Chapuy's bijection between one-face maps and decorated trees, cited there as reference [4], as a key tool in the proof of reference [6]. The bibliography entries for [4] and [6] are absent from this record. Consequently, that contextual description does not determine a precise bijection or theorem here.

Short English statement

Determine the large-genus distribution of a geometrically defined spine of a random hyperbolic surface, find natural geometric constructions of one-face maps, and seek a geometric adaptation of Chapuy's bijection for constructing random surfaces. A precise probability model, spine construction, convergence framework, and target bijection remain unspecified. The supplied record labels the questions open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
