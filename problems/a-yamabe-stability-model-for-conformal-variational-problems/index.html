Math-12830

A Yamabe stability model for conformal variational problems

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 1c asks to "Study variational problems arising from conformally invariant problems." This is a research agenda without a specified functional, domain, or conclusion. The aid places it under Thomas Branson's anti-conformal perturbations heading and identifies the following "Michael Eastwood" line as the next participant block, not attribution of this problem.

Its model is the Yamabe variational problem. Let $(M,g)$ be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension $n\ge3$, take the nonnegative Laplacian $\Delta_g=-\operatorname{div}_g\nabla_g$, and set \[ a_n=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2},\quad p=\frac{2n}{n-2},\quad L_g=a_n\Delta_g+R_g, \quad Y_g(u)=\frac{\int_M(a_n|\nabla u|^2+R_gu^2){\,\mathrm{d}} V_g} {(\int_M|u|^p{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g)^{2/p}},\qquad u>0. \] For $\widehat g=w^{4/(n-2)}g$, $w>0$, the aid reports \[ L_{\widehat g}\phi=w^{-(p-1)}L_g(w\phi),\qquad Y_{\widehat g}(\phi)=Y_g(w\phi). \] The positive critical-point equation is $L_gu=\kappa u^{p-1}$, with $\kappa=\int_MuL_gu{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g/\int_Mu^p{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g$.

Suppose $\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)=1$ and $R_g\equiv R$ is constant, so $u=1$ is a normalized critical point. Write \[ E_g(u)=\int_M(a_n|\nabla u|^2+Ru^2){\,\mathrm{d}} V_g, \qquad C_g(u)=\int_Mu^p{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g. \] For the constraint $C_g=1$, the multiplier in $E_g-\mu(C_g-1)$ is $\mu=2R/p$. Its constrained Hessian on $\int_Mv{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g=0$ is reported as \[ \mathcal H(v,v)=2\int_M\left(a_n|\nabla v|^2-(p-2)Rv^2\right){\,\mathrm{d}} V_g. \] Since $(p-2)/a_n=1/(n-1)$, for connected $M$ the exact infinitesimal nonnegative-stability criterion is \[ \lambda_1(\Delta_g)\ge\frac{R}{n-1}, \] where $\lambda_1$ is the first positive eigenvalue. Equality directions are the mean-zero eigenspace with eigenvalue $R/(n-1)$. If $R\le0$ and $M$ is connected, the Hessian is strictly positive on nonzero mean-zero variations. These are Hessian assertions, not a claim of strict nonlinear local minimality in every degenerate case.

If $M$ has $b_0(M)$ components but only one total-volume constraint, componentwise constants satisfying the global mean-zero condition form a $b_0(M)-1$-dimensional subspace. At $R=0$ these are exactly the additional zero modes; at $R>0$ they are negative directions; at $R<0$ they are positive. For nonconstant componentwise modes the same spectral threshold must be checked on each component. Thus connected stability cannot be transferred unchanged to a disconnected manifold.

The aid also invokes the Lichnerowicz--Obata sphere interpretation. In the explicit positive Einstein setting $\operatorname{Ric}_g=(n-1)kg$, $k>0$, one has $R=n(n-1)k$ and the relevant bound is $\lambda_1\ge nk$, with equality in the connected case characterized by the round sphere of curvature $k$. This interpretation needs its Ricci-curvature hypothesis; constant scalar curvature alone does not supply that eigenvalue bound. The model illustrates one precisely normalized conformal variational problem rather than resolving the full agenda.
