A full-rank subdeterminant is the determinant of a square submatrix of order equal to the rank of $A$. Integer feasibility asks whether the constraint system has a vector in $\mathbb Z^n$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:Ax\leq b\}$, where $A$ is integral and the absolute values of its full-rank subdeterminants are bounded by $\Delta$. For arbitrary $\Delta\geq3$, can one decide in polynomial time whether $P$ contains an integer vector? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The question does not specify whether $\Delta$ is fixed or encoded as part of the input. Full-rank determinant bounds are not replaced by bounds on every smaller square minor.

Source convention and attempted proximity route

The primary report explicitly calls the determinant bound a constant and assumes both $A$ and $b$ integral. We analyze that fixed-$\Delta$ setting without changing the more qualified catalogue wording. A possible enumeration route is to find an LP vertex and search an axis-aligned neighborhood whose radius depends only on $\Delta$ and the dimension. The following full-dimensional example defeats that unconditioned proximity claim. For an integer $M\ge1$, put \[ U_M=\begin{pmatrix}1&M\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_M=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:0\le U_Mx\le(1,1)^T\}. \] The four-row inequality matrix is formed by the two rows of $U_M$ and their negatives.
Proposition: Every full-rank subdeterminant has absolute value zero or three. The vertex $v_M=(-M/3,1/3)$ is at $\ell_\infty$ distance at least $M/3$ from every integer point in $P_M$.
Proof: A pair of opposite rows is dependent. Any independent pair is, up to signs and ordering, the two rows of $U_M$, whose determinant is three. Since $U_M$ is invertible, $P_M$ is the inverse image of a square and has nonempty interior; $v_M$ is the inverse image of $(0,1)$. For integer $x$, the constraint $0\le3x_2\le1$ forces $x_2=0$, after which $0\le x_1\le1$ gives exactly $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$. Their first-coordinate distances from $v_M$ are respectively $M/3$ and $1+M/3$. ∎
Thus no radius depending solely on $(n,\Delta)=(2,3)$ works around every LP vertex in the original coordinates. The large one-by-one minor $M$ also demonstrates why a bound on full-rank minors cannot be silently replaced by a bound on every minor. Integer-preserving coordinate preprocessing could evade this obstruction, and this family is easy to solve directly.

Verification and remaining gaps

For $M=30$, all six two-row determinants were computed and the two integer feasible points checked; the displayed vertex has distance ten from the nearest one. A general algorithm may choose different vertices or transformed coordinates. Neither feasibility hardness nor a new bounded-determinant algorithm follows.
