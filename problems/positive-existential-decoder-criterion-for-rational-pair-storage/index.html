Math-1576

Positive-existential decoder criterion for rational pair storage

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 14 asks for a Diophantine injection ${\mathbb{Q}}^2\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}$, motivated by coding blocks of universal quantifiers. The supplied research assessment identifies the intended full-domain algebraic target as \[ \exists P\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X,Y]\ \forall a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{Q}}\quad \left(P(a,b)=P(c,d)\ \Longrightarrow\ a=c\ \wedge\ b=d\right). \] A polynomial map has a Diophantine graph, but a function with an arbitrary positive-existentially definable graph is a more general notion of "Diophantine injection." The original wording does not expressly choose between these notions. Neither means merely an injection on a restricted subset of ${\mathbb{Q}}^2$. The aid reports that the polynomial question remains open and that every polynomial of total degree at most two fails to be injective on ${\mathbb{Q}}^2$; thus a polynomial solution would have degree at least three.

The source credits Jeroen Demeyer with the observation that a polynomial bijection ${\mathbb{N}}^2\to{\mathbb{N}}$ permits replacement of each consecutive universal block in a positive prenex formula over ${\mathbb{N}}$ by one universal quantifier, at the cost of additional existential quantifiers. The cited natural-number coding is from Davis, Math. Monthly 80, 236--237. Its essential logical feature is a total surjective decoding map, not just injectivity of a coding map.

Precisely, let $M$ be a nonempty structure and let $r\ge1$. A positive-existential formula is built from atomic formulas by conjunction, disjunction and existential quantification. Suppose $D(y,\bar x)$ is such a formula defining a function $M\to M^r$, so \[ (\forall y\in M)(\exists!\bar x\in M^r)D(y,\bar x), \qquad (\forall\bar x\in M^r)(\exists y\in M)D(y,\bar x). \] For any formula $\varphi(\bar x,\bar a)$ one then has \[ \forall\bar x\,\varphi(\bar x,\bar a) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall y\,\exists\bar x\, \left(D(y,\bar x)\wedge\varphi(\bar x,\bar a)\right). \] Expanding the existential witnesses in $D$ preserves the positive-prenex form and replaces the $r$ universal variables of that block by $y$.

One sufficient way to obtain this decoder is a total injective map $e:M^r\to M$ with positive-existential graph $G(\bar x,y)$, a positive-existential definition $C(y)$ of $M\setminus e(M^r)$, and a named or positive-existentially specified default tuple $\bar c$. Then \[ D(y,\bar x)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ G(\bar x,y)\ \vee\ \left(C(y)\wedge\bar x=\bar c\right) \] is a total surjective decoder. Without the noncode clause, the direct inverse-code rewrite already fails for the tautology $\forall\bar x\,(\bar x=\bar x)$ when $e$ is not onto: its proposed replacement would falsely require every $y$ to lie in the image. This is a limitation of that direct construction, not a proof that no other coding reduction can follow from a bare injection.

The source additionally says that observations in C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 328, 3--8 (1999) imply the desired injection from a generalized $abc$ conjecture. No exact conjecture, theorem or bibliographic identification beyond that citation is supplied, so this remains a reported conditional connection rather than an instantiated theorem.

Short English statement

Seek a polynomial injection from all rational pairs into the rationals. The research aid leaves that question open and gives a precise sufficient decoder condition for collapsing universal quantifier blocks; a non-surjective injection alone does not justify the direct inverse-code rewrite.
