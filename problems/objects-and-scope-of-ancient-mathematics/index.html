Definitions and mathematical statement

Nature and scope of the question. The supplied record poses a historiographical question concerning ancient Greek and Roman thought. It does not specify mathematical structures, axioms, or a proposition with formal quantifiers. In particular, it supplies no definitions of "mathematical object," "empirical phenomenon," or the sense in which such objects might be "distinct." These expressions therefore remain subjects of historical interpretation rather than formally defined terms.

Source question. In ancient Greek and Roman thought, what does mathematics study; are mathematical objects distinct from empirical phenomena; and how do the objects of arithmetic, geometry, and applied fields such as harmonics, astronomy, statics, dynamics, mechanics, and cartography relate?

Short English statement

The question asks how ancient Greek and Roman thinkers understood the objects of mathematics, their distinction from empirical phenomena, and their relationships across arithmetic, geometry, and the listed applied fields. The supplied record leaves the historical corpus and key concepts unspecified, so this remains a historiographical research question requiring human review.
