Math-3315

Sparse Graph Sequences with Prescribed Homomorphism Limits

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not explicitly specify the graph category or the edge-density convention. The following precise formulation adopts finite simple undirected graphs and edge density $p(G)=e(G)/\binom{v(G)}{2}$; these conventions require confirmation from the primary source. Here $v(H)=|V(H)|$ and $e(H)=|E(H)|$. For graphs $F,G$, let $\operatorname{hom}(F,G)$ denote the number of maps $\phi:V(F)\to V(G)$ such that $\{\phi(u),\phi(v)\}\in E(G)$ whenever $\{u,v\}\in E(F)$. For $v(G)\geq2$ and $p=p(G)>0$, define \[ t_p(F,G)=p^{-e(F)}v(G)^{-v(F)}\operatorname{hom}(F,G). \] Write $C_4$ for the cycle on four vertices and $K_3$ for the complete graph on three vertices.

Does there exist a sequence $(G_n)_{n\geq1}$ of such graphs, with $N_n=v(G_n)\geq2$ and $p_n=p(G_n)>0$, satisfying all of the following conditions?

  1. $N_n\to\infty$ and $p_n=N_n^{-o(1)}$, meaning \[ \frac{\log(1/p_n)}{\log N_n}\longrightarrow0. \]
  2. For every finite simple undirected graph $F$, including graphs with isolated vertices, the finite real limit \[ a_F=\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{p_n}(F,G_n) \] exists.
  3. $a_{C_4}=a_{K_3}=1$.
  4. The limits satisfy the uniform bound \[ \sup_{\substack{F\text{ finite simple undirected}\ e(F)\geq1}}a_F^{1/e(F)}<\infty. \]
  5. There is at least one finite simple undirected graph $F$ for which $a_F\neq1$.
The restriction $e(F)\geq1$ in the supremum makes the exponent meaningful; the supplied statement writes $\sup_F$ without this restriction. Edgeless graphs still occur in the limit condition and have normalized homomorphism count $1$. No connectedness restriction on $F$, and no additional requirement $p_n\to0$, is stated in the supplied record.

Short English statement

Under the explicitly stated graph and density conventions, ask whether graphs of growing order and subpolynomially small edge density can have normalized homomorphism limits for every graph, with triangle and four-cycle limits equal to $1$, a uniform exponential bound in the number of edges, and at least one limit different from $1$. The supplied record labels this problem open; that status is not independently verified here.
