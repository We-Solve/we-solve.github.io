Irreducible Coherent-Configuration Representations Without Polynomial Splitting

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A standard finite coherent configuration consists of a finite set $X$ and a partition $\mathcal R$ of $X\times X$ such that the diagonal is a union of members of $\mathcal R$, the transpose of every member belongs to $\mathcal R$, and, for all $R,S,T\in\mathcal R$, the number \[ \left|\{z\in X:(x,z)\in R,\ (z,y)\in S\}\right| \] is independent of the choice of $(x,y)\in T$. For a coefficient field $K$, let $A_R$ be the adjacency matrix of $R$, and let \[ \mathcal A_K=\operatorname{span}_K\{A_R:R\in\mathcal R\}\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_X(K). \] This is the associated matrix algebra. A representation is a unital algebra homomorphism $\rho:\mathcal A_K\to\operatorname{End}_K(V)$. It is irreducible if $V\ne0$ and its only invariant subspaces are $0$ and $V$. These definitions give a conventional framework; the supplied record does not specify the coefficient field or the representation used by the method in question.

Self-adjointness requires an adjoint operation $*$, with $a$ self-adjoint meaning $a^*=a$. The record does not identify this operation. For a finite-dimensional action $T$ of a chosen element $a$, write $\chi_T(t)=\det(tI-T)$. The condition that this polynomial split into distinct linear factors over a field $L$ means \[ \chi_T(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{\dim V}(t-\lambda_j),\qquad \lambda_j\in L,\qquad \lambda_i\ne\lambda_j\ \text{for }i\ne j. \] Here $V$ denotes the space of that action. The supplied record does not specify which action determines the characteristic polynomial or which field $L$ is intended.

Source question. For representations of a coherent-configuration matrix algebra, can the desired irreducible action be computed without assuming that a chosen self-adjoint element has a characteristic polynomial splitting into distinct linear factors over the relevant splitting field?

The target irreducible action, its input description, the representation method, the permitted field extensions, and the meaning of computation are unspecified. Consequently, the record does not determine a fully quantified algorithmic problem. Its background also describes the objective as avoiding factorization of the characteristic polynomial. Avoiding a factorization step and removing the distinct-linear-factor hypothesis are different requirements; their intended relationship remains unresolved. If the relevant field is a splitting field of this very polynomial, splitting follows by definition, whereas distinctness of the roots remains an additional condition.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a method for computing an irreducible action of a coherent-configuration matrix algebra can dispense with a distinct-root splitting assumption on a chosen self-adjoint element. The supplied background additionally asks about avoiding polynomial factorization. The field, adjoint, input representation, target action, and relationship between these two objectives require clarification.
