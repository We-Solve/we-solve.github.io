Math-118

Effective Enumeration of Rational Points on Curves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a number field, that is, a finite extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and let $C/F$ be a smooth, geometrically irreducible projective curve of genus $g(C)\geq 2$. Geometric irreducibility means that $C\times_F\overline F$ is irreducible. Write $C(F)$ for the set of $F$-rational points of $C$. For a projective presentation $C\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_F^n$, these are the projective coordinate tuples in ${\mathbb{P}}^n(F)$ satisfying the defining equations, with tuples identified under multiplication by a common nonzero element of $F$.

Algorithmic question. Does there exist a single algorithm which, for every number field $F$ and every such curve $C/F$, takes exact equations defining $C$, together with an effective finite presentation of $F$ and its coefficients, halts, and outputs a finite list of distinct points $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ such that \[ C(F)=\{P_1,\ldots,P_m\}? \] Here $m\geq 0$, and the output must be the empty list when $C(F)=\varnothing$. An effective presentation of the number field and exact coordinates is the computational interpretation of the supplied phrase "given equations"; the record does not prescribe a particular encoding. The input is promised to satisfy the stated geometric hypotheses.

Additional question in the original statement. The supplied original formulation also asks whether $\#C(F)$ can be bounded above using suitable known invariants of $C$. It does not specify which invariants are allowed, whether they must be computable from the input, or what effectiveness and uniformity are required of the bound. This additional target is retained here as an unresolved formulation qualification; it is absent from the supplied clean statement. The original wording also does not explicitly include geometric irreducibility, whereas the clean statement does.

The supplied literature assessment reports uniform cardinality bounds involving genus, field degree, and Jacobian rank, while distinguishing these from an algorithm producing the complete list. This is supplied dated curation, not an independently verified theorem statement or literature assessment.

Short English statement

The main question asks for one terminating algorithm that lists every rational point on any given smooth geometrically irreducible projective curve of genus at least $2$ over a number field, including certifying an empty list when appropriate. The original record additionally asks for cardinality bounds in unspecified known invariants; that part needs further formulation. The supplied open label and literature assessment are unverified curation.
