Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.11

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathfrak L$ be the class of locally compact topological groups with no small subgroups. A topological group $G$, with identity $e_G$, has no small subgroups if there is an open neighbourhood $U$ of $e_G$ such that every subgroup $K\leq G$ contained in $U$ is trivial: \[ K\leq G,\quad K\subseteq U\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K=\{e_G\}. \] For a group $H$, write \[ Z(H)=\{z\in H:zh=hz\text{ for every }h\in H\}. \] A kernel is called discrete when it is discrete in the subspace topology.

Requested direct-proof problem. Study extensions of groups in $\mathfrak L$ with the objective of giving a direct proof of the following assertion, without using the Gleason--Montgomery--Zippin solution of Hilbert's fifth problem: \[ \forall G\in\mathfrak L\;\exists H\in\mathfrak L\;\exists\text{ a continuous group homomorphism }\vartheta:G\longrightarrow H \] such that \[ \ker\vartheta\text{ is discrete},\qquad \vartheta(G)\cap Z(H)=\{e_H\}. \] The source specifies no particular type of extension to be studied.

Supplied motivation and unresolved qualifications. The source states that this assertion already follows from the solution of Hilbert's fifth problem, identifying $\mathfrak L$ with finite-dimensional Lie groups and invoking their local linearity. It further states that a direct proof would substantially shorten the proof of Hilbert's fifth problem, because the adjoint representation of $H$ would be faithful on $\vartheta(G)$. These are supplied background claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft. The record does not specify separation, countability, or connectedness conventions. The intended conventions for the identification with Lie groups, and the hypotheses needed for the stated faithfulness of the adjoint representation, require mathematical review, particularly when $H$ is disconnected. No such additional hypotheses have been silently imposed.

Short English statement

Dixon asks for a direct proof, through the study of extensions, that every locally compact group with no small subgroups admits a continuous homomorphism into another such group, with discrete kernel and image meeting the target's center only in the identity. The source presents the existence assertion as a known consequence of Hilbert's fifth problem; its supplied open label concerns the requested direct-proof problem. Topological conventions and the adjoint-representation motivation require review.
