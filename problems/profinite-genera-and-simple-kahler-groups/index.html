Math-3999

Profinite Genera and Simple Kähler Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A Kähler group is a group isomorphic to $\pi_1(X,x)$ for a connected compact Kähler manifold $X$. A group $G$ is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$, there exist a finite group $Q$ and a homomorphism $f:G\to Q$ with $f(g)\ne 1$. Its profinite completion is \[ \widehat G=\varprojlim_{\substack{N\trianglelefteq G\\[G:N]<\infty}}G/N, \] with its inverse-limit topology. Let $\mathcal K$ denote the class of Kähler groups and $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{rf}}$ its subclass of residually finite groups. For either comparison class $\mathcal C$, define \[ \operatorname{Gen}_{\mathcal C}(G) =\left\{[H]_{\cong}:H\in\mathcal C,\ \widehat H\cong_{\mathrm{top}}\widehat G\right\}, \] where $[H]_{\cong}$ is the abstract group isomorphism class of $H$, and $\cong_{\mathrm{top}}$ means isomorphism of topological groups.

The first supplied question asks whether \[ \forall G\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{rf}},\qquad \left|\operatorname{Gen}_{\mathcal C}(G)\right|<\infty. \] The supplied record calls this the finiteness of the $\mathcal K$-profinite genus, but does not supply its definition. In particular, it does not establish whether the comparison class is $\mathcal C=\mathcal K_{\mathrm{rf}}$ or $\mathcal C=\mathcal K$. These are distinct candidate formulations, and the intended choice requires source clarification.

A group $S$ is simple if $S\ne\{1\}$ and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $S$. The second supplied question, read literally, is \[ \exists S\in\mathcal K\quad S\text{ is simple}. \] The record does not explicitly require $S$ to be infinite. If infinite simplicity is intended, the formulation instead is \[ \exists S\in\mathcal K\quad \left(|S|=\infty\ \text{and}\ S\text{ is simple}\right). \] This additional condition is unresolved here. Residual finiteness is imposed on $G$ in the first question; the supplied wording imposes no such condition on $S$ in the second.

The background also mentions the related question \[ \exists H\in\mathcal K\quad H\text{ is not residually finite}. \] The supplied curation attributes a positive answer to Toledo. This is contextual information, not an additional unresolved target or an independently verified result in this draft.

Short English statement

The record asks whether every residually finite Kähler group has only finitely many Kähler groups with the same profinite completion, counted up to group isomorphism, and whether a simple Kähler group exists. The comparison class for the genus and any intended infinitude requirement for the simple group need clarification. Supplied dated curation attributes the related existence of a non-residually-finite Kähler group to Toledo.
