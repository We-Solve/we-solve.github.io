Research attempt: An information-loss test for a proposed JATS conversion

Consider a source language with nodes $(k,i,t)$: kind $k\in\{\text{theorem},\text{paragraph}\}$, identifier $i$, and text $t$. A proposed conversion emits only a target paragraph containing $t$, dropping $k$ and $i$. A theorem and an ordinary paragraph with the same text then have identical output. No inverse converter can reconstruct both source nodes: its one input would require two different outputs. Moreover a source cross-reference to $i$ has no retained target identity to resolve. Syntactic validity of the target paragraph does not repair either loss.

For a restricted reversible model, transmit an injective encoding of $(k,i,t)$ in target-supported fields or an accompanying typed record. If decoding reads those fields exactly, the composite decode-after-encode is the identity by componentwise equality. That identity requires representability and preservation of the fields; it does not follow merely because both source and target are XML. If a selected target profile cannot carry one component, a conversion can instead declare its loss explicitly and promise preservation only of the remaining observables.

Loss declarations must compose. Suppose conversion $f$ drops identifiers but preserves kind and text, and $g$ drops kind but preserves text. The composite preserves text, but cannot promise identifier or kind recovery. More generally if $I_f$ and $I_g$ are promised observables and the intermediate meaning is aligned, the universally justified common guarantee is limited to those preserved by both steps. The two-node collision witnesses why adding an apparently reversible second converter cannot reconstruct information already erased by the first.
