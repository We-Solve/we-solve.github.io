Math-15118

Polygonal Period Relations and Formal Multiple Zeta Values

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $n\geq3$, $M_{0,n}$ is the moduli space of smooth genus-zero curves with $n$ distinct ordered marked points, modulo isomorphism. Let $\mathcal P$ be the source's class of polygon pairs representing periods on these spaces. In the usual rational-algebra interpretation, let $A$ be the free commutative ${\mathbb{Q}}$-algebra on the corresponding formal symbols, and let $I$ be the ideal generated by the three specified relation families:
  1. invariance under the symmetric-group relabeling;
  2. one-point shuffle relations;
  3. product-map relations.
The proposed polygon algebra is $FC=A/I$. Let $\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{form}}$ denote the formal multiple-zeta-value algebra intended by the report. The conjectural target is an isomorphism \[ FC\cong\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{form}}, \] with the intended correspondence of polygon-period symbols and formal multiple-zeta values. The task is about the given period constructions, not an arbitrary abstract isomorphism of unrelated algebras.

The source also calls this equivalent to completeness of the three relation families among the corresponding periods. If these are numerical period relations, and $\operatorname{ev}:A\to{\mathbb{C}}$ sends each admissible symbol to its period, completeness means \[ \ker(\operatorname{ev})=I. \] A formal-algebra isomorphism alone need not imply that numerical equality: the evaluation map from formal multiple-zeta values may itself have a kernel. Thus "all relations" must distinguish relations in the intended formal presentation from actual numerical period identities. The extract gives no presentation of $\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{form}}$, formulas for the three generators of $I$, comparison map, or polygon admissibility and regularization conventions. Their missing definitions and the asserted equivalence remain explicit rather than being filled by a chosen double-shuffle or associator presentation.

Short English statement

Ask whether the polygon-pair algebra modulo relabeling, one-point shuffle and product-map relations is the intended formal multiple-zeta algebra. The source also claims these relations are complete; formal identities and numerical period relations must be distinguished before that equivalence is precise.
