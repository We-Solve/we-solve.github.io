Mirror-quintic periods, modular-type structures, and a conifold obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Definitions and notation.
The distinguished series is $\varpi_0(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}(5n)!/(n!)^5\,z^n$. With $\theta=z\,d/dz$, it satisfies the mirror-quintic Picard--Fuchs operator $\theta^4-5z(5\theta+1)(5\theta+2)(5\theta+3)(5\theta+4)$. Other independent solutions describe additional periods, possibly with logarithmic behavior. Modular or automorphic behavior requires a group, domain and transformation law.

Is $\sum \frac{(5n)!}{(n!)^5}z^n$ related to (non-holomorphic) forms? Is it related to automorphic objects? Do other solutions to the Picard-Fuchs equation have modular properties?
Scope and source qualification.
The Picard–Fuchs equation identifies the series without claiming a classical modular uniformization. The source asks about nonholomorphic forms and other automorphic objects as well as the distinguished period; all are retained. A direct Picard--Fuchs verification.
Put $a_n=(5n)!/(n!)^5$, $a_0=1$, and $\theta=z\,d/dz$. For $n\ge1$, factorial cancellation gives \[n^4a_n=5(5n-4)(5n-3)(5n-2)(5n-1)a_{n-1}. \] Comparing coefficients proves that $f(z)=\sum a_nz^n$ solves $[\theta^4-5z(5\theta+1)(5\theta+2)(5\theta+3)(5\theta+4)]f=0$. Conversely this recurrence uniquely determines a holomorphic power-series solution from its constant coefficient; thus all holomorphic solutions at zero are multiples of $f$. The ratio $a_{n+1}/a_n\to5^5$, so the convergence radius is exactly $5^{-5}$ by the ratio test. For $|z|$ below that radius the differentiated series converge normally, justifying the operator calculation analytically as well as formally.
A limitation of recognizing a differential equation.
The operator is fourth order, while only one holomorphic solution is obtained at zero. Additional local solutions can have logarithms; no claim that they are single-valued modular forms follows. A differential equation with rational coefficients alone does not provide an arithmetic group, an automorphy factor or a modular coordinate. In particular its monodromy representation and a proposed group action must be related by a separate construction.

This recurrence calculation is a known analytic verification, not a proof or disproof of the broad automorphic connection.
