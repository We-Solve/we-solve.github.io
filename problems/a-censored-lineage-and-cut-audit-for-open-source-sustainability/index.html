Testing a project-success count with censored lifetimes

To study sustainability one might count projects that have not yet failed as long-term successes. We isolate the right-censoring problem before proposing any causal success principle. Fix a common target duration $T$ and $N$ projects. Exactly $d$ are known to have ended before $T$, $s$ are observed to survive at least to $T$, and $c$ have observation end before $T$ while still active, with $d+s+c=N$. There is no assumption about how censoring occurs.
Proposition: The finite-population fraction $S(T)$ surviving to $T$ has the sharp identification interval \[ \frac{s}{N}\le S(T)\le\frac{s+c}{N}. \] Every integer numerator between these endpoints can occur with the same observed records.
Proof: The $s$ confirmed survivors contribute one each, the $d$ known failures zero, and each censored project an unknown zero or one. This gives the bounds. For any chosen subset of censored projects, assign their unobserved end times after $T$, and assign the others an end time strictly between their censoring time and $T$. Such an interval exists since they were censored before $T$. These assignments preserve every observed record and realize all possible counts from $s$ to $s+c$. ∎
For instance, at target $T=5$ suppose one project failed at time 1 and two were last observed active at time 2. The observed active fraction $2/3$ is not an identified five-year survival fraction: all values $0,1/3,2/3$ are possible. The attempted inference from current visibility to long-term success fails even before confounding, project lineage and shared dependencies are considered.
