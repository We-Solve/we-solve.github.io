Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For a lattice function $f$, the forward discrete gradient is $Df(\ell)=(f(\ell+e_i)-f(\ell))_{i=1}^d$, with $e_i$ the coordinate lattice vectors; $D^j$ is its iterated tensor-valued finite difference. The displacement $\bar u$ is measured relative to the reference lattice and satisfies the source's equilibrium equations and defect boundary conditions. The norm $|D^j\bar u(\ell)|$ is a fixed finite-dimensional tensor norm. The proposed constant $C$ is independent of differentiation order $j$ and far-field site $\ell$.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let $\bar u:\mathbb Z^d\to\mathbb R^d$ be an equilibrium displacement field for a crystalline defect. Is there a constant $C>0$ such that, for every $j\geq1$ and lattice site $\ell$ away from the defect,

$$\lvert D^j\bar u(\ell)\rvert\leq Cj!\lvert\ell\rvert^{1-d-j}$$

hold?

Source scope and qualification.
The report already gives bounds with order-dependent constants $C_j$; the target is the stronger common-$C$ factorial estimate. The defect model, stability and meaning of away from the defect must remain the source hypotheses.

Short English statement

Can the far-field regularity constants for crystalline-defect equilibria be controlled by a single factorial bound in every derivative order?

An all-order lattice model.
The source asks for one constant, not arbitrary order-dependent constants. A useful exact benchmark is $u(n)=1/n$ on positive integers, with $Du(n)=u(n+1)-u(n)$. Induction proves \[ D^ju(n)=\frac{(-1)^jj!}{n(n+1)\cdots(n+j)}. \] For the induction step the difference of the reciprocal products starting at $n+1$ and $n$, after putting them over the common product through $n+j+1$, has numerator $n-(n+j+1)=-(j+1)$. In particular \[ |D^ju(n)|\le j!\,n^{-j-1},\qquad j\ge1,n\ge1. \] This has precisely the power $1-d-j$ along a ray when $d=2$, and the constant is one for every order. It confirms that factorial control is compatible with discrete differences and is not automatically spoiled by arbitrarily long forward stencils in an outward direction.
Failed extrapolation.
This sequence is not asserted to be an equilibrium of the report's defect model. For a multi-dimensional displacement, mixed directions may move a stencil toward the defect, and nonlinear equilibrium coefficients can introduce order-dependent growth absent from the reciprocal identity. Neither the report's existing $C_j$ bounds nor this benchmark controls those effects. The first gap is a recurrence for the actual equilibrium derivatives whose constants close with the exact common-$C$ normalization in the statement, rather than a silently weaker $C A^j j!$. The proof checks all positive $n$, but supplies no new defect-equilibrium theorem.
