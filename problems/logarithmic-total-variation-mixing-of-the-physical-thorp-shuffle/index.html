Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For the physical Thorp shuffle on $n=2^d$ cards from a deterministic deck, is the worst-start mixing time at total-variation distance 1/4 Theta(d)? Require a universal linear-in-d upper bound, uniformly over starts, together with the support lower bound $||q_d^{*t}-Uniform||_TV\geq 1-2^{tn/2}/n!$ for every integer $t\geq 0$.

OpenAI claim summary for Math-2919

The optimal-order manuscript claims an unconditional total-variation upper bound after a fixed number of coordinate sweeps, each consisting of $d$ physical Thorp steps. Its support-size lower bound gives the opposing linear order, yielding $\Theta(d)$ mixing for the physical shuffle in the question. Several companions give unconditional sweep estimates as well. The partial-information Fourier manuscript is conditional on its matrix caps and cannot supply the physical mixing conclusion alone; that limitation belongs to this companion, rather than to the aggregate result.

Logarithmic total-variation mixing of the physical Thorp shuffle

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A strict permanent exponent below two for almost-uniform $S_4$ laws with uniform coordinate marginals is claimed to imply optimal Thorp mixing; Compatibility-entropy estimates are claimed to show optimal-order Thorp mixing after a fixed number of coordinate sweeps; Near-uniform bounded-size line laws are claimed to satisfy conditional Schatten-moment contraction, with an analytic transfer yielding binary Thorp mixing in order $d$; After an absolute number of deterministic Thorp coordinate sweeps, further-card endpoints are claimed nearly uniform on unoccupied sites in expectation conditional on revealed card paths; With half of the Thorp trajectories revealed, the remaining conditional permutation is claimed to approach uniform after a fixed number of sweeps; Near-uniform images of a random $7n/8$ label set and a vanishing sign bias are claimed enough for two independent permutation factors to approach uniform on $S_n$; The Thorp shuffle on $2^d$ cards is claimed to mix in order $d$ physical shuffles, with upper bound $1600d$ and lower bound $2d-O(1)$; Random-coordinate-frame estimates are claimed to give full Thorp mixing by $32800d$, improved to $512d$ using the Fourier companion; An absolute number of Thorp sweeps is claimed to reduce full-law total variation below $\tfrac12 n^{-5}$ uniformly over initial decks; Routing-density contraction is claimed to give full-permutation Thorp mixing after an absolute number of sweeps, hence order $\log N$ physical steps; A row--column Fourier comparison is claimed to give the same optimal logarithmic order of Thorp mixing; A fixed number of sweeps is claimed to make the full Thorp permutation density approach uniform in squared $L^2$, implying optimal total-variation order. Together they give a claimed affirmative answer in the matching scope.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For $N=2^d$ cards, one physical Thorp shuffle pairs corresponding cards in the two deck halves, independently orders each pair by a fair coin, and interleaves. Its law is $q_d$ on $S_N$; $U_N$ is uniform. A sweep uses each binary-coordinate matching once with independent fair switches, hence costs $d$ physical shuffles. Total variation means half the sum of absolute mass differences. If $T_N(\lambda)=\mathbb E\rho_\lambda(g)$ is the sweep average on the irreducible unitary $S_N$-representation of degree $D_\lambda$, put $B_N(\lambda)=T_N(\lambda)T_N(\lambda)^*$. Absolute $\eta>0$ and an even integer $r\ge2$ satisfy \[ D_\lambda\operatorname{Tr}B_N(\lambda)^r\le D_\lambda^{1-\eta} \quad(\lambda\vdash N), \] with unnormalized trace. Consequently a fixed number $q$ of independent forward sweeps has distance from $U_N$ tending to zero as $N\to\infty$, uniformly over initial decks.
Result 2.
Let $B$ be convolution by a probability measure on a finite group $G$, and $B_\rho$ its Fourier matrix in an irreducible unitary representation of dimension $D_\rho$. Suppose fixed $p>0$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$ satisfy \[ \sum_{\rho\ne\mathbf1}D_\rho\operatorname{Tr}|B_\rho|^p\le\varepsilon, \qquad |B_\rho|=(B_\rho^*B_\rho)^{1/2}. \] For a sequence of such groups, assume every nonzero nonconstant block has dimension at least $D_{\min}\to\infty$. If $\mu_M$ is the law after $M$ independent convolution steps and $2M>p$, then \[ 4\|\mu_M-U_G\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2 \le\varepsilon(\varepsilon/D_{\min})^{(2M-p)/p}\longrightarrow0. \] This is a conditional spectral-conversion result; its moment hypothesis must be supplied by the sweep estimates.

Its law is $q_d$ on $S_N$; $U_N$ is uniform. A sweep uses each binary-coordinate matching once with independent fair switches, hence costs $d$ physical shuffles. Total variation means half the sum of absolute mass differences. Let $K_\lambda=\mathbb E\rho_\lambda(g)$ be the Fourier matrix of one sweep. There are absolute $g>0,d_0$ such that, for $d\ge d_0$, \[ \|K_\lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le D_\lambda^{-g}\quad(\lambda\vdash 2^d). \] The sign block vanishes. For some absolute integer $w>0$, $w$ independent sweeps converge in total variation to uniform as $d\to\infty$, uniformly over deterministic starting decks.
Result 4.
For $N=2^d$ cards, one physical Thorp shuffle pairs corresponding cards in the two deck halves, independently orders each pair by a fair coin, and interleaves. Its law is $q_d$ on $S_N$; $U_N$ is uniform. A sweep uses each binary-coordinate matching once with independent fair switches, hence costs $d$ physical shuffles. Uniformly over starting decks and such lists, their image law at $256d$ physical steps has distance $o(1)$ from uniform on ordered injections $[k]\to V$ whenever $k\le7N/8$. At $1024d$ steps that distance is at most $N^{-3/2}$ for $k\le15N/16$ and sufficiently large $d$. The full-deck conclusion is \[ \|q_d^{*(2048d)}-U_N\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow0. \]

Number the remaining slots at its endpoints in binary order; their random assignment defines a conditional permutation law $\nu_{\mathcal E}$ on $S_{N/2}$. After $40000(400+1)$ sweeps, \[ \sup_{\text{initial layouts}}\mathbb E_{\mathcal E} \|\nu_{\mathcal E}-U_{N/2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\longrightarrow0. \] The full deck therefore converges to uniform after $16\,040\,400d$ physical shuffles. At every integer time $t\ge0$ its distance is at least $1-2^{tN/2}/N!$, giving mixing time $\Theta(d)$.

Let $X$ be a finite set, $G=S_X$, and $X=M_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup M_b$ a partition into nonempty blocks. Let $H_i=S_{M_i}$ fix the complement pointwise. Take a measure $f\ge0$ on $G$ with $f(G)\le1$. Arbitrary block sizes and restriction multiplicities are allowed.

Outline of the argument

The permanent inequality feeds a four-row moment recursion. Squared multiplicities are controlled through hook deficits, while encounter forests bound sparse stopping costs; summing these against representation dimensions and using independent forward sweeps gives full-law contraction. Entropy of compatible placements bounds regular representation moments and singular ranks. Conditional column exposures, inverse-color traces, and fractional-density interpolation give contraction through the different sparse and dense ranges, including asymmetric grids. Prescribed trajectories are assigned explicit costs in the representation calculation. Positive tensor densities and a hook-level moment induction establish a common perturbation interval, allowing analytic transfer from larger coordinate lines to the physical binary sweep. An adapted-noise argument tracks joint endpoint information against the revealed base trajectories. Predictable-domain and Walsh-coordinate estimates account for the cyclic schedule, and two-block autocorrelation plus Fourier transfer yields the stated $2048d$ full-deck bound.

A revealed switching environment defines a permutation kernel. Preparation and active intervals are truncated with controlled costs, then trace estimates give chunk contraction; a second route uses reset minorization and central relative increments to reach conditional uniformity. Central projections retain full Fourier matrices when information is taken over disjoint coset systems. Common trimming and branching degree counts bound the permitted coefficient spaces; keeping the observed partition random allows the partial information to control convolution globally. A conditional flow retains one sweep of memory and shows contraction of recent switching noise. Representation-degree estimates lift eight large marginals to the full group, and convolution comparison gives the total-variation bound; counting possible outputs gives the lower bound. Changing position frames makes online partial-card observations comparable to ordinary injection marginals. Representation-degree sums turn large-marginal control into full-law control, and independent shear comparisons strengthen the fixed-list estimate used by Fourier transfer.

Coarse smoothing handles dense representations, while a partial-deck deformation controls almost-full first rows. Angles between grid subgroup projections are tested on random subspaces, and a uniform moment induction sums their regular-trace contributions. Routing certificates and factorial moments control repeated irreducible copies. Tail smoothing treats all partial decks, while exact-injection contact cancellation and cycle traces give representation contraction that can be summed over dimensions. Signed representations on occupied cells are weighted by hook trimming. Row and column subgroup projections bound their Fourier operators, and Schatten interpolation combines dense contraction with separate sparse-representation estimates to control the whole permutation law. Signed tensor densities encode stabilizers and marked spin occurrences. Hook-entropy and marked-site trace bounds are combined with minimized tag and deletion budgets; inverse-density interpolation and paired diagram accounting control the complete Fourier sum.
