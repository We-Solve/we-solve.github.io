Question 5.2 — (Morita).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $g\geq3$, let $H=H_1(\Sigma_g;{\mathbb{Z}})$, let $\mathcal I_g$ be the Torelli group, and let $\mathcal K_g$ denote the Johnson kernel in the source's closed-surface convention. In the customary Johnson-homomorphism setting, \[1\longrightarrow\mathcal K_g\longrightarrow\mathcal I_g \xrightarrow{\pi}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge^3 H\right)/H\longrightarrow1,\] where the copy of $H$ uses the symplectic-form embedding $h\mapsto h\wedge\omega$. This identifies the standard meaning suggested by the excerpt's reference to exact sequence (11); that sequence and its normalization are not reproduced.

Morita's question is whether \[\forall g\geq3,\qquad H_1(\mathcal K_g;{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathcal K_g/[\mathcal K_g,\mathcal K_g]\text{ is finitely generated as an abelian group}.\] Equivalently, for each $g$ there should be finitely many elements whose images generate the entire abelianization. This is integral finite generation, not just finite-dimensional rational homology, and is a question about $\mathcal K_g$, not $H_2(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Q}})$.

The source reports large abelian quotients constructed by Birman--Craggs--Johnson [BC, Jo1] and Morita [Mo2]. It proposes a negative-answer strategy suggested by [BF], Theorem 5.1, following [McM]: use the action of $\mathcal K_g$ on $H_1(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})$ for a certain abelian cover $Y\to\Sigma_g$, compatible with its deck-group module structure, to obtain \[\rho:\mathcal K_g\to\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]),\] then analyze its image and the associated Bruhat--Tits--Serre tree. Neither the cover, representation nor valuation/tree is specified here. The suggested ingredient [GMV] is reported to give free quotients of arbitrarily high rank for $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}}[t])$ and $\operatorname{SL}_2(K[s,t])$ for any finite field $K$. That ambient-group report does not itself give such quotients of $\rho(\mathcal K_g)$.

The historical paragraph says that for $g\geq3$, $\mathcal I_g$ is finitely generated and $\mathcal K_g$ is not, and motivates studying $\pi^{-1}(L)$ for subgroups $L\leq(\bigwedge^3H)/H$. Those assertions about [BF] and $\mathcal K_g$ must be verified against the exact source and subsequent corrections before being treated as present theorems. They are preserved as reports, not adopted as proof of an answer. Even non-finite-generation of a group alone would not decide finite generation of its abelianization. The pullbacks interpolate between $\pi^{-1}(0)=\mathcal K_g$ and $\pi^{-1}((\bigwedge^3H)/H)=\mathcal I_g$; the excerpt does not state a specific theorem for all intermediate subgroups.

Short English statement

Is the integral abelianization of the Johnson kernel finitely generated in every genus at least three? The source's tree-action strategy and historical finite-generation assertions are retained with explicit verification caveats.
