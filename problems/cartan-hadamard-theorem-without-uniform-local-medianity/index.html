Math-4115

Cartan-Hadamard Theorem without Uniform Local Medianity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. For $a,b\in X$, its metric interval is \[ I_d(a,b)=\{u\in X:d(a,b)=d(a,u)+d(u,b)\}. \] A median of a triple $(a,b,c)\in X^3$ is a point in \[ I_d(a,b)\cap I_d(b,c)\cap I_d(c,a). \] The space is a median space if this intersection contains exactly one point for every triple in $X^3$. A path metric is a metric for which the distance between two points is the infimum of the lengths of continuous rectifiable paths joining them. Completeness means that every Cauchy sequence converges in $X$. Simply connected means path connected with every loop null-homotopic.

For a precise reading of the supplied local condition, give each neighborhood the restricted metric $d|_{U\times U}$. Under this reading, pointwise local medianity means \[ \forall p\in X\ \exists U_p\subseteq X\text{ open},\quad p\in U_p,\qquad \forall(a,b,c)\in U_p^3,\quad \#\left(U_p\cap I_d(a,b)\cap I_d(b,c)\cap I_d(c,a)\right)=1. \] Here the median must lie in $U_p$, and the distance equalities use the ambient metric $d$.

Question (supplied clean formulation). Suppose that $(X,d)$ is a complete, simply connected path-metric space satisfying this pointwise local median condition. Must \[ \forall(a,b,c)\in X^3,\qquad \#\left(I_d(a,b)\cap I_d(b,c)\cap I_d(c,a)\right)=1? \] No common positive lower bound on the sizes of the local neighborhoods is assumed; the question includes spaces for which no such uniform bound is available.

Unresolved qualification. The restricted-metric interpretation above makes the clean formulation precise, but the supplied excerpt does not establish that it is the intended convention. Using the intrinsic path metric of a neighborhood, or allowing a local triple's median to lie outside that neighborhood, gives a different local condition. The original excerpt asks to remove the word "uniform" from an antecedent statement that is not supplied. Consequently, the exact original uniform hypothesis and its relationship to this formulation require source review. The original field also appends separate group-theoretic questions, recorded in the notes; they are not hypotheses of this metric-space question.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether a complete, simply connected length space is globally median whenever each point has a neighborhood in which every triple has exactly one median, without a uniform neighborhood size. The precise meaning of a median within a neighborhood and the omitted antecedent of the original question require review. The supplied open status is a dated curation claim.
