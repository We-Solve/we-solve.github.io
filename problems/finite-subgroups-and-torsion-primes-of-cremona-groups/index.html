Finite Subgroups and Torsion Primes of Cremona Groups

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record leaves several essential conventions unspecified. The following gives a conditional formulation and identifies the unresolved parts; it does not supply missing hypotheses.

For a ground field $k$ and an integer $n\geq 1$, write $\mathbb A_k^n$ for affine $n$-space and use the standard notation \[ \operatorname{Aff}_n(k)=k^n\rtimes\operatorname{GL}_n(k),\qquad \operatorname{Cr}_n(k)=\operatorname{Bir}_k(\mathbb P_k^n). \] Here an element of $\operatorname{Aff}_n(k)$ acts by $x\mapsto Ax+b$, and $\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb A_k^n)$ denotes the group of regular $k$-automorphisms of affine space. Affine automorphisms are viewed as elements of the Cremona group through the standard affine chart. The source does not specify $k$, its characteristic, whether it is algebraically closed, or the admissible dimension range. Thus this notation does not select the intended ground-field hypotheses or settle whether dimension zero is included.

The notation $\operatorname{Aut}^*(\mathbb A^n)$ and the meaning of a torsion prime of these groups are not defined in the supplied record. Retain $\operatorname{Tors}(G)$ as the source's unspecified set of torsion primes. In particular, no identification with primes occurring as orders of group elements is made.

Subject to these unresolved conventions, the questions are as follows.

  1. For which admissible dimensions $n$ do there exist finite subgroups $H_1,H_2\leq\operatorname{Aff}_n(k)$ such that \[ H_1\cong H_2 \quad\text{and}\quad \nexists\,g\in\operatorname{Cr}_n(k)\text{ with }gH_1g^{-1}=H_2? \] The supplied wording does not settle whether the intended question concerns a prescribed dimension or existence in some dimension.
  2. For each admissible $n$, are all three sets \[ \operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Cr}_n(k)),\qquad \operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb A_k^n)),\qquad \operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Aut}^*(\mathbb A_k^n)) \] finite? The third expression retains the undefined starred notation from the source.
  3. Determine whether there is an admissible dimension $n$ for which \[ 7\in \operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Cr}_n(k)) \cup\operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb A_k^n)) \cup\operatorname{Tors}(\operatorname{Aut}^*(\mathbb A_k^n)), \] and, if so, determine the least such $n$. This follows the supplied wording "one of them"; the record does not explicitly request separate minima for the three families.
  4. Determine which analogous or modified Jordan-decomposition properties for connected reductive algebraic groups hold for these three groups. The record does not enumerate the properties to be tested or define the intended notions of semisimple and unipotent elements in these groups. Consequently, this part cannot yet be expressed as a definite collection of mathematical assertions.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether isomorphic finite affine transformation groups can fail to be conjugate by a Cremona transformation, whether three specified torsion-prime sets are finite, when the prime $7$ first occurs in their union, and which Jordan-decomposition properties extend to these groups. The ground field, torsion-prime convention, starred automorphism group, and precise Jordan-decomposition targets require clarification. The supplied status is partially solved; this is a curation label, not an independently verified conclusion.
