Multiplicity-Free Quantization and Orbit Counts

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns coadjoint orbits and quantization in a noncompact setting, but does not specify the admissible groups, subgroups, or quantization procedure. The following records its questions with these qualifications unresolved.

For the usual interpretation of the notation, let $G$ be the underlying Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and let $H$ be a Lie subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak h$. A coadjoint orbit $O_G\subseteq\mathfrak g^*$ is an orbit for the dual of the adjoint action of $G$; similarly, $O_H\subseteq\mathfrak h^*$ denotes an $H$-coadjoint orbit. The usual candidate for the map in the record is \[ \mu:O_G\longrightarrow\mathfrak h^*,\qquad \mu(\xi)=\xi|_{\mathfrak h}. \] The record does not explicitly define $\mu$, so this interpretation requires confirmation. With this interpretation, define \[ n(O_G,O_H):=\#\left(\mu^{-1}(O_H)/H\right), \] where the quotient denotes the set of $H$-orbits in $\mu^{-1}(O_H)$, and $\#$ denotes cardinality. Thus $n(O_G,O_H)\leq1$ means that this preimage is empty or consists of a single $H$-orbit; no finiteness assumption is imposed in defining the count.

Write $Q(O_G)$ for the representation assigned to $O_G$ by the intended quantization procedure, and $Q(O_G)|_H$ for its restriction to $H$. Where irreducible multiplicities are defined, multiplicity-free means that every such multiplicity is at most one, with the appropriate almost-everywhere interpretation if a direct-integral decomposition is intended. The record does not specify which representation category or decomposition convention applies.

For the intended class of data, the first question is whether \[ Q(O_G)|_H\text{ is multiplicity-free} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(\forall\,O_H\subseteq\mathfrak h^*\text{ an }H\text{-coadjoint orbit}\right)\; n(O_G,O_H)\leq1. \] The universal quantifier on the right ranges over every $H$-coadjoint orbit, as stated in the supplied record, without a supplied restriction to quantizable or integral orbits.

For a family $(O_G^\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$, write $\mu_\lambda$ for the corresponding map and \[ n(O_G^\lambda,O_H) :=\#\left(\mu_\lambda^{-1}(O_H)/H\right). \] The second question, reading multiplicity-freeness of the restrictions as a simultaneous hypothesis on the family, is whether \[ \begin{gathered} \bigl(\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\bigr)\; Q(O_G^\lambda)|_H\text{ is multiplicity-free} \\[2pt] \Longrightarrow \\[2pt] \bigl(\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\bigr) \bigl(\forall\,O_H\subseteq\mathfrak h^*\text{ an }H\text{-coadjoint orbit}\bigr)\; n(O_G^\lambda,O_H)\leq1. \end{gathered} \] Neither $\Lambda$, the construction of the family, nor the precise scope of the multiplicity-freeness hypothesis is supplied. Consequently, these are qualified formulations of the source questions, not an assertion that either statement holds for arbitrary Lie groups and quantizations.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether multiplicity-free restriction of a quantized coadjoint orbit is equivalent to every relevant inverse image containing at most one subgroup orbit, and whether multiplicity-free restrictions throughout a family force the same bound at every parameter. The source quantifies over all subgroup coadjoint orbits. Its group hypotheses, quantization, decomposition convention, and family parameters remain unspecified.
