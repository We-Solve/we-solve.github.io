Incompleteness and Self-Consistency Below Robinson Arithmetic

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $R$ denote the theory called Robinson's theory $R$ in the supplied record. Its axioms and language are not supplied, so its exact presentation remains to be confirmed. Fix a notion of interpretation, and write $A\preccurlyeq B$ when $B$ interprets $A$. Define strict interpretability by \[ A\lhd B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\preccurlyeq B\ \text{and}\ B\not\preccurlyeq A. \] The record does not specify the permitted interpretations or theory languages; the formulation below is conditional on fixing these conventions.

A theory is recursively axiomatizable if it admits a recursively enumerable set of axioms, and is consistent if it proves no contradiction. A consistent theory $S$ is incomplete if there is a sentence $\varphi$ of its language such that $S\nvdash\varphi$ and $S\nvdash\neg\varphi$. Define \[ \begin{aligned} G1(T)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad &\text{for every recursively axiomatizable consistent theory }S,\\ &T\preccurlyeq S\ \Longrightarrow\ S\text{ is incomplete}. \end{aligned} \] Let \[ \mathcal D=\{T:T\text{ is recursively axiomatizable and consistent},\ T\lhd R,\ G1(T)\}. \] The supplied corrected statement and dated assessment report that $\mathcal D\neq\varnothing$. This is supplied background, not an independently verified theorem here.

  1. Is $(\mathcal D,\lhd)$ well founded? Explicitly, does every nonempty subset $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal D$ contain a theory $T$ for which no $U\in\mathcal E$ satisfies $U\lhd T$? Equivalently, in the usual set-theoretic setting, is there no sequence $(T_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ in $\mathcal D$ with $T_{n+1}\lhd T_n$ for every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$?
  2. Does $G2$ hold for $R$, in the sense of the supplied corrected statement: \[ R\nvdash\operatorname{Con}(R)? \] Here $\operatorname{Con}(R)$ is intended to be an arithmetical sentence expressing that there is no $R$-proof of a contradiction. The effective presentation of $R$, proof coding, and internal consistency formula are not specified in the record; this question therefore requires those choices to be fixed before it is fully precise.

The original statement instead asks whether such a theory $T\lhd R$ exists and defines $G2$ using the opposite assertion, that a theory proves its own consistency. The supplied verification note says that the report uses nonprovability of consistency, records the existence result below $R$, and leaves well-foundedness and $G2$ open. The questions above follow that supplied correction while retaining this formulation conflict explicitly. The dated assessment's claim to answer the "first question" concerns the original existence question; it does not supply an answer to well-foundedness.

Short English statement

Consider recursively axiomatizable consistent theories strictly below Robinson's theory $R$ under interpretation, such that every recursively axiomatizable consistent theory interpreting them is incomplete. Ask whether this class is well founded under strict interpretation, and whether $R$ fails to prove its own consistency. The supplied record reports existence of such theories, but the interpretation conventions, presentation of $R$, and consistency sentence require clarification. Its original and corrected formulations disagree about the meaning of $G2$.
