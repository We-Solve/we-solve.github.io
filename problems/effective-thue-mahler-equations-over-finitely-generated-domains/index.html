Math-3456

Effective Thue–Mahler Equations over Finitely Generated Domains

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a commutative integral domain described in the source as an arbitrary finitely generated domain, and let \[ A^{\times}=\{u\in A:\text{there exists }v\in A\text{ with }uv=1\}. \] Let $F\in A[X,Y]$ be a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d\geq 3$, assumed in the source to have no multiple factors. Put \[ S_F(A)=\{(x,y)\in A^2:F(x,y)\in A^{\times}\}. \] For $(x,y),(x',y')\in S_F(A)$, define \[ (x,y)\sim(x',y') \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there exists }u\in A^{\times}\text{ such that }(x',y')=(ux,uy). \] This is the equivalence relation induced by simultaneous multiplication by a unit; homogeneity gives $F(ux,uy)=u^dF(x,y)$.

The supplied record states that $S_F(A)/{\sim}$ is finite. Taking this finiteness as a supplied premise, the question is whether, for every admissible input $A,F$, one can effectively determine a finite list \[ (x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_r,y_r)\in S_F(A),\qquad r\geq 0, \] such that \[ \forall(x,y)\in S_F(A)\ \exists! i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\ \exists u\in A^{\times}: (x,y)=(ux_i,uy_i). \] Thus the output must contain exactly one representative of each class, and must be empty if there are no solutions.

Unresolved qualifications. The record does not specify the base over which $A$ is finitely generated, its characteristic, or the effective presentation of $A$ and its elements. These choices are needed to make the algorithmic quantifier precise. For $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, the usual geometric interpretation of "without multiple factors" is that $F$ has no repeated linear factor over an algebraic closure of $K$; the supplied record does not explicitly distinguish this from factorization over $K$ or $A$. Consequently, this is a conditional statement of the supplied target, pending verification of its exact domain and factorization hypotheses; the asserted finiteness is not independently established here for every interpretation of an arbitrary finitely generated domain.

Short English statement

Given a binary form of degree at least three over a finitely generated domain, determine effectively one representative of every unit-scaling class of pairs whose form value is a unit, taking the supplied finiteness assertion as a premise. The domain hypotheses, meaning of repeated factors, and effective input representation require clarification. The supplied status is partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
