Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.81

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathfrak X$ be a class of finite groups. A subgroup is an $\mathfrak X$-subgroup if, as a group, it belongs to $\mathfrak X$. For a subset $S$ of a group, write $\langle S\rangle$ for the subgroup generated by $S$.

The question attributed to D. O. Revin asks whether there exists such a class $\mathfrak X$ satisfying all three conditions below.

  1. For every $H\in\mathfrak X$, every subgroup $K\le H$ belongs to $\mathfrak X$, and every homomorphic image of $H$ belongs to $\mathfrak X$.
  2. For every group $G$, without any finiteness assumption on $G$, and every pair of normal subgroups $N,M\trianglelefteq G$, \[ N,M\in\mathfrak X\quad\Longrightarrow\quad NM\in\mathfrak X, \qquad NM=\{nm:n\in N,\ m\in M\}. \] Here $N$ and $M$ are finite because $\mathfrak X$ consists of finite groups.
  3. For every integer $n\ge1$, there exist a finite group $G$ and an element $g\in G$ whose conjugacy class \[ D=g^G=\{x^{-1}gx:x\in G\} \] satisfies \[ \langle d_1,\ldots,d_n\rangle\in\mathfrak X \quad\text{for every }(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\in D^n, \qquad \langle D\rangle\notin\mathfrak X. \] The witnesses $G$ and $D$ may depend on $n$.

Qualification concerning condition (3). The displayed formulation interprets the source's phrase "any $n$ elements" as allowing repetitions, equivalently requiring the assertion for every nonempty subset of $D$ of cardinality at most $n$. The source does not explicitly specify whether the elements must be distinct. Under an exactly $n$ distinct elements interpretation, the corresponding requirement would instead be \[ \forall S\subseteq D,\quad |S|=n\ \Longrightarrow\ \langle S\rangle\in\mathfrak X, \] which is vacuous when $|D|<n$. The intended convention requires confirmation.

Supplied background qualification. The record reports that no class satisfying (1)--(3) can also be closed under extensions, attributing this result to D. O. Revin (2025). Here extension closure means that, for every finite group $E$ and every $K\trianglelefteq E$, \[ K\in\mathfrak X\ \text{ and }\ E/K\in\mathfrak X \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E\in\mathfrak X. \] This is a supplied literature assertion, not a result established or independently verified in this draft.

Short English statement

Does there exist a class of finite groups closed under subgroups, homomorphic images, and products of two normal members inside any group, but with the following property? For every positive integer $n$, some conjugacy class in a finite group has all subgroups generated by $n$ of its elements in the class, while the subgroup generated by the whole conjugacy class lies outside it. The meaning of "$n$ elements" needs confirmation as described above. The source labels the problem open and reports that extension closure rules out such a class.
