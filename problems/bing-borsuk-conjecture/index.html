Math-515

Bing–Borsuk conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a topological space $X$, homogeneity means that for every $x,y\in X$ there is a homeomorphism $h:X\to X$ with $h(x)=y$. Under the usual metrizable-space convention, $X$ is an absolute neighborhood retract (ANR) if whenever it is embedded as a closed subspace of a metrizable space $Y$, some neighborhood $U$ of $X$ in $Y$ admits a retraction $U\to X$. Let $\dim X=n<\infty$ denote covering dimension.

The Bing--Borsuk question in the supplied source is whether every $n$-dimensional homogeneous ANR $X$ is a topological $n$-manifold. With the usual boundaryless manifold convention, the conclusion means that $X$ is Hausdorff and second countable and every point has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. The source does not explicitly specify the ANR category, separability, local compactness, connectedness, or compactness; the precise standard formulation should be checked before treating these conventions as definitive. No compactness restriction is silently imposed, and no conclusion is substituted merely about homology manifolds.

The related modified conjecture, mentioned only in the research summary, asks whether every finite-dimensional homogeneous ANR is a homology manifold. Its local integral homology condition in dimension $n$ is \[ H_k(X,X\setminus\{x\};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong \begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},&k=n,\\0,&k\ne n,\end{cases} \qquad x\in X, \] with the ambient regularity and homology-manifold conventions still needing specification. This is a weaker related target, not the topological-manifold conclusion of the main question.

Short English statement

Must a finite-dimensional homogeneous absolute neighborhood retract be a topological manifold? The supplied assessment reports the conjecture open, known in dimensions at most two, and accompanied by substantial structural results. It separately reports a March 2018 counterexample announcement by Bryant and Ferry as unpublished; this is retained as an attributed announcement rather than an established disproof.
