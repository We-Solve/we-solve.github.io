Definitions and notation.
At a fixed scalar-wave frequency the spatial field satisfies a Helmholtz equation $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ away from the diffracting screen, with $k>0$. A diffraction problem includes an incident wave, boundary conditions on the screen and an outgoing radiation condition. Two finite slits introduce multiple edges and interactions absent from the single-slit setting.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Give a rigorous explicit solution of the fixed-frequency scalar-wave diffraction problem for two finite slits in the plane, including asymptotics of the solution.
Scope and source qualification.
The source does not fix slit geometry, screen boundary condition or incident field in its short formulation; these must be stated for any proposed solution. The Helmholtz description is explanatory context, not an invented choice of Dirichlet or Neumann target.

Find an explicit rigorous solution and asymptotic analysis for diffraction through two finite planar slits.

A specified aperture approximation.
Choose two parallel intervals of width $a>0$ centered at $\pm d/2$, with $d>a$, on an otherwise opaque planar screen. Prescribe a uniform aperture amplitude one and define its Fourier far-field approximation at transverse wave number $q$ by integrating over the two openings. Elementary integration yields \[F(q)=\int_{-d/2-a/2}^{-d/2+a/2}e^{-iqy}dy+\int_{d/2-a/2}^{d/2+a/2}e^{-iqy}dy =2a\,\frac{\sin(qa/2)}{qa/2}\cos(qd/2). \] At $q=0$, the continuous value is $2a$; the intensity is $4a^2\operatorname{sinc}^2(qa/2)\cos^2(qd/2)$, where $\operatorname{sinc}(z)=\sin z/z$ with value one at zero. This separates aperture-width zeros from interference zeros, except where they coincide.
The precise failure as an exact solution.
The uniform amplitude is an imposed aperture datum. In a Helmholtz screen problem the aperture trace is an unknown determined jointly with normal derivatives, edge singularities and the radiation condition. Summing two single-aperture responses also generally changes the field on the other aperture and cannot enforce both sets of boundary conditions independently. The Fourier calculation thus assumes the main quantity that an exact two-slit solution must determine.
Gap.
The first missing step is solving the coupled aperture boundary integral equation for a specified incident field and screen boundary condition, with enough edge control to justify far-field asymptotics. Neither the primary short source nor this test fixes a universal Dirichlet/Neumann choice. The displayed formula is a rigorous evaluation within an explicitly declared approximation, not an exact solution of the original diffraction problem or a theorem about all slit geometries.
