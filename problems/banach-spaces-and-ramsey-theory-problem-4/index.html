Banach Spaces and Ramsey Theory — Problem 4

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a Banach space over its scalar field $\mathbb K$. The supplied record does not specify whether $\mathbb K={\mathbb{R}}$ or $\mathbb K={\mathbb{C}}$; all scalar coefficients below belong to the same field as $X$. A sequence $(x_n)$ in $X$ is normalized and weakly null if $\|x_n\|=1$ for every $n$ and $f(x_n)\to0$ for every $f\in X^*$.

A normalized basic sequence $(e_i)$ in a Banach space $E$ is a spreading model generated by $(x_n)$ if, for every $k\geq1$, every $a_1,\ldots,a_k\in\mathbb K$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $N\geq1$ such that \[ \left|\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i x_{n_i}\right\|_X-\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\|_E\right|<\varepsilon \qquad\text{whenever }N\leq n_1<\cdots<n_k. \] Here a basic sequence means a Schauder basis for its closed linear span. Write $\mathrm{SP}_w(X)$ for the spreading models generated by normalized weakly null sequences in $X$, allowing passage to a subsequence before generation. Two basic sequences $(e_i)$ and $(f_i)$ are equivalent, written $(e_i)\sim(f_i)$, if there is $C\geq1$ such that, for every $k\geq1$ and every $a_1,\ldots,a_k\in\mathbb K$, \[ C^{-1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\| \leq \left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i f_i\right\| \leq C\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\|. \] For $c_0(\mathbb K)$, the space of scalar sequences tending to zero with the supremum norm, and $\ell_p(\mathbb K)$, the space of scalar sequences $(a_i)$ with $\sum_i|a_i|^p<\infty$, let $(u_i)$ denote the standard unit vector basis. Their finite-sum norms are respectively $\max_i|a_i|$ and $(\sum_i|a_i|^p)^{1/p}$.

Unique spreading model problem (S. A. Argyros, source label [AOST]). Is it true that, for every reflexive Banach space $X$ satisfying \[ \left|\mathrm{SP}_w(X)/\!\sim\right|=1, \] the unique equivalence class in $\mathrm{SP}_w(X)/\!\sim$ is represented by the standard unit vector basis of $c_0(\mathbb K)$ or of $\ell_p(\mathbb K)$ for some $1\leq p<\infty$? Equivalently, must every representative of that class be equivalent to one of those bases? The exponent $p$, when applicable, may depend on $X$.

The cardinality hypothesis requires exactly one class, including its existence. The interpretation above allows subsequence extraction; the record's informal assertion that all normalized weakly null sequences generate a unique model does not explicitly state this convention. The scalar-field scope and the unidentified reference [AOST] require source review.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether a reflexive Banach space with exactly one equivalence class of spreading models arising from normalized weakly null sequences must have that class equivalent to the usual basis of $c_0$ or $\ell_p$ for some $1\leq p<\infty$. The supplied record attributes the question to S. A. Argyros and labels it open; that status has not been independently verified.
