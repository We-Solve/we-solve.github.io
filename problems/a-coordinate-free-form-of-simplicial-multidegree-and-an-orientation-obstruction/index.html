Math-1358

A coordinate-free form of simplicial multidegree and an orientation obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The original asks how Baker--Norine's graphical Riemann--Roch theorem might extend to cell complexes, possibly through higher-dimensional critical or superstable configurations and higher-dimensional algebraic Riemann--Roch ideas. It does not specify a higher-dimensional divisor rank, degree, effective cone, canonical class, or target formula. The supplied partial result compares a published simplicial multidegree with a coordinate-free lattice quotient; it does not supply a full higher-dimensional Riemann--Roch theorem.

Fix a finite oriented cell complex and degree $i\geq1$, with integral cellular boundary maps $\partial_j:C_j\to C_{j-1}$. Give cellular bases their standard integral inner products. Write \[ L=L_i^{\mathrm{ud}}=\partial_{i+1}\partial_{i+1}^{\mathsf T},\qquad M=\ker_{\mathbb{Z}} L=\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}\partial_{i+1}^{\mathsf T},\qquad K_i=\ker_{\mathbb{Z}}\partial_i/\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb{Z}} L. \] Let $T(K_i)$ denote the torsion subgroup, not the full group when rational homology is nonzero. Pairing with $M$ defines \[ \delta:\operatorname{coker}L\to\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad \delta([c])(z)=\langle c,z\rangle. \] The aid's exact lattice statement is \[ 0\longrightarrow T(K_i)\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}L \xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow0. \] The kernel lattice $M$ is saturated, which makes the pairing map surjective. If $H_i(X;{\mathbb{Q}})=0$, then $K_i$ is finite and $T(K_i)=K_i$. Without that hypothesis the full critical quotient must not replace its torsion subgroup in the sequence.

For the Kim--Perkinson comparison, let $\mathcal H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_s\}\subset M$ be the Hilbert-basis family in their multidegree construction. Its asserted factorization is \[ \deg_{\mathrm{KP}}=j_{\mathcal H}\circ\delta,\qquad j_{\mathcal H}(\varphi)=(\varphi(h_1),\ldots,\varphi(h_s)). \] For the reported injective coordinate embedding it is necessary that the $h_j$ span $M\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}$, and under that explicit condition it is sufficient. The aid reports this injectivity for its intended construction, so its realizable degree lattice is $j_{\mathcal H}(\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}))$, canonically identified with $\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})$, not necessarily the whole coordinate lattice ${\mathbb{Z}}^s$. In this interpretation the kernel assertion of their Theorem 13 is the torsion kernel in the displayed exact sequence. The source summary does not define the Hilbert-basis cone, its orientation assumptions, or the spanning verification; these are indispensable reference details rather than a property of an arbitrary unspecified Hilbert basis.

The independent orientation obstruction concerns effectiveness. If an additive submonoid $\mathcal E\subset C_i\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^N$ contains every positive cellular basis vector and is invariant under independently reversing each coordinate orientation, then it contains both signs of every basis vector and hence $\mathcal E={\mathbb{Z}}^N$. Thus a fixed coordinatewise nonnegative effective cone cannot remain unchanged under all independent higher-cell orientation reversals without becoming the entire chain lattice. This does not forbid covariantly changing the cone with the orientation, or other definitions of effectiveness omitting these hypotheses. The coordinate-free degree comparison and this no-go lemma do not construct higher superstable objects or the missing Riemann--Roch rank theory.

Short English statement

The supplied partial result identifies a realizable simplicial multidegree lattice with the integral dual of the up-Laplacian kernel and isolates the torsion critical-group kernel. Its comparison needs the actual Hilbert-basis spanning hypotheses. A separate orientation lemma explains why an unchanged coordinatewise effective cone cannot be invariant under all cell reversals, leaving the full Riemann--Roch extension unresolved.
