Nonexistence of Geproci Sets in Linear General Position

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For terminology only, let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and let $Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$ be a finite reduced set of points. The set $Z$ is in linear general position if every subset of $r\leq n+1$ points spans a projective subspace of dimension $r-1$.

Under the usual spatial convention, a finite reduced set $Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3_k$ is geproci (general projection complete intersection) if there are positive integers $a,b$ and a nonempty Zariski open set of projection centers $p\in{\mathbb{P}}^3_k\setminus Z$ such that projection from each such $p$ is injective on $Z$, and its image in ${\mathbb{P}}^2_k$ is the scheme-theoretic complete intersection of two plane curves of degrees $a,b$ having no common component. In particular, $|Z|=ab$. This definition supplies a conventional meaning of the terminology; the supplied excerpt does not establish that this ambient dimension or these field hypotheses are the intended ones.

The source question is: "Is it true that such point configurations are never geproci?" If $\mathcal C$ denotes the class referred to by "such point configurations," its logical form is \[ \text{Is it true that }\quad \forall Z\in\mathcal C,\qquad Z\text{ is not geproci}? \] The antecedent defining $\mathcal C$ is missing. The supplied title and dated assessment associate the question with configurations in linear general position and with nontrivial examples having more than four points. They do not recover the ambient projective space, the field and characteristic, the exact meaning of "nontrivial," or all restrictions on the configurations or complete-intersection degrees. Thus they do not determine a precise class $\mathcal C$, and no unconditional quantified nonexistence conjecture can be recovered from this record.

The supplied registry marks the statement as unrecoverable. Its verification note describes it as a narrower duplicate of the geproci classification questions retained in OWR-11695864-001, and explicitly declines to promote a separate clean statement. That other record is not supplied here. The reciprocal-plane and Orlik--Terao passage following the question concerns a different topic and does not supply the missing antecedent.

Short English statement

The record asks whether a context-dependent class of point configurations can ever be geproci. Its title and supplied assessment suggest linear general position and nontrivial examples with more than four points, but the defining context is missing. The conventional terminology is explained above; the intended conjecture remains unrecovered and requires human mathematical review.
