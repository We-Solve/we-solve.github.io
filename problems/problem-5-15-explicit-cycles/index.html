Problem 5.15 — (Explicit cycles).

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal I_g$ and $\mathcal K_g$ be the source's Torelli group and subgroup $\mathcal K_g$. In the usual closed oriented surface setup $\mathcal I_g$ acts trivially on integral first homology, while $\mathcal K_g$ denotes the Johnson kernel, conventionally generated by separating twists in the appropriate genus range. The excerpt does not supply that definition, its genus hypotheses, or its surface convention.

Problem 5.15 (explicit cycles). Explicitly construct infinitely many linearly independent rational homology classes represented by cycles in each of \[ H_*(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Q}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_*(\mathcal K_g;{\mathbb{Q}}). \] For instance, in an explicitly specified chain model, the requested data are cycles $z_i$ with $\partial z_i=0$ such that for every finite rational combination, \[ \sum_{i=1}^{r}q_i[z_i]=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_1=\cdots=q_r=0. \] This requires independence of homology classes, not merely independence of chains. Whether genus is fixed, which genera are intended, and whether all cycles must lie in one degree are unstated; the homological reading follows the word "cycles" and the torsion-bearing degree-one context below. The source emphasizes the difficulty of finding even explicit nonzero cycles.

The following discussion reports Johnson's calculation, citing [Jo1] as a summary, \[ H_1(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong \left(\textstyle\bigwedge^3 H\right)/H\ \oplus B_2, \tag{16} \] where the source says $B_2$ consists of $2$-torsion. The usual interpretation is $H=H_1(S_g;{\mathbb{Z}})$, with the quotient using the symplectic embedding of $H$ in $\bigwedge^3H$, but the excerpt does not define this embedding, $B_2$, or the valid genus range. In particular, this cannot be a literal $H^1(-;{\mathbb{Z}})$ decomposition with torsion. The source contrasts an algebraic exterior-power part with the topological $B_2$ contribution from the Rochlin invariant [BC, Jo1]; the torsion contribution vanishes with rational coefficients and so does not itself furnish the requested rational families. It also makes an analogy with $H_1$ of the Torelli group for $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$, saying an algebraic part occurs there but the Rochlin part does not. This is retained as a qualitative source comparison, not an asserted identical module formula.

Short English statement

Construct explicit infinite families of independent rational homology classes for both the Torelli group and $\mathcal K_g$. The source's integral first-homology formula and Rochlin-derived torsion are context; neither fixes the degrees of the desired families nor supplies rational classes from the torsion term.
