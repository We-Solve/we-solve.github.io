Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A matroid consists of a finite ground set with a hereditary independence family satisfying exchange. It is strongly base orderable if for every two bases $B_1,B_2$ there is a bijection $\phi:B_1\to B_2$ such that for every $X\subseteq B_1$, both $(B_1\setminus X)\cup\phi(X)$ and $(B_2\setminus\phi(X))\cup X$ are bases. Its base polytope is the convex hull of base incidence vectors. The Integer Caratheodory Property requires that for every positive integer $k$ and integer $w\in kP$, one can write $w=\sum_i n_ix_i$, where the $x_i$ are affinely independent integer points of $P$, the $n_i$ are nonnegative integers, and $\sum_i n_i=k$.

Research attempt: Three separate decomposition routes for a composite source record

A common-base subclass: bipartite perfect matchings
Let the ground set be the allowed edges of a bipartite graph with equally many left and right vertices, with at least one vertex on each side. The empty graph instead has the singleton empty-vector polytope and its Integer Caratheodory Property is immediate. One partition matroid allows at most one edge at each left vertex, and the other at most one at each right vertex. Assume a perfect matching exists. Each matroid is strongly base orderable: for two bases, biject the unique chosen edges within each vertex block; swapping any subset of these matched pairs leaves exactly one edge in every block, in both bases. Their base polytopes are products of unit simplices, because a vector of nonnegative coordinates with block sums one is the convex average of choices, obtained by independently choosing one edge per block with those probabilities. Hence their intersection consists of nonnegative edge vectors with all row and column sums one.

Let $w$ be an integer vector in its $k$-fold dilation, $k\geq1$. Its positive-support bipartite graph has a perfect matching: for a set $U$ of left vertices, the total incident weight is $k|U|$, all lying on its neighboring right vertices, whose total available column weight is at most $k|N(U)|$. Thus $|N(U)|\geq|U|$. To recall the matching implication, a maximum matching failing to cover a left vertex yields an alternating-path search. With no augmenting path, all reached right vertices are matched to reached left vertices other than the initial unmatched one, and all neighbors of reached left vertices are reached. This would give a set with fewer neighbors than vertices, contradicting the inequality.

Choose a perfect matching incidence vector $x$ in the support and let $\lambda=\min_{e:x_e=1}w_e$. This is a positive integer. Subtraction leaves a nonnegative integer vector with every row and column sum $k-\lambda$. Repeat until the residual is zero. The positive multiplicities sum to $k$, and the original $w$ is their weighted sum of matching incidence vectors.

The chosen vectors are affinely independent, an assertion stronger than merely having an integer decomposition. At each step select an edge where the displayed minimum is attained; that edge occurs in the current matching and has zero weight in every later residual, so it occurs in no later chosen matching. In a linear dependence among the chosen vectors, take the earliest nonzero coefficient. Its selected edge has contribution only from that vector and earlier vectors; the earlier coefficients are zero and later vectors omit the edge. That coefficient must therefore be zero, a contradiction. Thus the vectors are even linearly independent, and in particular affinely independent. This proves the Integer Caratheodory Property for this common-base subclass.

An affine-independent integer decomposition of a product of simplices
Let $k\geq1$ be an integer. Consider nonnegative integer coordinates $w_{b,j}$ divided into finitely many nonempty blocks $b$, each satisfying $\sum_jw_{b,j}=k$. Form the cumulative sums $s_{b,j}=\sum_{i\leq j}w_{b,i}$ and sort all their distinct values together with zero and $k$: \[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_h=k. \] For each interval $(t_{\ell-1},t_\ell]$, choose in each block the coordinate whose cumulative interval contains it, and let $v_\ell$ be the corresponding zero-one vector with one chosen coordinate per block. This is unambiguous even with zero coordinates, because a nonempty interval lies inside exactly one positive-length cumulative interval per block. Assign multiplicity $n_\ell=t_\ell-t_{\ell-1}$. Lengths of the relevant intervals add to $w_{b,j}$, so \[ w=\sum_{\ell=1}^h n_\ell v_\ell,\qquad n_\ell\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad\sum_\ell n_\ell=k. \] To prove affine independence, suppose $\sum_\ell c_\ell v_\ell=0$ and $\sum_\ell c_\ell=0$. Each internal breakpoint $t_j$ is a cumulative sum in some block. The linear functional summing the corresponding initial coordinates of that block is one on $v_1,\ldots,v_j$ and zero on all later vectors. Applying it gives $\sum_{\ell\leq j}c_\ell=0$ for each $j<h$. Taking consecutive differences and using the total coefficient sum proves every $c_\ell=0$.
Application to acyclic directed arborescences
Let a directed acyclic graph have a first vertex $r$ in a topological order, no arcs entering $r$, and at least one incoming arc at every other vertex. Choose one incoming arc for each nonroot vertex. Following parent arcs backwards strictly decreases the order and must end at $r$, since every other vertex has a parent. Thus the chosen arcs connect every vertex to $r$ and form an outward rooted arborescence. Conversely an arborescence makes exactly these choices. The polytope is therefore a product of the incoming-arc simplices: the convex-hull equality follows by independently sampling each incoming choice from its specified probability vector. The construction above proves its Integer Caratheodory Property for every integer dilation. The graph with only $r$ has the singleton empty-vector polytope and has the property trivially.
An arbitrary tree can have a fractional maximal subtraction
Let $P$ be the spanning-tree polytope of $K_4$, $k=2$, and $w_e=1$ on all six edges. Two complementary Hamilton paths are spanning trees, so $w\in2P$. Choose the star $x$ at one vertex. On the remaining three vertices, every spanning tree has at most two internal edges. The star has none there, so a residual in $(2-\lambda)P$ must satisfy $3\leq2(2-\lambda)$, giving $\lambda\leq1/2$. At $\lambda=1/2$, the normalized residual has values $1/3$ on the three spokes and $2/3$ on the leaf-triangle edges. Average the nine trees formed by one of the three spokes and any two of the three leaf-triangle edges. Every such graph is a tree, and each spoke appears in three of them while each triangle edge appears in six. This proves feasibility and hence maximal value $1/2$ for the star. In contrast, choosing one of the Hamilton paths permits $\lambda=1$, since its complement is a tree; a chosen edge has weight one, so nonnegativity prevents any larger removal with nonnegative remaining scale. For $\lambda>2$, an unchosen edge would remain positive whereas a negative dilation of this nonnegative polytope has nonpositive coordinates, so no extra feasible values occur in this example.

Thus "choose any spanning tree and remove its largest multiple" is not a valid integer greedy rule. The original question asks for existence of a suitable tree, and this instance has such a tree; it is not a counterexample to that question.
