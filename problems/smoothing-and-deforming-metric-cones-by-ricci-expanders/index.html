Smoothing and Deforming Metric Cones by Ricci Expanders

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth Riemannian manifold $(\Sigma,h)$, its Riemannian cone away from the vertex is \[ C(\Sigma)\setminus\{o\}=(0,\infty)\times\Sigma, \qquad g_C={\,\mathrm{d}} r^2+r^2h. \] The vertex is supplied by metric completion when appropriate. This notation describes the smooth-link case; the source does not specify whether only such cones are intended, nor does it specify dimension, compactness of the link, boundary conditions, or permissible singularities.

A gradient Ricci expander is a smooth Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ with a smooth function $f\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{Ric}_g+\nabla_g^2f=\lambda g, \qquad \lambda<0. \] Here $\operatorname{Ric}_g$ is the Ricci tensor and $\nabla_g^2f$ is the Hessian. This uses the potential convention in which expansion corresponds to a negative soliton constant.

The supplied problem asks:

  1. Which metric cones admit a smoothing by a gradient Ricci expander asymptotic to the cone?
  2. Given a conical gradient Ricci expander, can it be deformed by an implicit-function construction, including under suitable variations of its asymptotic cone?
  3. Are the resulting deformations globally unique?
The interpretation of smoothing through an asymptotic cone is the geometric framework suggested by the wording; the source does not define it. In particular, it supplies no completeness requirement, convergence topology or decay rate, curvature hypotheses, deformation spaces, or equivalence relation for uniqueness. It also does not specify whether global uniqueness concerns all expanders with a prescribed cone or continuation of a local deformation family. Thus these are qualified research questions, and the supplied evidence does not determine a single fully quantified existence, deformation, or uniqueness assertion.

Short English statement

Determine which metric cones admit gradient Ricci expander smoothings, whether conical expanders admit implicit deformations, and whether those deformations are globally unique. The source leaves the admissible cones, asymptotic conditions, deformation spaces, and meaning of global uniqueness unspecified; these require mathematical review.
