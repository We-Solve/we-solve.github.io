Math-15249

Linkage and Vanishing in Kato–Milne Cohomology

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p$. For the standard interpretation of Kato--Milne cohomology, write $\Omega_F^r=\bigwedge_F^r\Omega^1_{F/\mathbb F_p}$, with $\Omega_F^0=F$, and let $\mathrm d$ denote the exterior derivative. For $n\geq 1$, set \[ H_p^n(F)=\operatorname{coker}\!\left(\wp:\Omega_F^{n-1}\longrightarrow \Omega_F^{n-1}/\mathrm d\Omega_F^{n-2}\right), \] where $\Omega_F^{-1}=0$ and the additive map $\wp$ is specified on logarithmic differential expressions by \[ \wp\!\left(a\,\frac{\mathrm d b_1}{b_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\mathrm d b_{n-1}}{b_{n-1}}\right) = (a^p-a)\,\frac{\mathrm d b_1}{b_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\mathrm d b_{n-1}}{b_{n-1}} \pmod{\mathrm d\Omega_F^{n-2}}, \] for $a\in F$ and $b_i\in F^\times$. The classes of these logarithmic differential expressions are the generating differential symbols. For $n=1$, the empty wedge is $1$, so $H_p^1(F)=F/\{a^p-a:a\in F\}$.

The supplied record asks whether the following implications hold for every field $F$ of characteristic $p$ and every degree $n$ in the intended range: \[ \begin{aligned} H_p^n(F)\text{ is linked}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_p^{n+2}(F)=0,\\ H_p^n(F)\text{ is triple linked}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_p^{n+1}(F)=0. \end{aligned} \] Here vanishing means that the indicated cohomology group is the zero group. These are two separate questions.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The supplied record says that linkage and triple linkage are defined through generating differential symbols, but it does not supply either definition. In particular, it does not specify the common factors, permitted symbol presentations, or quantifiers required for linkage. Accordingly, the two displayed implications retain the source's predicates and are schematic targets pending recovery of those definitions; no particular linkage convention is imposed here. The record also does not specify the intended range of $n$, including whether degree $1$ is included. The standard cohomology notation above does not resolve that omission.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether linkage of generating symbols in degree $n$ forces Kato--Milne cohomology to vanish in degree $n+2$, and whether triple linkage forces vanishing already in degree $n+1$. The precise linkage definitions and intended degree range are absent from the supplied record, so a fully specified formulation requires human review.
