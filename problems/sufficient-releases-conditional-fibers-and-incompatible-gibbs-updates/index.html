Attempt and finite models

The composite source does not fix one sampling model, disclosure rule, MCMC target or hidden-variable graph. We analyze two precise finite questions within it: whether a statistic chooses its conditional target, and whether perturbed full conditionals necessarily share a joint law.
The statistic alone does not determine a conditional target.
Let $\Omega$ be any finite sample space and $S:\Omega\to\mathcal S$. Choose any positive weight $h(x)$ and functions $g_\theta(s)>0$, and normalize $p_\theta(x)=h(x)g_\theta(S(x))/Z_\theta$. For each nonempty fiber, \[ \Pr_\theta(X=x\mid S=s) =\frac{h(x)}{\sum_{y:S(y)=s}h(y)}, \] because $g_\theta(s)$ and $Z_\theta$ cancel. Hence $S$ is sufficient, yet different choices of $h$ give different conditional targets with the same statistic. On a two-point fiber, $h=(1,2)$ gives probabilities $(1/3,2/3)$, whereas equal weights give the uniform target. Thus specifying margins or conditional-frequency summaries does not by itself determine a unique stationary law. A sampling family is needed.
Two incompatible positive binary full conditionals.
Let $X,Y\in\{0,1\}$ and prescribe \[ \Pr(X=1\mid Y=0)=\Pr(X=1\mid Y=1)=1/2, \quad\Pr(Y=1\mid X=0)=1/4, \quad\Pr(Y=1\mid X=1)=3/4. \] The first pair would force $X$ independent of $Y$ in any positive joint law: $p(1,y)=p(0,y)$ for each $y$. Such a law has identical $Y$ conditionals for the two $X$ values, contradicting the latter pair. So all the individual conditional probabilities are valid and positive, but no joint distribution has them simultaneously.
Nevertheless each scan rule has a stationary law.
With probability $\alpha\in(0,1)$ update $X$ from the first kernel, and otherwise update $Y$ from the second. Any stationary law must have $\Pr(X=1)=1/2$: the $Y$ update leaves this marginal fixed and the $X$ update sets it to $1/2$. Its $Y$ marginal must then also be $1/2$, since the other update sets that marginal to $(1/2)(1/4)+(1/2)(3/4)=1/2$. Put $c=\mathbb E[(2X-1)(2Y-1)]$. After an $X$ update this is zero. After a $Y$ update it is $1/2$, since $\mathbb E[2Y-1\mid X]=(2X-1)/2$. Stationarity requires $c=(1-\alpha)/2$. Marginals and this correlation determine all four probabilities uniquely: \[ \pi_{00}=\pi_{11}=(3-\alpha)/8, \qquad\pi_{01}=\pi_{10}=(1+\alpha)/8. \] They are positive and sum to one. The update calculations also show they reproduce their own two marginals and correlation after one step; those three numbers determine a binary joint law, so this proves stationarity directly, without an external Markov-chain theorem. The target changes with $\alpha$. Therefore the attempted inference "positive perturbed full conditionals define an intrinsic Gibbs target" is false. This example does not quantify disclosure safety.
