Math-3487

Shellability of Barycentric Subdivisions of Convex Complexes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A finite geometric simplicial complex $C$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a finite collection of Euclidean simplices, closed under taking faces, such that the intersection of any two simplices is empty or a common face. Its underlying space $|C|$ is the union of its simplices. Under the geometric interpretation of "convex," $|C|$ is convex: for every $x,y\in |C|$ and every $t\in[0,1]$, one has $(1-t)x+ty\in |C|$.

The barycentric subdivision $\operatorname{sd} C$ has a vertex $b_\sigma$, the barycenter of $\sigma$, for each nonempty face $\sigma$ of $C$. Its nonempty simplices correspond to strictly increasing chains \[ \sigma_0\subsetneq\sigma_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\sigma_k \] of nonempty faces of $C$, with associated vertices $b_{\sigma_0},\ldots,b_{\sigma_k}$.

A facet is an inclusion-maximal face. A finite simplicial complex is pure of dimension $d$ if every facet has dimension $d$. For $d\geq1$, a finite pure $d$-dimensional complex $K$ is shellable if its facets admit an ordering $F_1,\ldots,F_m$ such that, for every $j\in\{2,\ldots,m\}$, the subcomplex \[ \langle F_j\rangle\cap\bigcup_{i<j}\langle F_i\rangle \] is nonempty and pure of dimension $d-1$. Here $\langle F\rangle$ denotes the simplex $F$ together with all its faces. A nonempty zero-dimensional complex is shellable by convention.

Question, under the finite geometric interpretation. For every integer $n\geq0$ and every nonempty finite geometric simplicial complex $C$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ whose underlying space is convex, is $\operatorname{sd} C$ shellable? The dimension is the intrinsic dimension of $|C|$; full dimension in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is not required. Under this interpretation, $C$ is pure. The question concerns the first barycentric subdivision of the entire complex, including its boundary faces.

Short English statement

Does taking the first barycentric subdivision make every finite simplicial triangulation of a nonempty convex set shellable? This expresses the supplied question under an explicit geometric interpretation of "convex simplicial complex"; the source's intended conventions still require confirmation.
