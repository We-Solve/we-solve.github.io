Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A smooth Deligne--Mumford stack is a geometric object locally presented by smooth spaces with finite stabilizer groups. A quotient presentation $[X/G]$ specifies a space and a group action; distinct presentations may describe the same stack. The report associates a gauged sigma model to physically realizable presentations. A deformation changes the background or couplings infinitesimally; a massless spectrum consists of zero-mass physical excitations. The inertia stack records pairs of a point and an automorphism of that point and enters the proposed cohomological spectrum calculation.
Source scope and qualifications.
The original registry excerpt also includes infrared agreement of different quotient presentations, separately catalogued at ID 30000363. Both aspects of the excerpt are retained by this note. Ordinary stack deformation theory can fail to match the physical deformations, as the report's C^2/Z_2 example demonstrates.

The smallest twisted-sector calculation.
Let $X=\mathbb C^2$ and let $G=\{1,g\}$ act by $g(z_1,z_2)=(-z_1,-z_2)$. The inertia stack consists of points together with stabilizer elements. Since $G$ is abelian, its components are \[ I[X/G]=[X/G]\ \sqcup\ [\{0\}/G]. \] Indeed the identity fixes all of $X$, whereas solving $gz=z$ gives $2z=0$ and hence $z=0$ over $\mathbb C$. The second component is missed if one only keeps invariant differential forms on the original $X$.
Degree bookkeeping.
Each eigenvalue of $g$ on the tangent space at zero is $-1=e^{2\pi i(1/2)}$. The sum of the fractional exponents is one. If a proposed sector-cohomology prescription shifts real cohomological degree by twice this sum, its constant twisted-sector class sits in degree two. This statement is just the calculation within that explicitly specified prescription. It does not prove that the class is a normalizable massless state in the noncompact physical theory, nor that it integrates to a deformation.
Failure of the naive identification.
A deformation prescription using only the untwisted component cannot even represent this extra candidate sector. Conversely, adding inertia cohomology is not a proof of agreement with the physical spectrum: normalizability, projections, interactions, and obstruction equations must be supplied. The source's additional presentation-independence question, separately numbered 30000363, also remains open here; equality of this elementary stack invariant would not identify infrared theories. The first gap is constructing the physical state complex and a map from the candidate twisted class into its massless cohomology.
