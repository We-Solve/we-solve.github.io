Math-4225

Maximum-to-Average Independence Ratios in Triangle-Free Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the formulation below, let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite, nonempty, simple undirected graph. These graph conventions are needed to make the stated ratio precise but are not explicitly specified in the supplied record. Say that $G$ is triangle-free if no three distinct vertices are pairwise adjacent. Define \[ \mathcal I(G)=\{S\subseteq V: \text{no edge of }G\text{ has both endpoints in }S\}, \qquad \alpha(G)=\max_{S\in\mathcal I(G)}|S|. \] Here $\mathcal I(G)$ includes the empty independent set. If $I$ is chosen uniformly from $\mathcal I(G)$, define \[ m(G)=\mathbb E|I|=\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal I(G)}|S|}{|\mathcal I(G)|}, \qquad R(G)=\frac{\alpha(G)}{m(G)}. \] Since $V\ne\varnothing$, one has $m(G)>0$. Let $\delta(G)=\min_{v\in V}\deg_G(v)$.

The supplied record asks the following two questions:

  1. Does every such triangle-free graph satisfy \[ R(G)\ge \frac43? \]
  2. Does there exist a function $\varepsilon:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to[0,\infty)$, with $\lim_{d\to\infty}\varepsilon(d)=0$, such that, for every integer $d\ge0$ and every such triangle-free graph $G$ with $\delta(G)=d$, \[ R(G)\ge 2-\varepsilon(d)? \]
The second formulation interprets the source notation $2-o_d(1)$ as a bound uniform over all graphs of minimum degree $d$. The finite simple graph convention, exclusion of the graph with no vertices, inclusion of the empty independent set in the averaging family, and uniform interpretation of the asymptotic term require confirmation against the original source.

Short English statement

The questions compare the largest independent set in a triangle-free graph with the mean size of an independent set chosen uniformly from all independent sets. They ask whether this ratio is always at least $4/3$, and whether it is at least $2-\varepsilon(d)$, uniformly over graphs of minimum degree $d$, for some $\varepsilon(d)\to0$. The graph and averaging conventions are stated explicitly and require source confirmation.
