Matveev–Jaco–Rubinstein Lens-Space Complexity Conjecture

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record names the following the Matveev--Jaco--Rubinstein lens-space complexity conjecture. To make its statement precise, this draft uses Matveev complexity and regular continued fractions, as defined below. These conventions are customary interpretations of the terminology; the supplied record does not explicitly define them, so they require confirmation against the cited source.

Let $p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ satisfy \[ p>q>0,\qquad p>3,\qquad \gcd(p,q)=1. \] Using a standard lens-space convention, let \[ S^3=\{(z_1,z_2)\in{\mathbb{C}}^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1\}, \qquad \zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p), \] and define $L(p,q)$ to be the quotient of $S^3$ by the cyclic action generated by \[ (z_1,z_2)\longmapsto(\zeta z_1,\zeta^q z_2). \] The source does not specify its lens-space convention or whether orientation is retained; the complexity used here concerns the underlying PL manifold.

For a closed connected PL $3$-manifold $M$, a spine is a compact polyhedron $P$ of dimension at most $2$ onto which $M\setminus\operatorname{int}(B^3)$ collapses, where $B^3\subset M$ is an embedded PL ball. Such a spine is almost simple if the link of every point of $P$ embeds in the complete graph $K_4$, equivalently the $1$-skeleton of a tetrahedron. A true vertex is a point whose link is homeomorphic to $K_4$. Define the Matveev complexity $c(M)$ as the minimum number of true vertices among all almost simple spines of $M$.

Write the finite regular continued fraction of $p/q$ as \[ \frac pq=[a_0;a_1,\ldots,a_n] =a_0+\cfrac{1}{a_1+\cfrac{1}{\ddots+\cfrac{1}{a_n}}}, \] where $n\geq0$, $a_0\geq1$, $a_i\geq1$ for $1\leq i\leq n$, and $a_n\geq2$ when $n\geq1$. When $n=0$, the expression means $[a_0]=a_0$. Set \[ E(p,q)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_i. \] Here the source's phrase "partial denominators" is interpreted as all the coefficients in this regular continued fraction, including $a_0$. The source does not explicitly distinguish regular continued fractions from other continued-fraction conventions.

The conjectured statement, under these conventions, is \[ \text{for every }p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ with }p>q>0,\ p>3,\ \gcd(p,q)=1, \qquad c\left(L(p,q)\right)=E(p,q)-3. \] The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports results for infinite families and many small cases. Those are supplied literature-status claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

For every coprime pair of integers $p>q>0$ with $p>3$, the conjecture asserts that the Matveev complexity of $L(p,q)$ equals the sum of the coefficients of the regular continued fraction of $p/q$, minus three. The precise complexity, lens-space, and continued-fraction conventions require source confirmation. The supplied status is partially solved.
