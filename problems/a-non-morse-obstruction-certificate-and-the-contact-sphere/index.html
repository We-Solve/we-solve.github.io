Math-1480

A non-Morse obstruction certificate and the contact sphere

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

E. Lerman's Question $5.8$ asks for a proof of Kirwan surjectivity without Morse theory. In the usual compact Hamiltonian setting, with compact acting group $G$, moment map $\mu$, and an applicable regular level, the target map is restriction \[ H_G^*(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\longrightarrow H_G^*(\mu^{-1}(0);{\mathbb{Q}}), \] with quotient interpretation when its hypotheses permit. The source does not restate a complete version of the symplectic theorem. It motivates an alternative method by [CDM88]'s non-Morse connectedness and convexity proofs and their usefulness for contact moment maps or symplectic cones. It notes the failure of the usual critical-point/isotropy and properness ingredients there. This motivation does not assert that ordinary contact Kirwan surjectivity is always true.

The aid's non-Morse extension criterion uses only equivariant Mayer--Vietoris. Let $Z\subset X$ be invariant, choose invariant open sets $U,V$ with $U\cup V=X$, and assume $U$ equivariantly deformation retracts to $Z$. Write $W=U\cap V$. For $c\in H_G^k(Z;R)$, let $a\in H_G^k(U;R)$ be its corresponding class. Define \[ \operatorname{obs}(c)=[a|_W]\ \in\ H_G^k(W;R)/\operatorname{im}(H_G^k(V;R)\to H_G^k(W;R)). \] Exactness gives \[ c\in\operatorname{im}(H_G^k(X;R)\to H_G^k(Z;R)) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{obs}(c)=0. \] Thus this cokernel detects precisely the extension obstruction for the chosen cover. It is not a proof that the obstruction always vanishes for a moment map.

For a contact manifold $(M,\alpha)$ with a strict contact $G$-action, radial symplectization is \[ S=M\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},\qquad \omega={\,\mathrm{d}}(r\alpha),\qquad \mu_S(m,r)=r\mu_M(m). \] At zero, $\mu_S^{-1}(0)=\mu_M^{-1}(0)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}$, and radial projection gives equivariant homotopy equivalences of the full space and its zero level. Hence the contact and symplectization zero-level restriction maps are conjugate by cohomology isomorphisms. This assertion is specific to zero; it supplies no properness or surjectivity by itself.

The aid's explicit obstruction is Lerman's standard contact $S^3$ with action \[ \lambda(z_1,z_2)=(\lambda z_1,\lambda^{-1}z_2),\qquad \mu_M=|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2. \] Its image $[-1,1]$ is convex, every fiber is connected, and zero is regular. For a coefficient field $k$, the Borel map is \[ k[u]/(u^2)\longrightarrow\bigwedge_k(\eta),\qquad |u|=2,\ |\eta|=1,\quad u\longmapsto0, \] where $\eta^2=0$. Its kernel is $(u)$ and its graded-module cokernel is $k\eta$ in degree one. The nonextendable class survives radial symplectization. Therefore convexity and connectedness alone do not imply ordinary contact surjectivity, and a successful alternative proof must use additional hypotheses particular to its intended symplectic setting. The source's following Comment $5.9$ is cut off after "As mentioned in"; no missing claim is supplied.

Short English statement

The source asks for a non-Morse proof of Kirwan surjectivity. The aid gives an exact Mayer--Vietoris test for extending zero-level classes and shows the zero-level map is preserved by radial symplectization. Lerman's sphere still has a nonextendable degree-one class despite convex moment image and connected fibers, so those properties alone cannot prove a contact analogue.
