Optimal Complexity of Definable Cylindrical Decompositions

Definitions and mathematical statement

A semialgebraic subset of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a finite Boolean combination of sets defined by polynomial equalities and strict polynomial inequalities with real coefficients. More generally, definability is relative to a specified first-order structure and a convention concerning permitted parameters. The source does not specify that structure or parameter convention; its workshop context concerns o-minimality.

For a specified structure on ${\mathbb{R}}$, a finite partition $\mathcal D$ of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ into definable cells is cylindrical, with respect to a fixed coordinate order, if for every $1\leq k<n$ and every $C,D\in\mathcal D$, \[ \pi_k(C)=\pi_k(D)\quad\text{or}\quad \pi_k(C)\cap\pi_k(D)=\varnothing, \] where $\pi_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$. Here cells are understood in the usual recursive sense: in dimension one they are points or open intervals, and in higher dimensions they are graphs of continuous definable functions over cells or regions between continuous definable boundary functions, allowing unbounded regions. A partition is compatible with a set if that set is a union of its parts. These definitions describe a conventional real setting; the supplied record does not determine whether precisely this setting, compatibility condition, or coordinate convention is intended. It supplies no definition of a semi-cylindrical decomposition.

Source question. Can the quantitative bounds for semi-cylindrical or cylindrical decompositions of definable, even semialgebraic, sets be improved to optimal bounds in higher dimensions?
