p-Energies on Nonsmooth Metric-Measure Spaces

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X,d,\mu)$ denote a metric-measure space: $d$ is a metric on $X$, and $\mu$ is a measure on a specified measurable structure on $X$. The source does not specify that measurable structure, compatibility conditions between $d$ and $\mu$, or additional geometric or measure-theoretic assumptions. It also does not define a class of spaces called nonsmooth.

The supplied original excerpt gives the Euclidean reference expression \[ E_p(f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n}|\nabla f(x)|^p\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] where ${\,\mathrm{d}} x$ denotes Lebesgue measure and $|\nabla f|$ is the Euclidean norm of the gradient, whenever the expression is defined. The excerpt does not specify the range of $p$, the admissible functions $f$, or whether the gradient is classical or weak.

Research task, as formulated in the supplied registry. Develop an intrinsic notion of $p$-energy on nonsmooth metric-measure spaces, and characterize the geometric hypotheses under which constructions using Sobolev spaces, Dirichlet forms, upper gradients, and discrete approximations agree. A precise realization of this task must specify the allowed exponents $p$, a class of spaces $(X,d,\mu)$, an admissible function class $\mathcal F_p(X)$, and an energy $E_p^X$ on that class. It must identify the constructions being compared and state the exact meaning of their agreement, including their domains and normalizations.

The supplied record does not select these data or define agreement: it could mean equality of energies and domains, comparison up to constants, or agreement through a specified limiting procedure.
