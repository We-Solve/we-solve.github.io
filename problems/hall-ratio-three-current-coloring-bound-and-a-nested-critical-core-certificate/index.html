Math-1345

Hall ratio three: current coloring bound and a nested critical-core certificate

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple graph, $\Delta(G)$ its maximum degree, $\alpha(G)$ its independence number, and $\chi(G)$ its chromatic number. Its Hall ratio is \[ \rho(G)=\max_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq V(G)}\frac{|U|}{\alpha(G[U])}. \] The hypothesis $\alpha(H)\geq|V(H)|/3$ for every subgraph $H\subseteq G$ is equivalent to $\rho(G)\leq3$; it suffices to test induced subgraphs. In particular such a graph is $K_4$-free.

The source question, attributed there to "Jacque Verstraete," asks for a fixed positive power saving $\chi(G)\leq d^{1-\epsilon}$ when $d=\Delta(G)$ and $\rho(G)\leq3$. Literally quantified over every degree, this inequality fails for small examples such as a triangle ($d=2$, $\chi=3$). The substantive asymptotic interpretation is \[ \exists\epsilon\in(0,1)\ \exists d_0\ \forall G:\quad \rho(G)\leq3,\ \Delta(G)\geq d_0 \ \Longrightarrow\ \chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)^{1-\epsilon}. \] Equivalently one may allow an absolute multiplicative constant and restrict to positive maximum degree. This convention is made explicit because the source omits a degree threshold.

Its motivating examples are Kneser graphs $KG_{3t,t}$, whose vertices are the $t$-element subsets of a $3t$-element set, adjacent when disjoint: the source reports the hereditary independence condition and chromatic number $\Theta(\log d)$ for their maximum degree $d$.

The source also quotes a Shearer independence estimate for $K_4$-free graphs and then writes $\chi(G)\leq |V(G)|/\alpha(G)$. That latter inequality is reversed in general: $\chi(G)\geq |V(G)|/\alpha(G)$. Thus the printed deduction of a chromatic upper bound is not retained as valid. The attached aid instead attributes to Davies--Kang--Pirot--Sereni the directly applicable bound \[ \chi(G)\leq(\kappa_3+o(1))\frac{\Delta(G)}{\log\Delta(G)} \quad\text{for }\rho(G)\leq3,\quad\Delta(G)\to\infty, \] with a constant $\kappa_3$; it reports this as the strongest directly applicable bound at its run, not a fixed power saving.

The aid's additional certificate is: if $G$ is $k$-chromatic and $\rho(G)\leq3$, then it contains a nested chain $F_1\subset\cdots\subset F_k$ of vertex-critical $j$-chromatic graphs $F_j$, satisfying \[ \delta(F_j)\geq j-1,\qquad |V(F_{j-1})|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2|V(F_j)|}{3}\right\rfloor\quad(2\leq j\leq k). \] Here vertex-critical means that deleting any vertex lowers the chromatic number. A maximum independent-set deletion from $F_j$ leaves a $(j-1)$-chromatic graph containing $F_{j-1}$. In particular $|V(F_k)|\geq a_k$, where $a_1=1$, $a_j=\lceil3a_{j-1}/2\rceil$, and hence $|V(F_k)|\geq(3/2)^{k-1}$. This vertex-count certificate does not resolve the degree power-saving question.

Short English statement

Does Hall ratio at most three force a fixed power improvement over the maximum degree in coloring? The source needs an asymptotic degree convention and contains a reversed inequality. The supplied aid reports a logarithmic improvement and an exponential-size critical-subgraph certificate, rather than the requested power saving.
