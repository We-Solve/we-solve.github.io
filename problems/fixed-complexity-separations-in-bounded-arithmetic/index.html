Fixed Complexity Separations in Bounded Arithmetic

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\alpha$ denote the additional predicate parameter appearing in the source's relativized bounded-arithmetic notation. Write $S\vdash\sigma$ when the theory $S$ proves the sentence $\sigma$, and $S\nvdash\sigma$ when it does not. The symbols $T_m(\alpha)$ and $\mathrm{APC}_2(\alpha)$ retain their source meanings; the supplied record does not specify their languages or axioms. In particular, no identification of $T_m(\alpha)$ with a differently indexed standard hierarchy is assumed.

The notation $\Sigma_k^b(\alpha)$ refers to the source's intended level of the bounded-formula hierarchy relative to $\alpha$. Under the usual universal-closure interpretation, a $\forall\Sigma_k^b(\alpha)$ sentence has the form $\forall\vec{x}\,\theta(\vec{x})$, where $\theta\in\Sigma_k^b(\alpha)$ and all free variables of $\theta$ are included in $\vec{x}$. The exact language and syntactic hierarchy are not defined in the supplied record and require confirmation.

Question. Is there a fixed complexity level $k$ such that $T_{2n+1}(\alpha)$ can be separated from $T_{2n}(\alpha)$ by a $\forall\Sigma_k^b(\alpha)$ sentence?

To make the likely uniform reading explicit, let $I$ be the intended set of admissible indices $n$, and let $K$ be the intended set of complexity indices $k$; neither set is specified in the supplied record. If separation means provability in the higher indexed theory and nonprovability in the lower indexed theory, the uniform formulation is \[ \exists k\in K\;\forall n\in I\;\exists\sigma_n\in\forall\Sigma_k^b(\alpha)\quad \left(T_{2n+1}(\alpha)\vdash\sigma_n \ \land\ T_{2n}(\alpha)\nvdash\sigma_n\right). \] Here the separating sentence may depend on $n$, while $k$ is independent of $n$. This display is a conditional interpretation: the source leaves the quantifier over $n$, its range, and the direction of separation implicit. It must not be treated as an unqualified replacement for the source question.

The source additionally asks, under the words "in particular," whether \[ \exists n\in I\;\exists\tau\in\forall\Sigma_1^b(\alpha)\quad \left(T_{2n}(\alpha)\vdash\tau \ \land\ \mathrm{APC}_2(\alpha)\nvdash\tau\right). \] This second question has an explicit provability direction in the supplied record. Its asserted relationship to the first question cannot be checked without the intended theory definitions and comparison results.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether one fixed bounded-formula complexity suffices to distinguish the indicated successive theory levels. It also asks whether some even indexed theory proves a universally closed $\Sigma_1^b(\alpha)$ sentence that $\mathrm{APC}_2(\alpha)$ does not prove. The theory definitions and the first question's quantification over $n$ need clarification.
