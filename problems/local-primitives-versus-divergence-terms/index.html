Math-1526

Local primitives versus divergence terms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 30 proposes that a natural Riemannian top-degree density $S_g$ with conformally invariant total integral decomposes as \[ S_g=c\,\mathcal Q_g+L_g+G_g, \qquad\mathcal Q_g=Q_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g, \] where $L$ is pointwise conformally invariant as a density and $G$ has a natural local conformal primitive. Precisely, the last condition asks for a natural local density $F_g$ with \[ \left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} s}\right|_{s=0} \int_MF_{e^{2s\omega}g}=\int_M\omega G_g \quad\text{for every smooth }\omega. \] Here "local invariant" for $F$ must mean a diffeomorphism-natural local expression, not a conformally invariant density, whose total variation would vanish. Dimension, polynomiality, parity, locality and boundary conventions are absent in the original. The following precise result is in the aid's closed four-dimensional class.

Let $M$ be a closed Riemannian four-manifold, use $\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla$, full tensor curvature norms, $J=R/6$, and \[ Q_4=-\tfrac16\Delta R-\tfrac12|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+\tfrac16R^2, \qquad |W|^2=|\operatorname{Rm}|^2-2|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+\tfrac13R^2. \] Consider the complete parity-even polynomial critical-order span \[ S_g=(x|\operatorname{Rm}|^2+y|\operatorname{Ric}|^2 +zR^2+t\Delta R)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g, \qquad x,y,z,t\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] Universal conformal invariance of its integral, for every closed four-manifold, every metric and every conformal change, holds if and only if \[ x+y+3z=0. \] This is a universal coefficient criterion, not an equivalence inferred from invariance on just one special metric or conformal class.

Under that condition define \[ c=-4x-2y,\qquad a=t-\frac{2x+y}{3}. \] The aid gives the pointwise density identity \[ S_g=cQ_4\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g +x|W|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g +a\Delta R\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g. \] The middle term is a local conformal invariant density. The final term has the explicit natural local primitive \[ F_g=-3aJ^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g =-\frac a{12}R^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g, \] since on a closed four-manifold \[ \left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} s}\right|_{s=0} \int_MF_{e^{2s\omega}g} =a\int_M\omega\Delta R\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}_g. \] Thus the original stronger primitive requirement is verified throughout this declared four-dimensional class, including its exact coefficient and Laplacian convention.

The aid separately reports Alexakis's global conformal invariant decomposition in the polynomial complete-contraction class: a globally conformally invariant integral has a Pfaffian contribution, a local conformal invariant and a divergence. This is a related structural theorem, not by itself the AIM assertion. A divergence integrates to zero on a closed manifold, but that fact alone does not prove it is the conformal variational derivative of a natural local functional. General higher-dimensional local-primitive integrability remains outside the explicit four-dimensional result supplied here. Manifolds with boundary require boundary terms both in conformal variation and in integration by parts.

Short English statement

The key distinction is between a divergence and a local conformal variational derivative. The aid verifies the stronger AIM requirement for the full stated parity-even four-dimensional polynomial class, giving an explicit $R^2$ primitive. Alexakis's general divergence decomposition alone does not supply such a primitive in every dimension.
