Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.41

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a free group of rank $2$. For $x,y\in F$, write $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$, and let $[F,F]$ denote the subgroup generated by these commutators. For $g\in[F,F]$, its commutator length $\operatorname{cl}_F(g)$ is the least nonnegative integer $k$ such that $g$ is a product of $k$ commutators in $F$, with the empty product equal to $1$. Define its stable commutator length by \[ \operatorname{scl}_F(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cl}_F(g^n)}{n}, \] where $n$ ranges over positive integers; this limit exists by subadditivity. Put \[ S=\{\operatorname{scl}_F(g):g\in[F,F]\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}. \] The supplied source states as known background that, on this domain, \[ \operatorname{scl}_F(g)=0\iff g=1, \qquad \operatorname{scl}_F(g)\ge\tfrac12\quad\text{for every }g\in[F,F]\setminus\{1\}. \] D. Calegari asks the following questions.
  1. (a) Is $\tfrac12$ an isolated value of $S$? Explicitly, does there exist $\varepsilon>0$ such that \[ S\cap\left(\tfrac12-\varepsilon,\tfrac12+\varepsilon\right)=\{\tfrac12\}? \]
  2. (b) Does there exist an interval $J\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ such that $S\cap J$ is dense in $J$? Here density means \[ \forall x\in J\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists g\in[F,F]:\quad \operatorname{scl}_F(g)\in J\ \text{ and }\ \left|\operatorname{scl}_F(g)-x\right|<\varepsilon. \] The source does not specify whether $J$ must be nonempty or have positive length. A nondegenerate-interval formulation would additionally require that $J$ contain two distinct points. This restriction is not imposed silently here; the intended class of intervals requires clarification.
  3. (c) Does there exist $T\in{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ \forall q\in{\mathbb{Q}}\quad q\ge T\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists g\in[F,F]\text{ with }\operatorname{scl}_F(g)=q? \]

Short English statement

For a free group of rank $2$, consider the stable commutator lengths of elements of its commutator subgroup. The questions ask whether $1/2$ is isolated among these values, whether the values are dense in an interval, and whether they include every sufficiently large rational number. The intended nondegeneracy condition on the interval in part (b) is unspecified.
