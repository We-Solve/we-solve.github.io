A finite-sample Rao-Blackwell diagnostic between Kalman and particle filtering

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A filtering distribution is the conditional law of an unobserved state given observations up to the current time. The Kalman filter propagates means and covariances exactly in a linear Gaussian state-space model. A particle filter approximates a filtering law by weighted random samples and updates those weights using observations. Nonlinear recursive filtering generally requires a specified transition and observation model.

Research attempt: Moment closure fails in a scalar nonlinear observation model

The promised nonlinear recursive equation is absent from the source, so no specific recurrence can be solved faithfully. To test the potential shortcut "propagate only mean and variance as in Kalman filtering", choose the scalar observation $Y=X^2+Z$, with $Z\sim N(0,\tau^2)$ independent. A symmetric $\{ -1,1\}$ prior and a standard normal prior both have mean zero and variance one. Their predictive observation means are both one, but their predictive variances are respectively $\tau^2$ and $2+\tau^2$, because $E X^4$ equals one and three. The Gaussian fourth moment follows, for example, by twice integrating its density by parts using $\phi'(x)=-x\phi(x)$. Thus even predicting the next observation variance cannot be exact from the first two state moments alone for this nonlinear model.

For a standard normal prior, take $\tau=1$ and observe $Y=1$. Bayes' density is proportional to \[ \exp\{-x^2/2-(1-x^2)^2/2\} =\exp\{-1/2+x^2/2-x^4/2\}. \] Its log derivative is $x-2x^3$; the stationary points are zero and $\pm1/\sqrt2$. The second derivative $1-6x^2$ is positive at zero and negative at the other two, so the posterior is bimodal and not Gaussian. The quartic negative leading coefficient ensures the density is integrable. Linearizing $x^2$ at the prior mean gives derivative zero. A Kalman update based on that linearization has zero cross-covariance and makes no update, leaving a unimodal standard Gaussian. This supplies an exact mismatch, rather than an unsupported assertion that nonlinear filters can be difficult.

Particles can represent such a posterior in principle, but a finite sample does not guarantee both modes are represented. For $N$ independent exact posterior draws, symmetry gives probability $2^{1-N}$ that all fall on one side of zero. This follows from two disjoint events each of probability $2^{-N}$. It is only an elementary sampling diagnostic, not a particle-filter convergence theorem or the unpublished recursion sought by the participant.
