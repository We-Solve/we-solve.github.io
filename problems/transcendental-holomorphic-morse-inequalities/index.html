Math-1152

Transcendental Holomorphic Morse Inequalities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension $n$, and let $\alpha$ be a smooth real $d$-closed $(1,1)$-form. The reality convention is needed for its Hermitian eigenvalues and positivity. Fix a Hermitian metric $\omega$ and let $X(\alpha,\le1)$ be the locus where $\alpha$ has at most one negative eigenvalue relative to $\omega$. This inertia condition is independent of the chosen metric. Including points with a zero eigenvalue does not alter the integral below, since $\alpha^n$ vanishes there. Set \[ I(\alpha)=\int_{X(\alpha,\le1)}\alpha^n. \] The integral uses the signed top exterior power, not its absolute value: the index-one contribution has negative sign.

Write $[\alpha]\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,{\mathbb{R}})$ for the real Bott--Chern class, so representatives differ by $\sqrt{-1}\,\partial\bar\partial$ of real potentials. A Kähler current in this class is a closed real current \[ T=\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\,\partial\bar\partial\varphi\ge\varepsilon\omega \quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0, \] where $\varphi$ is a suitable quasi-plurisubharmonic potential. The usual transcendental volume is the supremum of the top-degree masses of positive representatives with analytic singularities, integrated on their regular loci; the convention here agrees with $\int_X\alpha^n$ for a Kähler form, without an extra factor $1/n!$.

The requested transcendental holomorphic Morse inequality is \[ I(\alpha)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad [\alpha]\text{ contains a K\"ahler current and } \operatorname{vol}([\alpha])\ge I(\alpha). \] The source states this for compact complex manifolds; no Kähler or projective hypothesis on $X$ is added. The literature assessment checked on 21 August 2026 attributes the problem to Demailly's transcendental holomorphic Morse inequalities and reports that weak inequalities, special geometries and partial volume estimates are known while the full general lower bound remains unestablished. This is a supplied dated assessment requiring independent verification.

Short English statement

A positive signed integral over the region where a closed real $(1,1)$-form has at most one negative direction should force its class to be big, with volume at least that integral. The target retains the full compact-complex setting.
