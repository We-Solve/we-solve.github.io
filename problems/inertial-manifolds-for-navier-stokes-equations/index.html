Inertial Manifolds for Navier–Stokes Equations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The curated question concerns the usual two-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations. Their differential expression is \[ \partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0, \] where $u$ is the velocity, $p$ is the pressure, and $\nu>0$ is the viscosity. The spatial domain, boundary conditions, forcing $f$, initial-data space, and associated phase space are not specified in the supplied record. This display therefore describes the equations only; it does not select an initial-boundary-value problem.

For a specified autonomous realization generating a solution semigroup $S(t):H\to H$, $t\geq0$, on a normed phase space $H$, a common semigroup formulation defines an inertial manifold to be a nonempty finite-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold $\mathcal M\subset H$ that is positively invariant, \[ S(t)\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M\qquad(t\geq0), \] and exponentially attracts every bounded subset of $H$: there exists $\beta>0$ such that for every nonempty bounded $B\subset H$ there exists $C_B<\infty$ satisfying \[ \sup_{u_0\in B}\inf_{v\in\mathcal M} \|S(t)u_0-v\|_H\leq C_B e^{-\beta t} \qquad(t\geq0). \] This is an explicit working convention, not a definition supplied by the source. Whether the intended definition additionally requires a global graph over specified modes, stronger regularity, or an exponential tracking property remains unresolved.

The source question is: Does the usual two-dimensional Navier--Stokes evolution admit an inertial manifold? Under the working convention above, for the intended realization this asks whether there exist $\mathcal M$ and $\beta>0$ with the stated properties. The supplied record does not identify that realization or quantify over possible domains, boundary conditions, viscosities, and forcings. Consequently, it does not determine a unique fully specified existence assertion. In particular, no universal quantifier over all such choices is asserted here. An autonomous semigroup formulation also requires a suitable forcing specification, which is absent.

The extracted original passage refers to the Navier--Stokes equations without explicitly specifying dimension; the curated statement specifies two dimensions. The supplied status is open. Its supplied literature assessment distinguishes the usual equations from regularized or hyperviscous models, for which it reports known inertial-manifold results. These are source qualifications and supplied assessments, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

The curated problem asks whether the usual two-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations possess a finite-dimensional invariant manifold that attracts solutions exponentially. The domain, boundary conditions, forcing, phase space, and precise inertial-manifold convention are missing, so the intended existence question still requires mathematical clarification. The supplied original excerpt does not explicitly state the dimension.
