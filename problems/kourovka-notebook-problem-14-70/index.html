Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.70

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, use the commutator convention $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and define left-normed iterated commutators by \[ [x,{}_1y]=[x,y],\qquad [x,{}_{k+1}y]=[[x,{}_ky],y]\quad(k\geq 1). \] For a positive integer $n$, the group $G$ is $n$-Engel if \[ \forall x,y\in G,\qquad [x,{}_ny]=1. \] Define its lower central series by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and \[ \gamma_{k+1}(G)=\langle [a,b]\mid a\in\gamma_k(G),\ b\in G\rangle\quad(k\geq 1). \] The group is nilpotent if $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1\}$ for some integer $c\geq 0$, and finitely generated if $G=\langle S\rangle$ for some finite subset $S\subseteq G$.

The existence question of B. I. Plotkin asks whether there are a positive integer $n$ and a finitely generated group $G$ such that \[ \left(\forall x,y\in G,\ [x,{}_ny]=1\right) \quad\text{and}\quad \left(\forall c\geq 0,\ \gamma_{c+1}(G)\neq\{1\}\right). \] Here $n$ is a single finite bound valid for all pairs of elements. The source does not specify a particular value of $n$.

The supplied comment dated 2017 states that finitely generated $n$-Engel groups are nilpotent for $n=2,3,4$. It also reports that E. Rips gives a course of lectures devoted to constructing non-nilpotent finitely generated $n$-Engel groups for sufficiently large $n$, and that the lectures are available on the Internet. No lecture reference, link, or numerical threshold is supplied. These statements are retained as dated source commentary, without independent verification or an inference that the requested construction has been established. The local record labels the problem open; that label is supplied registry metadata.

Short English statement

Does there exist a finitely generated, non-nilpotent group satisfying the $n$-Engel identity for some fixed positive integer $n$? The supplied 2017 comment records nilpotence for $n=2,3,4$ and reports lectures by E. Rips concerning constructions for sufficiently large $n$; the lecture reference and threshold are unspecified.
