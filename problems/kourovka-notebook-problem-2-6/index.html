Kourovka Notebook Problem 2.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group. Write $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{gen}}(G)$ for the set called the generalized central elements of $G$ in the supplied source, and write $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{nil}}(G)$ for its nil-kernel. These symbols are placeholders for the source terminology: the supplied record defines neither of these notions nor the term $NR$-group. Consequently, the formulation below requires those definitions before it can be a self-contained mathematical statement.

The supplied record states the forward implication \[ G\text{ is an }NR\text{-group} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal C_{\mathrm{gen}}(G)=\mathcal N_{\mathrm{nil}}(G). \] The question, attributed to V. G. Vilyatser, asks whether the converse holds: for every group $G$, does \[ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{gen}}(G)=\mathcal N_{\mathrm{nil}}(G) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ is an }NR\text{-group}? \] The record specifies no restriction to finite groups or to another class of groups; any restrictions implicit in the undefined terminology remain unresolved.

Short English statement

V. G. Vilyatser asks whether equality of a group's generalized central elements and its nil-kernel forces the group to be an $NR$-group. The supplied record states the reverse implication. Definitions of all three specialized notions are missing, so this draft retains the question provisionally and requires mathematical review.
