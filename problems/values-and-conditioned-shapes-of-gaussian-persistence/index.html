Values and Conditioned Shapes of Gaussian Persistence

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record describes a research programme rather than a single quantified conjecture. The following notation makes its questions explicit without supplying missing model hypotheses.

Under the real-valued, continuous-time interpretation suggested by persistence on an interval, let $X=(X_t)_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}$ be a stationary Gaussian process: every finite vector of its coordinates is jointly Gaussian, and its finite-dimensional distributions are invariant under time translation. No centering, variance normalization, covariance function, spectral measure, or sample-path regularity is specified in the source.

For $\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and $T>0$, distinguish the events \[ A^{>}_{T,\ell}=\{X_t>\ell\text{ for every }t\in[0,T]\}, \qquad A^{\ge}_{T,\ell}=\{X_t\ge\ell\text{ for every }t\in[0,T]\}. \] For a model and process version in which the chosen event is measurable, write \[ p_X^{\diamond}(T,\ell)=\mathbb P(A^{\diamond}_{T,\ell}), \qquad \diamond\in\{>,\ge\}. \] A conventional exponential persistence exponent is \[ \theta_X^{\diamond}(\ell) =-\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log p_X^{\diamond}(T,\ell), \] provided the limit exists, with $-\log 0=+\infty$. The source does not specify this normalization, guarantee existence of the limit, or settle whether strict or non-strict persistence is intended; the two boundary conventions must therefore remain distinct.

Whenever $p_X^{\diamond}(T,\ell)>0$, the conditioned process is described by \[ \mathcal L\left((X_t)_{0\le t\le T}\mid A^{\diamond}_{T,\ell}\right). \] The supplied questions are to compute persistence exponents in specific stationary Gaussian examples, determine the typical shape under this conditional law, and investigate extensions to non-Gaussian or non-stationary processes. No particular examples or extension classes are identified. An asymptotic interpretation of "typical shape" would require a specified limiting regime, time and height scalings, path space, and mode of convergence or concentration; none is supplied. Thus the record does not determine a unique limiting-shape assertion or a universal extension theorem.

The supplied dated curation labels the programme partially solved and reports results for conditioned shapes and extensions in several spectral-singularity classes, while describing the general shape and extension questions as open. The relevant classes and hypotheses are unspecified, and these literature claims are not independently verified here.

Short English statement

For stationary Gaussian processes, compute persistence exponents in concrete examples and describe paths conditioned to stay above a level $\ell$ throughout $[0,T]$. Investigate analogous questions for non-Gaussian or non-stationary models. The source leaves the examples, boundary convention, exponent normalization, and meaning of typical shape unspecified. Its partially solved designation is supplied dated curation.
