Verbatim supplied catalogue question

If a multivariate polynomial $P$ can be evaluated on rational inputs by a Boolean polynomial-time algorithm, must $P$ have a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit using only additions, subtractions, and multiplications?

Source scope and attempted route

The primary report really uses the terse single-polynomial wording, then explicitly discusses families and low degree in its explanation. The preserved catalogue leaves those asymptotic parameters unspecified. We test two proposed transfers under stated degree assumptions rather than silently choose a complete replacement conjecture.
Interpolation gives a finite circuit and its true size.
Let $P\in\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ have individual degree at most $D$. For $0\le i\le D$, define \[ L_i(t)=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le D\\j\ne i}}\frac{t-j}{i-j}. \] These rational-coefficient polynomials satisfy $L_i(j)=\mathbf1_{i=j}$. The tensor interpolation identity is \[ P(x)=\sum_{a\in\{0,\ldots,D\}^n}P(a)\prod_{j=1}^n L_{a_j}(x_j). \] For completeness, the difference has individual degree at most $D$ and vanishes on the whole grid. Induction on variables proves it is zero: fixing all but the last grid coordinates gives a univariate polynomial with $D+1$ roots and degree at most $D$, so its coefficients vanish on the smaller grid; apply induction to them. With rational constants allowed, each $L_i(x_j)$ has an addition/multiplication circuit using $O(D)$ operations, since the denominators $i-j$ are nonzero constant rational factors. Precompute all these polynomials in $O(n(D+1)^2)$ gates, then sum the $(D+1)^n$ products in $O(n(D+1)^n)$ more. Evaluating a Boolean polynomial-time oracle at all the grid points supplies the constants $P(a)$, but uses $(D+1)^n$ oracle calls. For fixed $n$ this is polynomial in $D$ and the relevant evaluation costs; for variable $n$ it is generally exponential. Thus interpolation does not prove the required family-size bound. It also produces rational constants, not a constant-free circuit without a separate construction of those constants.
Boolean gate arithmetization preserves only Boolean inputs.
On bits, AND is multiplication, NOT is $1-x$, and OR is $x+y-xy$. Replacing gates by these formulas gives a polynomial agreeing with the Boolean circuit on its finite Boolean domain, as induction on gates proves. But agreement on that domain does not identify a general polynomial on rational inputs. For example, $P(x)=x^2$ agrees with $Q(x)=x$ on $\{0,1\}$ and differs at $x=2$. More generally any multiple of $x_i^2-x_i$ vanishes on the Boolean cube. Hence a gatewise transfer of a circuit for values on bits may recover only a residue class modulo these relations, not $P$ itself. A Boolean evaluator on rational inputs instead operates on variable-length numerator/denominator encodings, so its bit gates are not functions on just the original $n$ field variables. Translating those bits into field operations without a complexity loss is precisely an additional unproved step, not an application of the three gate identities.
Even a small arithmetic circuit need not give short exact output.
Repeated squaring computes $x^{2^k}$ using $k$ multiplication gates. At rational input $x=2$, its integer value has $2^k+1$ binary digits. Writing that exact value takes at least that many output operations in the usual bit model. This is a complete output-length observation, not a counterexample to the source implication, whose premise requires fast Boolean evaluation. It shows why degree and output encoding cannot be ignored when comparing the two models or trying to reverse a simulation argument.

Adversarial audit and gap

The report passage was inspected, including the distinction from the adjacent constant-free class separation. The audit checked interpolation denominators, individual versus total degree, the number of grid points, and exact rather than compressed value output. We establish neither an efficient variable-dimension transfer nor a counterexample family meeting the Boolean-evaluation premise. No finite collection of Boolean input tests was promoted to an identity over rational inputs, and no numerical computation was performed.
