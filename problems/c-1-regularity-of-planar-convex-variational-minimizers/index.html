$C^1$ Regularity of Planar Convex Variational Minimizers

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question asks whether strict convexity of the integrand forces every planar Lipschitz minimizer to have continuous first derivatives. The following gives a precise scalar, interior interpretation; the source record does not specify all the conventions needed to identify this interpretation as the intended one.

Let \[ B_1=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:|x|<1\}, \] and let $F:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}$ be finite-valued and strictly convex, meaning that \[ F(tp+(1-t)q)<tF(p)+(1-t)F(q) \quad\text{for }p\ne q,\quad 0<t<1. \] For a scalar-valued Lipschitz function $u:B_1\to{\mathbb{R}}$, define \[ \mathcal E_F(u)=\int_{B_1}F(\nabla u(x))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] where $\nabla u$ is its almost-everywhere defined weak gradient. Under the compactly supported variation convention, call $u$ a minimizer if \[ \mathcal E_F(u)\leq\mathcal E_F(u+\varphi) \] for every scalar-valued Lipschitz function $\varphi$ with compact support in $B_1$.

The question under these conventions is whether \[ \text{for every such }F\text{ and every such minimizer }u, \qquad u\in C^1(B_1). \] Here membership in $C^1(B_1)$ means that $u$ has continuous classical first derivatives throughout the open disk; no boundary regularity is included in this interpretation.

The scalar target, the finite-valued domain of $F$, the variation class, and the interior meaning of $C^1$ are explicit interpretive choices, not hypotheses confirmed by the supplied record. In particular, the record gives no differentiability assumptions on $F$, no specified boundary data, and no precise definition of minimizer. Any intended additional hypotheses must be recovered from the cited source before this conditional formulation is accepted.

Short English statement

Does strict convexity of $F$ alone ensure that every Lipschitz minimizer of $\int_{B_1}F(\nabla u)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x$ in two dimensions is continuously differentiable in the interior? The displayed formulation uses an explicitly conditional scalar interpretation because the supplied record leaves several hypotheses and minimization conventions unspecified.
