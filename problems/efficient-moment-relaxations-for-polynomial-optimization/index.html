Exact infimum does not ensure a moment optimizer

The source explicitly separates computing the infimum from recovering minimizers if they exist, and discusses additional optimality equations. We test the bare unconstrained moment-relaxation route, not those strengthened methods. On the closed set $\mathbb R^2$ take \[ f(x,y)=(xy-1)^2+x^2. \] Its infimum is 0, approached at $(x,y)=(1/t,t)$ as $t\to\infty$, but no point attains it: $f=0$ would require both $x=0$ and $xy=1$.
Proposition: The order-two moment relaxation with $L(1)=1$ and positive semidefinite moment matrix indexed by $1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2$ has infimum 0 for $L(f)$, but has no feasible optimizer attaining that value.
Proof: Positive semidefiniteness means $L(p^2)\ge0$ for every polynomial $p$ of degree at most two, hence $L(f)=L((xy-1)^2)+L(x^2)\ge0$. Evaluation at $(1/t,t)$ defines a feasible functional with rank-one moment matrix and objective $t^{-2}$, proving infimum 0. If a feasible functional attained zero, both nonnegative summands would vanish. For a positive semidefinite bilinear form $\langle p,q\rangle=L(pq)$, $\langle p,p\rangle=0$ implies $\langle p,q\rangle=0$ for every allowed $q$: positivity of $\langle p+uq,p+uq\rangle$ for every real $u$ forces its linear coefficient to vanish. Taking $p=x,q=y$ therefore gives $L(xy)=0$. Taking $p=xy-1,q=1$ gives $L(xy)-L(1)=0$, so $L(xy)=1$, a contradiction. Thus no optimizer exists. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At $t=1,2,10,100$, exact rational point-evaluation moments gave objective $t^{-2}$ and a positive semidefinite rank-one matrix (the outer product of the monomial vector). Its $y^4$ diagonal entry grows as $t^4$, making the escaping feasible sequence explicit. The failed inference is that equality between an SDP infimum and the polynomial infimum automatically produces an optimal moment vector or extractable minimizer. The source already acknowledges nonattainment, so this example does not contradict its qualified formulation or its optimality-ideal constructions. It gives no general exact-relaxation criterion and no full algorithm for arbitrary closed noncompact semialgebraic sets.
