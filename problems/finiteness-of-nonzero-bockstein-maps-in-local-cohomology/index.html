Math-7304

Finiteness of Nonzero Bockstein Maps in Local Cohomology

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 0$ be an integer, let $R={\mathbb{Z}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and fix an ideal $\mathfrak a\subseteq R$. For an $R$-module $M$, define \[ \Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(M)=\{m\in M:\mathfrak a^t m=0\text{ for some integer }t\geq 1\}, \qquad H^k_{\mathfrak a}(M)=R^k\Gamma_{\mathfrak a}(M)\quad(k\geq 0). \] For each positive prime integer $p$, the short exact sequence of $R$-modules \[ 0\longrightarrow R/pR\xrightarrow{\iota_p}R/p^2R\xrightarrow{q_p}R/pR\longrightarrow 0, \qquad \iota_p(r+pR)=pr+p^2R, \] where $q_p$ is reduction modulo $p$, defines connecting homomorphisms \[ \beta_k^p:H^k_{\mathfrak a}(R/pR)\longrightarrow H^{k+1}_{\mathfrak a}(R/pR). \] These are the Bockstein homomorphisms in the question; local cohomology on the quotient modules uses the image of $\mathfrak a$.

Question. For every such $R$ and $\mathfrak a$, is there a finite set $S=S(R,\mathfrak a)$ of positive prime integers such that \[ \beta_k^p=0\qquad\text{for every prime }p\notin S\text{ and every integer }k\geq 0? \] The supplied statement leaves the quantification of $k$ implicit. The displayed formulation uses all nonnegative degrees. It is equivalent here to asking for finitely many exceptional primes separately for each fixed degree: if $\mathfrak a$ has $r$ generators, then $H^j_{\mathfrak a}(M)=0$ for $j>r$, so only finitely many degrees can contribute.

The supplied curation labels this question open and describes it as an unresolved-context fragment concerning closure and local-cohomology statements over polynomial rings over the integers. It characterizes the report as supplying supporting evidence rather than a proof of a general resolution. These are supplied qualifications, not an independently verified status assessment.

Short English statement

For a fixed ideal in a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over ${\mathbb{Z}}$, must the Bockstein maps on local cohomology modulo $p$ vanish in every degree for all but finitely many primes $p$? The supplied open label and source-verification claims require independent review.
