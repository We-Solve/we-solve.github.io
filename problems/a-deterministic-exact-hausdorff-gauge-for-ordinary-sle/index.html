A deterministic exact Hausdorff gauge for ordinary SLE

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: For $0<\kappa<8$, a deterministic gauge is claimed to give positive finite measure to each nontrivial compact positive-time SLE segment; The explicit proposed SLE gauge is $r^d(\log\log(1/r))^{(2-d)/2}$, $d=1+\kappa/8$; the exponent-one alternative is claimed not sigma-finite on nontrivial segments. Together they give a claimed affirmative answer in the matching scope.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For $0<\kappa<8$, let $\gamma$ be ordinary chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ from $0$ to $\infty$ in $\mathbb H$, with capacity $2t$. There exists a deterministic continuous nondecreasing gauge $h_\kappa$, vanishing at zero and positive elsewhere, such that almost surely \[ 0<\mathcal H^{h_\kappa}(\gamma([s,t]))<\infty \qquad\text{simultaneously for all real }0<s<t<\infty. \] Here $\mathcal H^h(E)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\inf\{\sum_jh(\operatorname{diam}U_j):E\subset\bigcup_jU_j,\ \operatorname{diam}U_j\le\delta\}$. The gauge depends only on $\kappa$.
Result 2.
Let $\gamma$ be ordinary chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ in $\mathbb H$, from $0$ to $\infty$, parametrized with capacity $2t$, and write $\Gamma=\gamma([0,\infty))$. Fix $0<\kappa<8$, put $d=1+\kappa/8$, $p=2-d$, and define near zero \[ h(r)=r^d\left(\log\log(1/r)\right)^{p/2}. \] Complete this gauge continuously and monotonically at larger radii. Using diameter-based Hausdorff measure, almost surely \[ 0<\mathcal H^h(\gamma([s,t]))<\infty\quad\text{for every real }0<s<t<\infty. \] For each deterministic $R>0$, $\mathbb E\mathcal H^h(\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R))\le C_{\kappa,R}<\infty$. In particular, the trace has finite $h$-measure in every rectangle $[-m,m]+i[0,m]$, simultaneously for integers $m\ge1$.

Outline of the argument

Finite stopped Green tilts give one-point laws, and regularized product supermartingales control simultaneous radius hits. Integrating collision singularities constructs a canonical trace mass; its moment growth selects a gauge with both lower density and upper covering estimates.

Localized stopped densities and Brownian boundary-occupation estimates give a uniform spatial tail bound. A positive mass with controlled ball density supplies the lower Hausdorff estimate, and carefully chosen spatial covers supply the upper bound; comparison of gauges then excludes the exponent-one choice.
