Minimum Triangular Edges above Turán Density

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Interpret a graph provisionally as a finite simple undirected graph; the supplied record does not explicitly specify this convention. Let $n\geq 1$ and $e$ be integers satisfying \[ \frac{n^2}{4}<e\leq\binom n2. \] The upper bound restricts attention to parameters for which such graphs exist. For a graph $G$, define its set of triangular edges by \[ T(G)=\left\{\{u,v\}\in E(G):\text{there exists }w\in V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}\text{ with }\{u,w\},\{v,w\}\in E(G)\right\}. \] Thus each edge contained in at least one triangle is counted once. Define \[ \operatorname{Tr}(n,e)=\min\left\{|T(G)|:|V(G)|=n,\ |E(G)|=e\right\} \] and \[ g(n,e)=\min\left\{e-bc:(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{3},\ a+b+c=n,\ \binom a2+ab+bc\geq e\right\}. \] The nonnegative integer domain for $a,b,c$ is supplied by the surrounding family definition.

For such a triple $(a,b,c)$, write $\mathcal T(a,b,c)$ for the family denoted $\operatorname{Tr}(a,b,c)$ in the source. Its members are described as graphs admitting a partition \[ V(G)=A\sqcup B\sqcup C,\qquad |A|=a,\quad |B|=b,\quad |C|=c, \] where $B$ and $C$ are independent sets, every possible edge between $B$ and $C$ is present, and there are no edges between $A$ and $C$. Writing $E_G(A,B)$ for the edges with one endpoint in each of $A,B$, the source additionally requires that the edges inside $A$ and between $A$ and $B$ form an "almost-complete graph" with \[ |E(G[A])|+|E_G(A,B)|>\binom{a-1}{2}+ab. \] Two qualifications remain unresolved: the record does not define "almost-complete" or specify whether it adds any condition beyond this strict inequality; and, although $a=0$ is allowed, it supplies no convention for $\binom{-1}{2}$ in that boundary case. Accordingly, this description preserves the supplied family but does not resolve its full meaning at every allowed parameter.

The requested all-$n$ assertions are that, for every feasible integer pair $(n,e)$ above, \[ \operatorname{Tr}(n,e)=g(n,e), \] and that every graph $G$ attaining this minimum satisfies \[ G\in\bigcup_{\substack{a,b,c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\a+b+c=n}}\mathcal T(a,b,c). \] The second assertion is subject to the unresolved family qualifications just stated.

The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports that the triangular-edge extremal conjecture has an exact formula for all sufficiently large $n$, while the extracted all-$n$ statement is not fully established in the cited literature. This is a supplied dated assessment, not an independently verified theorem or current literature determination; it does not separately specify the established scope of the assertion about every extremal graph.

Short English statement

Among $n$-vertex graphs with $e>n^2/4$ edges, minimize the number of edges belonging to triangles. The source asks whether the displayed function $g(n,e)$ always gives this minimum and whether every minimizing graph has the stated three-part structure. The meaning of "almost-complete" and the $a=0$ boundary case require clarification. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation, not independently verified here.
