Math-1732

The arc metric is geodesic and doubling preserves its geodesics

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a finite-type bordered hyperbolic surface with nonempty geodesic boundary, and let $\mathcal T(S)$ denote the reduced Teichmüller space used in the source. Boundary lengths may vary and boundary parametrizations are not retained. In the usual arc-metric convention, let $\mathcal A(S)$ be essential properly embedded arc classes with endpoints allowed to slide on the boundary, and include essential closed curves and boundary curves in the test set $\mathcal L(S)$. For each marked structure, arcs are represented by perpendicular geodesic arcs. Set \[ d_A(X,Y)=\log\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal L(S)}\frac{\ell_Y(\alpha)}{\ell_X(\alpha)}. \] The exact arc/curve and marking conventions require agreement with the cited reduced-space construction.

The original problem has two parts: is this asymmetric arc metric a length metric, and can every ordered pair be joined by a Thurston geodesic in the double that stays inside the symmetric doubled locus? More precisely, let $S^d$ and $X^d$ be the topological and hyperbolic doubles across the boundary, and let \[ j:\mathcal T(S)\longrightarrow\mathcal T(S^d),\qquad j(X)=X^d. \] The source's notation $\Gamma\subset\mathcal T(S)$ inside $\mathcal T(S^d)$ means $\Gamma\subset j(\mathcal T(S))$.

The aid attributes to Alessandrini--Disarlo the following affirmative result: for every ordered pair $X,Y$, there is a forward unit-speed arc geodesic \[ \gamma:[0,D]\to\mathcal T(S),\qquad D=d_A(X,Y),\quad \gamma(0)=X,\ \gamma(D)=Y, \] \[ d_A(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=t-s\qquad(0\leq s\leq t\leq D), \] which is a finite concatenation of generalized stretch segments. Thus the forward distance is realized by a path and equals its intrinsic forward length. This does not claim uniqueness or that one ordinary stretch segment suffices.

The aid also invokes the Liu--Papadopoulos--Su--Théret doubling identity \[ d_{\mathrm{Th}}(X^d,Y^d)=d_A(X,Y). \] Consequently $j\circ\gamma$ is a forward unit-speed Thurston geodesic with its entire image in $j(\mathcal T(S))$, answering the second question as well. In fact, for each fixed ordered pair, composition with $j$ gives a bijection between all parameterized forward unit-speed arc geodesics from $X$ to $Y$ and all parameterized forward unit-speed Thurston geodesics from $X^d$ to $Y^d$ whose images lie in this doubled locus. Its inverse is pointwise restriction by $j^{-1}$.

Thus the ambient-geodesic clause is exactly arc-metric geodesicity under the doubling identity. It is stronger than merely asserting a length-space axiom in the absence of a separate minimizing-path existence argument; length alone does not assert that the infimum is attained. The reported generalized-stretch theorem supplies that attainment in the stated reduced bordered setting.

Short English statement

The supplied literature answers both questions affirmatively: the arc metric has minimizing geodesics, and doubling identifies exactly those geodesics with Thurston geodesics that remain in the symmetric doubled locus.
