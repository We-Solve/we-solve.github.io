Math-13371

Major problems 8 — Say something general about the stable or unstable homotopy groups of spheres.

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer stem $j$, the stable homotopy group of spheres is \[ \pi_j^S=\varinjlim_r\pi_{j+r}(S^r), \] with suspension transition maps. Unstable groups retain the actual sphere dimension $k$, as $\pi_n(S^k)$. Fixing $k$ and letting $n\to\infty$ is not the stable colimit.

Major problem 8 (Mark Hovey's list). Say something general about stable or unstable homotopy groups of spheres. As a suggested growth question, the source attributes to Ravenel the possibility that the "size" of the $n$-th homotopy group of $S^k$ grows polynomially in $n$, perhaps even cubically. Hovey explicitly says he presumes the suggestion meant stable homotopy, while also proposing an unstable question. The ambiguity between these regimes must remain explicit.

Once a numerical size function $s$ and indexing convention are chosen, an eventual polynomial upper bound would mean \[ \exists C>0\ \exists d\geq0\ \exists n_0\ \forall n\geq n_0, \qquad s(n)\leq Cn^d, \] with the suggested cubic version using $d=3$. This only explains a possible upper-bound meaning of the source's growth wording, not a supplied exact conjecture. Cardinality, logarithm of cardinality, minimal generator count, and prime-primary rank are different functions. Some unstable groups have infinite cardinality, and $\pi_0^S\cong{\mathbb{Z}}$ is also infinite; a cardinality formulation must state its degree range. Positive stable stems are finite, so their ordinary integral rank is zero: the summary's repeated word "rank" cannot by itself define the intended nontrivial size.

For a fixed-sphere unstable version the bound's constants may depend on $k$; the excerpt does not state otherwise or fix prime localization. No particular size convention, uniformity in $k$, or asymptotic equivalent is silently chosen. The broad request for general information is retained rather than equated solely with the illustrative polynomial suggestion.

Short English statement

Seek general information on sphere homotopy groups, including Ravenel's suggested polynomial, possibly cubic, growth question. Stable versus fixed-sphere unstable indexing and the meaning of size are unresolved; ordinary rank, torsion exponent, and group order must not be conflated.
