Entropy of Serre Functors and Algebraicity

Definitions and mathematical statement

A graded matrix factorization of a homogeneous potential $w$ over a graded ring $R$ consists of graded modules and maps whose two compositions are multiplication by $w$, with grading shifts chosen to make these identities homogeneous. The supplied record does not specify $R$, the base field, the potential, the grading convention, or the construction of the category of factorizations.

For clarity, the following is a conditional framework for interpreting the entropy notation, rather than additional hypotheses attributed to the source. Let $\mathcal C$ be a triangulated category with shift $[1]$ and split generator $G$, meaning that the smallest full triangulated subcategory containing $G$ and closed under direct summands is $\mathcal C$. For $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$, define \[ \delta_t(G,E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{t n_j}:\begin{array}{l}0=E_0,E_1,\ldots,E_m,\quad E_m\cong E\oplus E',\\ E_{j-1}\longrightarrow E_j\longrightarrow G[n_j]\longrightarrow E_{j-1}[1]\\ \text{are distinguished triangles},\quad n_j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\end{array}\right\}. \] Here the infimum runs over finite towers and auxiliary objects $E'$; an empty admissible set has infimum $+\infty$. For an exact endofunctor $F\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal C$, a usual categorical entropy convention is \[ h_t(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\delta_t(G,F^nG), \] provided the limit and the required independence of $G$ hold in the chosen framework. No existence, finiteness, or independence assertion is supplied here. If $\mathcal C$ is linear over a field $k$ and has finite-dimensional morphism spaces, a Serre functor is an autoequivalence $S$ equipped with bifunctorial isomorphisms \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(X,Y)^*\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(Y,SX),\qquad (-)^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(-,k). \] The source does not state hypotheses ensuring the existence of such a functor or the applicability of this entropy convention.

Source question, with unresolved scope retained. For general categories $\mathcal C$ of graded matrix factorizations for which the relevant Serre functor and categorical entropy are defined, determine a geometric interpretation of \[ t\longmapsto h_t(S). \] More generally, determine whether $\exp(h_0(F))$ is always algebraic and identify the conditions under which algebraicity holds. If $h_0(F)$ is finite and real, the conventional interpretation of algebraicity is \[ \exists\,P\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]\setminus\{0\}\quad P\left(\exp(h_0(F))\right)=0. \] The source does not specify the admissible categories or functors $F$ for this broader question, the intended meaning of a geometric interpretation, or conventions for infinite entropy. Thus this is an explicitly qualified research question, not a fully specified universal conjecture.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a geometric meaning of the categorical entropy of Serre functors on categories of graded matrix factorizations, and asks when the exponential of entropy at $t=0$ is algebraic. The precise categories, functors, entropy hypotheses, and intended geometric interpretation remain unspecified. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
