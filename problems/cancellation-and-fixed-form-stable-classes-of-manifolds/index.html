Math-3578

Cancellation and Fixed-Form Stable Classes of Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a manifold $M$, let $\pi_1(M)$ denote its fundamental group, and let $|X|$ denote the cardinality of a set $X$. The supplied record writes $\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M)$ for the fixed-form homotopy stable class of $M$, with $\lambda$ denoting the relevant intersection form or equivariant intersection form. However, the supplied excerpt does not define this class: it does not specify its representatives, stabilization operation, equivalence relation, or the precise condition imposed by fixing $\lambda$. These choices must be recovered from the cited source before this notation gives a fully determined mathematical object.

The questions recorded under permanent UnsolvedMath ID $30005385$, source code $\texttt{catalogue entry}$, are the following.

  1. Can the Kreck--Schafer construction be systematized, using boundaries of thickenings of even-dimensional complexes as described in the supplied clean statement, to produce manifolds whose fixed-form homotopy stable classes have arbitrarily large cardinality? The intended unboundedness target is that, for every positive integer $N$, a manifold $M$ in the intended construction class exists with \[ \left|\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M)\right|>N. \] The record does not specify the dimensions, types of complexes, thickenings, or manifold category required for a systematic construction, nor whether the dimension, fundamental group, or form must remain fixed throughout such a family.
  2. For which fundamental groups are the classes $\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M)$ trivial, finite, or infinite, particularly when $M$ is a closed four-manifold? Here "trivial" is understood provisionally as consisting of one class. The record does not specify whether a proposed group criterion should hold for every eligible $M$ and $\lambda$, or should characterize the existence of examples. It also does not specify the additional hypotheses on $M$ or the treatment of boundary data.

The supplied excerpt places these questions alongside the following reported results, attributed there to Theorem 1, with reference labels [CCPS21a, CCPS21b, CCP22]. They are included as source-reported background, not as independently verified assertions.

  1. For every integer $k\geq2$ and every $n>0$, there exists a smooth, closed, simply-connected $4k$-manifold $M$ with hyperbolic intersection form and \[ \left|\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M)\right|>n. \] The domain of the threshold parameter $n$ is not explicitly stated in the excerpt.
  2. For every integer $k\geq2$ and every $n>0$, the excerpt reports a smooth, closed $4k$-manifold with hyperbolic equivariant intersection form. Its fundamental-group line and stable-class notation are typographically corrupted. An apparent reading is \[ \pi_1(M)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}, \qquad \left|\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M)\right|=\infty, \] but this reading requires checking against the source. The parameter $n$ occurs in the supplied quantifiers although it has no visible role in this conclusion.
  3. For every odd prime $q$, the excerpt reports a four-manifold $M_q$ with \[ \pi_1(M_q)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}, \qquad \lambda_{M_q}=(2q), \qquad \partial M_q\cong L(2q,1), \qquad \left|\mathcal S_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{st},h}(M_q)\right|=\infty. \] Here $L(2q,1)$ is the lens space named in the source. The coefficient ring and conventions for the displayed equivariant form $(2q)$, the boundary identification, and the boundary conditions in the stable class are not supplied.
The excerpt describes the higher-dimensional examples as smooth and describes the four-dimensional result as having nonempty boundary. These reported boundary examples do not by themselves specify an answer for closed four-manifolds.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether the indicated thickening construction can systematically produce arbitrarily large fixed-form homotopy stable classes, and how their cardinalities depend on the fundamental group, especially for closed four-manifolds. The source reports substantial higher-dimensional and boundary examples. A precise final formulation still requires the definition of the stable class, the intended quantifiers in the group question, and correction of a corrupted background theorem statement.
