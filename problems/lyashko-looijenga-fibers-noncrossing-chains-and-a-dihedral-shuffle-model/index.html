Math-1672

Lyashko-Looijenga fibers, noncrossing chains, and a dihedral shuffle model

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

D. Bessis and F. Chapoton's Problem 3.3 asks to interpret type A noncrossing partitions through the Lyashko--Looijenga (LL) covering of the complex sphere and to generalize the associated tree/cactus combinatorics to other types. The source recalls a relation to Goulden--Jackson cacti [27], a ramified-covering result [34], and the common count $n^{n-2}$ for the type A covering degree, labelled trees, and maximal chains of $NC_{A_{n-1}}$. Its description of assigning a critical-value polynomial to any complex-valued function requires qualifications: only suitable families with finitely many critical values and fixed multiplicity/normalization give such a finite covering.

For example, for monic centered degree-$n$ polynomials, $n\ge2$, the LL construction records the $n-1$ critical values, with multiplicity, as roots of a monic polynomial. Generic fibers refer to distinct critical values away from the discriminant. The precise sphere/cactus markings and normalization intended by the source are not reproduced, so a geometric cactus bijection is not invented here.

General structural identification reported in the aid. Let $W$ be an irreducible well-generated complex reflection group of rank $r$, Coxeter number $h$, reflection set $T$, and a specified Coxeter element $c$. Define reflection length $\ell_T$, absolute order \[ u\le_Tv\iff\ell_T(u)+\ell_T(u^{-1}v)=\ell_T(v), \qquad NC(W)=[1,c], \] and the ordered reduced reflection factorizations \[ \operatorname{Red}_T(c)=\{(t_1,\ldots,t_r)\in T^r: t_1\cdots t_r=c\}. \] A factorization gives the maximal chain of prefixes $1,t_1,t_1t_2,\ldots,c$, and a maximal chain recovers its factors by successive quotients. The aid attributes to Bessis labeling and trivialization an identification of a generic LL fiber with these ordered factorizations and hence maximal chains, all with cardinality \[ |\operatorname{Red}_T(c)|=\frac{r!h^r}{|W|}. \] For $W=A_{n-1}$, $r=n-1$, $h=n$, and $|W|=n!$, this is $n^{n-2}$. Labels depend on the usual generic-value and ordering choices; a bare numerical equality does not specify a canonical cactus or tree bijection.

Use the Hurwitz convention \[ \sigma_i:(\ldots,t_i,t_{i+1},\ldots) \longmapsto(\ldots,t_it_{i+1}t_i^{-1},t_i,\ldots). \] It preserves the total product. Any statement identifying covering monodromy with this convention must use compatible orientations and labeling choices.

Explicit dihedral model reported in the aid. For $I_2(m)$, $m\ge3$, write \[ W=\langle r,s:r^m=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle,\quad t_j=r^js\ (j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad c=r. \] Every reduced factorization is $F_j=(t_j,t_{j-1})$. The above generator of $B_2$ sends $F_j$ to $F_{j+1}$, giving an $m$-cycle, or the regular action of its cyclic quotient. This supplies $m$ labels in rank two, not a faithful finite action of the infinite braid group.

For $W=\prod_{a=1}^k I_2(m_a)$ and the componentwise Coxeter element, the aid describes the ordered reduced factorizations by \[ \operatorname{Sh}(2,\ldots,2)\times \prod_{a=1}^k{\mathbb{Z}}/m_a{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Here a shuffle is a word of length $2k$ with each color $a$ appearing twice, preserving each component's two-factor order. Its cardinality is \[ \frac{(2k)!}{2^k}\prod_{a=1}^k m_a. \] A Hurwitz move between different colors swaps commuting factors; a move on adjacent equal colors advances that component's cyclic coordinate. These are shuffle-enhanced component LL labels, not a formula obtained by assigning one highest-degree Coxeter number to an arbitrary reducible group. The aid's explicit model clarifies monodromy and chains for these products; it does not construct geometric cacti for every complex or infinite reflection type.

Short English statement

The source asks for an LL-covering explanation of noncrossing partitions, trees and cacti, and for extensions beyond type A. The aid reports a general identification with reduced reflection factorizations and maximal chains in the well-generated irreducible setting. It adds explicit cyclic dihedral labels and colored shuffles for their products, while leaving general geometric cactus constructions unspecified.
