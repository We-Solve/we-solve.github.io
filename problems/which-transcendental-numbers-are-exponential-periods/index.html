Math-2053

Which transcendental numbers are (exponential) periods

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the Kontsevich--Zagier integral convention, an ordinary period is a complex number whose real and imaginary parts are absolutely convergent integrals of rational functions with rational coefficients over semialgebraic subsets of some ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. Let $\mathcal P$ be this class. Exponential periods allow suitable algebraic/rational exponential integrands, schematically \[ \int_D R(x)e^{Q(x)}\,\mathrm dx, \] with algebraic data and the required convergence conditions. The exact exponential-period category, including complex coefficients and growth conditions, must be fixed; write it $\mathcal P_{\exp}$. The ordinary class is included by taking $Q=0$.

The question asks for a description or membership criteria for \[ \mathcal P\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \qquad\text{and/or}\qquad \mathcal P_{\exp}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{Q}}, \] where $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ denotes the algebraic numbers. These are different classification questions, and the parenthetical exponential does not make their known examples interchangeable. It does not specifically request an algorithm or a classification restricted to a stated family of constants.

For example, \[ \pi=\int_{x^2+y^2\le1}1\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy \] is an ordinary period, while \[ e=\int_{-1}^{\infty}e^{-x}\,\mathrm dx \] is an exponential period in the usual integral sense. Whether $e$ is an ordinary period is not settled by that representation. Thus the research summary's tentative period discussion must distinguish ordinary from exponential membership, and must not suggest that $\pi$'s ordinary-period status is unknown.

The supplied summary invokes the Kontsevich--Zagier conjecture but gives no precise statement. Its phrase algebraicity of periods when equal is not a usable formulation: equality of period representations does not make all periods algebraic. No relation conjecture is adopted as a necessary-and-sufficient classification of transcendental constants. The source supplies a broad research direction, not a single explicit universal membership conjecture.

Short English statement

Describe which transcendental numbers are ordinary periods and which are exponential periods. Keep these classes separate: $\pi$ is an ordinary period and $e$ is an exponential period, while the supplied broad classification and conjectural discussion remain unresolved.
