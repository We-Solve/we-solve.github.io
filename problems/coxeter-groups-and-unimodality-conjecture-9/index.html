Coxeter Groups and Unimodality — Conjecture 9

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite Coxeter system. Write $\ell(w)$ for the minimum length of an expression for $w$ in the generators $S$. The Bruhat order $u\leq v$ means that a reduced expression for $u$ occurs as a subword of a reduced expression for $v$. For $u\leq v$, set $\ell(u,v)=\ell(v)-\ell(u)$.

Use the standard normalization of the polynomials $\widetilde R_{x,y}(t)$, specified by $\widetilde R_{x,x}(t)=1$, $\widetilde R_{x,y}(t)=0$ when $x\not\leq y$, and the following recurrence. If $x<y$ and $s\in S$ satisfies $\ell(ys)<\ell(y)$, then \[ \widetilde R_{x,y}(t)= \begin{cases} \widetilde R_{xs,ys}(t),&\ell(xs)<\ell(x),\\ \widetilde R_{xs,ys}(t)+t\widetilde R_{x,ys}(t),&\ell(xs)>\ell(x). \end{cases} \] For $u\leq v$, put $d=\ell(u,v)$ and let $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$ satisfy $\varepsilon\equiv d\pmod 2$. As stated in the supplied source, $\widetilde R_{u,v}$ has degree $d$, has nonnegative integer coefficients, and contains only powers congruent to $d$ modulo $2$. Consequently there is a unique polynomial $Q_{u,v}(t)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}[t]$ such that \[ \widetilde R_{u,v}(t)=t^{\varepsilon}Q_{u,v}(t^2). \] Write \[ Q_{u,v}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_jt^j, \qquad m=\frac{d-\varepsilon}{2}. \] Here log-concavity means the coefficient inequalities $a_j^2\geq a_{j-1}a_{j+1}$ for every $1\leq j\leq m-1$; the condition is vacuous when $m\leq1$.

Conjecture 9 (Brenti, 2024). For every finite Coxeter system $(W,S)$ and every pair $u,v\in W$ with $u\leq v$, the polynomial $Q_{u,v}(t)$ is log-concave. This includes $u=v$, for which $Q_{u,u}(t)=1$. The supplied source describes this conjecture as a generalization of [BreDM, Conjecture 7.1]; the bibliographic identity of [BreDM] is not supplied.

Short English statement

Brenti conjectures that, for every comparable pair in the Bruhat order of a finite Coxeter group, removing the parity restriction from the powers of its $\widetilde R$-polynomial yields a polynomial whose coefficients are log-concave. The registry labels the conjecture open; this status has not been independently verified.
