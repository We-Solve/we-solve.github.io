Linear Systems and Eigenvalue Problems — Problem 8

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer and let $A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}$ be a random matrix whose entries are independent and identically distributed. The supplied problem offers three alternative types of entry law: Gaussian, Rademacher, or Bernoulli. Write $\mathbb K$ for the scalar field, which the source does not explicitly specify, and let $b\in\mathbb K^n$ be arbitrary. A Rademacher entry conventionally takes the values $-1$ and $1$, each with probability $1/2$; the source does not specify the parameters or normalization of the Gaussian law, or the parameter and value convention of the Bernoulli law.

The source asks whether, conditioned on an event of high probability, one can compute a highly accurate solution of \[ Ax=b \] faster than the $O(n^\omega)$ benchmark. Here $\omega$ conventionally denotes the exponent of square matrix multiplication over the chosen scalar field in an arithmetic-operation model; the source does not explicitly define its computational model.

This is an incompletely specified algorithmic question. A precise formulation requires a definition of high accuracy, including an error metric, a tolerance, and its dependence on $n$; a quantitative probability bound and identification of the randomness underlying the conditioning event; and a precise asymptotic meaning of faster than $O(n^\omega)$. The source does not specify whether the running-time bound is worst-case, expected, or probabilistic, or whether bit complexity, arithmetic complexity, input precision, and output precision are to be counted. It also leaves unclear whether the high-probability event must work simultaneously for every $b$, whether $b$ must be fixed independently of $A$, and whether any algorithmic randomness is included in the success probability. For discrete entry laws, singular matrices can occur; the source does not state whether the conditioning event guarantees invertibility or how inconsistent or nonunique systems are to be handled. No choices resolving these qualifications are imposed in this draft.

Short English statement

For a random matrix with independent identically distributed Gaussian, Rademacher, or Bernoulli entries and an arbitrary right-hand side, can one solve $Ax=b$ to high accuracy, on a high-probability event, faster than the $O(n^\omega)$ matrix-multiplication benchmark? The source leaves the accuracy, probability, computational model, distribution parameters, and treatment of singular systems unspecified.
