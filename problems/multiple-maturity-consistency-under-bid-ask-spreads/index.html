Math-15091

Multiple-Maturity Consistency Under Bid–Ask Spreads

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal D$ denote the given option-price data, and let $\mathcal T$ denote their set of maturities, with $|\mathcal T|>1$. Let $\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)$ be a proposed upper bound on the discounted bid--ask spread of the underlying asset.

The requested problem is to give explicit necessary and sufficient conditions, in terms of $\mathcal D$ and $\varepsilon$, for $\varepsilon$-consistency, and to determine the smallest admissible value of $\varepsilon$.

To express the optimization target without assuming attainment, write \[ \mathcal E(\mathcal D) :=\{\varepsilon\in[0,\infty):\mathcal D\text{ is }\varepsilon\text{-consistent in the intended source sense}\}, \qquad \varepsilon_{\inf}(\mathcal D):=\inf\mathcal E(\mathcal D), \] with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. The requested smallest admissible bound exists precisely when $\mathcal E(\mathcal D)$ has a least element; in that case it equals $\varepsilon_{\inf}(\mathcal D)$. Whether this infimum is admissible is part of making the requested minimum precise.

The phrase "$\varepsilon$-consistent in the intended source sense" is an unresolved predicate here. The supplied record does not define it or specify the option contracts, the interpretation of their quoted prices, the discounting convention, the admissible underlying-price processes, or the probabilistic and pricing requirements. Thus the displayed set records the intended target but does not supply a complete mathematical definition of consistency. In particular, the record does not specify when and in what probabilistic sense the discounted spread bound must hold. These hypotheses must be recovered from the cited source before a fully quantified model-specific statement can be given.

The supplied dated assessment distinguishes this multiple-maturity problem from the one-maturity case: it reports necessary and sufficient conditions for one maturity, and necessary conditions together with equivalent semiexplicit criteria for multiple maturities, while reporting no comparably explicit necessary-and-sufficient characterization for the latter. Accordingly, the intended target appears to be a more explicit characterization, rather than merely an equivalent criterion. This interpretation and the reported literature status require review.

Short English statement

For option prices at more than one maturity, characterize when they are consistent with a prescribed bound $\varepsilon$ on the underlying asset's discounted bid--ask spread, and determine the least feasible bound, including whether it is attained. The supplied record leaves the technical meaning of consistency unspecified. Its dated curation labels the problem partially solved and identifies a more explicit multiple-maturity characterization as the remaining target.
