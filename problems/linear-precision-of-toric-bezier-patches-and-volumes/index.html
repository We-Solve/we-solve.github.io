Math-14501

Linear Precision of Toric Bezier Patches and Volumes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses three classification questions, but does not specify all the data needed for a unique mathematical formulation.

For the elementary meaning of linear precision, let $d\geq 1$, let $A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a finite set, and let $D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a parameter domain. Suppose that functions $\lambda_a:D\to{\mathbb{R}}$, indexed by $a\in A$, satisfy \[ \sum_{a\in A}\lambda_a(x)=1\qquad(x\in D). \] Such functions have linear precision if \[ \sum_{a\in A}a\,\lambda_a(x)=x\qquad(x\in D). \] Equivalently, they reproduce every affine function: \[ \forall c\in{\mathbb{R}}\;\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^d\;\forall x\in D, \qquad \sum_{a\in A}(c+v\cdot a)\lambda_a(x)=c+v\cdot x. \] This explains the affine reproduction property; it does not supply the missing definition of the toric blending functions or the admissible domains in the source question. In particular, the source does not specify whether a precision identity must hold on the entire patch domain, including its boundary, or only where the defining expressions are defined.

For a field $k$, a birational self-map of projective three-space is a rational map \[ f:{\mathbb{P}}^3_k\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^3_k \] for which there is a rational map $g:{\mathbb{P}}^3_k\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^3_k$ satisfying \[ g\circ f=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathbb{P}}^3_k},\qquad f\circ g=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathbb{P}}^3_k} \] as rational maps, hence on appropriate dense open subsets. Here $k$ is a placeholder: the source does not choose a field or its characteristic.

Requested classifications. The supplied statement asks:

  1. Which toric Bezier patches have linear precision?
  2. Which toric Bezier volumes have rational linear precision?
  3. How can birational maps of projective $3$-space be classified?
The source does not define the admissible toric patches and volumes, their lattice configurations, weights, or parameterizations. It also does not define rational linear precision or specify which rational changes of parameter are allowed. Consequently, rational linear precision is retained as an unresolved source term; it must not be replaced merely by rationality of the blending functions. No equivalence relation or other classification criterion is specified for the third question, and no precise mathematical relationship among the three requested classifications is supplied. These omissions prevent a more specific unconditional classification statement.

Short English statement

The problem asks for classifications of toric Bezier patches with linear precision, toric Bezier volumes with rational linear precision, and birational maps of projective three-space. Linear precision means reproducing affine coordinates through the blending functions. The admissible toric data, the meaning of rational linear precision, and the field and classification criterion for projective maps require clarification.
