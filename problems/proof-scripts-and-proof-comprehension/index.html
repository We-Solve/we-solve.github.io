Proof Scripts and Proof Comprehension

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A proof script, in the supplied record, is a dialogue written around a mathematical proof. The intended subject is students' comprehension of proofs; the record does not give an operational definition or measurement of that comprehension.

The supplied conjecture is: "Proof scripts provide an excellent vehicle for investigating and enhancing proof comprehension." Its accompanying question asks whether proof scripts provide an effective method both for investigating students' proof comprehension and for improving it. These are distinct diagnostic and educational claims.

This is a qualitative mathematics-education research question. A precise quantified mathematical or statistical assertion cannot be extracted from the supplied formulation: it specifies neither the student population, the proofs and dialogues involved, the instructional setting, a comparison condition, a measure of comprehension, nor a criterion for effectiveness or excellence. No universal quantifier, effect size, or causal interpretation is specified. These qualifications remain unresolved; the conjecture is retained without imposing such choices.

Short English statement

The question asks whether dialogues written around mathematical proofs help assess and improve students' understanding of proofs. The supplied record leaves the population, intervention, assessment, and standard of effectiveness unspecified, so it does not determine a precise mathematical theorem or statistical hypothesis.
