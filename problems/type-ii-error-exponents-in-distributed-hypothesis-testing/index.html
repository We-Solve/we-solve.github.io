Type-II Error Exponents in Distributed Hypothesis Testing

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Distributed binary hypothesis testing distinguishes two specified joint data laws, $P$ under $H_0$ and $Q$ under $H_1$, from length-$n$ observations split among terminals. Communication is over noiseless links with a prescribed rate budget. Type-I error is $\alpha_n=\Pr_P[\text{decide }H_1]$ and type-II error is $\beta_n=\Pr_Q[\text{decide }H_0]$. At a fixed admissible type-I error level, the type-II exponent is $\liminf_{n\to\infty}-n^{-1}\log\beta_n$. The one-sensor model sends a message to a decision center; interactive two-sensor protocols permit messages depending on previous messages.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Determine the optimal type-II error exponent for rate-limited distributed hypothesis testing and for its interactive two-sensor variant. [S2]
Scope qualification.
Resource constraints, including expected versus maximum communication length, follow the cited testing model. General joint laws are requested; special testing-against-independence results are not asserted to settle them.

Literature and context check

The primary report, p. 698, explicitly distinguishes known testing-against-independence cases from its general-law and interactive questions. It also distinguishes maximum from expected communication resources. The following elementary example works through both distinctions; it is not a solution for general joint laws.

Research attempt: An exact independence example and the expected-length distinction

Under $H_0$, let $X^n=Y^n$ be a uniform binary string; under $H_1$, let $X^n,Y^n$ be independent uniform binary strings. A deterministic sensor partitions the $2^n$ strings into at most $M$ cells $C_j$, sending the cell index. The detector sees that index and $Y^n$. Allow a randomized decision, with probability $a_j(y)\in[0,1]$ of accepting $H_0$. Put $t_j=\sum_{y\in C_j}a_j(y)$. If type-I error is at most $\epsilon<1$, then \[ \sum_jt_j\geq(1-\epsilon)2^n. \] Under independence, \[ \beta={1\over4^n}\sum_j |C_j|\sum_y a_j(y) \geq {1\over4^n}\sum_j |C_j|t_j \geq {1\over4^n}\sum_jt_j^2 \geq{(1-\epsilon)^2\over M}. \tag{2} \] The middle step uses $t_j\leq|C_j|$; the last is Cauchy--Schwarz. Empty cells cause no problem. Thus for $0\leq R\leq1$ and $M\leq2^{nR}$, the type-II exponent is at most $R\log2$. Sending the first $\lfloor nR\rfloor$ bits and accepting precisely when they match the corresponding bits of $Y^n$ gives $\alpha=0$, $\beta=2^{-\lfloor nR\rfloor}$, and exponent $R\log2$. This proves the optimum in this explicitly deterministic one-way fixed-length model. With no communication, the identical marginal law of $Y^n$ also directly gives $\beta=1-\alpha\geq1-\epsilon$.

Now permit expected length at most $nR$, and private randomness at the sensor. Fix $0<\epsilon<1$ and $0<R<1-\epsilon$. With probability $\epsilon$ send a one-bit flag directing immediate rejection of $H_0$. Otherwise send the other flag followed by \[ \ell_n=\min\{n,\lfloor(nR-1)/(1-\epsilon)\rfloor\} \] initial bits of $X^n$; for sufficiently large $n$ this is nonnegative. The detector compares these bits to its own string. The messages form a prefix-free family: one branch is a single bit, and all messages in the other branch have the same length $1+\ell_n$. The expected length is exactly $1+(1-\epsilon)\ell_n\leq nR$. Its errors are \[ \alpha=\epsilon,\qquad \beta=(1-\epsilon)2^{-\ell_n}, \] so its exponent is $R\log2/(1-\epsilon)$, strictly greater than $R\log2$. The one-bit flag is included in the resource accounting. Thus importing $2$ as a converse for expected-length messages would be a concrete error: $2$ uses a bound on the total number of possible messages, which an expected-length constraint does not supply.

Verification and adversarial audit

For $R=0$ the fixed-length formula agrees with the marginal indistinguishability argument. For $R=1$ sending the entire string gives exponent $\log2$. The expected-length comparison restricts $0<R<1-\epsilon$ so that its claimed strict improvement does not exceed the full-observation exponent. Equation $2$ is proved for deterministic encoders; no unproved extension to randomized or interactive encoders is needed for the stated auxiliary theorem.
Audit corrections.
Maximum message count and expected length are not interchanged. A private random choice and its transmitted flag are explicitly costed; the known independence special case is identified as such.

Scope and remaining gaps

Neither the fixed-length independence calculation nor the explicit variable-length construction characterizes general $P,Q$, the general expected-resource optimum, or interactive two-sensor testing. The original target remains unresolved in this attempt.
