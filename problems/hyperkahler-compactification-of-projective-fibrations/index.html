Hyperkähler Compactification of Projective Fibrations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $\pi_U:M_U\to U$ for the given smooth projective fibration and $i:U\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^n$ for the supplied inclusion. A compactification of $M_U$ means a compact space $M$ with an open immersion $j:M_U\hookrightarrow M$ having dense image, in the geometric category intended by the source.

Compactification question. Characterize the given fibrations $\pi_U$ for which there exist a smooth hyper-Kähler compactification $(M,j)$ and an everywhere-defined regular morphism $\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}^n$ satisfying \[ \pi\circ j=i\circ\pi_U. \] The requested characterization is a necessary-and-sufficient condition on the input fibration; the record does not supply such a condition.

Unresolved conventions. The source does not specify the ground field, the geometric structure or openness of $U$, the allowed values of $n$, or whether "fibration" requires surjectivity and connected fibres. The usual complex-geometric interpretation would take the ground field to be ${\mathbb{C}}$, but this is an interpretation requiring confirmation. For a compact complex manifold, an irreducible holomorphic symplectic interpretation of "hyper-Kähler" requires that $M$ be Kähler and simply connected, with \[ H^0(M,\Omega_M^2)={\mathbb{C}}\omega, \qquad \omega\text{ everywhere nondegenerate}. \] The record does not specify whether this irreducible convention or a broader hyper-Kähler convention is intended. It also does not explicitly require $M$ to be projective. If $U$ is open, the potentially stronger requirement $j(M_U)=\pi^{-1}(U)$ is not explicitly stated and must be clarified before it is imposed. Thus the displayed extension condition records the supplied target, while these missing conventions prevent a unique fully specified formulation.

Short English statement

Determine exactly when a given smooth projective fibration over a subset of projective space admits a smooth hyper-Kähler compactification whose projection extends regularly to all of projective space. The intended geometric category, hyper-Kähler convention, and precise relation over $U$ require clarification.
