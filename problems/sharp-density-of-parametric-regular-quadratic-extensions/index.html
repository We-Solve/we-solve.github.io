Sharp Density of Parametric Regular Quadratic Extensions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For context, under the usual Galois-theoretic convention, a regular quadratic extension over a field $K$ is a Galois extension $E/K(T)$ of degree $2$, where $T$ is transcendental over $K$ and $K$ is algebraically closed in $E$. Such an extension is called $K$-parametric if every quadratic Galois extension $L/K$ is $K$-isomorphic to a specialization $E_t/K$ at some $t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)$ outside the branch locus. These definitions give conventional context; the supplied record does not specify its base field or its precise convention for parametricity.

The supplied problem refers to a proportion $f(H)$ of parametric regular quadratic extensions and records the estimate \[ f(H)=1-O\!\left(\frac{\log H}{\sqrt H}\right) \qquad(H\to\infty). \] If $f(H)$ is a proportion, the displayed estimate means that there exist constants $C>0$ and $H_0>1$ such that, for every admissible height bound $H\ge H_0$, \[ 0\le 1-f(H)\le C\frac{\log H}{\sqrt H}. \] The domain of admissible height bounds, the objects being counted, their equivalence relation, the height function, and the counting measure are not supplied, so this is only an interpretation of the recorded asymptotic notation, not a definition of its counting problem.

Recorded question. Can the error term in this estimate be removed unconditionally?

Unresolved formulation. The phrase "removed unconditionally" does not identify a precise mathematical conclusion. It could request an unconditional proof of a conditional estimate, a stronger error bound, or an exact equality such as $f(H)=1$ for all sufficiently large admissible $H$. The record does not choose among these assertions. Moreover, its dated literature assessment describes an unconditional gap involving scarcity of nontrivial rational points on quadratic twists, without specifying the curves, the meaning of nontriviality, or the implication connecting that scarcity to the displayed proportion. A precise conjecture cannot therefore be reconstructed from the supplied record alone.

Short English statement

The recorded problem asks whether an error term in a density estimate for parametric regular quadratic extensions can be removed unconditionally. The base field, counting family, height, and intended improvement are unspecified, so the exact assertion remains unresolved. The supplied open label and literature assessment require independent review.
