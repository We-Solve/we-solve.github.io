The Lyapin's Notebook — Problem 36

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A semigroup is a nonempty set with an associative binary operation. A semigroup $S$ is commutative if $ab=ba$ for all $a,b\in S$, and periodic if \[ \forall s\in S\;\exists m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad 1\le m<n\ \text{ and }\ s^m=s^n. \] An Abelian group $G$, written multiplicatively with identity $1_G$, is periodic if \[ \forall g\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad n\ge1\ \text{ and }\ g^n=1_G. \] For subsets $A,B\subseteq G$, define their setwise product by \[ AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}. \] The source does not specify whether its power semigroup includes the empty subset. To display the two possible conventions, put \[ \mathcal P_0(G)=\{A:A\subseteq G\},\qquad \mathcal P_1(G)=\{A\subseteq G:A\ne\varnothing\}, \] each equipped with setwise multiplication.

For a fixed choice $\delta\in\{0,1\}$, the corresponding formulation of Problem 36 asks: for every periodic commutative semigroup $S$, do there exist a periodic Abelian group $G$ and an injective map \[ \varphi:S\longrightarrow\mathcal P_\delta(G) \] such that \[ \forall a,b\in S,\qquad \varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)? \] The two values of $\delta$ represent alternative readings of the source, which does not select one explicitly.

For the supplied background qualification, a semigroup is regular if \[ \forall a\in S\;\exists x\in S,\qquad a=axa. \] The record reports that the assertion is true for all regular commutative semigroups and for some finite non-regular commutative semigroups, citing $[\mathrm{Bersh+Kubl}]$. This is a supplied background claim, not an independently verified result. Its wording does not explicitly repeat the periodicity hypothesis for the regular case; the intended scope and the missing bibliographic reference require review.

Short English statement

Does every periodic commutative semigroup admit an injective homomorphism into a semigroup of subsets of some periodic Abelian group, with multiplication given by setwise products? The source leaves the empty-subset convention unspecified. It labels the problem open and reports affirmative regular and selected finite non-regular cases; these supplied assessments require review.
