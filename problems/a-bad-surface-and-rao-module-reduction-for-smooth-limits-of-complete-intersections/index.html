Math-1327

A bad-surface and Rao-module reduction for smooth limits of complete intersections

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. In the flat-specialization interpretation, let $\mathcal C\subset{\mathbb{P}}_R^3$ be a flat projective family of curves over a DVR with residue field $k$, with smooth geometric generic fiber a complete intersection of surfaces of degrees $1\le a\le b$ and smooth special fiber $C$. The source asks whether $C$ must also be a complete intersection. Flatness and a trait base make precise the original wording "family," "general" and "special"; they are not explicit in the original sentence.

The attached aid states that the special fiber retains \[ \deg C=ab,\qquad p_a(C)=1+\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2,\qquad \omega_C\simeq\mathcal O_C(a+b-4), \] and satisfies $H^0({\mathbb{P}}_k^3,\mathcal I_C(a))\ne0$, so it lies on a degree-$a$ surface. Define its Hartshorne--Rao module by \[ M_C=\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H^1({\mathbb{P}}_k^3,\mathcal I_C(m)). \] Within this smooth subcanonical setting, the aid reports that $C$ is a complete intersection if and only if $M_C=0$. It also reports that a smooth containing surface of degree $a$ forces $C$ to be a complete intersection.

A direct analysis of planes and quadrics of every rank is said to prove the original assertion for $a\le2$. Consequently any counterexample to this flat characteristic-zero question must have $a\ge3$, nonzero Rao module, the displayed subcanonical equality, and every degree-$a$ containing surface singular. These are simultaneous necessary conditions from the aid, not a construction or a proof of the assertion for all $a$.

The original remark records negative answers if the characteristic is positive, if the ambient projective dimension is greater than $3$, or if the special fiber is allowed to be nonsmooth, and supplies Ellia--Hartshorne as the reference for those qualifications.

Short English statement

Must a smooth characteristic-zero limit of smooth complete-intersection space curves remain a complete intersection? The supplied aid proves the low first-degree cases and restricts any counterexample to nonzero Rao module and singular minimal-degree containing surfaces, while preserving the original counterexamples outside the stated hypotheses.
