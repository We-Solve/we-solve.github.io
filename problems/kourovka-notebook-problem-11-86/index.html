Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.86

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let \[ G=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\mid r_1=\cdots=r_m=1\rangle \] be a group given by a finite presentation. Write $F_n=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle$ for the free group on the displayed generators, and let $N=\langle\!\langle r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle\!\rangle_{F_n}$ be the normal closure of its relators, so that $G=F_n/N$.

The source asks whether every such group possesses, "in a natural way," a homomorphic image admitting a presentation \[ H=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\mid s_1=\cdots=s_m=1\rangle \] with the same displayed numbers of generators and relators, satisfying each of the following requirements, considered separately:

  1. (a) $H$ is torsion-free: for every $h\in H$ and every integer $k\geq 1$, the equality $h^k=1$ implies $h=1$.
  2. (b) The integral group ring ${\mathbb{Z}}[H]$ embeds in a skew field. Here ${\mathbb{Z}}[H]$ consists of finite formal sums $\sum_{h\in H}a_hh$, with $a_h\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, with multiplication induced by the group operation. A skew field is a nonzero, possibly noncommutative ring with identity in which every nonzero element is invertible; an embedding is understood as an injective identity-preserving ring homomorphism.
If the repeated generator names mean that the quotient map sends each displayed generator of $G$ to the corresponding displayed generator of $H$, then, writing \[ M=\langle\!\langle s_1,\ldots,s_m\rangle\!\rangle_{F_n}, \] the quotient requirement is precisely $N\subseteq M$. The source does not explicitly specify this compatibility, nor does it define "in a natural way." Thus the displayed algebraic conditions do not fully specify the intended construction or its naturality requirements.

The source describes (b) as the stronger assertion and states that an affirmative answer would give an explicit method for finding displayed generators \[ x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_{n-m}} \] that generate a free group in $G$. It attributes the existence of such elements to a theorem of N. S. Romanovskiĭ. This accompanying claim requires qualification: no hypothesis $n\geq m$ is stated, although the list has no meaning when $n-m<0$. The supplied wording also does not explicitly say whether the selected generators must freely generate a subgroup of rank $n-m$. The intended constructive content linking (b) to this method remains unspecified.

Short English statement

For every finitely presented group, can one naturally construct a quotient with a presentation having the same numbers of generators and relators, such that the quotient is torsion-free, or, more strongly as described by the source, its integral group ring embeds in a skew field? The meaning of naturality and the precise free-subgroup consequence require clarification.
