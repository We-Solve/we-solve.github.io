Exponential Separation of Roots of Bounded Coefficient Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $d\geq 1$, define \[ \mathcal P_d^{\ast}=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_jX^j:\ a_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\text{ for every }j,\ (a_0,\ldots,a_d)\neq(0,\ldots,0)\right\}. \] Thus $\mathcal P_d^{\ast}$ consists of the nonzero polynomials of degree at most $d$ with the specified coefficients. Under the conventional interpretation that roots are complex numbers and that the zero polynomial is excluded, the proposed question is whether \[ \exists s>0\ \forall d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall P,Q\in\mathcal P_d^{\ast}\ \forall\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}, \qquad \left(P(\alpha)=0\ \land\ Q(\beta)=0\ \land\ \alpha\neq\beta\right) \Longrightarrow |\alpha-\beta|>s^d. \] The constant $s$ must be independent of $d,P,Q,\alpha,\beta$. The polynomials $P$ and $Q$ may coincide; the condition concerns distinct root values, without any simplicity or multiplicity assumption.

Unresolved source qualifications. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify the root domain or exclude the zero polynomial. The displayed formulation makes the complex-root interpretation and nonzero-polynomial restriction explicit, subject to human confirmation. If the zero polynomial is admitted, its treatment must be specified before the source question has an unambiguous intended meaning. The supplied wording is interpreted as allowing roots of different polynomials; it does not explicitly say that both roots must belong to one polynomial. The positive-integer range for $d$ is conventional; including $d=0$ adds only a vacuous case under the nonzero-polynomial restriction.

Short English statement

Is there one positive constant $s$ such that, for every positive integer $d$, distinct complex roots drawn from any two nonzero polynomials of degree at most $d$ with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$ are more than $s^d$ apart? The complex-root domain, exclusion of the zero polynomial, and interpretation allowing different polynomials require confirmation from the source.
