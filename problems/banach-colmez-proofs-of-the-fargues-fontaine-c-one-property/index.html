Banach–Colmez Proofs of the Fargues–Fontaine C-One Property

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A field $K$ is called $C_1$ if, for every pair of integers $d\geq 1$ and $n>d$, every homogeneous polynomial $f\in K[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ of degree $d$ has a nonzero zero: \[ \exists\,(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in K^n\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\} \quad f(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=0. \] The classical setting motivating the question is the function field $k(C)$ of an integral algebraic curve $C$ over an algebraically closed field $k$. Tsen's dimension-count argument proves the $C_1$ property in this setting.

Proposed proof-construction problem. Can Tsen's dimension-count argument be adapted to the Fargues--Fontaine curve by using Banach--Colmez spaces in place of the affine spaces occurring in the classical argument?

To express the intended arithmetic conclusion, let $X$ denote the intended integral Fargues--Fontaine curve and put $K=\kappa(\eta_X)$, the residue field of its generic point. The proposed adaptation is intended to establish the displayed $C_1$ assertion for $K$. The question concerns constructing such an adaptation, rather than merely establishing the assertion by an unspecified method.

Short English statement

The question asks whether Banach--Colmez spaces can supply a version of Tsen's dimension-count proof showing that every degree-$d$ homogeneous equation in more than $d$ variables over the intended Fargues--Fontaine function field has a nonzero solution. The curve's base data and the precise adaptation remain unspecified.
