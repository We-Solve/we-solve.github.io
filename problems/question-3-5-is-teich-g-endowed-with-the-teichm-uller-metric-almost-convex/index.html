Question 3.5 — Is Teich(Σg), endowed with the Teichm¨ uller metric, almost convex?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\operatorname{Teich}(\Sigma_g)$ be the Teichmüller space of a genus-$g$ surface with the source's marking and boundary conventions. Its Teichmüller distance is conventionally \[ d_{\mathrm T}(X,Y)=\tfrac12\inf_f\log K(f), \] where $f$ ranges over quasiconformal maps in the marking class and $K(f)$ is maximal dilatation. Question 3.5 asks whether this metric space is almost convex.

The supplied excerpt does not give the definition of almost convex for this continuous metric space. A Cannon-style metric formulation would fix a basepoint $o$ and scale $R>0$ and ask for $C_R<\infty$ such that, for every radius $r$ and all $x,y$ on the sphere about $o$ with $d_{\mathrm T}(x,y)\leq R$, there is a rectifiable path from $x$ to $y$ of length at most $C_R$ lying in the closed ball of radius $r$ about $o$. Whether the source requires this for one fixed scale, every scale, one basepoint, or uniformly over all basepoints is not supplied. This is therefore a conditional interpretation rather than an imposed definition.

For context the source attributes to Cannon [Ca] almost convexity of fundamental groups of closed negatively curved manifolds with respect to any generating set, understood in the usual finitely generated word-metric setting. It reports his conjectural extensions to finite-volume manifolds and to nonpositively curved manifolds. These discrete group statements are background and do not establish the Teichmüller metric assertion.

Short English statement

Is Teichmüller space almost convex in its Teichmüller metric? The exact bounded-path definition and its basepoint and scale quantifiers must be recovered from the source context.
