Limiting Area Distribution of Random Self-Overlapping Polygons

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each integer $n\geq 3$, let $Z_n\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2\setminus\{0\}$ be a set of exactly $n$ vectors satisfying \[ \sum_{z\in Z_n}z=0. \] Following the supplied excerpt, call $Z_n$ generic if, for every pair of nonempty disjoint subsets $U,V\subset Z_n$ with $U\cup V\neq Z_n$, the vectors \[ \sum_{u\in U}u\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{v\in V}v \] are linearly independent. The side set is described as the set of vectors corresponding to boundary segments oriented counterclockwise.

The excerpt reports a theorem, attributed there to reference [2], asserting that the number of self-overlapping polygons with a fixed generic side set $Z_n$ is $(n-2)!$. However, the supplied record does not define precisely which self-overlapping polygons or immersed disks are counted, their equivalence relation, or the meaning of their area. Thus this count is preserved as a supplied assertion and does not by itself specify a probability space.

Once those definitions are supplied, write $\mathcal P(Z_n)$ for the intended finite collection, let $P_n$ be uniform on $\mathcal P(Z_n)$, and let $A_n$ be its intended area. If the reported counting convention applies, uniformity means \[ \Pr(P_n=P)=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\qquad(P\in\mathcal P(Z_n)). \] The requested limiting assertion has the formal shape: there exists a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on ${\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ A_n-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log n\ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm d}\ \mu, \] where $\log$ is the natural logarithm. Equivalently, for every bounded continuous function $f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[f\!\left(A_n-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log n\right)\right] =\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)\,\mu(\mathrm dx). \] The cleaned statement additionally asks to identify $\mu$.

Unresolved qualifications. This is a conditional statement template, not a fully specified conjecture. A limiting problem requires a sequence $(Z_n)_{n\geq3}$, together with its normalization and any asymptotic hypotheses. Genericity alone fixes no geometric scale: multiplying every side vector by a positive constant preserves genericity, while ordinary geometric area scales quadratically. Consequently, the stated centering cannot be assigned an unrestricted scope over arbitrary generic side sets without further information. It is also unspecified whether area means signed area, area counted with multiplicity, or area of the planar image. The excerpt's phrase "this random area" has an incomplete antecedent, so its connection to the fixed-side-set polygon family must be checked. Neither dependence of $\mu$ on the side-set sequence nor universality is specified reliably by the supplied material.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether the area of a uniformly sampled self-overlapping polygon, after subtracting $\frac{1}{2\pi}\log n$, converges in distribution; the cleaned statement also asks for the limiting law. A precise formulation still requires the polygon model, area convention, and a normalized sequence of generic side sets. The supplied open label and logarithmic expectation assessment are dated curation, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.
