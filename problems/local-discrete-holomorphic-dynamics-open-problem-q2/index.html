Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt concerns a holomorphic local dynamical system at a point $p$, but does not explicitly define either that term or $K_f$. The following formulation is conditional on the usual interpretation: $M$ is a finite-dimensional complex manifold, $U\subseteq M$ is an open neighbourhood of $p$, and $f\colon U\to M$ is holomorphic with $f(p)=p$. Define the forward-staying set by \[ K_f(U)=\{x\in U: f^j(x)\text{ is defined and belongs to }U\text{ for every integer }j\geq 0\}. \] Here $f^0(x)=x$, and subsequent iterates are defined recursively while their inputs lie in $U$. Equip $K_f(U)$ with the topology induced by $M$. This interpretation makes $f\colon K_f(U)\to K_f(U)$ a dynamical system. The excerpt does not confirm this definition of $K_f$, fix the neighbourhood $U$, or specify how the requested description should depend on shrinking $U$.

Problem Q2, conditional on this interpretation. For a given holomorphic local dynamical system $f$ at $p$, determine the dynamical structure of $(K_f(U),f)$. In particular, describe the asymptotic behaviour of the forward orbits $(f^j(x))_{j\geq 0}$, for $x\in K_f(U)$. Determine which such orbits converge to $p$, and whether other behaviour, described in the source as chaotic, occurs. Determine whether there exists $x\in K_f(U)$ such that \[ \overline{\{f^j(x):j\geq 0\}}^{\,K_f(U)}=K_f(U). \] Describe proper forward-invariant subsets $L\subsetneq K_f(U)$, meaning subsets satisfying $f(L)\subseteq L$, and the restricted dynamics $f|_L\colon L\to L$. The source does not define chaotic behaviour or impose nonemptiness, closedness, or another nontriviality condition on $L$; these qualifications remain unresolved.

The excerpt proposes comparison with a simpler system, for example a linear system, by local conjugacy. Its supplied definition is as follows. Let $f_i\colon U_i\to M_i$ be holomorphic local dynamical systems at $p_i$, for $i\in\{1,2\}$. They are holomorphically, smoothly, $C^k$, or topologically locally conjugated if there are open neighbourhoods $W_i\subseteq U_i$ of $p_i$ and a map $\varphi\colon W_1\to W_2$, respectively a biholomorphism, a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism, a $C^k$ diffeomorphism for a positive integer $k$, or a homeomorphism, such that $\varphi(p_1)=p_2$ and \[ \varphi^{-1}\left(W_2\cap f_2^{-1}(W_2)\right) =W_1\cap f_1^{-1}(W_1), \qquad f_1=\varphi^{-1}\circ f_2\circ\varphi \] on that common domain. The source states that such a conjugacy identifies the corresponding forward-staying sets: \[ K_{f_2|_{W_2}}=\varphi\left(K_{f_1|_{W_1}}\right). \] It also explains that local coordinates centred at $p$ reduce the setting to holomorphic local systems fixing $0\in{\mathbb{C}}^n$, where $n=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}M$, denoted there by $\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{C}}^n,0)$. These are contextual tools for the dynamical-description question; the excerpt does not assert that every system admits a linear model.

Short English statement

Abate's Problem Q2 asks for a description of local holomorphic dynamics: orbit behaviour, dense orbits, and dynamics on proper forward-invariant subsets. The excerpt discusses local conjugacy as a method of comparison. Its notation $K_f$ and its use of chaotic behaviour are not defined in the supplied record, so the displayed formulation explicitly uses a conditional forward-staying-set interpretation and requires human review.
