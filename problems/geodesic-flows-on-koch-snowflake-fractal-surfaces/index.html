Math-15644

Geodesic Flows on Koch Snowflake Fractal Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K_n$ denote the polygonal approximations in the Koch-snowflake construction, and let $K$ denote their limiting snowflake domain. The supplied record does not specify the indexing, normalization, or precise surface construction associated with these approximations.

Under the usual translation-surface interpretation, let $(X_n,\omega_n)$ be a chosen unfolding of the rational polygon $K_n$. A translation surface has coordinate changes given by translations away from its singularities. Its Veech group is \[ \Gamma_n=\{D f: f\in\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(X_n,\omega_n)\}\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{R}}), \] where $\operatorname{Aff}^{+}$ denotes the orientation-preserving affine self-homeomorphisms in translation coordinates. This interpretation supplies terminology; the record does not identify the particular unfoldings or any compatible maps between them.

An inverse limit of these groups requires specified homomorphisms $p_n:\Gamma_{n+1}\to\Gamma_n$. Given such maps, its underlying group would be \[ \varprojlim(\Gamma_n,p_n) =\left\{(g_n)_{n\geq 0}\in\prod_{n\geq 0}\Gamma_n: p_n(g_{n+1})=g_n\text{ for every }n\geq 0\right\}. \] No bonding homomorphisms are supplied, so this expression does not yet define a particular group. A topology would also need to be specified if a topological or dynamical role is intended.

Source question, with unresolved qualifications retained. Does the inverse limit of the Veech groups of the prefractal Koch-snowflake surfaces define a dynamically relevant object yielding a well-defined geodesic flow on a fractal flat surface dynamically equivalent to the Koch-snowflake billiard flow?

For this question to become a precise existence statement, one must specify the inverse system, the limiting surface and its flat or metric structure, its geodesic phase space and treatment of singularities, and a definition and domain of the billiard flow in $K$. The ordinary polygonal reflection rule does not by itself specify reflection at points of the limiting boundary without a tangent, or continuation through singular collisions. One must also specify the sense of dynamical equivalence, including whether it preserves time and whether it holds everywhere or only on a specified invariant subset. The supplied record makes none of these choices, and no particular choice is asserted here. Thus the retained target is a conjectural construction program rather than a fully specified theorem or conjecture.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether a compatible limit of the Veech groups of Koch-snowflake approximations can produce a fractal flat surface whose geodesic dynamics correspond to billiard dynamics in the limiting snowflake. The connecting maps, limiting geometry, boundary dynamics, and meaning of equivalence remain unspecified. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
