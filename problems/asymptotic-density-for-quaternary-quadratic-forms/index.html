Asymptotic Density for Quaternary Quadratic Forms

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $Q$ be a quaternary quadratic form, meaning a homogeneous quadratic expression in four variables. The supplied record does not specify its coefficient domain or any hypotheses concerning nondegeneracy, signature, or isotropy. For a real radius $r>0$, define \[ N_Q(r):=\#\left\{x=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^4:\ |x_i|\le r\text{ for }i=1,2,3,4,\ Q(x)=0\right\}. \] This count includes the zero vector and all integral solutions, without a primitivity restriction.

The supplied question asks for an appropriate main term $M_T(r)$ and asks whether the error \[ E_Q(r):=N_Q(r)-M_T(r) \] has an asymptotic density, after the normalization intended by the source, as $r\to\infty$.

Unresolved formulation. The record does not define $M_T(r)$, the subscript $T$, or the normalization. Its edited statement refers to a normalized error, whereas its displayed expression only subtracts the main term. Moreover, it does not define asymptotic density: in particular, it specifies neither a distribution or level set whose density is sought nor how radii are sampled. The admissible class of forms and whether the question concerns every such form or a particular form are also unspecified. Consequently, the supplied evidence determines the counting function above and a research question about its error, but does not determine a unique mathematical assertion about that error. These qualifications must be resolved before a precise density conjecture can be stated.

Short English statement

For a quadratic form in four variables, count its integral zeros in the box $[-r,r]^4$, subtract a suitable main term, and ask whether the resulting error has an asymptotic density under the intended normalization. The source leaves the class of forms, main term, normalization, and meaning of density unspecified, so this remains a qualified statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
