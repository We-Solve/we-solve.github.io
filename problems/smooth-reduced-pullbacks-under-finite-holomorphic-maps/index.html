Math-1697

Smooth reduced pullbacks under finite holomorphic maps

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f:({\mathbb{C}}^n,0)\to({\mathbb{C}}^n,0)$ be a finite holomorphic map germ, meaning that the induced homomorphism of convergent power-series local rings makes the source ring a finite module over the target ring. Let $(V,0)$ be a reduced complex analytic variety germ. Give its set-theoretic inverse image the reduced structure, \[ W=\left(f^{-1}(V)\right)_{\rm red}. \] The question proposed by Linda Rothschild is whether smoothness of $W$ at zero forces smoothness of $V$ at zero. Smoothness here means that the complex analytic germ is a complex submanifold, or equivalently that its local analytic ring is regular. It is essential that the hypothesis concerns the reduced inverse image, rather than requiring the entire scheme-theoretic pullback to be smooth.

The source records an affirmative answer when $\dim V=1$, and gives the following positive-characteristic warning. Over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic two, take \[ f(z_1,z_2,z_3)=(z_1^2,z_2^2,z_3+z_1z_2),\qquad V=\{w_3^2=w_1w_2\}\subset k^3. \] The pulled-back equation is \[ (z_3+z_1z_2)^2-z_1^2z_2^2=z_3(z_3+2z_1z_2). \] In characteristic two its reduced zero set is the smooth hyperplane $z_3=0$, although $V$ is singular. The scheme pullback has equation $z_3^2=0$ and is nonreduced. Over ${\mathbb{C}}$ the two distinct hypersurfaces in the factorization do not give a smooth inverse image at the origin, so this is not a complex-analytic counterexample.

The supplied research aid reports affirmative curve, hypersurface, and geometric-complete-intersection cases, leaving an unrestricted non-complete-intersection remainder unresolved by the sources it says it verified. For irreducible reduced $V$ and irreducible reduced $W$, it reports a degree identity. Let $d=\deg f$, let $\delta=\deg(f|_W)$ be the generic degree of the finite map onto $V$, and let $\nu$ be the generic length of the scheme-theoretic inverse image along its reduced support $W$, measured over its residue field. Then \[ d=\nu\delta. \] If $W$ is a smooth hypersurface with local equation $t=0$, the aid reports that a reduced defining equation of $V$ pulls back to a unit times $t^\nu$, and \[ \operatorname{ord}_W(\det Df)\ge\nu-1. \] The aid further reports a necessary-condition sieve for any remaining counterexample: the target would have to be normal, prefactorial, singular and non-lci, in ambient dimension at least four; its reduced inverse image would lie in the ramification divisor; the map would be nontriangular, of composite degree, with non-birational restriction to $W$. Here non-lci means not a local complete intersection. The precise meanings and hypotheses for "geometric complete intersection," "prefactorial," and triangularity, and the underlying literature references, are not fully supplied, so this sieve is retained as a reported reduction rather than an independently established classification.

The source motivation is a related but distinct question: for a real-analytic submanifold germ $M\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, when is $f(M)$ a manifold? It refers to [ER] without identifying that reference.

Short English statement

If the reduced inverse image of a complex variety under a finite holomorphic self-map is smooth, must the variety be smooth? The source distinguishes this complex-analytic problem from a characteristic-two counterexample, and the supplied aid reports special affirmative cases and degree constraints on any remaining counterexample.
