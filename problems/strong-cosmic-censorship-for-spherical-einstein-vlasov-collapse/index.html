Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The Einstein--Vlasov system couples $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}[f]$ to collisionless transport of a nonnegative phase-space density $f$ along the spacetime geodesic flow on the particle mass shell. The stress-energy tensor is obtained by integrating particle momenta against $f$. Asymptotically flat initial data describe an isolated system; spherical symmetry reduces the metric and matter fields to radial variables. Weak cosmic censorship asks for complete future null infinity for generic admissible data. Strong cosmic censorship concerns inextendibility of the maximal globally hyperbolic development in a specified regularity class. In the source's advanced Eddington--Finkelstein coordinates $(v,r)$, $ds^2=-a(v,r)b(v,r)^2\,dv^2+2b(v,r)\,dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, with $b>0$. A sphere of constant $v,r$ is trapped when $a<0$. The cited class of initial data begins with $a>0$ and is arranged so that a trapped surface subsequently forms. A curvature singularity means blow-up of curvature in the sense to be specified by the collapse statement.
Source scope and qualifications.
The claim concerns the particular shell-like initial-data class in the report's cited collapse theorem, not arbitrary data merely satisfying a>0 somewhere. The report proves future completeness of the event-horizon generator but only conjectures the central singularity and strong censorship. A regularity class for inextendibility remains necessary.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For spherically symmetric Einstein–Vlasov initial data in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates that evolve from $a>0$ to form a trapped surface, must a curvature singularity arise at the center, and does strong cosmic censorship hold?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: For the specified trapped-surface data, the report itself states only a conjecture that a central curvature singularity forms and strong cosmic censorship holds; no complete proof of this claim was found. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

For the prescribed spherical collapse data, prove formation of a central curvature singularity and strong cosmic censorship.

A central regularity requirement.
For the source metric, inversion of the $(v,r)$ block gives $g^{rr}=a$. Define the spherical mass function by $m(v,r)=r(1-a(v,r))/2$. Trapping $a<0$ at a sphere is therefore exactly $2m/r>1$ there. At a smooth regular spherical center, the areal radius agrees to first order with proper radial distance and the radial metric in regular local coordinates has expansion $1+O(r^2)$. Under this regular-center hypothesis $a=1+O(r^2)$, hence \[ m(v,r)=O(r^3)\qquad(r\downarrow0). \] Thus proving failure of this bound along an approaching central region would obstruct that regular-center geometry.
Trapping at a positive radius is insufficient by itself.
Smooth nonnegative mass profiles can satisfy $m(r)=cr^3$ near zero and $2m(r_0)/r_0>1$ at a positive radius. For example $m(r)=cr^3$ up to $r_0$ has the latter property when $2cr_0^2>1$. This is only a mass-profile example, not asserted to solve the Einstein--Vlasov constraints or evolution; it shows that the algebraic trapped-sphere inequality does not imply a central failure of the cubic bound at that time.
Remaining dynamical step.
The source already supplies a shell class forming a trapped surface and future completeness of the event-horizon generator. One still needs estimates forcing concentration toward the center and curvature blowup, then an inextendibility argument in a specified regularity class. The displayed local mass identities do not prove either. In particular central loss of smoothness would not automatically establish the strongest low-regularity versions of cosmic censorship. Both requested conclusions remain unresolved for the actual source data.
