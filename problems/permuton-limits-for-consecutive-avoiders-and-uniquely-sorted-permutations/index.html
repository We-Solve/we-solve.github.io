Math-14713

Permuton Limits for Consecutive Avoiders and Uniquely Sorted Permutations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each integer $n\geq 1$, let $S_n$ be the set of permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A sequence of distinct numbers has pattern $\tau\in S_k$ if its entries have the same relative order as those of $\tau$. A permutation $\pi\in S_n$ contains $\tau$ classically if some subsequence $\pi(i_1),\ldots,\pi(i_k)$, with $i_1<\cdots<i_k$, has pattern $\tau$. It contains $\tau$ consecutively if such an occurrence can be chosen with $i_j=i_1+j-1$ for every $j$. Avoidance means the absence of the relevant occurrence; in particular, a pattern of length greater than $n$ is automatically avoided. Write $\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau)$ and $\operatorname{Av}^{\mathrm{con}}_n(\tau)$ for the respective avoidance sets. For a fixed set $B$ of nonempty patterns, put \[ \operatorname{Av}^{\mathrm{con}}_n(B)=\bigcap_{\tau\in B}\operatorname{Av}^{\mathrm{con}}_n(\tau). \] The supplied consecutive-avoidance question does not specify $B$, its cardinality, or any restrictions on its members.

Define the West stack-sorting map on words with distinct entries recursively by \[ s(\varnothing)=\varnothing,\qquad s(LmR)=s(L)s(R)m, \] where $m$ is the largest entry of the word and juxtaposition denotes concatenation. Its restriction maps $S_n$ to $S_n$. Define \[ U_n=\{\pi\in S_n:|\{\sigma\in S_n:s(\sigma)=\pi\}|=1\}, \qquad T_n=s(S_n). \] Thus $U_n$ consists of uniquely sorted permutations, and $T_n$ consists of sorted permutations. For a fixed pattern $\tau\in S_k$, where $k\geq1$, possible pattern-restricted families are \[ U_n\cap\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau),\quad T_n\cap\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau), \] and their consecutive-avoidance counterparts. The source does not explicitly specify the avoidance convention for these sorted families.

A permuton is a Borel probability measure on $[0,1]^2$ whose two coordinate marginals are Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$. Associate to $\pi\in S_n$ the permuton $\mu_\pi$ with density $n$ on the union of the squares \[ \left(\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}\right]\times \left(\frac{\pi(i)-1}{n},\frac{\pi(i)}{n}\right], \qquad 1\leq i\leq n, \] and density zero elsewhere; choices on square boundaries do not affect the measure. Permuton convergence means weak convergence of measures.

Problem, with the limiting interpretation unresolved. Determine the permuton limits associated with consecutive-pattern avoiders, uniquely sorted permutations, and uniquely sorted permutations avoiding a fixed pattern. The supplied original excerpt additionally asks for the analogous investigation for sorted permutations, including pattern-restricted sorted permutations.

One precise deterministic interpretation, for any specified sequence of families $\mathcal C=(\mathcal C_n)_{n\geq1}$, is to characterize \[ \mathcal L(\mathcal C)=\left\{\mu:\begin{array}{l} \text{there are strictly increasing }n_j\to\infty\text{ and }\pi_j\in\mathcal C_{n_j}\\ \text{such that }\mu_{\pi_j}\Rightarrow\mu \end{array}\right\}. \] Here the relevant families are the avoidance and sorting families above, once the forbidden patterns and avoidance convention have been fixed. If the family is nonempty at only finitely many sizes, this set is empty.

A distinct probabilistic interpretation is to choose $\Pi_n$ uniformly from each nonempty $\mathcal C_n$ and determine the limiting law, or all subsequential limiting laws, of $\mu_{\Pi_n}$ as $n\to\infty$ through those sizes. A deterministic limiting permuton would mean that these laws converge to a point mass. Uniform sampling, convergence to a deterministic measure, and the choice between this probabilistic question and the deterministic limit-set question are not specified by the supplied record. These are explicit alternative formulations requiring review, not additional assumptions attributed to the source.

Short English statement

Describe the limiting permutation diagrams of consecutive-pattern avoiders and uniquely sorted permutations, including pattern-restricted versions. The original excerpt also proposes the same investigation for sorted permutations. The record leaves the forbidden consecutive patterns, the avoidance convention for sorted families, and the distinction between possible limits and limits of random permutations unresolved.
