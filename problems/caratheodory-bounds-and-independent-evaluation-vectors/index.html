Carathéodory Bounds and Independent Evaluation Vectors

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record contains a conjecture and a separate question about evaluation vectors. It does not define the notation sufficiently to identify a unique mathematical formulation.

For clarity, the following definitions describe a conditional homogeneous-form interpretation. Let $n\geq1$ and $d\geq0$ be integers, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $2d$ in $n$ variables. For a basis $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ of $V$ and a nonzero vector $v\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$, define \[ s_V(v)=(b_1(v),\ldots,b_m(v))\in{\mathbb{R}}^m. \] For $f\in V$, its real projective zero set is \[ Z_{{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}(f)=\{[v]\in{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}:f(v)=0\}. \] Homogeneity makes this zero set well defined. Replacing a representative $v$ by $tv$, where $t\neq0$, multiplies its evaluation vector by $t^{2d}$; hence linear independence of evaluation vectors is independent of the chosen representatives and of the basis of $V$.

For such a space, one possible moment cone is \[ \mathcal M(V)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_j s_V(v_j): r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\ w_j>0,\ v_j\in{\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus\{0\}\right\}. \] Its Carathéodory number is the least nonnegative integer $C$, if one exists, such that every element of $\mathcal M(V)$ has such a representation with at most $C$ summands. A corresponding maximal finite projective-zero count would be \[ \max\left\{\#Z_{{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}(f): f\in V,\ f(x)\geq0\ \text{for all }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n,\ \#Z_{{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}}(f)<\infty\right\}, \] provided this maximum exists. These definitions explain a possible interpretation only: the supplied record does not identify the space, cone, or class of forms used to define its $C_{2d}$ and $\beta(2d)$, or confirm that its notation $B_{n,2d}$ denotes this kind of space.

The conjecture as supplied is that, for every integer $d\geq3$, \[ \beta(2d)\leq C_{2d}\leq\beta(2d)+1. \] Its numerical meaning remains unresolved until the definitions of both quantities and their dependence on the number of variables are specified.

The accompanying question asks whether, whenever $f\in B_{n,2d}$ is nonnegative on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ and has finite projective zero set consisting of distinct points \[ \{z_1,\ldots,z_k\}, \] the vectors \[ s_{B_{n,2d}}(z_1),\ldots,s_{B_{n,2d}}(z_k) \] are linearly independent over ${\mathbb{R}}$. Under the conditional interpretation $B_{n,2d}=V$ above, choose nonzero representatives $v_i$ of $z_i$. The question is then whether \[ \left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i g(v_i)=0\ \text{for every }g\in V\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_1=\cdots=a_k=0 \] for every $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in{\mathbb{R}}^k$. If $k=0$, the empty family is linearly independent by convention. The source does not specify the ranges of $n,d$ for this separate question; its explicit restriction $d\geq3$ belongs to the conjecture. The projective space being real and the interpretation of $B_{n,2d}$ as a homogeneous polynomial space require confirmation from the source.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture places the Carathéodory number between a maximal finite projective-zero count and that count plus one, for $d\geq3$. A separate question asks whether the evaluations at all finitely many projective zeros of a nonnegative form are linearly independent. The defining spaces, parameter ranges, and meanings of several symbols require source confirmation. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
