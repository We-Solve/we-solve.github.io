Density and Closure of Mixed Hodge Loci

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety and let $V$ be a variation of mixed Hodge structures over $S$, as in the supplied statement. Write $\operatorname{HL}(S,V)\subseteq S$ for the Hodge locus intended by the source, subject to the unresolved conventions below. Define its Zariski closure by \[ Z(S,V):=\overline{\operatorname{HL}(S,V)}^{\mathrm{Zar}} =\bigcap_{\substack{Y\subseteq S\text{ Zariski closed}\\ \operatorname{HL}(S,V)\subseteq Y}}Y. \] Thus the Hodge locus is Zariski dense in $S$ precisely when $Z(S,V)=S$.

The requested problem is to determine, for the pairs $(S,V)$ under consideration, the geometric restrictions on $Z(S,V)$, and to characterize those pairs for which \[ \overline{\operatorname{HL}(S,V)}^{\mathrm{Zar}}=S. \] The source asks for a characterization and does not propose a particular necessary or sufficient condition.

The meaning of the Hodge locus requires further specification. Under a rational mixed Hodge structure convention, a Hodge class in a fiber $H$ means a morphism ${\mathbb{Q}}(0)\to H$ of mixed Hodge structures, where ${\mathbb{Q}}(0)$ is the one-dimensional rational Hodge structure of weight zero and type $(0,0)$. A Hodge locus can refer to loci where locally flat classes become Hodge classes, possibly in specified tensor constructions and Tate twists of $V$. The record does not specify those constructions, the coefficient lattice or field, or whether classes that persist generically are excluded. These choices are material: including the zero class without an exceptional-locus convention would give all of $S$.

The usual setting involves a complex algebraic variety and a variation on its associated analytic space, but the record does not explicitly state the base field. It also does not specify admissibility, polarizability of the weight-graded pieces, or connectedness or irreducibility of $S$. These hypotheses and the precise definition of $\operatorname{HL}(S,V)$ must be fixed before the requested characterization has a unique mathematical meaning; none is silently imposed here.

Short English statement

For a variation of mixed Hodge structures $V$ on a smooth quasi-projective variety $S$, describe the possible Zariski closures of its Hodge locus and characterize when that locus is Zariski dense in $S$. The source leaves the Hodge-locus convention and several hypotheses unspecified, so this is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
