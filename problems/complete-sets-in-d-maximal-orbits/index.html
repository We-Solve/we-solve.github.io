Math-14031

Complete Sets in D-Maximal Orbits

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A subset of ${\mathbb{N}}$ is computably enumerable (c.e.) if an algorithm enumerates its elements; this uses the usual natural-number coding convention. The supplied record does not define $D$-maximality, specify the action defining the orbits, identify which orbits are called "remaining," or specify the reducibility intended by "complete." Consequently, the following is a precise conditional formulation of its quantifiers, pending those definitions.

Let $\mathscr O$ denote the intended family of orbits of $D$-maximal c.e. sets. The source describes $\mathscr O$ as infinite. Let $\mathscr R\subseteq\mathscr O$ be the subfamily referred to as the remaining orbits, and let $\operatorname{Comp}(A)$ mean that $A$ is complete in the intended, presently unspecified sense. The question is whether \[ \exists F\subseteq\mathscr R\quad \left[|F|<\infty\ \land\ \forall O\in\mathscr R\setminus F\; \exists A\in O\;\operatorname{Comp}(A)\right]. \] Equivalently, is the family \[ \left\{O\in\mathscr R:\nexists A\in O\;\operatorname{Comp}(A)\right\} \] finite? The definitions of $\mathscr O$, $\mathscr R$, and $\operatorname{Comp}$ must be recovered from the cited report before this becomes a fully specified mathematical problem. Infinitude of $\mathscr O$ alone does not assert infinitude of $\mathscr R$.

Short English statement

Must every remaining orbit of $D$-maximal computably enumerable sets, with at most finitely many exceptions, contain a complete set? The supplied record leaves the orbit action, completeness notion, definition of $D$-maximality, and scope of "remaining" unspecified.
