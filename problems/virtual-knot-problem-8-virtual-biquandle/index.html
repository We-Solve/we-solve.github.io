Virtual-knot problem 8 — Virtual Biquandle

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A diagrammatic biquandle presentation assigns generators to semiarcs and crossing relations to their labels. A switch $B:X^2\to X^2$ describes a classical crossing (with $B^{-1}$ for the inverse crossing), and a virtual switch $V:X^2\to X^2$ describes a virtual crossing. The operations must satisfy the identities implementing all allowed classical, virtual, and mixed Reidemeister moves, including the appropriate nondegeneracy and type-I conditions. The excerpt does not fix a particular convention for these axioms.

Construction problem. Construct presentations of a virtual biquandle with a linear, noncommutative representation at classical crossings and interesting structure at virtual crossings. In a switch formulation the classical switch should admit the requested linear realization, for instance as an invertible block matrix on $X\oplus X$ for a module $X$ over a noncommutative coefficient ring. A free right-module convention makes left matrix multiplication a right-linear map; the source does not choose a ring or a module side. The virtual operation must be compatible with the classical one and give the intended additional structure.

For example, writing $B_1=B\times\operatorname{id}$, $B_2=\operatorname{id}\times B$, and similarly for $V$, compatibility in a standard braid-switch convention includes \[ B_1B_2B_1=B_2B_1B_2,\qquad V^2=\operatorname{id},\qquad V_1V_2V_1=V_2V_1V_2,\qquad V_1V_2B_1=B_2V_1V_2. \] These illustrate necessary braid-level relations in that convention, not a replacement for the full biquandle axioms or an asserted solution. A proposed presentation must also verify the remaining move relations and explain what mathematical information its virtual-crossing structure provides. The adjective "interesting" supplies no precise nontriviality or distinguishing criterion, and the meaning of "linear (non-commutative)" is not specified beyond the source wording.

The source credits Bartholomew and Fenn with an initial computer search producing various biquandle decorations at both classical and virtual crossings. The cited paper's bibliographic details and examples are not supplied.

Short English statement

Build virtual-biquandle presentations with a noncommutative linear rule at classical crossings and meaningful compatible rules at virtual crossings. Full move invariance and the intended significance of the virtual structure must be specified; the source reports preliminary computer-search examples by Bartholomew and Fenn.
