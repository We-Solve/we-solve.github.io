q-Hodge Filtrations and Habiro Descent

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q$ be an indeterminate and put $A={\mathbb{Z}}[[q-1]]$. For a smooth ${\mathbb{Z}}$-algebra $R$, write $\Omega^\bullet_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}$ for its de Rham complex, with Hodge filtration \[ F^i\Omega^\bullet_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}=\Omega^{\geq i}_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}. \] The induced filtration on de Rham cohomology is \[ F^iH^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(R/{\mathbb{Z}}) =\operatorname{im}\!\left( H^n(F^i\Omega^\bullet_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}})\longrightarrow H^n(\Omega^\bullet_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}})\right). \] The supplied questions do not specify their full class of geometric inputs. Smooth ${\mathbb{Z}}$-algebras are explicitly mentioned in the accompanying qualification, so the notation here describes that setting without asserting that it is the complete intended scope.

Question A: deformation of the Hodge filtration. Does the Hodge filtration admit a $q$-deformation on the intended $q$-de Rham object? At the complex level, let $K_R=q\text{-}\Omega_R$ denote that object over $A$. A filtered deformation would require a decreasing filtration $F_q^\bullet K_R$ and a filtered specialization equivalence \[ \left(F_q^\bullet K_R\right)\otimes_A^{\mathbf L} A/(q-1) \simeq \left(F^\bullet\Omega^\bullet_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}\right). \] This equation gives a complex-level interpretation of the question; the supplied record does not define $K_R$, specify the relevant category or completion conventions, or decide whether the requested filtration is on the complex or only on its cohomology. Any additional requirements of canonicity, functoriality, multiplicativity, or completeness must therefore be specified before this becomes a fully determined existence problem.

Question B: Habiro descent. The supplied coefficient ring is written \[ \mathcal H=\varprojlim_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}} {\mathbb{Z}}[q]^{\wedge}_{(q^m-1)}. \] For a positive integer $m$, the displayed completion means \[ {\mathbb{Z}}[q]^{\wedge}_{(q^m-1)} =\varprojlim_{k\geq1}{\mathbb{Z}}[q]/\left((q^m-1)^k\right). \] The record does not specify the indexing order or transition maps for the outer inverse limit, or its convention for ${\mathbb{N}}$; its formula is retained subject to that qualification. Once this inverse system and the intended coefficient map $\iota:\mathcal H\to A$ are fixed, the question asks whether $q$-de Rham cohomology descends along $\iota$. A complex-level interpretation asks for an object $D_R$ over $\mathcal H$, together with an equivalence \[ D_R\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathcal H,\iota}A\simeq K_R, \] or the corresponding completed derived base-change equivalence if the intended category requires completion. The source does not specify which interpretation is intended, whether descent concerns individual cohomology groups or a complex, or which structures and naturality must descend.

Supplied qualifications. The original excerpt states that Question A has a negative answer in general. It also announces, as upcoming work, that for every smooth ${\mathbb{Z}}$-algebra $R$ such that every prime \[ p\leq\dim(R/{\mathbb{Z}}) \] is invertible in $R$, the complex $q\text{-}\Omega_R$ admits a canonical $q$-deformation of the Hodge filtration. The supplied dated literature assessment further reports that canonical $q$-Hodge filtrations exist after inverting finitely many small primes and that Habiro descent is proved whenever such a filtration exists. These are source-reported qualifications, not independently verified assertions here. The record labels the problem partially solved and says that the unrestricted questions are not fully settled; it does not identify a precise remaining conjecture or all hypotheses of the reported descent result.

Short English statement

The record asks whether the Hodge filtration has a $q$-deformation and whether $q$-de Rham cohomology descends from ${\mathbb{Z}}[[q-1]]$ to the displayed Habiro ring. It reports a general obstruction to the first question, a canonical construction under a small-prime invertibility hypothesis, and conditional descent. The exact remaining target, descent category, and inverse-limit conventions require clarification.
