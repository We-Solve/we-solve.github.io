Boundaries of Groups and Kleinian Groups — Problem 69

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a metric space $(X,d)$ with basepoint $x_0$, normalized distance functions are \[ h_y(x)=d(x,y)-d(x_0,y). \] A standard horofunction is an appropriate limit of these functions as $y$ escapes, normalized by $h(x_0)=0$; for proper spaces one commonly uses uniform convergence on compact sets. Whether the source allows all metric-function limits or only boundary points is not stated.

Let $x_n=x_n(\omega)$ be the intended random path and, when the limit is finite and exists, write \[ A(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n d(x_0,x_n(\omega)). \] The source gives neither the probability law nor the metric/cocycle assumptions ensuring this drift. It does not say whether ergodicity makes $A$ constant.

Conjecture attributed to Anders Karlsson. Almost surely there exists one horofunction $h=h_\omega$ such that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac1n h(x_n(\omega))=A(\omega). \] The horofunction may depend on the path; no measurable-selection requirement is explicitly supplied.

The record separately reports Karlsson's approximation theorem with quantifiers \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists h_\varepsilon\ \exists N(\varepsilon)\ \forall n\geq N(\varepsilon):\qquad A-\varepsilon\leq-\frac1n h_\varepsilon(x_n)\leq A+\varepsilon. \] Here $h_\varepsilon$ is fixed for that tolerance and the whole subsequent tail, but may change with $\varepsilon$. This is not silently identified with one $h$ satisfying the exact limit. The hypotheses and probability scope of the cited approximation theorem are absent.

The research summary marks the exact statement solved and invokes horofunction drift, Kingman-style arguments, and Karlsson--Margulis. It names Karlsson's "Non-reversibility and equivalent conditions for convexity" without a precise matching theorem. These attributed claims do not authenticate the missing path model, drift conditions, or exact result in this excerpt. Neighboring local registry entries were checked and do not supply that context.

Short English statement

For the intended random path with linear escape rate $A$, find one horofunction whose negative values along the path grow at rate $A$ almost surely. The source already reports an epsilon-dependent approximation, which is weaker in its quantifiers. The path law and hypotheses behind the claimed exact theorem are missing.
