Belief aggregation

LLM status Open unverified

Statement

Original subject group: Asset pricing and financial markets

Audit classification: Explicit published open question. Section 6 explicitly requests better belief aggregation and risk-perception evidence. The particular finite-horizon equilibrium, survey kernel and identification problem are editorial.

Definitions and precise research target

Consider a finite-horizon market with agents $i=1,\ldots,N$, asset dividends $D_t$, prices $P_t$, wealth $w_{it}$, utility functions and explicitly specified portfolio constraints. Agent $i$ evaluates uncertainty using a subjective conditional law $\mathbb P_{it}$, which need not coincide with the objective data-generating law. Survey responses $b_{it}$ are noisy measurements of these laws under a stated measurement model. An equilibrium price process is generated by optimal demands $q_{it}$ and clearing $\sum_iq_{it}=Q_t$, where $Q_t$ is exogenous supply. For an unconstrained marginal agent, at dates $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ where the smooth interior conditions below hold, pricing satisfies \[P_t=E_{\mathbb P_{it}}[M_{i,t+1}(P_{t+1}+D_{t+1})\mid\mathcal F_{it}],\] with $M_{i,t+1}$ the agent's marginal-utility discount factor and $\mathcal F_{it}$ their information. The task is to characterize an empirically estimable aggregation map from the joint distribution of beliefs, risk perceptions, wealth and constraints to prices and volume. Determine conditions under which survey observations identify this map or its relevant derivatives. Explicitly allow the identity of marginal investors to change with prices and constraints. Quantify sensitivity to unobserved nonparticipants, reporting error and selective survey response. Matching average beliefs is insufficient if price-setting agents have different beliefs or risk tolerance.

Additional formal definitions and scope (editorial). Fix dates $0,\ldots,T$, the last distribution at $T$, and terminal ex-dividend price $P_T=0$. Each investor has an increasing concave utility $u_i$, discount factor $\beta_i\in(0,1)$, an information filtration $\mathcal F_{it}$, and a time-consistent subjective probability measure $\mathbb Q_i$; $\mathbb P_{it}$ means its regular conditional law rather than an unrestricted sequence of incompatible forecasts. The displayed marginal-utility formula is a smooth time-separable benchmark: consumption $c_{it}$ is measured in a declared consumption numeraire, lies in an open consumption domain, and the objective is $E_{\mathbb Q_i}[\sum_{s=0}^T\beta_i^s u_i(c_{is})]$. Require a well-defined objective, differentiable utility with $0<u_i'(c_{is})<\infty$, a feasible two-sided local asset perturbation, and integrability and differentiation-under-expectation conditions for the first-order condition. With these conditions, $M_{i,t+1}=\beta_i u_i'(c_{i,t+1})/u_i'(c_{it})$. State budget timing, including when dividends are received, consumption occurs and ex-dividend holdings are chosen. For nonsmooth preferences or binding portfolio restrictions, use the corresponding optimality conditions rather than asserting this equality. Portfolio feasibility includes a stated budget, short-sale and borrowing restrictions. Specify a common measurable history space carrying objective and subjective laws, dividends, information, choices and prices. Give realized information variables $H_{it}$ with $\mathcal F_{it}=\sigma(H_{it})$, and measurable conditional kernels $\mathbb P_{it}(\cdot\mid h)$. State assumptions ensuring their existence, such as standard Borel spaces. Versions at histories relevant under the objective law but null under $\mathbb Q_i$ must be specified as primitives or those histories explicitly excluded from the claimed domain. State how subjective laws describe endogenous future prices and how forecasts are updated in each comparison; neither a common prior nor rational expectations is imposed. Specify a survey response space and kernel $K_i(db\mid\mathbb Q_i,H_{it},w_{it})$, the elicited quantities and forecast horizons, and sampling probabilities, including nonresponse. Declare which features of subjective payoff uncertainty represent risk perceptions, separately from preferences governing risk tolerance. State which kernel components are known, and the joint measurement and response law across investors and dates; individual kernels alone do not impose independence. Distinguish market participation from inclusion in the survey population and survey response. The price map is parameterized by a declared admissible family of complete dynamic economies, rather than by contemporaneous marginal distributions alone. Each input specifies the joint history law, aggregate states, information dependence, endowments, preferences, beliefs, constraints, initial holdings and supply process, with any components held fixed explicitly listed. On a declared domain where equilibrium exists, a measurable selector is part of the specification; its output is an equilibrium price and allocation process, hence also the chosen volume process. A realized path is evaluated at a history, and an outcome law additionally uses the objective history law. A cross-sectional summary defines an aggregation map only if its sufficiency for that output is established. Characterize conditions for existence and aggregation within such families; no particular family, selector or universal existence claim is supplied by the research question. For a declared trading interval, let $q_{it}$ be closing holdings and specify opening holdings $q_{i,t-1}$, including $q_{i,-1}$. If $a_{it}$ is the signed allocation of primary issuance, repurchases and redemption during that interval, with $\sum_i a_{it}=Q_t-Q_{t-1}$, define secondary net purchases by $s_{it}=q_{it}-q_{i,t-1}-a_{it}$. The one-sided net reallocation is $V_t^{\rm net}=\frac12\sum_i|s_{it}|$. The original half-sum of holding changes is this measure when primary allocations vanish; it is not generally gross transaction volume. If secondary transactions are recorded as trades $\ell$ of nonnegative size $x_{\ell t}$, each counted once, gross volume is $V_t^{\rm gross}=\sum_\ell x_{\ell t}$. Repeated trades can give different gross volumes for the same closing holdings. Specify the empirical volume target, trading intervals, primary allocations and terminal-redemption treatment; these are not determined by holdings alone.

For the identification part, declare an admissible class $\Theta$ of structural models, including preferences, technologies, shock laws, measurement/sampling rules and any equilibrium selector. Declare the observable-history variable $O$, its sampling units and observational support, including which survey responses, response indicators, prices, trades, holdings, wealth and constraints are observed. For a declared target functional $\tau$, the identified set is $\mathcal I_\tau=\{\tau(\theta):\theta\in\Theta,\ \mathcal L_\theta(O)=\mathcal L_{\rm obs}(O)\}$; point identification means this set is a singleton. The target may be a specified aggregation function on a declared input domain or a specified feature of it; an observed price/volume law is a distinct target and does not by itself identify off-support structural responses. For any derivative target, specify the input path, fixed primitives, topology and output evaluation, and require the relevant limit to exist; at constraint or selector changes, report nonexistence or explicitly defined one-sided responses rather than assuming smoothness. An estimability claim must additionally state a sequence of sampling experiments and an estimator with a consistency, risk or coverage objective. Sensitivity analysis must declare the permitted deviations in latent participation, reporting and response mechanisms, their magnitude and the target discrepancy being assessed; finite informative bounds are not assumed. A $do(a)$ comparison, if pursued, replaces stated policy/technology equations while fixing the declared objective exogenous law and re-solving the remaining equations; also specify the intervention's timing, target outcome and subjective belief-response and measurement rules. This is an optional comparison convention, not an additional specified causal estimand. These definitions do not assert that an identification or estimation theorem holds without further restrictions.

Variants and assumption changes

  1. Known preferences, unbiased surveys (editorial). Assume complete participation, observed wealth/constraints and known survey kernel; characterize the equilibrium aggregation map. State whether complete participation refers to market participation, survey response or both. To use the unbiased-survey variant, declare a numerical elicited functional $g_{it}$, response coordinates and conditioning information, and impose the corresponding finite conditional-moment restriction $E[b_{it}-g_{it}\mid\mathbb Q_i,H_{it},w_{it},\text{response}]=0$. A known kernel alone implies neither this restriction nor identification of the latent law.
  2. Selective surveys and changing marginal investors (source-motivated extension). Permit latent risk tolerance and nonresponse, and derive identified price/belief sets rather than averaging surveys directly.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

These conventions apply to each entry unless explicitly replaced.

Models and identification. A primitive model $m$ specifies all probability laws, feasible choices, information, equilibrium and measurement rules used by its target. The admissible class $\mathcal M$ is fixed independently of outcomes. If $P_O(m)$ is its observable probability law and $\tau(m)$ the target, its identified set at observable law $P$ is \[ \mathcal I_\tau(P)=\{\tau(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_O(m)=P\}. \] Point identification means that this set is a singleton. An empty set signals incompatibility of data and assumptions. Coordinate bounds need not describe the joint set. Bounds are sharp only if every retained target is attainable or arbitrarily approachable by compatible models. Finite bounds require explicit restrictions on unobserved quantities; unrestricted confounding, hidden wealth, extrapolation or arbitrary equilibrium selection can yield uninformative sets.

Causal interventions. A potential outcome $Y_i(p)$ is the outcome under a complete policy regime $p$, including timing, financing, information and market spillovers. The target population and units are fixed before treatment. With interference, use the complete assignment vector or a justified exposure mapping; individual no-interference notation alone cannot identify an economy-wide intervention. A difference of mean potential outcomes does not require the cross-world joint distribution. Conditioning on post-treatment migration, survival, enrollment or employment changes the estimand and needs separate justification.

Statistical statements. An estimator, confidence set, forecast or test specifies a sampling law, model class and loss/coverage criterion. Uniform $(1-\alpha)$-coverage means $\inf_{m\in\mathcal M}\Pr_m\{\tau(m)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha$, or an explicitly asymptotic version. The whole parameter space trivially has coverage, so informativeness requires a stated width, risk or power objective. A forecasting improvement is relative to a named benchmark, information set and evaluation population.

Equilibrium and optimization. An equilibrium comprises feasible plans, optimality, beliefs where needed, budget constraints and all market/resource clearing. The primitives or a stated benchmark define each correspondence $\mathcal E(p;m)$. Nonemptiness is assumed or proved, never inferred just from compact policy sets. A selected-equilibrium target uses a declared selection rule; a robust target quantifies over all permitted equilibria. Use suprema or infima unless attainment is established. An $\varepsilon$-optimal policy is feasible and within $\varepsilon$ of the finite optimum value. Report an empty feasible set separately.

Welfare and accounting. Normative utility, interpersonal normalization, social weights, numeraire, discounting and the use of public receipts are primitives. Behavioral utility need not coincide with the welfare criterion. Household welfare includes ownership income and rents once; adding profits again to compensating variation generally double-counts them. A monetary equivalent is defined by an explicit transfer and price convention, with monotonicity and range conditions. Average effects, mechanism contributions, transfers and welfare losses are different targets. Infinite sums require convergence and dynamic feasible plans require terminal or no-Ponzi conditions.

Source versions. A working-paper date, revision date and journal publication year are distinguished. A DOI for a journal version is not automatically the DOI of an earlier manuscript. Locators refer to the stated version and printed pages where specified. A metadata-only check does not verify a claim about a full-text conclusion.
