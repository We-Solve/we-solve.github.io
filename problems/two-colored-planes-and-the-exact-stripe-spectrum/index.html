Math-1350

Two-colored planes and the exact stripe spectrum

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let a two-coloring be any map $\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to\{0,1\}$, without an unstated measurability assumption. A finite set occurs monochromatically if an isometric, hence congruent, copy has constant color; rescaling is not allowed unless explicitly specified. The source conjectures that every three-point set occurs, except possibly one equilateral congruence class specific to the coloring. Thus the exceptional side length may depend on $\chi$, and there is no proposed universal exceptional scale.

For each coloring define its exceptional equilateral spectrum \[ E(\chi)=\{s>0:\text{no monochromatic equilateral triangle of side }s\text{ exists}\}. \] The aid reports, using a theorem abbreviated EGMRSS in the summary, that the nonequilateral-triple conjecture is equivalent to $|E(\chi)|\leq1$ for every two-coloring. The exact side-length theorem and its hypotheses are not supplied, so that equivalence is retained as an attributed reduction, not reconstructed without its cited input.

For a precise plane-stripe special case, fix $h>0$ and a unit vector $u\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$, and set \[ \chi_{h,u}(x)=\left\lfloor\frac{\langle x,u\rangle}{h}\right\rfloor\pmod2. \] The stripes are half open. The aid's exact spectrum theorem states \[ E(\chi_{h,u})=\{2h/\sqrt3\}. \] Equivalently, an equilateral triangle is avoided exactly when its altitude equals $h$; every other positive side length has a monochromatic congruent copy. In particular $h=\sqrt3/2$ gives the source's unit-equilateral counterexample. This plane assertion is not transferred to higher-dimensional slab colorings, where additional orientations change the geometry.

The reported boundary-sensitive criterion uses projected vertex positions modulo $2h$. For a fixed orientation of a triangle, take the distinct residues of its three heights on the circle of circumference $2h$ and let $g_{\max}$ be their largest cyclic gap. A translation making that oriented triple monochromatic exists exactly when \[ g_{\max}>h. \] The strict sign matters: an arc of length exactly $h$ with both endpoints required does not fit inside a half-open stripe. Rotations are then varied in determining the equilateral spectrum.

The original note separately reports that for a fixed set $T$ of three collinear points there is a sixteen-coloring of $\mathbb E^N$ avoiding every congruent copy of $T$. Its dimension quantifier and full reference are not supplied in the literal record; it is a many-color avoidance statement, not a two-color counterexample to the plane conjecture. The stripe-spectrum result does not solve the arbitrary-coloring conjecture.

Short English statement

The exception concerns a fixed equilateral side length for each coloring, not an arbitrary rescaling. The supplied theorem finds exactly that one excluded length for alternating half-open stripes. A reported general equivalence uses an incompletely cited side-length theorem, while the full two-color conjecture remains unresolved here.
