Math-14992

Uniform Composition Powers of Rational Functions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K(X)$ denote the field of rational functions in an indeterminate $X$ over a field $K$. The supplied record does not specify $K$, its characteristic, or any additional hypotheses on it. For a nonzero rational function $r=A/B$, with coprime $A,B\in K[X]$, its degree is $\deg(r)=\max\{\deg A,\deg B\}$; constant rational maps have degree zero. Composition $u\circ v$ means substitution $u(v(X))$, whenever this is defined.

The source asks the following uniform factorization question. Given $u,f\in K(X)$, suppose that \[ \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\;\exists v\in K(X) \qquad u\circ v=f^n. \] Must there exist $w\in K(X)$ such that \[ u\circ w=f^{N}, \] where the positive integer $N$ depends only on $\deg(u)$? Under a literal uniform-exponent interpretation, this asks for an exponent $N(d)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, chosen uniformly for all admissible $u,f$ with $\deg(u)=d$, such that \[ \left(\exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\;\exists v\in K(X):u\circ v=f^n\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\exists w\in K(X):u\circ w=f^{N(d)}\right). \] This is a conditional formulation of the source question, not a fully specified assertion over all fields.

Unresolved qualifications. The notation $f^n$ is not defined in the supplied record. It could mean the ordinary power $(f(X))^n$, or the compositional iterate $f^{\circ n}$, defined by $f^{\circ1}=f$ and $f^{\circ(n+1)}=f\circ f^{\circ n}$ when these compositions are defined. These readings give different questions, and neither is selected here. The record also does not state whether $u,f,v,w$ must be nonconstant or satisfy degree restrictions. Constant cases require clarification, including whether all displayed substitutions are defined. Furthermore, a uniform exponent $N(d)$ and an exponent allowed to vary subject to $1\leq N\leq B(d)$ are different formulations; the question uses the former wording, whereas the supplied literature assessment refers to a degree-dependent bound.

The broader question asks when a reduction to irreducible fibered products of genus at most one produces multiple towers, including more complicated examples. For nonconstant rational maps $a,b:\mathbb P^1_K\to\mathbb P^1_K$, their fibered product is \[ C_{a,b}=\mathbb P^1_K\times_{\mathbb P^1_K}\mathbb P^1_K, \] with the two structure maps given by $a$ and $b$; its points satisfy $a(x)=b(y)$. However, the record does not identify the maps used in the reduction, define a tower or distinct towers, specify the transition maps, or state the desired classification. It also does not specify the base field for irreducibility or the precise genus convention. Thus the broader question cannot be sharpened into a uniquely determined mathematical statement from this record alone.

Supplied status qualification. The registry labels the overall problem open. Its dated curation attributes a later proof of the special factorization question, with a degree-dependent bound, to Hyde--Zieve, while describing the broader classification as open. These are supplied assessments, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

The special question asks whether a factorization $u\circ v=f^n$ forces a similar factorization at an exponent controlled only by $\deg(u)$. The meaning of powers, the field hypotheses, constant cases, and the precise uniformity remain unspecified. A broader question concerns multiple towers arising from genus-at-most-one fibered products, but the towers and reduction are not defined. Supplied dated curation reports the special question proved by Hyde--Zieve and the broader question open; this statement-only draft requires human mathematical review.
