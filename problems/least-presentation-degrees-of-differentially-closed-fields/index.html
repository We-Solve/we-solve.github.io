Math-1566

Least presentation degrees of differentially closed fields

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use countable ordinary differential fields of characteristic zero in the computable language of rings with one derivation $\delta$. Let $\mathsf{DCF}_0$ be their differentially closed theory. A copy has domain $\omega$ and an atomic diagram in this language. For a fixed countable $K\models\mathsf{DCF}_0$, define its presentation-degree spectrum by \[ \operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K) =\{\deg_TD(A):A\cong K,\ \operatorname{dom}(A)=\omega\}. \] Computable in a degree $\mathbf d$ means diagram Turing reducible to an oracle of that degree; "every copy computes $\mathbf d$" allows the reduction to depend on the copy.

Question 20, Harizanov. Given a Turing degree $\mathbf d$, is there a countable differentially closed field $K$ having a $\mathbf d$-computable copy while every copy computes $\mathbf d$? Equivalently, \[ \exists\mathbf e\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K)\ (\mathbf e\le_T\mathbf d), \qquad\forall\mathbf e\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K)\ (\mathbf d\le_T\mathbf e). \] Together these imply $\mathbf d\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K)$ and that it is the least degree in this spectrum. No uniform functional over all presentations is required.

Supplied characteristic-zero answer. The aid invokes Marker--Miller, Corollary 5.3, in the form \[ \forall K\models\mathsf{DCF}_0\text{ countable}\ \forall\mathbf b>\mathbf0\ \exists\mathbf e\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K) \quad\mathbf b\not\le_T\mathbf e. \] Thus no such spectrum is contained in any nonzero upper cone. The requested existence of some $K$ with both clauses is therefore possible exactly for $\mathbf d=\mathbf0$, witnessed by a computable model of $\mathsf{DCF}_0$. This does not assert that every countable differentially closed field has a computable presentation. Positive characteristic or several derivations are outside this supplied result.

Supplied countable simultaneous avoidance extension. The aid further asserts \[ \forall K\models\mathsf{DCF}_0\text{ countable}\ \forall\langle\mathbf b_i:i<\omega\rangle\ (\mathbf b_i>\mathbf0)\ \exists\mathbf e\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{at}}(K)\ \forall i<\omega\quad\mathbf b_i\not\le_T\mathbf e. \] There is one presentation degree avoiding every prescribed nonzero cone, not a separate degree for each cone. This is stronger than the individual avoidance statement; the aid attributes a finite-extension argument to it but gives no full proof or separate reference here. The assertion is existential, with no uniform effective procedure from arbitrary degree representatives.

Separate Section 1.12, Andrews. The appended heading "Spectrum of totally categorical theories" defines \[ \operatorname{Spec}(T)=\{\mathbf d:\text{some model of }T \text{ is computable in }\mathbf d\}. \] This is an upward oracle-computability spectrum ranging over models of a theory, distinct from exact diagram degrees of copies of one $K$. It is not an assumption of Question 20. The registry cleaned field inserts section-heading text into "differentially closed"; the original question and supplied interpretation are preserved instead.

Short English statement

Harizanov asks for a differentially closed field with a prescribed least presentation degree. Under the supplied ordinary characteristic-zero result, such a field exists exactly for the computable degree. The aid's stronger extension gives one copy avoiding any countable list of nonzero cones. The adjacent theory-spectrum definition is a different notion.
