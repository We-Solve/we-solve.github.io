Math-974

Motivic Poles and Bernstein–Sato Roots

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks whether poles of the motivic zeta function of a polynomial are roots of its Bernstein--Sato polynomial. The coefficient field, the version of the motivic zeta function, the meaning of a motivic pole, and the local or global scope are not specified.

For reference, in the conventional complex setting, let $n\geq 1$ and let $f\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ be nonzero and nonconstant. Write $D_{{\mathbb{A}}^n_{{\mathbb{C}}}}$ for the ring of polynomial differential operators. The global Bernstein--Sato polynomial $b_f(s)$ is the monic generator of the nonzero ideal \[ \left\{b(s)\in{\mathbb{C}}[s]: P(s)f^{s+1}=b(s)f^s\text{ for some }P(s)\in D_{{\mathbb{A}}^n_{{\mathbb{C}}}}[s]\right\}. \] The identity is interpreted in the formal module ${\mathbb{C}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n,f^{-1}][s]f^s$, with \[ \partial_{x_i}(g f^s)=\left(\partial_{x_i}g+s g f^{-1}\partial_{x_i}f\right)f^s. \] A local Bernstein--Sato polynomial instead uses differential operators with coefficients in a specified local ring at a specified point; neither that point nor a local convention is supplied.

Motivic zeta functions involve the class $\mathbb L$ of the affine line in an appropriate Grothendieck ring, often localized or completed. With the formal substitution $T=\mathbb L^{-s}$, a denominator factor \[ 1-\mathbb L^{-\nu}T^N,\qquad N>0, \] corresponds to the candidate value $s=-\nu/N$. Such a denominator factor alone does not establish a genuine pole. A precise pole convention and coefficient ring must be fixed; motivic expressions cannot simply be treated as ordinary complex meromorphic functions.

Proposed implication, with unresolved conventions. For every polynomial in the intended class, does every genuine motivic pole $\alpha$, expressed in the $s$-parameter, satisfy \[ b_f(\alpha)=0? \] Under the complex global interpretation above, this would ask, for every $n\geq1$ and every nonzero nonconstant $f\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, whether \[ \alpha\in\operatorname{Poles}_s(Z_f^{\mathrm{mot}})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_f(\alpha)=0. \] Here $Z_f^{\mathrm{mot}}$ and its pole set still require the missing choices. The complex global interpretation and the exclusions of zero and constant polynomials are explicitly conditional conventions, not hypotheses supplied by the record. The intended conclusion concerns actual Bernstein--Sato roots, rather than merely their exponentials as monodromy eigenvalues.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every genuine pole of a polynomial's motivic zeta function is a root of its Bernstein--Sato polynomial. The source leaves the field, local or global scope, zeta function variant, and pole convention unspecified, so a unique fully precise formulation requires review. Its supplied open status is a dated curation claim.
