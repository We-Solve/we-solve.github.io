Higher-even-dimensional Polyakov formulae

Definitions and mathematical statement

The reference to equation (40) is recovered from companion record 20002050. On a closed even-dimensional Riemannian manifold, let $A_g$ be a natural elliptic differential operator with positive-definite leading symbol, assumed to be a positive integral power of a conformally covariant operator. The source examples include GJMS operators and the square of the Dirac operator. Fix the spectral and zero-mode conventions needed for its zeta-regularized determinant; write $\det'$ when zero eigenvalues are omitted. For $\widehat g=e^{2\omega}g$, the reported formula in dimensions 2, 4 and 6 has the form \[ -\log\frac{\det' A_{\widehat g}}{\det' A_g} =\alpha\left(\tfrac12\int_M\omega P_{n,g}\omega\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g +\int_M\omega Q_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g\right) +C_F(\widehat g)-C_F(g)+H(\widehat g,g), \] where $C_F(g)=\int_MF_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g$ is a local counterterm, $P_n$ is the critical GJMS operator and $H$ is a kernel cocycle, zero when the kernel is zero. A counterterm sign can be included in $F$; hats and fractions are damaged in the extraction. Problem 29 asks whether an appropriate version holds in higher even dimensions, hence beyond the displayed 2, 4 and 6 cases.

The supplied aid reports a structural conditional reduction. If the local determinant anomaly is $\alpha Q+L+G$, where $L_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g$ is a pointwise conformally invariant density and $G$ has the stated natural local conformal primitive, integration along the conformal path gives the displayed Polyakov term plus \[ \int_M\omega L_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g, \] the local counterterm difference and the kernel cocycle. A bare divergence decomposition does not by itself provide the needed local primitive. For a nonzero type-B term $L$, if $\alpha\ne0$, replacing $Q$ by the operator-dependent choice $Q_A=Q+\alpha^{-1}L$ absorbs that linear term while preserving the $Q$ transformation law. If canonical $Q$ is fixed, the term must remain unless separately removed by a valid local counterterm. If $\alpha=0$, it cannot be absorbed by this division. The original question does not specify whether its $Q$ is fixed or operator dependent.

The aid also reports an exact spectral-power audit. For a nonnegative self-adjoint elliptic $B$, with zero modes omitted, literal powers satisfy \[ \zeta_{B^r}(s)=\zeta_B(rs),\qquad \log\det'(B^r)=r\log\det'B\qquad(r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}). \] No repeated-factor multiplicative anomaly is inserted into this identity. General products or incompatible spectral cuts are different questions. On a flat closed even torus, take the normalized critical operator $B=P_n=\Delta_+^{n/2}$, with constant kernel, and $A=B^r$. For constant $\omega=c$, the reported exact normalization is \[ -\log\frac{\det' A_{e^{2c}g}}{\det' A_g}=-nrc, \qquad H(e^{2c}g,g)=-nrc \] when the flat local terms use the usual vanishing normalization. Thus the kernel correction cannot be discarded solely because the metric is flat.

The next source problem proposes the natural-density decomposition as sufficient for the determinant program. The present aid gives an audit and conditional integration argument, not an unrestricted theorem proving the exact canonical-$Q$ formula in every higher even dimension.

Short English statement

Does the low-dimensional Polyakov determinant formula extend to higher even dimensions? The supplied aid identifies the missing type-B term, the choice between canonical and operator-dependent $Q$, and an exact flat-torus kernel normalization. Literal spectral powers introduce no repeated-factor anomaly.
