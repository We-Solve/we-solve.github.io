Parshin's Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q=p^r$, where $p$ is a prime and $r\geq 1$, and let ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ be the finite field with $q$ elements. For a smooth proper scheme $X$ over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, let $K_i(X)$ denote its Quillen algebraic $K$-group in degree $i$. An abelian group $G$ is torsion if every $g\in G$ is annihilated by some positive integer, which may depend on $g$.

Parshin's conjecture, as formulated in the supplied record. For every finite field ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, every smooth proper scheme $X$ over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, and every integer $i>0$, \[ K_i(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}=0. \] Equivalently, \[ \forall\alpha\in K_i(X)\quad \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\quad n\alpha=0. \] The assertion imposes no vanishing condition on $K_0(X)$ and does not assert that the positive-degree groups are finite.

The source also describes an equivalent formulation using rational motivic cohomology vanishing outside a predicted degree. It does not specify the cohomological degree, weight, or indexing convention, so that formulation cannot be reproduced precisely from the supplied record. The statement above retains the source's smooth proper hypothesis; the dated literature assessment instead refers to smooth projective varieties, and their relationship is not explained in the supplied evidence.

Short English statement

Parshin's conjecture here asks whether every element of every positive-degree algebraic $K$-group of a smooth proper scheme over a finite field has finite order. Equivalently, all these groups vanish after tensoring with ${\mathbb{Q}}$. The supplied motivic reformulation and literature assessment require clarification.
