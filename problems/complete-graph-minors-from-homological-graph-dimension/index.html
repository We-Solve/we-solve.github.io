Math-15120

Complete Graph Minors from Homological Graph Dimension

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite graph, and let $K_n$ denote the complete graph on $n$ vertices. A graph is a minor of $G$ if it can be obtained from $G$ by vertex deletions, edge deletions, and edge contractions. An induced subgraph on a vertex set contains every edge of $G$ whose endpoints lie in that set.

For a family $\mathcal H=(H_i)_{i\in I}$ of induced subgraphs of $G$, define its nerve by \[ N(\mathcal H)=\{\varnothing\}\cup\left\{J\subseteq I: J\text{ is finite and nonempty, and }\bigcap_{j\in J}V(H_j)\ne\varnothing\right\}. \] The supplied record describes $\gamma(G)$ as the largest homological degree $q$ for which some "connected family" of induced subgraphs has a nerve with nonzero $H_q$. It does not define "connected family" or specify homology coefficients.

To expose these unresolved choices, write $\mathcal C(G)$ for the intended class of such families and $A$ for a fixed nonzero coefficient group. With ordinary simplicial homology in nonnegative degrees, the proposed definition would be \[ \gamma_{\mathcal C,A}(G)=\max\left\{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}:\exists\mathcal H\in\mathcal C(G),\ H_q\left(N(\mathcal H);A\right)\ne0\right\}, \] provided this maximum exists. These parameters display missing specifications; they do not assert that the source intended every possible choice.

The supplied question is whether, for the intended invariant $\gamma$, \[ \forall\,G\text{ finite}\quad\forall\,d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\qquad \gamma(G)\ge d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K_{d+2}\text{ is a minor of }G. \] A complete formulation requires the source's admissibility condition for families, its coefficient convention, and its convention when the defining set has no maximum, including the empty-graph case. No connectedness assumption on $G$ itself is supplied.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the homological dimension obtained from nerves of suitable families of induced subgraphs detects exactly the complete graph minors: dimension at least $d$ should correspond to a $K_{d+2}$ minor, for every finite graph and every positive integer $d$. The meaning of a suitable "connected family" and the homology conventions remain unspecified.
