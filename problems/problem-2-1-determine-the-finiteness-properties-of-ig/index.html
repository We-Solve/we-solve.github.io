Problem 2.1 — Determine the finiteness properties of Ig.

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual closed-surface convention, let $S_g$ be a closed connected oriented surface of genus $g$ and define its Torelli group by \[ \mathcal I_g=\ker\left(\operatorname{Mod}(S_g) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(H_1(S_g;{\mathbb{Z}}))\right). \] The excerpt writes $I_g$ without restating the surface convention; boundary or marked-point variants must not be substituted silently. Interpret $H_k(\mathcal I_g)$ as integral group homology unless another coefficient system is specified. A group $G$ is of type $F_k$ if it has an Eilenberg--Mac Lane CW complex $K(G,1)$ with finite $k$-skeleton; $F_1$ is finite generation and $F_2$ finite presentability.

Problem 2.1 asks to determine the finiteness properties of $\mathcal I_g$: for which pairs $(g,k)$ is $H_k(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Z}})$ finitely generated as an abelian group, and for which pairs is $\mathcal I_g$ of type $F_k$? These are distinct questions; finite generation as a symplectic-group module is also different from finite generation as an abelian group.

The registry reports the following progress, subject to source verification: $\mathcal I_2$ is infinitely generated free (Mess, 1992); $\mathcal I_g$ is finitely generated for $g\geq3$ (Johnson, 1983), with genus one trivial in the closed convention. For $g\geq2$, it reports $\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal I_g)=3g-5$ (Bestvina--Bux--Margalit, 2007), hence vanishing homology for $k\geq3g-4$, and infinitely generated integral homology for $2g-3\leq k\leq3g-5$ (top degree from Bestvina--Bux--Margalit, extended range attributed to Gaifullin). It also reports finite-dimensional $H_2(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Q}})$ for $g\geq51$ and finite generation of $H_2(\mathcal I_g)$ as a symplectic module. Neither statement settles integral finite generation. The summary reports finite presentability for $g\geq3$ still open and separately attributes an infinite-generation statement for $H_*(\mathcal I_g;{\mathbb{Z}})$ at $g\geq7$ to Akita, without precisely specifying the latter's algebraic meaning.

Short English statement

Determine, by genus and degree, which integral Torelli homology groups are finitely generated and which finite-skeleton properties the Torelli groups satisfy. Rational homology and symplectic-module finiteness do not settle the integral question.
