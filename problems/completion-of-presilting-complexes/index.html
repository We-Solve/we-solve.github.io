Completion of Presilting Complexes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "Is every presilting complex with $n$ indecomposable summands already silting?" The excerpt does not specify the ambient category, the meaning of $n$, or how summands are counted. Consequently, the following is an explicitly conditional interpretation requiring confirmation against the source report.

Fix a field $k$ and a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra $A$. Let $\mathcal T=K^b(\operatorname{proj} A)$ be the homotopy category of bounded complexes of finitely generated projective right $A$-modules, and let $n$ be the number of isomorphism classes of simple right $A$-modules. For $P\in\mathcal T$, write $P[i]$ for its shift by $i$. The complex $P$ is presilting if \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal T}(P,P[i])=0 \qquad\text{for every integer }i>0. \] Let $\operatorname{thick}_{\mathcal T}(P)$ denote the smallest full triangulated subcategory of $\mathcal T$ containing $P$ and closed under direct summands. A presilting complex $P$ is silting if $\operatorname{thick}_{\mathcal T}(P)=\mathcal T$. A complex is basic if its indecomposable direct summands are pairwise nonisomorphic.

Under this conditional interpretation, the question is whether every basic presilting complex \[ P\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}P_j, \qquad P_j\text{ indecomposable},\qquad P_j\not\cong P_\ell\ \text{for }j\ne\ell, \] satisfies \[ \operatorname{thick}_{\mathcal T}(P)=\mathcal T. \] The finite-dimensional algebra setting, interpretation of $n$, basicness convention, and scope of the algebra and field parameters are not established by the supplied excerpt. No bound on the degrees occupied by the complex is stated. These qualifications must be resolved before this conditional formulation can be accepted as the precise source statement.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a presilting complex having the full intended number of indecomposable summands must generate its ambient category and hence be silting. The supplied excerpt leaves the category, the number $n$, and the summand-counting convention unspecified; the displayed algebraic formulation is conditional and requires human review.
