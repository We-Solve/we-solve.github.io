Math-1048

K-Regularity Criterion for Ring Regularity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the customary commutative, unital interpretation, an algebra $A$ essentially of finite type over a field $k$ is a localization $S^{-1}B$ of a finitely generated commutative $k$-algebra $B$. Such an algebra is Noetherian. Its Krull dimension $\dim A$ is the supremum of lengths of strictly increasing chains of prime ideals. For an integer $n\geq 1$, call $A$ $K_n$-regular if, for every integer $m\geq 1$, the inclusion into the polynomial algebra induces an isomorphism of algebraic $K$-groups \[ K_n(A)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}K_n\left(A[t_1,\ldots,t_m]\right). \] Call $A$ regular if, for every prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subseteq A$, the local ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is regular local; equivalently, its maximal ideal has a minimal generating set of cardinality $\dim A_{\mathfrak p}$.

Question (supplied formulation, associated in the background with Vorst's conjecture). For every field $k$ and every algebra $A$ essentially of finite type over $k$, with finite Krull dimension $d\geq 0$, does the implication \[ A\text{ is }K_{d+1}\text{-regular} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\text{ is regular} \] hold? No restriction on the characteristic of $k$ is stated in the supplied formulation.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether invariance of $K_{d+1}$ under adjoining any finite number of polynomial variables forces a $d$-dimensional algebra essentially of finite type over a field to be regular. This uses the customary commutative, unital interpretation.
