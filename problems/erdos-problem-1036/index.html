Statement

Yes. For each fixed $c>0$ there is $\beta(c)>0$ such that every sufficiently large $n$-vertex graph with no clique or independent set larger than $c\log n$ has at least $2^{\beta(c)n}$ pairwise nonisomorphic induced subgraphs. We reconstruct Shelah's proof from Erdős and Rényi conjecture, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9707226, with the separation and counting steps expanded. The proof below uses only elementary Ramsey bounds and probability; it does not import the desired exponential theorem.

Use base-two logarithms. Increase the given constant slightly, and choose \[ L=\lceil C\log n\rceil \] so that the hypothesis forbids homogeneous $L$-sets. Here and below "homogeneous" means complete or independent. Choose a fixed integer $k\ge2$ sufficiently large that \[ \frac Ck\log\left(e(k+2)\right)<\frac13. \] For all sufficiently large $n$, the elementary Ramsey bound gives \[ R(L,\lceil L/k\rceil)\le n^{1/2}.\tag{1} \] Indeed $R(s,t)\le\binom{s+t-2}{t-1}$ follows by induction using a vertex and its neighbours and non-neighbours. With $t=\lceil L/k\rceil$, the binomial bound $\binom ab\le(ea/b)^b$ bounds its base-two logarithm by $(L/k+1)\log(e(k+2))$, which is less than $(\log n)/2$ eventually. The same estimate holds with the two arguments exchanged. Set \[ m=R(k,k),\qquad \alpha=\beta=\frac1{8m}. \] Suppose, towards a contradiction, that the number $I(G)$ of induced isomorphism types is less than $2^{\beta n}$.

Statement and source

Yes. For each fixed $c>0$ there is $\beta(c)>0$ such that every sufficiently large $n$-vertex graph with no clique or independent set larger than $c\log n$ has at least $2^{\beta(c)n}$ pairwise nonisomorphic induced subgraphs. We reconstruct Shelah's proof from Erdős and Rényi conjecture, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9707226, with the separation and counting steps expanded. The proof below uses only elementary Ramsey bounds and probability; it does not import the desired exponential theorem.

Use base-two logarithms. Increase the given constant slightly, and choose \[ L=\lceil C\log n\rceil \] so that the hypothesis forbids homogeneous $L$-sets. Here and below "homogeneous" means complete or independent. Choose a fixed integer $k\ge2$ sufficiently large that \[ \frac Ck\log\left(e(k+2)\right)<\frac13. \] For all sufficiently large $n$, the elementary Ramsey bound gives \[ R(L,\lceil L/k\rceil)\le n^{1/2}.\tag{1} \] Indeed $R(s,t)\le\binom{s+t-2}{t-1}$ follows by induction using a vertex and its neighbours and non-neighbours. With $t=\lceil L/k\rceil$, the binomial bound $\binom ab\le(ea/b)^b$ bounds its base-two logarithm by $(L/k+1)\log(e(k+2))$, which is less than $(\log n)/2$ eventually. The same estimate holds with the two arguments exchanged. Set \[ m=R(k,k),\qquad \alpha=\beta=\frac1{8m}. \] Suppose, towards a contradiction, that the number $I(G)$ of induced isomorphism types is less than $2^{\beta n}$.

A small set detects large differences of neighbourhoods

Choose a random set $A$ by selecting each vertex independently with probability $p=\alpha/(2\log n)$. With probability at least $1/2$, \[ |A|\le\alpha n/\log n.\tag{2} \] This is Markov's inequality. Write $N(x)$ for the full neighbourhood of $x$, and put \[ D=\frac8\alpha(\log n)^2. \] For a pair $x,y$ with $|N(x)\mathbin\triangle N(y)|>D$, the probability that $A$ misses this symmetric difference is at most \[ (1-p)^D\le e^{-pD}=e^{-4\log_2n}\le n^{-4}. \] A union bound over fewer than $n^2$ pairs makes the probability of any failure less than $n^{-2}$. Thus we can fix $A$ satisfying $2$ and such that \[ N(x)\cap A=N(y)\cap A \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |N(x)\mathbin\triangle N(y)|\le D.\tag{3} \] Partition $V(G)\setminus A$ into $\ell$ classes by these neighbourhood patterns into $A$.

Too many patterns would already give exponentially many types

Choose one fixed representative from each class. For each subset of the representatives, take its induced graph together with all of $A$, and mark the vertices of $A$ by distinct fixed names. Two different representative subsets give different marked isomorphism types: an isomorphism fixing the marked vertices must preserve the adjacency pattern into $A$, and those patterns distinguish the representatives. Thus there are $2^\ell$ such marked types.

Each unmarked induced isomorphism type has at most $n^{|A|}$ ways to place these distinct marks. Consequently \[ 2^\ell\le I(G)n^{|A|}<2^{(\beta+\alpha)n}, \qquad \ell<\frac n{4m}.\tag{4} \] This is a count of marked isomorphism types, rather than labelled vertex subsets; the distinction is needed for $4$.

Pack homogeneous blocks and remove conflicts

Within each class greedily choose disjoint homogeneous sets $B_i$ of size $k$ until fewer than $m$ vertices remain. The definition of $m$ permits another choice whenever at least $m$ vertices remain. The unused vertices outside $A$ number less than $m\ell<n/4$. Also $|A|<n/4$ eventually. Thus the number $b$ of chosen blocks satisfies \[ b\ge n/(2k).\tag{5} \] Select a base vertex $x_i\in B_i$. Make a directed conflict $i\to j$, for distinct blocks, when $B_j$ meets $N(x_i)\triangle N(x)$ for some $x\in B_i$. By $3$, the union of these differences has at most $(k-1)D$ vertices. Disjointness of the blocks therefore bounds each outdegree by $(k-1)D$.

The underlying undirected conflict graph has average degree at most $2(k-1)D$, so it has an independent set of at least \[ \frac{b}{2(k-1)D+1} \tag{6} \] vertices. For completeness, uniformly order a graph's vertices and keep a vertex when it precedes all its neighbours. The kept set is independent and its expected size is $\sum_v1/(d(v)+1)$, at least the order divided by one plus the average degree, by Cauchy--Schwarz. This proves $6$.

Retain at least half of the blocks in $6$ so that they are all cliques, or all independent sets. The number $q$ retained satisfies \[ q\ge\frac{n}{4k(2(k-1)D+1)}\gg_c\frac n{(\log n)^2}>\sqrt n \tag{7} \] eventually. Between any two retained blocks $B_i,B_j$, adjacency is constant: lack of $i\to j$ makes every $x\in B_i$ have the same adjacency to a fixed $y\in B_j$ as $x_i$ does; lack of $j\to i$ makes that last adjacency equal to the adjacency between $x_i,x_j$.

The final asymmetric Ramsey contradiction

Apply $1$ to the $q$ base vertices. If the retained blocks are cliques, either the bases contain an independent $L$-set, or they contain a clique of size $\lceil L/k\rceil$. In the second case replace each base by its entire block. Constant complete adjacency between blocks gives a clique of size at least $k\lceil L/k\rceil\ge L$. If the blocks are independent sets, exchange the words clique and independent and use the symmetric version of $1$. Each case contradicts the hypothesis. Hence the assumption $I(G)<2^{\beta n}$ was impossible, proving the claimed exponential lower bound.

Assessment

All counting and selection arguments are supplied, including the conversion from neighbourhood patterns to unlabelled types and the removal of asymmetric conflicts. The constants depend only on the initial homogeneous-set bound, and the proof applies for all sufficiently large $n$. This is an expanded reconstruction of Shelah's known solution.
