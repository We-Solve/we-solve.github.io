$\beta$-ensembles

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let $B$ be standard Brownian motion on $[0,\infty)$ and $\beta>0$. Define the Dirichlet stochastic Airy operator $A_\beta=-d^2/dx^2+x+(2/\sqrt\beta)B^{\prime}$ through the closure of the quadratic form \[q_\beta[f]=\int_0^\infty(|f^{\prime}|^2+x|f|^2)\,dx+\frac2{\sqrt\beta}\int_0^\infty|f|^2\,dB\] initially on smooth compactly supported $f$ with $f(0)=0$; the last term can equivalently be read by integration by parts against the continuous Brownian path. Use the source's sign convention $H_\beta=-A_\beta$, whose eigenvalues decrease as $\lambda_1>\lambda_2>\cdots$. For $s\in\mathbb R$, the Airy kernel on $L^2(s,\infty)$ is $K_{\rm Ai}(x,y)=[\operatorname{Ai}(x)\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(y)-\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime}(x)\operatorname{Ai}(y)]/(x-y)$, continuously extended at $x=y$; $\operatorname{Ai}$ is the standard decaying Airy solution.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Use $H_\beta$ to obtain distributional information on its eigenvalues for general $\beta>0$, and in particular derive directly from $H_2$ the Tracy--Widom identity \[\mathbb P\{\lambda_1(B,2)\le s\}=\det(I-K_{\rm Ai})_{L^2(s,\infty)}.\] Here the determinant is the Fredholm determinant of the Airy integral operator. [S2]

Short English statement

Develop eigenvalue statistics for general beta through the stochastic Airy operator and obtain the beta-two Tracy--Widom formula directly from that operator.

A direct variational probability bound.
Let $f$ be a real smooth compactly supported function in $(0,\infty)$, normalized by $\int f^2=1$. Set $a_f=\int(f'^2+xf^2)$ and $v_f=\int f^4>0$. For the stochastic Airy quadratic form in the statement, its value on this deterministic test function is a Gaussian random variable: \[q_\beta[f]=a_f+(2/\sqrt\beta)Z_f,\qquad Z_f\sim N(0,v_f).\] Indeed the stochastic integral of a deterministic integrand is Gaussian, with variance the integral of its square; the same value agrees with the pathwise integration-by-parts definition. Under the stated self-adjoint form realization the lowest eigenvalue $\Lambda_0$ of $A_\beta$ is at most $q_\beta[f]$, and $\lambda_1=-\Lambda_0$. Therefore \[\mathbb P\{\lambda_1\le s\}\le \Phi\left(\frac{(a_f+s)\sqrt\beta}{2\sqrt{v_f}}\right),\] where $\Phi$ is the standard Gaussian distribution function. The inclusion used here is $\{\Lambda_0\ge-s\}\subseteq\{q_\beta[f]\ge-s\}$, which fixes the sign.
Scale test.
For a fixed smooth real $h$ supported in $(0,1)$ with $\int h^2=1$, take $f_L(x)=L^{-1/2}h(x/L)$, $L>0$. If $A=\int h'^2$, $B=\int yh(y)^2\,dy$, and $C=\int h^4$, then $a_{f_L}=A/L^2+BL$ and $v_{f_L}=C/L$. Thus an explicit family of upper bounds results by minimizing \[\Phi\left(\frac{\sqrt\beta}{2\sqrt C}(A L^{-3/2}+B L^{3/2}+sL^{1/2})\right) \] over $L>0$. No independence between different choices of $L$ is needed, since each is a separate upper bound.
Remaining obstruction.
The Rayleigh principle gives only one side and one trial state at a time. Recovering the full distribution requires controlling the infimum over all random admissible test functions. No Fredholm determinant or beta-two identity follows from replacing that random infimum by this deterministic one-parameter family. The calculation is an elementary variational bound, not a derivation of the source's requested identity or general eigenvalue laws.
