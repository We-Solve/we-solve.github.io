Nonlinear Stability of the Full Kerr Family

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An asymptotically flat vacuum initial data set $(\Sigma,\bar g,K)$ satisfies the Einstein constraints and has maximal globally hyperbolic development $(M,g)$ with $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$. The Kerr family describes rotating vacuum black holes by mass $m>0$ and specific angular momentum $a$; its subextremal black-hole range is $|a|<m$. Future null infinity $\mathcal I^+$ is the ideal endpoint boundary of outgoing null rays, and $J^-(\mathcal I^+)$ is its causal past. Completeness of $\mathcal I^+$ is the future-completeness condition in the report's asymptotically flat formulation.
Source scope and qualifications.
The primary formulation also requires a nonempty black-hole region and sufficiently fast asymptotic approach in the exterior. Smallness norm and decay topology are intentionally rough in the report. The ordinary subextremal stability target should not silently be enlarged to extremal horizons, where different phenomena occur, nor reduced to small angular momentum alone.
Full source formulation.
The report additionally requires $M\setminus J^-(\mathcal I^+)\ne\varnothing$ and that the metric on the exterior approach a final Kerr metric at a sufficiently fast rate. Those conditions are part of the intended nonlinear stability target.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Do sufficiently small perturbations of Kerr initial data for the vacuum Einstein equations have maximal developments with complete future null infinity whose exterior asymptotically approaches another Kerr solution?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Nonlinear stability of Kerr has been proved for sufficiently small angular momentum, but the extracted statement concerns the full Kerr family and the general subextremal range remains unresolved. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Show that small perturbations of rotating black holes keep a complete exterior and settle to another Kerr black hole.

A near-extremal coercivity check.
For Kerr parameters $m>0$, $|a|<m$, write $r_\pm=m\pm\sqrt{m^2-a^2}$. The surface-gravity coefficient is \[ \kappa=\frac{r_+-r_-}{2(r_+^2+a^2)} =\frac{\sqrt{m^2-a^2}}{2m(m+\sqrt{m^2-a^2})}. \] The second identity uses $r_+^2+a^2=2mr_+$. Thus $\kappa>0$ for each subextremal member but tends to zero as $|a|\uparrow m$.
What a redshift-type inequality would yield.
If a nonnegative perturbation energy obeyed $E'(t)+\kappa E(t)\leq F(t)$, multiplying by $e^{\kappa t}$ and integrating would give $E(t)\leq e^{-\kappa t}E(0)+\int_0^te^{-\kappa(t-s)}F(s)ds$. For constant forcing $F=f$, the resulting steady bound is $f/\kappa$, which is not uniform near extremality. If a nonlinear error is estimated only by $F(t)\leq C\epsilon E(t)$, absorption requires $C\epsilon<\kappa$. The allowed perturbation size can therefore shrink as the background approaches extremality.
Exact limitation.
No Einstein perturbation estimate of that form is proved here; it is an explicit audit of the dependence such an estimate would have. The source only asks stability at every fixed subextremal parameter, so loss of uniformity does not disprove it. Establishing the actual nonlinear exterior estimates, suitable parameter-dependent smallness, complete null infinity, a nonempty black-hole region and sufficiently fast convergence to final Kerr all remain unproved. Extremal stability is not silently included.
