Definitions and conventions.
A null cone is a hypersurface generated by null geodesics; its spacelike two-surface sections carry null expansions. Hawking energy is a quasilocal mass constructed from a section's area and the integral of the product of its two null expansions, using a fixed normalization. Trautman--Bondi mass is the total mass at a cut of future null infinity. A null Penrose inequality compares an appropriate initial horizon or section area with an asymptotic mass along a null hypersurface.

For general null cones, characterize convergence of the Hawking energy, compare its limit with the Trautman–Bondi mass, and derive the corresponding null Penrose inequality.

A round-cone normalization check.
In a spherical exterior take outgoing and ingoing null normals normalized to inner product $-2$, with expansions $\theta_+=2/r$, $\theta_-=-2f(r)/r$. For round sections of area $A=4\pi r^2$, define \[ m_H=\sqrt{A/(16\pi)}\left(1+\frac1{16\pi}\int\theta_+\theta_-\,dA\right). \] Since the product is constant on the sphere, substitution gives $m_H=r(1-f(r))/2$. For $f(r)=1-2M/r$, the result is exactly $M$ at every radius. At the round horizon $r=2M$, it equals $\sqrt{A/(16\pi)}$, so the expected Penrose relation is an equality in this benchmark.
The cancellation requiring control.
For $f(r)=1-2M/r+o(r^{-1})$, the same formula gives $m_H\to M$. Merely assuming $f(r)=1+o(1)$ would not suffice: $f=1-r^{-1/2}$ gives $m_H=\sqrt r/2$. This latter lapse is an asymptotic algebraic countercheck, not an asserted admissible vacuum cone.
Full-scope gap.
General cones have nonconstant expansions, shear and foliation dependence. The exact cancellation of the order-one term in the parentheses must persist with an integrable error, and an asymptotic coefficient must be identified with the correctly normalized Bondi mass. A limit alone also gives no monotonic comparison to the initial area. Proving those geometric estimates and monotonicity under the source hypotheses is the first gap. No foliation-independent limit or general null Penrose inequality is established.
