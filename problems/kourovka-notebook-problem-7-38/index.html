Kourovka Notebook Problem 7.38

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A profinite group is a topological group isomorphic to an inverse limit of finite discrete groups. A pronilpotent group is an inverse limit of finite nilpotent groups, and a pro-$p$ group is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups, where $p$ is a prime. Write $\mathfrak N$ for the class of all pronilpotent groups and $\mathfrak P_p$ for the class of all pro-$p$ groups.

Using the standard topological conventions, a variety of profinite groups is a nonempty class closed under taking closed subgroups, quotients by closed normal subgroups, and arbitrary Tikhonov products. A subvariety of a variety $\mathfrak U$ is a variety contained in $\mathfrak U$.

For a group $G$, let $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=[\gamma_n(G),G]$, with commutators taken in the underlying abstract group. The group $G$ is nilpotent if $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1\}$ for some integer $c\geq 0$. A variety $\mathfrak V$ is nilpotent, in the terminology of the supplied source, if every $G\in\mathfrak V$ is nilpotent. No common bound on the nilpotency classes is explicitly included in that definition.

The source calls $\mathfrak V$ locally nilpotent if every finitely generated group belonging to $\mathfrak V$ is nilpotent. It does not explicitly specify whether generation is topological or abstract. For the topological reading, put \[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathfrak V) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every }G\in\mathfrak V\text{ with }G=\overline{\langle S\rangle} \text{ for some finite }S\subseteq G\text{ is nilpotent}. \] For the literal abstract-generation reading, put \[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathfrak V) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every }G\in\mathfrak V\text{ with }G=\langle S\rangle \text{ for some finite }S\subseteq G\text{ is nilpotent}. \] These readings are retained separately pending clarification of the source's convention.

Questions attributed to O. V. Mel'nikov (1980), catalogue entry. For $\mathcal L$ interpreted according to the intended generation convention, ask:

  1. (a) Does every subvariety $\mathfrak V\subseteq\mathfrak N$ containing a non-nilpotent group contain a subvariety $\mathfrak W\subseteq\mathfrak V$ such that $\mathcal L(\mathfrak W)$ holds and $\mathfrak W$ contains a non-nilpotent group?
  2. (b) For a given prime $p$, does every subvariety $\mathfrak V\subseteq\mathfrak P_p$ containing a non-nilpotent group contain a subvariety $\mathfrak W\subseteq\mathfrak V$ such that $\mathcal L(\mathfrak W)$ holds and $\mathfrak W$ contains a non-nilpotent group?
The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports a negative answer to an unspecified subproblem. It does not identify whether that report concerns (a), (b), or both, or specify its dependence on $p$.

Short English statement

Mel'nikov asks whether every non-nilpotent variety of pronilpotent groups contains a locally nilpotent subvariety that is still non-nilpotent, and asks the analogous question for pro-$p$ groups at a fixed prime $p$. The meaning of finite generation needs confirmation. The supplied partial-solution label and negative-answer report do not identify the affected part.
