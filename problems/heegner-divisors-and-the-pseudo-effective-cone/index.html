Heegner Divisors and the Pseudo-Effective Cone

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $X_d=\mathcal F_{2d}$ for the space intended in the source. The supplied record does not define this space, the admissible values of $d$, or the meaning of "primitive Heegner divisor." Consequently, the following is a conditional formulation; these missing specifications must be supplied before it becomes a fully determined mathematical problem.

Fix an admissible parameter $d$, a common finite-dimensional real vector space $V_d$ of divisor classes on $X_d$, and a class map $D\mapsto[D]\in V_d$. Let $\mathscr H_d^{\mathrm{prim}}$ denote the intended family of effective primitive Heegner divisors. Define its generated cone by finite nonnegative real combinations: \[ C_d^{\mathrm{prim}} =\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i[H_i]: r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\ a_i\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq0},\ H_i\in\mathscr H_d^{\mathrm{prim}}\right\}. \] Using the same divisor-class convention, define the pseudo-effective cone by \[ \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_d) =\overline{\operatorname{cone}\{[D]:D\text{ is an effective divisor on }X_d\}}^{\,V_d}, \] where the bar denotes closure in the usual topology of $V_d$. This closed cone is the object called $\operatorname{Eff}(\mathcal F_{2d})$, the pseudo-effective cone, in the supplied question.

The requested comparison is \[ C_d^{\mathrm{prim}}\stackrel{?}{=} \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_d), \qquad\text{or}\qquad C_d^{\mathrm{prim}}\subsetneq \overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_d). \] The inclusion presupposes that the specified Heegner divisors are effective and that both cones use the same class map. The source does not say whether the generated Heegner cone itself should be closed; replacing it by $\overline{C_d^{\mathrm{prim}}}$ would give a potentially different question.

The notation suggests a moduli space of polarized K3 surfaces of degree $2d$, but the supplied record does not specify whether polarizations are primitive, ample, or merely quasi-polarizations, nor whether a coarse space, stack, or compactification is intended. These choices, the divisor equivalence relation, and any boundary divisors must be fixed. The record also does not specify whether the comparison concerns a particular $d$, every admissible $d$, or dependence on $d$. No choice among these interpretations is asserted here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every pseudo-effective divisor class on the intended space $\mathcal F_{2d}$ belongs to the cone generated by primitive Heegner divisor classes, or whether that cone is strictly smaller. The moduli space, degree range and quantifier, divisor-class convention, meaning of primitive Heegner divisor, and closure convention require clarification.
