Research attempt: Basis dependence and a normalized perturbation certificate

For a square invertible evaluation matrix $V$ of a chosen polynomial basis at a design, the raw Euclidean condition number depends on basis scaling. For example $V=I_2$ has condition number one, while replacing its basis by the same two functions scaled by $K$ and $1$ gives $VS=\operatorname{diag}(K,1)$, with condition number $K$ for $K\ge1$. The interpolation space and its predictions are unchanged after the inverse coefficient change. Thus minimizing a raw condition number across arbitrarily normalized bases is not an intrinsic design criterion.

A comparison that removes this particular ambiguity uses a perturbed evaluation matrix $V'=V+E$ for the same basis, and the transfer matrix \[ T=V'V^{-1}=I+EV^{-1}. \] It maps the vector of values on the original design to the interpolated values on the perturbed design. An invertible basis change replaces $(V',V)$ by $(V'S,VS)$ and leaves $T$ unchanged, since $(VS)^{-1}=S^{-1}V^{-1}$. If $\|EV^{-1}\|_2=\eta<1$, the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give \[ (1-\eta)\|u\|_2\le\|Tu\|_2\le(1+\eta)\|u\|_2. \] The lower bound gives injectivity; since $T$ is square, it is invertible. Applying the lower bound to $T^{-1}v$ gives $\|T^{-1}\|_2\le(1-\eta)^{-1}$, and hence $\kappa_2(T)\le(1+\eta)/(1-\eta)$. This is a proved certificate conditional on an actual perturbation bound, rather than an order-selection rule.

Small coordinate perturbations alone cannot supply a uniform certificate. With the basis $\{1,x\}$ and design $\{0,\varepsilon\}$, \[ V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},\quad V'=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&\varepsilon+\delta\end{pmatrix},\quad T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-\delta/\varepsilon&1+\delta/\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}. \] At $\delta=-\varepsilon$ the perturbed points coincide and $T$ is singular. The physical change $|\delta|$ can be arbitrarily small. Even if coincident designs are excluded, $\delta$ arbitrarily close to $-\varepsilon$ makes the inverse unbounded: the determinant $1+\delta/\varepsilon$ tends to zero while $\|T\|_2\ge1$. Thus separation or an appropriate relative perturbation restriction is indispensable for this route.
