Group Actions on 1-Manifolds — Question 13

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a compact smooth one-dimensional manifold $M$ and $0<\gamma<1$, let $\operatorname{Diff}^{1+\gamma}(M)$ denote the group of bijections $f:M\to M$ such that $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are continuously differentiable and their derivatives are $\gamma$-Hölder continuous in smooth local coordinates. Thus, on compact coordinate neighborhoods, the coordinate representatives satisfy bounds of the form \[ |Df(x)-Df(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\gamma}, \] with finite constants depending on the map and the coordinate neighborhood, and likewise for $f^{-1}$. At boundary points, derivatives are understood one-sided. No orientation-preserving restriction is imposed in this notation.

An abstract group embedding is an injective group homomorphism; it is not required to arise from conjugating the given action.

For each pair of real numbers $0<\alpha<\beta<1$, does there exist a finitely generated subgroup \[ \Gamma\leq\operatorname{Diff}^{1+\alpha}(M) \] such that there is no injective homomorphism \[ \Gamma\longrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}^{1+\beta}(M)? \] Equivalently, must there exist such a $\Gamma$ for which every homomorphism $\varphi:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Diff}^{1+\beta}(M)$ has $\ker\varphi\ne\{e\}$? The group may depend on $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $M$.

The supplied source says "circle/interval." The circle version uses $M=S^1$; under the compact-interval convention, the interval version uses $M=[0,1]$, with diffeomorphisms mapping the boundary to itself. The source record does not specify the interval, whether both versions are requested separately, or whether orientation preservation is intended. These qualifications remain unresolved; the compact-interval and unrestricted-group conventions above are explicit interpretations, not additional supplied hypotheses.

Short English statement

For every $0<\alpha<\beta<1$, can a finitely generated group act faithfully by $C^{1+\alpha}$ diffeomorphisms of the circle or interval while admitting no faithful action by $C^{1+\beta}$ diffeomorphisms of the same manifold? The interval and orientation conventions require clarification.
