Borwein Sign Conjectures

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied passage refers to a polynomial $P_n(q)$ defined in its equation (1), but that equation is absent from the supplied record. Consequently, the family $P_n$, its coefficient domain, and its degree are not specified here. The passage also invokes an earlier convention concerning zero coefficients without reproducing it. These omissions prevent a complete, unconditional formulation.

Subject to recovering that definition, let \[ P_n(q)^r=\sum_{k=0}^{d_{n,r}}a_{n,r,k}q^k, \qquad d_{n,r}=\deg\left(P_n(q)^r\right), \] for each positive integer $n$ and $r\in\{2,3\}$. The extracted question asks whether the coefficients, in increasing order of exponent, have the repeating sign pattern $+,-,-$. If the omitted convention permits zero coefficients in every sign position, and the omitted definition gives real coefficients, the proposed assertion is precisely \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\;\forall r\in\{2,3\}\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}, \quad 0\le k\le d_{n,r}\Longrightarrow \begin{cases} a_{n,r,k}\ge0,&k\equiv0\pmod3,\\ a_{n,r,k}\le0,&k\equiv1\text{ or }2\pmod3. \end{cases} \] This weak-inequality formulation is conditional on the missing zero convention; it is not a verified replacement for that convention. The case $r=2$ is attributed to Peter Borwein as the Second Borwein Conjecture, and the case $r=3$ to Chen Wang as the cubic conjecture. The passage separately describes the Third Borwein Conjecture as involving modulus $5$; that distinct conjecture is not the cubic assertion formulated here.

Short English statement

For every positive integer $n$, the question concerns whether both the square and cube of the source's Borwein polynomial have nonnegative coefficients at exponents divisible by $3$ and nonpositive coefficients at the other exponents, subject to the source's missing convention about zeros. The defining polynomial formula is also missing, so this remains a conditional statement-only draft. Supplied dated curation labels the second conjecture proved and the cubic conjecture partially proved; these labels have not been independently verified.
