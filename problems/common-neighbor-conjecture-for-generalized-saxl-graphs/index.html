Math-6899

Common-Neighbor Conjecture for Generalized Saxl Graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be a finite set and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ be a primitive permutation group. Here primitive means transitive with no $G$-invariant partition other than the partition into singletons and the one-part partition. For $B\subseteq\Omega$, its pointwise stabilizer is \[ G_{(B)}=\{g\in G:g(\beta)=\beta\text{ for every }\beta\in B\}. \] A base is a subset $B\subseteq\Omega$ with $G_{(B)}=\{1\}$, and the base size is \[ b(G)=\min\{|B|:B\subseteq\Omega,\ G_{(B)}=\{1\}\}. \] Assume $b(G)\geq2$. Define the generalized Saxl graph $\Sigma(G)$ to be the simple undirected graph with vertex set $\Omega$ in which distinct vertices $\alpha,\beta$ are adjacent precisely when there exists a base $B$ of cardinality $b(G)$ containing both vertices. Write $N_{\Sigma(G)}(\alpha)$ for the set of neighbors of $\alpha$.

Common-Neighbor Conjecture for Generalized Saxl Graphs. Is it true that, for every such $G$, \[ \forall\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\quad \alpha\neq\beta\ \Longrightarrow\ N_{\Sigma(G)}(\alpha)\cap N_{\Sigma(G)}(\beta)\neq\varnothing? \] Equivalently, for every distinct $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$, must there exist $\gamma\in\Omega\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\}$ and bases $B_1,B_2\subseteq\Omega$ satisfying \[ |B_1|=|B_2|=b(G),\qquad \{\alpha,\gamma\}\subseteq B_1,\qquad \{\beta,\gamma\}\subseteq B_2? \] The two bases need not coincide. When $b(G)=2$, this definition gives the usual Saxl graph. The phrase "every two vertices" is interpreted above as distinct vertices. Allowing equal vertices gives an equivalent formulation here: transitivity ensures that every vertex belongs to a minimum base, and $b(G)\geq2$ therefore ensures that every vertex has a neighbor.

Short English statement

For every finite primitive permutation group with minimum base size at least two, join two distinct points when they occur together in a minimum base. The question asks whether every pair of distinct points has a common neighbor. The minimum bases witnessing the two adjacencies may differ. The supplied record reports partial results, but the general question and its literature status require human review.
