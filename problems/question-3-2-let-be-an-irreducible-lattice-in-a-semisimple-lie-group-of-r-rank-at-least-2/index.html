Question 3.2 — Let Γ be an irreducible lattice in a semisimple Lie group of R-rank at least 2.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $L$ be a semisimple Lie group of real rank at least $2$, and $\Gamma\le L$ an irreducible lattice. A lattice is discrete with finite Haar covolume. In the standard semisimple setting irreducibility is formulated through projections to noncompact factors; the source does not supply connectedness, center, compact-factor or precise irreducibility conventions. Let $F_n$ be a free group of the source's allowed rank and $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)=\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)/\operatorname{Inn}(F_n)$.

Question 3.2 asks whether \[ \forall\rho:\Gamma\longrightarrow\operatorname{Out}(F_n) \text{ a homomorphism}:\qquad |\rho(\Gamma)|<\infty. \] The rank range for $n$ is not given. The question concerns every abstract homomorphism, not just injections or a special representation family.

The source reports a positive result for nonuniform lattices, reference [16], meaning the lattice quotient is noncompact. It says the argument uses its named Kazhdan--Margulis finiteness theorem and virtual abelianness of solvable subgroups of $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$, reference [5]. Exact theorem and lattice hypotheses require those references. The source does not claim this settles the uniform-lattice case.

It also reports Farb--Masur [32] answering the analogous finite-image question for mapping-class-group targets, using Kaimanovich--Masur [56] on random walks in Teichmüller space, with [54] and an alternative approach in [6]. These comparison results are source-era context and do not replace the $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ target or supply a current audit of the full question.

Short English statement

Must every homomorphism from an irreducible higher-real-rank lattice to a free-group outer automorphism group have finite image? The source reports the nonuniform case and a mapping-class analogue, with their precise hypotheses and current general status absent.
