Math-1642

Robust failure in nonconvex stochastic Hamilton-Jacobi homogenization

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Takis Souganidis asks about stochastic homogenization of the random Hamilton--Jacobi equation \[ F\left(D^2u^\varepsilon,Du^\varepsilon,u^\varepsilon, x/\varepsilon,x,\omega\right)=0, \qquad\varepsilon\downarrow0, \] where $F$ is nonconvex and stationary ergodic in its fast spatial variable. The extraction specifies no domain, boundary conditions, scaling of a viscous term, ellipticity, coercivity, properness, or solution class. In particular, it is a research question, not a fully quantified assertion that every stationary-ergodic nonconvex operator homogenizes.

A precise meaning of stationarity can be supplied by a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ with a measure-preserving ${\mathbb{R}}^d$-action $\tau_z$, ergodic in the sense that invariant events have probability zero or one, and \[ F(X,p,r,y+z,x,\omega)=F(X,p,r,y,x,\tau_z\omega). \] Here $X\in\operatorname{Sym}_d$, $p\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$, $r\in{\mathbb{R}}$, and $y,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$. Nonconvexity usually refers to the gradient variable $p$, but the source does not say so explicitly. Once a well-posed family of viscosity-solution boundary or initial value problems has been fixed, the homogenization target is almost-sure convergence, in a specified topology, to the solution of an appropriate deterministic effective equation, for example \[ \overline F(D^2u,Du,u,x)=0. \] The operator $\overline F$, admissible data, and convergence mode are part of the missing formulation; this display is a target template rather than an asserted theorem.

Separate auxiliary parabolic comparison statement. The supplied research aid considers problems of the form \[ u_t+\mathcal A^\varepsilon(D^2u,Du,u,x,t,\omega)=0, \qquad v_t+\mathcal B^\varepsilon(D^2v,Dv,v,x,t,\omega)=0, \] with identical initial and, when applicable, boundary data. Assume suitable viscosity comparison for these problems and the shifted subsolutions and supersolutions, admissible common solution classes, a uniform operator bound $|\mathcal A^\varepsilon-\mathcal B^\varepsilon|\le\delta$, and a scalar-variable Lipschitz constant $L\ge0$ for the reference operator, uniformly over the arguments used. The aid reports the stability estimate \[ |u(x,t)-v(x,t)|\le\delta\,a_L(t),\qquad a_L(t)=\begin{cases}t,&L=0,\$e^{Lt}-1)/L,&L>0.\end{cases} \] Consequently, if at a fixed $(x_*,t_*)$ the reference family has an almost-sure subsequential gap \[ \limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}u^\varepsilon(x_*,t_*) -\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}u^\varepsilon(x_*,t_*)\ge g>0, \] then the perturbed family's gap is at least $g-2\delta a_L(t_*)$, on the same event. A positive lower bound rules out convergence there. The uniform operator bound and comparison assumptions are essential; stationarity and ergodicity alone do not give this estimate.

The aid attributes to Ziliotto a two-dimensional first-order counterexample with limiting values 1 and 2 at $(0,1)$, without supplying its full Hamiltonian, data, or normalization. Conditional on that stated normalization, the common stationary-ergodic environment, a scalar-independent Hamiltonian, and the required comparison assumptions, any admissible Hamiltonian at uniform distance $\delta<1/2$ has gap at least $1-2\delta>0$. This describes openness relative to the admissible comparison class on that environment, not an unrestricted open set of all random operators. The aid also reports failure in a viscous two-dimensional subclass, but supplies neither a citation nor the scaling needed to identify it with the displayed stationary equation.

For a separate proper elliptic comparison problem with identical boundary data, if the reference operator satisfies \[ F(X,p,r+s,x)-F(X,p,r,x)\ge\kappa s \quad(s\ge0),\qquad\kappa>0, \] and the comparison and uniform perturbation assumptions hold, the aid reports the bound $\|u-v\|_\infty\le\delta/\kappa$. These robustness statements do not by themselves specify which of the original nonconvex stationary problems homogenize.

Short English statement

Souganidis asks for stochastic homogenization beyond convex Hamiltonians. The source omits the hypotheses and data needed for a single theorem. The supplied aid reports counterexamples in particular parabolic subclasses and a comparison estimate that preserves a positive nonconvergence gap under sufficiently small admissible uniform perturbations. Their relation to the displayed stationary equation requires explicit hypotheses and scaling.
