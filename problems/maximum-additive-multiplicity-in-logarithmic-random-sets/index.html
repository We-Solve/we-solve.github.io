Maximum Additive Multiplicity in Logarithmic Random Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{N}}_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$. Let $(\xi_i)_{i\geq1}$ be independent Bernoulli random variables satisfying \[ \mathbb P(\xi_i=1)=\frac1i,\qquad \mathbb P(\xi_i=0)=1-\frac1i, \] and define the logarithmic random set \[ A=\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}:\xi_i=1\}. \] In particular, $1\in A$ almost surely. The positive-integer indexing is required for the supplied probabilities $1/i$ to be defined.

The supplied problem asks to determine \[ \max_x r_A(x), \] where $r_A(x)$ counts representations of $x$ as a sum of elements of $A$.

Unresolved formulation. The source record does not specify whether summands must be distinct, whether repetitions are allowed, whether order matters, or whether the number of summands is fixed or arbitrary. For example, under the additional convention that representations are finite subsets of distinct elements and that $x$ is a positive integer, the representation function would be \[ r_A^{\mathrm{dist}}(x)=\#\left\{B\subseteq A: B\text{ is finite and }\sum_{b\in B}b=x\right\},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}. \] This is only a possible interpretation, not a convention established by the supplied record. The domain of $x$, the treatment of the empty sum if $x=0$ is admitted, and the intended probabilistic meaning of "determine" are also unspecified. In addition, the source writes a maximum over an unrestricted domain without specifying whether it is attained or whether an extended supremum is intended. Consequently, the supplied evidence does not determine a unique precise mathematical target. No truncation, growth parameter, or almost-sure assertion is added here.

Short English statement

Independently include each positive integer $i$ in $A$ with probability $1/i$, and determine the largest number of representations of a number as a sum of elements of $A$. The representation convention, range of the represented number, interpretation of the unrestricted maximum, and intended probabilistic conclusion require clarification.
