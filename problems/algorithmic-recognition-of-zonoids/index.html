Math-3602

Algorithmic Recognition of Zonoids

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\geq 1$ be an integer, and let $p\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$. Set \[ K_p=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:p(x)\geq 0\}. \] The input is promised to satisfy the following hypotheses: $K_p$ is compact and convex, has nonempty interior in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, and is centrally symmetric, meaning that there exists $c\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$ such that $K_p=2c-K_p$. The source does not require the center to be the origin.

For $a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$, write $[a,b]=\{(1-t)a+tb:0\leq t\leq 1\}$. A zonotope is a finite Minkowski sum of line segments, \[ Z=[a_1,b_1]+\cdots+[a_m,b_m] =\{z_1+\cdots+z_m:z_j\in[a_j,b_j]\},\qquad m\geq 1. \] For nonempty compact sets $A,B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$, their Hausdorff distance is \[ d_H(A,B)=\max\left\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_2,\ \sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}\|b-a\|_2\right\}. \] A convex body $K$ is a zonoid if there exists a sequence of zonotopes $(Z_n)_{n\geq1}$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ with $d_H(Z_n,K)\to0$.

The supplied recognition question asks for an algorithm which, given a polynomial $p$ satisfying the promise above, terminates and correctly decides whether \[ \exists (Z_n)_{n\geq1}\quad \left[\text{each }Z_n\text{ is a zonotope and }\lim_{n\to\infty}d_H(Z_n,K_p)=0\right]. \] No behavior on inputs violating the promise is specified.

The computational formulation remains unresolved: the source specifies neither the allowed polynomial coefficients nor their effective representation or the model of computation. Writing $p\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ describes the mathematical polynomial, without asserting that arbitrary real coefficients have finite algorithmic descriptions. The source also does not specify whether $d$ is fixed or is part of the input. Thus the displayed predicate is precise, but the requested decision problem requires these additional specifications.

The supplied original excerpt contains a separate, corrupted discussion of generic discotopes and a truncated conjecture. It does not provide a complete alternative formulation or additional usable hypotheses for this recognition question.

Short English statement

Given a polynomial whose nonnegative set is a centrally symmetric convex body, determine algorithmically whether that body is a Hausdorff limit of finite sums of line segments. The coefficient encoding, computation model, and treatment of dimension remain unspecified. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
