Math-3901

Profinite Rigidity in the Coxeter Galaxy

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, its profinite completion is \[ \widehat G=\varprojlim_{\substack{N\trianglelefteq G\\[G:N]<\infty}}G/N, \] with its inverse-limit topology. Given a specified class $\mathcal C$ of groups containing $G$, profinite rigidity of $G$ within $\mathcal C$ means \[ \forall H\in\mathcal C,\qquad \widehat H\cong\widehat G\ \text{as topological groups}\ \Longrightarrow\ H\cong G. \] The supplied record does not specify the comparison class or formulate a precise profinite-rigidity conjecture. Its title concerns profinite rigidity, but its substantive excerpt instead states Question 3.1, the Reachability and Coloring Problem, and ends mid-sentence.

The reachability question can be recorded only as the following conditional formulation. Let $\Gamma=(V,E)$ denote the intended Coxeter galaxy, once its vertices, edges, and vertical and horizontal moves have been defined. A move scheme is represented abstractly by a relation $R\subseteq V\times V$ of permitted applications. Does there exist a finite family $\mathcal M$ of permitted vertical and horizontal move schemes such that, for every $u,v\in V$ lying in the same connected component of $\Gamma$, there are $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}$, vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_k$, and schemes $R_1,\ldots,R_k\in\mathcal M$ satisfying \[ v_0=u,\qquad v_k=v,\qquad (v_{j-1},v_j)\in R_j\quad(1\leq j\leq k)? \] Each application must be an allowed move in the intended galaxy. The excerpt further asks whether an algorithm can output which kinds of moves are needed along a path. Under the interpretation that this means constructing a labelled path, the requested output would consist of such a finite sequence together with the move kinds used at its steps, for every input pair in the same component. The source does not settle whether a stronger classification of necessary move kinds or termination on disconnected input pairs is intended.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied excerpt does not define the Coxeter galaxy, its permitted presentations or ranks, its edge relation, or either kind of move. Consequently, the displayed formulation is a template, not a fully specified mathematical problem. The record marks the statement as unrecoverable and supplies a verification note saying that the profinite-rigidity phrase is a section heading followed by a proved theorem for ranks at most three. Neither the precise theorem nor a corresponding general conjecture is supplied. No such theorem or conjecture is reconstructed here.

Short English statement

The excerpt asks whether finitely many kinds of vertical and horizontal moves connect every pair of vertices in each component of the Coxeter galaxy, and whether an algorithm can identify the move kinds along a path. The galaxy and moves are undefined in the supplied excerpt, and its relationship to the profinite-rigidity title remains unresolved.
