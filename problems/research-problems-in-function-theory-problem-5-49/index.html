Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.49

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the weight-zero function convention, let a group $\Gamma$ act by conformal transformations on a plane domain $D$, and let $f$ be a nonconstant meromorphic function on $D$ with $f(\gamma z)=f(z)$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$ wherever defined. At a point $z_0$ where $f$ is holomorphic, write its Taylor expansion in a specified coordinate as \[ f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n(z-z_0)^n. \] Its gap pattern is the set of indices at which $a_n=0$, or equivalently its nonzero coefficient support. If that support is infinite, list its positive indices as $n_1<n_2<\cdots$. A Hadamard-gap expansion means that for some $q>1$, \[ n_{j+1}/n_j\geq q \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j. \] Finite Taylor polynomials are a separate case, not an infinite Hadamard-gap sequence.

L. A. Rubel asks which gap patterns are possible for nonconstant automorphic functions, in particular whether a Hadamard-gap expansion can occur. The source expects a sharp answer to depend on the automorphy group. It does not specify the group class, domain, expansion center, coordinate, or whether a weight or character is allowed instead of weight-zero invariance. Thus a universal exclusion or an existence claim for a particular group cannot be inferred from the question alone; the definitions above make one standard interpretation explicit.

The source relates the question to a theorem attributed to Rényi, with "no citation," saying that a nonconstant periodic entire function cannot have more than half of its Taylor coefficients zero. The meaning of "more than half" is not defined (for example, which asymptotic density or finite counting statement is intended), so no precise coefficient-density inequality is asserted here. The supplied summary reports the automorphic-gap question open as of the 2018 edition, with no recent resolution located.

Short English statement

Rubel asks how sparse the Taylor coefficients of an automorphic function can be, especially whether their nonzero indices can grow geometrically. The answer is expected to depend on the group, which is unspecified. A related periodic-function coefficient claim is quoted without a citation or precise density convention.
