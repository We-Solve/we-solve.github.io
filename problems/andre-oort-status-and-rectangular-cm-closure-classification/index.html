Math-337

Andre-Oort status and rectangular CM closure classification

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the standard pure-Shimura interpretation, let $S$ be a Shimura variety over ${\mathbb{C}}$. A special subvariety is an irreducible component arising from a Shimura subdatum and the permitted Hecke translates; special points are the zero-dimensional cases, equivalently CM points in this setting. Conjecture 51 of Y. André and F. Oort asserts that for every subset $\Sigma$ of special points, \[ \overline\Sigma^{\mathrm{Zar}}\text{ is a finite union of special subvarieties}. \] Equivalently, every irreducible subvariety with Zariski-dense special points is special. The assigned extraction suppresses the ambient variety and ends mid-sentence in its literature discussion; no mixed-Shimura extension is intended here.

The supplied status assessment distinguishes three levels. It reports that the missing historical continuation says the one-dimensional-closure case was known in 2004 under the intended Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Klingler--Yafaev's published conditional theorem covers arbitrary pure Shimura varieties under the GRH hypotheses of that theorem. Tsimerman's published result proves the assertion unconditionally for $\mathcal A_g$, with standard Hodge-type and abelian-type consequences. Pila--Shankar--Tsimerman, arXiv:2109.08788v4, states the unconditional maximal-special-subvariety theorem for arbitrary Shimura varieties, but the literature located in the supplied check still treated it as a preprint. These publication and conditionality qualifications accompany the registry solved label and are not replaced by an independently updated status claim.

The attempt additionally gives an explicit elementary family in $Y(1)^n\cong{\mathbb{A}}^n_{{\mathbb{C}}}$, where $Y(1)$ is the elliptic $j$-line without its cusp and singular moduli are its CM $j$-values. Let \[ \Sigma=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{r}\prod_{i=1}^n A_{\ell i}, \] where each $A_{\ell i}$ is a set of singular moduli and $r$ is finite. Omit any rectangle with an empty factor. For each remaining rectangle let $F_\ell=\{i:A_{\ell i}\text{ is finite}\}$, and for each choice $a\in\prod_{i\in F_\ell}A_{\ell i}$ put \[ X(F_\ell,a)=\{z\in Y(1)^n:z_i=a_i\text{ for }i\in F_\ell\}. \] The aid's exact closure formula is \[ \overline\Sigma^{\mathrm{Zar}}= \bigcup_{\ell}\ \bigcup_{a\in\prod_{i\in F_\ell}A_{\ell i}} X(F_\ell,a). \] An infinite subset of $Y(1)$ is Zariski dense, while a finite one remains its singleton choices, explaining the product closure. Each slice is special and irreducible, of dimension $n-|F_\ell|$. The irreducible components are exactly the distinct inclusion-maximal slices, using \[ X(F,a)\subset X(F',a') \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F'\subset F\text{ and }a_i=a'_i\ (i\in F'). \] For nonempty $\Sigma$, the closure dimension is $\max_\ell(n-|F_\ell|)$; for an empty union the closure and component set are empty. This classifies finite unions of Cartesian CM families only, not correlated CM sets or the general André--Oort theorem.

Short English statement

André--Oort asks for special irreducible components in the Zariski closure of any set of special points. The supplied status distinguishes conditional and published results from a general preprint theorem. Its extra worked case computes closures of finite unions of Cartesian CM families in powers of the $j$-line as explicit coordinate special slices.
