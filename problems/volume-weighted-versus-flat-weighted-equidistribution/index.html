Math-326

Volume-weighted versus flat-weighted equidistribution

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether two measures $\nu_T,\bar\nu_T$ converge to normalized Haar probability on $\Gamma\backslash G$. Their preceding definitions, lattice hypotheses, flat family, and cutoff are not included. The supplied aid treats a raw-volume-cutoff family of periodic flats and the following two normalizations. For any nonempty finite cutoff family $\mathcal F_T$, give each flat $F$ a volume $v_F>0$ and normalized probability $\mu_F$. Put $M_T=|\mathcal F_T|$, $\overline v_T=M_T^{-1}\sum_Fv_F$, and define \[ \nu_T=\frac{\sum_Fv_F\mu_F}{\sum_Fv_F},\qquad \bar\nu_T=\frac1{M_T}\sum_F\mu_F. \] These formulas make the aid's volume-weighted versus flat-weighted convention explicit; matching their names to the omitted source definitions requires verification. The actual question is convergence of both to $m_{\Gamma\backslash G}$, not merely comparison of them.

Let ${\mathbb{E}}_T$ be uniform averaging over $\mathcal F_T$ and set $a_F(f)=\mu_F(f)$. The aid's exact identity is \[ \nu_T(f)-\bar\nu_T(f)= \frac{\operatorname{Cov}_T(v_F,a_F(f))}{\overline v_T}. \] Define $\operatorname{rMAD}_T={\mathbb{E}}_T|v_F-\overline v_T|/\overline v_T$ and $\operatorname{CV}_T=\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}_T(v_F-\overline v_T)^2}/\overline v_T$. With $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\eta)=\sup_A|\mu(A)-\eta(A)|$, the uniform estimate is \[ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_T,\bar\nu_T)\leq\tfrac12\operatorname{rMAD}_T \leq\tfrac12\operatorname{CV}_T. \] The factor one half is sharp for abstract mutually singular component measures. Thus $\operatorname{rMAD}_T\to0$ suffices to transfer convergence and mass control between normalizations. More generally, vanishing of the normalized covariance for every bounded continuous $f$ suffices for them to have the same weak probability limit. None of these small-variation or decorrelation hypotheses is established for the actual geometric cutoff family.

The nonimplication is already visible in abstract two-point models, not geometric counterexamples. With equally many $\delta_0$ components of volume one and $\delta_1$ components of volume two, the flat average is $(\delta_0+\delta_1)/2$ but the volume average is $(\delta_0+2\delta_1)/3$. With twice as many volume-one $\delta_0$ components as volume-two $\delta_1$ components, the volume average is $(\delta_0+\delta_1)/2$ but the flat average is $(2\delta_0+\delta_1)/3$. Repeating labeled components gives arbitrarily large finite families. Therefore neither normalization's abstract convergence implies the other's without geometric input.

The aid reports positive rank-one and arithmetic $PGL_3$ modular results but says the literal higher-rank raw-volume problem remains open in general. The precise weighting, packet, and ordering hypotheses of those results are absent. The source also quotes uniqueness of the ergodic maximal-entropy Haar measure, reference [113]; that alone does not prove either convergence, prevent escape of mass, or establish the required entropy bounds.

Short English statement

The supplied comparison identifies the exact bias between volume-weighted and equally weighted flat averages as a normalized covariance. It gives a sharp total-variation bound and sufficient conditions for transferring a limit, but does not verify those conditions for the source family. Abstract examples show why one convergence statement alone does not imply the other; the actual higher-rank Haar-equidistribution question remains separately qualified.
