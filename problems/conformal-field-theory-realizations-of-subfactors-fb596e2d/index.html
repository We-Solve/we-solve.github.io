Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
A subfactor is an inclusion $N\subset M$ of von Neumann algebras with trivial centers. Its index and standard invariant encode the size and higher relative-commutant structure of the inclusion. In an operator-algebraic conformal field theory, interval or local observable algebras and sectors can produce subfactor inclusions. To say a subfactor arises in conformal field theory requires identifying the inclusion or invariant that the construction realizes. The primary report concerns finite Jones index, with the intended conformal realization recovering the standard invariant or at least its quantum double. This finite-index context is explicit in Vaughan Jones's contribution, even though the catalogue sentence abbreviates it.

Why index alone cannot specify a realization.
Even at the algebraic level, recovering a standard invariant requires more than reproducing a scalar size. For a concrete illustration, take pointed fusion rules on a finite group $G$: simple symbols $X_g$ multiply by $X_gX_h=X_{gh}$. Both groups $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ and $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ have four symbols of dimension one and the same total squared dimension four, but their multiplication tables are not isomorphic. The first has an element of order four, whereas every nonidentity element in the second squares to the identity.
What this tests.
If a proposed conformal realization checks only a matching total dimension, it cannot distinguish even these simple tensor-data possibilities. An equivalence respecting simple objects and products preserves element orders in this pointed case, so the difference is intrinsic. This is a calculation about candidate invariant data, not an assertion that the two examples give a particular index-four subfactor or that their conformal realizations are unavailable.
Source-level gap.
The source concerns finite-index subfactors and asks for the actual inclusion/standard invariant or at least its quantum double. A positive-energy local conformal net must additionally supply interval algebras, covariance and locality, and its sectors must be matched to the required invariant. None is constructed from arbitrary subfactor data here. The first missing step is such a reconstruction with a verified tensor/braiding comparison, not merely an index match. The Asaeda--Haagerup instance 30002840 and the repeated broad program 30004278 remain within that distinction.
