Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 8.10

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $C$ be a nontrivial finite group, and let $H=F/N$ be a finitely presented group containing a subgroup isomorphic to $C^n$ for every integer $n\ge1$, where $C^n$ denotes the direct product of $n$ copies of $C$. In the finite-presentation interpretation, $F$ is a fixed finite-rank free group and $N\trianglelefteq F$ is its presentation kernel. Choose embeddings $\iota_n:C^n\hookrightarrow H$, and amalgamate two copies of $H$ using the corresponding copies of $\iota_n$: \[ G_n=H*_{C^n}H,\qquad N_n=\ker(F*F\longrightarrow G_n),\qquad G_n\cong(F*F)/N_n. \] For $K\trianglelefteq L$, let $d_L(K)$ be the minimum cardinality of a subset of $K$ whose normal closure in $L$ equals $K$. The question asks whether, for every such setup, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}d_{F*F}(N_n)=\infty, \quad\text{that is,}\quad \forall R\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \exists n_R\ \forall n\ge n_R:\ d_{F*F}(N_n)\ge R. \] The embeddings and their possible compatibility as $n$ varies are not specified in the excerpt. The displayed construction uses the same chosen embedding in the two copies of $H$; an interpretation using different embeddings should be identified separately.

A finite group $C$ is perfect when $C=[C,C]$. The supplied partial-progress summary claims an affirmative answer for every nonperfect finite $C$, via linear growth in the number of generators of $H_2(G_n;{\mathbb{Z}})$ and a homological relator lower bound. It attributes this to a new argument "above," which is not included in the record. It reports the perfect case open, identifies that case as the one relevant to the relation-gap problem and the source construction, and says the relation-gap problem remained open in literature checked through December 2024. Its further assertions that known computable lower bounds stay bounded in the perfect case and that this case matches the difficulty of Bridson--Tweedale's "putative relation gap" are supplied assessments, not verified equivalences here.

Short English statement

For the doubles of a fixed finitely presented group amalgamated along larger direct powers of a finite group, must the normal number of defining relations grow without bound? The supplied summary claims the nonperfect case and leaves the perfect case open, but its claimed new argument is absent.
