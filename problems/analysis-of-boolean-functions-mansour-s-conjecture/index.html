Analysis of Boolean Functions — Mansour's Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $n$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and equip $\{-1,1\}^n$ with the uniform probability measure. For $S\subseteq[n]$, define \[ \chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i,\qquad \widehat f(S)=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\{-1,1\}^n}f(x)\chi_S(x), \] where $\chi_\varnothing=1$. Thus \[ f(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\widehat f(S)\chi_S(x), \qquad \sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\widehat f(S)^2=1 \] for a Boolean function $f\colon\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$.

A disjunctive normal form (DNF) is a disjunction of terms, each term being a conjunction of literals. Under the truth convention $+1=\mathrm{true}$, a positive literal on coordinate $i$ tests $x_i=1$, and a negative literal tests $x_i=-1$. Here the size of a DNF means its number of terms. This is the standard interpretation used in this draft; the supplied excerpt does not explicitly define DNF size. The Fourier spectrum of $f$ is $\varepsilon$-concentrated on $\mathcal F\subseteq 2^{[n]}$ if \[ \sum_{S\subseteq[n],\ S\notin\mathcal F}\widehat f(S)^2\leq\varepsilon. \] This uses squared Fourier mass under the uniform measure, another convention left implicit in the supplied excerpt.

Mansour's Conjecture (supplied formulation). There exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that, for every positive integer $n$, every integer $s\geq2$, every function $f\colon\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ computable by a DNF with at most $s$ terms, and every $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]$, there exists $\mathcal F\subseteq 2^{[n]}$ satisfying \[ |\mathcal F|\leq s^{C\log(1/\varepsilon)} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum_{S\notin\mathcal F}\widehat f(S)^2\leq\varepsilon. \] Here $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm; changing its base only changes the absolute constant. The constant $C$ is independent of $n,s,f,\varepsilon$. Using "at most $s$ terms" expresses the usual size-bound formulation of the supplied statement.

Supplied qualifications and background. The source also records a weaker version with $|\mathcal F|\leq s^{O_\varepsilon(1)}$: for each fixed $\varepsilon$, the exponent may depend on $\varepsilon$, but not on $n,s,f$. It reports that the weak bound $s^{O(1/\varepsilon)}$ follows from the Fourier Entropy--Influence Conjecture. It further reports that Klivans, Lee, and Wan, reference label [KLW10], proved the conjecture for "almost all" polynomial-size DNF formulas, "appropriately defined." No distribution, asymptotic regime, or precise meaning of "almost all" is supplied, so this qualification is retained without a formal exceptional-set claim. Finally, the source attributes to Mansour, reference label [Man95], the upper bound \[ (s/\varepsilon)^{O(\log\log(s/\varepsilon)\,\log(1/\varepsilon))}. \] These are supplied background reports, not independently verified results or a new assessment of the conjecture's status.

Short English statement

Mansour's Conjecture asks whether every Boolean function computed by a DNF with at most $s>1$ terms has all but $\varepsilon$ of its squared Fourier mass on at most $s^{C\log(1/\varepsilon)}$ subsets of coordinates, for one universal constant $C$. The registry labels the problem open. The conventions and background claims recorded here require human mathematical and source review.
