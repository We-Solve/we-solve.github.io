Scope recovered from Question 13

The question asks about transferring Lauder's deformation method to other methods without a linear factor, and about computational use of Fesenko's results. It does not specify the variable in that factor, a uniform input model, or a particular Fesenko formula to implement. The lecture does supply a differential-system transport mechanism. The following attempt isolates what this mechanism proves algebraically and the first analytic step that it does not justify. No general complexity improvement or consequence of an unspecified formula is claimed.
Formal transport, with a complete proof.
Let $K$ be a characteristic-zero field, $A(t)$ an $r$ by $r$ matrix over $K[[t]]$, and $p$ a positive integer. There is a unique matrix $U(t)$ satisfying $U'=AU$ and $U(0)=I$: writing power series gives \[ (n+1)U_{n+1}=\sum_{i+j=n}A_iU_j, \] which recursively determines every coefficient. Its constant term is invertible, so its formal inverse exists, also recursively by equating coefficients of $UU^{-1}=I$. For any constant matrix $F_0$, put \[ F(t)=U(t)F_0U(t^p)^{-1}. \] The product rule and differentiation of $VV^{-1}=I$ show that \[ F'=AF-p t^{p-1}F A(t^p),\qquad F(0)=F_0. \] Conversely if this equation holds, differentiating $U(t)^{-1}F(t)U(t^p)$ gives zero, since the two connection terms cancel. A formal series in characteristic zero with derivative zero is constant. The constant is $F_0$, proving the formula and uniqueness. This is a conventional left-action normalization; the transposed or semilinear normalization in a cohomology implementation must be checked separately. No equality between unmentioned implementations is assumed.
The attempted improvement and its first gap.
One tempting route is to compute $F_0$ cheaply on a special fiber, transport formally, and simply substitute the target parameter. Formal solvability alone does not justify this substitution. For example in $\mathbb Q_p[[t]]$ with odd $p$, take $r=1$, $A(t)=(1-t)^{-1}$, $U(t)=(1-t)^{-1}$ and $F_0=1$. Then \[ F(t)=\frac{1-t^p}{1-t}=1+t+\cdots+t^{p-1}. \] At $t=2$ the rational functions $U(t)$ and $U(t^p)$ are both defined, but the series $\sum_{j\ge0}2^j$ does not converge $p$-adically: its terms have valuation zero, whereas terms of any convergent series must tend to zero. Thus evaluating separately the two transported series is unjustified even though their rational combination has a perfectly good value. In this example cancellation repairs the issue, but it requires recognizing the rational identity. It is not supplied by the formal differential equation alone.
Why initial precision does not automatically propagate.
For a matrix over a valued field set $v(M)$ equal to the minimum valuation of its entries, with $v(0)=+\infty$. Every product entry is a sum of products, so $v(MN)\ge v(M)+v(N)$ by the nonarchimedean triangle inequality. Consequently, replacing $F_0$ by $F_0+E$ with $v(E)\ge N$ changes a well-defined specialization of $F$ by a matrix of valuation at least \[ N+v(U(t))+v(U(t^p)^{-1}). \] This lower bound need not be $N$. In the scalar case multiplication by $p^{-s}$ loses exactly $s$ digits: $p^{-s}p^N=p^{N-s}$. Therefore a promised precision at a special fiber is not by itself a promised precision at the target. Bounds on denominators, evaluation domains and required truncation lengths are part of the algorithm, not consequences of the displayed transport identity.

Audit and unresolved parts

The primary Question 13 and Lauder lecture were inspected. The proof checks the order of the matrix factors rather than treating them as commuting scalars. The explicit counterexample concerns a proposed evaluation step, not the correctness of Lauder's method. It does not establish a lower bound on all deformation algorithms. No implementation, point count, or performance benchmark was run. The other half of the question supplies no particular Fesenko computational construction in the question text, and this attempt proves no consequence for it. A specified algorithm with convergence and precision bounds, followed by a cost comparison in specified parameters, remains necessary.
