Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A hidden-shift instance consists of functions $f_0,f_1$ on a group $G$ related by $f_1(x)=f_0(xs)$ for an unknown shift $s$, with the additive version $f_1(x)=f_0(x+s)$ for abelian groups. The task is to recover a compatible shift from the stipulated function access. The Heisenberg group over $\mathbb F_p$ consists of upper-unitriangular $3\times3$ matrices, represented by triples $(a,b,c)$ with multiplication $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab')$. The task is a hidden-shift algorithm on this nonabelian group.

Research attempt: A central cyclic reduction and the loss in abelianization

Let $H_p$ consist of triples with multiplication $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab')$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Its center contains $z_t=(0,0,t)$, since $gz_t=z_tg=(a,b,c+t)$. Given an injective cyclic hidden-shift pair $h_0,h_1:\mathbb F_p\to\mathcal Y$, with $h_1(c)=h_0(c+t)$, define \[ F_0(a,b,c)=(a,b,h_0(c)),\qquad F_1(a,b,c)=(a,b,h_1(c)). \] Both are injective and $F_1(g)=F_0(gz_t)$. Each coherent evaluation uses exactly one evaluation of the corresponding $h_b$ plus copying the two field coordinates; no search for $t$ is hidden in the construction. If an algorithm recovers a right shift $(u,v,w)$ for this pair, then equality of the first two output coordinates for all $a,b,c$ forces $u=v=0$, and injectivity of $h_0$ forces $w=t$. Hence an injective Heisenberg hidden-shift algorithm also solves cyclic hidden shift over $\mathbb F_p$, with the same number of original oracle evaluations and only the stated coordinate overhead.

To see why abelianization alone cannot retrieve this central coordinate, the inverse of $(a,b,c)$ is $(-a,-b,-c+ab)$, as multiplication verifies. A direct calculation gives \[ [(a,0,0),(0,b,0)]=(0,0,ab), \] where $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. Every central element arises by choosing $a=1$, $b=t$. For any one-dimensional character $\chi:H_p\to\mathbb C^\times$, commutativity of scalar multiplication gives $\chi([g,h])=1$. Thus $\chi(z_t)=1$ for every $t$. In fact $\chi(a,b,c)$ factors through $(a,b)$: decompose $(a,b,c)=(a,b,0)z_c$ and use the preceding identity. Translation by $z_t$ therefore supplies no phase at all to these characters. A method retaining only one-dimensional-character shift phases has discarded precisely the unknown coordinate in the constructed family.

This is not an unconditional lower bound on all Heisenberg algorithms. Higher-dimensional representations or other uses of the oracle might retain the missing information. Nor does reduction from cyclic hidden shift prove that a fast Heisenberg algorithm is impossible. It establishes that a proposed shortcut via the abelian quotient has not bypassed cyclic hidden shift.

As a finite boundary check, an injective right-shift instance can always be solved by comparing $F_1(e)$ with $F_0(g)$ for all $p^3$ elements: equality holds exactly at the shift. On the central family, testing $z_t$ costs only $p$ candidates. Neither is polynomial in $\log p$, and the source does not specify whether "fast" means such a bound or a different improvement.
