Math-2176

3.3 (Agol) — Injective surfaces with only double curves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a closed hyperbolic $3$-manifold. A $\pi_1$-injective immersed surface is a smooth immersion $f:S\to M$ of a closed surface such that $f_*:\pi_1(S)\to\pi_1(M)$ is injective. Having only double curves of self-intersection means that every multiple image point has exactly two preimages, the two local sheets meet transversely along a curve, and no triple or higher intersection points occur. Embedded surfaces are allowed as the case with empty self-intersection locus.

Agol's question, item 3.3, asks whether every such $M$ admits a closed $\pi_1$-injective surface with this double-curve property. In the intended essential-surface reading the surface is nontrivial (customarily of negative Euler characteristic). That condition is not explicit in the excerpt: allowing an embedded $2$-sphere would make the literal $\pi_1$-injectivity condition vacuous and would not capture the research question. The surface's connectedness, orientability and precise essentiality conventions, and the orientability of $M$, need source confirmation.

The supplied background additionally suggests a bounded-volume statement: for every $V>0$, all but finitely many closed hyperbolic $3$-manifolds with $\operatorname{Vol}(M)<V$ should have the requested surface. The finite exceptions may depend on $V$, and manifolds are understood up to the usual isometry/homeomorphism classification. This is a supplied expectation, attributed to possible extensions of Cooper--Long and Tao Li, rather than a theorem asserted here.

Short English statement

Does every closed hyperbolic $3$-manifold have an essential closed immersed surface that injects on fundamental groups and has no self-intersections beyond transverse double curves? A bounded-volume finite-exception version is also suggested.
