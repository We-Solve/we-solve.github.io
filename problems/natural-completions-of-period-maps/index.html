Natural Completions of Period Maps

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $B$ be a smooth projective variety, let $Z\subset B$ be a reduced normal-crossing divisor, and set $U=B\setminus Z$. Here reduced means that each component of the divisor has multiplicity one, and the normal-crossing condition means that locally its branches are coordinate hyperplanes. Suppose that a period map \[ \Phi:U\longrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D \] is given, and write $\mathcal P=\Phi(U)$. The notation $D$ denotes the period domain in the supplied statement, and $\Gamma$ denotes the group occurring in its quotient; their precise defining data are not supplied.

Problem. For these data, determine what natural completions of $\Phi$ to maps defined on all of $B$ exist, and determine their properties. Schematically, an extension to a target $\overline{\mathcal P}$ containing $\mathcal P$, with inclusion $j:\mathcal P\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal P}$, would satisfy \[ \overline\Phi:B\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal P}, \qquad \overline\Phi|_U=j\circ\Phi_{\mathcal P}, \] where $\Phi_{\mathcal P}:U\to\mathcal P$ is $\Phi$ with codomain restricted to its image. Thus every point of $Z$ must lie in the domain of the sought extension.

This diagram records only the extension requirement, not a complete definition of the requested completion. The source does not specify the category of the target and maps, the topology or geometric structure on $\mathcal P$, the density requirement, or a criterion making a completion natural. It also does not state the base field or the detailed hypotheses on the period map. Accordingly, the requested classification and properties remain qualified by these unresolved choices; no particular algebraic or topological completion theorem is asserted here.

Short English statement

Given a period map on the complement of a reduced normal-crossing divisor in a smooth projective variety, determine natural ways to extend it over the entire variety by completing its image, and describe the resulting properties. The intended category, period-map hypotheses, and meaning of natural completion require clarification.
