Components of Rational-Curve Mapping Spaces

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question does not specify a ground field. Conditional on choosing a field $k$, let $X$ be a smooth projective $k$-variety and let $\operatorname{Mor}_k(\mathbb P^1_k,X)$ denote the scheme of morphisms from $\mathbb P^1_k$ to $X$. Its functor of points sends a $k$-scheme $T$ to \[ \operatorname{Hom}_T(\mathbb P^1_k\times_k T, X\times_k T). \] For a scheme $Y$, write $\operatorname{Irr}(Y)$ for the set of irreducible components of its underlying topological space.

The supplied problem asks: for a smooth projective variety $X$, determine the number \[ \#\operatorname{Irr}\left(\operatorname{Mor}_k(\mathbb P^1_k,X)\right). \] Here the entire morphism scheme is written: no degree or curve class is fixed, constant morphisms are included, and morphisms are not identified under automorphisms of $\mathbb P^1_k$. These conventions explain the displayed notation; the source record does not establish whether a more restricted space was intended.

The ground field, its characteristic, and whether components are to be counted over $k$ or after extension to an algebraic closure remain unspecified. If geometric components are intended, the expression to count is instead \[ \#\operatorname{Irr}\left(\operatorname{Mor}_{\overline{k}}(\mathbb P^1_{\overline{k}},X_{\overline{k}})\right). \] No finiteness assertion is supplied. The accompanying literature assessment concerns Fano varieties, whereas the stated question concerns all smooth projective varieties; a Fano hypothesis cannot be inferred from the statement. These unresolved qualifications prevent a uniquely specified counting problem.

Short English statement

Given a smooth projective variety $X$, determine how many irreducible components its space of maps from $\mathbb P^1$ has. The supplied statement leaves the ground field and component convention unspecified, and does not restrict the degree or impose a Fano hypothesis.
