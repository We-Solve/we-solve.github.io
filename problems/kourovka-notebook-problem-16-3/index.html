Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.3

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. For $x\in G$, define its conjugacy class and centralizer by \[ x^G=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\},\qquad C_G(x)=\{g\in G:gx=xg\}. \] Thus $|x^G|=[G:C_G(x)]$. Say that $G$ has pairwise distinct conjugacy class sizes if \[ \forall x,y\in G,\qquad x^G\ne y^G\ \Longrightarrow\ |x^G|\ne |y^G|. \] Let $\mathbb S_3$ denote the symmetric group on three letters, and let $F(G)$ denote the Fitting subgroup of $G$, namely its largest nilpotent normal subgroup.

The question attributed to Z. Arad asks whether every finite group $G$ with pairwise distinct conjugacy class sizes satisfies \[ G\cong\mathbb S_3. \] Unresolved boundary condition. The supplied statement does not exclude the trivial group. Pairwise distinctness of conjugacy class sizes is vacuous when there is only one conjugacy class, so whether a nontriviality hypothesis was intended must be clarified. No such hypothesis has been silently inserted here.

The supplied source additionally reports that the proposed conclusion holds when $G$ is solvable, citing J. Zhang, and that $F(G)\ne\{1\}$ is known under the problem's hypotheses, citing Z. Arad, M. Muzychuk, and A. Oliver. These are supplied literature claims, not independently verified assertions; their intended scope is subject to the same unresolved nontriviality qualification.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether a finite group whose distinct conjugacy classes all have different sizes must be the symmetric group on three letters. The source reports a positive result for solvable groups and a nontriviality result for the Fitting subgroup. The omission of a possible assumption that the group is nontrivial requires clarification.
