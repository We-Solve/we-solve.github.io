Math-1120

Chain Independence of Rank Gradient

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely generated residually finite group. Here residually finite means that for every $g\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}$, there is a homomorphism from $\Gamma$ to a finite group whose value at $g$ is not the identity. For a finitely generated group $H$, let $d(H)$ denote the minimum cardinality of a generating set of $H$.

Interpret a normal chain with trivial intersection in the standard sense as a sequence \[ \Gamma\geq\Gamma_1\geq\Gamma_2\geq\cdots, \qquad \Gamma_n\trianglelefteq\Gamma, \qquad [\Gamma:\Gamma_n]<\infty, \qquad \bigcap_{n\geq1}\Gamma_n=\{1\}. \] The finite-index requirement is implicit in the supplied rank-gradient formulation; it is made explicit here. No assumption of strict inclusions, torsion-freeness, or infinitude of $\Gamma$ is imposed. Define \[ \operatorname{RG}(\Gamma,(\Gamma_n)) =\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(\Gamma_n)-1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_n]}. \] For such chains this limit exists by the Schreier index inequality.

Conjecture (chain independence of rank gradient). For every finitely generated residually finite group $\Gamma$ and every two descending chains $(\Gamma_n)_{n\geq1}$ and $(\Lambda_n)_{n\geq1}$ of finite-index subgroups normal in $\Gamma$, each with trivial intersection, one has \[ \operatorname{RG}(\Gamma,(\Gamma_n)) =\operatorname{RG}(\Gamma,(\Lambda_n)). \] This is the conjecture recorded by the supplied source, not a claimed theorem. The source's label that it is open is supplied dated curation, not an independently verified status assessment.

Short English statement

For a finitely generated residually finite group, consider descending chains of finite-index normal subgroups whose intersection is the identity. The conjecture asks whether the limiting number of generators minus one, divided by subgroup index, has the same value for every such chain.
