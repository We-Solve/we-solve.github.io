Some Open Problems in Elasticity — Bifurcation theory

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The requested setting is nonlinear elastostatics with mixed displacement--traction boundary conditions. A representative parameter-dependent equilibrium problem on a reference body $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ is \[ \begin{cases} -\operatorname{Div}P(x,\nabla y;\lambda)=b(x;\lambda)&\text{in }\Omega,\\ y=g(\cdot;\lambda)&\text{on }\Gamma_D,\\ P(x,\nabla y;\lambda)n=t(\cdot;\lambda)&\text{on }\Gamma_N, \end{cases} \] where $y:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^d$ is a deformation, $P$ is the first Piola--Kirchhoff stress, $b$ a body-force density, $t$ a reference traction, $n$ the outward unit normal, and $\Gamma_D,\Gamma_N$ are the displacement and traction portions of the boundary. In a differentiable hyperelastic model one has $P=D_FW(x,F;\lambda)$ for a stored-energy density $W$. These equations explain the terminology; the record does not restrict the task to this particular dependence of loads or stress on the variables, specify $d$, or supply constitutive and regularity hypotheses.

Given a chosen function-space formulation and a known equilibrium branch $(\lambda,y_0(\lambda))$, a local bifurcation point is a branch point approached by equilibria lying off that branch. A local theory would give hypotheses and criteria for the existence and structure of such nearby branches. A global theory would describe their continuation and the associated connected sets of equilibria beyond a neighborhood of a bifurcation point. The source does not propose a particular global alternative or conclusion.

Problem. Develop local and global bifurcation theories for nonlinear elastostatics with mixed displacement--traction boundary conditions. Both types of boundary data must be accommodated together. The body geometry, admissible deformation space, constitutive laws, parameterization of loads, compatibility conditions, and target existence/continuation assertions are not supplied, so the problem is an open research program rather than a fully specified theorem.

The supplied summary reports that local bifurcation theory is well developed in many elastostatic settings, including some mixed-boundary cases, while a fully general local and global theory under realistic hypotheses and general mixed conditions remains open or partial. This is the record's qualified progress assessment; no particular theorem or reference is supplied to delimit those established cases.

Short English statement

Develop criteria and continuation results for branches of nonlinear elastic equilibria when displacement is prescribed on part of the boundary and traction on the rest. The source seeks both local and global theory but leaves the material laws, admissible spaces, and precise conclusions unspecified.
