Noiseless Open Cores and Tame Real-Field Expansions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal R$ be an expansion of the ordered real field $({\mathbb{R}},<,+,\cdot,0,1)$. For this provisional formulation, "definable" means first-order definable with real parameters. The supplied excerpt does not specify its parameter convention, so this convention requires confirmation against the source.

For each integer $n\geq 1$, let $\mathcal O_n(\mathcal R)$ consist of the $\mathcal R$-definable open subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, with its Euclidean topology. Define the open core by \[ \mathcal R^{\circ}=\left({\mathbb{R}},<,(P_U)_{U\in\bigcup_{n\geq1}\mathcal O_n(\mathcal R)}\right), \] where $P_U$ is a predicate interpreted as $U$, with the appropriate arity. A structure on ${\mathbb{R}}$ is called noiseless if every definable subset of every ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, for $n\geq1$, either has nonempty interior or is nowhere dense. Explicitly, for every such set $A$, \[ \operatorname{int}(A)\neq\varnothing \quad\text{or}\quad \operatorname{int}(\overline A)=\varnothing. \] Here closure and interior are taken in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$.

Conjectural assertion reconstructed in the supplied registry. For every expansion $\mathcal R$ of the ordered real field, if ${\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ is not $\mathcal R$-definable, then $\mathcal R^{\circ}$ is noiseless. Equivalently, the proposed conclusion is \[ \forall n\geq1\;\forall A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n\text{ definable in }\mathcal R^{\circ},\qquad \operatorname{int}(A)\neq\varnothing \ \lor\ \operatorname{int}(\overline A)=\varnothing. \] The original excerpt refers only to the open core of "such a structure"; its preceding context and the footnote attached to "open core" are absent. Thus the displayed hypothesis follows the supplied reconstructed statement and is not independently established from the excerpt alone.

Additional research question. Classify expansions of the real field that do not define ${\mathbb{Z}}$, using a common notion of tameness. The record specifies neither this notion nor an equivalence relation, invariant, or desired classification theorem, so this part does not determine a precise mathematical assertion.

Short English statement

The reconstructed conjecture says that an expansion of the real field which cannot define the integers has an open core in which every definable set, in every finite positive dimension, either contains an open set or is nowhere dense. The source also asks for a classification by an unspecified notion of tameness. Missing context and parameter conventions require human review.
