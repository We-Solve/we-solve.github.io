Real Places of Mapping Torus Trace Fields

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns surface types denoted by $( -\chi,p)$, where $\chi$ is the Euler characteristic. The record does not define $p$, specify the class of surfaces, or state the conventions for hyperbolicity and trace fields. Consequently, the following is a conditional formulation under explicitly stated standard conventions; these conventions require confirmation against the cited report.

Suppose that $S_{g,p}$ denotes a connected orientable surface obtained by deleting $p$ points from a closed surface of genus $g$, where $g,p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$. Thus \[ \chi(S_{g,p})=2-2g-p. \] For an orientation-preserving homeomorphism $f:S_{g,p}\to S_{g,p}$, define its mapping torus by \[ M_f=(S_{g,p}\times[0,1])/\left((x,1)\sim(f(x),0)\right). \] In this conditional formulation, a hyperbolic mapping torus means that $M_f$ admits a complete hyperbolic metric of finite volume. For such a metric, let \[ \rho:\pi_1(M_f)\longrightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{C}}) \] be its holonomy representation, let $q:\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{C}})\to\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{C}})$ be the quotient map, and define the ordinary trace field by \[ K(M_f)={\mathbb{Q}}\left(\operatorname{tr}(A):A\in q^{-1}(\rho(\pi_1(M_f)))\right). \] A real place here means a field embedding $K(M_f)\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}$.

Under these conventions, the question is whether \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every }g,p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\text{ with }2g-2+p>1,\\ \text{there exists an orientation-preserving homeomorphism }f:S_{g,p}\to S_{g,p}\\ \text{such that }M_f\text{ admits a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric}\\ \text{and }K(M_f)\text{ admits an embedding into }{\mathbb{R}}. \end{gathered} \] The source quantifies over every surface type $( -\chi,p)$ with $-\chi>1$. Its identification with the genus-and-puncture formulation above is conditional on the unstated conventions. In particular, punctures versus boundary components, orientability, finite volume, and ordinary versus invariant trace field must be checked. The background also refers to excluded cases without listing them; no exclusions beyond the displayed inequality have been added.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every allowed surface type with Euler characteristic less than $-1$ occurs as the fiber of a hyperbolic mapping torus whose trace field embeds into ${\mathbb{R}}$. The explicit formulation uses stated genus, puncture, orientability, hyperbolicity, and trace-field conventions that the supplied record does not fully specify. It is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
