Math-1554

Scalar parameters for divisorial valuation rings

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Flenner's Question 11 asks for one binary first-order ring formula $\phi(x,y)$ such that \[ \forall K\text{ finitely generated}\ \forall v\text{ rank-one divisorial on }K \ \exists a\in K:\quad \mathcal O_v=\{b\in K:K\models\phi(b,a)\}. \] The formula is uniform across fields and valuations; $a$ is one scalar, not a tuple whose length depends on dimension. Finitely generated is understood over the prime base, with the intended arithmetic/geometric divisorial convention requiring specification. No quantifier-complexity bound on $\phi$ is stated.

The aid reports fixed-dimension tuple-parameter and restricted-curve results as substantial weaker progress, but supplies no exact theorem statements or full citations. They do not settle the displayed all-fields one-scalar question.

Its automorphism obstruction is explicit. Let $k$ be a finitely generated field, for example $\mathbb Q$ or a finite field, and let $t,u$ be algebraically independent. In \[ K=k(t,u),\qquad \mathcal O_{v_t}=k[t,u]_{(t)}, \] the divisor valuation has $v_t(t)=1$, $v_t(u)=0$. The field automorphism \[ \sigma(t)=t,\qquad\sigma(u)=tu,\qquad\sigma|_k=\operatorname{id} \] fixes $k(t)$ pointwise but does not preserve $\mathcal O_{v_t}$, since \[ v_t(u/t)=-1,\qquad \sigma(u/t)=u,\qquad v_t(u)=0. \] Any set first-order definable with parameters from $k(t)$ must be fixed by this automorphism. Therefore the aid concludes that $\mathcal O_{v_t}$ is not definable over $k(t)$, even with a finite tuple from that subfield; in particular no scalar $a\in k(t)$, including the uniformizer $t$, can be its sole parameter.

This rules out a natural restricted parameter choice, not every scalar in $K$. It does not exclude parameters involving both independent variables, nor establish failure of the original uniform formula. The exact question remains open in the supplied report.

Short English statement

The source asks for a single ring formula with one scalar parameter that works for every divisorial valuation on every finitely generated field. The aid rules out parameters from a natural smaller subfield in a two-variable example: an automorphism fixes those parameters but moves the valuation ring. This does not rule out other scalar parameters or solve the full uniformity question.
