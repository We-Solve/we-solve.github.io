D. Damanik: Quantum Mechanics and Quasicrystals — Problem

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D={\mathbb{N}}_{\ge0}$ for a one-sided sequence or $D={\mathbb{Z}}$ for a two-sided sequence, and let $s:D\to\mathcal A$. For a finite offset pattern $\tau=(0=\tau_0<\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{k-1})$ of integers, define \[ \mathcal P_s(\tau)=\{(s(n+\tau_0),\ldots,s(n+\tau_{k-1})):n\in D\}, \qquad p_s^*(k)=\sup_\tau|\mathcal P_s(\tau)|. \] The offsets are nonnegative, so every sampled index is in $D$. A sequence is pattern Sturmian in the usual maximal-pattern-complexity sense when \[ \forall k\ge1,\qquad p_s^*(k)=2k. \] In particular exactly two symbols occur, by $k=1$. This uses arbitrary finite offset sets, not just consecutive blocks as in ordinary Sturmian complexity.

The problem is to classify all such $s$ separately for $D={\mathbb{N}}_{\ge0}$ and $D={\mathbb{Z}}$, including whichever recurrent and nonrecurrent sequences occur in either domain. A classification must specify all parameters and exceptional cases of the proposed constructions, and how shift or symbol relabeling is treated if equivalence classes are used.

The supplied research summary asserts a complete resolution as of 2025: recurrent sequences are described by irrational-circle two-interval codings and nearly simple Toeplitz subshifts, while nonrecurrent sequences are described as very close to constant or by nonrecurrent rotation codings. It supplies no precise reference, construction definitions, or parameter restrictions. Recurrent is not synonymous with two-sided, and nonrecurrent is not synonymous with one-sided; the summary's parenthetical identifications do not replace the two classification tasks. Very close to constant is not a mathematical class without a specified sparsity rule. The claimed complete classification is therefore retained as unverified metadata rather than an explicit exhaustive theorem.

Short English statement

Classify one-sided and two-sided sequences whose maximal complexity over every $k$-position pattern is exactly $2k$. The supplied solved assessment lacks a precise classification reference and conflates index direction with recurrence.
