Bilinear Wave–Schrödinger Restriction Without Transversality

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a spatial dimension $d$, write $\Delta_x=\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_{x_j}^2$. A free Schrödinger wave satisfies $(i\partial_t+\Delta_x)u=0$, and a free wave satisfies $(\partial_t^2-\Delta_x)v=0$. These definitions describe the interaction named in the supplied statement; the record does not determine whether the intended wave factor is a full wave or a particular half-wave component.

The supplied problem asks: determine the admissible exponents and the sharp dependence on localization scales in bilinear restriction estimates for wave--Schrödinger interactions after removing the transversality assumption. Here an admissible choice must make the intended bilinear estimate hold uniformly over its prescribed class of inputs, and sharp scale dependence means optimal dependence of its constant on the prescribed localization parameters. The record does not supply that estimate, its input and output norms, the localization parameters, or the transversality condition being removed. Consequently, this is an explicitly incomplete formulation of the requested target, and no particular quantified inequality or exponent range can be asserted from the supplied evidence.

The quoted background concerns a related transference result for free Schrödinger waves in four spatial dimensions, on ${\mathbb{R}}^{1+4}$, extending estimates to an endpoint inhomogeneous Strichartz space $Z$. The displayed norm in the supplied excerpt is corrupted. It contains the identifiable term $\|u\|_{L_t^\infty L_x^2({\mathbb{R}}^{1+4})}$ and a forcing term involving $(i\partial_t+\Delta_x)u$, but the complete forcing norm cannot be recovered unambiguously. Thus $Z$ is not fully defined here, and neither the four-dimensional setting nor this particular solution space is silently imposed on the missing wave--Schrödinger estimate. The excerpt describes a result, rather than stating the requested nontransverse conjecture. The supplied curation connects the question with regularity theory for the Zakharov equation without specifying a corresponding regularity threshold.

Short English statement

The question asks which bilinear wave--Schrödinger estimates survive without transversality, with optimal exponents and scale dependence. The supplied excerpt omits the actual inequality and contains a damaged definition of the endpoint space, so a complete precise conjecture requires further source recovery and human mathematical review. Its open status is supplied curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
