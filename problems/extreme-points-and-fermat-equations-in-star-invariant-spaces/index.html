Extreme Points and Fermat Equations in Star-Invariant Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns two questions about spaces denoted by \[ K^p_\theta=H^p\cap\theta H^p_0, \] where $\theta$ is an inner function. This formula is reproduced exactly as supplied. The underlying Hardy-space domain, the admissible range of $p$, and the definition of $H^p_0$ are not specified.

There is a material notation issue. Under the usual unit-disc convention, $H^p$ denotes a Hardy space of holomorphic functions on $\mathbb D=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$, $H^p_0=\{f\in H^p:f(0)=0\}$, and an inner function is a bounded holomorphic function whose radial boundary values have modulus one almost everywhere on $\partial\mathbb D$. With these conventions, the displayed formula gives $K^p_\theta=\theta H^p_0$. The customary star-invariant-space formula instead uses conjugated boundary functions: \[ H^p\cap\theta\overline{H^p_0}, \qquad \overline{H^p_0}=\{\overline{u}:u\in H^p_0\}, \] with the intersection interpreted through boundary values. These conventions explain the possible discrepancy; they are not a verified correction of the supplied record. The intended definition must be resolved before either question has an unambiguous target.

For any chosen interpretation of $K^\infty_\theta$ and its inherited Hardy-space norm, let \[ B_\theta=\{f\in K^\infty_\theta:\|f\|_\infty\leq1\}. \] A point $f\in B_\theta$ is an extreme point if, whenever $u,v\in B_\theta$ and $0<t<1$ satisfy $f=tu+(1-t)v$, one has $u=v=f$. The first question is to characterize all extreme points of $B_\theta$ for an arbitrary inner function $\theta$.

The second question concerns solutions of \[ f^n+g^n=h^n, \qquad f,g,h\in K^p_\theta,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad n\geq3, \] where the equality is an identity of the represented functions. The source exempts triples spanning a one-dimensional subspace. Under the interpretation that the question asks whether every solution is proportional, its proposed assertion is \[ \forall f,g,h\in K^p_\theta,\qquad f^n+g^n=h^n \ \Longrightarrow\ \dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{f,g,h\}\leq1, \] for every inner $\theta$, every admissible $p$, and every integer $n\geq3$. Equivalently, the question asks whether a solution spanning a subspace of dimension at least two can exist. This is a conditional rendering of the apparent nonproportional-solution question: the record does not explicitly state the nonexistence assertion, specify whether individual functions must be nonzero, or address the zero-dimensional span of the all-zero triple. No range of $p$ is supplied, and membership of the powers in $K^p_\theta$ is not asserted.

Short English statement

The record asks for a description of the extreme points of the unit ball of $K^\infty_\theta$, and whether $f^n+g^n=h^n$, for integers $n\geq3$, can have solutions in $K^p_\theta$ that are not all proportional. The space definition may be missing complex conjugation; the Hardy-space domain, range of $p$, and treatment of zero functions also require clarification. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
