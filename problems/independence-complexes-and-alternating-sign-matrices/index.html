Independence Complexes and Alternating-Sign Matrices

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a graph $G=(V,E)$, its independence complex $\operatorname{Ind}(G)$ is the abstract simplicial complex whose simplices are the finite independent subsets of $V$, meaning subsets containing no edge of $G$. The source does not specify finiteness or graph conventions, nor the coefficient ring or whether cohomology is reduced.

Edge-replacement problem. Let $G$ be a graph for which $\operatorname{Ind}(G)$ has, in the source's words, "exactly one cohomology generator," and let $e$ be an edge of $G$. For an edge with distinct endpoints $u,v$, replacing $e$ by a path of $3k+1$ edges means deleting $e$, introducing $3k$ new internal vertices, and joining $u$ to $v$ through those vertices, while retaining the other vertices and edges. Denote the resulting graph by $G_k$. This construction makes sense for integers $k\geq 0$; the source does not specify whether the requested family includes $k=0$.

For each admissible pair $(G,e)$, find a closed formula and a natural generating function, as $k$ varies, for the quantity described in the source as "the largest weight of the cohomology representatives" of $\operatorname{Ind}(G_k)$. The supplied record does not define weight, the allowed representatives, their normalization, or the maximization being requested. Moreover, "exactly one cohomology generator" does not specify a cohomology theory, degree, or coefficient structure. Consequently, this is a qualified statement of the requested target, not a definition of a numerical invariant; those missing data must be supplied before a precise formula or generating function can be sought.

Alternating-sign-matrix realization problem. An alternating-sign matrix of order $n\geq 1$ is an $n\times n$ matrix with entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$, with each row and column summing to $1$, and with the nonzero entries in every row and column alternating in sign. Find independence complexes realizing the symmetry classes or versions of alternating-sign matrices intended by the source's reference to Kuperberg. The supplied record neither enumerates these classes nor defines "realizing": it does not specify a bijection, an enumerative identity, or a cohomological construction. These choices remain unresolved and must not be inferred from the word "realizing."

Short English statement

The first question asks how a specified cohomological weight changes when an edge is replaced by a path of $3k+1$ edges. The second asks for independence complexes corresponding to Kuperberg's alternating-sign-matrix symmetry classes. The supplied record leaves the weight, cohomology conventions, and intended correspondence undefined, so both targets require clarification before a fully precise formulation is possible.
