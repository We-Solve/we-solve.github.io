Math-4438

Large-Deviation Asymptotics for Triangle Counts

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 3$ be an integer and $p\in[0,1]$. The binomial random graph $G_{n,p}$ has vertex set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with each unordered pair of distinct vertices present as an edge independently with probability $p$. Its triangle count is \[ X=X_{n,p}=\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\{i,j\},\{i,k\},\{j,k\}\in E(G_{n,p})\}}, \qquad \mu_{n,p}=\mathbb{E}X_{n,p}=\binom{n}{3}p^3. \] For a real deviation parameter $\delta$, define the logarithmic lower-tail quantity \[ L_n(p,\delta)=-\log\Pr\!\left(X_{n,p}\leq(1-\delta)\mu_{n,p}\right), \] where $\log$ is the natural logarithm and $-\log 0=+\infty$. Allowing real $\delta$ here defines the expression; it does not resolve the intended deviation range of the source problem.

The supplied corrected statement asks for the asymptotic behavior of $L_n(p_n,\delta)$ as $n\to\infty$ throughout the remaining unresolved sparse regimes, particularly \[ p_n=O(n^{-1/2}). \] The original statement explicitly includes sparse sequences $p_n\to0$. The source does not specify the range of $\delta$, whether it is fixed or may vary with $n$, the precise collection of unresolved parameter regimes, or whether the requested asymptotics mean an asymptotic equivalent, a limiting normalized rate, or matching bounds. Thus this is an asymptotic determination problem whose full quantifiers require clarification; no particular normalization or universal asymptotic formula is asserted here.

The original supplied statement also asks for the upper-tail quantity \[ U_n(p,\delta)=-\log\Pr\!\left(X_{n,p}\geq(1+\delta)\mu_{n,p}\right). \] The corrected statement retains only the lower-tail target. This difference in scope remains unresolved: the upper-tail request is preserved as source history, without asserting that it remains part of the intended current problem. The source also supplies no boundary exclusions or separate treatment of $p=0$, $p=1$, or deviation endpoints.

Short English statement

Determine the leading logarithmic probability that a binomial random graph has fewer triangles than its expectation by the prescribed relative deviation, especially when $p=O(n^{-1/2})$. The deviation range and the required precision of the asymptotics are unspecified. The original record also requests upper-tail asymptotics, whereas the corrected statement requests only lower tails.
