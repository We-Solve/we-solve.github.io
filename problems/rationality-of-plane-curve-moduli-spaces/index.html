Rationality of Plane-Curve Moduli Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a nonnegative integer degree $d$, let \[ V_d=\left(\operatorname{Sym}^d({\mathbb{C}}^3)\right)^*, \] the vector space of homogeneous degree-$d$ polynomial forms on ${\mathbb{C}}^3$. Its projectivization ${\mathbb{P}}(V_d)$ parametrizes nonzero forms up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar. A form $f$ defines the plane curve $\{f=0\}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^2_{{\mathbb{C}}}$. The group $G=SL_3({\mathbb{C}})$ acts by change of coordinates, with $(g\cdot f)(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$.

The supplied question asks whether \[ M_d={\mathbb{P}}(V_d)/SL_3({\mathbb{C}}) \] is rational for each of the fifteen degrees for which the source reports rationality as unknown. The record does not give those fifteen degrees. Write $E$ for that unspecified source-dependent set, with $|E|=15$; this notation does not identify its members or assert that precisely fifteen degrees remain unresolved today.

If the displayed quotient is intended as a rational quotient, its rational function field is \[ K_d={\mathbb{C}}\left({\mathbb{P}}(V_d)\right)^{SL_3({\mathbb{C}})}. \] Under this interpretation, the requested statement is \[ \forall d\in E\quad \exists n_d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\quad K_d\cong_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{C}}(t_1,\ldots,t_{n_d}), \] where the $t_i$ are algebraically independent over ${\mathbb{C}}$. Equivalently, each corresponding rational quotient is birational over ${\mathbb{C}}$ to a projective space. Here rationality means rationality itself, rather than stable rationality.

The supplied record does not specify the quotient construction or whether a smooth, irreducible, or other restricted locus of plane curves is intended. Thus the invariant-field formulation above is an explicit conditional interpretation of the displayed quotient. Identification of $E$ and confirmation of the intended quotient and curve locus are required to obtain a fully specified statement.

Short English statement

The source asks whether the moduli spaces of complex plane curves are rational in fifteen exceptional degrees. Those degrees are not listed. If the quotient means a rational quotient, the question asks whether its invariant function field is purely transcendental over ${\mathbb{C}}$ for every degree in that missing list. The quotient convention and intended curve locus also require confirmation; the supplied open status is a dated curation claim.
