Math-6572

Harmonic Test Inequalities Above the First Eigenvalue

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^m$ be a bounded, sufficiently smooth domain. For a standard spectral interpretation of the supplied statement, let $-\Delta_D$ denote the positive Dirichlet Laplacian on $\Omega$, and write its eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, as \[ 0<\lambda_1(\Omega)\leq\lambda_2(\Omega)\leq\cdots. \] Choose an orthonormal basis $(u_j)_{j\geq1}$ of $L^2(\Omega)$ satisfying \[ -\Delta u_j=\lambda_j u_j\quad\text{in }\Omega, \qquad u_j|_{\partial\Omega}=0. \] Here $\langle h,u_j\rangle=\int_\Omega h\,\overline{u_j}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x$. Interpret an admissible harmonic test function as $h\in L^2(\Omega)$ with $\Delta h=0$ in the distributional sense. No boundary condition on $h$ is supplied. The normalization, integrability, and harmonicity conventions above make the expression meaningful, but are not explicitly specified in the source record.

For $\lambda\notin\{\lambda_j:j\geq1\}$, define \[ Q_\lambda(h)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_j-\lambda} \left|\langle h,u_j\rangle\right|^2. \] Under these conventions this series is absolutely convergent. The well-defined, nonresonant portion of the supplied question is: for every such $\Omega$ and every \[ \lambda\in(\lambda_1(\Omega),\infty) \setminus\{\lambda_j:j\geq1\}, \] does there exist a harmonic $h\in L^2(\Omega)$ such that $Q_\lambda(h)<0$?

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied statement actually quantifies over every $\lambda>\lambda_1(\Omega)$, including Dirichlet eigenvalues. At those parameters its displayed fractions are undefined, and no limiting convention, spectral projection, or other interpretation is supplied. Thus the nonresonant formulation above covers only the meaningful portion of that quantifier and does not resolve the full stated question. The admissible dimensions and the meaning of sufficiently smooth are also unspecified. The supplied background reports a one-dimensional failure and refers to a higher-dimensional setting, without explicitly stating the intended dimension restriction.

Short English statement

For each admissible domain and each parameter above its first Dirichlet eigenvalue, the question asks whether a square-integrable harmonic function can make the displayed weighted sum of squared eigenfunction coefficients negative. The sum has a standard interpretation away from the Dirichlet spectrum. Its interpretation at eigenvalues, the intended dimensions, and the precise regularity assumptions remain unresolved. The supplied open label is dated curation, not an independently verified status.
