Statement

For $c\in[0,1]$, define \[ S_c:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}: \varphi(n)<cn\},\qquad f(c):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{X}|S_c\cap [1,X]|, \] where existence of the limit is given. Question: Is it true that for every $x\in(0,1)$, either $f'(x)$ does not exist, or it exists but equals $0$? Equivalently: is there no $x$ for which the (classical) derivative exists and is strictly positive?

Problem 50: derivatives of the totient distribution function (Round 8)

1) ROUND-8 OBJECTIVE

Path (C) (obstruction / strategy barrier). Round 7 produced a dense $G_\delta$ set \[ \Omega\subset (0,1)\setminus\mathcal E, \qquad \mathcal E:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}:n\ge 1\right\}, \] on which all four one-sided Dini derivatives are maximally oscillatory: \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty \qquad(x\in\Omega). \] This still left one gap in the obstruction picture: Round 7 only asserted that one-sided secant slopes become arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large. It did not yet identify the full set of intermediate slopes that actually occur.

The new objective is therefore to strengthen Round 7 from mere oscillation to exact slope realization. We prove that on the same residual set $\Omega$, every positive slope occurs exactly on both sides at arbitrarily small scales. Equivalently, the finite one-sided secant spectrum at every $x\in\Omega$ is all of $[0,\infty)$.

This is a genuine strengthening of Round 7: any hypothetical point where $G'(x)$ exists and is a finite positive number must avoid not merely a residual set of large/small oscillation, but a residual set on which every positive local secant slope is realized infinitely often on both sides.

2) Round-7 FOUNDATION USED

We use the following earlier-round results as black boxes.

  • Round 1. The limiting distribution function \[ G(u):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\#\left\{n\le X:\frac{\varphi(n)}n\le u\right\} \qquad(0\le u\le 1) \] exists, is continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,1]$, and the associated measure $\mu=dG$ is purely singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. In particular, \[ G'(x)=0\qquad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }x\in(0,1). \]

  • Round 3. The value set \[ \mathcal E:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}:n\ge 1\right\} \] is countable and dense in $[0,1]$, and every $t\in\mathcal E$ has infinite left derivative: \[ \lim_{h\to 0^+}\frac{G(t)-G(t-h)}{h}=+\infty. \]

  • Round 7 main theorem. There exists a dense $G_\delta$ set \[ \Omega\subset (0,1)\setminus\mathcal E \] such that for every $x\in\Omega$ one has \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty. \] Here \[ \underline D_-G(x):=\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \qquad \overline D_-G(x):=\limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \] \[ \underline D_+G(x):=\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}, \qquad \overline D_+G(x):=\limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}. \]

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-8)

The new observation is very simple but powerful:

For fixed $x$, the one-sided secant quotients are continuous as functions of the scale $h$.

Once Round 7 gives "values below $c$" and "values above $c$" arbitrarily close to $h=0$, continuity in $h$ forces an exact solution of the equation "secant slope $=c$" at an intermediate scale. Doing this for every $c>0$ upgrades one-sided Dini oscillation to a complete local secant-spectrum theorem.

Concretely, we will prove:

For every $x\in\Omega$, every $c>0$, and every sufficiently small radius $\delta>0$, there exist $h_-,h_+\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ \frac{G(x)-G(x-h_-)}{h_-}=c, \qquad \frac{G(x+h_+)-G(x)}{h_+}=c. \]

Thus every positive slope is attained exactly, on both sides, at arbitrarily small scales around every $x\in\Omega$.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-8)

The exact Round 7 gap is that "liminf $=0$ and limsup $=\infty$" does not by itself state what happens at intermediate values. We close that gap as follows.

  1. Fix $x\in\Omega$ and $c>0$.
  2. Use Round 7 to find arbitrarily small scales where the left quotient is below $c$ and also arbitrarily small scales where it is above $c$.
  3. Since the map $h\mapsto [G(x)-G(x-h)]/h$ is continuous on $(0,x)$, the intermediate value theorem gives a smaller scale with quotient exactly $c$.
  4. Repeat on the right.
  5. Package the result into a local "secant spectrum" theorem and derive an interval corollary: every nonempty open interval contains a point $x\notin\mathcal E$ at which any prescribed positive slope is realized on both sides, hence also by the whole secant through those two points.

This rules out a new class of strategies: one cannot hope to locate a point of finite positive derivative by first excluding a particular local slope $c>0$ from nearby one-sided secants.

5) WORK (ROUND-8)

5.1. One-sided secant quotients as functions of the scale

For $x\in(0,1)$ and $0<h<\min\{x,1-x\}$ define \[ Q_h^-(x):=\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \qquad Q_h^+(x):=\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}. \] Since $G$ is continuous, for each fixed $x$ the functions \[ h\longmapsto Q_h^-(x)\qquad(0<h<x), \qquad h\longmapsto Q_h^+(x)\qquad(0<h<1-x) \] are continuous.

For $x\in(0,1)$ define the finite one-sided secant spectra \[ \Sigma_-(x):=\left\{L\in[0,\infty):\exists h_n\to0^+\text{ with }Q_{h_n}^-(x)\to L\right\}, \] \[ \Sigma_+(x):=\left\{L\in[0,\infty):\exists h_n\to0^+\text{ with }Q_{h_n}^+(x)\to L\right\}. \]

5.2. Exact realization of every positive slope on the residual set

Theorem (Exact one-sided secant-spectrum on the Round 7 residual set): Let $\Omega\subset(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$ be the dense $G_\delta$ set furnished by Round 7, so that for every $x\in\Omega$, \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty. \] Then for every $x\in\Omega$, every $c>0$, and every \[ 0<\delta<\min\{x,1-x\}, \] there exist $h_-,h_+\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ Q_{h_-}^-(x)=\frac{G(x)-G(x-h_-)}{h_-}=c, \qquad Q_{h_+}^+(x)=\frac{G(x+h_+)-G(x)}{h_+}=c. \] Equivalently, for every such $x$ and $\delta$, \[ (0,\infty)\subseteq\{Q_h^-(x):0<h<\delta\} \qquad\text{and}\qquad (0,\infty)\subseteq\{Q_h^+(x):0<h<\delta\}. \] In particular, for every $x\in\Omega$, \[ \Sigma_-(x)=\Sigma_+(x)=[0,\infty). \]
Proof: Fix $x\in\Omega$, $c>0$, and $0<\delta<\min\{x,1-x\}$.

We first treat the left side. Since $\underline D_-G(x)=0$, there exists $a\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ Q_a^-(x)<c. \] Since $\overline D_-G(x)=+\infty$, there exists $b\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ Q_b^-(x)>c. \] Let \[ u:=\min\{a,b\},\qquad v:=\max\{a,b\}. \] Then $0<u\le v<\delta$, and the continuous function $h\mapsto Q_h^-(x)$ on $[u,v]$ takes one value below $c$ and one value above $c$. By the intermediate value theorem, there exists $h_-\in[u,v]\subset(0,\delta)$ such that \[ Q_{h_-}^-(x)=c. \]

The right side is identical. Since $\underline D_+G(x)=0$, choose $a'\in(0,\delta)$ with \[ Q_{a'}^+(x)<c, \] and since $\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty$, choose $b'\in(0,\delta)$ with \[ Q_{b'}^+(x)>c. \] Applying the intermediate value theorem to the continuous map $h\mapsto Q_h^+(x)$ on $[\min\{a',b'\},\max\{a',b'\}]$ yields $h_+\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ Q_{h_+}^+(x)=c. \]

This proves the exact slope realization statement.

To identify the finite secant spectra, first note that for any $c>0$ and each integer $m\ge1$, applying the argument above with \[ \delta_m:=\min\left\{\frac1m,\frac{x}{2},\frac{1-x}{2}\right\} \] produces $h_{-,m},h_{+,m}\in(0,\delta_m)$ with \[ Q_{h_{-,m}}^-(x)=c, \qquad Q_{h_{+,m}}^+(x)=c. \] Hence $h_{\pm,m}\to0^+$ and so $c\in\Sigma_-(x)\cap\Sigma_+(x)$.

Next, because $\underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0$, there exist sequences $u_m\to0^+$ and $v_m\to0^+$ with \[ Q_{u_m}^-(x)\to0, \qquad Q_{v_m}^+(x)\to0, \] so $0\in\Sigma_-(x)\cap\Sigma_+(x)$.

Finally, by definition every element of $\Sigma_\pm(x)$ is nonnegative, so \[ \Sigma_-(x)=\Sigma_+(x)=[0,\infty). \] ∎

5.3. Interval realization of any prescribed positive slope

Corollary (Every interval contains exact secants of every positive slope): Let $I\subset(0,1)$ be a nonempty open interval and let $c>0$. Then there exist points \[ a<x<b, \qquad x\in I\setminus\mathcal E, \qquad [a,b]\subset I, \] such that \[ \frac{G(x)-G(a)}{x-a}=c, \qquad \frac{G(b)-G(x)}{b-x}=c. \] Consequently, \[ \frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=c. \] Thus every nonempty open interval contains a secant of exact slope $c$.
Proof: Because $\Omega$ is dense in $(0,1)$ and $\Omega\subset(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$, choose \[ x\in\Omega\cap I. \] Set \[ \delta:=\tfrac12\min\{x-\inf I,\sup I-x\}>0. \] Then $0<\delta<\min\{x,1-x\}$ and \[ [x-\delta,x+\delta]\subset I. \] Applying Theorem Theorem (Exact one-sided secant-spectrum on the Round 7 residual set) with this $x$, the given $c$, and this $\delta$, we obtain $h_-,h_+\in(0,\delta)$ such that \[ \frac{G(x)-G(x-h_-)}{h_-}=c, \qquad \frac{G(x+h_+)-G(x)}{h_+}=c. \] Define \[ a:=x-h_-,\qquad b:=x+h_+. \] Then $a<x<b$ and $[a,b]\subset I$. Moreover, \[ \frac{G(x)-G(a)}{x-a}=c, \qquad \frac{G(b)-G(x)}{b-x}=c. \] Adding the two identities gives \[ G(b)-G(a)=[G(b)-G(x)]+[G(x)-G(a)] =c(b-x)+c(x-a)=c(b-a), \] which is exactly \[ \frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=c. \] ∎

5.4. A sharper formulation of the obstruction

Theorem Theorem (Exact one-sided secant-spectrum on the Round 7 residual set) says that on a dense $G_\delta$ set disjoint from $\mathcal E$, the finite one-sided secant spectrum is already maximal: \[ \Sigma_-(x)=\Sigma_+(x)=[0,\infty). \] So any point $x$ where $G'(x)$ exists and is a finite positive number must avoid a residual set on which every positive local slope occurs infinitely often on each side.

This rules out another class of proof strategies. In particular, no argument can hope to prove the existence of a positive derivative by first isolating a neighborhood (or a large topological set of points) where some fixed local slope $c>0$ is forbidden for nearby one-sided secants: Round 8 shows that every such slope occurs everywhere on a residual set.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Does continuity in the scale $h$ really hold? Yes. For fixed $x$, the maps \[ h\mapsto \frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \qquad h\mapsto \frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h} \] are quotients of continuous functions by the nonzero continuous function $h$, hence continuous on their natural domains.

  • Do we truly get values both below and above $c$ inside every small interval $(0,\delta)$? Yes. The identities \[ \underline D_\pm G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_\pm G(x)=+\infty \] mean exactly that for every $\delta>0$ there are scales smaller than $\delta$ where the quotient is below $c$, and also scales smaller than $\delta$ where it is above $c$.

  • What about the slope $c=0$? Exact equality $Q_h^\pm(x)=0$ is impossible for $h>0$ because $G$ is strictly increasing. Our theorem therefore states exact realization only for $c>0$. The value $0$ enters the spectrum as a limit point, which is why \[ \Sigma_\pm(x)=[0,\infty) \] rather than $(0,\infty)$.

  • Why does the whole secant $[a,b]$ have slope $c$ in Corollary Corollary (Every interval contains exact secants of every positive slope)? Because the left and right increments from the midpoint $x$ each have slope $c$, and these increments add: \[ G(b)-G(a)=[G(b)-G(x)]+[G(x)-G(a)]=c(b-x)+c(x-a)=c(b-a). \]

  • Does this really go beyond Round 7? Yes. Round 7 gave only lower/upper Dini information. Round 8 identifies the full finite one-sided secant spectrum on a residual set and proves exact realization of every positive slope on both sides at arbitrarily small scales.

7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 8 strengthens Round 7 from one-sided oscillation to a full local secant-spectrum theorem. There exists a dense $G_\delta$ set \[ \Omega\subset(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E \] such that for every $x\in\Omega$ and every $c>0$, the slope $c$ is realized exactly by arbitrarily small left secants and also by arbitrarily small right secants based at $x$. Equivalently, \[ \Sigma_-(x)=\Sigma_+(x)=[0,\infty) \qquad(x\in\Omega). \]

As a consequence, every nonempty open interval contains secants of every positive slope, and even contains points $x\notin\mathcal E$ from which a prescribed slope $c$ is realized on both sides simultaneously.

This is a strict advance beyond Round 7 and rules out another family of proof strategies. Any hypothetical point where $G'(x)$ exists and is a finite positive number must now avoid a residual set on which all positive one-sided local slopes occur infinitely often.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-7)

The new tool is a systematic passage from the symmetric quotient to the four one-sided Dini derivatives. For $x\in(0,1)$ define \[ \underline D_-G(x):=\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \qquad \overline D_-G(x):=\limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}, \] \[ \underline D_+G(x):=\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}, \qquad \overline D_+G(x):=\limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}. \]

The genuinely new theorem of this round is:

There exists a dense $G_\delta$ set $\Omega\subset(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$ such that for every $x\in\Omega$, \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty. \]

This is strictly stronger than Round 6, which only gave the corresponding statement for the symmetric quotient.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-7)

After Round 6, the exact gap was: generic symmetric oscillation does not force one-sided oscillation on each side separately. To close that gap we prove four statements.

  1. The sets \[ A_-:=\{x:\underline D_-G(x)=0\}, \qquad A_+:=\{x:\underline D_+G(x)=0\} \] are both dense $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$, and in fact have full Lebesgue measure.

  2. The sets \[ B_-:=\{x:\overline D_-G(x)=+\infty\}, \qquad B_+:=\{x:\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty\} \] are both dense $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$.

  3. Therefore \[ \Omega_0:=A_-\cap A_+\cap B_-\cap B_+ \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$.

This overcomes the main Round 6 limitation: it shows that maximal oscillation occurs on both sides separately, not merely after symmetrization.

5) WORK (ROUND-7)

5.1. One-sided quotients and a $G_\delta$ description

For $0<h<1$ define the one-sided secant quotients \[ Q_h^-(x):=\frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}\qquad(h<x<1), \] \[ Q_h^+(x):=\frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}\qquad(0<x<1-h). \] For fixed $h$, the functions $x\mapsto Q_h^-(x)$ and $x\mapsto Q_h^+(x)$ are continuous on their natural domains, since $G$ is continuous. For fixed $x$, the maps $h\mapsto Q_h^-(x)$ and $h\mapsto Q_h^+(x)$ are also continuous for $0<h<\min\{x,1-x\}$.

For integers $N,m\ge1$ define \[ V^-_{N,m}:=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)}\left\{x\in(q,1):Q_q^-(x)<1/N\right\}, \] \[ V^+_{N,m}:=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)}\left\{x\in(0,1-q):Q_q^+(x)<1/N\right\}, \] \[ U^-_{N,m}:=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)}\left\{x\in(q,1):Q_q^-(x)>N\right\}, \] \[ U^+_{N,m}:=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)}\left\{x\in(0,1-q):Q_q^+(x)>N\right\}. \] Each of these sets is open in $(0,1)$.

Lemma: With the notation above, \[ A_-:=\{x\in(0,1):\underline D_-G(x)=0\}=\bigcap_{N=1}^\infty\bigcap_{m=1}^\infty V^-_{N,m}, \] \[ A_+:=\{x\in(0,1):\underline D_+G(x)=0\}=\bigcap_{N=1}^\infty\bigcap_{m=1}^\infty V^+_{N,m}, \] \[ B_-:=\{x\in(0,1):\overline D_-G(x)=+\infty\}=\bigcap_{N=1}^\infty\bigcap_{m=1}^\infty U^-_{N,m}, \] \[ B_+:=\{x\in(0,1):\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty\}=\bigcap_{N=1}^\infty\bigcap_{m=1}^\infty U^+_{N,m}. \] In particular, all four sets $A_-,A_+,B_-,B_+$ are $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$.
Proof: We prove the identity for $A_-$; the other three are identical.

Suppose first that $x\in A_-$. Then for every $N,m$ there exists $h\in(0,1/m)$ such that $Q_h^-(x)<1/N$. By continuity of $h\mapsto Q_h^-(x)$, this strict inequality persists for all $h'$ in a neighborhood of $h$. Choose a rational $q$ in that neighborhood with $0<q<1/m$. Then $Q_q^-(x)<1/N$, so $x\in V^-_{N,m}$. Since $N,m$ were arbitrary, $x\in\bigcap_{N,m}V^-_{N,m}$.

Conversely, if $x\in\bigcap_{N,m}V^-_{N,m}$, then for every $N,m$ there exists a rational $q\in(0,1/m)$ with $Q_q^-(x)<1/N$. Hence for every $N$ and every neighborhood of $0$ there exists $h$ in that neighborhood such that $Q_h^-(x)<1/N$. This is exactly the statement that $\liminf_{h\to0^+}Q_h^-(x)=0$, i.e. $x\in A_-$. ∎

5.2. Residual and full-measure flatness on both sides

Theorem (One-sided lower Dini derivatives vanish generically): The sets $A_-$ and $A_+$ are dense $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$. Moreover, both have full Lebesgue measure.
Proof: By Lemma Lemma, both sets are $G_\delta$. We next prove density.

Let $x\in A_{\rm sym}$, where $A_{\rm sym}$ is the dense $G_\delta$ set from Round 6. Then there exists a sequence $h_n\to0^+$ such that \[ \frac{G(x+h_n)-G(x-h_n)}{2h_n}\to0. \] But \[ \frac{G(x+h_n)-G(x-h_n)}{2h_n}=\frac{Q_{h_n}^-(x)+Q_{h_n}^+(x)}{2}, \] and both $Q_{h_n}^-(x)$ and $Q_{h_n}^+(x)$ are nonnegative because $G$ is increasing. Therefore \[ Q_{h_n}^-(x)\to0, \qquad Q_{h_n}^+(x)\to0. \] So $x\in A_-\cap A_+$. Hence \[ A_{\rm sym}\subseteq A_-\cap A_+. \] Since $A_{\rm sym}$ is dense, both $A_-$ and $A_+$ are dense.

For the full-measure statement, Round 1 gives $G'(x)=0$ for Lebesgue-a.e. $x\in(0,1)$. At every such point, both one-sided difference quotients converge to the same derivative $0$. Hence $\underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0$ for Lebesgue-a.e. $x$, so both $A_-$ and $A_+$ have full Lebesgue measure. ∎

5.3. Residual blow-up on both sides

Theorem (One-sided upper Dini derivatives blow up on a residual set): The sets $B_-$ and $B_+$ are dense $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$.
Proof: By Lemma Lemma, both sets are $G_\delta$.

We first prove that $B_-$ is dense. By Round 3, every $t\in\mathcal E$ satisfies \[ \lim_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(t)-G(t-h)}{h}=+\infty, \] so $\mathcal E\subseteq B_-$. Since $\mathcal E$ is dense in $(0,1)$, the set $B_-$ is dense.

We now prove that $B_+$ is dense. Fix a nonempty open interval $I=(a,b)\subset(0,1)$ and integers $N,m\ge1$. Set \[ \delta:=\frac{b-a}{3}>0. \] Because $\mathcal E$ is dense, choose \[ t\in\mathcal E\cap(a+\delta,b-\delta). \] Again using Round 3, the left derivative at $t$ is $+\infty$, so there exists \[ 0<h<\min\{1/m,\delta/2\} \] with \[ Q_h^-(t)=\frac{G(t)-G(t-h)}{h}>N. \] By continuity of $q\mapsto Q_q^-(t)$, the inequality $Q_q^-(t)>N$ also holds for all $q$ in some neighborhood of $h$. Choose a rational \[ q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m) \] in that neighborhood; in particular $q<\delta$. Set \[ x:=t-q. \] Then \[ a<t-\delta<x<t<b, \] so $x\in I$, and \[ Q_q^+(x)=\frac{G(x+q)-G(x)}{q}=\frac{G(t)-G(t-q)}{q}=Q_q^-(t)>N. \] Hence $x\in I\cap U^+_{N,m}$. Since $I,N,m$ were arbitrary, every $U^+_{N,m}$ is dense; therefore \[ B_+=\bigcap_{N,m}U^+_{N,m} \] is dense. ∎

5.4. Main Round-7 theorem: residual maximal one-sided oscillation

Theorem (Residual one-sided oscillation outside $\mathcal E$): The set \[ \Omega_0:=A_-\cap A_+\cap B_-\cap B_+ \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$. Consequently, the set \[ \Omega:=\Omega_0\setminus\mathcal E \] is also a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$. For every $x\in\Omega$ one has \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty. \] In particular, no one-sided derivative of $G$ exists finitely at any point of $\Omega$, and therefore $G$ is not differentiable at any point of $\Omega$.
Proof: By Theorem Theorem (One-sided lower Dini derivatives vanish generically), the sets $A_-$ and $A_+$ are dense $G_\delta$. By Theorem Theorem (One-sided upper Dini derivatives blow up on a residual set), the sets $B_-$ and $B_+$ are dense $G_\delta$. Therefore their intersection $\Omega_0$ is dense $G_\delta$.

Since $\mathcal E$ is countable, its complement $(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$ is also a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$. Thus \[ \Omega=\Omega_0\cap\left((0,1)\setminus\mathcal E\right) \] is dense $G_\delta$ as well. The displayed identities for the four Dini derivatives are exactly the defining properties of $A_-,A_+,B_-,B_+$. If $G'(x)$ existed as a finite real number at some $x\in\Omega$, then all four one-sided Dini derivatives would equal that finite value, contradiction. ∎

Corollary (Sharper obstruction to a finite positive derivative): Let \[ D_+:=\{x\in(0,1): G'(x)\text{ exists and }0<G'(x)<\infty\}. \] Then \[ D_+\subseteq (0,1)\setminus\Omega. \] In particular, $D_+$ is meager, disjoint from $\mathcal E$, contained in a Lebesgue-null set, and disjoint from a dense $G_\delta$ set on which all four one-sided Dini derivatives are maximally oscillatory.
Proof: The inclusion $D_+\subseteq(0,1)\setminus\Omega$ follows immediately from Theorem thm:main-round7. The meagerness follows because $\Omega$ is dense $G_\delta$. Disjointness from $\mathcal E$ was already proved in Round 3. Finally, by Round 1 we have $G'(x)=0$ for Lebesgue-a.e. $x$, so $D_+$ is contained in a Lebesgue-null set. ∎

5.5. What proof strategies are ruled out?

Round 6 ruled out interval-wise regularity arguments based on the symmetric quotient. Theorem thm:main-round7 is stronger: on a residual set outside $\mathcal E$, each side separately exhibits arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large secant slopes. Thus any approach that first tries to obtain meaningful one-sided slope control on a nontrivial topological set cannot succeed. In particular, no strategy that seeks differentiability by proving either \[ \frac{G(x)-G(x-h)}{h}\asymp 1 \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{G(x+h)-G(x)}{h}\asymp 1 \] on a dense or residual set can be viable.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Could symmetric flatness fail to imply one-sided flatness? No. If \[ \frac{Q_h^-(x)+Q_h^+(x)}{2}\to0 \] along a sequence and both terms are nonnegative, then each term must itself tend to $0$ along that sequence. This is exactly why $A_{\rm sym}\subseteq A_-\cap A_+$.

  • Is the $G_\delta$ description correct? Yes. The only subtle point is replacing an arbitrary scale $h$ by a rational scale $q$. For fixed $x$, the maps $h\mapsto Q_h^\pm(x)$ are continuous on their natural domains, so strict inequalities survive under small perturbations of $h$.

  • Does the proof of density of $B_+$ really use only established results? Yes. The proof uses only Round 3's fact that every $t\in\mathcal E$ has infinite left derivative, together with density of $\mathcal E$ and continuity in the step-size parameter. No new external theorem is invoked.

  • Could $\Omega$ accidentally intersect $\mathcal E$? By construction it does not: we explicitly remove $\mathcal E$ at the final step. This is important, because points of $\mathcal E$ already have a different certified pathology from Round 3.

  • Endpoints. All one-sided quotients are considered only for $x\in(0,1)$ and sufficiently small $h$, so there is no boundary ambiguity.

7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

There exists a dense $G_\delta$ set $\Omega\subset(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$ such that for every $x\in\Omega$, \[ \underline D_-G(x)=\underline D_+G(x)=0, \qquad \overline D_-G(x)=\overline D_+G(x)=+\infty. \] So, outside the special value set $\mathcal E$, there is still a residual set of points with maximal one-sided irregularity.

1) ROUND-6 OBJECTIVE

Path (C) (obstruction / strategy barrier). Round 5 proved that the set \[ B:=\left\{x\in(0,1):\limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=+\infty\right\} \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$, and that $G$ is nowhere locally Hölder of any positive order. 2) Round-5 FOUNDATION USED

We use the following earlier-round results as black boxes.

The limiting distribution function \[ G(u):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\#\left\{n\le X:\frac{\varphi(n)}n\le u\right\} \qquad(0\le u\le1) \] exists, is continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,1]$, and the associated probability measure $\mu:=dG$ is purely singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. In particular, \[ G'(x)=0\qquad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }x\in(0,1). \]

  • Round 3 foundation. The value set $\mathcal E$ is countable and dense in $[0,1]$, and for every fixed $t\in\mathcal E$ one has the sharp local asymptotic \[ G(t)-G(t-h)\sim \frac{e^{-\gamma}K(t)}{\log(t/h)} \qquad(h\to0^+), \] with $K(t)>0$. In particular, \[ \frac{G(t)-G(t-h)}{h}\to+\infty \qquad(h\to0^+). \]

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-6)

The genuinely new ingredient is the complementary residual flatness theorem \[ A:=\left\{x\in(0,1):\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=0\right\} \quad\text{is dense $G_\delta$ and of full Lebesgue measure.} \]

This is not contained in Round 5: there we showed only that generic symmetric slopes become arbitrarily large. Here we prove that they also become arbitrarily small. Intersecting the two residual sets yields a much sharper statement:

On a dense $G_\delta$ set, the symmetric difference quotient oscillates all the way from $0$ to $+\infty$.

As a corollary, every nonempty interval contains subintervals with arbitrarily small average slope and also subintervals with arbitrarily large average slope. Thus no proof strategy based on establishing any interval-wise lower or upper regularity can succeed.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-6)

After Round 5, the exact unresolved structural issue was this: although finite differentiability points were already known to form a meager set, Round 5 did not identify a generic mechanism of oscillation. In particular, it remained possible that generic points only had very large symmetric slopes, with no complementary small-scale flattening.

We therefore prove the following precise claims.

  1. Residual flatness. The set \[ A:=\left\{x\in(0,1):\liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=0\right\} \] is dense $G_\delta$ in $(0,1)$, and in fact has full Lebesgue measure.

  2. Residual oscillation. The intersection \[ C:=A\cap B \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$; hence for every $x\in C$, \[ \liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=0, \qquad \limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=+\infty. \] Since $\mathcal E$ is countable, the set $C\setminus\mathcal E$ is also dense $G_\delta$.

  3. Interval oscillation of average slopes. For every nonempty open interval $I\subset(0,1)$, \[ \inf_{\substack{a<b\\ [a,b]\subset I}}\frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=0, \qquad \sup_{\substack{a<b\\ [a,b]\subset I}}\frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=+\infty. \] The second equality is Round 5; the first is new.

This overcomes the Round 5 limitation by showing that generic behavior is not merely unbounded above, but genuinely oscillatory.

5) WORK (ROUND-6)

5.1. The residual flatness set

For $0<h<\tfrac12$ and $x\in(h,1-h)$ define \[ S_h(x):=\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}. \] Since $G$ is continuous, for each fixed $h$ the function $x\mapsto S_h(x)$ is continuous on $(h,1-h)$.

Define \[ A:=\left\{x\in(0,1):\liminf_{h\to0^+}S_h(x)=0\right\}. \]

Lemma: The set $A$ is a $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$.
Proof: For integers $N,m\ge1$, define \[ W_{N,m}:=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)}\left\{x\in(q,1-q):S_q(x)<1/N\right\}. \] Each set in braces is open in $(0,1)$ because $x\mapsto S_q(x)$ is continuous; hence each $W_{N,m}$ is open.

We claim that \[ A=\bigcap_{N=1}^\infty\bigcap_{m=1}^\infty W_{N,m}. \]

If $x\in A$, then for every $N,m$ there exists $h\in(0,1/m)$ such that $S_h(x)<1/N$. Because $h\mapsto S_h(x)$ is continuous on $(0,\min\{x,1-x\})$, the strict inequality persists on a neighborhood of $h$; choosing a rational $q$ in that neighborhood with $0<q<1/m$ gives $x\in W_{N,m}$. Thus $x\in\cap_{N,m}W_{N,m}$.

Conversely, if $x\in\cap_{N,m}W_{N,m}$, then for every $N,m$ there exists $q\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1/m)$ with $S_q(x)<1/N$. Hence for every $N$ and every neighborhood of $0$ there exists $h$ in that neighborhood with $S_h(x)<1/N$. This is exactly the statement that \[ \liminf_{h\to0^+}S_h(x)=0. \] Therefore $A=\cap_{N,m}W_{N,m}$, and $A$ is $G_\delta$. ∎

Theorem (Residual flatness): The set $A$ is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$. Moreover, $A$ has full Lebesgue measure.
Proof: By Lemma Lemma, it remains to prove density and full Lebesgue measure.

Full measure. At every point $x$ where $G'(x)$ exists and equals $0$, the symmetric quotient $S_h(x)$ tends to $0$ as $h\to0^+$. By the Round 1 foundation, this happens for Lebesgue-a.e. $x\in(0,1)$. Hence $A$ has full Lebesgue measure.

Density. Let $I\subset(0,1)$ be a nonempty open interval. Since $A$ has full Lebesgue measure, $I\cap A\neq\varnothing$; in particular $I$ contains some point $x$ where $G'(x)=0$. Fix $N,m\ge1$. Because $S_h(x)\to0$ as $h\to0^+$, there exists $h_0>0$ such that \[ 0<h<h_0\implies S_h(x)<1/N. \] Choose $h\in(0,h_0)$ so small that also \[ h<1/m \qquad\text{and}\qquad [x-h,x+h]\subset I. \] By continuity of $h'\mapsto S_{h'}(x)$, the inequality $S_{h'}(x)<1/N$ still holds for all $h'$ in a neighborhood of $h$; choose a rational $q$ in that neighborhood with $0<q<1/m$. Then $x\in I\cap W_{N,m}$. Thus every nonempty open interval meets $W_{N,m}$, so each $W_{N,m}$ is dense. By Lemma Lemma, $A=\cap_{N,m}W_{N,m}$ is dense $G_\delta$. ∎

5.2. Residual oscillation of the symmetric quotient

Recall from Round 5 that \[ B:=\left\{x\in(0,1):\limsup_{h\to0^+}S_h(x)=+\infty\right\} \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$.

Theorem (Residual $0$--$\infty$ oscillation): The set \[ C:=A\cap B \] is a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$. For every $x\in C$ one has \[ \liminf_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=0, \qquad \limsup_{h\to0^+}\frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}=+\infty. \] Consequently, $G$ is not differentiable at any point of $C$.

Moreover, \[ C\setminus\mathcal E \] is also a dense $G_\delta$ subset of $(0,1)$.

Proof: By Theorem Theorem (Residual flatness) and the Round 5 foundation, both $A$ and $B$ are dense $G_\delta$ subsets of $(0,1)$. Therefore their intersection $C$ is dense $G_\delta$. The displayed liminf/limsup identities are immediate from the definitions of $A$ and $B$. If $G'(x)$ existed as a finite real number at some $x\in C$, then the symmetric quotient would converge to that value, contradicting the coexistence of liminf $0$ and limsup $+\infty$.

Since $\mathcal E$ is countable, $(0,1)\setminus\mathcal E$ is dense $G_\delta$. Intersecting it with the dense $G_\delta$ set $C$ shows that $C\setminus\mathcal E$ is dense $G_\delta$. ∎

5.3. Oscillation of average slopes on every interval

Corollary (Every interval contains arbitrarily flat and arbitrarily steep subintervals): For every nonempty open interval $I\subset(0,1)$, \[ \inf_{\substack{a<b\\ [a,b]\subset I}}\frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=0, \qquad \sup_{\substack{a<b\\ [a,b]\subset I}}\frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}=+\infty. \] In particular, there is no nonempty interval $I\subset(0,1)$ and no constant $c>0$ such that \[ G(b)-G(a)\ge c(b-a) \qquad(a<b,\ [a,b]\subset I). \]
Proof: The second assertion is exactly the Round 5 interval upper-slope barrier with exponent $\alpha=1$.

For the first assertion, fix a nonempty open interval $I$. By Theorem Theorem (Residual flatness), the set $A\cap I$ is nonempty. Choose $x\in A\cap I$. By definition of $A$, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $h>0$ so small that \[ [x-h,x+h]\subset I \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{G(x+h)-G(x-h)}{2h}<\varepsilon. \] Setting $a:=x-h$ and $b:=x+h$ yields \[ \frac{G(b)-G(a)}{b-a}<\varepsilon. \] Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, the infimum is $0$.

The final statement follows immediately from the identity of the infimum with $0$. ∎

5.4. Consequence for proof strategies

Round 5 already ruled out any approach based on proving a local upper Hölder/Lipschitz bound on some interval. Corollary Corollary (Every interval contains arbitrarily flat and arbitrarily steep subintervals) now rules out the opposite kind of strategy as well: one cannot first prove a uniform positive lower slope on any nonempty interval and then hope to deduce a positive derivative from that.

Thus both interval-wise upper regularity and interval-wise lower regularity fail everywhere. Any eventual proof or disproof of Erdős's question must therefore be genuinely pointwise and cannot proceed through any nontrivial interval of regular behavior.
