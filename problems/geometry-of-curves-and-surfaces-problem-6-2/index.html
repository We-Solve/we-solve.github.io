Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 6.2

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual three-dimensional setting, let $\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be a Euclidean circle. The source asks whether there exists a compact embedded surface $S$ with $\partial S=\Gamma$, constant mean curvature, and $S$ not contained in any round sphere. Being a piece of a sphere is a geometric condition, not merely a condition on genus.

In a conventional nontrivial formulation one requires $S$ connected and sufficiently smooth, with \[ H=\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}{2}=H_0\ne0 \] for a consistent normal. Then the question is whether such an embedded nonspherical $S$ exists, without a stated disk-topology, one-sided-of-the-boundary-plane, or contact-angle condition.

The source does not explicitly exclude $H_0=0$. With the ordinary definition of constant mean curvature that includes zero, a planar disk spanning $\Gamma$ is not a piece of a finite-radius sphere, so the intended unresolved question needs a nonzero-curvature convention or a convention counting planes as limiting spherical pieces. Connectedness likewise needs checking to exclude unrelated additional closed components.

The supplied summary claims a negative answer for all embedded surfaces by Nitsche/Alexandrov-type rigidity, and existence of immersed nonspherical examples of Kapouleas type. It supplies no precise theorem, example, or hypotheses supporting those assertions. In particular, rigidity under extra topology, side, or boundary-angle hypotheses cannot silently settle the unrestricted embedded question, and an immersed example does not meet embeddedness.

Short English statement

Can an embedded compact surface with one circular boundary have nonzero constant mean curvature without being spherical? The zero-curvature convention and unsupported blanket rigidity claim in the supplied summary require checking.
