Virtual-knot problem 19 — Vassiliev Invariants

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For classical knots and an abelian coefficient group $A$, a Vassiliev invariant extends to singular diagrams by \[ v(K_\times)=v(K_+)-v(K_-), \] and has finite type of degree at most $d$ if this extension vanishes on diagrams with more than $d$ singular double points. The source does not fix $A$, orientation conventions, or the virtual finite-type formulation used in its key extension question.

Compound problem: Vassiliev invariants. Understand the connection between virtual-knot polynomials and Vassiliev invariants of virtual knots in Kauffman's sense. The source reports prior work, but its citation and the family of polynomials to be related are absent.

The key question asks whether every classical finite-type invariant extends to a finite-type invariant of long virtual knots, specifically in the formulation of an unprovided reference. Let $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{long}}$ be classical long knot classes, $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{vir}}^{\mathrm{long}}$ long virtual knot classes, $i$ the natural inclusion, and $\operatorname{cl}$ classical closure. Once coefficients and the intended virtual filtration are fixed, its quantifiers are \[ \forall v\text{ of finite classical type},\quad \exists V:\mathcal K_{\mathrm{vir}}^{\mathrm{long}}\to A \text{ of finite virtual type},\quad V(i(L))=v(\operatorname{cl}(L)) \quad\forall L\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{long}}. \] The excerpt does not require the virtual extension to have the same degree as $v$; if a degree-preserving statement is intended, it must be recovered from the cited formulation rather than added here.

The source also reports a natural alternative notion of Vassiliev invariants for virtual knots with a different finite-type condition, and asks that it be investigated. In common virtual-knot settings one can distinguish singularization using a positive-minus-negative classical crossing from a derivative using a classical-minus-virtual crossing. These can produce different filtrations, but the missing citations prevent unambiguously assigning the extension question to a particular one. The alternative is not identified with the first notion or silently substituted for it.

Short English statement

Relate virtual-knot polynomials to virtual Vassiliev invariants, and ask whether every classical finite-type invariant has some finite-type extension to long virtual knots. The source distinguishes competing virtual finite-type definitions; the missing reference is essential to choosing the intended one.
