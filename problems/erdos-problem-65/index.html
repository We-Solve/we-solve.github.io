Statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple graph with $n:=|V(G)|$ and $m:=|E(G)|=kn$ edges (so $k=m/n$ may be non-integer). Let $\mathcal{L}(G)=\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}: G \text{ contains a cycle of length }\ell\}$, and write its elements in increasing order $a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_t$. Define \[ S(G):=\sum_{i=1}^t \frac{1}{a_i}. \] Questions:

  1. Does there exist an absolute constant $c>0$ such that for all $k\ge1$ and all graphs $G$ with $m=kn$ edges, \[ S(G)\ge c\log k?\]
  2. For fixed $n$ and $m=kn$, is $S(G)$ minimized (among all graphs on $n$ vertices with $m$ edges) by a complete bipartite graph?

Restatement from Math-5404-A3 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.

Review and correction of the claimed "disproof" for Erdős Problem #65

Verdict

The arithmetic observation in the proposed write-up is correct, but it does not solve Erdős Problem #65 as stated in the original source. It only disproves an over-generalised reformulation that asks for a complete bipartite minimiser for every exact pair $(n,m)$. The original 1981 strengthening of Erdős is narrower: it asks about graphs with exactly $k(n-k)$ edges, i.e. precisely the edge counts realised by $K_{k,n-k}$.

Accordingly, the claims

"FULL SOLUTION --- COUNTEREXAMPLE / DISPROOF"   and   "COMPLETION: 100%"
are incorrect for the original open problem.

1. Three different formulations that must be separated

The literature contains three related but distinct questions.

  1. (A) The 1975 exact-edge minimisation problem. Erdős wrote: for graphs $G(n;\lfloor kn\rfloor)$, determine or estimate the minimum of \[ \sum_{r\in \mathcal C(G)} \frac1r. \] He said it seemed likely that this minimum is $(\tfrac12+o(1))\log k$.

  2. (B) The 1981 strengthened conjecture. Erdős then proposed a sharper statement: if $n\ge 2k$, then for every graph $G(n;k(n-k))$, \[ \sum_{u_i\in \mathcal C(G)} \frac1{u_i} \ge \frac12\left(\frac12+\frac13+\cdots+\frac1k\right), \] and in his own words this means the sum is minimum for the complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes $k$ and $n-k$.

  3. (C) The over-generalised exact-$(n,m)$ reformulation. For every fixed exact pair $(n,m)$, minimise $S(G)$ over all $n$-vertex $m$-edge graphs and ask whether the minimiser is complete bipartite.

The proposed write-up correctly disproves (C), but the original conjectural minimiser statement in Erdős 1981 is (B), not (C).

2. What is correct in the proposed write-up

The following observation is valid.

Proposition 2.1. Let $n=2t+1$ with $t\ge 2$. A complete bipartite graph on $n$ vertices has exactly $a(n-a)$ edges for some integer $a$, so the possible edge counts are \[ a(2t+1-a)\qquad(0\le a\le 2t+1). \] The largest such value is $t(t+1)$, attained at $a=t,t+1$, and the next largest is \[ (t-1)(t+2)=t(t+1)-2. \] Hence there is no complete bipartite graph on $2t+1$ vertices with exactly \[ m=t(t+1)-1 \] edges.

Therefore, if one asks the universal exact-edge question (C), then that question is false: the class of graphs with $(n,m)=(2t+1,t(t+1)-1)$ is nonempty and finite, so a minimiser of $S(G)$ exists, but no complete bipartite graph lies in that class.

3. Why this does not solve the original open problem

The counterexample above does not apply to Erdős's 1981 strengthening, because that conjecture only concerns edge counts of the form \[ m=k(n-k), \] exactly the counts realised by the candidate extremal graph $K_{k,n-k}$.

So the right conclusion is:

The proposed argument refutes only the over-broad formulation (C). It does not refute, and does not settle, the original conjecture (B).

In particular, the sentence

"The minimal corrected exact-edge question is then: whenever $m=a(n-a)$, is the minimum attained by $K_{a,n-a}$?"
is not a new correction at all: it is essentially Erdős's original 1981 strengthened conjecture.

4. What the recent survey actually gives

Montgomery's 2025 survey states that in forthcoming work with Milojević, Pokrovskiy, and Sudakov, there is a threshold $d_0$ such that for every $d\ge d_0$, among all $n$-vertex graphs with at least $d(n-d)$ edges, \[ \sum_{\ell\in \mathcal C(G)} \frac1\ell \] is minimised exactly by the complete bipartite graph $K_{d,n-d}$.

This is a threshold theorem, but it also immediately implies the corresponding exact-edge statement for the special edge count $m=d(n-d)$: since $K_{d,n-d}$ belongs to the class of graphs with exactly $d(n-d)$ edges, uniqueness in the larger "at least" class forces it also to be the minimiser in the exact class.

Thus the survey gives genuine partial progress on the original 1981 conjecture, for all sufficiently large $d$.

5. Specific mistakes in the original draft

  1. The phrase "under the exact-edge interpretation of Problem 65" is misleading. The original 1981 source does not formulate the complete-bipartite conjecture for arbitrary exact pairs $(n,m)$.

  2. Theorem 5.3 is mathematically correct only as a disproof of the over-generalised formulation (C), not of Erdős Problem 65 in its original strengthened form.

  3. The sentence calling the "corrected exact-edge question" the version with $m=a(n-a)$ is historically inaccurate: that is already the original 1981 conjecture.

  4. The note says the large-$d$ survey theorem only addresses a threshold formulation. That is incomplete. It also yields the exact-edge statement for $m=d(n-d)$.

  5. The final verdict "FULL SOLUTION --- COUNTEREXAMPLE / DISPROOF" is false as a verdict on the original open problem.

  6. The completion estimate "100%" is false for the original problem.

  7. The bibliographic reference "EMS Magazine 138 (2025/2026 issue)" should be corrected to EMS Magazine 138 $2025$.

  8. If one keeps a statement ranging over $0\le a\le n$, then degenerate cases $a=0,n$ should be separated from formulas involving harmonic numbers. For cycle questions, the natural nontrivial range is $1\le a\le n-1$.

6. Corrected final conclusion

Correct conclusion. The proposed note does not provide a satisfactory solution to Erdős Problem #65. What it does show is the following:

If one over-generalises the minimiser question to arbitrary exact edge classes $(n,m)$, then that over-generalised statement is trivially false because many edge counts are not realised by any complete bipartite graph on $n$ vertices.
This is a valid clarification, but it is not a solution of the original conjecture, which already restricts to the exact edge count $m=k(n-k)$.

The best current picture from the checked sources is therefore:

  • the broad lower-bound problem was introduced earlier and has asymptotically sharp lower bounds;
  • Erdős's 1981 complete-bipartite strengthening remains open in general;
  • Montgomery's 2025 survey reports a forthcoming exact extremal theorem proving the conjectured complete-bipartite minimiser for all sufficiently large $d$, in the class of graphs with at least $d(n-d)$ edges, and hence also for the exact edge count $d(n-d)$.

If $G$ contains $C_3$, then $S(G)\ge 1/3>1/4$. If $G$ contains $C_4$, then $S(G)\ge 1/4$. So assume that $G$ has no $C_3$ and no $C_4$.

Since $42>b_{23}=\operatorname{ex}(23;\{C_3,C_4,C_6\})=36$ by (eq:n23exact), $G$ must contain a $6$-cycle. If $G$ also contains a $5$-cycle, then \[ S(G)\ge \frac15+\frac16>\frac14, \] and we are done.

It remains only to consider the case that $G$ has no $C_5$. Then $G$ has no $C_3,C_4,C_5$ and has exactly $42=a_{23}$ edges, so by Lemma lem:23-unique $G$ is isomorphic to the unique graph $H$ from that lemma. But $6,8\in\mathcal C(H)$, so \[ S(G)=S(H)\ge \frac16+\frac18=\frac{7}{24}>\frac14. \] Thus every $23$-vertex $42$-edge graph satisfies $S(G)\ge 1/4$, with equality attained by $K_{2,21}$. ∎

5.4. Main Round-5 theorem

Theorem (The $K_{2,n-2}$ line through $n=23$): For every integer $7\le n\le 23$, \[ \min\{S(G): |V(G)|=n,\ |E(G)|=2n-4\}=\frac14. \] The minimum is attained by the complete bipartite graph $K_{2,n-2}$.
Proof: For $7\le n\le 12$ this is exactly $F2$, and for $n=13$ it is exactly $F3$. For $14\le n\le 22$ it is Proposition prop:14-22. For $n=23$ it is Proposition prop:23-42. This exhausts the range $7\le n\le 23$. ∎

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Use of Round 4 results. I used Round 4 only as foundation for the already solved cases $7\le n\le 13$ and the formula $S(K_{2,n-2})=1/4$. No Round 4 proof was reproved.

  • Dependence on external tables. The argument for $14\le n\le 23$ uses only exact table entries (not asymptotics): the ANU page explicitly states that when neither "ne" nor "ng" is preceded by $\ge$, the Turán number and all extremal graphs are exact. That hypothesis holds for every table entry used here.

  • Strict inequalities for $14\le n\le 22$. These were checked entry-by-entry in (eq:strict14to22). There is no hidden asymptotic step.

  • The $n=23$ boundary case. Here the $C_5$-forcing inequality fails exactly, since $a_{23}=42=2\cdot 23-4$. The proof therefore isolates the no-$C_5$ case and handles it separately via the unique extremal graph $H$. This is the only place where the argument uses an explicit graph.

  • Computational content in Lemma lem:23-unique. The computational step is exact and finite: one decodes a published sparse6 graph and reads off the listed edges, which visibly contain one $6$-cycle and one $8$-cycle. No random search or heuristic is used.

  • Why equality cannot occur away from $K_{2,n-2}$. Every non-$K_{2,n-2}$ case handled in the proof actually gives a strict inequality $S(G)>1/4$ (either from a triangle, from $C_5$ and $C_6$, or from $C_6$ and $C_8$ in the exceptional $n=23$ graph).

7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 5 proves a substantially stronger theorem than Round 4: for every $7\le n\le 23$, among all graphs with $n$ vertices and $2n-4$ edges, \[ \min S(G)=\frac14, \] and the complete bipartite graph $K_{2,n-2}$ is a minimiser. Thus the entire $a=2$ complete-bipartite line is now settled up to $n=23$. The first unresolved value on this line is $n=24$. The full Erdős--Hajnal minimiser question for arbitrary $(n,m)$ remains open.
