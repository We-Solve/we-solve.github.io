Math-5229

Exact horizontal Plancherel identities for the diagonal Diffie--Hellman sum

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be prime, $\theta$ a generator of ${\mathbb{F}}_p^*$, $a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_p$, and $e_p(z)=\exp(2\pi i z/p)$. J. Bourgain's source asks for a good upper bound on a sum extracted as $\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_p}e_p(a\theta^x+b\theta^{x^2})$. Exponents of $\theta$ naturally live modulo $p-1$, so an index $x\in{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is not canonical without integer representatives. The coefficient restrictions and desired saving are also unspecified; $(a,b)=(0,0)$ precludes a nontrivial bound valid for all coefficients.

The attached aid distinguishes the periodic and representative conventions: \[ T(a,b)=\sum_{x=0}^{p-2}e_p(a\theta^x+b\theta^{x^2}),\qquad S(a,b)=\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}e_p(a\theta^x+b\theta^{x^2}). \] The first is a sum over ${\mathbb{Z}}/(p-1){\mathbb{Z}}$, and the second fixes the usual representatives of ${\mathbb{F}}_p$. Their exact endpoint relation is \[ S(a,b)=T(a,b)+e_p(a+b). \] For every fixed $b$, the aid gives the exact horizontal second moments \[ \sum_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_p}|T(a,b)|^2=p(p-1),\qquad \sum_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_p}|S(a,b)|^2=p(p+2). \] Consequently, for every $H>0$, \[ \#\{a:|T(a,b)|>H\sqrt p\}\le\frac{p-1}{H^2},\qquad \#\{a:|S(a,b)|>H\sqrt p\}\le\frac{p+2}{H^2}. \] These bounds are uniform in the fixed second coefficient but average over the first; they do not give a square-root bound for every coefficient pair or identify the exceptional coefficients.

The coefficient line $b=0$ is evaluated exactly: \[ T(0,0)=p-1,\quad S(0,0)=p,\qquad T(a,0)=-1,\quad S(a,0)=e_p(a)-1\quad(a\ne0). \] The general worst-case pointwise cancellation requested by the source remains outside these moment and endpoint identities.

Short English statement

Bound the mixed exponent sum after fixing its index convention. The supplied aid gives exact second moments and exceptional-set estimates in one coefficient, plus the zero-second-coefficient line, rather than a worst-case pointwise theorem.
