Math-7354

Palindromic Recurrent Words Outside Morphic Class $P$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a finite nonempty alphabet, let $A^*$ be the set of finite words over $A$, including the empty word $\varepsilon$, and write $u^{\mathrm{rev}}$ for the reversal of a finite word $u$. A palindrome is a word $u$ satisfying $u=u^{\mathrm{rev}}$. For a one-sided infinite word $w\in A^{\{0,1,2,\ldots\}}$, let $\operatorname{Fact}(w)$ denote its finite contiguous factors. The word $w$ is uniformly recurrent if, for every nonempty $u\in\operatorname{Fact}(w)$, there exists an integer $L\geq |u|$ such that every length-$L$ factor of $w$ contains $u$. It contains arbitrarily long palindromes if \[ \forall n\geq 1\ \exists u\in\operatorname{Fact}(w):\quad |u|\geq n\quad\text{and}\quad u=u^{\mathrm{rev}}. \] A morphism $\sigma:A^*\to A^*$ respects concatenation. It is primitive if there exists an integer $k\geq1$ such that, for every $a,b\in A$, the letter $b$ occurs in $\sigma^k(a)$. Such a morphism is nonerasing and acts on one-sided infinite words by concatenating letter images. Under the usual common-prefix convention, $\sigma$ belongs to class $P$ if there exist palindromes $p\in A^*$ and $q_a\in A^*$, one for each $a\in A$, such that \[ \sigma(a)=p q_a\qquad(a\in A). \] Here the empty word is allowed as a palindrome.

Conditional precise formulation. If "arise from primitive morphisms" means "are fixed points of primitive morphisms on their own alphabet," the supplied question asks whether there exist a finite nonempty alphabet $A$ and a one-sided infinite word $w$ whose occurring letters are exactly $A$, such that $w$ is uniformly recurrent, contains arbitrarily long palindromic factors, and satisfies \[ \begin{gathered} \exists\sigma:A^*\to A^*:\quad \sigma\text{ is primitive and }\sigma(w)=w,\\ \forall\tau:A^*\to A^*:\quad \left(\tau\text{ is primitive and }\tau(w)=w\right) \Longrightarrow \tau\notin P. \end{gathered} \] Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not define class $P$, specify whether infinite words are one-sided or two-sided, or define "arise from." The displayed formulation uses the stated one-sided fixed-point interpretation and the stated common-prefix definition of class $P$; these conventions require confirmation. If generation instead allows a letter-to-letter coding of a primitive fixed point on another alphabet, or refers to the factor language rather than an exact fixed point, both the generating objects and the universal quantifier must be specified anew. The supplied wording also requires confirmation that "none" excludes every primitive morphism generating the same word, as in the display. No unique unconditional formalization follows from the supplied excerpt alone.

Short English statement

Under the direct fixed-point interpretation, the question asks whether a uniformly recurrent infinite word can have arbitrarily long palindromic factors and be fixed by a primitive morphism, while no primitive morphism fixing that word belongs to class $P$. The intended generation relation and class convention remain unresolved. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
