Definitions and mathematical statement

Research proposal and limits of formulation. The supplied record asks: "Can infinitesimal analysis be given a rigorous yet concrete foundation using a calculus based on historical geometric insights?" Its accompanying verification describes the intended direction as historical and foundational research on machine-based calculus.

The record does not specify a mathematical meaning for "concrete," the historical geometric constructions or machines to be used, or the scope of "infinitesimal analysis." It supplies no underlying domain, axioms, admissible operations, class of functions, notion of infinitesimal, boundary conditions, or criteria by which a proposed foundation would satisfy the request. Consequently, neither definitions of the intended objects nor a precise quantified existence statement can be recovered without adding substantive hypotheses.

The question is therefore retained as a foundational research proposal, with its target unresolved. The supplied statement status is unrecoverable; the accompanying verification explicitly says that this is not a sufficiently specified mathematical conjecture or construction problem and that no separate clean statement is promoted. This draft does not replace the proposal with an invented formalization.

The supplied classification is partially_solved. The supplied literature assessment says that tractional and constructive approaches provide rigorous partial formalisms, while no generally accepted concrete foundation based on the proposed historical constructions has been completed. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical facts or a precise description of which parts have been solved.

Short English statement

The proposal asks whether historical geometric and machine-based approaches can support a rigorous, concrete foundation for infinitesimal analysis. The source does not define the intended constructions or success criteria, so a precise mathematical problem cannot be recovered. Its supplied partially solved label and literature assessment require independent review.
