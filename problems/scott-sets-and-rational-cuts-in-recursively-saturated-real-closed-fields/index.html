Math-1564

Scott sets and rational cuts in recursively saturated real closed fields

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the computable language of ordered fields. A real closed field is recursively saturated if every recursive partial type over finitely many of its elements which is finitely satisfiable in the field is realized there. A Scott set is a nonempty family $S\subseteq\mathcal P({\mathbb{N}})$ closed under Turing reducibility and finite Turing joins, such that every infinite binary tree computable from an element of $S$ has a path whose code belongs to $S$.

The source asks which standard systems, in the Macintyre--Marker convention for recursively saturated real closed fields, are possible, suggesting "all Scott sets." To specify the representation convention, call a structure $S$-saturated when each complete type of a finite tuple is computable from a member of $S$, and every partial type over a finite tuple, computable from a member of $S$ and finitely satisfiable in the structure, is realized. The supplied research aid reports that the associated Scott set is unique for the effectively perfect theory of real closed fields, and that every Scott set is represented by an $S$-saturated real closed field. It attributes the full representation theorem to Dolich, Knight, Lange and Marker (2015). Thus, in that standard-system convention, the reported answer is precisely all Scott sets. The original extract does not itself give the Macintyre--Marker coding definition; its identification with this $S$-saturation formulation is part of the cited result requiring source verification.

The parenthetical reference to rational cuts requires care. Fix a computable enumeration $(q_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of ${\mathbb{Q}}$, identify cuts with their characteristic sets in this enumeration, and put \[ k_S=\{r\in{\mathbb{R}}:\{n:q_n<r\}\in S\},\qquad L_r^-=\{q\in{\mathbb{Q}}:q<r\},\qquad L_q^+=\{u\in{\mathbb{Q}}:u\le q\}. \] For an ordered field $K$, its literal rational-cut shadow is \[ \mathcal C(K)=\left\{\{q\in{\mathbb{Q}}:q<a\}:a\in K\right\}. \] The supplied aid reports the following refinement for an $S$-saturated real closed field: \[ \mathcal C(K)=\{\varnothing,{\mathbb{Q}}\}\ \cup\ \{L_r^-:r\in k_S\}\ \cup\ \{L_q^+:q\in{\mathbb{Q}}\}. \] Here the two improper cuts come from infinitely negative or positive elements. Both cuts at a rational endpoint occur: the closed lower cut is realized by an element infinitesimally above that rational. Accordingly, the displayed family of literal cuts is not literally the same object as $S\subseteq\mathcal P({\mathbb{N}})$. The aid also reports that for every Scott set $S$ one may choose such a field with $|K|=|S|$, and that this is the minimum possible cardinality for representing $S$. These refinements are recorded as supplied research claims, not as independently established results.

The appended heading about models of $\aleph_1$-categorical theories and Andrews belongs to the next section and adds no hypothesis to Question 16.

Short English statement

Determine the standard systems of recursively saturated real closed fields. The supplied literature assessment reports that every Scott set occurs, and distinguishes that standard system from the field's literal family of rational cuts, including improper cuts and both rational-endpoint conventions.
