Math-1022

Inverse-Galois and Decidability Problems for Profinite Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

  1. Prescribed tame inertia. Let $G$ be a finite group, and let $C_1,\ldots,C_{m(G)+r}$ be conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of $G$. For a cyclic subgroup $H\leq G$, its conjugacy class is $[H]_G=\{gHg^{-1}:g\in G\}$. The source assumes that these classes generate $G$, but does not specify whether this means that representatives $H_i\in C_i$ can be chosen with $\langle H_1,\ldots,H_{m(G)+r}\rangle=G$, or that the subgroups in their combined conjugacy classes generate $G$.

    The question asks for a finite Galois extension $L/{\mathbb{Q}}$, together with an isomorphism $\iota:\operatorname{Gal}(L/{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\sim}G$, whose inertia groups have the prescribed conjugacy classes $C_1,\ldots,C_{m(G)+r}$, and which is tamely ramified. For a rational prime $\ell$ and a prime $\mathfrak P$ of $L$ above $\ell$, the inertia subgroup $I_{\mathfrak P}$ consists of decomposition-group elements acting trivially on the residue field; its conjugacy class is independent of the choice of $\mathfrak P$. Tameness at finite primes means $\ell\nmid e(\mathfrak P/\ell)$ for every such pair. The source does not define $m(G)$, quantify or constrain $r$, specify whether the list enumerates all ramified finite primes with multiplicity, or prescribe behavior at the real place. Consequently, the exact quantified inertia-realization assertion remains unresolved.

  2. Regular realization of finite $p$-groups. A finite $p$-group is a finite group of order $p^n$, where $p$ is prime and $n\geq0$. A finite Galois extension $K/{\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, with $t$ transcendental over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, is regular over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ if every element of $K$ algebraic over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ belongs to ${\mathbb{Q}}$. Equivalently, in a common algebraic closure of ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, one has $K\cap\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}={\mathbb{Q}}$. The regular-realization question is \[ \text{for every prime }p\text{ and every finite }p\text{-group }P, \quad \exists\,K/{\mathbb{Q}}(t)\text{ finite Galois and regular over }{\mathbb{Q}} \quad\text{such that}\quad \operatorname{Gal}(K/{\mathbb{Q}}(t))\cong P. \] The original statement instead writes $K\cap{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}$. Since ${\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq K$, that equality alone imposes no regularity condition. The formulation above uses the standard meaning of the explicitly supplied word "regular"; the discrepancy requires review.

  3. Surface-group completions and Galois groups. Under the usual convention that the surface is closed, connected, and orientable, its genus-$g$ fundamental group is \[ \Sigma_g=\left\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\ \middle|\ \prod_{i=1}^{g}[a_i,b_i]=1\right\rangle, \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1},\qquad g\geq2. \] This surface convention is not expressly specified in the record. Write \[ \widehat{\Sigma_g}=\varprojlim_{N\trianglelefteq\Sigma_g,\ [\Sigma_g:N]<\infty}\Sigma_g/N, \qquad \widehat{\Sigma_g}^{\,(p)}=\varprojlim_{N\trianglelefteq\Sigma_g,\ \Sigma_g/N\text{ a finite }p\text{-group}}\Sigma_g/N. \] For a field $F$, let $G_F=\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{sep}}/F)$ be its absolute Galois group, and let $G_F^{(p)}$ denote its maximal pro-$p$ quotient, the inverse limit of its continuous finite $p$-group quotients. All isomorphisms here are topological group isomorphisms.

    For each $g\geq2$, the original statement asks whether there is a field $F$ with \[ G_F\cong\widehat{\Sigma_g}. \] It also asks, with a prime $p$ understood as a parameter, whether there is a field $F$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{char}(F)\ne p, \qquad G_F^{(p)}\cong\widehat{\Sigma_g}^{\,(p)}. \] The record gives no characteristic condition for the first question and does not expressly quantify $p$. Its cleaned statement describes the pro-$p$ completion as an absolute Galois group "in the corresponding characteristic." That wording does not specify the characteristic and may ask for $G_F\cong\widehat{\Sigma_g}^{\,(p)}$, which is different from the maximal-pro-$p$-quotient formulation above. Both readings must remain visible pending source review.

  4. Decidability of open subgroups. A profinite group $P$ is projective if, for every continuous epimorphism $\alpha:A\twoheadrightarrow B$ of profinite groups and every continuous homomorphism $f:P\to B$, there is a continuous homomorphism $\widetilde f:P\to A$ with $\alpha\circ\widetilde f=f$. An open subgroup $U\leq P$ is given the induced profinite topology.

    The record does not specify the language, theory, presentation, or effectiveness convention intended by "decidable." Conditional on a fixed intended meaning, write $D(P)$ for that property. The cleaned statement asks whether \[ \forall\,P\text{ projective profinite},\qquad D(P)\ \Longrightarrow\ \left(\forall\,U\leq P\text{ open},\ D(U)\right). \] The original statement asks whether there exist a projective profinite group $P$ and an open subgroup $U\leq P$ such that $D(P)$ holds and $D(U)$ fails. With a fixed meaning of decidability, these are complementary yes-or-no questions about the same closure property. No definition of $D$ is selected here.

Short English statement

This record bundles four questions: realizing finite groups over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with prescribed tame inertia, realizing finite $p$-groups regularly over ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, realizing surface-group completions as absolute Galois groups or maximal pro-$p$ Galois quotients, and preservation of decidability under passage to open subgroups of projective profinite groups. Several parameters and conventions are missing, and the supplied versions differ on the pro-$p$ target and on the polarity of the decidability question. These qualifications require human mathematical review.
