Area–Angular-Momentum Inequalities with Nonpositive Cosmological Constant

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For an axially symmetric horizon or surface $S$, $A$ is its area and $J_S$ its quasilocal angular momentum in geometrized units. The relevant surface class in the report includes stable minimal surfaces and stable marginally outer trapped surfaces; stability and matter energy conditions constrain admissible geometries. An area--charge inequality compares $A$ with the electromagnetic charges. Equality rigidity identifies all data saturating the sharp bound, rather than merely constructing one equality example.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Extend the area–angular-momentum inequality $A\geq8\pi|J_S|$ to cosmological constant $\Lambda\leq0$, determine the role of extremal anti–de Sitter geometries in the sharp estimate, and prove the equality rigidity statement for the area–charge inequality.
Source scope and qualification.
The source $A\geq8\pi|J_S|$ is the reference inequality. The question asks for the appropriate $\Lambda\leq0$ extension and extremal AdS role, rather than presupposing that its unchanged right-hand side is sharp in every case. The charge rigidity task is also retained.

Short English statement

Find sharp horizon area bounds with zero or negative cosmological constant and classify equality in the related charge bound.

An extremal charge benchmark.
Test the radial lapse $f(r)=1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2+r^2/L^2$, $L>0$, of the spherical charged AdS family. A double horizon at $r=r_0>0$ requires $f(r_0)=f'(r_0)=0$. The derivative equation gives $M=Q^2/r_0-r_0^3/L^2$; substituting it into the first equation yields \[ Q^2=r_0^2+3r_0^4/L^2,\qquad M=r_0+2r_0^3/L^2. \] For its round area $A=4\pi r_0^2$, this reads $Q^2=A/(4\pi)+3A^2/(16\pi^2L^2)$. The extra term is positive, and vanishes in the flat-cosmological limit $L\to\infty$. Thus the unchanged flat charge-area equality is not the appropriate extremal reference at finite AdS radius.

The first missing step is the stability inequality and its sharp minimization with the cosmological term in the actual surface class.
