Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.66

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group, with identity subgroup denoted by $1$. Define its lower central series by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{k+1}(G)=[\gamma_k(G),G]$. The group has nilpotency class exactly $n\geq 1$ if $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=1$ and $\gamma_n(G)\neq 1$. No finiteness or finite-generation hypothesis on $G$ is imposed.

Define the upper central series by $\zeta_0(G)=1$ and \[ \zeta_{k+1}(G)/\zeta_k(G)=Z\left(G/\zeta_k(G)\right). \] The source calls $D_k(G)$ the $k$-th dimension subgroup without specifying coefficients. Under the conventional integral interpretation, let $\varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}[G]\to{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the augmentation map, $\varepsilon(\sum_g a_g g)=\sum_g a_g$, let $\Delta(G)=\ker\varepsilon$, and set \[ D_k(G)=\{g\in G:g-1\in\Delta(G)^k\},\qquad k\geq 1. \] This coefficient convention requires confirmation from the original source; the formulation below is conditional on it.

For each integer $n\geq 1$, put \[ A_n=\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}:\text{there exists a group }G\text{ of nilpotency class exactly }n\text{ with }D_m(G)\neq 1\}, \] \[ B_n=\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}:\text{there exists a group }G\text{ of nilpotency class exactly }n\text{ with }D_n(G)\not\subseteq\zeta_m(G)\}. \] The source writes \[ f(n)=\max A_n,\qquad g(n)=\max B_n. \] Here the nonnegative index convention for $B_n$ is explicit: the source does not specify whether $m=0$ is allowed. Restricting to positive indices can leave this set empty, already when $n=1$. The notation $\max A_n$ does not assert that a finite maximum exists; part (b) asks precisely for boundedness of $A_n$. The displayed definition of $g$ retains $D_n(G)$ exactly as supplied. Since $\zeta_n(G)=G$ for a group of class $n$, its admissible indices satisfy $m<n$.

The questions are:

  1. (a) What is $f(3)$?
  2. (b) Is $A_n$ bounded, equivalently does $f(n)$ exist as a finite integer, for every integer $n\geq 1$? This question is attributed specifically to B. I. Plotkin.
  3. (c) Is the growth of $f(n)$ and $g(n)$ polynomial, exponential, or intermediary? These growth categories and their comparison conventions are not defined in the supplied record; interpreting the question for $f$ as a finite-valued function also depends on part (b).
The supplied source reports, with attribution to N. Gupta and Yu. V. Kuz'min, that both $f(n)-n$ and $g(n)$ tend to infinity as $n\to\infty$. This is retained as a supplied literature assertion, not as an independently verified result; its interpretation for $f$ requires resolving the maximum convention.

Short English statement

For nilpotent groups of class exactly $n$, determine how far nontrivial dimension subgroups can persist, compute $f(3)$, and study the growth of this extremal index and the supplied upper-central-series quantity $g(n)$. The coefficient ring, index conventions, existence of maxima, and meaning of the growth categories require review.
