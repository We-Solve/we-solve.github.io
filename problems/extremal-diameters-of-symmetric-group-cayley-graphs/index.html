Math-939

Extremal Diameters of Symmetric-Group Cayley Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer, and let $S_n$ be the group of permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For a subset $A\subseteq S_n$ satisfying $\langle A\rangle=S_n$, define the undirected Cayley graph $\Gamma_n(A)$ to have vertex set $S_n$, with distinct vertices $g,h$ adjacent precisely when $h=gs$ for some $s\in A\cup A^{-1}$, where $A^{-1}=\{a^{-1}:a\in A\}$. Thus inverses are available as single steps. Let $d_A(g,h)$ be the minimum number of edges in a path from $g$ to $h$, and define \[ \operatorname{diam}\Gamma_n(A)=\max_{g,h\in S_n}d_A(g,h),\qquad D(n)=\max_{\substack{A\subseteq S_n\\\langle A\rangle=S_n}}\operatorname{diam}\Gamma_n(A). \] The singleton graph for $n=1$ has diameter zero. Replacing $A$ by $A\cup A^{-1}$ does not change this graph, so this formulation also covers the convention of requiring an inverse-closed generating set.

Proposed diameter bound. The supplied statement asks to prove \[ \forall n\geq 1,\qquad D(n)\leq \frac{1}{2}n^2+4n. \] Equivalently, the displayed upper bound is to hold for every generating subset of $S_n$, with distance measured in the undirected graph defined above. The positive-integer range makes explicit the supplied phrase "for every $n$"; the record gives no separate lower threshold.

Proposed extremal families: incomplete specification. The record also asks to prove or disprove that certain explicit generating families, described as related to involutions in a "square-with-whiskers" pattern, maximize the diameter for every $n$. If $W_n\subseteq S_n$ denotes one intended generating set, its proposed extremality would mean \[ \langle W_n\rangle=S_n, \qquad \operatorname{diam}\Gamma_n(W_n)=D(n). \] This is only a schema: the supplied record contains no permutations defining $W_n$, no construction of the pattern, and no parameter or admissible-size specifications for the plural families. It therefore does not determine a precise extremality conjecture or whether every member, or a selected member, is intended to attain the maximum. These data must be supplied before that part can be fully formulated.

The excerpt reports extensive searches, exhaustive in some cases, supporting the proposed extremality for $n\leq 15$; it does not specify which cases were exhaustive. This is supplied computational evidence, not a proof for all $n$. The excerpt also contains incomplete references to a question attributed to "V." and a nilpotent-group conjecture involving "J."; neither supplies enough information to formulate an additional mathematical target.

Short English statement

The problem proposes that every undirected Cayley graph generated by permutations of $n$ symbols has diameter at most $\frac12 n^2+4n$. It also proposes that particular "square-with-whiskers" generating sets attain the largest possible diameter, but their construction is missing from the supplied record. Reported computations support extremality through $n=15$, with exhaustiveness asserted only for some cases.
