Dolnikov’s Colorful Transversal Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a compact convex set $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ and a vector $t\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$, its translate is $K+t=\{x+t:x\in K\}$. A finite family $\mathcal F$ is pierced by at most three points if there exists a set $P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ with $|P|\leq 3$ such that $P\cap A\neq\varnothing$ for every $A\in\mathcal F$. The piercing points need not belong to $K$. An empty family is pierced vacuously.

Dol'nikov's colorful transversal conjecture. For every compact convex set $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ and every three finite families $\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2,\mathcal F_3$ whose members are translates of this same set $K$, assume that \[ \forall i,j\in\{1,2,3\},\quad i\neq j\ \Longrightarrow\ \left(\forall A\in\mathcal F_i\ \forall B\in\mathcal F_j,\ A\cap B\neq\varnothing\right). \] Then \[ \exists j\in\{1,2,3\}\ \exists P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2,\qquad |P|\leq 3\quad\text{and}\quad \forall A\in\mathcal F_j,\ P\cap A\neq\varnothing. \] No intersection condition is imposed on two members of the same family. No nonempty-interior assumption on $K$, or nonemptiness assumption on the families, is added. Here "three points" is expressed as "at most three points"; any smaller piercing set can be enlarged to three distinct points in ${\mathbb{R}}^2$.

Short English statement

Dol'nikov's conjecture asks whether three finite families of translates of one compact convex planar set, with every pair from different families intersecting, must have one family that can be met by a common set of at most three points. The supplied record reports partial results; this statement-only draft requires human mathematical and source review.
