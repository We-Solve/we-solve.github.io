A correlated equilibrium uses a joint recommendation distribution for which following recommendations is optimal against every allowed unilateral deviation. Quantum equilibria use shared quantum states and local operations. A semidefinite hierarchy imposes progressively longer operator-moment positivity constraints.

A semidefinite program optimizes a linear functional of a symmetric matrix subject to affine constraints and positive semidefiniteness. The condition $X\succeq0$ means $v^TXv\geq0$ for every real vector $v$.

An $n$-qubit pure state is a unit vector in $(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}$, considered up to global phase. A quantum algorithm applies permitted quantum operations and measurements; complexity depends on the gate and input-access model.

Literature and context check

The primary report, printed pp. 248--250, defines quantum equilibrium by all unilateral POVM deviations, displays finite-dimensional dual optimality conditions, and explicitly warns that a standard commuting-operator hierarchy need not target the tensor-product supremum. The computations below verify a fixed-state two-action special case and test a tempting substitution of classical incentive constraints. They do not construct the requested global hierarchy.

Research attempt: Exact finite best responses and the hidden-advice obstruction

Fix a finite-dimensional state and all other players' POVMs. For one player and one type, let Hermitian operators $V_0,V_1$ encode the two action payoffs, including the prior and other outcomes. A two-outcome POVM is $(M,I-M)$ with $0\preceq M\preceq I$. Its payoff is \[ \operatorname{Tr}V_1+\operatorname{Tr}(M K),\qquad K=V_0-V_1. \] Diagonalize $K$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_i$. The diagonal entries $m_{ii}$ of $M$ in this basis lie in $[0,1]$, so \[ \operatorname{Tr}(MK)=\sum_i\lambda_i m_{ii}\leq\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i =\operatorname{Tr}K_+. \] Equality is attained by the projection onto the positive eigenspaces of $K$. Thus the exact best-response value is $\operatorname{Tr}V_1+\operatorname{Tr}K_+$, with a concrete optimal measurement.

The dual certificate is equally explicit: take $W=V_1+K_+$. Then $W-V_1=K_+\succeq0$ and $W-V_0=K_-\succeq0$, where $K=K_+-K_-$. For any POVM $(M_0,M_1)$, \[ \sum_a\operatorname{Tr}(M_aV_a)\leq\sum_a\operatorname{Tr}(M_aW)=\operatorname{Tr}W. \] The inequality uses positivity of the trace of the product of two PSD matrices: $\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\operatorname{Tr}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})\geq0$. The optimal projector attains this bound. More generally, for any finite number of outcomes, matrices satisfying $W\succeq V_a$ and equality of achieved payoff to $\operatorname{Tr}W$ suffice to certify a best response by exactly this trace argument. No general dual-existence theorem is needed for that sufficient direction.

Classical incentive constraints do not certify a given quantum implementation.
Consider two players paid one if their output bits agree and zero otherwise, with no input types. Use shared state \[ \rho=\tfrac12|00\rangle\langle00|+\tfrac12|11\rangle\langle11|. \] Bob measures in the computational basis. Alice uses the trivial POVM $M_0=M_1=I/2$, producing an independent fair output. The observed outcome distribution is uniform on all four pairs. It satisfies all classical recommendation-deviation inequalities: conditioned on either own recommendation, the other's bit is still fair, so any relabeling or randomized replacement has agreement probability $1/2$.

But Alice can instead measure her qubit in the computational basis and agree with Bob with probability one. Hence this state/measurement tuple is not a quantum equilibrium. In the operator formula, $V_0=\tfrac12|0\rangle\langle0|$, $V_1=\tfrac12|1\rangle\langle1|$, and the prescribed measurement obtains $1/2$ while the positive-eigenspace best response obtains one. This gives an explicit hidden-advice deviation missed by constraints only on the classical displayed recommendations. It does not prove that the uniform behavior has no other quantum-equilibrium realization: an implementation with independent advice can remove the hidden information.

The original hierarchy must therefore retain enough operator/state structure to constrain all permitted local measurements, not just output relabelings. Replacing tensor factors by commutation also changes the ambient model unless a further argument establishes the intended correspondence. Neither the finite best-response certificates nor the displayed counterexample provides that correspondence or compactness/convergence of an unbounded-dimensional optimization.
