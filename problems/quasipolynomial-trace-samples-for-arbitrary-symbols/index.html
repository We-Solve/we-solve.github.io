CS-136 · Math-2978

Quasipolynomial trace samples for arbitrary symbols

Status OpenLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge2$ and $0<p\le1$. A deletion trace retains each symbol of a length-$n$ string independently with probability $p$, reports retained symbols exactly and preserves their order. The decoder knows $n,p$ and receives independent traces. Does there exist an absolute $C>0$ and a decoder recovering every input string with probability at least $2/3$ from at most \[ \left\lceil\exp\left(Cp^{-1}(\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6\right)\right\rceil \] traces? The symbols need not be binary. No restriction is placed on the decoder's computational cost.

Short English statement

For every length $n\ge2$ and known retention probability, can exactly observed symbols be reconstructed from the stated quasipolynomial number of traces, without a time bound?

Quasipolynomial trace samples for arbitrary symbols

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that worst-case trace reconstruction has quasipolynomial sample bounds with logarithm $O((\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6)$ at fixed retention, including general exactly observed symbols, without an algorithmic guarantee.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Each source symbol is retained independently with probability $p\in(0,1]$, and the output consists of the retained symbols in order. The decoder knows the length $n$ and retention probability $p$ and receives independent traces. For every $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ its reconstruction success must be at least $2/3$. For every $n\ge2$, the claimed worst-case trace sample bound is \[ N\le\exp\left(O(p^{-1}(\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6)\right). \] The constants are absolute. For $q=1-p>0$ and $\mathcal X=1+\log n/(1+\log(1/q))$, the refined bound is \[ \log N=O(p^{-1}(\log n)\mathcal X^2(1+\log\mathcal X)^6). \] For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $q\le n^{-\varepsilon}$ therefore permits polynomially many traces. Exact observation of retained symbols also allows general alphabets. This is a sample-complexity claim, with no comparable running-time assertion.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-10-05), gap probes use compact spectral masks and a latest-anchor induction to keep a signal during shuffling and scale transfer. Radial compactification and stopping-stage interpolation produce separation of trace laws; the argument then reduces general symbols to the detected binary distinction.
