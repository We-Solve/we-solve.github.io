Ancient Ovals as Post-Singularity Limit Flows

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A smooth hypersurface mean curvature flow in Euclidean space is a family of immersions $X(\cdot,t):M^n\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$ whose normal velocity equals its mean curvature vector: $(\partial_tX)^\perp=\vec H$. An ancient solution is a solution defined for all times $s\in(-\infty,S)$, for some finite $S$. The term ancient oval ordinarily refers to a compact, convex ancient solution that is not a self-similarly shrinking round sphere. The supplied record does not specify the dimension or the additional geometric hypotheses intended by this term, so this description does not resolve the intended ancient-oval class.

Let $T_1$ denote the first singular time of an initially smooth flow. Discussing a singularity at a time $t_*>T_1$ requires a continuation of the flow through its first singular time; no continuation framework is specified in the source record.

A usual form of parabolic rescaling is \[ \mathcal M^{(j)}_s =\lambda_j\left(M_{t_j+\lambda_j^{-2}s}-x_j\right), \qquad \lambda_j\to\infty, \qquad (x_j,t_j)\to(x_*,t_*). \] A limit of such rescalings, in an appropriate convergence topology, is one possible meaning of a limit flow at $(x_*,t_*)$. Fixing every center to $(x_*,t_*)$ gives the more specific tangent-flow construction. The record does not specify the permitted centers, scales, convergence, or whether this more specific construction is intended.

The supplied question is: Can ancient ovals arise as limit flows at singularities of a mean curvature flow after the first singular time?

Its existential target is a continued mean curvature flow, a singular spacetime point $(x_*,t_*)$ with $t_*>T_1$, and a limiting ancient oval arising from rescalings at that singularity. The ambient dimension, admissible initial flows, continuation, ancient-oval class, and meaning of limit flow remain unspecified. Consequently, this target is a statement schema requiring those choices, rather than a uniquely determined quantified proposition.

Short English statement

The question asks whether an ancient oval can occur as a blow-up limit at a singularity strictly later than the first singular time of a mean curvature flow. A precise formulation requires the intended dimension, class of flows, continuation through singularities, and definition of blow-up limit. The supplied record labels the problem open; that status is supplied dated curation, not independently verified here.
