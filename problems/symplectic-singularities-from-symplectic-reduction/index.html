Symplectic Singularities from Symplectic Reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: When does symplectic reduction produce a symplectic singularity?

Under the usual complex algebraic convention, a symplectic singularity is a normal complex algebraic variety $X$ whose smooth locus $X_{\mathrm{reg}}$ admits a regular two-form $\omega$ satisfying $\mathrm d\omega=0$, such that $\omega$ is nondegenerate at every point of $X_{\mathrm{reg}}$, and such that for every resolution of singularities $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$, the form $\pi^*\omega$, initially defined on $\pi^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{reg}})$, extends to a regular two-form on all of $\widetilde X$. The extension is not required to be nondegenerate.

With this convention as a possible interpretation of the target, the problem asks for conditions on symplectic reduction data under which the resulting space has this property. The source does not specify the reduction data or the category: it gives no ambient symplectic space, acting group, moment map, reduction value, or quotient construction. It also does not specify whether the form on the smooth locus must be the one induced by reduction, or whether normalization or another modification of the quotient is allowed. Consequently, the supplied question is a characterization problem with unresolved scope, rather than a fully specified universally quantified assertion. The definition above explains a conventional meaning of the target; the source does not establish that this particular convention is intended.

Short English statement

Find conditions ensuring that a symplectic reduction has symplectic singularities. In the conventional complex algebraic interpretation, this requires normality, a symplectic form on the smooth locus, and extension of that form across every resolution. The source leaves the reduction setting and the precise intended scope unspecified.
