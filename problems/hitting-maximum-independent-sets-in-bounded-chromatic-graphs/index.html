Math-5032

Hitting Maximum Independent Sets in Bounded-Chromatic Graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the conventional interpretation that the graphs are finite, simple, and undirected, let $G=(V,E)$ be a nonempty graph and put $n=|V|$. These graph conventions are not explicitly specified in the supplied record. An independent set is a subset of $V$ containing no adjacent pair of vertices. Define \[ \alpha(G)=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq V\text{ is independent}\}, \qquad \mathcal M(G)=\{I\subseteq V:I\text{ is independent and }|I|=\alpha(G)\}. \] Let $\chi(G)$ be the least number of colors in a proper vertex coloring, and define \[ h(G)=\min\left\{|S|:S\subseteq V,\ S\cap I\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }I\in\mathcal M(G)\right\}. \] Thus the sets to be met are maximum independent sets, not merely inclusion-maximal independent sets.

The requested bounded-chromatic-number conjecture asks whether \[ \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}\ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}\ \forall G, \qquad \left(|V(G)|\ge N\ \text{and}\ \chi(G)\le k\right) \Longrightarrow h(G)\le\varepsilon |V(G)|. \] Here $G$ ranges over graphs with the conventions stated above, and $N$ may depend on $k$ and $\varepsilon$. Equivalently, for every sequence $(G_j)_{j\ge1}$ with $|V(G_j)|\to\infty$ and a uniform bound on $\chi(G_j)$, must \[ \frac{h(G_j)}{|V(G_j)|}\longrightarrow0? \] The growth condition on the orders makes explicit the asymptotic interpretation of the supplied statement. The original statement labels the problem open even when $\chi(G)=3$; the supplied dated assessment describes the case of $3$-colorable graphs as still open.

For context, the source attributes to Bollobás, Erdős, and Tuza (1991) the stronger conjecture that, for every sequence of graphs of orders $n_j\to\infty$, if there is a constant $c>0$ such that $\alpha(G_j)\ge c n_j$ for all sufficiently large $j$, then $h(G_j)/n_j\to0$.

The following additional remarks are preserved as supplied source claims, not as independently verified results. The source attributes to Hajnal (1965) the assertion that $\alpha(G)>n/2$ implies $h(G)=1$. It also describes graphs $G=G_n$ with \[ \alpha(G)>n/4,\qquad \chi(G)\le8,\qquad h(G)>n/2, \] and graphs with \[ \alpha(G)=\left(1/2-o(1)\right)n, \qquad h(G)>(\log n)^{0.999}. \] The source says that these constructions settle questions of Friedgut, Kalai, and Kindler, and of Dong and Wu, without supplying the precise questions. Finally, it states that regular graphs with $\alpha(G)>0.250001n$ satisfy the bound transcribed as $h(G)\le O(n\log n)$, and says that this applies in particular to regular $3$-colorable graphs.

These contextual bounds have unresolved transcription problems: a detached square-root sign occurs before the first construction, and another occurs before the regular-graph remark. Their intended placements and radicands cannot be recovered from the supplied text. Taken literally for unbounded graph orders, the displayed construction with $\chi(G)\le8$ and $h(G)>n/2$ would contradict the requested conjecture. No correction to either bound is assumed here; these remarks require source review before they can be used mathematically.

Short English statement

For each fixed bound on the chromatic number, must every sufficiently large graph have a set of vertices meeting every maximum independent set whose size is an arbitrarily small fraction of the graph's order? The supplied record presents this as a conjecture and labels the overall status partially solved. Corrupted square-root notation in the contextual claims prevents those claims from being interpreted reliably.
