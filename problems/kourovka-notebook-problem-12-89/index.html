Kourovka Notebook Problem 12.89

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ denote an infinite connected graph. For a graph $H$, write $V(H)$ for its vertex set and $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$ for its full automorphism group. Under the usual interpretation that primitivity refers to the action on vertices, this action is primitive if it is transitive and the only $\operatorname{Aut}(H)$-invariant equivalence relations on $V(H)$ are equality and the universal relation.

The problem, proposed by V. I. Trofimov in 1992, is to characterize all such $\Gamma$ for which there exists a sequence $(\Gamma_n)_{n\geq 1}$ of finite connected graphs satisfying \[ \operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma_n)\text{ acts primitively on }V(\Gamma_n) \quad\text{for every }n\geq 1, \qquad \Gamma_n\longrightarrow\Gamma. \] Here convergence must have the meaning specified in the cited paper of Trofimov, Algebra and Logic 28, no. 3 (1989), 220--237. The supplied record refers to that paper for definitions but does not reproduce them. Thus the displayed convergence condition is a reference to an unavailable definition, not a specified topology or convergence criterion. The graph conventions, any local finiteness requirement, and any rooting or other auxiliary structure required by that definition remain unresolved. The interpretation of primitivity as the vertex action also requires confirmation from that reference.

Short English statement

Characterize the infinite connected graphs that arise as limits of sequences of finite connected graphs with primitive automorphism groups, using Trofimov's cited definitions. The supplied record omits those definitions, so the convergence notion and associated graph conventions require clarification.
