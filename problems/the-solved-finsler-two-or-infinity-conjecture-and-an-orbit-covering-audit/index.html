Math-275

The solved Finsler two-or-infinity conjecture and an orbit-covering audit

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F:TS^2\to[0,\infty)$ be a smooth strongly convex Finsler metric away from the zero section: $F(x,av)=aF(x,v)$ for $a>0$, and the fiber Hessian of $F^2/2$ is positive definite. Reversibility $F(x,-v)=F(x,v)$ is not assumed. A prime closed geodesic is a periodic geodesic not obtained by iterating a shorter one. Count orbits modulo time translation in the unit-speed geodesic flow; opposite orientations, when both are geodesics, represent distinct flow orbits.

The source conjecture, explicitly about irreversible metrics, is that the number $N(F)$ of distinct prime closed geodesics satisfies \[ N(F)=2\quad\text{or}\quad N(F)=\infty. \] The supplied research aid reports the stronger conclusion for every possibly irreversible Finsler metric, without bumpiness or transversality assumptions. It attributes the result to a 2026 JAMS theorem of Cristofaro-Gardiner, Hryniewicz, Hutchings and Liu. This is a supplied solved-in-literature assessment, not independent verification of that theorem or its exact hypotheses.

For the contact interpretation let $S_FS^2=\{(x,v):F(x,v)=1\}$, diffeomorphic to ${\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^3$, and equip it with its Hilbert contact form. Its Reeb orbits are the unit-tangent lifts of geodesics. The aid notes that $H^2({\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^3;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}$ is torsion and reports that the stated Reeb theorem applies directly.

The supplied covering reduction is as follows. For a connected regular finite covering of degree $D$, a simple downstairs periodic orbit whose monodromy has order $o$ has $D/o$ distinct simple lifts, each of period $o$ times its downstairs period. In the universal double cover $S^3\to{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^3$, let $N_0$ and $N_1$ count downstairs simple orbits contractible and noncontractible in the unit tangent bundle. The reported count is \[ N_{\mathrm{up}}=2N_0+N_1. \] According to the aid, in the exactly-two case both unit-tangent orbits are noncontractible: $N_0=0$, $N_1=2$; each has one doubled-period lift and the lifted flow again has exactly two simple orbits. Both prime orbits are reported irrationally elliptic, meaning their transverse return-map eigenvalues are $e^{\pm2\pi i\theta}$ with $\theta\notin{\mathbb{Q}}$; all iterates are therefore nondegenerate, so the metric is bumpy. Conversely, a prime geodesic whose unit-tangent orbit is contractible forces infinitely many prime closed geodesics under these reported results. Contractibility here is in $S_FS^2$, not in the simply connected base sphere.

The original historical support was a result of Hofer--Wysocki--Zehnder on contact $S^3$, projected to the Finsler sphere under bumpiness and transverse intersections of stable and unstable manifolds of hyperbolic closed geodesics. It also cites Harris--Paternain and notes that the geodesics in Katok's example are elliptic. These earlier qualifications must not be mistaken for hypotheses of the aid's reported unrestricted conclusion.

Short English statement

The source conjectures that an irreversible Finsler two-sphere has exactly two or infinitely many prime closed geodesics. The supplied 2026 research aid labels this solved for all Finsler metrics and reports additional exact-two rigidity and covering consequences. These dated literature and orbit-counting claims remain subject to source and mathematical review.
