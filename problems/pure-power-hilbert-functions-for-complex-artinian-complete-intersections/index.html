Pure-power Hilbert functions for complex Artinian complete intersections

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a mathematical result relevant to this question in the selected manuscript. In characteristic zero, the Eisenbud--Green--Harris Hilbert-function comparison is claimed for ideals containing a regular sequence, using a new commuting-form model over division coefficients.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Let $F$ have characteristic zero, $S_F=F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and $I$ contain a homogeneous regular sequence of length $1\le c\le n$ with degrees $2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_c$. The claimed monomial ideal $J$ contains $P_F=(x_1^{a_1},\ldots,x_c^{a_c})$ and satisfies \[ \dim_F(S_F/J)_d=\dim_F(S_F/I)_d\qquad(d\ge0). \] It may be selected as lex-plus-powers: outside $P_F$, each degree uses the lexicographically greatest $\dim I_d-\dim(P_F)_d$ monomials, with $x_1>\cdots>x_n$. The construction theorem over $\mathbb C$, for a full regular sequence $f_i$, additionally supplies a finite-dimensional central division algebra $\Delta$ over a finitely generated extension $k/\mathbb C$ and a commutative graded algebra $k[x]\subseteq A\subseteq\Delta[x]$, finite over $k[x]$, generated by commuting linear forms. Its distinguished forms satisfy \[ t_i^{a_i}=c_if_i+\sum_{\ell<i}B_{i\ell}f_\ell+\sum_{d>i}C_{id}t_d, \] with $c_i\in k^\times$, $B_{i\ell}\in A_{a_i-a_\ell}$, $C_{id}\in A_{a_i-1}$; all monomials in the $t_i$ form a left $\Delta$-basis of $\Delta[x]$.

Outline of the argument

The central construction replaces variables by commuting linear forms with coefficients in a division algebra while retaining an ordered polynomial basis. Triangular identities model the regular sequence by powers; monomial compression gives the Artinian comparison, and reduction plus coefficient descent handles shorter sequences.

Write-up

Split-factor Eisenbud–Green–Harris and an exact regularity test

**Problem:** TOP-30006989

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Abedelfatah’s theorem supplies the pure-power Hilbert-function comparison whenever every form in the contained regular sequence splits into linear factors; the report also proves a finite linear-algebra criterion for regularity of such split sequences and the complete-intersection Hilbert-series case.

Checked literature theorem

Let $S=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and let $I\subseteq S$ be homogeneous and contain a regular sequence $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ with $\deg f_i=a_i$, $2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_n$. If every $f_i$ is a product of linear forms over $\mathbb C$, then a homogeneous ideal $J$ containing $x_1^{a_1},\ldots,x_n^{a_n}$ exists with

$$\dim_\mathbb C(S/I)_d=\dim_\mathbb C(S/J)_d\qquad\text{for every }d\ge0.$$

This is Corollary 4.3 of Abed Abedelfatah's *On the Eisenbud–Green–Harris Conjecture*, arXiv:1212.2653v1 (11 December 2012); the work was subsequently published in Proceedings of the AMS 143(1) (2015). The split-factor result is credited to that work. Its full induction is not newly proved here. The report keeps the additional splitting hypothesis; over $\mathbb C$, a multivariate homogeneous polynomial need not factor into linear factors merely because the field is algebraically closed.

The primary preprint also states a refinement, Corollary 4.4: splitting is needed only for $f_3,\ldots,f_n$, using the known two-variable case. To minimize dependencies, the advertised scope here is the all-split version of Corollary 4.3.

A usable exact regularity criterion

Suppose

$$f_i=\prod_{j=1}^{a_i}\ell_{ij},$$

where each $\ell_{ij}$ is a nonzero homogeneous linear form. Then $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ is a regular sequence **if and only if every choice of one factor from each row**, $\ell_{1j_1},\ldots,\ell_{nj_n}$, is linearly independent.

To prove this, their common zero locus is the union, over all such choices, of the intersections of the $n$ selected hyperplanes. Indeed, $f_i(x)=0$ means at least one of its factors vanishes at $x$, independently for each $i$. The selected hyperplanes meet only at the origin exactly when their coefficient matrix is invertible. Thus the independence condition is equivalent to the common zero locus of all the $f_i$ being just the origin.

By the Hilbert Nullstellensatz, that condition is equivalent to $(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ being primary to the homogeneous maximal ideal. The polynomial ring is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension $n$, so a homogeneous system of parameters of length $n$ is a regular sequence; conversely a homogeneous regular sequence of full length has zero-dimensional quotient. This establishes the criterion. It is a finite test with $\prod_i a_i$ determinant conditions; repeated factors are allowed.

The criterion also explains why selecting arbitrary individual factors of a known split regular sequence gives a linear coordinate system, an observation used in Abedelfatah's induction. It does **not** say that checking only one choice of factors proves regularity.

A whole class of regular split sequences

For an explicit infinite family, take

$$\ell_{ij}=x_i+\sum_{k>i}c_{ijk}x_k$$

with arbitrary complex coefficients and arbitrary positive factor counts $a_i$. Every coefficient matrix obtained by choosing one factor in each row is upper triangular with diagonal entries one. Its determinant is one. Hence the $f_i=\prod_j\ell_{ij}$ form a regular sequence for every choice of the coefficients. The literature theorem applies to **every homogeneous ideal containing this sequence**, not only to the ideal generated by it.

This is a substantive distinction: the quotient Hilbert function of a complete intersection alone is easy, whereas allowing arbitrary extra generators is the Eisenbud–Green–Harris comparison target.

The complete-intersection case without splitting

For completeness, when $I=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ itself, no splitting hypothesis is needed. Multiplication by a homogeneous nonzerodivisor of degree $a$ gives the exact sequence

$$0\to R(-a)\xrightarrow{\,f\,}R\to R/(f)\to0,$$

so its Hilbert series is multiplied by $1-t^a$. Iterating along the regular sequence yields

$$H_{S/I}(t)=\frac{\prod_i(1-t^{a_i})}{(1-t)^n}
=\prod_i(1+t+\cdots+t^{a_i-1}).$$

The pure-power complete intersection has the same series, its basis being monomials with $0\le e_i<a_i$. Equality of formal-series coefficients gives equality of Hilbert functions in every degree. This proves that subcase directly, while leaving arbitrary extra generators to the credited split-factor theorem.

Earlier claim and remaining scope

The earlier revision reports a much broader unformalized 2026 OpenAI claim using division coefficients. This report does not validate that manuscript or infer that its all-sequence result is established. It supplies a checked classical theorem with its exact hypothesis and an explicit regularity criterion that makes the class testable. The general Hilbert-function comparison for arbitrary nonsplit sequences and extra generators is not proved here.
