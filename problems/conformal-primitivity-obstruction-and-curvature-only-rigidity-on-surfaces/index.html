Math-296

Conformal-primitivity obstruction and curvature-only rigidity on surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual critical-$Q$-curvature setting, fix an even dimension $n\geq2$ and consider closed oriented Riemannian manifolds. Let $S_g$ be a natural top-degree form, locally determined by the metric and finitely many derivatives, with \[ \int_M S_{e^{2\omega}g}=\int_M S_g\qquad(\omega\in C^\infty(M)). \] The source conjectures a universal decomposition \[ S_g=cQ_g+L_g+G_g. \] Here $Q_g$ denotes the critical $Q$-curvature density, $c$ is constant, $L_{e^{2\omega}g}=L_g$ is a local conformally invariant density, and $G$ has a local conformal primitive: a natural local density $F_g$ satisfying \[ \left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}\right|_{t=0}\int_M F_{e^{2t\omega}g} =\int_M\omega G_g \quad\text{for every }g,\omega. \] The extract does not state dimension parity, closedness, normalization of $Q$, or the exact allowed natural-invariant class; these are explicit interpretation requirements rather than verified missing words.

The source allows any preferred version of $Q$, saying that its ambiguity is addition of an $L$-term. For $\widehat g=e^{2c(\widehat g,g)}g$, its displayed elementary two-metric primitive for $L$ is \[ \tfrac12\int_M c(\widehat g,g)(L_{\widehat g}+L_g). \] This is a two-metric transgression; it is not the assertion that the invariant one-metric integral $\int L_g$ has nonzero variation. The source contrasts it with a more interesting primitive for $Q$, not defined in this extract.

The supplied aid reports an additional necessary variational condition. If $G$ is a smooth local conformal gradient as above, define $D_g\eta$ by \[ \left.\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}\right|_{0}G_{e^{2t\eta}g}=(D_g\eta)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g. \] Commuting second conformal variations requires formal self-adjointness: \[ \int_M\omega D_g\eta\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g =\int_M\eta D_g\omega\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_g\qquad(\omega,\eta\in C^\infty(M)). \] This Helmholtz condition is necessary, not asserted sufficient here. The aid notes that Alexakis's solution of the Deser--Schwimmer decomposition does not formally settle this exact conjecture: a divergence residual is not automatically a conformal variational gradient with the required local primitive.

It also reports a complete zero-derivative two-dimensional special case. If $f\in C^1({\mathbb{R}})$ and $\int_Mf(K_g)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A_g$ is conformally invariant for every metric on every closed connected oriented surface, then \[ f(t)=at\qquad(t\in{\mathbb{R}}) \] for one $a\in{\mathbb{R}}$; conversely these functions work. With normalization $Q_g=K_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A_g$ in dimension two, this gives precisely multiples of the $Q$-density. It does not classify densities involving curvature derivatives or prove the unrestricted decomposition.

Short English statement

The source conjectures that a natural density with conformally invariant integral splits into a $Q$-curvature term, a local conformal invariant and a term with a local variational primitive. The supplied aid distinguishes that primitive requirement from a divergence decomposition and classifies the curvature-only surface case.
