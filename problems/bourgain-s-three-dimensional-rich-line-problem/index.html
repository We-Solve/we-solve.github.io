Bourgain's three-dimensional rich-line problem

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge2$ be an integer, let $\mathcal L$ be a set of $n^2$ distinct affine lines in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$, and let $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be finite. Interpret "each containing $n$ points" as \[ |\ell\cap S|\ge n\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal L). \] Assume no $n$ lines are coplanar, equivalently each affine plane contains at most $n-1$ members of $\mathcal L$. J. Bourgain's source problem asks for a lower bound on $|S|$. Its OCR example "$n3-\le$" is interpreted by the aid as a request of the form $n^{3-\varepsilon}$; the exact exponent typography is not independently recovered here.

The supplied aid reports that the published Guth--Katz rich-line theorem gives the stronger sharp result: there is an absolute $c>0$ such that every configuration satisfying these hypotheses has \[ |S|\ge cn^3. \] The constant is independent of $n,S,\mathcal L$. Sharpness here means the power $3$, not an exact leading constant. The aid presents this as a supplied literature resolution, requiring verification of the cited theorem rather than a new proof.

A separate reported construction varies the coplanarity cap. For integers \[ n\ge2,\qquad n\le s\le n^2,\qquad s\mid n^2, \] the aid asserts a configuration of exactly $n^2$ distinct lines and a finite point set $S_{n,s}$ such that every line has $n$ selected incident points, every plane contains at most $s$ lines, and \[ |S_{n,s}|\le4n^{7/2}s^{-1/2}. \] "Selected" does not assert that the full set has exactly $n$ incidences on each line. The proof is said to use designated parallel block planes while also controlling every other plane, but coordinates and proof artifacts are absent. Since $s\ge n$, this construction has a relaxed cap and is not automatically an example satisfying the original strict $n-1$ cap, even when $s=n$. It is retained as an additional parameterized result, not conflated with the original theorem.

Short English statement

Bourgain's problem asks how many points are needed for $n^2$ lines in three-space, each rich in points, when fewer than $n$ lines lie in any plane. The supplied aid reports the sharp cubic lower bound from Guth--Katz and separately gives a construction for larger allowed plane caps.
