Planar Steklov Isospectral Domains

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "Do there exist non-isometric planar domains that are isospectral for the Steklov eigenvalue problem?" The admissible class of domains is not specified, so the following is a formulation template with unresolved hypotheses.

Let $\mathcal C$ be a specified class of planar domains $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ for which the unweighted Steklov problem \[ \begin{cases} \Delta u=0 & \text{in }\Omega,\\ \partial_{\nu}u=\sigma u & \text{on }\partial\Omega \end{cases} \] is well defined and has a discrete spectrum. Here $\nu$ is the outward unit normal, $\partial_\nu u$ is the normal derivative, and an eigenfunction $u$ must be nonzero and belong to the function space appropriate to the chosen boundary regularity and interpretation of the equations. Write $\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{St}}(\Omega)$ for the multiset of Steklov eigenvalues, including zero and counting multiplicities. Two such domains are Steklov isospectral when these multisets agree.

If "non-isometric" means that no Euclidean rigid motion carries one domain onto the other, the question takes the form \[ \exists\,\Omega_1,\Omega_2\in\mathcal C:\quad \operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{St}}(\Omega_1) =\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{St}}(\Omega_2) \quad\text{and}\quad \nexists\,Q\in O(2),\ b\in{\mathbb{R}}^2 \text{ such that }Q\Omega_1+b=\Omega_2. \] Here $O(2)=\{Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times2}:Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I\}$.

The source does not determine $\mathcal C$: boundedness, boundary regularity, connectedness, simple connectivity, and any boundary weight are not explicitly addressed. The displayed boundary condition uses the usual unweighted interpretation, which requires confirmation. The intended meaning of isometry also requires confirmation. Consequently, this template must not be read as selecting a smooth, bounded, simply connected, or otherwise restricted version of the problem.

Short English statement

The question asks whether two planar domains can have the same Steklov eigenvalues, with multiplicities, while being non-isometric. The permitted domains and precise boundary formulation require clarification. The supplied record labels the problem open, but its conflicting verification and literature notes do not establish a verified mathematical status.
