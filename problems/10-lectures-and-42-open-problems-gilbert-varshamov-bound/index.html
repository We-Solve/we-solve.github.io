Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A binary code of block length $n$ is a subset $C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$. Its minimum distance is the least Hamming distance between distinct codewords, where Hamming distance counts differing coordinates. Write $A_2(n,d)$ for the largest size of a binary code with minimum distance at least $d$, and define \[ R_2(\delta)=\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac1n\log_2 A_2(n,\lceil\delta n\rceil), \qquad 0<\delta<\tfrac12. \] The binary entropy function is $H_2(\delta)=-\delta\log_2\delta-(1-\delta)\log_2(1-\delta)$. The Gilbert--Varshamov bound asserts $R_2(\delta)\ge1-H_2(\delta)$.

The source asks two distinct questions. First, construct deterministic explicit code families attaining this rate: for each fixed $\delta$ and $\varepsilon>0$, obtain arbitrarily large block lengths with relative distance at least $\delta$ and rate at least $1-H_2(\delta)-\varepsilon$, together with a construction and encoding procedure polynomial in the block length. A linear construction can be specified by a generator matrix; nonlinear codes require another succinct, efficiently evaluable encoder.

Second, is $R_2(\delta)=1-H_2(\delta)$ throughout this interval, or can binary codes asymptotically exceed the bound? This extremal question allows nonlinear codes and places no computational restriction on them. Efficient decoding is a separate desirable property, not an extra hypothesis of the two stated questions. Exponential enumeration of all codewords does not supply the requested efficient explicitness.

Short English statement

Construct explicit binary codes attaining the Gilbert–Varshamov rate, and determine whether that rate is optimal among all binary codes.

Attempt and results

The two questions have different quantifiers. An efficiently explicit family requires a polynomial-time construction and encoder at arbitrarily large lengths for each fixed $0<\delta<1/2$ and $\varepsilon>0$. The extremal rate question permits every binary code, linear or nonlinear. Neither question is settled by enumerating exponentially many words.
Random linear coding and the attempted conditional-expectation route.
Let $G\in\mathbb F_2^{k\times n}$ have independent uniform entries. For each nonzero message $u\in\mathbb F_2^k$, the word $uG$ is uniform on $\mathbb F_2^n$: each column has a nonzero linear functional applied to it, so its output bit is uniform, and columns are independent. Let $B$ count nonzero $u$ with $w(uG)<d$. Then \[ \mathbb EB=(2^k-1)2^{-n}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom nj. \] If this is less than one, some $G$ has $B=0$. Such $G$ is injective on messages, since $d\ge1$ excludes a nonzero message mapping to zero, and its code has $2^k$ words and minimum distance at least $d$ by linearity. One can make this existence proof deterministic by revealing matrix entries and choosing the value that does not increase $\mathbb E[B\mid\text{revealed entries}]$. At each step the two conditional expectations average to the previous one. The final nonnegative integer $B$ is less than one, hence zero. However, the direct evaluator sums over $2^k-1$ messages. For each message, the revealed entries determine some output parities while the remaining random output coordinates are independent unbiased bits, allowing an exact binomial-tail computation. This gives an exponential-size straightforward algorithm, not the requested polynomial-time construction when $k=\Theta(n)$. The attempted derandomization lacks a polynomial-time representation and evaluation of that sum. No lower bound on all possible evaluators is claimed.
Entropy scale and why a polynomial factor does not settle it.
For $0<q<1/2$ and $r=\lfloor qn\rfloor$, put $t=q/(1-q)<1$. For $j\le r$, $t^j\ge t^r$, so the binomial theorem gives \[ \sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom nj\le t^{-r}(1+t)^n \le t^{-qn}(1+t)^n=2^{nH_2(q)}. \] This rederives the entropy exponent in the existence argument (with any fixed rate slack absorbing integer rounding). If a lower bound on code size is multiplied by a factor $f(n)$ with $1\le f(n)\le Cn^a$, the normalized base-two logarithm changes by at most $(\log_2 C+a\log_2 n)/n\to0$. Thus such an improvement of cardinality alone does not prove $R_2(\delta)>1-H_2(\delta)$.
A finite illustration, not an asymptotic construction.
Let $H$ be the $3$ by $7$ matrix whose columns are the seven distinct nonzero binary triples. Its rank is three, so $\ker H$ has dimension four. No word of weight one or two is in the kernel, since no column is zero and no two columns agree. Three columns $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ sum to zero, so the minimum distance is exactly three. This code has sixteen words, while the elementary volume expression is $2^7/(1+7+21)=128/29$. The strict finite improvement says nothing by itself about a positive asymptotic rate gap.

Context and adversarial audit

Jiang and Vardy's primary abstract, arXiv:math/0404325, was consulted on 11 October 2026. Its extra factor is proportional to the logarithm of a Hamming-ball volume, hence at most linear in block length. We do not adopt the source-row summary's inference that this settles asymptotic optimality of the binary entropy rate. Its finite-size improvement and the target rate question are kept separate. The calculations here are classical, reproduced to expose the failed inference and computational gap. The audit checked encoder succinctness, injectivity, fixed-rate slack, and linear versus nonlinear scope. No actual code enumeration or theorem prover was used.
