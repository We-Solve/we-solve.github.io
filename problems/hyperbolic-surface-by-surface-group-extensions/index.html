Math-865

Hyperbolic Surface-by-Surface Group Extensions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Sigma_g$ and $\Sigma_h$ denote closed connected orientable surfaces of genera $g,h$. The intended nontrivial surface-group setting normally has $g,h\ge2$, but the extraction does not state this range explicitly; it must be confirmed because allowing genus zero changes the existence question.

The source asks whether a word-hyperbolic group $\Gamma$ can fit into a short exact sequence \[ 1\longrightarrow\pi_1(\Sigma_g) \xrightarrow{\iota}\Gamma \xrightarrow{q}\pi_1(\Sigma_h) \longrightarrow1. \] Exactness means $\iota$ is injective, $q$ is surjective, and $\ker q=\operatorname{im}\iota$. No splitting of the extension is assumed. Word hyperbolicity means that a Cayley graph for a finite generating set has uniformly thin geodesic triangles; no realization as a negatively curved manifold is required.

There are two distinct questions:

  1. Does such an extension exist in the intended closed higher-genus setting? The bare existence wording does not specify whether $g,h$ are fixed inputs or whether some pair is sought. At minimum it asks for existence of an example; existence for every prescribed pair is a stronger assertion not silently added.
  2. If such groups exist, for each fixed admissible pair $(g,h)$ are there only finitely many such groups? With the usual group-isomorphism interpretation, this asks whether \[ \#\{[\Gamma]_{\cong}:\Gamma\text{ is word-hyperbolic and admits the displayed extension}\}<\infty. \] This counts abstract groups, not marked injections, quotient maps, or extension equivalence classes; the exact counting convention requires source confirmation.

The supplied assessment distinguishes known free-quotient or special convex-cocompact constructions from the requested closed-surface quotient. Such examples are not substituted for the stated quotient group. No uniform bound as the genera vary, no semidirect-product hypothesis, and no specific monodromy representation is added.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether a word-hyperbolic group can be an extension of one closed surface group by another, and whether only finitely many such group types occur for fixed genera. The genus range, existence quantifier, and equivalence used for counting must be checked.
