Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.29

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, its centre is \[ Z(G)=\{g\in G: gx=xg\text{ for every }x\in G\}. \] The group $G$ is locally nilpotent if every finitely generated subgroup of $G$ is nilpotent. Here a group $H$ is nilpotent if its lower central series, defined by $\gamma_1(H)=H$ and $\gamma_{r+1}(H)=[\gamma_r(H),H]$, reaches the identity subgroup after finitely many steps; $[A,B]$ denotes the subgroup generated by the commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$.

The supplied question asks whether there exists a locally nilpotent group $G$ such that $Z(G)=\{1\}$ and $G$ satisfies the weak maximal condition for normal subgroups.

The record does not define this last condition. A candidate interpretation, requiring confirmation against the source, is that there is no infinite ascending sequence \[ N_0<N_1<N_2<\cdots,\qquad N_i\trianglelefteq G, \] with $[N_{i+1}:N_i]$ infinite for every $i\geq 0$. Under this interpretation, the question is \[ \exists\,G\quad \begin{cases} \text{every finitely generated subgroup of }G\text{ is nilpotent},\\ Z(G)=\{1\},\\ \text{no infinite ascending sequence of normal subgroups of }G\\ \text{has all successive indices infinite}. \end{cases} \] This is a conditional formalization, pending verification of the named chain condition. The source also does not explicitly require $G\neq\{1\}$; whether nontriviality is intended must be checked before adopting a definitive statement.

Short English statement

L. A. Kurdachenko asks whether a locally nilpotent group can have trivial centre while satisfying the weak maximal condition for normal subgroups. The supplied record leaves the chain condition undefined and does not explicitly require the group to be nontrivial.
