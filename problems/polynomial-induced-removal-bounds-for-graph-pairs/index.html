Polynomial Induced Removal Bounds for Graph Pairs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not specify the graph category or the normalization of edit distance. The following gives a conditional formulation using finite simple undirected graphs and edge additions or deletions normalized by the square of the number of vertices; these conventions require source confirmation.

For a finite simple graph $H$, let $\mathcal P_H$ be the class of finite simple graphs containing no induced subgraph isomorphic to $H$. Put \[ \mathcal P_{H_1,H_2}=\mathcal P_{H_1}\cap\mathcal P_{H_2}. \] For a graph $G$ on $n\geq 1$ vertices and a graph property $\mathcal P$, define \[ d(G,\mathcal P)=\frac{1}{n^2}\min\left\{|E(G)\mathbin{\triangle}E(F)|:V(F)=V(G),\ F\in\mathcal P\right\}, \] with value $+\infty$ if the set over which the minimum is taken is empty. Under this convention, $G$ is $\eta$-close to $\mathcal P$ when $d(G,\mathcal P)\leq\eta$. The empty-set convention and the non-strict inequality are explicit reconstruction choices, not specifications supplied by the record.

For fixed $H_1,H_2$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, consider the admissible thresholds \[ \begin{split} D_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)=\bigl\{\delta\in[0,1]:\ &\text{for every finite simple graph }G,\\ &\bigl(d(G,\mathcal P_{H_1})\leq\delta\ \text{and}\ d(G,\mathcal P_{H_2})\leq\delta\bigr)\\ &\qquad\Longrightarrow d(G,\mathcal P_{H_1,H_2})\leq\varepsilon\bigr\}. \end{split} \] The two individual approximations may use different edited graphs; the conclusion requires one edited graph avoiding both forbidden induced subgraphs. The source defines $\delta_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)$ as the largest admissible threshold. Its existence as a maximum is not justified in the supplied record. A precise threshold version uses \[ \delta^*_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)=\sup D_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon). \] Here the restriction $\delta\in[0,1]$ is an explicit convention. When the source's largest threshold exists under these conventions, it equals $\delta^*_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)$.

Questions. With the preceding qualifications, the standard polynomial-lower-bound interpretation of the first question is: \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every fixed pair }H_1,H_2,\ \text{do there exist }c>0,\ C>0,\ \varepsilon_0\in(0,1)\ \text{such that}\\ \delta^*_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)\geq c\varepsilon^C \qquad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0)? \end{gathered} \] The second question is: \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every real }a>0,\ \text{do there exist fixed graphs }H_1,H_2,\ K>0, \ \varepsilon_0\in(0,1)\ \text{such that}\\ \delta^*_{H_1,H_2}(\varepsilon)\leq K\varepsilon^a \qquad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0)? \end{gathered} \] Thus the graphs in the second question may depend on $a$, but not on $\varepsilon$. The source does not explicitly define whether "polynomial in $\varepsilon$" means this lower bound, a two-sided polynomial estimate, or another formulation. Consequently these displayed questions are a conditional reconstruction, pending confirmation of that meaning and of the distance and threshold conventions.

Short English statement

If a graph can be edited a little to avoid $H_1$, and separately edited a little to avoid $H_2$, how much editing guarantees avoidance of both at once? The questions ask whether a polynomial threshold always suffices and whether pairs can require arbitrarily large powers of $\varepsilon$. The precise conventions and the meaning of "polynomial" require confirmation.
