Independent-set hardness with a three-colorability promise

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 106; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that three-colorable graphs are NP-hard to distinguish from graphs of any fixed sufficiently small independence ratio, implying hardness of finding any fixed larger number of colors under that promise.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For each fixed real $0<\delta<1/3$, the claimed deterministic polynomial-time reduction maps every $3$CNF formula $\varphi$ to a nonempty finite simple unweighted graph $G_{\varphi}$ such that \[ \varphi\text{ satisfiable}\Rightarrow\chi(G_{\varphi})\le3, \qquad\varphi\text{ unsatisfiable}\Rightarrow\alpha(G_{\varphi})<\delta|V(G_{\varphi})|. \] Here $\alpha$ is maximum independent-set size. The three-coloring is not supplied as part of the graph, and polynomial degree and constants may depend on $\delta$.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 106; 2026-09-24), vertices encode compatible tuple answers on layers. An independent set yields bounded subchain lists, and distributional alignment chooses a shared layer law so the coefficient test decodes a strategy contradicting the projection gap.
