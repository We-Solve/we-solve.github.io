Congruence Normality in Simplicial Arrangements

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathscr S$ denote the intended class of simplicial hyperplane arrangements. In the usual real setting, an arrangement is a collection of hyperplanes, its chambers are the connected components of their complement, and simpliciality means that its chambers are simplicial cones in the central essential case. The supplied record does not specify finiteness, centrality, essentiality, ambient dimension, or the treatment of nonessential or affine arrangements; this description therefore does not fix the scope of $\mathscr S$.

Write $\operatorname{CN}(\mathcal A)$, $\operatorname{Free}(\mathcal A)$, $\operatorname{Tight}(\mathcal A)$, and $\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal A)$ for congruence normality, freeness, tightness, and the shard digraph, respectively, in the conventions intended by the cited report. These are source-dependent terms: their definitions are not supplied in the record. In particular, the record does not specify any choice of base chamber or associated lattice used to define congruence normality and the shard digraph. Thus the following is a statement of the requested research targets, with unresolved conventions retained explicitly.

  1. Classify the arrangements $\mathcal A\in\mathscr S$ for which $\operatorname{CN}(\mathcal A)$ holds. The equivalence relation intended for this classification is not specified.
  2. Determine the relationship between $\operatorname{CN}(\mathcal A)$ and $\operatorname{Free}(\mathcal A)$ for $\mathcal A\in\mathscr S$, including which implications hold and under what hypotheses. No implication is asserted by the supplied statement.
  3. Identify useful properties of $\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal A)$ that can be described or interpreted in terms of the arrangement $\mathcal A$. The requested properties and the criterion of usefulness are not specified.
  4. Determine the smallest number of hyperplanes in a tight arrangement that is not congruence normal. More explicitly, if $\mathscr T$ denotes the intended class of finite tight arrangements, determine \[ \min\left\{\lvert\mathcal A\rvert:\mathcal A\in\mathscr T,\ \neg\operatorname{CN}(\mathcal A)\right\}, \] provided this set is nonempty, and determine whether such arrangements exist. Here $\lvert\mathcal A\rvert$ is the number of hyperplanes. The record does not specify whether $\mathscr T$ is restricted to simplicial arrangements, which ranks are allowed, or the precise meaning of tightness.
  5. Determine the behavior of congruence normality under restrictions and other arrangement operations. For a linear arrangement and a subspace $X$, the usual restriction is \[ \mathcal A^{X}=\{H\cap X:H\in\mathcal A,\ X\not\subseteq H\}, \] with repeated hyperplanes identified. The record does not specify the allowed subspaces $X$, whether this convention is intended, the other operations to consider, or hypotheses ensuring that the resulting arrangement lies in the domain of congruence normality. Preservation, reflection, and necessary additional hypotheses remain questions rather than asserted properties.

Short English statement

Classify congruence-normal simplicial arrangements, relate congruence normality to freeness, interpret properties of the shard digraph through arrangement theory, find the smallest tight counterexample to congruence normality, and study restrictions and other operations. The source leaves several definitions and scope choices unspecified, so this statement-only draft requires human mathematical review.
