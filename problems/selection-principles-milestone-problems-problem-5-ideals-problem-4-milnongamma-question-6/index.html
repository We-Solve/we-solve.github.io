Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a topological space, and give every subset $Y\subseteq X$ the subspace topology. Under the usual cover conventions, a Borel $\omega$-cover of $X$ is a countable family $\mathcal U$ of Borel subsets of $X$ such that \[ X\notin\mathcal U \quad\text{and}\quad \forall F\subseteq X\;\left(F\text{ finite}\Longrightarrow\exists U\in\mathcal U\;(F\subseteq U)\right). \] Write $\mathcal B_\Omega(X)$ for the collection of these covers. Borel sets and covers of a subspace $Y$ are understood relative to $Y$.

The property $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal B_\Omega,\mathcal B_\Omega)$ holds for $X$ if, for every sequence $(\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ from $\mathcal B_\Omega(X)$, there are choices $U_n\in\mathcal U_n$ such that \[ \{U_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\in\mathcal B_\Omega(X). \] The property $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal B_\Omega,\mathcal B_\Omega)$ holds for $X$ if, for every such sequence, there are finite subfamilies $\mathcal F_n\subseteq\mathcal U_n$ such that \[ \bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal F_n\in\mathcal B_\Omega(X). \] Here the union is a family of selected Borel sets.

For a property $P$, hereditariness means that every space under consideration with $P$ has $P$ on each of its subspaces. The two questions are whether one can prove, separately for \[ P\in\left\{\mathsf S_1(\mathcal B_\Omega,\mathcal B_\Omega),\ \mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal B_\Omega,\mathcal B_\Omega)\right\}, \] the implication \[ \forall X\;\left(P(X)\Longrightarrow\forall Y\subseteq X\;P(Y)\right), \] where $X$ ranges over the source's intended class of spaces. Thus the displayed formulation retains an unresolved domain qualification; it does not silently replace the intended class by all topological spaces. The cover definitions above use standard conventions that also require checking against the full source.

For context, the supplied source reports that none of the properties involving open covers considered in that paper is hereditary, whereas $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal B,\mathcal B)$ and all properties of the form $\Pi(\mathcal B_\Gamma,\mathcal B)$ are hereditary, citing [ideals]. It also reports that $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal B_\Omega,\mathcal B_\Gamma)$ is not hereditary, citing [MilNonGamma]. Here $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal B_\Gamma$ conventionally denote countable Borel covers and countably infinite Borel $\gamma$-covers, respectively; in a $\gamma$-cover each point belongs to all but finitely many members. Short English statement

For each of the two Borel $\omega$-cover selection properties, ask whether every subspace of a space having that property must also have it. The first property selects one set from each cover; the second selects finitely many. In either case, the selected sets must together form an $\omega$-cover. The intended class of spaces and the meaning of provability remain unspecified in the supplied excerpt.
