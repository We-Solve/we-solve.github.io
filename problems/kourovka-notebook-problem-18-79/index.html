Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.79

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a field and let $G$ be a finitely generated group. The group algebra $A=K[G]$ consists of finite formal sums $\sum_{g\in G}a_g g$, with coefficients in $K$, and multiplication induced by the group multiplication and extended bilinearly. Its Jacobson radical $\mathcal J(A)$ is the intersection of its maximal left ideals. A two-sided ideal $I$ of $A$ is nilpotent if $I^n=0$ for some integer $n\geq 1$. Define \[ N(A)=\sum_{\substack{I\trianglelefteq A\\ I\text{ a nilpotent two-sided ideal}}} I, \] where the sum denotes the smallest two-sided ideal containing all such ideals; each element of this sum is a finite sum of elements from those ideals.

Is it true that, for every field $K$ and every finitely generated group $G$, \[ \mathcal J(K[G])=N(K[G])? \] No restriction on the characteristic of $K$ is stated. The supplied source reports that the equality is known when $G$ is solvable or linear. This special-case assertion is retained as supplied, without independent verification. For the word "linear," the supplied record does not specify a representation field or its relation to $K$.

Short English statement

For every finitely generated group and every field, must the Jacobson radical of the group algebra equal the ideal generated by all its nilpotent two-sided ideals? The supplied record labels this question open and reports affirmative results for solvable or linear groups.
