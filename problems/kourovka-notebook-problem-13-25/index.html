Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.25

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(G,\tau)$ be a topological group, with identity element $e$. An ultrafilter on the underlying set of $G$ is a family $p\subseteq\mathcal P(G)$ such that $\varnothing\notin p$, $G\in p$, $p$ is closed under finite intersections and passage to supersets, and for every $A\subseteq G$, exactly one of $A$ and $G\setminus A$ belongs to $p$. It converges to $e$ if every $\tau$-neighborhood of $e$ belongs to $p$.

For ultrafilters $p,q$ on $G$, use the standard product determined by \[ A\in p\cdot q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{x\in G:x^{-1}A\in q\}\in p, \qquad A\subseteq G, \] where $x^{-1}A=\{y\in G:xy\in A\}$. Under the usual interpretation, write \[ \tau(G)=\{p:p\text{ is an ultrafilter on }G\text{ converging to }e\}, \] with this multiplication. The topology here is the given topology $\tau$; the ultrafilters are on the full power set of the underlying group.

Question (E. G. Zelenyuk, 1995). For every topological group $(G,\tau)$ such that $\tau(G)$ is finite, must \[ \forall p\in\tau(G),\qquad p\cdot p=p \] hold? Thus the requested conclusion is that every element of $\tau(G)$ is idempotent; commutativity is not requested.

Qualifications requiring review. The supplied record refers to Protasov's paper for the ultrafilter semigroup construction without giving its definitions. The displayed multiplication is the standard interpretation and should be checked against that reference. The record does not specify whether the principal ultrafilter at $e$ is included. Including or excluding that ultrafilter does not change finiteness or the assertion that every element is idempotent, since it is the identity for ultrafilter multiplication. No separation axiom for the topological group is explicitly supplied; none is added here.

Supplied comment of 2005. The record reports that the assertion was proved when the underlying group $G$ is countable, citing E. G. Zelenyuk (2000). This is a supplied historical qualification, not an independently verified result.

Short English statement

E. G. Zelenyuk asks whether a finite semigroup of ultrafilters converging to the identity of a topological group must consist entirely of idempotents. The supplied 2005 comment reports a positive answer for countable groups. The precise ultrafilter convention and any intended separation assumptions require source review.
