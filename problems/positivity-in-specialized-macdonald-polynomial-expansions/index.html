Math-7334

Positivity in Specialized Macdonald-Polynomial Expansions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A partition $\lambda=(\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_\ell>0)$ has size $|\lambda|=\sum_i\lambda_i$. Let $X=(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$ be a formal alphabet, let $q$ be an indeterminate, and write $s_\mu(X)$ for the Schur symmetric function indexed by a partition $\mu$. For a homogeneous symmetric function $F$ of degree $n$ over ${\mathbb{Q}}(q)$, define its Schur coefficients by \[ F=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_\mu(q)s_\mu(X),\qquad [s_\mu]F=a_\mu(q). \] Here ${\mathbb{N}}[q]$ means polynomials in $q$ with nonnegative integer coefficients.

The supplied question is: for every partition $\lambda\vdash n$ and every $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$, does \[ [s_\lambda]\!\left(\frac{J_\lambda(X;q,q^k)}{(1-q)^n}\right)\in{\mathbb{N}}[q] \] hold? The same partition $\lambda$ indexes both $J_\lambda$ and the Schur coefficient being requested.

The symbol $J_\lambda(X;q,t)$ denotes the Macdonald symmetric function referred to in the supplied record, but that record does not specify its normalization or any alphabet convention beyond the displayed formula. Thus the question above is conditional on the intended meaning of $J_\lambda$ and on the specialization being defined. The record also leaves unspecified whether $0\in{\mathbb{N}}$ for the parameter $k$, and whether the empty partition is included. Its dated background assessment says that the actual conjectural statement is missing, whereas its statement-verification field reports that the displayed question was checked against the official report. These conflicting source qualifications remain unresolved.

Short English statement

For each partition $\lambda$ of $n$ and natural number $k$, the supplied question asks whether the coefficient of $s_\lambda$ in $J_\lambda(X;q,q^k)/(1-q)^n$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. The normalization and parameter boundary conventions require clarification, and the supplied source assessments conflict about the completeness of this formulation.
