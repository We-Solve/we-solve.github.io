Math-1404

Random central planes and approximate convexity

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq2$ and let $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be compact with $0<|S|<\infty$, where $|\cdot|$ is $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Assume its volume centroid is the origin: $|S|^{-1}\int_S x\,dx=0$. Let $\mathcal K$ consist of all compact convex sets with nonempty interior and also $\varnothing$, and let $S\mathbin{\triangle}C=(S\setminus C)\cup(C\setminus S)$. The source chooses \[ C_*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{C\in\mathcal K}|S\mathbin{\triangle}C| \] and calls $S$ $\eta$-convex if $|S\mathbin{\triangle}C_*|\leq\eta|S|$. Equivalently one may describe the optimal normalized discrepancy $\inf_{C\in\mathcal K}|S\mathbin{\triangle}C|/|S|$; replacing attainment by an infimum alone would need care at an exact endpoint.

Choose $X,Y$ independently and uniformly with respect to volume on $S$. Almost surely they span a two-dimensional linear subspace $H(X,Y)$; put $P(X,Y)=S\cap H(X,Y)$. Denote by $\mu_S$ the resulting distribution on central two-planes, or equivalently the induced distribution of these sections. Santosh Vempala's proposed implication is that there is an absolute constant $A>0$ such that, for all such $n,S$ and tolerances $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$, \[ \Pr\left(P(X,Y)\text{ is convex}\right)\geq1-\varepsilon \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |S\mathbin{\triangle}C_*|\leq A n\varepsilon |S|. \] This makes the source's $O(n\varepsilon)$ conclusion explicit with a constant independent of $n$, $S$, and $\varepsilon$. The positive-volume, dimension, tolerance-range, and independence conventions are needed to define the sampling statement; they are not spelled out in the damaged excerpt. Convexity in the premise concerns the actual section as a set, whereas the conclusion measures discrepancy only up to null sets.

The supplied machine aid emphasizes that $\mu_S$ is generally not Haar measure on $G(n,2)$. Relative to the rotation-invariant probability measure $\nu$, it reports a density proportional to \[ W_S(H)=\int_{(S\cap H)^2}[x,y]^{n-2}\,dx\,dy, \qquad [x,y]=\sqrt{\|x\|_2^2\|y\|_2^2-\langle x,y\rangle^2}, \] where integration within $H$ uses two-dimensional Lebesgue measure; thus the normalization is $d\mu_S(H)=W_S(H)d\nu(H)/\int W_S\,d\nu$.

The aid also reports the following robust missing-chord bound. If $B(a,\rho),B(b,\rho)\subseteq S$ while $B((a+b)/2,\rho)\cap S=\varnothing$, with $\rho>0$ and $\kappa_n=|B(0,1)|$, then \[ \Pr\left(P(X,Y)\text{ is not convex}\right) \geq\left(\frac{\kappa_n\rho^n}{|S|}\right)^2. \] It consequently reports exact convexity from zero failure probability for full-dimensional regular-closed compact sets, where regular closed means $S=\overline{\operatorname{int}S}$. In dimension two the sampled plane is almost surely ${\mathbb{R}}^2$, so the failure probability is zero or one according as $S$ is convex or not. These restricted facts are retained as supplied partial progress; the regular-closed assumption is not silently inserted into the original compact-set conjecture.

Short English statement

Sample two volume-uniform points and examine the central plane they span. The conjecture says that if almost all these sections are convex, then the set differs from a convex body by only an $O(n\varepsilon)$ fraction of its volume. The sampling favors planes according to the set itself. The supplied partial results describe this weighting and robust nonconvexity, with a separate regularity assumption for exact setwise convexity.
