The Generalized Star Height Problem

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 134; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that for an arbitrary finite alphabet and a regular language on it, a generalized expression needs at most thirteen nested star levels; In manuscript 2 (family 134; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that every regular language has generalized star height at most four without changing its alphabet; In manuscript 3 (family 134; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that three nested star levels suffice to express any regular language using Boolean operations and complementation in its original free monoid.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
A generalized regular expression may use union, concatenation, complementation and Kleene star. Its star height is the greatest nesting depth of star, and $h_{\Sigma}(L)$ is the least such height among expressions for $L$. The claim is the uniform bound \[ \forall\text{ finite }\Sigma\ \forall\text{ regular }L\subseteq\Sigma^*:\quad h_{\Sigma}(L)\le13. \] The alphabet and language may vary; the numerical bound stays fixed.
Manuscript 2.
If $h_{\Sigma}(L)$ is the minimum Kleene-star nesting depth for expressions formed using union, concatenation, complement and star, the claimed universal result is \[ \forall\text{ finite }\Sigma\ \forall\text{ regular }L\subseteq\Sigma^*:\quad h_{\Sigma}(L)\le4. \] The bound concerns generalized expressions, for which complement is permitted.
Manuscript 3.
For a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ and regular language $L$, minimize star nesting depth over expressions using union, concatenation, complementation and Kleene star. The manuscript's claimed bound is \[ h_{\Sigma}(L)\le3\qquad\text{for every such }\Sigma,L. \] Thus a single integer bounds the generalized star height across all regular languages and finite alphabets.

Bounded Generalized Star Height

Definitions and mathematical statement

Research attempt: the exact unary case and the limits of state elimination

Status. The universal-alphabet boundedness question remains unresolved here. We prove that every unary regular language has generalized star height at most one and that this bound is attained. The inspected thesis [S2] treats the generalized problem; its terminology includes complement, unlike ordinary star height.

1. Following a unary automaton to its eventual cycle

Let $L\subseteq\{a\}^*$ be accepted by a deterministic automaton with $q$ states. Write $s_n$ for the state reached after $a^n$. Among $s_0,\ldots,s_q$ two agree, say $s_\mu=s_{\mu+p}$ with $0\leq\mu<\mu+p\leq q$. Determinism implies $s_{n+p}=s_n$ for all $n\geq\mu$. Let $E$ be the finite accepted lengths below $\mu$, and let \[R=\{r:0\leq r<p,\ s_{\mu+r}\text{ is accepting}\}.\] Then the exact expression \[L=\bigcup_{n\in E}\{a^n\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{r\in R} a^{\mu+r}(a^p)^*\tag{1}\] has no nested stars. In $1$, powers denote literal finite concatenations, not iteration operators. Empty $E$ or $R$ give empty unions. The construction is effective with $\mu+p\leq q$ and uses no complementation.

2. Why height zero cannot do the same job

Every star-free expression over a unary alphabet defines a finite or cofinite set of words. Prove this by structural induction. The atomic languages are finite. Union and complement preserve the family of finite/cofinite sets. For concatenation identify words with their lengths in $\mathbb N_0$, so concatenation is sumset. Two finite sets have finite sumset. If one factor is empty the sumset is empty. A nonempty finite set plus a cofinite set is cofinite, since one translate already contains all sufficiently large integers. Two cofinite sets also have cofinite sumset: fix one element in one factor and translate the other. This verifies the induction for every operation allowed at height zero.

Conversely a finite language is a finite union of literal words, and a cofinite one is the complement of such a union. Hence unary generalized height zero is exactly finite/cofinite. The language $\{a^{2n}:n\geq0\}$ is neither finite nor cofinite, so its generalized height is exactly one. The bound in the unary theorem is sharp, not merely an artifact of $1$.

3. Why an automaton-size bound is not a universal constant

For a general finite alphabet, state elimination represents paths using recurrence \[R^{(k)}_{ij}=R^{(k-1)}_{ij}\ \cup\ R^{(k-1)}_{ik}(R^{(k-1)}_{kk})^*R^{(k-1)}_{kj},\] where paths at stage $k$ may use only the first $k$ states internally. This gives a finite expression, but the newly introduced star may contain earlier stars. The recurrence therefore supplies a bound growing with the number of eliminated states, not a constant independent of the input automaton. Having complement available does not automatically flatten that nesting; an identity doing so must be proved.

4. Checks and residual obstruction

The diagnostic enumerates all unary deterministic automata with at most four states, initial state 0 and every accepting subset. It compares direct acceptance with the periodic representation $1$ for lengths through $4q+10$. The proof, rather than that finite test, establishes equality at all lengths. The height-zero argument is deliberately unary: over two letters there are infinite noncofinite star-free languages, for instance all words containing the letter $a$, expressible using the complement of the empty language and concatenation. Thus one cannot extend the finite/cofinite characterization to larger alphabets. A universal bound on generalized height for arbitrary alphabets and arbitrary transition structures is the exact missing conclusion.

Write-up

A decidable height-zero class: all languages with aperiodic syntactic monoid

Claim type: Literature report

Exact scope: A regular language has generalized star height zero exactly when its finite syntactic monoid is aperiodic; from any DFA this property is decidable and a star-free expression can be constructed by a terminating search.

The substantial equivalence is Schützenberger’s 1965 theorem [1]: the languages obtained from the empty language, singleton letters and the singleton empty-word language by union, complement relative to Σ*, and concatenation are exactly the languages recognized by finite monoids having no nontrivial subgroup. On a finite alphabet, that expression class is precisely generalized star height zero. Equivalently one may start with Σ* as an additional constant and use difference instead of complement; in the complement formulation Σ* is simply the complement of the empty language. The theorem is credited literature; its algebraic inductive proof is not reproduced.

Here is the effective consequence, including its stopping justification. Given a complete DFA, remove inaccessible states and minimize it. Its transition monoid M is obtained by composing the finitely many letter transformations until closure; there are at most q^q transformations on its q states. For a minimal DFA this is the syntactic monoid. A finite transformation monoid is aperiodic exactly when f^q=f^{q+1} for every f∈M. Indeed any eventual transformation cycle of length greater than one prevents that equality; if every transformation has only fixed-point cycles, its functional graph enters its fixed points within q steps. Equivalently every generated cyclic subsemigroup is eventually constant, which rules out nontrivial subgroups and is forced by their absence.

The finite closure and these power comparisons decide aperiodicity. If it fails, the imported theorem certifies that the language does not have generalized height zero. If it holds, enumerate finite star-free expressions over the alphabet, construct their finite automata by the usual Boolean and concatenation constructions, and test language equivalence with the input DFA by product-automaton reachability. The theorem guarantees that some expression matches, so this search halts in every positive case. Together these procedures give both a decision algorithm and a constructive star-free description for the declared class. No elementary complexity or expression-size bound is asserted.

This covers arbitrary finite alphabets, not just unary languages. For example languages requiring a fixed sequence of letters as a subsequence have star-free descriptions Σ* a_1 Σ* … a_r Σ*, with each Σ* written as a complement rather than a star. Their natural prefix-progress automata have nondecreasing state transitions, so every word transformation has only fixed-point cycles, consistent with the criterion.

The argument does not put all regular languages at height zero, and it does not bound their general heights. Nontrivial group behavior in the syntactic monoid is a precise obstruction to zero, not a proof that arbitrarily high generalized height is necessary.

Relation to earlier contributions. The earlier exploratory contribution proves the exact unary height-one case, while another record announces broad unverified claims. The present primary theorem and explicit decision/construction procedure treat the aperiodic class over arbitrary alphabets and identify exactly when height zero is possible.
