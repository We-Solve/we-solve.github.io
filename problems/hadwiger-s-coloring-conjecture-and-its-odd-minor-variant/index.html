OpenAI claim summary for Math-2452

The counterexample manuscripts claim graphs with $\chi(G)>h(G)$. Since every odd clique minor is also an ordinary clique minor, these examples violate both coloring assertions on this page. The list-coloring manuscript separately supplies the weaker estimate $\chi(G)\le C h(G)$; that estimate alone neither proves nor refutes either coefficient-one assertion.

Hadwiger’s Coloring Conjecture and Its Odd-Minor Variant

OpenAI's claim

Chromatic number beyond clique-minor size: The counterexample paper claims arbitrarily large finite graphs with $\chi_f(G)>h(G)$, hence also $\chi(G)>h(G)$; no independent vertex set in an example contains three vertices.

Colin de Verdière chromatic obstruction: The manuscript claims finite graphs whose fractional chromatic number is greater than $\mu(G)+1$, and therefore whose ordinary chromatic number violates the same bound; such examples exist at arbitrarily large orders.

Linear list-coloring in clique-minor size: The paper claims a universal constant $C$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C h(G)$ for every finite nonempty graph; the factor is independent of the graph and its order.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
There are finite simple graphs $G$ of arbitrarily large order $m$ with independence number $\alpha(G)\le2$ and maximum size of a pairwise-touching matching less than $m/100$. The resulting corollary states \[h(G)<26m/75+2/3<m/2\le\chi_f(G)\le\chi(G),\] where $h(G)=\max\{t:K_t\text{ is a minor of }G\}$ and $\chi_f,\chi$ are fractional and ordinary chromatic numbers. Thus the exact inequalities $\chi\le h$ and $\chi_f\le h$ fail; no conclusion about replacing $h$ by a fractional minor parameter is supplied.
Mathematical claim 2.
The claimed counterexamples are finite simple graphs of arbitrarily large order $m$ with \[\alpha(G)\le2,\qquad\mu(G)+1<m/2\le\chi(G),\] where $\mu(G)$ is the Colin de Verdière spectral graph invariant. The fractional-coloring corollary strengthens the obstruction to \[\chi_f(G)\ge m/\alpha(G)\ge m/2>\kappa(G)+1\ge\mu(G)+1\ge h(G).\] Here $\kappa$ is its strong-Arnold-property-free spectral relaxation, and $h$ is the largest complete-minor order. In particular both $\chi\le\mu+1$ and $\chi_f\le h$ are refuted. A further corollary gives $\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C(\mu(G)+1)$ with an absolute $C$.
Mathematical claim 3.
For every finite nonempty simple graph $G$ there is an absolute integer $C\ge1$, independent of $G$, such that \[\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C h(G),\qquad h(G)=\max\{t:K_t\text{ is a minor of }G\}.\] The list chromatic number is the least $k$ for which every assignment of $k$ available colors per vertex permits a proper coloring. Consequently $\chi(G)\le Ch(G)$. The constant-factor bound does not imply the conjectured coefficient $1$.

Outline of the argument

Chromatic number beyond clique-minor size: The graph construction uses Boolean moment representations and paired frames to control compatible vertex distributions. Entropy projection, fingerprints for independent sets, and a capacitated-cut argument transfer the distributional constraints to finite graphs. The resulting obstruction limits clique-minor size, while the small independence number gives a larger fractional chromatic number.

Colin de Verdière chromatic obstruction: Allowed channel images are thinned while preserving the primal distribution, supplying enough compatible pairs for the conflict estimates. A Boolean moment argument restricts low-rank matrix representations. Entropy and finite sampling then produce graphs for which every well-signed matrix with a single negative eigenvalue has too much rank to support the proposed chromatic inequality.

Linear list-coloring in clique-minor size: For small minor-free graphs, a random global partition of colors gives each vertex many eligible bins. Stable-set sampling repeatedly reduces fractional chromatic complexity among eligible vertices. If the reduction failed, separator and connectivity arguments would assemble disjoint clique models into the excluded minor; the resulting small-graph bound feeds the global list-coloring argument.
