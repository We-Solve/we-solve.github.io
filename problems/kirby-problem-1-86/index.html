Kirby Problem 1.86

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite graph. For a tame embedding $f:\Gamma\hookrightarrow S^3$, use its compact exterior \[ E(f\Gamma)=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(f\Gamma), \] where $N$ is a regular neighbourhood; this specifies the usual meaning of graph complement in the supplied handlebody discussion. A handlebody is a compact orientable 3-manifold homeomorphic to a regular neighbourhood of a finite connected graph. Following the background, call an embedded graph unknotted when its exterior is a connected sum of handlebodies, allowing the disconnected-subgraph case through that convention. The source does not explicitly fix the embedding category or all empty-graph conventions.

For $D\subseteq E(\Gamma)$, write $\Gamma-D$ for deletion of the interiors of the selected edges, retaining their endpoints and hence possible isolated vertices. The intended up-to-$k$-deletion reading is \[ f\text{ is }k\text{-free}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall D\subseteq E(\Gamma),\ |D|\leq k:\ f(\Gamma-D)\text{ is unknotted}. \] It includes $D=\varnothing$. This explicitly interprets the background's duplicated malformed subgraph phrase, without changing the source record.

An unbounded-$k$ definition is \[ \operatorname{fr}(\Gamma)=\sup\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}:\exists f:\Gamma\hookrightarrow S^3\text{ that is }k\text{-free}\}. \] One must permit $\infty$ when the admissible $k$ are unbounded; deletion conditions stabilize once $k\geq|E(\Gamma)|$. The background calls the invariant an integer but does not state a cap on $k$ or an infinity convention. Capping at the number of edges would change small-graph readings of the problem, so no such cap is silently imposed.

With a consistent convention for this index, the three supplied assertions are:

  1. Every finite connected cubic graph, meaning degree three at every vertex, has $\operatorname{fr}(\Gamma)\geq2$. The source does not restrict this part to bridgeless graphs.
  2. Every finite graph has $\operatorname{fr}(\Gamma)\geq2$, including the scope of disconnected graphs intended by the source.
  3. There exists a finite graph with $\operatorname{fr}(\Gamma)=2$, not merely index at least two.
The source defines the invariant first for connected graphs but then asks part (b) for every graph; the extension and small-case conventions need source review.

The supplied background and triage dated 2026-08-17 attribute to Thompson the universal lower bound one, and report $\operatorname{fr}(K_6)=8$ and Petersen-graph index 4. Neither is an exact-index-two example. They also report index at least two for cubic graphs satisfying the orientable cycle double cover conjecture. For bridgeless cubic graphs the background describes that conjecture as a strong embedding in a closed orientable surface, with complementary regions disks whose closures are disks; it says a sufficiently suitable Heegaard-surface realization in $S^3$ yields the conditional freeness result. Thus a connected bridgeless cubic graph of index one would refute that conjecture; no such graph is supplied. The background contrasts planar examples and $K_5$, which can have all subgraphs unknotted, with Conway--Gordon's obstruction for $K_7$. The registry status is open and the dated triage labels the row partially solved, retaining all three target assertions as unproved in general.

Short English statement

Can every graph be embedded in the 3-sphere so that deleting any two edges still leaves an unknotted spatial graph, and does some graph have exactly this maximum degree of freeness? The cubic case has a supplied conditional result from orientable cycle double covers. The deletion, embedding, disconnected-graph and infinity conventions need explicit source alignment.
