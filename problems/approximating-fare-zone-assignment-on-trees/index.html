Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Given a tree $G=(V,E)$ and weighted commodity paths $(P_i,w_i,u_i)$, choose $y_e\in\{0,1\}$ to maximize $\sum_{e\in E}y_e\sum_{i:e\in P_i}w_i$ subject to $\sum_{e\in P_i}y_e\leq u_i$ for every $i$. What approximation guarantees are achievable on trees, particularly when the maximum number of paths sharing an edge is bounded by a parameter $k$? [S2]

Attempted bounded-congestion exchange route

We use the displayed hard-budget formulation, in the restricted but natural case of nonnegative rational path weights and nonnegative integer budgets. Delete any edge lying on a zero-budget path; those edges can never be selected. Put $c_e=\sum_{i:e\in P_i}w_i\ge0$ and assume the congestion bound $k\ge1$. Repeatedly select a highest-weight edge whose addition is feasible, until the selected set $A$ is inclusion-maximal. This is a polynomial-time greedy algorithm by checking the listed path budgets.
Proposition: The resulting weight is at least $1/k$ times the optimum weight.
Proof: Suppose $A\subseteq B$ are feasible and $A+e$ is feasible. Adding $e$ to $B$ violates at most $k$ budgets, since only paths containing $e$ can be violated. For each budget still violated, remove one edge of $(B\setminus A)\cap P_i$. Such an edge exists: $B$ already met that budget, its violation is by exactly one, and $A+e$ meets it. At most $k$ removals make $B+e$ feasible without deleting $A$. Start $B$ at an optimum $O$ and maintain a feasible $B$ containing the current greedy prefix. If the chosen $e$ is already in $B$, no exchange is needed; otherwise use the preceding removals and insert $e$. Every removed edge was individually feasible for addition to the previous prefix, hence has weight at most $c_e$. Charge its weight to $e$. An original optimum edge chosen while still in $B$ is charged to itself instead. These cases charge at most $k$ original optimum edges per greedy edge (at most one in the latter case). At termination, no unchosen edge can remain in $B$, because it could be added to the maximal greedy set. Thus every original optimum edge has been charged, and $c(O)\le k c(A)$. ∎
This is the standard elementary exchange argument for column-sparse cardinality packing, proved here to fix scope; no novelty or original-target completion is claimed. It does not use the tree structure beyond the congestion bound. The next attempted improvement is to round the natural LP as if it were integral. A star with three edges and a unit-weight, unit-budget path between every pair of leaves defeats that step. Each edge has weight two; the three pair constraints permit $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$, of value three, while an integer feasible solution chooses at most one edge, of value two. The tight-constraint matrix has determinant two, so the fractional point is an LP vertex.

Verification and remaining gaps

All eight binary choices for this star and the three tight LP constraints were checked. A better tree-specific approximation, optimal dependence on congestion, and stronger rounding remain unproved. The newer fare-zone paper cited by the catalogue uses dropout and pricing, which cannot silently replace this hard-budget problem.
