Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A Turing degree is an equivalence class of subsets of ${\mathbb{N}}$ under mutual Turing computability; write $\mathbf a\leq_T\mathbf b$ for the induced ordering. The supplied record does not define degrees of categoricity or degrees of decidable categoricity. The following makes the question precise under the conventional presentation-based interpretation; its agreement with the source terminology requires review.

Work with countable structures in computable first-order languages, presented on computable domains contained in ${\mathbb{N}}$. A presentation is computable if its atomic diagram is computable, and decidable if its elementary diagram (all first-order formulas with parameters that hold in the presentation) is computable. An isomorphism is computable in a degree $\mathbf d$ if its graph is computable relative to a representative of $\mathbf d$.

For a computable structure $\mathcal A$, let $C_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf d)$ mean that every computable presentation $\mathcal B\cong\mathcal A$ admits an isomorphism $\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ computable in $\mathbf d$. A degree $\mathbf d$ is a degree of categoricity if there exists such an $\mathcal A$ for which \[ C_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf d)\quad\text{and}\quad \forall\mathbf e\,\left(C_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf e)\Longrightarrow\mathbf d\leq_T\mathbf e\right). \] For a decidable structure $\mathcal A$, let $D_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf d)$ mean that every decidable presentation $\mathcal B\cong\mathcal A$ admits an isomorphism $\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ computable in $\mathbf d$. Under the interpretation used here, $\mathbf d$ is a degree of decidable categoricity if there exists such a decidable $\mathcal A$ for which \[ D_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf d)\quad\text{and}\quad \forall\mathbf e\,\left(D_{\mathcal A}(\mathbf e)\Longrightarrow\mathbf d\leq_T\mathbf e\right). \] In both definitions, least degree, rather than merely a minimal degree, is required, and the isomorphism may depend on the presentation.

The source states that every degree of decidable categoricity is a degree of categoricity. Its main question is whether \[ \forall\mathbf d\;\left(\mathbf d\text{ is a degree of categoricity} \Longrightarrow \mathbf d\text{ is a degree of decidable categoricity}\right). \] The witnessing structures in the two definitions need not be the same.

The source further conjectures that this implication holds for degrees $\mathbf d\geq_T\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)}$, where $\alpha$ is a sufficiently large computable ordinal, and suggests $\alpha=\omega$. Here $\mathbf 0^{(\alpha)}$ denotes the degree of the transfinite iterated Turing jump of the empty set, using an effective ordinal notation; in particular, \[ \mathbf 0^{(\omega)}=\deg_T\!\left(\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\emptyset^{(n)}\right). \] The explicit suggested case asks whether \[ \forall\mathbf d\geq_T\mathbf 0^{(\omega)}\;\left(\mathbf d\text{ is a degree of categoricity} \Longrightarrow \mathbf d\text{ is a degree of decidable categoricity}\right). \] The phrase "sufficiently large" supplies no definite ordinal threshold or precise quantification over ordinal notations.

Finally, a d.c.e. degree is a Turing degree containing a set $U\setminus V$, where $U,V\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}$ are computably enumerable. The source asks what happens for d.c.e. degrees $\mathbf d$. In the context of its main question, this asks whether every d.c.e. degree that is a degree of categoricity is also a degree of decidable categoricity; the wording does not specify whether a broader classification is intended.

Short English statement

Does every degree of categoricity also occur as a degree of decidable categoricity? The source conjectures an affirmative answer above a sufficiently high computable jump, suggesting $\mathbf 0^{(\omega)}$, and separately asks about d.c.e. degrees. The definitions used here are an explicit conventional interpretation requiring source confirmation. The registry labels the problem open; this is supplied metadata, not an independently verified status assessment.
