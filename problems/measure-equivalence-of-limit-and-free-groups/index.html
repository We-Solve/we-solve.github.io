Measure Equivalence of Limit and Free Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard convention that a limit group is a finitely generated fully residually free group: a finitely generated group $G$ such that for every finite subset $S\subseteq G\setminus\{1_G\}$, there is a homomorphism $\phi:G\to F_2$ satisfying $\phi(s)\ne 1_{F_2}$ for every $s\in S$, where $F_2$ is the free group on two generators.

Two countable groups $G$ and $H$ are measure equivalent if there exists a nonzero standard $\sigma$-finite measure space $(\Omega,\mu)$ with commuting, measure-preserving, essentially free actions of $G$ and $H$, each admitting a measurable fundamental domain of finite positive measure. Here essential freeness means that almost every point has trivial stabilizer for the respective action. A fundamental domain for the $G$-action is a measurable set $D_G\subseteq\Omega$ whose translates $\{gD_G:g\in G\}$ partition $\Omega$ up to null sets; the definition for $H$ is analogous.

For an at most countable set $B$, write $F(B)$ for the free group with basis $B$, represented by reduced words in the symbols $B\cup B^{-1}$. The source does not restrict the rank of the target free group. Accordingly, the formulation below permits any at most countable basis, including the empty basis and a one-element basis; any intended restriction to nonabelian or finite-rank targets would require clarification.

Question. Is it true that for every limit group $G$, there exists an at most countable set $B$ such that $G$ and $F(B)$ are measure equivalent? \[ \forall G\;\left(G\text{ is a limit group}\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists B\text{ at most countable}:\ G\text{ is measure equivalent to }F(B)\right). \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify conventions for trivial or abelian limit groups. The fully residually free convention stated above includes them; any intended exclusion remains to be checked against the original report.

Short English statement

Does every finitely generated fully residually free group admit a measure-equivalence coupling with some free group? Such a coupling consists of commuting essentially free measure-preserving actions on one measure space, with a finite positive measure fundamental domain for each action. The supplied statement leaves the target free group's rank unspecified.
