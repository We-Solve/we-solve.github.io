Math-15775

Invariant Orders on Finitely Generated Infinite Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a finitely generated infinite group. A partial order on $\Gamma$ is a relation $\preceq$ that is reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive: for all $x,y,z\in\Gamma$, \[ x\preceq x,\qquad (x\preceq y\ \text{and}\ y\preceq x)\Longrightarrow x=y,\qquad (x\preceq y\ \text{and}\ y\preceq z)\Longrightarrow x\preceq z. \] It is a total order if, additionally, every $x,y\in\Gamma$ satisfy $x\preceq y$ or $y\preceq x$. A partial or total order is left-invariant if \[ x\preceq y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad gx\preceq gy \qquad(g,x,y\in\Gamma), \] and bi-invariant if \[ x\preceq y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad gxh\preceq gyh \qquad(g,h,x,y\in\Gamma). \] Question as supplied. For a finitely generated infinite group $\Gamma$, does there exist an invariant order relation on $\Gamma$? Consider separately the four combinations \[ \begin{array}{c|cc} & \text{partial order} & \text{total order}\\ \hline \text{left-invariant} & \text{existence?} & \text{existence?}\\ \text{bi-invariant} & \text{existence?} & \text{existence?} \end{array} \] Unresolved qualifications. Under the definitions above, the equality relation is an admissible bi-invariant partial order. The supplied record does not specify whether partial orders must be nontrivial, for example by requiring distinct comparable elements. Such a requirement is therefore not imposed here, but its absence prevents identifying a substantive intended partial-order question. The record also does not specify whether the target is universal existence for all finitely generated infinite groups, an existence criterion for a given such group, or a question restricted to a particular class of groups suggested by the workshop context. No additional restriction is inferred from that context.

The supplied literature assessment says that the report distinguished partial and bi-invariant orders and stated that nonexistence of a bi-invariant partial order had not been proved even for $\mathrm{SL}(3,{\mathbb{Z}})$, the group of determinant-one $3\times3$ integer matrices. This is retained as a supplied source qualification, not as an independently verified assertion. In particular, its intended meaning requires clarification of the partial-order convention.

Short English statement

The question asks about left-invariant and bi-invariant orders on a finitely generated infinite group, considering both total and partial orders. The intended group quantifier and any nontriviality requirement for partial orders remain unclear. The supplied open-status assessment requires independent review.
