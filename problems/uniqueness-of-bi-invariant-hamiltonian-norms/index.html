Math-3688

Uniqueness of Bi-Invariant Hamiltonian Norms

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Hamiltonian-norm question. If a $G$-invariant norm on normalized Hamiltonians induces a genuine bi-invariant distance on the Hamiltonian group $G$, must it be equivalent to the oscillation norm $\operatorname{osc}(F)=\max F-\min F$? The source does not supply the symplectic manifold or analytic hypotheses on the norm.

Under the usual closed connected symplectic convention, let $(M^{2m},\omega)$ be fixed, $G=\operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega)$, and $\mathcal H=\{F\in C^\infty(M,{\mathbb{R}}):\int_MF\,\omega^m=0\}$. Adjoint invariance means $\nu(F\circ\phi^{-1})=\nu(F)$ for all $\phi\in G$, $F\in\mathcal H$. For a smooth normalized time-dependent Hamiltonian $H_t$, define $\ell_\nu(H)=\int_0^1\nu(H_t)\,dt$, whenever this integral is meaningful, and\[d_\nu(\phi,\psi)=\inf\{\ell_\nu(H):\phi_H^1=\phi^{-1}\psi\}.\] The genuine-distance hypothesis is $d_\nu(\phi,\psi)>0$ for $\phi\ne\psi$, in addition to the standard metric requirements. A nonzero norm on functions can still induce a degenerate path pseudodistance, so these conditions are distinct.

The requested norm equivalence convention is the existence of constants $0<c\le C<\infty$, depending on the norm and fixed manifold but not on $F$, such that\[c\,\operatorname{osc}(F)\le\nu(F)\le C\,\operatorname{osc}(F)\quad\text{for every }F\in\mathcal H.\] It is not equality of norms, and merely asking whether the induced distances are equivalent would be a different conclusion. The source specifies neither continuity of $\nu$ in the smooth or uniform topology nor a domination assumption, measurability along paths, completeness, or a chosen class of norms. These analytic restrictions must not be added or dropped silently. Likewise, on an open manifold normalized versus compactly supported Hamiltonians and the target Hamiltonian group need separate definitions; the displayed closed-manifold setup is explicit and conditional.

The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the item open and reports no authoritative resolution of the extracted claim in its search. It gives no norm-regularity hypotheses or comparison with precise later theorems. Its dated assertion is retained as provenance, not independently applied to every interpretation of the norm class.

Short English statement

Does nondegeneracy of the path distance force a Hamiltonian-invariant function norm to satisfy uniform two-sided oscillation bounds? The norm may be nondegenerate even when its induced distance is not. The manifold and continuity assumptions are missing and affect the scope of the question.
