Math-14443

Curvature-Derivative Invariants of Homogeneous Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth Riemannian manifold with Levi--Civita connection $\nabla$ and curvature tensor $R$. The metric is homogeneous if its isometry group acts transitively on $M$, and Einstein if $\operatorname{Ric}_g=\lambda g$ for a constant $\lambda$. A geodesic-orbit metric is one for which every geodesic is an orbit of a one-parameter group of isometries. A homogeneous presentation $M=G/H$ is naturally reductive if it admits an $\operatorname{Ad}(H)$-invariant decomposition $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak m$ such that the metric inner product on $\mathfrak m$ satisfies \[ \langle [X,Y]_{\mathfrak m},Z\rangle+\langle Y,[X,Z]_{\mathfrak m}\rangle=0 \qquad(X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak m). \] Here natural reductivity and the geodesic-orbit property are understood to hold for a suitable transitive isometry group; the supplied record does not specify a particular group for the general questions.

The source denotes by $k_o$ the constant Jacobi osculating rank and by $k_S$ the Singer invariant. It defines \[ k_t(M,g)=\min\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}:Y_0^{(k+3,2)}R\equiv0\right\}, \] where $Y_0^{(k+3,2)}R$ is called the completely traceless Cartan component. For expressing the finiteness questions, set $k_t=+\infty$ when this set is empty. The supplied record does not give the construction of this component, the precise definition and constancy requirement for $k_o$, or the indexing convention for $k_S$. These source-dependent definitions must be recovered before the numerical questions and inequalities constitute a fully specified mathematical problem; no convention for them is silently imposed here.

Subject to those unresolved definitions, the supplied problem asks the following.

  1. Determine $k_t$ for the intended homogeneous metrics on \[ \operatorname{Sp}(2)/\operatorname{SU}(2),\qquad \left(\operatorname{SO}(3)\times\operatorname{SU}(3)\right)/U^\bullet(2),\qquad \operatorname{SU}(3)/T^2, \] and on Kaplan's six-dimensional geodesic-orbit nilmanifold. The subgroup embeddings, the meaning of $U^\bullet(2)$, and the intended metrics are not specified in the supplied record. The nilmanifold is named but its Lie algebra and metric are not supplied. Thus these names alone do not specify the requested numerical computations.
  2. Compute $k_o$, $k_t$, and $k_S$ for further homogeneous Riemannian spaces. This is an open-ended request; no further list is supplied.
  3. For each of the three classes consisting of naturally reductive, geodesic-orbit, and arbitrary homogeneous Riemannian manifolds, determine whether \[ \text{for every }(M,g)\text{ in the class, there exists }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0} \text{ such that }Y_0^{(k+3,2)}R\equiv0. \]
  4. Determine the intended converse relating finite $k_t$ to homogeneity. The clean statement asks whether finite $k_t$ characterizes complete homogeneous spaces, whereas the original statement merely asks whether the converse holds. In particular, it is unresolved whether the intended assertion is \[ \text{for every complete Riemannian }(M,g),\qquad k_t(M,g)<\infty\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (M,g)\text{ is homogeneous}, \] or whether finiteness itself is intended to imply both completeness and homogeneity. These alternatives are recorded without selecting one. The use of $k_t$ on potentially nonhomogeneous manifolds also requires the missing tensor definition.
  5. Determine whether every homogeneous Einstein Riemannian manifold has $k_o<\infty$, with $k_o$ understood in the source's intended sense.
  6. Characterize the equality cases in the source-stated inequalities \[ k_t\leq k_o,\qquad k_S\leq k_o, \] and, in the Einstein case, \[ k_S\leq k_t. \] The record presents these as inequalities whose equality cases are sought. It does not supply their proofs or full applicability conditions, including any finiteness or constancy hypotheses; those conditions require review.
This is a statement-only transcription with explicit unresolved qualifications, not a verification of the inequalities, the invariant definitions, or any literature-status claim.

Short English statement

The problem asks for curvature-derivative invariants of specified and further homogeneous Riemannian spaces, universal finiteness results, a converse relating finite $k_t$ to complete homogeneity, and equality cases in several stated inequalities. Essential invariant conventions, example metrics and subgroup embeddings, and the precise converse remain unspecified. The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
