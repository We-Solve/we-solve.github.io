Math-1864

Dissipation bounds for flow past an obstacle

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $B$ be a fixed obstacle in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ and let $\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^3\setminus B$ be the exterior fluid region. The source specifies viscous incompressible flow with \[ u(x,t)\longrightarrow U_\infty\ne0\qquad(|x|\to\infty). \] A customary model, per unit fluid density, is \[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u, \qquad\nabla\cdot u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega, \] with viscosity $\nu>0$. The source does not supply obstacle regularity, a boundary condition on $\partial B$, a solution class, or whether the problem is steady or time dependent. In particular, no-slip $u=0$ on the fixed obstacle is a common convention but is not imposed here without source support.

With $D(u)=\tfrac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})$, the standard total viscous dissipation rate per unit density is \[ \varepsilon(t)=2\nu\int_{\Omega}|D(u)(x,t)|^2\,dx, \] when finite. A stationary rate or long-time averaged rate \[ \overline\varepsilon=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T\varepsilon(t)\,dt \] are distinct possible targets; the source does not choose between them or an instantaneous bound with initial-data dependence.

Problem. Obtain realistic rigorous upper bounds on the energy-dissipation rate for this three-dimensional exterior flow past the fixed obstacle with nonzero uniform velocity at infinity. Once the setting is fixed, a typical goal is an explicit bound in terms of viscosity, incoming speed, and obstacle geometry. If a characteristic length $L$ is chosen, one can express sharpness through $\operatorname{Re}=|U_\infty|L/\nu$ and a dimensionless rate $\overline\varepsilon/(|U_\infty|^3L^2)$, but the source prescribes neither $L$ nor a Reynolds-number scaling. "Realistic" therefore needs a specified physical or asymptotic criterion, not an invented numerical bound.

The supplied research summary reports rigorous drag/dissipation bounds in special geometries and says fully general physically sharp bounds for arbitrary obstacles remain partially open. It gives no precise results or citations. This is retained as an attributed progress assessment rather than a verified theorem or current literature conclusion.

Short English statement

Find physically sharp upper bounds for dissipation in viscous incompressible flow past a fixed three-dimensional obstacle with nonzero uniform velocity at infinity. The boundary condition, solution class, averaging convention, geometry class, and meaning of realistic sharpness must be specified before a unique bound can be formulated.
