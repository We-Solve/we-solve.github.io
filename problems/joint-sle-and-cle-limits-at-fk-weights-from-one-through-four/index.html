Joint SLE and CLE limits at FK weights from one through four

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: The later interface theorem extends the square-lattice FK claim through $q=4$, with the same SLE parameter relation and a separate complete nested-loop limit; For $1\leq q<4$, critical square-lattice FK interfaces are claimed to converge as curves to the predicted SLE, with a separate convergence theorem for the complete nested plane CLE collection. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Fix $q\in[1,4]$, $p_q=\sqrt q/(1+\sqrt q)$, and \[ \kappa(q)=\frac{4\pi}{\arccos(-\sqrt q/2)}. \] Use the square-lattice random-cluster law with edge-subset weights $p_q^{|A|}(1-p_q)^{|E|-|A|}q^{k_\xi(A)}$, where $\xi$ wires one boundary arc and leaves the other free. For a bounded Jordan domain with two marked boundary points, approximate its boundary uniformly by simple lattice polygons, preserving the marks. The claim gives \[ \eta_n\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{SLE}_{\kappa(q)} \] for the Dobrushin interface in uniform curve distance modulo increasing reparametrization. Under the free infinite-volume law, its full nested loop collection satisfies \[ \Gamma_\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{nested whole-plane }\operatorname{CLE}_{\kappa(q)} \] in the spherical matching topology: all loops above a shrinking diameter threshold are matched with shrinking uniform spherical error. Both limits hold along every mesh sequence, with $q$ fixed.
Result 2.
Fix $1\le q<4$, $p_q=\sqrt q/(1+\sqrt q)$, and $\kappa(q)=4\pi/\arccos(-\sqrt q/2)$. The square-lattice critical random-cluster model is claimed to satisfy two limits: its wired/free Dobrushin strand in any marked bounded Jordan domain converges to chordal $\operatorname{SLE}_{\kappa(q)}$; the complete interface loop collection under the free infinite-volume law converges to nested whole-plane $\operatorname{CLE}_{\kappa(q)}$. The first uses uniformly convergent marked simple-polygon boundary parametrizations and uniform curve distance modulo increasing reparametrization. The second uses spherical matching of all loops of diameter exceeding a shrinking threshold, retaining every nesting level. The result is for fixed $q<4$; it asserts no uniformity as $q\to4$.

Outline of the argument

The tiled comparison and angular-transfer method is supplemented by endpoint estimates at $q=4$. Boundary current tests identify the driving process, while full exploration and localization retain macroscopic loops; at $q=1$ this also yields the bond-percolation crossing formula.

Positive tiled laws and short-distance comparisons localize the interface in irregular disks. Angular transfer modes and a four-boundary-change current observable identify the Loewner driver; exploration iteration then matches the nested loops, retaining their multiplicities and traversal structure.
