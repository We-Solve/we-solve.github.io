Asymptotic Point Counts on Curve Moduli Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q=p^r$, where $p$ is prime and $r\geq 1$ is an integer, and let ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ be the field with $q$ elements. For each integer $g\geq 2$, interpret $\mathcal M_g$, explicitly called a coarse moduli space in the supplied record, as the coarse moduli space over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ of smooth, proper, geometrically connected, unmarked curves of genus $g$. This uses the standard interpretation of the notation; the supplied statement does not explicitly specify these curve hypotheses. No compactification or boundary of singular stable curves is included under this interpretation.

Define \[ N_g(q)=\#\mathcal M_g({\mathbb{F}}_q), \qquad \mathcal M_g({\mathbb{F}}_q)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{F}}_q}\left(\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{F}}_q,\mathcal M_g\right). \] Thus $N_g(q)$ counts rational points of the coarse moduli space. It is not defined here as a count of ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-isomorphism classes of curves or as a sum weighted by reciprocals of automorphism-group orders.

For each fixed prime power $q$, does there exist a real number $L_q$ with $0<L_q<\infty$ such that \[ \lim_{\substack{g\to\infty\\g\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ g\geq 2}} \frac{N_g(q)}{q^{3g-3}}=L_q\,? \] The question concerns the limit through all integer genera, with $q$ held fixed. The curve-moduli interpretation above requires confirmation against the original problem discussion.

Short English statement

For a fixed finite field ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, count its rational points on the coarse moduli space of smooth genus-$g$ curves. Does dividing this count by $q^{3g-3}$ give a sequence converging to a finite, strictly positive number as $g$ tends to infinity? The standard curve-moduli interpretation is implicit in the supplied source and requires review.
