Definitions and conventions.
For a finite lattice region $\Lambda$, the free energy per site at inverse temperature $\beta>0$ is $-(\beta|\Lambda|)^{-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_\Lambda}$, with the thermodynamic limit defining $f^{2d}$. The Heisenberg ferromagnet has nearest-neighbor spin-$S$ interactions favoring alignment, with the report's ground-energy normalization; $S$ is an allowed positive half-integer spin. The displayed asymptotics concern large $\beta S$. A matching lower bound must recover the leading coefficient and the requested asymptotic error strength.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the two-dimensional quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet, the free energy obeys

$$f^{2d}(\beta,S)\leq-\frac\pi{24}S^{-1}\beta^{-2}\left(1-O\left((\beta S)^{-\kappa}\right)\right)$$

for every $\kappa<1/3$. Can one prove a matching lower bound?

Source scope and qualification.
The Hamiltonian normalization determines the coefficient $\pi/24$. It must not be changed by an unreported coupling rescaling. The meaning of matching, including the range of $\kappa$, remains the comparison asked in the report.

Short English statement

Prove the lower free-energy estimate needed to make the two-dimensional ferromagnet's spin-wave prediction sharp.

Recovering the proposed spin-wave coefficient.
For independent bosons with continuum quadratic dispersion $\varepsilon(k)=S|k|^2$ in two dimensions, the formal thermodynamic free energy density is the convergent integral \[ f_{\rm sw}=\frac1\beta\int_{\mathbb R^2}\frac{d^2k}{(2\pi)^2}\log(1-e^{-\beta S|k|^2}). \] Expand $-\log(1-z)=\sum_{n\ge1}z^n/n$; its nonnegative terms justify integrating the negative of this expression term by term. The Gaussian integral is $\int d^2k\,e^{-n\beta S|k|^2}=\pi/(n\beta S)$. Hence \[ f_{\rm sw}=-\frac1{4\pi S\beta^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}=-\frac\pi{24S\beta^2}, \] using the elementary Euler sum $\sum n^{-2}=\pi^2/6$.
The infrared obstruction.
The same approximation has divergent particle density: for sufficiently small $|k|$, $e^{\beta S|k|^2}-1\le2\beta S|k|^2$, so the density integral dominates a positive constant times $\int_0^\delta dr/r$. Free energy is integrable at the origin, while density is not. Therefore controlling spin-wave interactions by a uniform small boson density cannot follow from this free-energy computation in infinite volume.
