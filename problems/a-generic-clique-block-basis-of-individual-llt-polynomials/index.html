Math-5440

A generic clique-block basis of individual LLT polynomials

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks for other interesting symmetric-function bases simply related to LLT polynomials. It is exploratory, not a single yes/no conjecture. Its historical remarks say that Schur functions occur among LLTs; Lam includes Hall--Littlewood and skew Schur functions; Morse gives $s^{(3)}_{(2,1,1)}$ as a purported non-LLT $k$-Schur example; and modified Macdonald polynomials, and conjecturally $\nabla(e_n)$, have weighted LLT expansions using ribbon tuples and shifted-column tuples. These remarks suppress normalization and definition choices and are not fresh status assertions.

The aid constructs an explicit family over ${\mathbb{Q}}(q)$. For an ordered complete attacking block of $r$ unicellular LLT cells, use inversion normalization \[ C_r(X;q)=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r\ge1} q^{\#\{a<b:i_a>i_b\}}x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r},\qquad C_0=1. \] For a partition $\lambda$, let $C_\lambda=\prod_{r\in\lambda}C_r$, treating repeated parts with multiplicity. This product is itself the unicellular LLT for an ordered disjoint union of clique blocks, with no attacks between blocks. The choice of inversion normalization is essential to its specialization statements.

For every degree $n$, the aid reports that $\{C_\lambda:\lambda\vdash n\}$ is a basis of $\Lambda_n\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}(q)$. With matching partition order for rows and columns, its transition determinant to the complete basis $h_\lambda=\prod_{r\in\lambda}h_r$ is \[ D_n(q)=\prod_{\lambda\vdash n}\prod_{r\in\lambda} \prod_{j=1}^{r-1}(1-q^j). \] Hence at a complex specialization $q=a$ the degree-$n$ family is a basis exactly when $a^j\ne1$ for $1\le j\le n-1$. The condition is empty in degrees zero and one. At $q=0$ it is the complete basis, while at $q=1$ every degree-$n$ member becomes $h_1^n$; this is a rank-one collapse when $n\ge2$. Over all degrees simultaneously, a fixed root of unity eventually causes failure, whereas a non-root-of-unity specialization avoids these determinant zeros.

This supplies one generic basis of individual LLTs, not a classification of all interesting bases or a claim that every $k$-Schur function is an LLT. The aid flags the historical Morse example as convention-dependent and inconsistent with a later bandwidth-three realization attributed to Miller. Neither the exact $k$-Schur/LLT convention nor that later theorem is supplied, so the conflicting example is retained as a reference/normalization issue rather than silently resolved.

Short English statement

The aid gives a partition-indexed basis of individual clique-block LLTs at generic parameter, with an exact determinant describing every bad numerical specialization. It also records that the source's historical $k$-Schur example depends on conventions and later literature not fully supplied here.
