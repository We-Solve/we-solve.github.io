Exact containment when the outer zonotope has fixed generator co-rank

Exact containment when the outer zonotope has fixed generator co-rank

Problem: prb_85d3f5d619ac47e28c66b73728a92fcb

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: For rational full-row-rank H in Q^(d x m), centered zonotope containment is decidable in binom(m,d-1) poly(L) bit operations. With m=d+c and fixed c this is polynomial in total encoded input length L.

Use exactly the centered convention Z(G)=G[-1,1]^n and Z(H)=H[-1,1]^m. Assume H has row rank d>=1 and all entries are binary-encoded rational numbers. Let L include dimensions, all matrix entries, and their encodings. The claimed regime is fixed excess c=m-d, not fixed dimension d.

For each (d-1)-subset S of columns of H, compute its rank. If the rank is d-1, compute a nonzero rational normal u_S to its span. Test
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n|u_S\cdot g_i|\le\sum_{j=1}^m|u_S\cdot h_j|. \tag{1}
\]
Return containment exactly when all tests pass. Both signs have the same inequality because both zonotopes are centered; arbitrary translations would require their support terms too.

To prove completeness, maximizing u over a segment [-h_j,h_j] chooses an endpoint when u dot h_j is nonzero and the entire segment otherwise. Thus the exposed face of Z(H) at u is a translate of the zonotope generated by the columns perpendicular to u. Its dimension is the rank of those columns. Every facet therefore has normal perpendicular to d-1 independent columns, and its two support inequalities occur among (1). Conversely we may test redundant directions without harm. A full-dimensional polytope is the intersection of its facet halfspaces: if a point outside it were in every such halfspace, a segment from an interior point to it would first leave through a proper face, contained in a facet whose inequality it then violates. Hence passing all facet tests is sufficient. The formula h_Z(G)(u)=sum |u dot g_i| follows by independent maximization of segment coefficients, and proves necessity as well.

In d=1 the empty column set yields the one-dimensional normal and one support test, so the enumeration remains correct. Rank-deficient H is not covered by the advertised theorem; it must first be treated as a subspace-containment problem rather than assigned a fictitious d-dimensional facet description.

Rational elimination and kernel computation for one subset, and all dot products and comparisons in (1), cost polynomially many bit operations in L. Fraction-free determinant algorithms or ordinary rational elimination with reduced fractions suffice: each intermediate number is a ratio of minors after pivoting, with polynomial bit length by the determinant expansion/Hadamard bound. We use the conservative poly(L) notation, including memory/address access. There are binom(m,d-1) subsets. When m=d+c this equals binom(d+c,c+1)<= (d+c)^(c+1), giving polynomial bit time for every fixed c. The exponent depends on c; this is neither an f(d)poly(L) algorithm nor a contradiction of dimension-parameterized hardness results. No real input is silently treated as a finite bit string.

A separate attempted route for the repeated record

This fragment repeats the dimension-parameterized containment question. The external W[1]-hardness result below materially qualifies its dated literature assessment. We test the common proposal to enumerate all sign regions of generator scalar products and describe that as FPT simply because dimension is fixed. For $n\ge d\ge1$, choose normals $g_i=(1,t_i,\ldots,t_i^{d-1})$ with $t_i=i$. Any $d$ are independent: their determinant is the nonzero Vandermonde product. Let $R(n,d)$ be the number of full-dimensional regions cut out by the central hyperplanes $g_i^Tu=0$.
Proposition: \[ R(n,d)=2\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{n-1}{j}. \] Thus explicit region enumeration on this family is not bounded by $f(d)n^C$ for any fixed exponent $C$ and finite function $f$.
Proof: In dimension one there are two regions. If $n\le d$ independent hyperplanes are linearly equivalent to coordinate hyperplanes and produce $2^n$ regions, agreeing with the formula using zero binomial coefficients. For $n>d\ge2$, add the last hyperplane. The old hyperplanes restrict to a central arrangement on it in dimension $d-1$, with every $d-1$ restricted normals independent because any $d$ original normals are independent. Each region of the restricted arrangement cuts one old region into two, so $R(n,d)=R(n-1,d)+R(n-1,d-1)$. Induction and Pascal's identity yield the formula. For fixed $d$, it grows as a positive constant times $n^{d-1}$. Given $C$, fix $d>C+1$ and let $n$ increase; the ratio $R(n,d)/n^C$ is unbounded, contradicting any such enumeration bound. ∎
Within each region every sign is fixed, so the segment support expression is linear there. This explains the attractiveness of enumeration, but also its dimension-dependent exponent. The argument counts explicit regions; it is not a lower bound against algorithms that implicitly handle many regions at once.

Literature qualification and a support-test route

The source report uses $Z_0(G)=\sum_i[0,g_i]$, while the catalogue defines the centered $Z_c(G)=G[-1,1]^n$. They satisfy \[ Z_0(G)=\tfrac12\sum_i g_i+\tfrac12 Z_c(G). \] The center displacement matters in containment and cannot be discarded. The later primary paper cited below establishes W[1]-hardness of the source non-containment problem, so a general FPT containment algorithm in its rational-input model would imply $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$. This is attributed literature, not a newly proved resolution here; the original metadata remain preserved. Our attempted simplification is to certify containment from the $2d$ coordinate support inequalities. For the centered convention take \[ G=\binom11,\qquad H=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&-1/2\end{pmatrix}. \]
Proposition: The two coordinate projections of $Z_c(G)$ and $Z_c(H)$ agree, but $Z_c(G)\nsubseteq Z_c(H)$.
Proof: Both coordinate ranges are $[-1,1]$. A point of $Z_c(H)$ has coordinates $((s+t)/2,(s-t)/2)$ with $|s|,|t|\le1$, equivalently $|x+y|\le1$ and $|x-y|\le1$. The point $(1,1)\in Z_c(G)$ violates the first inequality. In support notation, maximizing a linear form over independent segment coefficients gives $h_{Z_c(G)}(u)=\sum_i|u^Tg_i|$; hence at $u=(1,1)$ the two supports are two and one. ∎
This also produces a source-convention example: replace each generator by the pair consisting of it and its negative. Then the noncentered sum of these paired segments equals the original centered zonotope, with center zero. Thus the coordinate-test failure is not an artifact of the convention.
