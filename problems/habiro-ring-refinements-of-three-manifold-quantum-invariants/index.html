Habiro-Ring Refinements of Three-Manifold Quantum Invariants

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $Y$ be a rational homology $3$-sphere, meaning that $H_i(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_i(S^3;{\mathbb{Q}})$ for every $i$. The quantum invariants below are understood in their usual setting of closed, connected, oriented $3$-manifolds; the supplied excerpt does not specify their orientation and normalization conventions. In this setting the source's hypothesis $b_1(Y)=0$, where $b_1(Y)=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}H_1(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})$, is equivalent to the rational homology sphere condition. Write $\operatorname{Spin}^c(Y)$ for the set of isomorphism classes of $\operatorname{Spin}^c$ structures on $Y$.

Define the Habiro ring by \[ \widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[q]}=\varprojlim_{n\geq1}{\mathbb{Z}}[q]\big/\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-q^k)\right). \] For a root of unity $\zeta$, evaluation induces a well-defined homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_{\zeta}:\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[q]}\to{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta]$: the defining product vanishes at $\zeta$ once $n$ is at least its order.

Put \[ A_Y=H^1(Y;{\mathbb{C}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad B_Y=H^1(Y;{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\subseteq A_Y, \] using the inclusion of coefficient groups. The source uses the mod-$2$ cohomological refinement $\operatorname{WRT}_r(Y,\omega)$ for $\omega\in B_Y$, and the Costantino--Geer--Patureau-Mirand invariant $N_r(Y,\omega)$ for $\omega\in A_Y\setminus B_Y$, at admissible levels $r$. Let $\mathcal T(Y,\omega)$ denote the version of Reidemeister--Turaev torsion required by the source. Retain its explicit hypothesis \[ \mathcal T(Y,\omega)\ne0\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in A_Y\setminus B_Y. \] Subject to the admissibility and normalization conventions of the source, define \[ \widetilde N_r(Y,\omega)= \begin{cases} \operatorname{WRT}_r(Y,\omega),&\omega\in B_Y,\\[2pt] \displaystyle\frac{N_r(Y,\omega)}{\mathcal T(Y,\omega)},&\omega\in A_Y\setminus B_Y. \end{cases} \] The particular torsion, the invariant normalizations, and the admissible levels are not defined in the supplied excerpt.

Habiro-ring existence conjecture (source Conjecture 2). For every such $Y$, subject to the stated torsion hypothesis, there exists a family \[ \left(I_s(Y)\right)_{s\in\operatorname{Spin}^c(Y)}\in \prod_{s\in\operatorname{Spin}^c(Y)}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[q]} \] such that, simultaneously for every source-admissible level $r$ and cohomology class $\omega\in A_Y$, \[ \widetilde N_r(Y,\omega)= \sum_{s\in\operatorname{Spin}^c(Y)} C_s(Y,\omega)\, \operatorname{ev}_{e^{2\pi i/r}}\left(I_s(Y)\right). \] The family depends on $Y$ and $s$, not on $r$ or $\omega$. The coefficients $C_s(Y,\omega)$ are prescribed universal coefficients from the source, with dependence on $r$ suppressed in its notation; they also involve certain homotopy-type invariants of $Y$. They are not additional unknowns to be chosen to satisfy the equation. Their formula and the full admissibility conditions are absent, so this is a qualified reconstruction rather than a self-contained specification of those data.

The source motivates this conjecture through its preceding Conjecture 1, attributed there to references [3, 2], asking for series $\widehat Z_s(Y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[q]]$, convergent for $|q|<1$, whose weighted limits at $q=e^{2\pi i/r}$ recover $\widetilde N_r$. It states that root-of-unity limits alone do not ensure uniqueness of such series, because nonzero series may have zero limits at all roots of unity. The displayed Habiro-ring conjecture explicitly asserts existence; a separate uniqueness assertion for the indexed family is not supplied. The excerpt reports verification of the preceding conjecture for an unspecified family in reference [2], a reduction to an unspecified analogous conjecture for links in $S^3$, and a possible analogous conjecture for $\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$ versions of CGP invariants in reference [5]. It also says that the torsion hypothesis holds for a generic $3$-manifold, asks how it might be avoided, notes that its conventions differ slightly from [2], and mentions a possible version for $b_1(Y)>0$. None of these unspecified extensions or reported results supplies missing hypotheses for the statement above.

Short English statement

For each rational homology $3$-sphere satisfying the source's torsion nonvanishing hypothesis, seek one Habiro-ring element for each $\operatorname{Spin}^c$ structure. Their evaluations at roots of unity should recover the combined refined WRT and torsion-normalized CGP invariant through the source's prescribed coefficients, for every admissible level and cohomology class. The coefficient formulas, admissibility rules, and normalization conventions remain unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
