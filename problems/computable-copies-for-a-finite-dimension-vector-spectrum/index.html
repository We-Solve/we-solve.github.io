Math-1561

Computable copies for a finite dimension-vector spectrum

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $I(T,\lambda)$ for the number of isomorphism types of $T$-models of cardinality $\lambda$. In the intended countable-theory context, the source asks whether \[ \forall\lambda\geq\aleph_1\quad I(T,\lambda)\leq\lambda \] forces every such model to have a $\lambda$-computable presentation, allowing the extra hypothesis $V=L$ if helpful. It says such theories are $\omega$-stable and non-multidimensional and records a positive $\aleph_1$-categorical case. Those surrounding theorem and computability conventions are not reproduced fully.

The aid gives a noncategorical special class. Fix $m\geq2$, and let $T_m$ be the complete theory of $m$ named infinite unary predicates partitioning the universe, with no further structure. A model is classified by its finite dimension vector \[ (\kappa_0,\ldots,\kappa_{m-1}),\qquad \kappa_i\text{ infinite cardinals},\qquad\max_i\kappa_i=\lambda. \] For every uncountable $\lambda$, the aid reports $I(T_m,\lambda)\leq\lambda$. At $\lambda=\aleph_1$, each coordinate is either $\aleph_0$ or $\aleph_1$, with at least one large coordinate, so \[ I(T_m,\aleph_1)=2^m-1. \] This is genuinely noncategorical for $m\geq2$.

For every regular uncountable $\lambda$, its computable presentation has domain the ordinals below $\lambda$. Use disjoint columns \[ C_i=\{\omega\cdot\beta+i:\beta<\lambda\},\qquad0\leq i<m. \] Choose a designated $j$ with $\kappa_j=\lambda$. For $i\ne j$, use all of $C_i$ if $\kappa_i=\lambda$, and only its terms with $\beta<\kappa_i$ if $\kappa_i<\lambda$. Assign every leftover ordinal to predicate $j$. That predicate contains $C_j$, so still has cardinality $\lambda$; the construction defines a genuine partition and does not leave unused ordinals outside the model.

The aid reports the predicates uniformly $\Delta_1(L_\lambda)$ from a finite ordinal index coding the small coordinates, the designated component and flags for $\lambda$-sized coordinates. This is an explicitly parameter/index-sensitive meaning of $\lambda$-computable; the stage ordinal $\lambda$ is not used as an element of $L_\lambda$. It is not a claim of parameter-free computation from $m$ alone.

Thus the model-count hypothesis holds at all uncountable sizes in this class, and presentations are supplied at every regular uncountable size under the specified convention. No corresponding singular-cardinal presentation theorem, or complete answer for arbitrary $T$, follows from the construction. The original all-$\lambda$ scope and its undefined computational convention remain qualifications.

Short English statement

The aid supplies a noncategorical family satisfying the source's small-spectrum hypothesis: finitely many named infinite pure pieces. Every regular uncountable-sized model has an explicit ordinal-column presentation, with the finite dimension vector encoded in its allowed index. This is a special case and does not settle all theories, singular cardinals or parameter-free conventions.
