Math-6963

Ball Conjecture for Twisted Bruhat Intervals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system and let $\theta$ be the intended involutive automorphism, with the usual Coxeter-system convention $\theta(S)=S$. Impose exactly the source's no-odd-order condition: \[ \theta(s)\ne s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{ord}(s\theta(s))\text{ is even or infinite} \qquad(s\in S). \] Thus infinite order is permitted, and the order-one product for a fixed generator is not excluded.

Use the standard set of twisted identities \[ \iota(\theta)=\{\theta(w^{-1})w:w\in W\}, \] and let $\operatorname{Br}(\iota(\theta))$ be this set with the order induced from Bruhat order on $W$. For comparable $u<v$ in this poset, let $\Delta(u,v)$ be the order complex of the open interval: its simplices are chains $u<x_1<\cdots<x_k<v$. This is the usual topological interpretation of an interval in the stated sphere/ball question; the order complex of the whole closed interval would instead have cone points.

The conjectural strengthening asks whether every such interval complex is either a PL sphere or a ball. A PL sphere is a simplicial complex piecewise-linearly homeomorphic to a standard sphere; a ball is at least required to be homeomorphic to a closed Euclidean ball. The source does not explicitly say whether the latter must also be a PL ball.

The original statement reports Theorem 7 as giving the weaker conclusion "PL sphere or ${\mathbb{Z}}$-acyclic", where acyclic means vanishing reduced integral homology in every degree. The requested strengthening replaces that acyclic alternative by a ball; acyclicity alone is not identified with the ball property. The extract does not state the endpoint/empty-complex dimension conventions or explicitly define the twisted-identity notation and preservation of $S$, so the standard interpretations above remain subject to source confirmation.

Short English statement

Under the specified no-odd-order condition, must every twisted-identity Bruhat interval have the topology of a PL sphere or a ball? The source reports a sphere-or-integrally-acyclic theorem and asks to strengthen the acyclic alternative to a ball.
