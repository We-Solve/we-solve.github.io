Kähler, Projective, and Quasiprojective Group Realizability

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is a Kähler group if there exist a connected compact Kähler manifold $X$ and a base point $x\in X$ such that $G\cong\pi_1(X,x)$. For this draft, use the standard smooth complex conventions: $G$ is a projective group if $G\cong\pi_1(Y,y)$ for some connected smooth complex projective variety $Y$ and $y\in Y$; it is a quasiprojective group if $G\cong\pi_1(U,u)$ for some connected smooth complex quasiprojective variety $U$ and $u\in U$. Here quasiprojective means isomorphic to a Zariski-open subvariety of a complex projective variety, and fundamental groups are taken in the analytic topology. The source does not explicitly specify smoothness or the ground field for its algebraic realizations; these conventions therefore require confirmation against the cited report, particularly whether singular varieties are intended to be allowed.

Classification question. Given a specified class $\mathcal C$ of groups, characterize each of the following subclasses up to group isomorphism: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal C_{\mathrm K}&=\{G\in\mathcal C: G\text{ is a Kähler group}\},\\ \mathcal C_{\mathrm P}&=\{G\in\mathcal C: G\text{ is a projective group}\},\\ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{QP}}&=\{G\in\mathcal C: G\text{ is a quasiprojective group}\}. \end{aligned} \] Thus, for each of the three realization types, seek necessary and sufficient conditions on a member $G\in\mathcal C$ for the corresponding realization to exist. The supplied statement requests a natural class of groups but specifies neither that class nor a meaning of naturality. This is consequently a classification-question template, rather than a fully specified classification problem for a particular class.

Short English statement

For a specified class of groups, determine exactly which members occur as fundamental groups of compact Kähler manifolds, smooth complex projective varieties, or smooth complex quasiprojective varieties. The source leaves the class unspecified, and the smooth complex conventions require confirmation. Its open status is supplied dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
