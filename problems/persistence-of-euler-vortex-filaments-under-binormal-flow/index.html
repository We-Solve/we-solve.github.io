Persistence of Euler Vortex Filaments Under Binormal Flow

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^3$ denote the spatial domain, which the supplied record does not specify. For a sufficiently regular velocity field $u:D\times I\to{\mathbb{R}}^3$ and pressure $p:D\times I\to{\mathbb{R}}$, the incompressible Euler equations are \[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0, \] and the vorticity is $\omega=\nabla\times u$. These equations specify only the interior differential equations; the domain, boundary or far-field conditions, and solution class remain unresolved.

For a smooth regular curve with an arc-length parametrization $X(s,\tau)$, so that $|\partial_sX|=1$, binormal flow is expressed geometrically by \[ \partial_\tau X=\partial_sX\times\partial_s^2X. \] Here $\tau$ denotes the binormal-flow time parameter. Its relation to Euler time $t$, including any circulation-dependent or concentration-dependent rescaling, is not specified by the source. No assumption that the curve is closed, embedded, or noncompact is supplied.

Source question, with unresolved qualifications. For three-dimensional incompressible Euler evolution whose initial vorticity is concentrated along a smooth spatial curve, does the vorticity remain concentrated around a curve for finite times, and can that concentration remain close, for a significant time, to a filament evolving by binormal flow?

The record does not define initial or subsequent concentration, a distance measuring closeness, or the meaning of a significant time. It also does not specify whether the assertion concerns every suitably concentrated initial datum or the construction of some family of such data. Consequently, the supplied question does not determine a single fully quantified mathematical conjecture. A precise formulation must specify the admissible data and curves, the Euler solution class, the concentration and approximation criteria, any small-parameter limit and circulation normalization, the time correspondence, and the interval on which the claims are required. These choices are left unresolved rather than imposed here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether vorticity initially concentrated near a smooth spatial curve stays concentrated near a moving curve under three-dimensional Euler evolution, and whether that curve follows binormal flow for a meaningful time. The supplied record leaves the concentration criterion, approximation scale, time scale, domain, and quantifiers unspecified, so this is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
