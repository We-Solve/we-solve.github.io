Scalar Curvature Question [?14]: How common are Ricci ﬂat metrics on compact simply connected manifolds X which admit metrics with positive sca

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth manifold $X$, let \[ \begin{split} \mathcal R_0(X)&=\{g:g\text{ is a smooth Riemannian metric on }X,\ \operatorname{Ric}_g=0\},\\ \mathcal R_+(X)&=\{g:g\text{ is a smooth Riemannian metric on }X,\ \operatorname{Scal}_g(x)>0\text{ for all }x\in X\}. \end{split} \] Gromov asks how common Ricci-flat metrics are on compact simply connected manifolds $X$ for which $\mathcal R_+(X)\ne\varnothing$. In particular, the existence aspect concerns those $X$ satisfying \[ \pi_1(X)=0,\qquad \mathcal R_+(X)\ne\varnothing,\qquad \mathcal R_0(X)\ne\varnothing. \] The two nonemptiness conditions concern potentially different metrics on the same smooth manifold. A Ricci-flat metric has zero scalar curvature, so it cannot itself belong to $\mathcal R_+(X)$.

The phrase "how common" has no supplied counting rule, topology, measure, or moduli-space convention. It may ask for the prevalence of such manifolds, or for the size of $\mathcal R_0(X)$ on a fixed admissible $X$; these are different targets. No dimension or boundary convention is given. Thus the existence condition can be stated precisely, but it does not by itself replace the broader prevalence question.

Short English statement

On compact simply connected manifolds that support positive scalar curvature metrics, investigate the existence and prevalence of Ricci-flat metrics. The two curvature conditions apply to different metrics, and the meaning of prevalence remains unspecified.
