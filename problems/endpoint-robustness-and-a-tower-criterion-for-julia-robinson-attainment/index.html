Math-1584

Endpoint robustness and a tower criterion for Julia Robinson attainment

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ be the field of totally real algebraic numbers, and let ${\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq K\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}$. Write $\mathcal O_K$ for its algebraic integers. For $s>0$ define \[ B_K(s)=\{\alpha\in\mathcal O_K: 0\le\sigma(\alpha)\le s\text{ for every }{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-embedding } \sigma:{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)\to{\mathbb{R}}\}, \qquad A_K=\{s>0:|B_K(s)|=\infty\}. \] Put $\lambda_K=\inf A_K$ in the extended real line, with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. The nonvacuous attainment question attributed to Julia Robinson asks, when $\lambda_K<\infty$, whether \[ |B_K(\lambda_K)|=\infty, \quad\text{equivalently }\lambda_K\in A_K. \] The source reports that such attainment implies undecidability of the first-order theory of $\mathcal O_K$, and gives $\lambda_{{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}=4\in A_{{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}$. Its literal formulation does not exclude $A_K=\varnothing$; for a number field $K$, every $B_K(s)$ is finite, so $A_K=\varnothing$ and the extended-real infimum is not an element of $A_K$. Thus a universal attainment assertion needs the finite-threshold qualification. The extracted final inclusion in ${\mathbb{Q}}$ has lost the algebraic-closure bar in the intended totally real algebraic-field notation; a literal reading as subfields of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ would not express this question.

The supplied research aid reports the following exact reductions, leaving the general finite-threshold question open. For every finite $s>0$, replacing the closed condition $[0,s]$ by $(0,s)$ changes $B_K(s)$ by only finitely many elements. An endpoint-zero conjugate forces $\alpha=0$, and an endpoint-$s$ conjugate forces $\alpha$ to be one of the finitely many conjugates of $s$ when $s$ is an algebraic integer. Consequently the open- and closed-interval conventions have identical infinitude behavior at each finite threshold, including attainment.

If $K\subseteq L\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}$, then \[ B_K(s)=B_L(s)\cap\mathcal O_K,\qquad A_K\subseteq A_L,\qquad\lambda_L\le\lambda_K. \] If their thresholds agree and are finite, attainment for $K$ implies attainment for $L$. For any nested number-field exhaustion \[ K_1\subseteq K_2\subseteq\cdots,\qquad K=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K_n, \] and any finite $\lambda_K$, the aid gives the equivalent criterion \[ \lambda_K\in A_K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left\{|B_{K_n}(\lambda_K)|:n\ge1\right\} \text{ is unbounded in }{\mathbb{N}}. \] The counts are finite; no algorithm to compute them or the threshold is asserted. The aid also reports the universal lower bound $\lambda_K\ge4$ for finite thresholds, by a Kronecker argument. If $K$ contains infinitely many distinct elements of the form \[ 2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1},\qquad \zeta\text{ a root of unity}, \] then these are algebraic integers with every conjugate in $[0,4]$, forcing $\lambda_K=4$ with attainment. This recovers the printed example $K={\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{tr}}$. The exhaustion, monotonicity and endpoint facts are partial criteria, not a proof of attainment for every infinite totally real extension.

Short English statement

Ask whether the first finite threshold admitting infinitely many totally real integers in the conjugate box $[0,s]$ is itself attained. The aid gives equivalent open-box and tower-count criteria and verifies threshold $4$ for the maximal totally real field; empty threshold sets must be treated separately.
