Math-319

Higher-rank Anosov actions and the rank-one-factor boundary

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source conjectures that every "irreducible" Anosov ${\mathbb{Z}}^k$ or ${\mathbb{R}}^k$ action, $k\geq2$, is $C^\infty$-conjugate to an algebraic action. The quotation marks are substantive: no exact irreducibility hypothesis is supplied. In the surrounding framework an Anosov action has an element normally hyperbolic relative to the orbit foliation, and algebraic actions arise from automorphisms or translations on the intended infrahomogeneous Lie-group spaces. Compactness, action equivalence, and the exact rank-one-factor exclusion need a precise choice before this becomes a standalone quantified conjecture.

The aid supplies an obstruction family at that missing hypothesis boundary. Let $A\in GL(m,{\mathbb{Z}})$ and $B\in GL(s,{\mathbb{Z}})$ be hyperbolic toral automorphisms, with $m,s\geq2$. The Cartesian algebraic action on $\mathbb T^m\times\mathbb T^s$ is $\alpha_0(p,q)=(A^p,B^q)$. For arbitrarily small positive $\epsilon$, choose a smooth Anosov perturbation $f_\epsilon$ of $A$ fixing $0$ with \[ Df_\epsilon(0)=(1+\epsilon)A. \] A local bump perturbation can realize this while preserving sufficiently small $C^1$ distance. Define $\alpha_\epsilon(p,q)=(f_\epsilon^p,B^q)$. These are smooth commuting Cartesian actions, generator-$C^1$ close to $\alpha_0$, and structural stability conjugates them topologically to $\alpha_0$. Every off-axis element, meaning $p,q\ne0$, is an Anosov diffeomorphism. Elements on an axis retain an identity factor.

At their common fixed point $(0,0)$, \[ |\det D\alpha_\epsilon(p,q)|=(1+\epsilon)^{mp}. \] For $p\ne0$ this differs from one, whereas every affine toral diffeomorphism has linear part in $GL(m+s,{\mathbb{Z}})$ and absolute derivative determinant one. A $C^1$ conjugacy preserves this fixed-point determinant, so $\alpha_\epsilon$ is not $C^1$-conjugate to any affine toral action.

The obstruction survives $GL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})$ reparametrization because a new basis has a vector with nonzero first coordinate. It survives restriction to every finite-index subgroup because that subgroup contains $(a,0)$ for some $a>0$. On multiplication characteristic covers of the torus, choose the lifts fixing the origin as in the aid; the covering derivative conjugates the lifted derivative to the original one, preserving the determinant obstruction. No assertion for arbitrary different deck-translated lifts without a common fixed point is added.

These examples have explicit rank-one Cartesian factors, so they are not counterexamples to an appropriately rank-one-excluding irreducibility conjecture. They show that higher rank, topological algebraicity, and Anosov off-axis elements alone do not ensure smooth affine conjugacy. The aid says precise modern descendants have major positive results and counterexamples, but supplies no full statements or citations, and no conclusion for every possible interpretation of the quoted hypothesis follows. The trailing "Some partial results on" is incomplete following prose.

Short English statement

The source's irreducibility condition is undefined, so the supplied Cartesian perturbations test the boundary of the question rather than refute it. They remain topologically algebraic and Anosov off the axes, but a nonunit fixed-point Jacobian prevents $C^1$ affine conjugacy, even after the stated finite-index and covering operations. Their visible rank-one factors explain why a precise irreducibility hypothesis is necessary.
