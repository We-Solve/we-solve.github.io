Math-247

A resonant obstruction to the displayed physical-space maximal estimate

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x$. Carbery's Question 13 juxtaposes two distinct maximal estimates. The Fourier-space spherical partial sums are \[ S_Rf(x)=\int_{|\xi|\leq R}\widehat f(\xi)e^{2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\xi, \qquad S_*f(x)=\sup_{R>0}|S_Rf(x)|. \] The first question, equation (0.18), asks whether $\|S_*f\|_2\leq C_n\|f\|_2$, initially for Schwartz functions, with a constant independent of $f$.

The second question, equation (0.19), concerns instead the literal physical-space kernels \[ K_R(y)=e^{i|y|}|y|^{-(n+1)/2}\mathbf1_{\{|y|\leq R\}},\qquad T_Rf=f*K_R, \] and asks whether \[ \left\|\sup_{R>0}|T_Rf|\right\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)}\leq C_n\|f\|_2. \] For this literal interpretation take $n\geq2$, when the kernel is locally integrable at the origin and integrable for each finite $R$. At $n=1$, the displayed kernel is not locally integrable and requires an additional interpretation or regularization not supplied in the source. No lower cutoff, subtraction, or replacement by a two-branch oscillatory kernel is implicit in (0.19).

The attached aid reports that (0.19), with precisely this kernel, is false. Writing $m_R=\widehat{K_R}$ and $e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)$, it claims \[ m_R((2\pi)^{-1}e_1) =(2\pi)^{(n-1)/2}e^{i\pi(n-1)/4}\log R+O_n(1) \quad(R\to\infty). \] Its stated consequence is \[ \|T_R\|_{L^2\to L^2}\geq c_n\log R-C_n\quad(n\geq2), \] for suitable positive constants, and a stronger lower bound of order $R^{(n-3)/2}$ for $n\geq4$, using zero frequency. Thus even the individual truncations do not have a uniform $L^2$ bound, according to the aid.

For a modified leading kernel \[ (ae^{i|y|}+be^{-i|y|})|y|^{-(n+1)/2}\mathbf1_{\{|y|\leq R\}},\qquad a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}, \] the aid says that cancellation of this resonant logarithm requires \[ ae^{i\pi(n-1)/4}+be^{-i\pi(n-1)/4}=0. \] This is only a necessary cancellation condition for the indicated logarithmic contribution, not a sufficient condition for a maximal estimate.

The aid expressly says that this obstruction refutes only (0.19); it leaves the spherical Fourier partial-sum problem (0.18) open for $n\geq2$. These are supplied unreviewed counterexample and status claims. In particular, the registry's solved label must not be interpreted as a resolution of the higher-dimensional spherical partial-sum problem.

Short English statement

Separate the spherical Fourier partial-sum maximal question from a displayed physical-space outgoing-wave truncation. The supplied aid reports that the latter fails because its individual truncation norms grow, while explicitly leaving the former unresolved in dimensions at least two.
