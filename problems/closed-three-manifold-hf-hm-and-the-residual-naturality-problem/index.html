Math-302

Closed three-manifold HF=HM and the residual naturality problem

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question $1.29$, written in 2003, asks for the conjectured equivalence of Ozsváth--Szabó theory and Seiberg--Witten theory, and notes Yi-Jen Lee's proposed approach. The word equivalence can mean groups, modules, exact systems, chain constructions or a cobordism-natural TQFT; the source does not fix one of these strengths.

The aid reports that Kutluhan--Lee--Taubes established an integral, relatively graded, module-compatible equivalence of the three-flavor Heegaard Floer exact system with the corresponding balanced monopole Floer system for every closed oriented three-manifold $Y$ and fixed $\operatorname{Spin}^c$ structure $\mathfrak s$. The Heegaard flavors are $HF^-,HF^\infty,HF^+$; the corresponding monopole flavors require the standard matching of orientation, duality and notation. The stated compatibility includes the coefficient/module actions, conventionally encoded by ${\mathbb{Z}}[U]\otimes\bigwedge^*(H_1(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})/\mathrm{torsion})$, and the exact-system maps. Precise flavor and grading conventions must be checked in the primary theorem. This resolves the source's group-system interpretation, not automatically every stronger functorial interpretation.

The aid says that a full cobordism-map/TQFT naturality theorem is not supplied merely by that equivalence statement and was not verified in the primary literature checked. This is a qualified scope assessment, not a claim of a new universal impossibility or an independently updated open status.

Its formal comparison criterion is: after fixing coefficients, orientation, $\operatorname{Spin}^c$, flavor, duality and grading conventions, let $\Phi_Y,\Psi_Y:HF(Y)\to HM(Y)$ be two compatible equivalence families and put \[ \Delta_Y=\Psi_Y^{-1}\Phi_Y\in\operatorname{Aut}(HF(Y)). \] For a fixed decorated cobordism $W:Y_0\to Y_1$, write $F_W$ and $G_W$ for the matched HF and HM maps. If $\Phi_{Y_1}F_W=G_W\Phi_{Y_0}$, then \[ \Psi_{Y_1}F_W=G_W\Psi_{Y_0} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta_{Y_1}F_W=F_W\Delta_{Y_0}. \] Thus the residual question is an explicit automorphism-commutation condition, not a consequence of isomorphic groups alone.

For the mod-two plus tower at $S^3$, with its standard absolute grading, \[ HF^+(S^3;{\mathbb{F}}_2)\cong\mathcal T^+ ={\mathbb{F}}_2[U,U^{-1}]/U{\mathbb{F}}_2[U],\qquad\deg U=-2, \] every grading-degree-zero ${\mathbb{F}}_2[U]$-module automorphism is the identity. Hence a discrepancy satisfying exactly those conventions is forced to be trivial there. This single-object normalization does not prove the commutation condition for all cobordisms or manifolds.

Finally a $U$-equivariant module isomorphism restricts to isomorphisms of $\ker U^m$ and of their successive layers \[ \ker U^m/\ker U^{m-1}\qquad(m\geq1). \] The aid's transfer statement is in this module setting; it does not identify these layers with an additional Floer flavor without further comparison data. The appended "Fifth day" is the next heading, not part of the equivalence question.

Short English statement

The aid reports the closed-three-manifold group/module/exact-system HF--HM equivalence, while distinguishing full cobordism naturality. It reduces comparison of two equivalence families to a discrepancy commuting with cobordism maps. A mod-two graded plus-tower discrepancy is trivial at $S^3$, and $U$-kernel layers transfer under a module isomorphism.
