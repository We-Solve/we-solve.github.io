Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 6.1

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and $\mathbb F_p$ the field with $p$ elements. Under the finite-resolution convention, a group $H$ is of type $\mathrm{FP}$ over $\mathbb F_p$ if the trivial left $\mathbb F_p[H]$-module $\mathbb F_p$ has an exact projective resolution \[ 0\longrightarrow P_m\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_0\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p\longrightarrow0 \] for some finite $m$, with every $P_i$ finitely generated. A group is virtually of this type if it has a finite-index subgroup of this type. For $g\in G$, put \[ C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}. \] Question (Ian Leary). For every prime $p$, every group $G$ virtually of type $\mathrm{FP}$ over $\mathbb F_p$, and every $g\in G$ with order exactly $p$, is $C_G(g)$ virtually of type $\mathrm{FP}$ over $\mathbb F_p$? Equivalently, must there exist $L\leq C_G(g)$ with $[C_G(g):L]<\infty$ having the stated finiteness property?

The record reports an affirmative answer from Hamilton's Theorem E, with the stronger centralizer conclusion for an arbitrary $p$-subgroup $P\leq G$, where $C_G(P)=\{x\in G:xy=yx\text{ for every }y\in P\}$. The supplied statement does not define $\mathrm{FP}$: if it instead intends $\mathrm{FP}_{\infty}$ (a resolution with finitely generated projective modules in every degree, with no finite-length requirement), that is a different convention and requires checking against the cited theorem.

Short English statement

If a group is virtually homologically finite over $\mathbb F_p$, must the centralizer of an element of order $p$ have the same virtual finiteness property? The supplied literature summary reports yes, and a stronger result for centralizers of $p$-subgroups.
