Foliations and Diffeomorphism Groups — Question 49 (Calegari)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Identify the circle $S^1$ with the ideal boundary of the hyperbolic disk $\mathbb H^2$. A geodesic lamination is a closed subset of the space of unordered pairs of distinct points of $S^1$, such that the corresponding hyperbolic geodesics have pairwise disjoint interiors. Its leaves are these geodesics, and its gaps are the connected components of their complement in $\mathbb H^2$. The lamination is very full if every gap is a finite-sided ideal polygon, meaning that its vertices lie on $S^1$ and its sides are hyperbolic geodesics.

A circle action of a group $G$ is a homomorphism $\rho:G\to\operatorname{Homeo}(S^1)$. A lamination $\Lambda$ is invariant under this action if, for every $g\in G$ and every leaf with endpoints $\{x,y\}$, the pair $\{\rho(g)(x),\rho(g)(y)\}$ is also the endpoint pair of a leaf of $\Lambda$. The action is faithful if $\rho$ is injective. The group $G$ is residually finite if \[ \forall g\in G\setminus\{1_G\}\quad \exists\text{ a finite group }F\quad \exists\text{ a homomorphism }q:G\to F \quad q(g)\ne 1_F. \]

Source request, with unresolved action conventions. The supplied record attributes to Calegari the conjectural assertion that a laminar group preserving a very full lamination is residually finite. Its concluding request asks that a group acting on the circle with a very full invariant lamination be shown to satisfy the finite-separation condition above.

The record does not define "laminar group" or specify whether the circle action must be faithful or orientation preserving. If faithfulness is intended, the action hypothesis must include injectivity of $\rho$; if orientation preservation is intended, its image must lie in $\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(S^1)$. Neither convention is silently imposed here. Consequently, the admissible class of actions remains unresolved, and the source request is not asserted as a theorem for arbitrary, possibly nonfaithful actions. The record supplies no countability or finite-generation hypothesis.

Short English statement

The supplied question, attributed to Calegari, asks whether preserving a circle lamination whose gaps are all finite-sided ideal polygons forces the acting group to be residually finite. The source leaves faithfulness and orientation conventions unspecified, so these hypotheses require clarification before the intended conjecture has a definitive formulation.
