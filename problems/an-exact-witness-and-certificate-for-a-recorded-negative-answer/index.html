Research attempt: Verifying the recorded counterexample through an exact repair certificate

For a standalone check take $D=\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(0,1)\}$ and $\mathcal O=\{1,x,y,xy\}$. Evaluation on $D$ is injective on $\operatorname{span}\mathcal O$: a vanishing $a+bx+cy+dxy$ gives $a=0$, $b+c+d=0$, $2b+2c+4d=0$, and $c=0$, hence all coefficients vanish. The four monomials are the standard monomials of $(x^2,y^2)$. Their interpolation of its generators yields \[ f_1=x^2-xy,\qquad f_2=y^2-xy+x-y. \] Both vanish everywhere on $x=y$, so the common zero $(3,3)$ verifies the reported negative answer.

To test a stronger replacement for the failed corner-generation route, set $h=xy^2-3xy+2x$ and $J=(f_1,f_2,h)$. Here one can prove the exact ideal identity $J=I(D)$, not only equality of zero sets. In the quotient by $J$ the relations are \[ x^2=xy,\quad y^2=xy-x+y,\quad xy^2=3xy-2x,\quad x^2y=xy^2=3xy-2x. \] The last identity follows by multiplying $f_1$ by $y$. Multiplication by either variable therefore preserves the span of the four residue classes of $\mathcal O$: products of $1,x,y$ reduce by the first two relations, and products of $xy$ reduce by the last two. Since that span contains $1$, induction on total degree shows that every monomial, hence every polynomial, reduces to it. Thus $\dim_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[x,y]/J\le4$.

All three generators vanish on $D$, so evaluation descends from this quotient to $\mathbb Q^D$. The four classes are independent under evaluation by the earlier calculation. Every residue class consequently has a unique representative in $\operatorname{span}\mathcal O$, and a residue class vanishing on all four points is zero. Thus $\ker(\mathrm{evaluation})=J=I(D)$, as claimed. The same proof works over $\mathbb C$, because the evaluation determinant remains nonzero. In particular the repaired quotient has no extra multiplicities: it is isomorphic as an algebra to the fourfold product of the coefficient field by evaluation.

This calculation pinpoints the missing information in the original corner test. Closing the proposed quotient basis under multiplication needs the degree-three relation $h$, which is absent from interpolation of the two minimal generators alone.
