Some Questions — Question 38

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A bounded-degree graphing is a measurable graph $G=(X,\mu,E)$ on a probability space, with uniformly bounded vertex degrees and the usual measure-preservation condition. An expansion convention is the existence of $c>0$ such that \[ \int_A |\{y\notin A:(x,y)\in E\}|\,d\mu(x)\geq c\mu(A) \quad(0<\mu(A)\leq1/2). \] Strong ergodicity means that every sequence of measurable sets asymptotically invariant under the measure-preserving generators is trivial in measure: if $\mu(T_iA_m\mathbin{\triangle}A_m)\to0$ for each generator $T_i$, then $\mu(A_m)(1-\mu(A_m))\to0$.

Recorded question. Let $G$ be a bounded-degree expander graphing, or a strongly ergodic bounded-degree graphing. Let $H$ be a finite graph. If $G$ weakly contains $H$, must $G$ factor onto $H$?

The record leaves both relations undefined. One standard labeled, measure-preserving interpretation can be made explicit as follows. Realize the graphings by a common finite set of measure-preserving generator maps, say $T_1,\ldots,T_d$ on $(X,\mu)$ and $S_1,\ldots,S_d$ on the uniformly measured vertex set $V(H)$. For each radius $r$ and number of colors $k$, take the closed set of probability laws of rooted radius-$r$, generator-labeled, $k$-colored neighborhoods, over all measurable vertex colorings. Weak containment of $H$ in $G$ means that every such law attainable in $H$ is approximable by such laws from $G$, at every $r,k$. A factor map in this interpretation is a measurable map $\phi:X\to V(H)$ satisfying \[ \phi_*\mu=\text{uniform measure on }V(H),\qquad \phi(T_i x)=S_i\phi(x)\quad\text{for almost every }x\text{ and every }i. \] The question then asks whether the approximate finite colored statistics force this exact measurable factor, under either stated ergodicity/expansion hypothesis. This labeled convention is explanatory: the source must be checked for whether it instead intends unlabeled graphings, a locally bijective graph map, or a weaker graph-homomorphism notion, and for the measure on $H$.

The supplied research summary says that dropping the expansion/strong-ergodicity restriction gives a false general statement, due to counterexamples in the factor-of-i.i.d./graphing setting. It says the restricted version appears to remain open. The counterexamples are not supplied, and their reported existence is not a claimed answer to the restricted question.

Short English statement

If an expanding or strongly ergodic graphing can approximate the finite colored statistics of a finite graph, must it admit an exact measurable factor onto that graph? The definitions of weak containment and factor need source confirmation. Reported unrestricted counterexamples do not settle the restricted question.
