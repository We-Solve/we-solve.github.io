The source permits discount factor $\beta\in(0,1]$ and explains that favorable separability is needed for useful dimension dependence. We choose its undiscounted case $\beta=1$ and an exactly separable control system. The attempted extension from a small feedback approximation error to a good infinite-horizon controller fails even here unless the error preserves appropriate structure.

Exact dimension-linear feedback and a performance identity

For $d\ge1$, $a\in\mathbb R$, let $\dot x=ax+u$ and \[ J(x_0,u)=\int_0^\infty(\|x(t)\|^2+\|u(t)\|^2)dt, \quad q=\sqrt{a^2+1},\quad p=a+q>0. \] Controls are locally square integrable and trajectories satisfy the ODE; infinite cost is allowed.
Proposition: The optimal feedback is $u=-px$ and the optimal value is $p\|x_0\|^2$. For a scalar gain perturbation $u=-(p+\delta)x$ with $q+\delta>0$, the exact excess cost is \[ J(x_0,u)-p\|x_0\|^2=\frac{\delta^2}{2(q+\delta)}\|x_0\|^2. \] The optimal linear feedback is represented exactly by a ReLU network with $2d$ hidden units.
Proof: The identity $p^2-2ap=1$ gives \[ \|x\|^2+\|u\|^2=\|u+px\|^2-\frac{d}{dt}(p\|x\|^2). \] For any finite-cost control, integrability of $\|x(t)\|^2$ provides a sequence $T_j\to\infty$ with $x(T_j)\to0$. Integrating the identity to $T_j$ and passing to the limit yields $J=p\|x_0\|^2+\int_0^\infty\|u+px\|^2dt\ge p\|x_0\|^2$. The feedback $-px$ has $x(t)=e^{-qt}x_0$ and attains equality. Infinite-cost controls cannot do better.

For the perturbed gain, $x(t)=e^{-(q+\delta)t}x_0$, so direct integration gives $J=(1+(p+\delta)^2)\|x_0\|^2/[2(q+\delta)]$. Subtracting $p\|x_0\|^2$ and using $1+p^2=2pq$ leaves the stated expression. Finally $x_j=\operatorname{ReLU}(x_j)-\operatorname{ReLU}(-x_j)$ realizes each coordinate with two units, followed by multiplication by $-p$. ∎

Uniformly small bias can give infinite cost

In one dimension replace the exact feedback by $\widetilde u(x)=-px+\varepsilon$ with any nonzero constant $\varepsilon$. Its uniform error is exactly $|\varepsilon|$, but the closed-loop trajectory tends to $\varepsilon/q\ne0$. Thus its state-cost integral is infinite, even when $x_0=0$ and the optimal cost is zero. The ODE remains stable toward a shifted equilibrium; the failure is of undiscounted optimal regulation, not of existence of trajectories. Discounting can remove this particular infinite-cost conclusion, so $\beta=1$ is essential.

Verification and remaining gaps

Numerical algebra checks the gain-performance identity for several $a,\delta$ and the ReLU identity. The proof shows that representation size and uniform approximation alone do not establish general optimal feedback guarantees. Nonlinear dynamics, training complexity, certified stability and dimension dependence beyond this separable family remain unresolved.
