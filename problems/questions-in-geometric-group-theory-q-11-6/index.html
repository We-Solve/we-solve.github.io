Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 11.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Thompson's group $F$ is the group of increasing piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of $[0,1]$ having finitely many breakpoints, all dyadic rational, and slopes integral powers of two. Composition is the group operation. It is finitely generated, so choose a finite word metric. Give $F\times\mathbb Z$ a product word metric; changing either finite generating set does not change the quasi-isometry question.

Metric spaces $X,Y$ are quasi-isometric if there is a map $f:X\to Y$ and constants $L\geq1,C\geq0$ such that \[ L^{-1}d_X(x,x')-C\leq d_Y(fx,fx')\leq Ld_X(x,x')+C, \] and every point of $Y$ is within distance $C$ of the image.

Is $F$ quasi-isometric to $F\times\mathbb Z$? More generally, does there exist any finitely generated group $G$ for which $G$ and $G\times\mathbb Z$ are quasi-isometric?

The second part is an existence question, not the plainly false assertion that every finitely generated group has this property. The map is allowed to mix the product coordinates, and no compatibility with a subgroup inclusion or projection is required. Having quasi-isometric embeddings in both directions would not alone suffice: the definition also demands a coarsely surjective map. The added factor has its unbounded integer-line metric, so replacing it by a finite cyclic group changes the issue. Neither equality of growth rates nor abstract isomorphism is being asserted as the required criterion.

Short English statement

Is Thompson’s group F quasi-isometric to F times the integers, and can any finitely generated group absorb such a factor?
