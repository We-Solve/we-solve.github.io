Math-3658

Mordell–Lang Finiteness Outside Anomalous Loci

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write ${\mathbb{C}}^*={\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}$, and equip the algebraic torus $T=({\mathbb{C}}^*)^n$ with coordinatewise multiplication. The coordinatewise exponential map is \[ \exp\colon{\mathbb{C}}^n\longrightarrow T,\qquad (z_1,\ldots,z_n)\longmapsto(e^{z_1},\ldots,e^{z_n}). \] Under the usual interpretation of the source notation, $A$ is a complex algebraic subvariety of $T$, $L$ is a complex linear subspace of ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, and $G$ is a subgroup of $T$. These interpretations are necessary to read the question in its customary setting, but are not explicitly specified in the supplied statement. For an abelian group $B$, the usual meaning of finite rank is \[ \operatorname{rank}(B):=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(B\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}})<\infty. \] In particular, this convention does not require finite generation.

The source does not define its anomalous points. For comparison, a customary convention for an irreducible algebraic subvariety $A\subseteq T$ calls a positive-dimensional irreducible subvariety $Y\subseteq A$ anomalous if there exist an algebraic subgroup $H\subseteq T$ and $t\in T$ such that \[ Y\subseteq A\cap tH, \qquad \dim Y>\max\{0,\dim A+\dim H-n\}. \] The anomalous locus is then the union of such $Y$. This is an explanatory convention, not a verified identification of the source's intended definition; the source also does not specify irreducibility of $A$.

Source question, with unresolved qualifications retained. For every choice of the above data, with $n$ a positive integer and $k$ a nonnegative integer under the usual dimension conventions, suppose that \[ \operatorname{codim}_{T}A>k,\qquad \dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}L=k,\qquad \exp(L)\subseteq G,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\left(G/\exp(L)\right)<\infty. \] Let $A'\subseteq A$ be what the source calls "a subvariety of its anomalous points." Is \[ \#\left((A\setminus A')\cap G\right)<\infty? \] The wording describes $A'$ as contained in the anomalous locus; it does not say that $A'$ contains the entire anomalous locus or equals it. The intended definition of anomalous points and the intended requirement on $A'$ must therefore be resolved before this becomes an unambiguous conjecture. No replacement of $A'$ by the entire anomalous locus is made here.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether a subgroup containing the exponential of a $k$-dimensional complex linear space, with finite-rank quotient, meets an algebraic subvariety of codimension greater than $k$ in only finitely many points after deleting a specified exceptional subvariety $A'$. The anomalous-point convention and the extent of the deletion are unresolved. The supplied open-status label is dated curation, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.
