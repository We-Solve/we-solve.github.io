Math-7356

Pattern Characterization of Fractional Powers in Words

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be an alphabet, let $A^*$ be the set of finite words over $A$, and put $A^+=A^*\setminus\{\varepsilon\}$. A factor is a contiguous finite subword. A pattern is a nonempty finite word over an alphabet $V$ of variables. A nonerasing morphism $h:V^*\to A^*$ satisfies $h(uv)=h(u)h(v)$ and $h(a)\ne\varepsilon$ for every variable $a\in V$. A factor matches a pattern $p$ if it equals $h(p)$ for such a morphism.

The source does not define an $\alpha$-power. Under the exact-exponent convention, a nonempty word $w$ is an $\alpha$-power if there exist $u\in A^+$, an integer $k\geq 1$, and a proper prefix $v$ of $u$, possibly empty, such that \[ w=u^k v,\qquad \alpha=k+\frac{|v|}{|u|}. \] A different possible intended convention is a word admitting such a representation with exponent at least $\alpha$. These conventions must not be identified without clarification; in particular, the exact-exponent definition only produces rational exponents, whereas the supplied parameter ranges over all noninteger real numbers greater than $1$.

Write $\mathcal T_\alpha(A)\subseteq A^+$ for the class of powers under the intended convention, and let $\mathcal W(A)$ denote the intended class of test words. With those choices fixed, the source asks whether, for each $\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $\alpha>1$, there exists a finite or infinite set $P\subseteq V^+$ such that \[ \begin{aligned} \forall x\in\mathcal W(A),\quad &\bigl(\exists w\in\mathcal T_\alpha(A):\ w\text{ is a factor of }x\bigr)\\ &\quad\Longleftrightarrow \bigl(\exists p\in P\ \exists h:V^*\to A^*\text{ nonerasing}:\ h(p)\text{ is a factor of }x\bigr). \end{aligned} \] The set $P$ must work for every test word $x$; the witnessing pattern and morphism may depend on $x$. The source does not specify the size of $A$, whether $x$ is finite or infinite, or whether one set $P$ must work uniformly over all alphabets. Thus this is a conditional formalization of the supplied question, with those quantifiers and the power convention requiring clarification.

Short English statement

For each noninteger real $\alpha>1$, can occurrences of $\alpha$-powers be characterized exactly by occurrences of patterns whose variables are replaced by nonempty words? The power convention, alphabet quantifiers, and class of test words remain unspecified. The supplied literature assessment concerns sum-square avoidance, a different stated problem, and does not establish the status of this question.
