Orbit Decompositions in Wonderful Group Schemes

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not specify the hypotheses on the field $k$, its valuation ring $\mathfrak{o}$, the group $G$, or the parameter $f$, nor does it give the construction of the object called the wonderful group scheme $G_{x,f}$. The following question is therefore conditional on recovering those data and hypotheses from the cited source.

Write $\mathcal{B}(G)$ for the Bruhat--Tits building intended in the source. Let $C\subseteq\mathcal{B}(G)$ be a chamber containing $x$, and put \[ I:=G(k)_{C,0},\qquad X:=G_{x,f}(\mathfrak{o}), \] where the source identifies $I$ as the associated Iwahori subgroup and $X$ denotes the set of $\mathfrak{o}$-valued points. The convention for chambers and whether boundary points are permitted are not specified.

Orbit-classification question. For the intended action of $I\times I$ on $X$, classify all orbits. More explicitly, after the action \[ \alpha:(I\times I)\times X\longrightarrow X \] has been specified, determine a parameter set $A$ and a complete invariant $q:X\to A$ such that $q$ is surjective and, for every $u,v\in X$, \[ q(u)=q(v) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists(i_1,i_2)\in I\times I:\ v=\alpha((i_1,i_2),u). \] This is the requested analogue of the classification of $(B\times_k B)$-orbits in the wonderful compactification mentioned in the excerpt. Neither $B$ nor the action on $X$ is defined in the supplied material; in particular, a left--right multiplication formula is not imposed here.

Stable-piece question. Describe the diagonal-stable pieces of $X$ intended by the source, in analogy with the stable-piece decomposition of the wonderful compactification. For a group $H$, its diagonal subgroup is \[ \operatorname{Diag}(H):=\{(h,h):h\in H\}\subseteq H\times H. \] The original excerpt specifically refers to $G_{x,f}(\mathfrak{o})^{\operatorname{Diag}}$-stable pieces in $G_{x,f}(\mathfrak{o})$. It does not define the relevant diagonal action or the pieces. Thus no precise partition, parametrization, or equivalence with diagonal-subgroup orbits can be asserted from this record alone.

The excerpt motivates both questions by similarities with the wonderful compactification when $f=0$. It describes its Theorems 1 and 3 as possible first steps toward a Bruhat--Tits theory for wonderful compactifications, and expresses an expectation of extending the results to more general wonderful varieties in the sense of its reference [4]. These are source qualifications and motivations, not additional established conclusions or specified extension statements.

Short English statement

For a chamber $C$ containing $x$, classify the orbits of the associated Iwahori subgroup product $G(k)_{C,0}\times G(k)_{C,0}$ on $G_{x,f}(\mathfrak{o})$, and describe the intended diagonal-stable pieces, by analogy with the wonderful compactification. The construction, hypotheses, actions, and meaning of stable pieces require clarification from the source before this becomes a fully specified mathematical problem.
