Math-829

AS-Gorenstein Properties of Noetherian Hopf Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

AS-Gorenstein question. Is every noetherian Hopf algebra AS-Gorenstein? Let $H$ be a Hopf algebra over the intended field $k$, with coproduct, counit $\varepsilon:H\to k$, and antipode. In the usual two-sided noetherian convention, $H$ satisfies the ascending chain condition on both left and right ideals. The one-dimensional trivial left and right modules $k$ are defined through $\varepsilon$.

A standard ungraded definition of Artin--Schelter Gorenstein of dimension $d\ge0$ requires finite injective dimensions\[\operatorname{injdim}_H H=\operatorname{injdim}_{H^{\mathrm{op}}}H=d\] and\[\operatorname{Ext}_H^i(k,H)=0=\operatorname{Ext}_{H^{\mathrm{op}}}^i(k,H)\quad(i\ne d),\qquad \dim_k\operatorname{Ext}_H^d(k,H)=\dim_k\operatorname{Ext}_{H^{\mathrm{op}}}^d(k,H)=1.\] The question asks whether, for every such $H$, some finite $d$ satisfies these conditions. The dimension may depend on $H$. No triviality of the residual one-dimensional module actions is required by the displayed dimension condition. The source does not define AS-Gorenstein or specify a graded convention; the standard augmented ungraded interpretation is explicit and requires source checking.

The assertion must not be strengthened silently to AS-regularity, which additionally involves finite global dimension. Nor are commutativity, affineness, finite Gelfand--Kirillov dimension, a polynomial identity, finite dimensionality, or a bijective antipode assumed merely from the word Hopf. The field and exact noetherian convention are not stated in the excerpt. The original passage also asks how preceding finite-dimensional ideas generalize to infinite-dimensional Hopf algebras, but those ideas are not reproduced; that programmatic request is retained without inventing an additional theorem.

The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 attributes the generalization and AS-Gorenstein questions to Brown--Goodearl and labels them open. It supplies a Brown report reference but no precise theorem scope or comparison of subclasses. This dated assessment requires independent verification and is not a newly checked statement of current status.

Short English statement

Must every noetherian Hopf algebra have finite two-sided injective dimension and the one-dimensional trivial-module Ext pattern defining AS-Gorensteinness? This does not require finite global dimension or impose extra finiteness conditions absent from the source. The preceding finite-dimensional generalization program lacks its context.
