Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.41

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be a countably infinite set, and let $\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ be its group of all permutations. For a cardinal $\kappa\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\aleph_0\}$, define the set of $\kappa$-sections by \[ \mathcal S_\kappa(\Omega)=\left\{\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega): |\mathcal P|=\kappa,\ \bigcup_{A\in\mathcal P}A=\Omega,\ A\cap B=\varnothing\text{ for distinct }A,B\in\mathcal P,\ |A|=\aleph_0\text{ for every }A\in\mathcal P\right\}. \] Thus the blocks of a section are not labeled. Put $I_k=\{1,\ldots,k\}$ for finite $k\geq2$, and $I_{\aleph_0}={\mathbb{N}}$. An ordered $\kappa$-section is an indexed family $(A_i)_{i\in I_\kappa}$ whose distinct blocks form a member of $\mathcal S_\kappa(\Omega)$.

A subgroup $G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ acts on sections by $g\mathcal P=\{g[A]:A\in\mathcal P\}$, and on ordered sections by $g(A_i)_{i\in I_\kappa}=(g[A_i])_{i\in I_\kappa}$. Transitivity on sections means that for every $\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\in\mathcal S_\kappa(\Omega)$ some $g\in G$ satisfies $g\mathcal P=\mathcal Q$. Transitivity on ordered sections requires, for every two such indexed families, a single $g\in G$ satisfying $g[A_i]=B_i$ for every index $i$. Transitivity on $\Omega$ means that for all $x,y\in\Omega$ some $g\in G$ satisfies $g(x)=y$.

The supplied record contains the following questions, retaining its part labels.

  1. (b) For a specified finite integer $k\geq2$, does there exist $G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ that is transitive on $\mathcal S_k(\Omega)$ but not transitive on $\mathcal S_{k+1}(\Omega)$? Here failure of transitivity means that there exist $\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\in\mathcal S_{k+1}(\Omega)$ such that $g\mathcal P\ne\mathcal Q$ for every $g\in G$. This is the finite-parameter reading of the source: the source does not explicitly specify the range or outer quantifier for $k$, or require $G$ to be transitive on $\Omega$ in this part. Consequently, whether existence is requested for every finite $k$, or for some $k$, remains unresolved. If $k=\aleph_0$ were included and $k+1$ meant cardinal addition, the two section cardinalities would coincide.
  2. (c) Does there exist $G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ such that $G$ is transitive on $\Omega$, transitive on $\mathcal S_{\aleph_0}(\Omega)$, and $G\ne\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$?
  3. (a), archived For a specified $\kappa\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\aleph_0\}$, does there exist a subgroup $G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ transitive on $\Omega$ and on $\mathcal S_\kappa(\Omega)$, but not transitive on ordered $\kappa$-sections? The inclusion of $\aleph_0$ here follows the explicit range in the supplied archive commentary; the question text itself only writes $k\geq2$.

The supplied archive attributes an affirmative answer to (a) to P. M. Neumann and gives the construction \[ G_U=\{g\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega):\mathrm{Fix}(g)\in U\},\qquad \mathrm{Fix}(g)=\{x\in\Omega:g(x)=x\}, \] where $U$ is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\Omega$. Here an ultrafilter is a family $U\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega)$ containing $\Omega$, excluding $\varnothing$, closed under finite intersections and supersets, and containing exactly one of $A$ and $\Omega\setminus A$ for every $A\subseteq\Omega$; nonprincipal means that it contains no finite subset. The archive asserts that this single group has the required section and ordered-section behavior for every $2\leq\kappa\leq\aleph_0$. This is a supplied historical assertion, not a proof or an independently verified result in this draft.

The supplied main-section commentary labels (c) solved affirmatively, describes a 2026 AI solution by Achyuth Jayadevan as confirmed by S. M. Corson, and also reports a 2025 result of S. M. Corson and S. Shelah establishing consistency of an affirmative answer with ZFC. These are distinct supplied claims: consistency with ZFC is not itself an assertion of a proof in ZFC. The registry labels the overall record partially solved; no independent status assessment is made here.

Short English statement

For permutations of a countably infinite set, ask whether transitivity on partitions into $k$ infinite blocks can hold while transitivity on partitions into $k+1$ infinite blocks fails. The record also asks for a proper, point-transitive subgroup transitive on countably infinite partitions, and archives the question of transitivity on unlabeled partitions without transitivity on labeled partitions. Parts (a) and (c) have supplied affirmative solution reports; the parameter quantifier in (b) remains unclear.
