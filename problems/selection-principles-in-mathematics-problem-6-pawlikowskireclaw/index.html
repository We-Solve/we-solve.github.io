Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 6 ([pawlikowskireclaw])

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a class $\mathcal J$ of sets of reals, define its Borel conjecture by \[ \operatorname{BC}(\mathcal J):\quad \forall X\in\mathcal J\; (X\text{ is countable}). \] Here countable means finite or countably infinite.

The supplied excerpt does not define its selection-principle notation. The following gives a conventional reconstruction, subject to verification against the source. For $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$, let $\mathrm B(X)$ consist of countable families $\mathcal U$ of Borel subsets of the subspace $X$ such that \[ \bigcup\mathcal U=X \quad\text{and}\quad X\notin\mathcal U. \] In this reconstruction, countable families may be finite. Say that $X$ satisfies $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm B,\mathrm B)$ if \[ \forall (\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\mathrm B(X)^{{\mathbb{N}}}\; \exists (\mathcal F_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\; \left[ \left(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \mathcal F_n\subseteq\mathcal U_n \text{ is finite}\right) \land \bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal F_n\in\mathrm B(X) \right]. \] Empty finite selections are allowed. The union in the last expression is a family of selected Borel sets.

Question. Is Borel's Conjecture for $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm B,\mathrm B)$ consistent? Under the usual interpretation that consistency is with $\mathrm{ZFC}$, the requested consistency statement is \[ \operatorname{Con}\!\left( \mathrm{ZFC}+ \left[\forall X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}},\quad X\models\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm B,\mathrm B) \ \Longrightarrow\ X\text{ is countable}\right] \right). \] The excerpt does not explicitly specify the background theory or the cover conventions used above; these are reconstruction qualifications requiring review.

The source also states that Borel's Conjecture is consistent for all but three properties in its "Borel case." The excerpt does not identify that collection or the three exceptions, so this contextual assertion is retained as supplied context and does not resolve the question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether it is consistent that every set of reals satisfying the Borel finite-selection property is countable. That property permits finitely many choices from each cover in any sequence of countable Borel covers, with the chosen sets together covering the space. The cover conventions and the interpretation of consistency with $\mathrm{ZFC}$ are reconstructed and require source verification.
