Full-Range Calderón–Zygmund Estimates for the $p$-Laplacian

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

On the domain intended by the report, consider \[ \operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du) =\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F). \] In the usual scalar elliptic interpretation, $\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ is open, $p>1$, $u$ is scalar valued, $F:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^n$, and $Du$ is the distributional gradient. These domain, dimension, parameter, and solution-class conventions are not explicitly supplied.

When the two fluxes are locally integrable, the displayed equation means \[ \int_\Omega |Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x =\int_\Omega |F|^{p-2}F\cdot D\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x \qquad(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)). \] The nonlinear flux at zero is interpreted as zero. For a finite exponent $q>0$, membership in $L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}$ means finite integral of the $q$-th power on every compactly contained subdomain, including the quasi-norm interpretation if $q<1$.

The requested implication is \[ F\in L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Du\in L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega) \qquad\text{throughout the source's full range }q\ge p-1. \] The lower endpoint $q=p-1$ is explicitly included in the source and is not silently replaced by $q>p-1$, weak-$L^q$, or a smaller open range. Whether the range includes $q=\infty$ is not stated; no infinite-exponent assertion is added. The supplied statement asks for strong local membership, not an explicit norm estimate or boundary regularity result.

The admissible solution notion is crucial. Energy solutions in $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and very weak or other distributional solutions have different built-in integrability, especially below $p$. The source gives no a priori gradient integrability, admissibility or approximation condition, scalar-versus-system convention, or restrictions on $p,n$. These must be recovered before asserting the implication for every distributional solution. The cited parabolic extension is contextual and does not automatically settle this elliptic equation.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the gradient inherits the same strong local integrability as the data throughout the stated exponent range, including $q=p-1$. The solution class and endpoint conventions are missing, and no weak-space or parabolic substitute is imposed.
