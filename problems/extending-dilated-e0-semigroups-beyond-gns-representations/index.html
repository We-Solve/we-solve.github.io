Extending Dilated E0-Semigroups Beyond GNS Representations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A Markov semigroup $T_t$ is a continuous semigroup of unital completely positive maps on the specified operator algebra $B$. A dilation consists of an embedding $i:B\to A$, a conditional expectation or compression $p:A\to B$, and endomorphisms $\vartheta_t$ with $T_t=p\vartheta_t i$. The GNS construction for $p$ gives a Hilbert $B$-module $E$ and a represented algebra in $\mathcal B^a(E)$. An automorphic dilation has invertible $*$-automorphisms; an inner one is implemented by unitaries. Respecting the GNS kernel means that the kernel of this representation is carried into itself, so the dilation descends to the represented algebra. Extending to $\mathcal B^a(E)$ means finding unital endomorphisms of the full adjointable-operator algebra agreeing with that descended semigroup.
Source scope and qualifications.
Both kernel invariance and the full-algebra extension are needed. The phrase not too big is conjectural; generation by i(B) and its time shifts is the report's proposed example of minimality, not a proved sufficient condition.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let a Markov semigroup have a dilation to an automorphism or $E_0$-semigroup on an algebra $A$, and suppose $A$ is generated by the image of the original algebra and its time shifts. Must the dilation respect the GNS kernel, and must the induced $E_0$-semigroup on the represented subalgebra extend to all of $\mathcal B^a(E)$? What weaker minimality hypotheses on $A$ suffice?

Short English statement

Find minimality assumptions ensuring that a general Markov dilation descends through its GNS representation and extends to all adjointable module operators.

A sufficient descent condition in the scalar automorphic case.
Let $A$ be a unital $C^*$-algebra with state $\phi$ and automorphism $\alpha$ satisfying $\phi\circ\alpha=\phi$. On the GNS dense domain define $U[a]=[\alpha(a)]$. The identity \[ \|[\alpha(a)]\|^2=\phi(\alpha(a^*a))=\phi(a^*a) \] makes this well defined and isometric. Surjectivity follows from $\alpha^{-1}$, so $U$ is unitary. It implements $U\pi(a)U^*=\pi(\alpha(a))$. Therefore $\ker\pi$ is invariant in both directions, and conjugation by $U$ extends the descended automorphism to all $B(\mathcal H_\phi)$.
Continuity and the semigroup distinction.
For a point-norm continuous state-preserving automorphism group $\alpha_t$, the estimate $\|[\alpha_t(a)]-[a]\|\leq\|\alpha_t(a)-a\|$ gives strong continuity of $U_t$ on a dense domain and then everywhere. If $\alpha$ is only an endomorphism, the same construction gives an isometry, whose conjugation map need not be unital on all $B(\mathcal H_\phi)$: $UIU^*=UU^*$ can be a proper projection. Thus the extension argument relies essentially on invertibility.
Original-target gap.
The source uses a conditional-expectation GNS module and does not assume this scalar state invariance. Minimal generation by time shifts has not been shown to imply the needed kernel invariance or a unitary module implementation. The calculation proves a restricted sufficient case, not the proposed minimality conjecture, and yields no weaker minimality criterion for general $E_0$ dilations.
