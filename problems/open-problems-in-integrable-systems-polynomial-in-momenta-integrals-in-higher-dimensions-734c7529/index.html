Open Problems in Integrable Systems — Polynomial in momenta integrals in higher dimensions.

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a connected symmetric space in the intended metric category, and let $\nabla$ be its Levi--Civita connection. A symmetric contravariant tensor $K$ of rank $r\ge1$ is a Killing tensor when \[ \nabla^{(i_0}K^{i_1\cdots i_r)}=0. \] A Killing vector field $X$ satisfies $\mathcal L_Xg=0$. Write $\odot$ for the symmetrized tensor product. The question is whether, for every such $M$, rank $r$, and Killing tensor $K$, there exist finitely many Killing vector fields and real constants such that \[ K=\sum_{j=1}^N a_j\, X_{j,1}\odot\cdots\odot X_{j,r},\qquad a_j\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] The coefficients are constants, not arbitrary functions on $M$.

Equivalently, on $T^*M$ with its canonical Poisson bracket and geodesic Hamiltonian \[ H(x,p)=\tfrac12 g^{ij}(x)p_ip_j, \] let $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{poly}}$ be the real algebra of smooth fiberwise-polynomial functions of finite degree satisfying $\{H,I\}=0$. For a Killing vector $X$, the corresponding linear integral is $I_X(x,p)=p(X_x)$. The equivalent algebra-generation question is \[ \mathcal I_{\mathrm{poly}}\stackrel{?}{=}{\mathbb{R}}[I_X:X\text{ a Killing vector field}], \] with constants included. Homogeneous polynomial integrals correspond to Killing tensors by $I_K(x,p)=K^{i_1\cdots i_r}(x)p_{i_1}\cdots p_{i_r}$.

The supplied research summary reports a negative answer in general, attributing counterexamples to Matveev--Nikolayevsky, arXiv:2312.16518. It supplies no particular counterexample or its hypotheses. The excerpt does not specify Riemannian versus pseudo-Riemannian signature, local versus global symmetric spaces, or local versus global tensors; the global-tensor reading above requires the corresponding source category to be checked.

Short English statement

Are all Killing tensors on a symmetric space built from Killing vector fields using sums and symmetric products? Equivalently, are polynomial geodesic integrals generated by linear ones? The supplied summary reports counterexamples to the general assertion.
