Definitions and notation.
The target concerns observed spacings between real cars parked on a long street. A microscopic stochastic model specifies the actions and interactions of individual drivers and cars, from which spacing statistics are derived. GUE and GOE are Gaussian unitary and orthogonal random-matrix ensembles; their normalized local-spacing laws are different. Random sequential interval deposition is one possible parking model, not the model prescribed by this source.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Develop a microscopic model for parked-car spacings explaining the source’s observed random-matrix behavior: GUE statistics for two-way-street parking on the right and GOE statistics for left-side parking on one-way streets.
Scope and source qualification.
The source refers to empirical spacing studies by Šeba and others. A theorem for the classical random sequential adsorption model alone would be a different target unless it explains those observations.

Short English statement

Build a microscopic model explaining the different random-matrix spacing statistics observed for parked cars.

A local-gap model and what it can explain.
Consider $N\ge2$ nonnegative gaps $s_i$ with total free length $L$, and postulate a density on the simplex proportional to $\prod_i s_i^\beta$, where $\beta>-1$. This is a modelling choice, not a derived driver law. Integrating out $s_2,\ldots,s_N$ by scaling them with $L-s_1$ gives a one-gap density \[p_N(s)=C_N s^\beta(L-s)^{(N-1)(\beta+1)-1},\quad0<s<L, \] where $C_N$ normalizes the integral. At fixed $N$, the small-gap exponent is exactly $\beta$, so choosing $\beta=1$ or $2$ can mimic different repulsion powers.
The large-system marginal.
Take $L=N$, giving mean gap one. The law is equivalently obtained by independent gamma variables $Y_i$ of shape $\beta+1$ and common rate one, setting $s_i=NY_i/\sum_jY_j$; the change of variables from total $\sum Y_j$ and normalized proportions factors the gamma density. Since the sample mean tends to $\beta+1$, each fixed $s_i$ converges in distribution to $Y_i/(\beta+1)$. Its density is \[p(s)=\frac{(\beta+1)^{\beta+1}}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}s^\beta e^{-(\beta+1)s},\quad s>0. \] The mean and variance of the gamma variables are finite, so even Chebyshev's inequality suffices for the required convergence of their mean. This limiting law has an exponential tail; choosing a repulsion exponent has not selected a random-matrix spacing law.
First gap.
The gap-product ansatz inserts $\beta$ by hand and gives no mechanism by which traffic direction or parking side changes it. More fundamentally, a local one-gap interaction need not reproduce the long-range correlations of a random-matrix point process. A microscopic driver-and-car rule leading to the observed two symmetry classes, with a check against empirical observations, remains missing. Random sequential deposition would likewise require a demonstrated connection to those observations. No current empirical claim or universal parking law is made here.
