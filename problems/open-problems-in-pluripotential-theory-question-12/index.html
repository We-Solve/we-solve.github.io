Open Problems in Pluripotential Theory — Question 12

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For notation, let $n\geq 1$ and let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ be a domain. This is a conventional local framework for interpreting the question; the supplied excerpt does not specify the dimension or domain. Write $\operatorname{PSH}(\Omega)$ for the plurisubharmonic functions on $\Omega$: upper semicontinuous functions whose restrictions to complex lines are subharmonic or identically $-\infty$. The condition $u\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ means that $u$ is essentially bounded on every relatively compact open subset of $\Omega$.

Fix the normalization $dd^c=i\partial\bar\partial$. For locally bounded plurisubharmonic $u$, interpret $(dd^c u)^n$ as the Bedford--Taylor complex Monge--Ampère measure. Define its range by \[ \mathcal R(\Omega)=\left\{\mu:\ \mu\text{ is a positive Radon measure on }\Omega,\quad \mu=(dd^c u)^n\text{ for some }u\in\operatorname{PSH}(\Omega)\cap L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\right\}. \] The normalization is an explicit convention for this draft, not one specified in the supplied excerpt.

Question attributed to Bedford, as recalled in Question 12. Can membership in $\mathcal R(\Omega)$ be characterized by a uniform integrability condition?

The supplied formulation does not specify the family of functions or measures to which uniform integrability should apply, a reference measure or capacity, or whether the condition is local or global. Thus it does not supply a definite necessary-and-sufficient condition that can be written as an equivalence. It also gives no boundary conditions or total-mass restriction. These omissions remain unresolved rather than being added as hypotheses.

The surrounding discussion concerns bounded and continuous plurisubharmonic functions, and also mentions quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. The displayed Bedford question specifically concerns $\operatorname{PSH}\cap L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}$; continuity, global boundedness, and the quasi-plurisubharmonic setting are not imposed in that question.

Short English statement

Bedford asks whether the measures obtained as complex Monge--Ampère measures of locally bounded plurisubharmonic functions admit a characterization by uniform integrability. The excerpt leaves the proposed integrability condition and several ambient specifications unstated, so this remains a qualified question rather than a fully specified equivalence.
