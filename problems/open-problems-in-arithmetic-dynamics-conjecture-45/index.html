Open Problems in Arithmetic Dynamics — Conjecture 45

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field, and let $f\in K(x)$ have degree $d\geq 2$. Regard $f$ as a morphism ${\mathbb{P}}^1\to{\mathbb{P}}^1$, so that iteration is defined even when an orbit passes through infinity. Write $f^{\circ n}$ for its $n$-fold iterate. A point $\alpha\in K\subset{\mathbb{P}}^1(K)$ is periodic if $f^{\circ m}(\alpha)=\alpha$ for some integer $m\geq 1$.

For each integer $n\geq 1$, choose relatively prime polynomials $P_n,Q_n\in K[x]$ such that \[ f^{\circ n}(x)=\frac{P_n(x)}{Q_n(x)}. \] Factor the nonzero polynomial $P_n-\alpha Q_n$ over $K$ as \[ P_n(x)-\alpha Q_n(x)=c_n\prod_{j=1}^{r_n}h_{n,j}(x)^{e_{n,j}}, \] where $c_n\in K^\times$, the $h_{n,j}$ are distinct monic irreducible polynomials of positive degree, and $e_{n,j}\geq 1$. For a nonzero constant polynomial the product is empty. Use the following interpretation of eventual stability over $K$: \[ (f,\alpha)\text{ is eventually stable over }K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists B\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\ \forall n\geq 1, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^{r_n}e_{n,j}\leq B. \] Thus irreducible factors are counted with multiplicity. The supplied record does not define eventual stability; this explicit conventional interpretation, including the multiplicity convention, requires confirmation against the cited source.

Conjecture (Jones--Levy, 2017; supplied reference MR3704363, Conjecture 1.2). For every number field $K$, every rational function $f\in K(x)$ of degree at least $2$, and every $\alpha\in K$ satisfying \[ \forall m\geq 1,\qquad f^{\circ m}(\alpha)\neq\alpha, \] the pair $(f,\alpha)$ is eventually stable over $K$. The bound $B$ may depend on $K$, $f$, and $\alpha$. The hypothesis excludes periodic points but does not exclude nonperiodic preperiodic points.

Short English statement

For a rational map of degree at least two over a number field and a nonperiodic $K$-rational point $\alpha$, the conjecture predicts a uniform bound on the number of irreducible factors over $K$ of the numerators of $f^{\circ n}(x)-\alpha$, counted with multiplicity, as $n$ varies. The supplied registry labels this conjecture open; that status is not independently verified.
