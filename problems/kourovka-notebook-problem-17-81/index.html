Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.81

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a free group, with no restriction on its rank, and let $r_1,r_2,r_3\in G$. For $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, let $R_i=\langle r_i\rangle^G$ denote the smallest normal subgroup of $G$ containing $r_i$. For subgroups $A,B\leq G$, write \[ [A,B]=\langle a^{-1}b^{-1}ab : a\in A,\ b\in B\rangle. \] Set $N=R_1\cap R_2\cap R_3$ and \[ D_3^{+}=[R_1,R_2\cap R_3]\,[R_2,R_1\cap R_3]\,[R_3,R_1\cap R_2]. \] These commutator subgroups are normal in $G$ and contained in $N$, so their product is a normal subgroup of $N$.

The source defines, for normal subgroups $R_1,\ldots,R_n$, \[ [[R_1,\ldots,R_n]] =\prod_{\substack{I\cup J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\\ I\cap J=\varnothing}} \left[\bigcap_{i\in I}R_i,\bigcap_{j\in J}R_j\right]. \] It does not specify whether $I$ and $J$ must both be nonempty. With that restriction, its denominator for $n=3$ is precisely $D_3^{+}$.

Question under the nonempty-partition interpretation. For every free group $G$ and every triple $r_1,r_2,r_3\in G$, is \[ \frac{R_1\cap R_2\cap R_3}{D_3^{+}} \] torsion-free? Explicitly, must \[ \forall x\in N\ \forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\qquad x^m\in D_3^{+}\ \Longrightarrow\ x\in D_3^{+} \] hold?

Unresolved convention. If empty parts are included and an empty intersection is interpreted as $G$, the displayed source definition instead gives \[ D_3^{0}=D_3^{+}[G,N]. \] The literal question under that convention concerns $N/D_3^{0}$. The supplied record does not explicitly resolve this distinction; the nonempty-partition interpretation is consistent with its stated two-subgroup comparison.

The source attributes to B. Hartley and Yu. Kuzmin the result that $(R_1\cap R_2)/[R_1,R_2]$ is free abelian when each $R_i$ is the normal closure of one element of a free group. It also states that the analogous quotient can have nontrivial torsion for $n\geq4$, and that the three-subgroup question has an affirmative answer when none of $r_1,r_2,r_3$ is a proper power in $G$. Here a proper power means an element of the form $s^k$ with $s\in G$ and an integer $k\geq2$. These are supplied background assertions, with the partition convention still requiring confirmation; the question itself imposes no proper-power restriction.

Short English statement

For three elements of any free group, take their normal closures and intersect them. Must the quotient by the product of the three indicated commutator subgroups be torsion-free? The source reports an affirmative answer when none of the elements is a proper power. Its partition notation leaves unresolved whether empty parts contribute additional commutators.
