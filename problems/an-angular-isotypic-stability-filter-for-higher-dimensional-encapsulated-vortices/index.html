Math-1621

An angular-isotypic stability filter for higher-dimensional encapsulated vortices

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $N\ge3$ and consider $u:{\mathbb{R}}^N\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^M$ solving either \[ J u_t=\Delta u+g(u)\quad\text{(NLS)},\qquad u_{tt}=\Delta u+g(u)\quad\text{(NLKG)}, \] where $J$ is invertible and skew-symmetric and the source assumes continuous radial $g(y)=h(|y|^2)y/|y|$ for $y\ne0$, continuously extended at $0$. Warchall's standing waves are \[ u(x,t)=e^{\mu Kt}\Phi(x),\qquad \Phi(x)=\widehat\psi(x/|x|)w(|x|), \] where $K$ is real skew-symmetric, $K=J^{-1}$ for NLS, $K^2=-I$ for NLKG, and \[ \widehat\psi:S^{N-1}\to S^{M-1},\qquad \Delta_S\widehat\psi=-l(l+N-2)\widehat\psi. \] The possible angular degrees are restricted by the target dimension $M$. The question asks under what conditions these encapsulated vortices are stable or unstable. The source cites localized profiles under suitable hypotheses including $f'(0)<0$ and $\int_0^a f(s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s>0$ for some $a>0$, and notes Galilean or Lorentz boosts as routes to traveling waves; it does not provide a complete existence or stability hypothesis list.

There is a sign conflict in the supplied setup. For the literal NLS equation, $K=J^{-1}$ and $e^{+\mu Kt}$ imply \[ \Delta\Phi+g(\Phi)+\omega\Phi=0, \qquad\omega=-\mu, \] whereas the source prints $\omega=+\mu$. For NLKG the same substitution consistently gives $\omega=\mu^2$. Thus the radial profile equation is \[ w''+\frac{N-1}{r}w'-\frac{l(l+N-2)}{r^2}w+f(w)=0, \] with $f(w)=\operatorname{sgn}(w)h(w^2)+\omega w$ for $w\ne0$, interpreted through its continuous extension. The source's NLS phase signs cannot all be imposed simultaneously.

The aid proves only a conditional linear high-angular-sector filter. Assume now $g$ is $C^1$ and a radial gradient, the chosen profile is smooth and localized, and \[ L=-\Delta-Dg(\Phi)-\omega I, \quad -Dg(0)-\omega I\ge\gamma I\ (\gamma>0),\quad C=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\ne0} |x|^2\max\{\lambda_{\max}(Dg(\Phi(x))-Dg(0)),0\}<\infty. \] Assume also a compact group of combined spatial and orthogonal target symmetries fixes $\Phi$, commutes with the evolution matrices, and preserves the operators. In one invariant isotypic sector $\sigma$, let $\Lambda_\sigma$ be an angular lower bound, meaning every angular section in that sector satisfies \[ \int_{S^{N-1}}|\nabla_Sv|^2 \ge\Lambda_\sigma\int_{S^{N-1}}|v|^2. \] Then $\Lambda_\sigma>C$ yields the reported positive quadratic-form bound \[ \langle Lv,v\rangle\ge \|\partial_rv\|_2^2 +(\Lambda_\sigma-C)\|r^{-1}v\|_2^2 +\gamma\|v\|_2^2. \] For the co-rotating linearizations \[ Jv_t=-Lv\quad\text{(NLS)},\qquad v_{tt}+2\mu Kv_t=-Lv\quad\text{(NLKG)}, \] this gives conserved positive quadratic energies, respectively $\tfrac12\langle Lv,v\rangle$ and $\tfrac12(\|v_t\|_2^2+\langle Lv,v\rangle)$, excluding finite-energy exponential growth within that sector. If only finitely many sectors have $\Lambda_\sigma\le C$, all possible exponential instabilities are confined to them. This finiteness condition is an additional hypothesis. Scalar spherical-harmonic degrees need not separately be invariant, since the angular dependence of $Dg(\Phi)$ can couple them; combined isotypic sectors retain their multiplicities. The aid does not prove stability of the remaining low sectors or nonlinear orbital stability of the full vortex.

Short English statement

Determine stability of higher-dimensional encapsulated vortices in vector NLS or NLKG. The supplied aid identifies a source sign conflict and, under additional smoothness, gap and symmetry hypotheses, excludes linear exponential growth in sufficiently high combined angular sectors; low sectors and nonlinear stability remain to be analyzed.
