Math-1890

Antipodes of symmetric convex bodies

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed dimension $d\geq2$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, meaning a compact convex set with nonempty interior, centrally symmetric about $c$, so $K=2c-K$. For $p,q\in\partial K$, let \[ d_{\partial K}(p,q)=\inf_\gamma\operatorname{length}(\gamma),\qquad \Delta(K)=\sup_{u,v\in\partial K}d_{\partial K}(u,v), \] where $\gamma$ ranges over rectifiable curves in $\partial K$ joining $p$ to $q$. The antipode of $p$ is $2c-p$.

Must every diametral pair on the surface be antipodal? More explicitly, is it true that for every admissible centrally symmetric convex body $K$ and every $p,q\in\partial K$, \[ d_{\partial K}(p,q)=\Delta(K)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=2c-p\,? \] The source asks in particular for rectangular boxes; in dimension three these are, up to a Euclidean motion, $K=\prod_{j=1}^3[-a_j,a_j]$ with all $a_j>0$. It does not explicitly state the ambient dimension of the general question; the displayed formulation makes the dimension a parameter rather than asserting that a particular range is supplied.

The research summary tentatively reports, with re-verification required, that the diameter of a box surface is attained by antipodal vertices, attributed to Vîlcu's intrinsic-geodesy results. Existence of such a maximizing pair does not by itself imply the source's stronger statement about every maximizing pair. The summary describes the general centrally symmetric case as still open to its knowledge.

Short English statement

If two points on the boundary of a centrally symmetric convex body have the greatest possible intrinsic surface distance, must they be opposite under the central symmetry? The question concerns every maximizing pair, including for rectangular boxes.
