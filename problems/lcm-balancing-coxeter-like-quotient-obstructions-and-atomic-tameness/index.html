Math-1674

LCM balancing, Coxeter-like quotient obstructions, and atomic tameness

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

P. Dehornoy's Problem 4.2, split typographically as "Problem 4. 2.," has three parts. (1) Is a cancellative finitely generated monoid with lcm's necessarily Garside, equivalently does it have a Garside element? (2) Can the Coxeter-group/Cayley-graph source of good Artin Garside structures be systematized for general Garside groups? (3) For an atomic Garside monoid, a Garside element $\Delta$ and a length $\ell$, is \[ \ell(\Delta^k)\leq Ck|\Delta|\qquad(k\geq1) \] true for a constant independent of $k$? The source omits whether both left and right lcm's are required, unit/Noetherian hypotheses, the kind of Coxeter-like object, and the meanings of $\ell$ and $|\Delta|$.

For the first part the aid supplies a sufficient atom-level certificate. Work with a unit-free atomic cancellative monoid generated by a finite atom set $\mathcal A$, with the relevant left and right lcm hypotheses. An atom is a nonunit not decomposable into two nonunits. Let $\Delta$ be the common right lcm of the atoms, so each atom left-divides $\Delta$. If an atom permutation $\tau$ satisfies \[ a\Delta=\Delta\tau(a)\qquad(a\in\mathcal A), \] then the aid asserts that $\Delta$ is a Garside element: its left and right divisor sets agree, are finite, and generate the monoid. These balanced-divisor and finiteness conditions are part of the Garside convention. The certificate adds quasi-centrality; it does not show that the original lcm assumptions always imply it.

For the second part the aid rules out a specific universal recipe, not every Coxeter analogue. If a marked Garside structure has $\Delta=a^2$ for an atom $a$, any quotient making all atoms involutions maps both $1$ and $\Delta$ to the identity. Hence it cannot be injective on $\operatorname{Div}(\Delta)$. The example \[ M=\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2\rangle^+,\qquad\Delta=a^2=b^2, \] has the standard Garside divisor set $\{1,a,b,\Delta\}$ and gives this obstruction to preserving the simple-divisor geometry via the naive atom-square quotient. It does not prove absence of all possible associated Coxeter-like objects.

For the third part the aid distinguishes three length conventions. If $\ell$ is shortest atomic word length and $|\Delta|=\ell(\Delta)$, subadditivity gives the claim with $C=1$. If instead "length" permits an arbitrary superadditive atomicity norm, $M=\langle a\rangle^+$, $\Delta=a$ and $\ell(a^j)=j^2$ give a quadratic counterexample to a uniform linear bound; this is not shortest or maximal factorization length. Finally let $\ell_{\max}(x)$ be the maximal length of an atomic factorization and suppose a positive additive grading $g:M\to{\mathbb{N}}_0$ exists, with $g(a)>0$ for all atoms. With $m=\min_{a\in\mathcal A}g(a)>0$, \[ \ell_{\max}(\Delta^k)\leq\frac{k\,g(\Delta)}m. \] This yields the requested linear form for any fixed positive reference $|\Delta|$, with the corresponding constant. It does not settle maximal atomic length in the absence of a positive grading. The undefined source length convention is therefore decisive for interpreting a positive or negative answer.

Short English statement

Dehornoy asks about Garside existence, Coxeter-like models, and linear growth of lengths of Garside powers. The aid gives a quasi-centrality certificate, an obstruction to the naive involution quotient, and different answers for different length notions. None of these distinctions should be collapsed into one general solution.
