Kourovka Notebook Problem 5.56

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, write \[ [G,G]=\langle [x,y]:x,y\in G\rangle, \qquad [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy. \] This is the derived subgroup of $G$. A group is perfect if $G=[G,G]$. A group is locally nilpotent if every finitely generated subgroup is nilpotent. Here, exponent $p$ means that every element satisfies $g^p=1$ and at least one element has order $p$. The alternative convention allowing the trivial group does not change either existence question below.

Part (a). Is the following assertion true? \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every prime }p>3\text{ and every finite group }G \text{ of exponent }p,\\ \text{there exist a finite group }H\text{ of exponent }p \text{ and an injective homomorphism }\iota:G\longrightarrow[H,H]. \end{gathered} \] The supplied registry places this part among the remaining parts of the problem; this is a supplied status assessment, not an independently verified claim of openness.

Part (b), archived as solved in the supplied record. Does there exist a prime $p$ and a nontrivial locally nilpotent group $G$ of exponent $p$ such that \[ G=[G,G]? \] The question quantifies existentially over the prime; it does not ask for such a group for every prime. Nontriviality is explicit in the accompanying archive account, although it is omitted from the wording of the question itself. It is therefore included here as the intended qualification. The source adds that such a group has no maximal subgroups, where a maximal subgroup means a proper subgroup contained in no larger proper subgroup.

Supplied historical background. The archive reports an affirmative answer to part (b). Its stated construction uses a non-soluble variety $\mathfrak V$, a class of groups specified by identities with no common finite bound on derived length. Let $F$ be the free group in $\mathfrak V$ on generators $x_1,x_2,\ldots$, and let $\varphi:F\to F$ be the homomorphism determined by \[ \varphi(x_i)=[x_{2i-1},x_{2i}]\qquad(i\geq1). \] The archive states that \[ D=\varinjlim\left(F\xrightarrow{\varphi}F\xrightarrow{\varphi}F\xrightarrow{\varphi}\cdots\right) \] is nontrivial and satisfies $D=[D,D]$. It further reports that the Kostrikin variety of locally nilpotent groups of prime exponent $p\geq5$ is non-soluble, citing Razmyslov and also Kolmakov, and credits a letter of Khukhro and L'vov with the account. It cites Vaughan-Lee and Wiegold for another example answering the question. These are preserved source reports, not new proofs or independently verified literature conclusions; the record does not provide a separate definition of the named Kostrikin variety.

Short English statement

Part (a) asks whether, for every prime $p>3$, each finite group of exponent $p$ embeds into the derived subgroup of another finite group of exponent $p$. Part (b) asks for a nontrivial locally nilpotent perfect group of some prime exponent. The supplied record labels the whole problem partially solved and archives an affirmative answer to part (b); these labels and the historical claims require human source review.
