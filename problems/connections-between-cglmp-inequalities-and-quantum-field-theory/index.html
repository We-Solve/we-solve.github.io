Definitions and conventions.
For two parties with settings $1,2$ and outcomes in $\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$, $d\ge2$, the Collins--Gisin--Linden--Massar--Popescu (CGLMP) inequality is a Bell inequality constraining local hidden-variable correlations. In the report it is written using expectations of outcome differences reduced modulo $d$. The quantum Fourier transform on $\mathbb C^d$ is the unitary $F_d|j\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{2\pi ijk/d}|k\rangle$. Fourier-transform measurement bases enter the report's numerically optimized high-dimensional Bell experiments.
Source scope and qualifications.
The catalogue title and clean statement expand QFT as quantum field theory. The primary report explicitly discusses the Quantum Fourier Transform immediately before asking for the CGLMP--QFT link. Thus the source-context question concerns the Fourier transform; no quantum-field-theory connection is asserted here. The catalogue wording is preserved below to make this discrepancy visible.

The source-corrected Fourier question.
Work with the primary-source meaning of QFT, the quantum Fourier transform; the retained catalogue phrase "quantum field theory" is not the target of this calculation. Let $d\geq2$, $\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|c_j|^2=1$, and $|\psi\rangle=\sum_jc_j|j,j\rangle$. For real phases $\alpha_x,\beta_y$, define orthonormal measurement vectors \[ |a_x\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_j e^{2\pi i j(a+\alpha_x)/d}|j\rangle, \quad |b_y\rangle=d^{-1/2}\sum_j e^{2\pi i j(-b+\beta_y)/d}|j\rangle. \] Orthogonality follows from the finite geometric sum for distinct integer outcomes. Their probability is \[ p(a,b|x,y)=d^{-2}\left|\sum_jc_j e^{-2\pi i j(a-b+\alpha_x+\beta_y)/d}\right|^2. \] There are exactly $d$ pairs with a fixed residue $a-b=k$, so the difference probability is $d^{-1}$ times the squared modulus in this formula with $a-b=k$.
Concrete connection and its limit.
Every CGLMP functional written as a weighted sum of modular-difference probabilities becomes a quadratic form $c^*Hc$ after these substitutions. Explicitly a term with weight $w$ and phase offset $t$ contributes $H_{j\ell}=(w/d)e^{2\pi i(j-\ell)t/d}$. Thus Fourier phases convert cyclic outcome differences into interference between Schmidt coefficients. This is a direct explanation of why the transform is compatible with the inequality's cyclic structure.

The calculation holds also for nonuniform $c_j$ and arbitrary real offsets; at integer offsets and uniform $c_j$, the geometric sum enforces a sharp residue, a useful normalization check. It does not show that these bases maximize the Bell functional over all measurements and states. Proving that reduction, or identifying another precise interpretation of the source's requested "link", is the first gap. No quantum-field-theory connection is inferred.
