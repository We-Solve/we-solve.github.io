Math-15061

Parallel Harmonic Spinors on Closed Ricci-Flat Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth Riemannian manifold. Here closed means compact and without boundary, and Ricci-flat means that its Ricci curvature tensor satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}_g=0$. The supplied statement imposes no dimension restriction and does not specify a spin structure.

To express the spinor question, suppose additionally that $M$ admits a spin structure $\sigma$. Let $\Sigma_{g,\sigma}M$ denote its complex spinor bundle, let $\nabla^{g,\sigma}$ be the spin connection induced by the Levi--Civita connection, and let $D_{g,\sigma}$ be the associated Dirac operator. In a local orthonormal frame $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, \[ D_{g,\sigma}\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_j\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla^{g,\sigma}_{e_j}\psi, \] where $\mathbin{\cdot}$ denotes Clifford multiplication. A spinor is globally parallel if $\nabla^{g,\sigma}_X\psi=0$ for every smooth vector field $X$ on $M$, and is harmonic if $D_{g,\sigma}\psi=0$.

The supplied question is: if $(M,g)$ is closed and Ricci-flat, does it admit a globally parallel harmonic spinor? Under the additional interpretation that a spin structure $\sigma$ has been specified and that the requested spinor must be nonzero, the existence condition is \[ \exists\,\psi\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\Sigma_{g,\sigma}M),\qquad \psi\not\equiv0,\qquad \nabla^{g,\sigma}\psi=0,\qquad D_{g,\sigma}\psi=0. \] The harmonic condition follows directly from the parallel condition by the displayed definition of $D_{g,\sigma}$; it is retained to match the source wording.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The original and cleaned statements do not assume that $M$ is spin, whereas the supplied literature assessment describes a question about closed Ricci-flat spin metrics. The record does not specify whether spinness is an intended hypothesis or part of the requested conclusion. Even under a spin hypothesis, it does not specify whether the conclusion concerns a prescribed spin structure, every spin structure, or the existence of some spin structure admitting such a spinor. These are distinct quantifier choices, so the displayed condition is a conditional formulation, not a resolution of the missing quantifier. The source also omits the word "nonzero"; that requirement is an explicit interpretation needed to exclude the zero spinor. No passage to a covering manifold is stated or inserted.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every closed Ricci-flat manifold carries a nonzero globally parallel harmonic spinor. A precise version requires clarification of the spin hypothesis and the quantifier over spin structures. The nonzero requirement is implicit rather than explicit in the supplied wording. The registry labels the problem partially solved, but that label and its literature assessment are supplied curation requiring independent review.
