Math-14664

Inertia Actions on Torsion Points under Supersingular Reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ denote the field in the supplied record, $X/K$ a geometrically connected curve of genus $g>1$, $J$ its Jacobian, and $x_0\in X(K)$. The record uses the Albanese embedding \[ \iota_{x_0}\colon X\hookrightarrow J,\qquad x\longmapsto [x-x_0]. \] Define \[ J(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}=\{P\in J(K): [n]P=0\text{ for some integer }n\geq 1\}, \qquad T_K=\iota_{x_0}(X)(K)\cap J(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}. \] Here $[n]$ denotes multiplication by $n$ on $J$. Thus $T_K$ is the source's $X_{\mathrm{tors}}$, expressed as a subset of the Jacobian using the specified base point.

The supplied question is: for every such $X,J,x_0$, if $J$ has supersingular good reduction over $K$, does \[ \forall\sigma\in I_K\;\forall P\in T_K,\qquad \sigma(P)=P \] hold?

This formulation has unresolved qualifications. The record does not specify the field $K$, a valuation, its residue characteristic, or the definition of $I_K$. In the usual discretely valued setting, good reduction means that $J$ extends to an abelian scheme over the valuation ring; supersingular good reduction means that its special fiber becomes isogenous over an algebraic closure of the residue field to a product of supersingular elliptic curves. An inertia subgroup is defined relative to the valuation and a choice of its prolongation to a separable closure. These explanations describe the conventional setting, but that setting is not supplied as an explicit hypothesis.

There is also a substantive point-domain ambiguity: the displayed set consists of $K$-rational points, which are fixed by the usual action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)$. Consequently, the supplied rational-point notation does not specify the intended nontrivial inertia question. A possible intended set is \[ T_{\overline K}=\iota_{x_0}(X)(\overline K)\cap J(\overline K)_{\mathrm{tors}}, \] with the question whether inertia fixes every element of this set, but replacing $T_K$ by $T_{\overline K}$ is an unverified correction and is not adopted here. The source also does not explicitly state smoothness or properness hypotheses for $X$. These issues require human mathematical review before a definitive formulation can be accepted.

Short English statement

The supplied problem asks whether supersingular good reduction of a curve's Jacobian forces inertia to fix all torsion points lying on the curve embedded using a chosen rational base point. The field and valuation hypotheses are missing, and the written set uses $K$-rational points, whose usual Galois action is already trivial. The intended geometric-point formulation therefore remains unresolved. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved.
