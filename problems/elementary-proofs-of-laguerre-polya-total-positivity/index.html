Elementary Proofs of Laguerre–Pólya Total Positivity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an interval $I\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}$ and a real sequence $(a_k)_{k\in I-I}$, the associated Toeplitz matrix is $T=(a_{j-i})_{i,j\in I}$. A matrix is totally nonnegative if every finite square minor has nonnegative determinant. Some literature calls this property "total positivity"; strict total positivity instead requires every such determinant to be positive. The supplied excerpt does not specify its convention, its index set, or any boundary convention for the sequence.

A common convention defines $\mathrm{LP}^{+}$ to consist of the nonzero real entire functions that are locally uniform limits on ${\mathbb{C}}$ of nonzero real polynomials having nonnegative coefficients and only real, nonpositive zeros. This definition is supplied for orientation: the excerpt does not define $\mathrm{LP}^{+}$, and its precise convention, including treatment of the zero function and normalization, requires confirmation.

The excerpt attributes to the Aissen--Edrei--Whitney--Schoenberg theorem, with report references [1, 3], the schematic characterization \[ T\text{ is totally positive} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A(t)=\frac{f(t)}{g(-t)},\qquad f,g\in\mathrm{LP}^{+}, \] where $A$ is the generating function corresponding to $T$, and the denominator must not be identically zero. This display records the supplied characterization; it does not assert the equivalence for every index set and sequence in the preceding definition. The excerpt does not specify whether $A$ is an ordinary or bilateral generating function, its convergence domain, whether the identity is formal or analytic, or the necessary normalization and denominator conditions.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks for a more straightforward, described in the registry as elementary, proof of a theorem characterizing totally positive Toeplitz matrices by generating functions of the form $f(t)/g(-t)$, with $f,g\in\mathrm{LP}^{+}$. It also mentions Okounkov's proof. The excerpt omits conventions and hypotheses needed to state the theorem completely.
