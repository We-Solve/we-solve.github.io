Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

Kerr parameters are the mass $M>0$ and rotation parameter $a$; extremality is $|a|=M$. Teukolsky equations govern particular spin-field components on this background. A fixed azimuthal mode has angular dependence $e^{im\varphi}$ for a fixed integer $m$. Outgoing and ingoing components are the source's null-frame components at the future horizon.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
On an extremal Kerr spacetime with $|a|=M$, prove energy boundedness and energy decay for fixed-azimuthal-mode solutions of the Teukolsky equations, with the predicted pointwise decay away from the future event horizon, decay of the outgoing component on the horizon, and generic growth of the ingoing component there.
Scope and source qualification.
Decay away from the horizon, decay of the outgoing horizon component and generic growth of the ingoing component are different required conclusions.

Short English statement

Prove the stated boundedness, decay and horizon-growth behavior for fixed angular modes of the Teukolsky fields on extremal Kerr.

A degenerate-horizon transport test.
To isolate why bounded exterior fields need not control transverse horizon derivatives, consider the auxiliary smooth equation \[ 2\partial_v\partial_r\psi+\partial_r(r^2\partial_r\psi)=0, \qquad r\geq0. \] This is a model equation, not the spin-dependent Teukolsky equation. Restricting to $r=0$ gives $\partial_v(\partial_r\psi)=0$, hence $\partial_r\psi(v,0)=H$, fixed by initial data. Differentiating once in $r$ and then restricting gives \[ 2\partial_v\partial_r^2\psi(v,0)+2H=0, \quad \partial_r^2\psi(v,0)=\partial_r^2\psi(0,0)-Hv. \] A nonzero conserved horizon derivative therefore forces linear growth of the next derivative in this model.
Genericity and norm check.
On any initial-data space where evaluation $\psi\mapsto\partial_r\psi(0,0)$ is a nonzero continuous linear functional, the condition $H=0$ is a closed codimension-one kernel; its complement is open and dense. This is a precise model meaning of generic growth. It says nothing about decay of $\psi(v,0)$, which this horizon identity does not determine, or about energy norms with degenerate weights.
