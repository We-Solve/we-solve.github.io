When concatenation is score averaging

Definitions and mathematical statement

Section A.6, "Thursday Open Discussion," is a collection of workshop prompts rather than a single formal problem. Lou Billera asks whether sequence-to-tree procedures are coherent with concatenation regarded as averaging; Penny asks about different concatenations and parameter counts. No sequence model, tree space, inference rule, or definition of averaging/coherence is specified. The following score formulation is the supplied aid's separate partial analysis of that prompt.

Let $\mathcal T$ be a finite nonempty candidate set, with data blocks $j=1,\ldots,m$. For candidate $T$, let $\ell_j(T,\theta_j)$ be a real score, with finite attained maximum \[ s_j(T)=\max_{\theta_j}\ell_j(T,\theta_j). \] For weights $w_j\ge0$, fully partitioned nuisance parameters give \[ S_{\mathrm{part}}(T,w) =\max_{(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m)}\sum_jw_j\ell_j(T,\theta_j) =\sum_jw_js_j(T). \] The aid reports that the weights for which $T$ is a possibly tied winner form the closed convex polyhedral cone \[ W_T=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\ge0}^m: \sum_jw_j(s_j(T)-s_j(U))\ge0\ \text{for every }U\in\mathcal T\right\}. \] In particular $T$ wins for every nonnegative weight vector exactly when it wins every individual block. This uses weak winners; at $w=0$, all candidates tie, so it is not a unique-winner statement. The aid reports that retaining only the block-winning topology labels cannot in general determine the winner of the sum: score margins discarded by those labels can change the result.

If nuisance parameters are instead linked, specify a common nonempty parameter space $\Theta$ and define \[ S_{\mathrm{shared}}(T,w)=\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_jw_j\ell_j(T,\theta), \qquad A_j(T)=\operatorname*{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\ell_j(T,\theta). \] Assuming the individual and shared maxima are finite and attained, the aid reports \[ S_{\mathrm{shared}}(T,w)\le S_{\mathrm{part}}(T,w), \] with equality exactly when \[ \bigcap_{j:w_j>0}A_j(T)\ne\varnothing. \] For all-zero weights the intersection is $\Theta$. Without attainment, equality of suprema need not yield a common optimizer. An explicit two-block example is reported to show that linking parameters can reverse the selected candidate, but its scores are not supplied. These are score-level criteria and do not assert equivalence to a geometric average of inferred trees.

Confidence sets and likelihood contours are discussed near branch-transition points, including short-edge maximum-likelihood trees, with possible relevance of curvature. Holmes asks about directions and multivariate distances; Diaconis mentions vector-valued or partially ordered distances. Epstein asks about alternatives to trees, and St. John suggests networks with non-tree portions. Vert proposes repeatedly generating data from a tree and re-estimating a tree by maximum likelihood, asking about the resulting walk. Shareshian asks whether two-colored trees could model orthology and paralogy. None is supplied with the spaces, probability kernels, loss functions, or axioms needed for a unique theorem. Short English statement

The discussion asks several questions about trees, sequence concatenation, and geometry. The supplied aid treats one precise score version: independently profiled block scores add, winner regions are polyhedral cones, and linking nuisance parameters preserves a candidate's score exactly when the block optimizers intersect.
