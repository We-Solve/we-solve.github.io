Scalar Curvature Question [?16]: Singularities are Unstable

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The conjecture concerns hypersurfaces in an ambient $n$-manifold, hence submanifolds or currents of dimension $n-1$, not arbitrary codimension. A natural homological formulation fixes a smooth closed $X^n$ and considers the space $\operatorname{Met}^\infty(X)$ of smooth Riemannian metrics with its $C^\infty$ topology. For integral cycles, an area-minimizer in a class $a\in H_{n-1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})$ is a current $T$ with $[T]=a$ and \[ \mathbf M_g(T)\le\mathbf M_g(S)\qquad\text{for every integral cycle }S\text{ with }[S]=a. \] Nonsingularity means that its support has no singular points; multiplicity one is not silently required.

In this formulation, define \[ \mathcal R(X)=\{g\in\operatorname{Met}^\infty(X): \text{every such homological minimizer has empty singular set}\}. \] A generic-metric version asks that $\mathcal R(X)$ contain a residual subset, meaning a countable intersection of open dense sets in the metric space of metrics. The weaker arbitrarily-small-perturbation statement asks only \[ \forall g\ \forall\text{ neighborhood }\mathcal U\ni g, \qquad \mathcal U\cap\mathcal R(X)\ne\varnothing. \] Residual genericity and density are not identical assertions. The extracted source uses both expressions without specifying topology or exact quantifiers. Nor does it fix closedness, integral versus mod-two coefficients, homological minimizers versus fixed-boundary or local minimizers, or whether one class or all classes are treated simultaneously. The displayed homological model is therefore explicit but conditional on those scope choices.

The source attributes the belief to Brian White and reports confirmation by Nathan Smale in 1993 for ambient dimension $n=8$, with a stated extension of the Schoen--Yau theorem to that dimension. The research summary also mentions later Chodosh--Li generic-regularity progress, without a precise theorem; it reports the all-dimensional statement unresolved. Neither its loose all-codimension wording nor its unspecified later results are promoted beyond the hypersurface question.

Short English statement

Should generic smooth metrics make volume-minimizing hypersurfaces nonsingular, as reported in ambient dimension eight? The minimization class, coefficient group, topology, and density versus residual-generic quantifiers need to be fixed.
