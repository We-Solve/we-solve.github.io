Research attempt

Literature and scope

Problem 45 is a collection of Bayesian questions, including prediction and identifiability. I tested whether a uniform prior on a convenient latent parameterization can be used as if it were uniform on the observable model. The following exact predictive calculation shows that it cannot. This is an elementary prior-dependence example, not a new Bayesian algebraic method.
Proposition: Two priors on parameterizations of the full Bernoulli observable family give posterior predictive success probabilities $2/3$ and $7/12$ after one observed success.
Proof: First put the uniform prior on the observable success probability $q\in[0,1]$. For a prior on $q$, observing a single success weights it by $q$, so the probability of a further success is $E(q^2)/E(q)$. Uniform $q$ gives $E(q)=1/2$ and $E(q^2)=1/3$, hence $2/3$.

Now write $q=(a+b)/2$ as an equal-weight mixture of two Bernoulli components and put independent uniform priors on $a,b\in[0,1]$. The observable image is still the whole interval: take $a=b=q$ for any desired $q$. But \[ E(q)=\tfrac12,\qquad E(q^2)=\tfrac14\left(E(a^2)+2E(a)E(b)+E(b^2)\right) =\tfrac14(1/3+1/2+1/3)=7/24. \] Its predictive success probability is therefore $7/12$, which differs from $2/3$. This calculation already integrates over the latent parameters; no unobserved component assignments were fixed. ∎

Failed route and original-scope comparison

The two likelihood families on observable distributions coincide, but their pushed-forward priors do not. Thus replacing an unidentifiable mixture by its observable image while retaining the phrase "uniform prior" changes the inference. A prior measure must be pushed forward explicitly before quotienting parameters. The two-dimensional latent integration here is exactly tractable; it does not give general posterior modes, model-fit criteria, Macaulay2 algorithms or Edgeworth corrections requested in the broader source discussion.
