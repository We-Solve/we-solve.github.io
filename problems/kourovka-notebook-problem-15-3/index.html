Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.3

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite group $G$, let $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ denote its ordinary irreducible complex characters; this uses the customary interpretation of the unqualified word "characters" in the supplied record. For $\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)$, its degree is $\chi(1)$, and its kernel is $\ker\chi=\{g\in G:\chi(g)=\chi(1)\}$. The character is faithful if $\ker\chi=\{1\}$, and non-linear if $\chi(1)>1$. Define the pointwise product by $(\alpha\beta)(g)=\alpha(g)\beta(g)$ for every $g\in G$. A group is solvable if its derived series, given by $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$, reaches the trivial group after finitely many steps.

Problem (I. M. Isaacs, Kourovka Notebook 15.3). Does there exist a finite solvable group $G$ and characters $\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ such that \[ \ker\alpha=\ker\beta=\{1\},\qquad \alpha(1)>1,\qquad \beta(1)>1,\qquad \alpha\beta\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)? \] No requirement that $\alpha\ne\beta$ is stated in the source.

The supplied record states that examples exist for non-solvable groups, including $\mathrm{SL}_2(5)$ with two irreducible characters of degree $2$. It further attributes to I. Zisser the result that the alternating group $\mathbb{A}_n$ admits such characters if and only if $n$ is a square exceeding $4$. The alternating-group notation repairs an apparent typesetting error in the supplied text. The record describes the cited work of I. M. Isaacs as giving evidence, but no proof, that solvable examples do not exist. These contextual claims and the supplied open label are source reports, not independently verified assessments.

Short English statement

Can a finite solvable group have two faithful irreducible complex characters, each of degree greater than $1$, whose pointwise product is irreducible? The supplied source reports non-solvable examples and evidence, but no proof, against solvable examples.
