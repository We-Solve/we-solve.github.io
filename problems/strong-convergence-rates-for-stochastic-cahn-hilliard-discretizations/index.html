Strong Convergence Rates for Stochastic Cahn–Hilliard Discretizations

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Supplied mathematical target. Determine optimal strong convergence rates for temporal discretizations of the stochastic Cahn--Hilliard--Cook equation.

Meaning of a rate, conditional on a specified formulation. Once the stochastic equation, its solution, a temporal approximation, and a strong error functional have been specified, let $\mathcal E(\tau)\geq 0$ denote that error for time step $\tau>0$. An algebraic convergence rate $\alpha>0$ means that there exist constants $C<\infty$ and $\tau_0>0$, independent of $\tau$, such that \[ \mathcal E(\tau)\leq C\tau^\alpha \qquad\text{for every admissible }0<\tau\leq\tau_0. \] Calling a rate optimal additionally requires a specified comparison class and a precise sharpness criterion; a supremum of admissible exponents need not itself be attained.

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not state the stochastic differential equation or specify the spatial domain, dimension, boundary conditions, coefficients, nonlinear potential, driving noise and its covariance, initial data and their regularity, solution concept, time interval, temporal discretization scheme, or treatment of any spatial discretization. It also does not specify the norm, moment order, temporal evaluation convention, or uniformity class defining strong error. Consequently, $\mathcal E$ and the intended meaning of optimality cannot be instantiated from this record. The displayed bound explains rate terminology only; it does not supply missing hypotheses or assert a convergence result. The target remains a research question requiring these specifications before a precise mathematical proposition can be stated.

Short English statement

Determine the best possible strong convergence rates for time discretizations of the stochastic Cahn--Hilliard--Cook equation. The supplied record leaves the equation, assumptions, numerical methods, error criterion, and meaning of optimality unspecified, so it does not determine a single precise problem.
