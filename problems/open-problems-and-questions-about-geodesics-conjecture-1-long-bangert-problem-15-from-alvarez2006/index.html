Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Conjecture 1 (Long, Bangert, Problem 15 from [Alvarez2006])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^2$ be the two-dimensional sphere. Under the usual smooth, strongly convex convention, a Finsler metric is a function $F:TS^2\to[0,\infty)$, continuous everywhere and smooth away from the zero section, such that $F(x,v)>0$ for $v\ne0$, $F(x,\lambda v)=\lambda F(x,v)$ for every $\lambda>0$, and the fibre Hessian of $\tfrac12F^2$ is positive definite at every nonzero tangent vector. It is irreversible if $F(x,v)\ne F(x,-v)$ for some $(x,v)\in TS^2$.

A closed geodesic is a nonconstant periodic geodesic of $F$. It is prime if it is not a multiple traversal of a shorter closed geodesic. For a precise provisional counting convention, parametrize geodesics with $F$-speed one and identify two prime periodic geodesics exactly when they differ by a translation of the parameter. Let $\mathcal P(F)$ denote the resulting set.

The supplied conjecture, attributed to Long and Bangert and identified as Problem 15 in $[\mathrm{Alvarez2006}]$, is \[ \text{for every irreversible Finsler metric }F\text{ on }S^2, \qquad \#\mathcal P(F)=2\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal P(F)\text{ is infinite}. \] The supplied context reports that V. Bangert and Y. Long proved the lower bound of two distinct prime closed geodesics, citing $[\mathrm{Ban\text{-}Lon2010}]$ and $[\mathrm{Long2006}]$; that report is retained as source context rather than independently verified here.

The source excerpt does not specify regularity assumptions or define when closed geodesics are distinct. The smooth Finsler convention and the parameter-translation convention above are explicit provisional interpretations requiring review. In particular, the excerpt does not resolve whether distinctness is instead determined by image in $S^2$, or whether opposite traversals are identified when both are geodesics.

Short English statement

The conjecture says that every irreversible Finsler metric on $S^2$ has exactly two or infinitely many distinct prime closed geodesics. Prime means that repeated traversals are excluded. The source leaves the precise distinctness convention and regularity assumptions unstated; the conventions used in this draft require mathematical review.
