Polar Cylinders and Affine Complements of Del Pezzo Surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ denote the ground field, which is not specified in the supplied record. Under the usual convention, a cylinder in a $k$-variety $X$ is a nonempty Zariski-open subset $U\subseteq X$ admitting a $k$-isomorphism \[ U\cong Z\times_k{\mathbb{A}}^1_k \] for a nonempty affine $k$-variety $Z$. Call $X$ cylindrical if it contains such a subset, and non-cylindrical otherwise. These definitions concern the existence of an open cylinder, rather than a product decomposition of all of $X$.

For a projective surface $S$ with $K_S$ ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier, the conventional meaning of a $(-K_S)$-polar cylinder is a cylinder of the form \[ S\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D), \qquad D\geq 0,\qquad D\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}-K_S, \] where $D$ is an effective ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier ${\mathbb{Q}}$-divisor and $\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ denotes ${\mathbb{Q}}$-linear equivalence. The source does not specify its divisor convention; this conventional interpretation requires confirmation.

The supplied question concerns a del Pezzo surface $S$ embedded in its ambient weighted projective space $P$, with affine complement $P\setminus S$. Its background assessment refers specifically to del Pezzo hypersurfaces. For each pair $(S,P)$ in the intended class, the proposed equivalence is \[ \begin{aligned} &S\text{ contains no }(-K_S)\text{-polar cylinder}\\ &\hspace{2em}\Longleftrightarrow P\setminus S\text{ is non-cylindrical}. \end{aligned} \] The supplied record labels the forward implication as known. Thus its remaining proposed implication is \[ P\setminus S\text{ is non-cylindrical} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\text{ contains no }(-K_S)\text{-polar cylinder}. \] These are source-reported assertions and status qualifications, not independently verified results here.

The intended class of pairs $(S,P)$ is unresolved: the record supplies no ground-field assumptions, singularity hypotheses, weights, hypersurface degree, or precise embedding conditions. In particular, it does not determine whether "del Pezzo surface" means a smooth projective surface with ample anticanonical divisor or a specified class of singular surfaces. Consequently, the display preserves the extracted mathematical target but does not assert the equivalence for every field, every singular del Pezzo surface, or every weighted-projective embedding.

Short English statement

For the intended del Pezzo hypersurfaces, the question asks whether having no anticanonically polar cylinder is equivalent to the ambient affine complement containing no open cylinder. The supplied record reports the forward implication as proved and the converse as conjectural. The ground field, allowed singularities, embedding hypotheses, and precise polar-divisor convention require clarification before this becomes a fully specified universally quantified statement.
