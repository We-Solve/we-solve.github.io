Strict Positivity for Fourth-Order Quantum Diffusion

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
$\mathbb T^d=\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$ is the flat periodic torus, $d\ge1$, and $n(x,t)$ is a nonnegative particle density. $\nabla$, $\operatorname{div}$, and $\Delta$ are spatial gradient, divergence, and Laplacian. The fourth-order density equation is interpreted through the source's weak formulation and entropy estimates when $n$ can vanish, since the displayed quotient is not pointwise defined at vacuum. Strict positivity must be specified either almost everywhere or for an appropriate regular representative.
Source scope and qualifications.
Positive total mass is necessary for a meaningful strict-positivity assertion: zero initial mass gives the zero density in a mass-conserving formulation. The source's brief n_0>=0 notation does not explicitly exclude that boundary case. Pointwise versus almost-everywhere positivity and the chosen weak-solution class must also be fixed; no maximum principle is assumed for this fourth-order equation.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let $n\geq0$ solve $\partial_t n+\operatorname{div}\!\left(n\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt n}{\sqrt n}\right)\right)=0$ on the $d$-dimensional torus, with nonnegative initial datum $n_0$. Is $n(\cdot,t)$ strictly positive for every $t>0$ even if $n_0$ vanishes somewhere?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Global nonnegative weak solutions are known, but instantaneous strict positivity from data with zeros is still not established in the cited theory. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Determine whether fourth-order quantum diffusion immediately removes vacuum points from a nonzero density.
