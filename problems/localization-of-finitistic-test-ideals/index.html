Math-4416

Localization of Finitistic Test Ideals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $R$ be a commutative Noetherian integral domain of characteristic $p$. The ring $R$ is $F$-finite if its Frobenius map $F:R\to R$, $r\mapsto r^p$, is finite. Let $0\ne\mathfrak a\subseteq R$ be an ideal and let $t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}$.

We use the following convention for the finitistic test ideal of the pair $(R,\mathfrak a^t)$. For $e\geq0$, put $q=p^e$, and let $R^{(e)}$ denote the bimodule with usual left multiplication and right multiplication $u\cdot r=ur^q$. For an $R$-module $M$, put \[ F_R^e(M)=R^{(e)}\otimes_R M,\qquad z^{[q]}=1\otimes z. \] For each finitely generated $R$-module $M$, define \[ 0_M^{*\mathfrak a^t} = \left\{z\in M:\ \exists c\in R\setminus\{0\}\ \exists e_0\geq0\ \forall e\geq e_0,\quad c\,\mathfrak a^{\lceil t p^e\rceil}z^{[p^e]}=0 \text{ in }F_R^e(M)\right\}. \] Here the displayed vanishing means that every element of $c\mathfrak a^{\lceil tp^e\rceil}$ annihilates $z^{[p^e]}$. Define \[ \tau_{\mathrm{fg},R}(\mathfrak a^t) = \bigcap_{M\ \text{finitely generated over }R} \operatorname{Ann}_R\!\left(0_M^{*\mathfrak a^t}\right). \] The notation $\mathfrak a^t$ denotes the ideal together with its real exponent in this definition; it does not assert that an ordinary ideal power with nonintegral exponent has been defined.

Localization question. For every such $F$-finite ring $R$, nonzero ideal $\mathfrak a$, positive real number $t$, and multiplicatively closed subset $S\subseteq R$ with $1\in S$ and $0\notin S$, does the equality of ideals in $S^{-1}R$ \[ S^{-1}\tau_{\mathrm{fg},R}(\mathfrak a^t) = \tau_{\mathrm{fg},S^{-1}R}\!\left((S^{-1}\mathfrak a)^t\right) \] hold? The right-hand ideal is defined by the same construction over $S^{-1}R$, using all finitely generated $S^{-1}R$-modules.

Unresolved source qualifications. The supplied record does not explicitly define $\tau_{\mathrm{fg}}$, specify the ambient domain of $t$, or state whether $0$ is excluded from $S$. The formulation above explicitly uses the finitistic generalized tight-closure definition, positive real exponents, and nonzero localization. These conventions require confirmation against the cited source. If $0\in S$ is intended, the localization is the zero ring, outside the domain convention used above, and the supplied record gives no corresponding test-ideal convention. The record supplies no additional hypotheses or precise special cases for its qualification that the question remains unresolved beyond additional hypotheses.

Short English statement

Does the finitistic test ideal of every pair $(R,\mathfrak a^t)$, with $R$ an $F$-finite Noetherian domain of positive characteristic, commute with localization? The displayed formulation makes explicit the conventions for generalized tight closure, positive real exponents, and multiplicative sets avoiding zero.
