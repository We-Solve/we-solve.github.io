Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.60

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $H$ has finite special rank if there exists an integer $r\geq 0$ such that every finitely generated subgroup of $H$ can be generated by at most $r$ elements. A group $G$ is locally graded if every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup $K\leq G$ has a proper subgroup of finite index, equivalently, a nontrivial finite quotient. A group is periodic if every element has finite order.

The statement $G=AB$, for subgroups $A,B\leq G$, means the setwise equality \[ G=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}. \] No normality assumption on either factor is imposed.

Question (N. S. Chernikov, Kourovka Notebook 13.60). For every locally graded group $G$ and all subgroups $A,B\leq G$ satisfying $G=AB$, does finite special rank of both $A$ and $B$ imply finite special rank of $G$? Explicitly, if there exist integers $r_A,r_B\geq 0$ such that every finitely generated subgroup of $A$, respectively $B$, admits a generating set of size at most $r_A$, respectively $r_B$, must there exist an integer $r_G\geq 0$ such that every finitely generated subgroup of $G$ admits a generating set of size at most $r_G$? The question asks for existence of such a bound for each $G$; no particular bound in terms of $r_A$ and $r_B$ is specified.

Supplied qualification. The source reports an affirmative answer under the additional assumption that both $A$ and $B$ are periodic, and attributes this case to the 1990 paper of N. S. Chernikov cited below. This reported result has not been independently verified here. The supplied registry labels the general question as open; that label is source metadata rather than an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

If a locally graded group is the setwise product of two subgroups, each having a uniform finite bound on the number of generators of its finitely generated subgroups, must the whole group have such a bound? The source reports an affirmative answer when both factors are periodic.
