The Real-Zero Polynomial Amalgamation Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $s\geq 1$, use the following at-origin convention: a polynomial $f\in{\mathbb{R}}[u_1,\ldots,u_s]$ is real-zero if $f(0)\ne 0$ and, for every $v\in{\mathbb{R}}^s$, every complex root of the univariate polynomial $t\mapsto f(tv)$ is real. Nonzero constant polynomials satisfy this condition. Write ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, and interpret degree as total degree. The supplied record does not explicitly define real-zero or specify a normalization such as $f(0)=1$; the convention above makes the displayed question precise and requires source confirmation.

Amalgamation question, conditional on the literal scalar-variable reading. Is the following assertion true? For every $d\in{\mathbb{N}}_0$ and every pair of real-zero polynomials \[ p\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y],\qquad q\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,z],\qquad \deg p\leq d,\quad \deg q\leq d, \] where $x,y,z$ are individual indeterminates, if \[ p(x,0)=q(x,0)\quad\text{as polynomials in }{\mathbb{R}}[x], \] then there exists a real-zero polynomial $r\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y,z]$ with $\deg r\leq d$ such that the polynomial identities \[ r(x,y,0)=p(x,y),\qquad r(x,0,z)=q(x,z) \] hold?

Unresolved scope qualification. The record does not specify whether $x,y,z$ denote individual variables or tuples of variables. If they denote tuples, the same identities require corresponding zero tuples, and the dimensions and their quantifiers must be specified. The scalar formulation above is therefore an explicit conditional reading, not a verified identification of the intended general conjecture. In particular, the supplied literature assessment refers to a remaining two-shared-variable conjecture, which makes this distinction material.

Short English statement

The question asks whether two real-zero polynomials that agree on their shared variables can be extended to one real-zero polynomial without increasing the prescribed degree bound. The source leaves the scalar-versus-tuple interpretation and the real-zero convention unspecified. Its open status and literature assessment are supplied dated curation, not independently verified conclusions.
