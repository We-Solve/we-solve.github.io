A compact-tail reduction for adelic unipotent measure rigidity

Definitions and mathematical statement

L. Silberman's source request is to extend unipotent-flow measure rigidity to the adelic setting. It does not specify the global field, adelic acting subgroup, quotient, or whether full infinite-adic factors are involved. The aid separates characteristic-zero finite-$S$ classification, attributed to Margulis--Tomanov and Ratner, from a general full-adelic or parabolic formulation, which is not automatically covered.

Its precise compact-tail statement is the following. Let commuting groups $L,K$ act continuously on a suitable locally compact second-countable space $X$, with $K$ compact and a well-behaved orbit quotient $q:X\to X/K$. Finite $L$- and $K$-invariant measures on $X$ correspond bijectively by $q_*$ to finite $L$-invariant measures $\nu$ on $X/K$. The inverse uses normalized Haar orbital measures: \[ \int_X\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu =\int_{X/K}\left(\int_K\varphi(xk)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} m_K(k)\right){\,\mathrm{d}}\nu([x]). \] The inner expression is independent of the orbit representative; no free-action hypothesis is needed when stabilizers are handled by the Haar orbit measure. The aid states \[ \mu\text{ is jointly }LK\text{-ergodic} \Longleftrightarrow\nu\text{ is }L\text{-ergodic}. \] It does not replace joint ergodicity by $L$-ergodicity upstairs.

For an adelic application over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, let $Y=\mathbf G({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathbf G(\mathbb A)$, let $S$ be finite including the archimedean place, and choose a maximal-compact tail $K^S$ outside $S$. Assume the requisite finite-double-class and arithmetic hypotheses so that \[ Y/K^S\cong\coprod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma_j\backslash G_S \] is a finite disjoint union of $S$-arithmetic lattice quotients. If $L\subseteq G_S$ is in the characteristic-zero unipotent-generated classification setting, every finite measure invariant under both $L$ and the full $K^S$ is recovered without loss from its finite-level measure, and the finite-$S$ classification applies to the latter. The exact algebraic-group, lattice, and unipotent-generation hypotheses of the cited classification are not supplied.

The aid emphasizes a proper-tail obstruction. Already on a product $Z\times K$, with $L$ acting on $Z$ and a proper compact subgroup $U\subsetneq K$ acting on the second factor, the product of an $L$-ergodic measure on $Z$ and Haar measure on one $U$-coset is jointly $LU$-ergodic and has the same projection to $Z$, but is not $K$-invariant. Thus invariance under a compact unipotent tail does not, on those assumptions alone, imply maximal-compact tail invariance.

It also works with the upper unipotent subgroup $\mathbf U\subset\operatorname{SL}_2$, whose closed orbit in $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb A)$ is \[ \mathbf U({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathbf U(\mathbb A)\cong{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\mathbb A. \] This compact additive quotient has normalized Haar probability and illustrates full adelic unipotent invariance without silently replacing it by maximal-compact tail invariance. The detailed orbit calculation and chosen tail are not fully supplied in the record.

The original trailing discussion expects algebraically manageable finite ergodic measures for other parabolic-like systems. For connected semisimple Lie $H\subseteq G$ and parabolic $P\subseteq H$, it reports that finite $P$-invariant measures on $G/\Gamma$ are $H$-invariant [146]. The precise quotient and theorem hypotheses require checking; this historical Lie-group assertion is not itself a proved infinite-adelic analogue. The aid's compact-tail reduction leaves the missing general full-adelic step separate.

Short English statement

The supplied compact-tail theorem gives an exact finite-level reduction only for measures invariant under the whole compact tail. Smaller unipotent-tail invariance does not imply that hypothesis. Finite-$S$ classification and the original broader adelic/parabolic request therefore remain distinct.
