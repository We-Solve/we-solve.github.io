Math-2240

Question 6.4 — ((Non)residual finiteness).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $G=\operatorname{Mod}_{g,1}$ in the source's convention, and let \[1\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow\widetilde G\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1\] be its stated universal central extension. A central extension has kernel contained in the center; universality means every central extension of $G$ receives a unique compatible homomorphism from $\widetilde G$. In the usual perfect-group setting its kernel identifies with $H_2(G;{\mathbb{Z}})$; the source does not specify the genus range or whether the one distinguished feature is a puncture, marked point or boundary component. These conventions and perfectness must be fixed before naming this extension. No cyclic-kernel description is imposed.

A group $A$ is residually finite if \[\forall a\in A\setminus\{1\},\ \exists\text{ finite group }F,\ \exists\phi:A\to F,\quad\phi(a)\ne1.\] The question asks whether $\widetilde{\operatorname{Mod}}_{g,1}$ is residually finite, or instead has a nonidentity element invisible in every finite quotient. The source recalls Grossman's residual finiteness of $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ and $\operatorname{Mod}_{g,1}$, but that of the quotient alone does not answer the central-extension question. Given that quotient property, elements outside $Z$ are already separated through $G$, so finite detection of the remaining nontrivial central-kernel elements is the essential additional requirement.

The source also recalls residual finiteness of $\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})$, with \[\bigcap_{N\geq1}\ker\left(\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})\right)=\{1\}.\] This is context about that integral matrix group, not the requested extension.

The research summary's statement that finitely generated subgroups of a residually finite mapping class group need not be residually finite is incompatible with the definition: restriction of a separating finite quotient separates any nonidentity subgroup element. Residual finiteness passes to every subgroup. That summary claim is therefore flagged rather than used to reinterpret the question as a subgroup problem.

Short English statement

Determine residual finiteness of the specified universal central extension of $\operatorname{Mod}_{g,1}$. Known residual finiteness of the base group does not settle the extension, and the subgroup assertion in the research summary is erroneous.
