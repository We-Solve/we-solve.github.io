Coefficientwise index algebra.
Suppose a specified compact model has a formal graded virtual bundle $\mathcal E(q,y)=\sum_{n\ge0,m\in\mathbb Z}E_{n,m}q^ny^m$, with finitely many $m$ at each $n$, and elliptic Fredholm operators $D_{n,m}$ twisted by those bundles. Its formal index series is \[Z(q,y)=\sum_{n,m}\operatorname{ind}(D_{n,m})q^ny^m,\quad \operatorname{ind}D=\dim\ker D-\dim\ker D^*. \] Additivity follows directly for direct sums, because kernels and adjoint kernels split. Thus each coefficient is an integer but is a signed count, not automatically the dimension of a state space or a nonnegative enumerative invariant.
A supersymmetric cancellation test.
In a finite-dimensional graded complex $(C^\bullet,d)$, let $a_k=\dim C^k$, $b_k=\dim\operatorname{im}(d:C^{k-1}\to C^k)$, and $h_k=\dim H^k$. Rank-nullity gives $a_k=b_k+h_k+b_{k+1}$. Alternating summation cancels the $b$ terms and proves $\sum_k(-1)^ka_k=\sum_k(-1)^kh_k$. This supplies the algebraic mechanism by which paired states cancel in a protected trace. It also shows that agreement of an ungraded total count is not the desired graded-index comparison.
