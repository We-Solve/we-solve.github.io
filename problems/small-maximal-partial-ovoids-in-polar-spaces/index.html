Math-6937

Small Maximal Partial Ovoids in Polar Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q$ be an odd prime power and let ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ be the field with $q$ elements. For a nondegenerate quadratic form $f$ on a finite-dimensional ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-vector space $V$, its singular projective points are the one-dimensional subspaces $\langle v\rangle$ with $f(v)=0$. A subspace is totally singular if $f$ vanishes identically on it; the Witt index is the maximum dimension of such a vector subspace. In the standard notation, $Q^+(2n+1,q)$ arises from a vector space of dimension $2n+2$ and Witt index $n+1$, $Q(2n,q)$ from dimension $2n+1$ and Witt index $n$, and $Q^-(2n+1,q)$ from dimension $2n+2$ and Witt index $n$. These definitions apply for positive integers $n$, but the supplied record does not specify the intended range of $n$ in the conjecture.

Two distinct singular points are collinear when their span is a two-dimensional totally singular vector subspace. A partial ovoid is a set $S$ of singular points containing no two distinct collinear points, equivalently meeting each maximal totally singular projective subspace in at most one point. It is maximal if no strictly larger set of singular points is a partial ovoid. Maximality here is with respect to inclusion, not cardinality.

The supplied conjecture says that the listed polar spaces have no maximal partial ovoids of size $O(q)$. To describe one possible precise asymptotic interpretation, for each fixed $n$ and each family $\mathcal P$ among the three families above, put \[ m_{\mathcal P,n}(q)=\min\{\lvert S\rvert:S\text{ is a maximal partial ovoid of }\mathcal P_n(q)\}. \] If the intended assertion excludes linearly bounded examples along every unbounded sequence of odd prime powers, its fixed-$n$ formulation would be \[ \forall C>0\;\exists q_0\;\forall q\ge q_0\text{ an odd prime power},\qquad m_{\mathcal P,n}(q)>Cq, \] or equivalently \[ \lim_{\substack{q\to\infty\\q\text{ an odd prime power}}}\frac{m_{\mathcal P,n}(q)}{q}=+\infty. \] This is a conditional formulation, not a resolved interpretation of the source. The admissible values of $n$, whether $n$ is fixed, the permitted dependence of asymptotic constants on $n$ and the family, and whether bounded examples on subsequences are excluded are not specified. Merely excluding a single $O(q)$-bounded construction valid for every sufficiently large odd prime power would be a different assertion. These qualifications require clarification before an unconditional quantified statement can be assigned to the source.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether maximal partial ovoids in the three listed families of quadrics must grow faster than linearly in the odd field order $q$. A maximal partial ovoid is an inclusion-maximal set of mutually noncollinear singular points. The intended dimension range and asymptotic quantifiers remain unresolved.
