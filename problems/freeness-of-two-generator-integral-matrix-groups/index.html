Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Free generation by $A,B$ means no nonempty reduced word in $A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}$ evaluates to the identity, where reduction cancels adjacent inverse pairs. Uniform decidability requires one terminating procedure for all encoded dimensions.

The group $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ consists of integer matrices with integer inverses, and $\mathrm{SL}_n$ imposes determinant one. The subgroup generated by matrices consists of finite products of them and their inverses. A centralizer consists of elements commuting with the specified element.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Given two integral matrices $A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, decide whether the subgroup $\langle A,B\rangle$ is freely generated by $A$ and $B$. Is this decision problem solvable uniformly in $n$? [S2]

A complete elementary free-pair certificate

Let \[ A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}. \]
Proposition: No nonempty freely reduced word in $A,A^{-1},B,B^{-1}$ evaluates to the identity. Thus these matrices freely generate a group on the given two generators.
Proof: In $\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$ put $X=\{(x,y):|x|>|y|\}$ and $Y=\{(x,y):|y|>|x|\}$. For every nonzero integer $r$, $A^r(x,y)=(x+2ry,y)$ sends $Y$ into $X$, since $|x+2ry|\ge2|r||y|-|x|>|y|$. Similarly $B^r$ sends $X$ into $Y$.

Group a nonempty reduced word into alternating nonzero powers of $A$ and $B$. If its first and last blocks are both powers of $A$, apply it to any point of $Y$, reading blocks from right to left: the image alternates into $X,Y,\ldots,X$, so it differs from the starting point in $Y$. The same argument applies with the two letters interchanged. It includes a single block.

If the first and last blocks use different letters, suppose the first is $A^r$ and the last a power of $B$. Choose an integer $s\ne0,-r$. The conjugate $A^s w A^{-s}$ has first block $A^{s+r}$ and last block $A^{-s}$, both nonzero, with alternating nonzero blocks between. The previous argument proves this conjugate nonidentity, hence $w$ is nonidentity. If the first letter is $B$, use the analogous conjugation. These cases exhaust all reduced words. ∎

What finite searches can and cannot prove

For arbitrary integral unimodular inputs, their inverses are integral by the adjugate formula and determinant $\pm1$. Enumerating nonempty reduced words by length and multiplying exactly eventually finds a relation whenever one exists; on a free pair it never halts. This is precisely a semidecision for nonfreeness, not a decision algorithm.

Passing to a modulus does not turn a modular relation into an integral relation. For every integer $q\ge2$, the displayed matrix satisfies $A^q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2q\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which is the identity modulo $q$ but not over $\mathbb Z$. Indeed every finite matrix group has relations even if the original pair is free. Finite checks can certify the absence of short relations, but the unbounded quantifier needs an argument such as the cone certificate above.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact enumeration checks all reduced words through a fixed finite length for the displayed pair and verifies modular false positives. The proof, not that enumeration, handles arbitrary word length. No algorithm deciding every pair in every dimension is obtained, and no undecidability claim is inferred from the failure of these searches.
