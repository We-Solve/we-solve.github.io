The two construction targets

The source, printed pages2990--2991, distinguishes good quantum LDPC codes from locally testable classical codes, then asks for good locally testable quantum codes and linear-rate PCPs. The Panteleev--Kalachev primary abstract was checked: its quantum goodness and classical local-testability claims are distinct. Neither is silently substituted for the simultaneous quantum requirements. We attempt two elementary constructions and give complete obstructions. The original construction agenda remains unresolved by them.

Why copying a classical check matrix is not enough

For a binary matrix $H$ of rank $r$ with $N$ columns, CSS commutation requires $H_XH_Z^T=0$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Indeed a tensor product $X^u$ and $Z^v$ commute with sign $(-1)^{u\cdot v}$, because the one-qubit $X,Z$ anticommute exactly at coordinates where both exponents are one. Taking $H_X=H_Z=H$ does not ensure this: rows $(1,1,0)$ and $(0,1,1)$ have dot product one.

A tempting repair is \[ H_X=H_Z=[H\ H]. \] Now $H_XH_Z^T=HH^T+HH^T=0$, row weights double and column degrees remain those of $H$. Each matrix has rank $r$. The $X$- and $Z$-type generator families jointly have $2r$ independent commuting generators: a relation with zero Pauli support requires the two binary exponent sums to vanish separately. Their group does not contain $-I$, since the commutation condition removes signs when reordering generators and the independent chosen generators have no nontrivial zero support relation. Averaging the group gives a projector of trace $2^{2N-2r}$, because all nonidentity Paulis are traceless. Thus the code encodes $2N-2r$ qubits on $2N$ physical qubits. It preserves a positive rate when $r/N$ is bounded below one.

However, whenever $r<N$ its quantum distance is at most two. Since the row space of $H$ is proper, some standard basis vector $e_i$ does not belong to it. The Pauli $X$ supported on the two copies of coordinate $i$ has binary support $(e_i,e_i)$ and weight two. It commutes with every $Z$ check, as $He_i+He_i=0$, and with every $X$ check. It is not in the stabilizer group: a pure-$X$ stabilizer has support $(u,u)$ with $u$ in the row space of $H$, whereas $e_i$ is outside. A product involving $Z$ generators can be pure $X$ only when its total $Z$ support vanishes, in which case it contributes no new $X$ support. This is a nontrivial logical Pauli of weight two. The attempted commutation repair therefore destroys the required linear distance even before local testability is considered.

Bounded check weight is not a soundness proof

For an explicit classical diagnostic, take the binary repetition code on an even cycle of length $N\ge4$, checked by adjacent parities $x_i+x_{i+1}$. Every check has weight two and every bit appears in two checks. A word equal to one on a consecutive half-cycle and zero elsewhere violates exactly two checks. Its distance from either constant codeword is $N/2$. Uniformly sampling one check rejects with probability $2/N$, while the normalized distance from the code is $1/2$. Any inequality of the form rejection probability at least $\alpha$ times normalized distance therefore has $\alpha\le4/N$. Bounded locality and degree alone do not establish constant local-testability soundness. This example has low rate and is not a counterexample to the existence of good locally testable codes.

A linear-size proof whose constant-query gap vanishes

For the PCP side, storing a Boolean assignment gives linear proof length, and querying one clause has bounded query cost. But soundness is not automatic. On variables $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ consider the $n+1$ clauses \[ x_1,\quad \neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}\ (1\le i<n),\quad \neg x_n. \] The formula is unsatisfiable: the first clause and all implications force every variable true, contradicting the last clause. Nevertheless deleting any one clause makes it satisfiable. Deleting the first allows all zeros; deleting the last allows all ones; deleting the implication at $i$ allows ones through $i$ followed by zeros. These assignments violate exactly the corresponding deleted clause.

For any distribution on the clauses, some clause has selection probability at most $1/(n+1)$. Use the assignment violating only that clause. The verifier that samples and checks one clause then accepts this proof of an unsatisfiable instance with probability at least $1-1/(n+1)$, even though it reads at most two proof bits. Thus choosing a clever distribution for the same one-clause verifier does not fix the gap. With $q$ independent clause samples, the same proof is rejected with probability at most $q/(n+1)$ by the union bound. A bounded-query constant-gap PCP needs a different encoding or checking mechanism; proof length and locality alone are not enough.
