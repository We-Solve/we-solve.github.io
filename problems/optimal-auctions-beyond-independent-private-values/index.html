Statement

Original atlas ID: M7. Formulation: editorial specification of a research family.

Definitions and assumptions

Fix $n$ bidders, a finite set $K$ of indivisible objects, a feasible allocation set $X$, and finite private signals $s_i\in S_i$. An environment $\omega$ specifies a joint signal law $\pi_\omega$, possibly interdependent values $v_{i,\omega}(x,s)$, nondecreasing utility over money $U_{i,\omega}$, private liquidity bounds $b_{i,\omega}(s_i)$, and entry costs. A mechanism $m$ is a finite extensive-form auction with perfect recall, including any entry and information-acquisition stages, feasible allocation lotteries, and transfer rules. Realized payments must meet the specified budget constraints. Let $\mathcal E(m,\omega)$ be its nonempty set of sequential-equilibrium assessments, using the finite-game consistency definition; every admissible auction and environment must meet this requirement. Admissible mechanisms must satisfy the declared ex-ante or interim participation requirement, with utility evaluated under the environment's actual risk preferences.

Formal research question

For a stated collection $\Omega$ of such environments and feasible mechanism class $\mathfrak M_F$, characterize \[ \sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_F}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e\in\mathcal E(m,\omega)} \mathbb E_{m,\omega,e}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n t_i-C(x)\right], \] where $t_i$ is the bidder-to-seller payment and $C(x)$ the declared seller cost. Investigate restrictions giving a tractable optimal or approximately optimal format when correlation, interdependence, liquidity, risk, and entry coexist. If the objective is efficiency instead, replace revenue with total surplus and explicitly decide how entry and risk-bearing costs enter it. Prove an approximation bound relative to the same feasible class, not to an unrelated independent-private-values optimum.

Interpretation and scope

This formulation records the source atlas's extensions without pretending that one scalar virtual-value formula covers all of them. Interdependent values mean that utility depends on others' true signals even after allocation; correlation of private values concerns their joint distribution and is a distinct feature. Risk aversion concerns monetary utility and must not be conflated with a hard liquidity constraint. Entry is an equilibrium decision whose response can change both participation and information. A finite-type, quasilinear direct-mechanism problem with fixed entry is frequently expressible as a linear program; that computational benchmark is not being proposed as an unsolved theorem. Continuous types, richer information acquisition, and practical format restrictions require their own guarantees.

Source audit and status

Myerson (1981), Milgrom--Weber (1982), and Maskin--Riley (2000) are verified through publisher or author primary records. Original link [13] correctly identifies Myerson. Milgrom--Weber analyzes affiliated/interdependent auction environments under restrictions, and Maskin--Riley studies asymmetry; neither provides a universal best auction across all listed features. The shorthand title "beyond independent private values" is therefore too broad to define a single theorem-level open problem. The robust extensive-form objective above is an editorial research-family specification. Many subcases have established solutions, and any claimed new open instance must name its primitive restrictions, solution concept, and feasible auction formats.

Theorem (Compact posted-price family with no robust optimizer): There is a one-bidder, one-object instance satisfying the stated finite-game and budget requirements for which the robust supremum equals $1$ but is not attained, even though the parameter set of admissible prices is compact.
Proof: The bidder's value is the known number $1$, monetary utility is quasilinear, budget is $1$, entry cost and seller cost are zero, and $\Omega$ is a singleton. Let $\mathfrak M_F$ consist exactly of posted prices $p\in[0,1]$, followed by the bidder's accept/reject decision. Accept allocates the object and charges $p$; reject allocates nothing and charges zero. Each game is finite, with perfect recall, and all realized payments meet the budget.

For $p<1$, accepting gives utility $1-p>0$, strictly above rejection. The unique sequentially rational outcome is acceptance, and worst-equilibrium revenue is $p$. For $p=1$, both actions give zero utility. Every mixture is sequentially rational, including rejection with probability one. There is only one information set at the bidder's decision, so each of these strategies is the limit of completely mixed strategies with the same trivial beliefs and is a sequential-equilibrium assessment. Hence worst-equilibrium revenue at $p=1$ is zero. Participation holds in every case.

It follows that \[ W(p)=\begin{cases}p,&0\le p<1,\\0,&p=1,\end{cases} \qquad \sup_{p\in[0,1]}W(p)=1, \] but $W(p)<1$ for every admissible $p$. For every $0<\epsilon<1$, the same-class price $p=1-\epsilon$ attains $V^*-\epsilon$; it is also a $(1-\epsilon)$ approximation here since $V^*=1$. This proves both the nonattainment and its explicit approximation without changing the benchmark. ∎

Proposition (A zero-value environment forces a trivial robust optimum): Suppose $\Omega$ contains an environment with all bidders' values identically zero, quasilinear utility, outside utility zero, and nonnegative entry costs. Suppose every admitted equilibrium satisfies ex-ante participation for every bidder, seller costs are nonnegative, and the null mechanism with zero cost is feasible. Then $V^*=0$.
Proof: In the zero-value environment, participation gives ${\mathbb{E}}[-t_i-\text{entry cost}_i]\ge0$, hence ${\mathbb{E}} t_i\le0$ for every bidder. Thus expected seller profit is at most zero in every admitted equilibrium of every admissible mechanism in this environment. Nonemptiness of the equilibrium sets makes the robust infimum meaningful and gives $W(m)\le0$ for every $m$. The null mechanism gives profit zero in every environment and has a trivial equilibrium, so $V^*\ge0$. Combining the inequalities proves the claim. This conclusion is for the explicitly quasilinear zero-value environment; arbitrary flat monetary utility would not justify the same transfer bound. ∎
Proposition (Exact finite-outcome truthful-equilibrium programme): Fix a finite $\Omega$, finite signal sets with full-support laws $\pi_\omega$, exogenous information, and no endogenous entry or further information acquisition. Fix a finite set $O$ of outcomes, each including a feasible allocation and a transfer vector that is budget-feasible for every possible true signal in every environment. Fix finite utility numbers $u_{i,\omega}(o,s)$ and seller profits $v_0(o)=\sum_i t_i(o)-C(x_o)$. Robust Bayesian incentive compatibility and interim participation of a direct lottery mechanism, evaluated at truthful reporting, are finite linear constraints. Maximizing worst-environment truthful-equilibrium profit over this fixed-outcome class is an attained finite linear programme whenever its feasible set is nonempty.
Proof: Let $\lambda_{r,o}$ be the probability of outcome $o$ when the report profile is $r$. Require $\lambda_{r,o}\ge0$ and $\sum_o\lambda_{r,o}=1$ for each report profile. For every environment, bidder, true signal $s_i$, and alternative report $r_i$, impose \[ \sum_{s_{-i}}\pi_\omega(s_{-i}\mid s_i) \sum_o\left(\lambda_{(s_i,s_{-i}),o}-\lambda_{(r_i,s_{-i}),o}\right) u_{i,\omega}(o,s)\ge0. \] These are exactly the conditional expected utility comparisons under truthful reports of other bidders, including arbitrary correlation and interdependent values through the coefficients. Interim participation imposes \[ \sum_{s_{-i}}\pi_\omega(s_{-i}\mid s_i) \sum_o\lambda_{s,o}u_{i,\omega}(o,s)\ge u^{\rm out}_{i,\omega}(s_i). \] For example $u_{i,\omega}(o,s)=U_{i,\omega}(v_{i,\omega}(x_o,s)-t_i(o))$ is a permitted specified utility convention. Nonlinearity of $U$ does not make these constraints nonlinear in the lottery probabilities: its values are already fixed coefficients. Universal outcome budget feasibility ensures that a deviation is not surreptitiously removed or admitted according to a misreported liquidity limit.

Introduce $\rho$ and require for every $\omega$ that \[ \rho\le\sum_s\pi_\omega(s)\sum_o\lambda_{s,o}v_0(o). \] Maximize $\rho$. Every displayed constraint is linear in $\lambda,\rho$. The feasible $\lambda$ form a closed subset of a finite product of simplexes and hence a compact set. The minimum of the finitely many continuous expected-profit functions is continuous on that set. If nonempty, it attains its maximum, giving an optimal $\lambda$ and corresponding $\rho$. This proves the finite optimization and attainment assertion; input encoding and numerical accuracy must be specified before any bit-complexity claim. ∎

Essential limitation. Bayesian incentive compatibility certifies truthful reporting, not every equilibrium's payoff. The programme therefore does not reformulate $\inf_{e\in\mathcal E(m,\omega)}$. Transferring its value to the original objective requires an additional implementation theorem.

Example (Entry creates a good and a bad sequential equilibrium): There are two bidders and two objects, one assigned object valued at $1$ by each bidder. Values are known, utility quasilinear, budgets are $1$, and seller costs are zero. Each bidder simultaneously chooses whether to enter. Entry costs $1/4$, paid to an outside party. If both enter, each receives its assigned object and pays the seller $1/2$. If only one enters, no object is allocated and the seller charges nothing. If neither enters, nothing happens.

Writing $N$ for no entry and $E$ for entry, the bidder payoff matrix is \[ \begin{array}{c|cc} &N&E\\\hline N&(0,0)&(0,-1/4)\\ E&(-1/4,0)&(1/4,1/4) \end{array} \] Both $(N,N)$ and $(E,E)$ are strict Nash equilibria: an isolated entrant loses $1/4$, while with another entrant entry gains $1/4$. Encode the simultaneous choices as a finite extensive form in which the second player does not observe the first choice. Each Nash profile, together with the beliefs induced by the other player's action, is sequentially rational and is the limit of completely mixed behavioral trembles with Bayesian beliefs. Thus both are sequential equilibria. Seller profit is $0$ at $(N,N)$ and $1$ at $(E,E)$.

The option of not entering gives each bidder zero against every opponent action, so participation holds in every equilibrium, including the mixed one. All payments meet budgets. Seller profit is nonnegative for every outcome. Hence the worst-equilibrium profit is exactly zero despite the profitable equilibrium. This explicitly demonstrates the missing selection/entry step in transferring a fixed-entry truthful benchmark to the robust extensive-form objective.

Finite posted-price guarantees with correlation, risk, and liquidity

Target and restricted domain

The catalogue target is a broad research family: maximize worst-case revenue across environments and sequential equilibria when correlation, interdependence, risk, budgets, and entry may coexist. We study a precise subfamily with a sharp finite-support benchmark. There is one object, zero seller cost, no entry or information-acquisition cost, and $n$ bidders with finite private types. A type specifies a monetary private value $v_i\geq0$, a budget $b_i\geq0$, and a strictly increasing utility function $U_i$ of monetary surplus. Values are private, but their joint law, budgets, and risk preferences can be arbitrarily correlated. Define willingness $w_i=\min\{v_i,b_i\}$ and $W=\max_iw_i$.

Fix a known finite grid $0<z_1<\cdots<z_m$ containing every positive willingness in every environment; $w_i=0$ is allowed. Set $z_0=0$ and \[ H=\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{z_k-z_{k-1}}{z_k},\qquad \alpha_k=\frac{z_k-z_{k-1}}{H z_k}. \tag{1} \] Thus $(\alpha_k)$ is a probability vector. Benchmark mechanisms are finite extensive forms with commitment, realized nonnegative payments bounded by budgets, and ex-post individual rationality in every realized allocation/payment outcome. An allocated bidder has monetary surplus $v_i-t_i$, a nonrecipient has $-t_i$, and ex-post participation means utility at least $U_i(0)$. Random allocations are permitted but participation applies outcome by outcome. These are stronger restrictions than the target's possible interim participation convention; both the proposed auction and the benchmark obey the same restrictions.

Myerson's characterization concerns a different specified informational domain [Myerson]. Prior-free randomized pricing and competitive-ratio analysis have established antecedents [BS,EM]. We do not assert novelty for the harmonic-support calculation. Its role here is to give complete finite proofs, retain adverse equilibrium selection, and specify exactly which extensions are covered.

A guarantee against every sequential equilibrium

Fix an order of bidders. Draw $k$ publicly with probabilities $\alpha_k$, announce price $p_k=(1-\eta)z_k$ for $0<\eta<1$, and offer the object once to each bidder in that order until someone accepts. A bidder never receives another offer after rejecting. Refusal costs zero; acceptance is feasible only within the bidder's budget. This is a finite extensive-form mechanism.
Theorem (Pointwise revenue guarantee): For every environment in the restricted domain, every type profile in its support, and every sequential equilibrium of the posted-price auction, expected revenue conditional on that profile satisfies \[ {\mathbb{E}}[\mathrm{Rev}\mid(v_i,b_i,U_i)_i]\geq\frac{1-\eta}{H}W. \tag{2} \] Every admissible benchmark mechanism, under every equilibrium satisfying the specified ex-post participation requirement, has realized revenue at most $W$. Consequently, if $V^*$ denotes the catalogue's robust maximin revenue over this same benchmark class, the posted-price auction earns worst-case revenue at least $(1-\eta)V^*/H$. It also attains this factor relative to the optimal revenue for each fixed environment. The approximation ratio is asserted only when the comparison benchmark is positive.
Proof: An offered bidder with $p<w_i$ can afford the price and obtains strictly positive monetary surplus from acceptance. Strict increase of $U_i$ makes acceptance strictly preferable to rejection, whose payoff is zero because there is no later offer. This comparison is independent of all beliefs about other bidders and of the shape of $U_i$. Sequential rationality therefore forces acceptance at every reached information set with $p<w_i$.

If $p<W$, take the first bidder with $w_i>p$. Either a preceding bidder already accepted, producing revenue $p$, or this bidder receives the offer and accepts. In either case the seller receives $p$ with probability one. If $W=z_j$, every $k\leq j$ has $p_k<z_k\leq W$. Revenue is nonnegative at other prices, so its conditional expectation is at least \[ \sum_{k\leq j}\alpha_k(1-\eta)z_k =\frac{1-\eta}{H}\sum_{k\leq j}(z_k-z_{k-1}) =\frac{1-\eta}{H}z_j. \] If $W=0$, nonnegative revenue gives (2) directly. Indifference at other prices cannot reduce this lower bound.

For the upper bound, an unallocated bidder's ex-post participation and strict increase of $U_i$ require $-t_i\geq0$. Together with nonnegative payments this gives $t_i=0$. The sole allocated bidder, if any, must have $t_i\leq v_i$ and $t_i\leq b_i$. Thus total revenue is at most $W$ outcome by outcome, even for randomized mechanisms.

Integrating (2), and taking the infimum over equilibria and environments, gives at least $(1-\eta)\inf_\omega{\mathbb{E}}_\omega W/H$. Every benchmark mechanism has worst-case revenue at most $\inf_\omega{\mathbb{E}}_\omega W$, so this proves the robust maximin comparison. Applying the same upper bound separately to each environment proves the environmentwise comparison. The finite posted-price game has sequential equilibria: backward induction at each offer prescribes acceptance, refusal, or any mixture at a tie, and the finite tree permits consistent beliefs from completely mixed trembles. Thus the equilibrium infima used here are nonempty. ∎

A matching upper bound for a one-buyer ratio

The robust maximin revenue and a ratio to each environment's optimum are different objectives. The following sharpness result concerns the latter. Consider one risk-neutral buyer, budget at least $z_m$, and possible values $z_1,\ldots,z_m$. The ambiguity set contains the point-mass environment at each value. In the point-mass environment $j$, the supremum of revenue under adverse equilibrium selection is $z_j$: prices strictly below $z_j$ approach it and participation bounds revenue above by it.
Lemma (A finite menu inequality): Suppose a finite menu of expected allocations $x_j\in[0,1]$ and payments $t_j$ satisfies \[ z_jx_j-t_j\geq0,\qquad z_jx_j-t_j\geq z_jx_k-t_k\quad\text{for all }j,k. \] Then \[ \sum_{j<m}\left(\frac1{z_j}-\frac1{z_{j+1}}\right)t_j +\frac{t_m}{z_m}\leq1, \qquad \min_j\frac{t_j}{z_j}\leq\frac1H. \tag{3} \]
Proof: Participation for type 1 gives $t_1/z_1\leq x_1$. Incentive compatibility for type $j\geq2$ against report $j-1$ gives $(t_j-t_{j-1})/z_j\leq x_j-x_{j-1}$. Adding yields \[ \frac{t_1}{z_1}+\sum_{j=2}^m\frac{t_j-t_{j-1}}{z_j}\leq x_m\leq1. \] Collecting the coefficients of $t_j$ proves the first inequality. They are positive, and their scalar product with $(z_j)_j$ equals $1+\sum_{j=2}^m(z_j-z_{j-1})/z_j=H$. If every $t_j/z_j$ exceeded $1/H$, the first inequality would be violated. The weak conclusion follows. ∎
Theorem (Sharp supremal competitive ratio): Among all finite committed one-buyer mechanisms in this subfamily, the supremum guaranteed ratio of revenue to the corresponding point-mass optimum, with worst-case sequential-equilibrium selection, is exactly $1/H$.
Proof: Fix a mechanism. For each value $z_j$, choose any sequential-equilibrium buyer strategy and denote its expected allocation/payment by $(x_j,t_j)$. Because there is only one strategic buyer and the mechanism is committed, a buyer of value $z_j$ can mimic the entire strategy chosen by value $z_k$. The random mechanism has the same distribution under that mimicry. Root optimality and participation imply exactly the inequalities of Lemma Lemma (A finite menu inequality). Thus for some $j$ this chosen equilibrium has revenue at most $z_j/H$. The worst equilibrium in that environment has no higher revenue, proving the upper bound. Three exact consistency diagnostics

Arbitrary off-path beliefs can fail

Players 1 and 2 simultaneously choose Out or In, receiving $0$ for Out and $-1$ for In regardless of subsequent play. If someone enters, player 3 moves without seeing who entered. Its information set has nodes Both, Only1, and Only2. Action $L$ pays it $1$ at Both and $0$ otherwise; action $R$ pays $0$ at Both and $1$ otherwise. This has a finite perfect-recall representation. Both early players strictly choose Out. On-path Bayes equations alone permit belief one on Both and choice $L$, which is then a best reply. It is not sequentially consistent. For completely mixed entry probabilities $p,q$, \[ \mu(\mathrm{Both}) =\frac{pq}{pq+p(1-q)+(1-p)q} =\frac{pq}{p+q-pq}\le\min\{p,q\}. \] As $p,q\to0$, consistency forces belief zero on Both. Every sequential equilibrium therefore prescribes $R$. Removing the strategically off-path information set would lose this restriction.

Different information sets need a common witness

Let player 1 choose Out, $L$, or $R$, with Out terminal. After $L$ or $R$, player 2 has an information set $I$ with a forced action, then an information set $J$, still unable to distinguish $L$ from $R$. Perfect recall holds; payoffs may all be zero. Every completely mixed profile gives the same $L/R$ posterior at both sets. When the limiting strategy chooses Out with probability one, either posterior separately can approach any value, but their limits must coincide. Independent information-set witnesses would wrongly permit $\mu_I(L)=1$, $\mu_J(L)=0$.

Varying the parameter in the closure perturbs Nature

Nature chooses $A$ with probability $p$, and $B$ otherwise. Player 1 observes the state and chooses Enter or Out; after entry player 2 cannot distinguish the states. For $0<p<1$, use entry probabilities $1/2$ at $A$ and $p^2$ at $B$, completed to fully mixed profiles. The entry posterior of $A$ is \[ \frac{p/2}{p/2+(1-p)p^2}\longrightarrow1\quad(p\downarrow0). \] At fixed $p=0$, state $A$ is Nature-impossible. Entry at $B$ is live, even if the limiting strategic probability is zero, and strategic trembles force its posterior to be $B$ with probability one. Closing over varying parameters gives the wrong assessment; (eq:consistency) keeps this game fixed.

The expected seller profit is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-72-65-76-65-6e-75-65} \operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e)=\sum_z P_z(c,\sigma)\,r_z(m). \end{equation} Beliefs constrain equilibria but do not replace actual path probabilities in this expression. After primitive graphs are lifted, it is semialgebraic. At fixed parameters it is a continuous polynomial in behavior. In (eq:revenue), $c$ is the Nature vector of $p=(m,\omega)$ given by $\operatorname{Graph}(p,c,u)$, and $z$ ranges over the terminal set $Z_S$ of the legal actual-support game of the stratum $S$ containing $p$. In formulas an atom $\operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e)\bowtie v$, with $\bowtie$ one of $=$ and $\ge$, stands for the disjunction over strata $S$ of \[ \mathcal P(p)\wedge\exists c,u\left[\operatorname{Graph}(p,c,u)\wedge \operatorname{Supp}_S(c)\wedge \sum_{z\in Z_S}P_z(c,\sigma)\,r_z(m)\bowtie v\right]. \] Thus $c$ and $u$ are bound in (eq:worstfixed), (eq:guarantee), and all later formulas. Where the graphs are total and single-valued, exactly one stratum and one graph vector qualify, so the atom compares $v$ with (eq:revenue) at the actual Nature vector. Theorem Theorem (Fixed-game existence and compactness) makes the inner worst-equilibrium value an attained minimum. Its value relation is exactly \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-77-6f-72-73-74-66-69-78-65-64} \begin{split} \operatorname{Worst}(m,\omega,v):={}& \exists e\,[\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e)\wedge\operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e)=v]\\ &{}\wedge \forall e'\,[\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e')\Rightarrow \operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e')\ge v]. \end{split} \end{equation} In particular the universal clause includes unfavorable equilibria.

Write $\mathcal F(m)$ for robust feasibility. It includes $D(m)$, uniform graph and game validity under every $\omega\in\Omega$, physical allocation and true-prefix-cash constraints from Section sec:validity, and the declared participation inequalities for every $\omega$ and every $e$ satisfying $\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e)$. One may retain the redundant explicit check $\exists e\,\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e)$. It is proved true for every valid game.

All parts are finite first-order formulas. Graph totality and uniqueness, probability constraints, legal-action guards, observation equalities, and utility monotonicity are uniformly verified hypotheses or quantified predicates. The finite combinatorial recall tests are performed on the legal templates. The remaining budget and allocation tests are imposed on every structurally live legal history. The participation conditions include the true-probability version of (eq:interimIR) or the declared ex-ante alternative. In (eq:interimIR), $\Pr(z)=P_z(c,\sigma)$ and $\Pr(s_i)=\sum_{z\in Z_S:\,s_i(z)=s_i}P_z(c,\sigma)$, with $c$ and $u$ bound as in the revenue atoms. These predicates and their universal quantifiers are passed to the same complete algebraic procedure.

The robust order is one design choice before all environments and all equilibria: \[ \exists m\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega\quad \forall e\in\mathcal E(m,\omega). \] Validity is required separately for every environment. It cannot be hidden solely in an equilibrium implication with a false antecedent. Legality and support patterns are functions of the actual parameters, not extra favorable choices for the designer. First decide whether $\Omega$ and $\{m:\mathcal F(m)\}$ are nonempty. If either is empty, report that fact and no optimizer.

A finite robust value without compactness assumptions

Assume the environment and feasible design sets are nonempty. For each feasible mechanism define \[ W(m)=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e:\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e)} \operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e). \] No minimum over environments or maximum over mechanisms is presumed. For fixed $m$, its finitely many finite terminal seller profits are independent of hidden $\omega$. Therefore \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-65-61-66-62-6f-75-6e-64-73} \min_z r_z(m)\le\operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e)\le\max_z r_z(m) \end{equation} for every environment and equilibrium. In particular $W(m)$ is finite even when $\Omega$ is not compact.

Exact real-algebraic sample-point extraction, detailed below, gives an algebraic feasible $m_0$ and an algebraic $\omega_0\in\Omega$. The first point is extracted from the actual quantified feasibility condition $\mathcal F$, not merely from $D$. Set \[ L_0=\min_z r_z(m_0). \] Then $V^*=\sup_{\mathcal F(m)}W(m)\ge W(m_0)\ge L_0$. At the fixed environment $\omega_0$, true terminal cash feasibility for every feasible mechanism gives \[ \sum_i\tau_i\le\sum_i\max_{s_i}b_i(\omega_0,s_i). \] Nonnegative external cash costs only reduce the cash available for seller payments. Hence \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-76-61-6c-75-65-62-6f-75-6e-64} V^*\le B_0:= \sum_i\max_{s_i}b_i(\omega_0,s_i)-\min_{x\in X}C(x)<\infty. \end{equation} Indeed every equilibrium at $\omega_0$ satisfies this pointwise upper bound, and the infimum over all environments is no larger. For an alternative funding convention the same argument is used only when its own finite, effectively specified uniform seller-profit envelope has been supplied and proved. Unbounded external funding is not included in the finite-value claim. Thus $V^*$ is a finite real number by explicit cash bounds, without equilibrium continuity or strict-obedience assumptions.

Exact value, attainment, and same-class approximations

Define the lower-guarantee predicates \begin{align} A(m,t)&:=\mathcal F(m)\ \wedge\ \forall\omega,e\, [\,\Omega(\omega)\wedge\operatorname{SE}(m,\omega,e) \Rightarrow\operatorname{Rev}(m,\omega,e)\ge t\,],\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-75-61-72-61-6e-74-65-65}\\ B(t)&:=\exists m\,A(m,t).\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-68-72-65-73-68-6f-6c-64} \end{align} Because all equilibrium fibers are nonempty, $A(m,t)$ means precisely $\mathcal F(m)$ and $W(m)\ge t$, whether or not the inner environment infimum is attained. The truth set of $B$ is nonempty, downward closed, and has finite supremum $V^*$. Define \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-56-61-6c-75-65} \begin{split} \operatorname{Value}(v):={}& [\,\forall t\ (B(t)\Rightarrow t\le v)\,]\\ &{}\wedge [\,\forall\eta>0\ \exists t\ (B(t)\wedge t>v-\eta)\,]. \end{split} \end{equation} These are real quantifiers; positivity is an ordinary polynomial guard.
Lemma (The value formula): Formula (eq:Value) holds exactly for $v=V^*$. Its quantifier-free form defines a real-algebraic singleton.
Proof: The first clause says $v$ is an upper bound for the truth set of $B$. The second says that no smaller number is an upper bound. These are exactly the defining properties of its finite supremum. Apply complete real quantifier elimination to obtain an equivalent Boolean combination of rational polynomial sign conditions in $v$. If none of its nonzero polynomials vanished at the unique solution, all their signs would stay unchanged in a neighborhood. The Boolean formula would then be constant there, contradicting the singleton property. A nonzero rational polynomial therefore vanishes at $V^*$, making it real algebraic. Real-root isolation and sign determination select the correct root. ∎
Lemma (Exact sample witnesses): A nonempty semialgebraic set over real-algebraic coefficients has a constructible algebraic sample point.
Proof: Use an exact sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition. In its first coordinate choose an appropriate algebraic root or a rational point between adjacent roots. Proceed successively in the chosen fiber. Section values are algebraic over the field generated by the preceding coordinates, hence algebraic over $\mathbb Q$. Open sectors, including unbounded ones, contain algebraic sample points. The exact algorithm supplies their polynomials, isolating data, and the cells to which they belong. ∎
This explains the extraction used in Section sec:bounds. All statements invoke a complete classical algebraic algorithm with the hypotheses verified in Theorem Theorem (Exact symbolic assessment set).

Exact attainment is decided by \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-74-74-61-69-6e-6d-65-6e-74} \exists m\,A(m,V^*). \end{equation} If true, Lemma Lemma (Exact sample witnesses) extracts an algebraic optimizer. If false, the correct report is nonattainment. For every rational $\varepsilon>0$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-70-73-69-6c-6f-6e} \exists m\,A(m,V^*-\varepsilon) \end{equation} is true by the definition of supremum and downward closure. Extracting a point gives a mechanism in the original feasible class satisfying \[ \inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e\in\mathcal E(m_\varepsilon,\omega)} \operatorname{Rev}(m_\varepsilon,\omega,e)\ge V^*-\varepsilon. \] For any real $\varepsilon>0$, existence follows by choosing a smaller positive rational tolerance. Exact algebraic coordinate names are the output convention. Rational or decimal rounding is not part of this guarantee: it may change tie incentives or violate an equality constraint. No truthful-report revelation principle, equilibrium selection, discretization continuity, or uniform strict-incentive margin is used.

A rational-interval certificate alternative

Exact value extraction is unnecessary for a requested approximation. Choose rational $L\le L_0$ and $U\ge B_0$, and keep the witness $A(m_0,L)$. Query $B((L+U)/2)$. If true, extract a witness and replace $L$ by the midpoint. If false, replace $U$ by it: downward closure implies $V^*\le U$, even when the supremum is unattained. For positive initial width, after at most \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-69-73-65-63-74-69-6f-6e} \max\left\{0, \left\lceil\log_2\frac{U_{\mathrm{initial}}-L_{\mathrm{initial}}} {\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\} \end{equation} steps, $U-L\le\varepsilon$. Handle zero initial width separately with no queries. The saved lower witness satisfies \[ L\le W(\widehat m)\le V^*\le U. \] Every query is decided by the same complete real-algebraic algorithm. A checkable output contains the compiled formulas, exact mechanism coordinates, certificates for $A(\widehat m,L)$, the false threshold decisions actually used, and the initial cash upper bound. With exact $V^*$, one can instead certify $A(\widehat m,V^*-\varepsilon)$ directly.

Simpler formats, nonattainment, and threshold equality

Let $S$ be a declared semialgebraic subformat of the same design domain, inheriting the same environments, constraints, and equilibrium correspondence. Actual $\varepsilon$-achievability is exactly \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-69-6d-70-6c-65} \exists m\,[S(m)\wedge A(m,V^*-\varepsilon)]. \end{equation} Elimination decides this sentence and sample extraction constructs a witness when it is true. It does not replace sequential equilibrium by a truthful or designer-favorable equilibrium.

If publicly specified primitive data $\xi$ vary semialgebraically, construct $\operatorname{Value}(\xi,v)$ by (eq:Value). Eliminating $v,m$ from \[ \exists v,m\,[\operatorname{Value}(\xi,v)\wedge S(\xi,m) \wedge A(\xi,m,v-\varepsilon)] \] returns a quantifier-free polynomial-sign criterion on $(\xi,\varepsilon)$. This is an effective exact characterization of the data on which the specified simple format achieves the guarantee. It permits explicit restrictions on prices, symmetry, messages, rounds, and information acquisition.

If the simple class is nonempty, let $V_S$ be its supremum. The inequality $V_S\ge V^*-\varepsilon$ is weaker at equality: the supremum may be unattained. A strict gap $V^*-V_S<\varepsilon$ suffices. At equality, actual attainment of that threshold in $S$ is required. Formula (eq:simple) treats this boundary exactly. Sections sec:bounds--sec:simple, together with the equilibrium construction, prove Theorem Theorem (Exact robust design in the declared class).

Example (Prior tie-driven nonattainment): This example is retained from the prior Pro attempt [PriorPro]. A single bidder has known value $1$, linear monetary utility, budget $1$, zero entry cost, and zero seller cost. The environment set is a singleton and the design class consists of posted prices $p\in[0,1]$, followed by accept or reject. For $p<1$, acceptance is strictly optimal and worst-equilibrium revenue equals $p$. At $p=1$, every acceptance mixture is sequentially rational, including certain rejection. The unique decision information set has trivial Bayes belief, and every such mixture is a limit of completely mixed strategies with that belief. Thus all are sequential equilibria and worst revenue is zero: \[ W(p)= \begin{cases}p,&0\le p<1,\\0,&p=1.\end{cases} \qquad \sup_{p\in[0,1]}W(p)=1. \] Every mechanism meets the true budget and participation constraints. The compact price domain has no robust optimizer. For $0<\varepsilon<1$, $p=1-\varepsilon$ attains the same-class bound $V^*-\varepsilon$. Moreover, for the subformat $S=[0,a)$, $0<a<1$, we have $V_S=a$ without attainment. At $\varepsilon=1-a$, the supremal inequality holds with equality, but (eq:simple) is false.

Imported algebraic algorithms and bit complexity

We use complete real-closed-field quantifier elimination and algebraic sample-point extraction. Basu [Basu2014], Theorem 2.4 and Section 2.1.2 (PDF page 6), give the standard CAD arithmetic complexity. Theorem 2.27 (PDF page 16) gives effective elimination with quantifier-block and free-variable counts, including integer intermediate and output bit-size bounds. The detailed underlying algorithm is cited there as reference [25].

Let $k$ be the total number of real variables in the fully compiled formula, $s$ its polynomial-predicate count, $d_0$ the maximum degree, and $L$ the rational coefficient bit length. These counts include legality and support cases, primitive-graph variables, continuation and tremble variables, feasibility constraints, all optimization quantifiers, and free public-data coordinates. They are not merely the number of bidders or advertised price parameters. General CAD has an arithmetic bound of the form \[ (s d_0)^{\,2^{O(k)}}. \] For the bit statement, use dense polynomial encoding and clear constant rational denominators by positive integer multiples. A polynomial has at most $N=\binom{k+d_0}{k}$ coefficients; if their numerators and denominators have at most $L$ bits, the cleared integer coefficients have at most $L_{\mathbb Z}=(N+1)L$ bits. Put each compiled formula in prenex form, renaming bound variables apart and counting the renamed variables in $k$. Each atom becomes a sign condition on one of at most $s$ polynomials or a disjunction of two such conditions; $d_0$ is unchanged and the formula length grows by a constant factor. For a prenex formula with $\ell$ free variables and quantifier blocks of sizes $k_i$, with $\Pi_i$ the product over the blocks, Basu, Pollack, and Roy [BPR1996], Theorem 1.3.1 (pp. 1004--1005), compute an equivalent quantifier-free formula with $s^{(\ell+1)\Pi_i(k_i+1)}d_0^{(\ell+1)\Pi_iO(k_i)}$ arithmetic operations in the coefficient ring. The output is a disjunction of at most that many conjunctions, each of at most $s^{\Pi_i(k_i+1)}d_0^{\Pi_iO(k_i)}$ sign conditions on polynomials of degree at most $d_0^{\Pi_iO(k_i)}$. Since $\ell+\sum_ik_i\le k$, the exponent of $s$ is at most $2^k$. Their algorithms are well behaved $p.~1004$: over $\mathbb Z$, all intermediate and output integers have bit size at most the input bit size times the algebraic part $d_0^{(\ell+1)\Pi_iO(k_i)}$ of the complexity, and counting Boolean operations multiplies the operation count by a factor linear in the length of the input formula. At fixed total variable and degree bounds, the effective-QE theorem's operation counts are polynomial in predicate count and formula length, and its intermediate integer lengths are polynomial in coefficient length. Exact integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial bit cost in the dense input size and the requested rational accuracy input length. A rational $\varepsilon=a_\varepsilon/b_\varepsilon$ enters through one more variable $t$ and the atom $b_\varepsilon t-b_\varepsilon v +a_\varepsilon=0$. A formula containing $V^*$ is used with $V^*$ replaced by a variable $v$ constrained by $\operatorname{Value}(v)$, as in Section sec:simple.

For the bit statement, algebraic witnesses are taken from the quantifier-free output rather than from the cylindrical construction of Lemma Lemma (Exact sample witnesses). Let $\Psi(y)$, $y\in\mathbb R^\ell$, be satisfiable and quantifier free, with $s'$ polynomials in $\mathbb Z[y]$ of degree at most $d'$ and coefficient bit size at most $L'$. The sample-points subroutine of [BPR1996], Section 3.1.3 (pp. 1028--1029), returns univariate representations $(f,g_0,\dots,g_\ell)\in\mathbb Z[T]^{\ell+2}$. Their associated points $(g_1(\theta)/g_0(\theta),\dots,g_\ell(\theta)/g_0(\theta))$, with $\theta$ a real root of $f$ and $g_0(\theta)\ne0$, meet every connected component of the realization of every realizable sign condition on these polynomials $p.~1007$. The univariate sign determination of Section 3.1.4.3 $p.~1030$ gives the Thom encoding of each such root, that is, the signs of the derivatives of $f$ there, and the signs of the $s'$ polynomials at the associated point. These two steps are the well-behaved algorithm of Theorem 1.3.3 described in Section 3.2 $p.~1031$. They use $s'^{\,\ell+1}d'^{\,O(\ell)}$ arithmetic operations, and the polynomials $f,g_j$ have degree $O(d')^\ell$ and integer coefficients of bit size $L'd'^{\,O(\ell)}$ (proof of Theorem 4.1.1, p. 1031). The set defined by $\Psi$ is a nonempty union of such components, so evaluating $\Psi$ on the computed sign vectors selects an associated point that satisfies $\Psi$. Its univariate representation and Thom encoding form the algebraic witness. For an elimination output as above, $s'$ is polynomial in $s$, while $d'$ and $L'/L_{\mathbb Z}$ are bounded at fixed $k$ and $d_0$. Hence the witnesses, including $V^*$ as the witness of the one-variable formula (eq:Value), have descriptions of polynomial size and are computed at polynomial bit cost. The algorithm of Theorem Theorem (Exact robust design in the declared class) makes a bounded number of such eliminations and extractions. The bisection alternative adds the number of calls in (eq:bisection). After $n$ halvings each queried threshold is the initial lower endpoint plus $j2^{-n}$ times the initial width, with $0\le j\le2^n$, so its bit length is linear in $n$ and in the bit lengths of the initial endpoints.

This proves tractability at fixed compiled structural size. It proves no polynomial bound in unrestricted game size, no efficiency claim as the structural dimension varies, and no strict fixed-parameter-tractability claim. All formulas are explicitly constructed from finite input before the imported algorithm is applied. A full CAD run for the all-friction family below has not been performed or claimed.

A nonlinear family with all frictions active

Two bidders compete for one object, with zero seller cost. Bidder $i$ initially knows $(z_i,\beta_i)$. Here $z_i\in\{0,1\}$, while $\beta_i$ independently selects an initial cash budget $1$ or $2$, each with probability $1/2$. Budgets are independent across bidders and independent of the $z$'s. The joint signal law is \[ \pi_\rho(0,0)=\pi_\rho(1,1)=\frac{1+\rho}{4},\qquad \pi_\rho(0,1)=\pi_\rho(1,0)=\frac{1-\rho}{4}, \qquad \rho\in[1/4,1/2]. \] All profiles have positive probability, and the signals are correlated. If $i$ wins, its monetary value is \[ v_i=\frac14+z_i+\frac32z_j. \] Thus the realized value depends on the other's true signal even after allocation; this is genuine interdependence. Entry costs $1/32$ cash, and a further optional private signal costs $1/32$ cash, both paid to outside parties. The acquired binary report of $z_j$ has accuracy $\tau\in[9/10,19/20]$, with noises independent conditional on the true signals. Both reports have positive probability in every state.

Bidder $i$'s monetary utility has $\eta_i\in[1/100,1/50]$ and is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-74-69-6c-69-74-79-65-78-61-6d-70-6c-65} U_\eta(x)= \begin{cases} (1+4\eta)x+4\eta,&x<-2,\\ x-\eta x^2,&-2\le x\le3,\\ (1-6\eta)x+9\eta,&x>3. \end{cases} \end{equation} Values and first derivatives agree at the endpoints. The derivative decreases from $1+4\eta$ to $1-6\eta>0$ on the middle interval and is constant outside. Hence the extension is globally increasing and concave and is strictly concave on the relevant monetary outcomes. It is rational-semialgebraic and $U_\eta(0)=0$. Terminal utility is applied to value received minus net seller payments minus the actual external cash costs; these costs are not subtracted again in utility units.

Use an independent fair public priority draw, and offer the object serially at price $1$, once to each bidder. The calculations below condition on whichever bidder is drawn first. An invited bidder may decline entry at no cost, or enter, optionally buy the report, and accept or reject. It never receives another offer. Acceptance requires the actual affordable cash payment. Seller revenue excludes the external fees.

Strict acquisition by a low-signal, high-budget first bidder

For the first bidder with $z_i=0$ and budget $2$, put \[ p=\Pr(z_j=1\mid z_i=0)=\frac{1-\rho}{2}\in[1/4,3/8]. \] At $\tau=9/10$, consider the complete continuation: enter, acquire, and accept exactly after a positive report. Its terminal monetary outcomes $Z$ are \[ \frac{11}{16}\quad(z_j=1,+),\qquad -\frac{13}{16}\quad(z_j=0,+),\qquad -\frac1{16}\quad(-). \] The probabilities are respectively $9p/10$, $(1-p)/10$, and $9/10-4p/5$. Direct substitution gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-63-71-75-69-73-69-74-69-6f-6e-6d-6f-6d-65-6e-74-73} \mathbb E Z=\frac34p-\frac{11}{80},\qquad \mathbb E Z^2=\frac{456p+89}{1280}. \end{equation} In particular $\mathbb E Z\ge1/20$, while \[ \eta\,\mathbb E Z^2 \le\frac1{50}\frac{260}{1280}<\frac1{200}. \] Therefore $\mathbb E U_\eta(Z)>0$ throughout the parameter range. For example, at $\rho=1/3$, the two moments are $9/80$ and $241/1280$.

The positive-report posterior is \[ r_+=\frac{\tau p}{\tau p+(1-\tau)(1-p)}\ge\frac34, \] and the negative-report posterior satisfies \[ r_-=\frac{(1-\tau)p}{(1-\tau)p+\tau(1-p)}\le\frac1{16}. \] The bounds follow by monotonicity of the likelihood odds in $p,\tau$; their extreme values occur at $(p,\tau)=(1/4,9/10)$ and $(3/8,9/10)$, respectively. After both fees are sunk, acceptance gives $11/16$ or $-13/16$; rejection gives $-1/16$. At posterior $r$, the utility difference is exactly \[ \frac34(2r-1)-\eta\,\frac{168-48r}{256}. \] For a positive report, $r\ge3/4$ gives $168-48r\le132$. Its monetary part is at least $3/8$, and the absolute risk correction is at most $\eta(132/256)<1/50$, so acceptance is strict. For a negative report, the monetary part is already negative and the risk correction makes acceptance worse; rejection is strict.

Increasing $\tau$ above $9/10$ improves this policy's utility. Its derivative with respect to $\tau$ is \[ p[U_\eta(11/16)-U_\eta(-1/16)] +(1-p)[U_\eta(-1/16)-U_\eta(-13/16)]>0. \] This moves probability from a false acceptance to rejection, and from rejection to a correct acceptance. Thus the positive entry-and-acquisition payoff is uniform over the entire stated accuracy range.

Without acquiring information, entry followed by acceptance has mean monetary outcome \[ \frac32p-\frac{25}{32}\le-\frac7{32}. \] On all these outcomes $U_\eta(x)\le x$, so its expected utility is negative. Entering and rejecting yields $U_\eta(-1/32)<0$; mixing these choices cannot improve their expected-utility maximum. Declining entry yields zero. The strictly positive acquiring policy, whose report-contingent choices are strict, therefore forces this type to enter and acquire in every sequential equilibrium. Full continuation rationality at the first invitation compares these complete policies using the true conditional prior; no favorable off-path belief is assumed. Once these actions are taken, both reports occur with positive probability and their Bayesian continuation choices are the strict ones above. Later bidder behavior cannot change this bidder's payoff because it never receives another offer.

Its largest cash requirement is \[ 1+\frac1{32}+\frac1{32}=\frac{17}{16}\le2. \] A budget-$1$ bidder cannot fund price $1$ even without acquiring, since entry plus acceptance costs $1+1/32>1$. Acquisition does not create cash. Entry would then only incur a loss, so this bidder strictly declines entry. Liquidity therefore changes actual conduct.

A worst-equilibrium same-class guarantee

A first bidder with $z_i=1$ and budget $2$ has value at least $5/4$. It strictly enters and accepts without information: entry and acceptance give strictly positive monetary surplus in every state, and acceptance is optimal after any report. Information cannot improve that already optimal decision and its strictly positive cash cost reduces utility. This type has probability $1/4$. Thus every sequential equilibrium in every displayed environment gives seller revenue at least $1/4$; later sale payments are nonnegative.
Proposition (All-friction same-class bound): Let $\mathfrak M_F$ be any declared feasible class for this environment family that contains the price-$1$ serial mechanism, uses the same true initial budgets and prefix cash constraints, and has zero seller cost. Its robust value satisfies \[ \frac14\le V^*\le4, \qquad W(m_{\mathrm{serial}})\ge\frac1{16}V^*. \]
Proof: The lower bound was proved for every equilibrium and environment. Each of the two true budgets is at most $2$. Every legal terminal of every same-class mechanism therefore has total net seller payments at most $4$, independent of participation or risk preferences. Hence $V^*\le4$, and $W(m_{\mathrm{serial}})\ge1/4\ge V^*/16$. ∎
This uses the same feasible class on both sides. The information acquisition is strictly active on a positive-probability type, changes its action with the report, and remains costly. Correlation, interdependence, strict monetary risk aversion, and conduct-changing private liquidity all coexist.

A continuous design family containing this mechanism lets the two serial prices vary independently over $[3/4,5/4]$, and chooses the public priority order with probability $\lambda_0\in[1/4,3/4]$. These are three real mechanism parameters. Each bidder has finitely many entry, acquisition, and accept/reject choices. Initial private signals, budgets, and acquired reports remain private, while the bidder remembers its own choices and observed priority and refusals. Accepting is permitted only when the actual cash payment can be made. Cash-action legality changes at finitely many affine comparisons between the offered price and remaining cash; the guards agree within each information set. The game family is consequently a finite union of rational semialgebraic legal strata with perfect recall. The environment parameters $(\rho,\tau,\eta_1,\eta_2)$ vary over the stated rational intervals. Nature laws, payoffs, and seller-independent cash costs all have rational-semialgebraic descriptions. The seller uses the announced prices and observed acceptances, not hidden state labels or unknown environment parameters.

The declared participation constraint is interim, with benchmark $u_i^{\mathrm{out}}\equiv0$ in (eq:interimIR). Interim participation holds in every equilibrium of this family. At a bidder's first invitation, no entry guarantees zero for every hidden state and every later play. Sequential rationality gives nonnegative expected actual utility at that information set. Averaging over invitations and the public priority draw preserves the inequality conditional on each initial private type. No invitation incurs no cost. Realized losses on some acquired-report branches are possible; the condition is interim participation, not outcome-by-outcome ex-post IR.

Scope and prior attempts

The canonical formulation asks for a stated collection of environments and a stated feasible mechanism class. The result here covers the entire declared semialgebraic design continuum for arbitrary finite input templates, including all legal and Nature-support boundary strata. It is not a universal characterization across every unbounded auction tree or arbitrary unrepresented monetary utility. The environment specification is common knowledge within each bidders' game; the seller's rules remain independent of hidden environment and signal information except through actual observations.

The exact consistency condition keeps the mechanism and environment fixed, uses one common completely mixed assessment witness, and imposes no equilibrium requirement on that witness. Full continuation comparisons, true liquidity, endogenous entry and acquisition, and universal equilibrium participation and revenue are retained. The finite robust bound comes from actual cash constraints, not equilibrium continuity. The value and witness formulas distinguish a supremum from an attained maximum and actual simple-format achievability from an unattained supremal threshold. The explicit nonlinear family shows that the five frictions can be simultaneously substantive, with a same-class approximation guarantee.

The prior Ultra attempt [PriorUltra] proves a restricted harmonic posted-price guarantee with private values, free participation and information, private budgets and monetary risk under stronger outcome-by-outcome participation. Its stated limitations include interdependence, costly entry and acquisition, and the broader interim benchmark. The prior Pro attempt [PriorPro] supplies the attributed nonattainment example, equilibrium-selection obstructions, a fixed-entry selected-truthful-equilibrium lottery program, and a finite-catalogue bound conditional on supplied worst-equilibrium enclosures.

The present proof explicitly constructs the full equilibrium and feasibility formulas and applies an imported complete real-algebraic algorithm to the entire stated design class. Exact symbolic sequential-equilibrium computation and real quantifier elimination have established precedents [KW1982,GEN2024,Basu2014]. The mathematical algorithm theorem is proved, but a full CAD execution for the all-friction family and practical large-game performance are not claimed.
