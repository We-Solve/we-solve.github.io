Optimal auctions beyond independent private values

Statement

Original atlas ID: M7. Formulation: editorial specification of a research family.

Definitions and assumptions

Fix $n$ bidders, a finite set $K$ of indivisible objects, a feasible allocation set $X$, and finite private signals $s_i\in S_i$. An environment $\omega$ specifies a joint signal law $\pi_\omega$, possibly interdependent values $v_{i,\omega}(x,s)$, nondecreasing utility over money $U_{i,\omega}$, private liquidity bounds $b_{i,\omega}(s_i)$, and entry costs. A mechanism $m$ is a finite extensive-form auction with perfect recall, including any entry and information-acquisition stages, feasible allocation lotteries, and transfer rules. Realized payments must meet the specified budget constraints. Let $\mathcal E(m,\omega)$ be its nonempty set of sequential-equilibrium assessments, using the finite-game consistency definition; every admissible auction and environment must meet this requirement. Admissible mechanisms must satisfy the declared ex-ante or interim participation requirement, with utility evaluated under the environment's actual risk preferences.

A member of this research family must supply a complete induced game for every admitted pair $(m,\omega)$. Its data include finite histories and action sets, information partitions, chance probabilities, and terminal allocation and transfer outcomes. The finite-game requirement applies to the induced game, not to the number of mechanisms in the design class. Allocation lotteries must have a specified finite-game representation. The treatment of Nature-impossible histories must be declared so that the stated consistency definition is applicable; strategically off-path information sets cannot simply be discarded. Continuous-action or continuously supported outcome games require a separately stated extension and equilibrium convention.

For each bidder, the data must specify a finite real terminal utility $u_{i,m,\omega}(z)$ at every terminal history $z$, evaluated by expected utility in the induced game. Its construction from $v_{i,\omega}$ and $U_{i,\omega}$ must state the monetary argument and its domain, initial wealth where relevant, and the placement of transfers, entry costs and information-acquisition costs. No composition such as $U(v-t)$ or $v+U(-t)$ is implicit. Nondecreasing monetary utility alone does not impose strict monotonicity or risk aversion; any such additional restriction must be stated. The game specification must also identify common-knowledge primitives, initial private information, acquisition technologies and costs, observations and timing, and the bidders' prior and information about $\omega$. The seller's infimum over environments does not itself specify bidders' beliefs. Seller rules may depend only on the information and commitment opportunities declared available to the seller.

The declared liquidity convention must specify budget domains, payment dates, terminal caps or cash constraints along histories, treatment of fees, refunds, subsidies and borrowing, and how privately known constraints restrict actions or are enforced. It must identify which histories are tested, including its treatment of off-equilibrium and Nature-impossible histories. The participation convention must supply outside-option utilities, decision timing, conditioning information for interim comparisons, treatment of sunk costs and zero-probability types, and quantifiers over environments and equilibria. These conventions are inputs defining $\mathfrak M_F$; no particular funding account, participation timing or equilibrium participation selector is chosen for the whole family. Every mechanism in $\mathfrak M_F$ must meet its declared admissibility requirements in every $\omega\in\Omega$, including the nonemptiness of $\mathcal E(m,\omega)$.

Formal research question

For a stated collection $\Omega$ of such environments and feasible mechanism class $\mathfrak M_F$, characterize \[ \sup_{m\in\mathfrak M_F}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\inf_{e\in\mathcal E(m,\omega)} \mathbb E_{m,\omega,e}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n t_i-C(x)\right], \] where $t_i$ is the bidder-to-seller payment and $C(x)$ the declared seller cost. Investigate restrictions giving a tractable optimal or approximately optimal format when correlation, interdependence, liquidity, risk, and entry coexist. If the objective is efficiency instead, replace revenue with total surplus and explicitly decide how entry and risk-bearing costs enter it. Prove an approximation bound relative to the same feasible class, not to an unrelated independent-private-values optimum.

For each instance, $\Omega$ and $\mathfrak M_F$ must be nonempty and stated as inputs, together with seller costs and common monetary units. Write $W(m)$ for the two inner infima and $V$ for the displayed supremum. Existence of each expectation does not ensure that $W(m)$ or $V$ is finite; any finite-value or approximation claim must state and justify the bounds it requires. No null mechanism, nonnegative guarantee or attainment is presumed.

A structural characterization need not be an algorithm or a finite algebraic test. A claim of computational tractability must additionally specify the primitive encoding or access model, representation of $\Omega$ and mechanisms, output and accuracy requirements, runtime variables, and whether the task is design optimization, equilibrium computation or certification of worst-equilibrium performance. An approximation claim must state its error convention and quantifiers. For example, an additive guarantee is $W(\widehat m)\ge V-\varepsilon$ for a declared $\varepsilon>0$ and finite $V$, with $\widehat m\in\mathfrak M_F$; a multiplicative guarantee requires explicit sign and scale conditions and cannot be inferred for possibly negative net revenue. No one approximation convention is imposed on the family. An efficiency instance must separately supply its welfare functional, units and any interpersonal weights, including the treatment of transfers, outside options, entry, information costs and risk. Monetary surplus and expected utility are not interchangeable welfare criteria.

Interpretation and scope

This formulation records the source atlas's extensions without pretending that one scalar virtual-value formula covers all of them. Interdependent values mean that utility depends on others' true signals even after allocation; correlation of private values concerns their joint distribution and is a distinct feature. Risk aversion concerns monetary utility and must not be conflated with a hard liquidity constraint. Entry is an equilibrium decision whose response can change both participation and information. A finite-type, quasilinear direct-mechanism problem with fixed entry is frequently expressible as a linear program; that computational benchmark is not being proposed as an unsolved theorem. Continuous types, richer information acquisition, and practical format restrictions require their own guarantees.

Source audit and status

Myerson (1981), Milgrom--Weber (1982), and Maskin--Riley (2000) are verified through publisher or author primary records. Original link [13] correctly identifies Myerson. Milgrom--Weber analyzes affiliated/interdependent auction environments under restrictions, and Maskin--Riley studies asymmetry; neither provides a universal best auction across all listed features. The shorthand title "beyond independent private values" is therefore too broad to define a single theorem-level open problem. The robust extensive-form objective above is an editorial research-family specification. Many subcases have established solutions, and any claimed new open instance must name its primitive restrictions, solution concept, and feasible auction formats.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Unless an entry states otherwise, agent and firm sets are finite. State and action spaces are measurable, strategies and outcome maps are measurable, probability laws are specified, and expectations exist for the targets under discussion. Infinite-horizon discount factors lie in $(0,1)$ and discounted objectives are finite. Norms, tolerances and complexity input sizes must be specified for computational comparisons. Constants or primitives that appear locally are inputs, not empirical facts asserted by this catalogue.

Identification. A statistical model $m\in\mathcal M$ implies an observable law $P_m$ and target $\theta(m)$. At law $P$, its identified set is \[ \Theta(P;\mathcal M)=\{\theta(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_m=P\}. \] Point identification holds when this set is a singleton, or equivalently when $P_{m_1}=P_{m_2}$ implies $\theta(m_1)=\theta(m_2)$ for all compatible models. Sharp bounds mean bounds attained, or approached when the boundary is not attained, by compatible models. Writing this set is a definition, not a solution: the substantive task is to establish informative restrictions and derive or estimate the set. An unrestricted-confounding model may give only trivial bounds.

Inference. Identification, estimability and valid uncertainty quantification are separate questions. Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$. A confidence procedure $C_n$ over a declared law class $\mathcal P$ has asymptotic uniform coverage at level $1-\alpha$ when \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P}\Pr_P\{\theta(P)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha. \] For set-identified targets the coverage event must be defined separately, for example coverage of the full identified set. If an entry uses identification-indexed models instead of uniquely indexed laws, the same coverage convention is applied over those models. Pointwise limits do not establish uniform validity, and asymptotic validity does not guarantee finite-sample precision. Sample sizes, dependence structures and weak-identification sequences are part of each application.

Counterfactuals. $Y_i(a)$ is a potential outcome under a specified intervention, including its timing, implementation and versions. It is not $Y_i$ conditional on voluntarily choosing $a$. A full intervention vector permits interference; shorthand scalar treatment notation does not silently impose no interference. Assignment, selection, attrition, anticipation and measurement must be specified. A claim about an instrument's local effect is not automatically a population policy effect.

Economic equilibrium. A counterfactual model includes best responses, constraints, feasibility, market clearing, state transitions and expectations consistent with the stated information. When several equilibria exist, either a declared selector is an input or the target is set-valued. Comparisons across policy regimes must use the stated selection convention. Reduced-form relationships are not presumed invariant after an equilibrium-changing intervention.

Optimization and welfare. Optimization is over the stated feasible set. If attainment is not established, $\sup$ or $\inf$ and $\varepsilon$-optimal choices replace an asserted maximizer or minimizer. For example, with value $V=\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}W(a)<\infty$, an $\varepsilon$-optimal set is $\{a\in\mathcal A:W(a)\geq V-\varepsilon\}$ for $\varepsilon>0$. Benefits, costs, risk and health or environmental outcomes can be aggregated only using declared units and conversion weights. Interpersonal utility weights, the treatment of fiscal transfers and foreign profits, discounting, and the behavioral welfare criterion are explicit inputs. A comparative-static derivative additionally requires existence and appropriate differentiability of the selected counterfactual.

Locators identify the relevant abstract, model, section or result at the level actually checked; a bibliographic or abstract-level verification is not represented as inspection of an entire proof. Working papers are distinguished from later publications and changing versions. Source summaries are paraphrased. All URL checks and editorial formulations refer to the audit date stated above.
