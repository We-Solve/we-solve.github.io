Math-1703

Top-degree W1 solvability and a finite-order open-mapping reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm P^n$ be a domain with nonempty smooth boundary, pseudoconvex in the original problem. Use a fixed smooth Hermitian metric, for example the Fubini--Study metric, to define form norms and Sobolev spaces. For $0\leq p\leq n$ and $1\leq q\leq n$, Mei-Chi Shaw asks whether every \[ f\in C^\infty_{(p,q)}(\overline\Omega),\qquad\overline\partial f=0, \] admits a $(p,q-1)$-form $u\in W^1(\Omega)$ satisfying $\overline\partial u=f$ in distributions. The barred operator, smoothness up to the boundary, and the exponent $\varepsilon$ below are reconstructed from the supplied OCR-verification information; the canonical extraction loses bars and replaces $\varepsilon$ with a control character.

The original comment says solutions are known in $L^2(\Omega)$ and in $W^\varepsilon(\Omega)$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ depending on $\Omega$, citing [CSW], and says the $W^1$ question was not known even with real-analytic boundary. These are supplied historical assertions, not a new assessment of the literature.

The aid reports an unconditional top-degree case: for every proper smoothly bounded domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm P^n$ and every $0\leq p\leq n$, there is $C=C(\Omega,p)>0$ such that every $f\in L^2_{(p,n)}(\Omega)$ has a primitive $u\in W^1_{(p,n-1)}(\Omega)$ with \[ \overline\partial u=f,\qquad\|u\|_{W^1}\leq C\|f\|_{L^2}. \] Pseudoconvexity and a separate closedness hypothesis on top-degree $f$ are unnecessary for this asserted endpoint result. The presence of a genuine boundary is retained; the statement is not extended to the entire compact projective space. The aid leaves the general pseudoconvex problem open in degrees $1\leq q\leq n-1$.

Its finite-order reduction concerns the smooth-data assertion exactly. Define the closed Fréchet space \[ Z^\infty_{p,q}(\overline\Omega)=\{f\in C^\infty_{(p,q)}(\overline\Omega):\overline\partial f=0\} \] with the standard $C^m$ seminorms up to the boundary. For each fixed $\Omega,p,q$, the assertion \[ \text{every }f\in Z^\infty_{p,q}(\overline\Omega)\text{ has a }W^1\text{ primitive} \] is reported equivalent to the existence of an integer $m\geq0$ and $C>0$ such that for every such $f$ one can choose a primitive satisfying \[ \|u\|_{W^1}\leq C\|f\|_{C^m(\overline\Omega)}. \] The finite-order norm may equivalently be a maximum of finitely many defining seminorms. Constants and $m$ may depend on the domain and bidegree. This is an existence estimate from an open-mapping argument, not an assertion of a continuous linear right inverse, and it does not yield an $L^2$-to-$W^1$ estimate in the unresolved degrees.

Short English statement

Ask for a one-derivative Sobolev solution of $\overline\partial u=f$ for smooth closed data on a pseudoconvex projective domain. The supplied aid isolates an unrestricted top-degree estimate and reformulates the remaining qualitative assertion as a finite-order smooth-data existence bound.
