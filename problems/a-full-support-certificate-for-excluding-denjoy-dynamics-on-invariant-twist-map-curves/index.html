Math-1629

A full-support certificate for excluding Denjoy dynamics on invariant twist-map curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $\mathbb T={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$. John Mather's question asks whether there exist a $C^3$ twist map $f:\mathbb T\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb T\times{\mathbb{R}}$ and an invariant circle $\Gamma$ such that $f|_\Gamma$ has irrational rotation number but is not topologically conjugate to a rigid rotation. A conjugacy means a homeomorphism $q:\Gamma\to\mathbb T$ with $q\circ f|_\Gamma\circ q^{-1}(\theta)=\theta+\alpha$ for some $\alpha\notin{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$. For an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism with lift $F:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$, its rotation number is $\lim_{n\to\infty}(F^n(x)-x)/n$ modulo ${\mathbb{Z}}$. The source recalls the existence of $C^1$ Denjoy examples on the circle and asks whether their behavior can occur on invariant curves of $C^3$ twist maps.

The source does not state its exact twist-map hypotheses or regularity of $\Gamma$. In the usual monotone-twist convention a lift $(\Theta,R)$ has $\partial_r\Theta>0$; area preservation, exactness, essentiality of the curve, and the sign convention must be checked against the intended setting. The aid's reduction applies to invariant graphs $\Gamma=\{(\theta,\gamma(\theta))\}$ with an induced orientation-preserving irrational circle homeomorphism $h$.

Its reported full-support criterion is \[ h\text{ is conjugate to }R_\alpha \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists\text{ invariant Borel probability }\mu \text{ with }\operatorname{supp}\mu=\mathbb T. \] Full support means every nonempty open arc has positive measure. Thus any counterexample in this graph setting must have a proper Cantor support $K$ for its invariant probability, and every component arc $I$ of $\mathbb T\setminus K$ is wandering: $h^n(I)$ are pairwise disjoint for distinct integers $n$. The whole invariant graph then strictly contains the measure support; in particular it cannot itself be the support of an invariant minimizing probability in the relevant variational twist-map setting.

The aid emphasizes two limitations. First, every irrational orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism is chain recurrent at every point, including Denjoy examples. Here chain recurrence means that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $x$ there is an $\varepsilon$-pseudo-orbit $x=x_0,\ldots,x_N=x$, $N\ge1$, satisfying $d(h(x_j),x_{j+1})<\varepsilon$. It therefore does not certify conjugacy. Second, a $C^2$ invariant graph, giving a $C^2$ induced circle diffeomorphism in this setting, is excluded as a counterexample by Denjoy's theorem. None of these reductions constructs a $C^3$ twist-map example or proves that none exists.

Short English statement

The question is whether a smooth ambient twist map can carry an irrational invariant circle with Denjoy dynamics. The supplied reduction says such a circle must contain wandering arcs outside its invariant measure's Cantor support. Full-support invariant measure excludes the pathology; chain recurrence alone does not.
