Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the usual group language, with multiplication, inversion, and the identity constant. A quasiidentity is a universally quantified formula \[ \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\;\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}u_i(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=v_i(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ u(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=v(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\right), \] where all displayed terms are group terms and the empty conjunction, when $m=0$, is true. For a group $G$, let $\operatorname{QId}(G)$ be the set of all quasiidentities valid in $G$.

A set $B\subseteq\operatorname{QId}(G)$ is a basis of this theory if, for every group $H$, \[ H\models B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\models\operatorname{QId}(G). \] The basis is independent if, for every $\varphi\in B$, there exists a group $H_\varphi$ such that \[ H_\varphi\models B\setminus\{\varphi\},\qquad H_\varphi\not\models\varphi. \] Thus consequence and independence are taken relative to the axioms of groups, with arbitrary groups as models.

Question (V. A. Gorbunov, 1984; Kourovka Notebook 9.6). Is it true that for every finite group $G$, every independent basis $B$ of $\operatorname{QId}(G)$ is finite?

This formulation reads "an independent basis" as quantifying over every independent basis that exists. It does not assert that every finite group has an independent basis. The supplied record does not explicitly distinguish this reading from a question about existence of a finite independent basis, and does not specify its conventions for logical consequence; these interpretations require source review.

Short English statement

For each finite group, must every independent collection of quasiidentities that axiomatizes all its quasiidentities be finite? Independence means that removing any member loses a consequence, with consequence evaluated among all groups. This reading does not assume that an independent basis exists.
