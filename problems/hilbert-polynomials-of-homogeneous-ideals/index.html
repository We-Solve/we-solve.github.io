Hilbert Polynomials of Homogeneous Ideals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question asks whether there exist a polynomial ring $S$ and a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq S$ such that the Hilbert polynomial of $S/I$ is \[ p_{S/I}(t)=t^2. \] The coefficient ring, grading, and number of variables are not specified in the supplied statement.

Under the conventional interpretation of a polynomial ring with its standard grading over a field, let $k$ be a field and put $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$, where $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$ and $\deg x_j=1$. An ideal $I\subseteq S$ is homogeneous if it is generated by homogeneous polynomials. Write $(S/I)_d$ for the degree-$d$ component and define the Hilbert function by \[ H_{S/I}(d)=\dim_k (S/I)_d,\qquad d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}. \] The Hilbert polynomial $p_{S/I}(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]$ is the unique polynomial satisfying $p_{S/I}(d)=H_{S/I}(d)$ for all sufficiently large integers $d$.

For a specified field $k$, the corresponding precise existence question is whether \[ \begin{gathered} \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\;\exists I\subseteq k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]\text{ homogeneous}\;\exists d_0\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\;\forall d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq d_0}:\\ \dim_k\left(k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/I\right)_d=d^2. \end{gathered} \] This equality is required only in sufficiently large degrees. The source does not specify whether $k$ is fixed, existentially quantified, or universally quantified, or whether it has additional hypotheses. Consequently, the displayed formulation is conditional on the stated grading and coefficient-field convention; these conventions require confirmation. The accompanying assessment discusses a broader numerical characterization where lex-segment analogues fail, but supplies no formulation of that broader target or its relationship to this existence question.

Short English statement

Ask whether a homogeneous quotient of a polynomial ring can have Hilbert polynomial $t^2$, meaning that its graded dimensions eventually equal the squares of the degrees under the standard field-based interpretation. The coefficient field, grading conventions, and relationship to the broader background discussion remain unresolved.
