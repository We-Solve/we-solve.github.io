Math-15669

Sharp Bounds for Alternating Involution Periods

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be an alphabet and let $A^*$ denote the set of finite words over $A$, including the empty word $\varepsilon$. The source does not specify whether $A$ is finite. A morphic involution is a map $\theta:A^*\to A^*$ satisfying \[ \theta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\qquad \theta(uv)=\theta(u)\theta(v),\qquad \theta(\theta(u))=u \quad (u,v\in A^*). \] For a word $w$, its length is denoted by $|w|$, and a contiguous factor is a word $u$ for which $w=xuy$ for some $x,y\in A^*$.

The supplied record describes $\tau_\theta(w)$ as the maximum length of a $\theta$-unbordered factor of $w$, and $\pi_\theta^{\mathrm{alt}}(w)$ as the shortest alternating $\theta$-period of $w$. However, it supplies neither a definition of a $\theta$-border nor a definition of an alternating $\theta$-period. Consequently these descriptions do not determine the two quantities precisely. In particular, the admissible factors and period lengths, the treatment of partial terminal blocks, and the conventions for empty words or empty sets of candidates remain unspecified.

Source problem, with unresolved terminology retained. Determine the sharp relation between $\tau_\theta(w)$ and $\pi_\theta^{\mathrm{alt}}(w)$ for a morphic involution $\theta$ on $A^*$, analogous to the ordinary relation between the maximum length of an unbordered factor and the shortest period. The source does not identify the ordinary relation intended as the analogy, state a proposed inequality or equality, or specify whether additional parameters such as $|w|$ are permitted. The intended universal scope over alphabets, involutions, and words also requires clarification. This is therefore a statement of the supplied research question with explicit gaps, rather than a fully specified conjecture.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a sharp comparison between the longest $\theta$-unbordered factor and the shortest alternating $\theta$-period of a finite word. The supplied record omits the definitions needed to make that comparison precise, including boundary conventions and the intended ordinary-period analogy.
