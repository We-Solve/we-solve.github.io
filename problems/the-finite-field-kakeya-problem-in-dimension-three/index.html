Math-1373

The finite-field Kakeya problem in dimension three

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q$ be any prime power and let ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ be the field of order $q$. A full affine line in ${\mathbb{F}}_q^3$ is $a+{\mathbb{F}}_q v=\{a+tv:t\in{\mathbb{F}}_q\}$, where $v\ne0$. Directions are one-dimensional linear subspaces, so scalar multiples of $v$ specify the same direction. A finite-field Besicovitch or Kakeya set is $E\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_q^3$ such that for every direction some full affine line in that direction is contained in $E$.

The damaged source question, attributed to T. Tao, is recovered by the aid as seeking a fixed $\varepsilon>0$ improving the historical Wolff exponent $5/2$: prove a lower bound on the scale $q^{5/2+\varepsilon}$ for every such $E$. The extraction prints "Wolf" and corrupts the final exponent. The literal coefficient-one version $|E|\geq q^{5/2+\varepsilon}$ should be understood with a sufficiently-large-field qualification if obtained by absorbing an absolute constant.

The supplied aid reports Dvir's polynomial-method solution with the full exponent three: \[ |E|\geq\binom{q+2}{3}\geq\frac{q^3}{6}. \] Consequently every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ gives the literal requested inequality for all sufficiently large prime powers $q$. Exponent three is optimal in scale because $|E|\leq q^3$. The aid separately describes a sharper result of Bukh and Chao as \[ |E|\geq\frac{q^3+q}{4}, \] and says that this asymptotically matches constructions. This is a supplied literature assessment, not an independent claim of a newly checked sharp result.

The aid also reports a partial-direction interpolation. Let $S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_q^2$, and suppose \[ \forall(u,v)\in S\ \exists a_{u,v}\in{\mathbb{F}}_q^3:\quad a_{u,v}+{\mathbb{F}}_q(u,v,1)\subseteq E. \] For every integer $m$ with $0\leq m\leq q-1$, it claims \[ |S|>mq\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |E|\geq\binom{m+3}{3}. \] Equivalently, for $S\ne\varnothing$, putting $r=\lceil|S|/q\rceil$ gives $|E|\geq\binom{r+2}{3}$. For $S=\varnothing$ the direction condition is vacuous and only the trivial lower bound zero is intended. The directions $(u,v,1)$ are precisely this normalized non-horizontal chart; no horizontal directions are required by the partial-direction claim. The strict inequality $|S|>mq$, integer range, and requirement of full lines are material.

Short English statement

The recovered question asks for a power improvement over the historical $q^{5/2}$ Kakeya bound in three dimensions. The aid reports Dvir's exponent-three solution, a sharper bound attributed to Bukh--Chao, and an additional exact bound when only some normalized directions are represented.
