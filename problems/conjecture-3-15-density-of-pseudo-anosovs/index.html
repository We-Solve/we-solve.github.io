Conjecture 3.15 — (Density of pseudo-Anosovs).

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=\operatorname{Mod}_g$ and $P\subseteq G$ be the pseudo-Anosov mapping classes, those admitting a representative with transverse invariant measured foliations expanded and contracted by factors $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$, $\lambda>1$. The printed conjecture is \[ d(P)=1, \] where the preceding definition of $d$ is missing.

If the intended density is uniform word-ball density for a fixed finite generating set $S$, define $B_S(R)=\{h:\|h\|_S\leq R\}$. In that interpretation the precise assertion is existence of the limit and \[ \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{|P\cap B_S(R)|}{|B_S(R)|}=1. \] This is an explicitly conditional interpretation: the source does not repeat the exhaustion, limit convention or generating-set quantifier.

The accompanying source distinguishes this conjecture from J. Maher's result that a suitable random walk lands in $P$ with probability tending to $1$, and I. Rivin's result for a particular sampling scheme via $\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})$. It expressly says that the conjecture does not appear to follow directly from those results. A statement $\mu^{*n}(P)\to1$ for a walk law is not automatically the displayed uniform-ball statement. The supplied research summary calls the problem solved in a generic/density sense conditional on its interpretation, but supplies no exact theorem identifying that sense with $d$. That discrepancy remains unresolved rather than being used to declare the printed conjecture solved.

Short English statement

Pseudo-Anosov mapping classes should have density one in the source's intended sense. Random-walk genericity and the possible uniform word-ball formulation must be distinguished.
