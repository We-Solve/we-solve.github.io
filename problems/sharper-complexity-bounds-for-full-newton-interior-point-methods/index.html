Literature and context check

Roos's primary report, printed pages 116--117, states an $O(n\log(M/\varepsilon))$ infeasible-method bound, where $M$ incorporates initial gap and residual sizes, together with initialization and bounded-optimum assumptions. It conjectures an improvement by $\sqrt n$. The source deliberately modifies the residual targets so that full steps approach feasibility gradually. The following analyzes a tempting direct Newton route and a strictly feasible central special case; neither is silently substituted for the source's modified infeasible algorithm.

Research attempt: Newton positivity and a feasible central-step estimate

Full raw Newton steps need not remain positive
Consider standard primal-dual feasibility for $A=(1,1)$, $b=1$, $c=(0,1)$, at \[ x=(1/2,1/2),\qquad y=-1,\qquad s=(1,2). \] This point is strictly positive and exactly primal and dual feasible. The raw Newton equations targeting componentwise complementarity $\mu'=1/12$ are \[ A\Delta x=0,\quad A^T\Delta y+\Delta s=0,\quad s\circ\Delta x+x\circ\Delta s=\mu'{\bf1}-x\circ s. \] They have \[ \Delta x=(1/6,-1/6),\qquad \Delta s=(-7/6,-7/6),\qquad \Delta y=7/6. \] For the two complementarity equations the left sides are $1/6-7/12=-5/12$ and $-1/3-7/12=-11/12$, equal to the required right sides. After a full step, $x^+=(2/3,1/3)$ but $s^+=(-1/6,5/6)$, so positivity is lost. This rejects the premise that full Newton steps are automatically safe at positive feasible points; it does not refute the carefully chosen steps and neighborhoods of the source method.

For any Newton step satisfying the displayed complementarity equation, expansion gives the exact remainder identity \[ (x+\Delta x)\circ(s+\Delta s)-\mu'{\bf1} =\Delta x\circ\Delta s. \tag{1} \] Thus feasibility of the linear equations alone does not control centrality or positivity after the step.

A square-root scale in a restricted central calculation
Now assume exact primal-dual feasibility and exact centrality $x_is_i=\mu>0$. Set $\mu'=(1-\theta)\mu$ with $0<\theta<1$ and use the feasible Newton equations. Define $u_i=\Delta x_i/x_i$ and $v_i=\Delta s_i/s_i$. Then \[ u+v=-\theta{\bf1},\qquad u\cdot v=\frac{\Delta x\cdot\Delta s}{\mu}=0, \] because $A\Delta x=0$ and $\Delta s=-A^T\Delta y$. Hence \[ \lVert u\rVert_2^2+\lVert v\rVert_2^2=n\theta^2, \qquad \max(\lVert u\rVert_\infty,\lVert v\rVert_\infty)\leq\sqrt n\theta. \] Therefore $\theta<1/\sqrt n$ ensures both updated vectors are positive. Also \[ \lVert u\circ v\rVert_2^2=\sum_i u_i^2v_i^2 \leq\left(\sum_i u_i^2\right)\left(\sum_i v_i^2\right), \] so $1$ yields \[ \frac{\lVert x^+\circ s^+-\mu'{\bf1}\rVert_2}{\mu'} \leq\frac{n\theta^2}{2(1-\theta)}. \] For example, $\theta=1/(4\sqrt n)$ gives positivity and relative centrality residual at most $1/24$, since $1-\theta\geq3/4$. Thus a full step with fractional parameter decrease of order $n^{-1/2}$ is rigorously safe in this exactly central feasible setting.
The first missing inference for the original algorithm
The new point is only approximately central. Repeating the same calculation without re-establishing a suitable invariant is invalid, because its hypotheses were exact centrality. For an infeasible iteration there is an additional obstruction: if $A\Delta x=r_p$ and $A^T\Delta y+\Delta s=r_d$, then \[ \Delta x\cdot\Delta s=\Delta x\cdot r_d-r_p\cdot\Delta y, \] which need not vanish or have a favorable sign. The orthogonality estimate producing the square-root scale can therefore not be transferred to the source's residual-reducing method without additional control. A proof of the required neighborhood invariance, residual coupling, initialization bounds and progress at every iteration is absent here. One favorable central step does not imply an $O(\sqrt n\log(M/\varepsilon))$ algorithm.

Verification and adversarial audit

The explicit counterexample satisfies both feasibility equations before and after the raw Newton step, and directly checks both linearized complementarity equations; only the needed positivity fails. The small-step bound checks the numerical constant $1/24$ and uses exact centrality explicitly. The dot-product formula for infeasible residuals identifies the precise lost orthogonality. No iteration bound is deduced from a single-step estimate.
