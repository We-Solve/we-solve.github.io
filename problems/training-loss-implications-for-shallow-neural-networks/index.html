Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A shallow scalar-output network conventionally has one hidden layer: $f(x)=b_0+\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\sigma(w_j^{\mathsf T}x+b_j)$ for a specified activation $\sigma$. Given training pairs $(x_i,y_i)$ and loss $\ell$, empirical training loss is $\widehat R(f)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f(x_i),y_i)$. Population prediction risk under a distribution $P$ is $R_P(f)=\mathbb E_{(X,Y)\sim P}[\ell(f(X),Y)]$; generalization concerns the gap $R_P(f)-\widehat R(f)$ or a bound on $R_P(f)$. Small training loss alone does not specify a guarantee without assumptions on the sample, network class, and target distribution.

A width-three network rules out a loss-only implication

The primary passage discusses implications of different training losses and possible equivalence to exact interpolation. Its extraction does not specify a population-risk theorem. We test one natural but overstrong reading: zero training loss should by itself bound prediction risk of a shallow network. Let $n\ge1$, let training inputs be independent uniform variables on $[0,1]$, and let every label be zero. Use squared loss and ReLU $\rho(z)=\max\{0,z\}$. Set $h=1/(2n)$ and, for any amplitude $A>0$, define the width-three network \[ f(x)=\frac{2A}{h}\big[\rho(x)-2\rho(x-h/2)+\rho(x-h)\big]. \]
Proposition: With probability at least $1/2$ its empirical squared loss is zero, while its population risk is exactly $A^2h/3$.
Proof: The network vanishes outside $[0,h]$, rises linearly from zero to $A$ on $[0,h/2]$, and falls linearly to zero on $[h/2,h]$. The event that all inputs avoid $[0,h]$ has probability $(1-h)^n\ge1-nh=1/2$; the inequality follows from a union bound on any input entering the interval. On that event all predictions at training points vanish. The population squared loss is its squared integral, namely \[ 2\int_0^{h/2}(2Ax/h)^2\,dx=A^2h/3. \] ∎
Choosing $A=\sqrt{3R/h}$ makes that risk any prescribed $R>0$ while keeping the same positive-probability zero-loss event. The construction uses only three hidden units but has unbounded weight scale. A bounded norm, Lipschitz constant, output range, or a particular algorithmic selection rule would change the question; none is supplied in the extracted implication. This is not a statement that a specified training algorithm must select this network.
