Finite central dimension of algebraic finitely generated division rings

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. An algebraic division ring is claimed to be generated as an algebra by two elements while having infinite dimension over its actual center.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
There is a division ring $D$, countable as a set, with $\operatorname{char}D=0$ and $Z(D)=F$, generated by two elements over its center, such that \[ D=F[x,y]=F(x,y),\qquad[D:F]=\infty,\qquad \forall z\in D\ \exists0\ne f_z\in F[t]:\ f_z(z)=0. \] Algebraicity of each element therefore coexists with infinite central dimension in this claimed finitely generated example. The notation $F[x,y]$ here means the unital algebra generated by $x,y$ over the centre $F$, permitting noncommutative words.

Outline of the argument

A countable schedule imposes algebraic relations on successive polynomial expressions. Formal-series lifting inside finite-dimensional division-algebra models retains two independent deformation directions; trace constraints and conjugation calculations preserve generation, and a separate descent argument prevents the final center from making the dimension finite.
