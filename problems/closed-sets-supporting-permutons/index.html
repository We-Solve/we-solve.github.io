Math-6550

Closed Sets Supporting Permutons

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X=[0,1]^2$ carry its usual topology and Borel $\sigma$-algebra, and let $\lambda$ denote Lebesgue probability measure on $[0,1]$. A permuton is a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on $X$ whose two coordinate marginals are both $\lambda$; explicitly, for every Borel set $A\subseteq[0,1]$, \[ \mu(A\times[0,1])=\lambda(A),\qquad \mu([0,1]\times A)=\lambda(A). \] Its topological support is \[ \operatorname{supp}(\mu)=\{z\in X:\mu(B_{{\mathbb{R}}^2}(z,r)\cap X)>0\text{ for every }r>0\}. \] Problem. Characterize all closed sets $S\subseteq X$ for which there exists a permuton $\mu$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{supp}(\mu)=S. \] Thus the requested measure must satisfy both $\mu(X\setminus S)=0$ and $\mu(B_{{\mathbb{R}}^2}(s,r)\cap X)>0$ for every $s\in S$ and every $r>0$.

This formulation follows the supplied clean statement, which asks for exact topological support in the square. The supplied original excerpt instead says a closed set in $[0,1]$, while its figure caption refers to subsets of the unit square. That dimensional discrepancy remains a source issue. The phrase "support a permuton" in the caption could also mean merely carrying a permuton, whereas exact support is explicitly required by the clean statement. The broader introductory discussion of arbitrary given real distributions is treated as motivation; the stated permuton problem requires uniform marginals.

Short English statement

Determine precisely which closed subsets of the unit square are the exact supports of probability measures having uniform marginals on both coordinates. The supplied clean statement specifies the square, although the original excerpt contains a conflicting interval reference.
