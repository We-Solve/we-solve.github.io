Question 4.7 — Forg,h≥ 2, does there exist a short exact sequence: 1→π1(Σg)→ Γ→π1(Σh)→ 1 for which Γ is a word hyperbolic group?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For integers $g,h\ge2$, let $\Sigma_g,\Sigma_h$ be the source's genus-$g$ and genus-$h$ surfaces, conventionally closed and oriented. Question 4.7 asks for a short exact sequence \[ 1\longrightarrow\pi_1(\Sigma_g)\longrightarrow\Gamma \longrightarrow\pi_1(\Sigma_h)\longrightarrow1 \] with $\Gamma$ word hyperbolic. Exactness means the first map is injective, the last is surjective, and its kernel is precisely the first image. Word hyperbolicity refers to uniformly thin geodesic triangles in a finite-generator Cayley graph. For fixed $g,h$ these are the requested groups; the source's wording should be checked for whether it asks for every pair or existence for some pair in that range.

The extension gives an outer action of the quotient surface group on the kernel surface group. The source, by an argument from its Theorem 4.2, says this produces a purely pseudo-Anosov surface subgroup of $\Gamma_g$. The notation $\Gamma_g$, orientation conventions and theorem hypotheses are not supplied, so that consequence is retained as a source report rather than independently proved or used to alter the exact-sequence request.

The first contextual comment states that such a $\Gamma$ cannot be the fundamental group of a closed complex-hyperbolic surface. It explains, through [20], [25] and [17], that an assumed realization $X=\mathbb H^2_{{\mathbb{C}}}/\Gamma$ would yield a nonsingular holomorphic fibration $X\to\Sigma_h$ inducing this exact sequence. The obstruction and precise hypotheses are not included. The second comment attributes a construction idea to Ian Agol and introduces a Thurston theorem in [40], but the theorem statement is absent and is not reconstructed.

The supplied summary links the problem to convex-cocompact monodromy and anticipates a positive construction, while reporting no established example of the requested word-hyperbolic surface-by-surface group. That expectation is not a theorem or a completed construction.

Short English statement

Can an extension of one closed genus-at-least-two surface group by another be word hyperbolic? The source connects this to purely pseudo-Anosov surface subgroups and reports a complex-hyperbolic obstruction, but the referenced proofs and Agol/Thurston construction idea are incomplete in the extract.
