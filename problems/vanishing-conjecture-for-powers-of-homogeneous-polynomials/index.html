Math-955

Vanishing Conjecture for Powers of Homogeneous Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each positive integer $n$, work in ${\mathbb{C}}[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ and define the constant-coefficient differential operator \[ \Delta=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\partial^2}{\partial X_i^2}. \] Let $P$ be homogeneous of degree four. The notation $\Delta^m$ means composition of $\Delta$ with itself $m$ times, while $P^m$ means ordinary multiplication of the polynomial. Every vanishing equality below is an identity in the polynomial ring.

The Vanishing Conjecture in the source asks whether \[ \left[\forall m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}:\quad\Delta^m(P^m)=0\right] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left[\exists M\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\ \forall m\ge M:\quad \Delta^m(P^{m+1})=0\right] \] for every such $P$ and every number of variables. The threshold $M$ may depend on $P$ and $n$; no uniform numerical bound is requested. The conclusion requires vanishing at every sufficiently large integer, not only along a subsequence. The hypothesis is the full collection of identities for all positive $m$, not merely harmonicity $\Delta P=0$. The same exponent $m$ applies to the differential operator in both clauses; only the polynomial power changes from $m$ to $m+1$.

Short English statement

For a homogeneous quartic over ${\mathbb{C}}$, does vanishing of every iterated-Laplacian expression $\Delta^m(P^m)$ force $\Delta^m(P^{m+1})$ to vanish for all sufficiently large $m$? The eventual threshold may depend on the polynomial.
