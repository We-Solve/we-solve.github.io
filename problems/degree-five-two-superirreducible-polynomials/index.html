Math-14061

Degree-Five Two-Superirreducible Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $R\in\{{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}\}$, write $\operatorname{Irr}_{R[x]}(h)$ if $h\in R[x]$ is nonzero, is not a unit, and every factorization $h=uv$ in $R[x]$ has a unit factor. The units of ${\mathbb{Z}}[x]$ are $\pm1$, whereas the units of ${\mathbb{Q}}[x]$ are all nonzero rational constants. Composition means $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$.

Define \[ \mathcal G_{\leq2}=\{ax^2+bx+c:a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},\qquad \mathcal G_{1,2}=\{ax^2+bx+c:a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ (a,b)\ne(0,0)\}. \] Thus $\mathcal G_{\leq2}$ includes constants and the zero polynomial, interpreting its degree bound by coefficient form; $\mathcal G_{1,2}$ consists exactly of the polynomials of degree one or two.

The supplied reconstructed question asks whether there exists $f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]$ of degree five whose composition with every integer polynomial of degree at most two is irreducible. With the coefficient-form interpretation above and a specified ring $R[x]$, its literal formulation is \[ \exists f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\quad \left(\deg f=5\ \land\ \forall g\in\mathcal G_{\leq2},\ \operatorname{Irr}_{R[x]}(f\circ g)\right). \] The original extracted question instead uses the term $2$-superirreducible, without supplying its definition. If that term is intended to quantify only over nonconstant substitutions, the corresponding candidate formulation is \[ \exists f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\quad \left(\deg f=5\ \land\ \forall g\in\mathcal G_{1,2},\ \operatorname{Irr}_{R[x]}(f\circ g)\right). \] These are conditional formulations, not an assertion that the source equates them. The intended substitution class, treatment of the zero polynomial, and irreducibility ring require clarification. In particular, constant substitutions produce constant compositions, whose irreducibility depends on the ring convention. The supplied evidence does not resolve these qualifications.

Short English statement

The question concerns a degree-five polynomial with integer coefficients whose compositions with all permitted integer polynomials of degree at most two remain irreducible. The supplied wording leaves unresolved whether substitutions must be nonconstant and whether irreducibility is taken in ${\mathbb{Z}}[x]$ or ${\mathbb{Q}}[x]$. A definitive statement of the intended $2$-superirreducibility problem requires these conventions.
