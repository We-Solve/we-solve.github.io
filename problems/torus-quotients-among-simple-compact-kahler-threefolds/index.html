Math-3542

Torus Quotients among Simple Compact Kähler Threefolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A complex threefold has complex dimension $3$. A normal complex space is ${\mathbb{Q}}$-factorial if every Weil divisor has a positive integral multiple that is Cartier. Terminal singularities mean that the canonical divisor $K_X$ is ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier and every exceptional prime divisor over $X$ has strictly positive discrepancy: on a resolution $f\colon Y\to X$, the coefficients of exceptional divisors in \[ K_Y-f^*K_X=\sum_E a(E,X)E \] are strictly positive.

Interpret a subvariety here as a closed complex analytic subvariety. The intended simplicity hypothesis is that there exist proper closed analytic subsets $Z_j\subsetneq X$, indexed by $j\in{\mathbb{N}}$, such that for every \[ x\in X\setminus\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}Z_j \] there is no irreducible closed analytic subvariety $V\subsetneq X$ with $x\in V$ and $1\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}V\leq2$. This specifies the convention for a very general point. The word proper is necessary: the supplied wording says only "positive-dimensional subvariety," which would otherwise include $X$ itself. This intended interpretation requires confirmation against the original source.

Question. Let $X$ be either a smooth compact Kähler threefold or a normal ${\mathbb{Q}}$-factorial compact Kähler threefold with at most terminal singularities. Assume the simplicity hypothesis just stated. Must there exist a complex torus \[ T={\mathbb{C}}^3/\Lambda, \] where $\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{C}}^3$ is a discrete cocompact lattice of rank $6$ over ${\mathbb{Z}}$, and a finite group $G$ acting holomorphically on $T$, such that \[ X\dashrightarrow T/G \] is bimeromorphic? Here $T/G$ is the complex analytic quotient, and bimeromorphic means that the spaces have isomorphic dense open subsets whose complements are proper closed analytic subsets. The action is not assumed to be free or to preserve the origin of $T$.

Supplied status qualification. The registry labels this problem partially solved. Its dated assessment reports a proved restricted terminal-threefold statement without divisors, including a finite cover by a torus, while reporting an acknowledged proof gap for the broader assertion about simple compact Kähler threefolds. The precise hypotheses and meaning of the restricted statement and its finite cover are not supplied, so that statement is not formulated here as a theorem. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every simple compact Kähler threefold in either of the stated smooth or terminal ${\mathbb{Q}}$-factorial classes is bimeromorphic to a finite holomorphic quotient of a three-dimensional complex torus. Simplicity is interpreted as excluding positive-dimensional proper analytic subvarieties through a very general point. The supplied partial-solution assessment and its reported proof gap remain qualifications requiring review.
