Definitions and notation.
On $\ell^2(\mathbb Z^\nu)$, the source defines $(Hu)(n)=\sum_{|j|=1}u(n+j)+V_\omega(n)u(n)$, where the site potentials are i.i.d. uniform on $[a,b]$. The matrix element $e^{itH}(n,0)$ is $\langle\delta_n,e^{itH}\delta_0\rangle$, and $n^2$ means $|n|^2$. Thus $M_2(t)=\sum_n|n|^2|e^{itH}(n,0)|^2$ is the second moment of position for a particle initially at the origin. The target is diffusive $M_2(t)\sim ct$, conditional on an absolutely continuous regime.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the Anderson model in dimension $\nu\geq3$ and disorder strengths $|b-a|$ admitting absolutely continuous spectrum, prove that $\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^\nu} n^2|e^{itH}(n,0)|^2$ grows asymptotically like $ct$ as $t\to\infty$.
Scope and source qualification.
The statement is conditional on a regime with absolutely continuous spectrum, whose existence is a related problem. The catalogue does not specify whether the asymptotic is almost sure, averaged over disorder, or energy-restricted; that ambiguity is retained rather than replaced by one stronger assertion.

Short English statement

Show that a particle in the conducting regime of the Anderson model spreads diffusively.

The zero-disorder obstruction.
Take the endpoint case $a=b=v_0$, a deterministic potential. On the Fourier torus $[-\pi,\pi]^\nu$, the Hamiltonian is multiplication by $h(k)=v_0+2\sum_{j=1}^\nu\cos k_j$, and the initial vector $\delta_0$ transforms to the constant function one with normalized Haar measure. Position multiplication corresponds to differentiation in $k$. Therefore Parseval's identity gives the exact formula \[M_2(t)=\sum_j\int\left|\partial_{k_j}e^{ith(k)}\right|^2\frac{dk}{(2\pi)^\nu}=4t^2\sum_j\int\sin^2k_j\frac{dk}{(2\pi)^\nu}=2\nu t^2.\] The spectral measures in this model are absolutely continuous: a single coordinate variable $2\cos k_1$ has density $1/(\pi\sqrt{4-E^2})$ on $(-2,2)$, and adding the other independent cosine variables convolves this density with probability measures. Weighted Fourier spectral measures are also absolutely continuous, by absolute continuity of their measure with respect to Haar measure and its energy pushforward. Thus absolutely continuous spectrum alone does not force diffusion.
A robust upper bound for nonzero disorder.
For any bounded diagonal potential, the commutator $[X_j,H]$ is a difference of two unit shifts and has norm at most two. Integrating the Heisenberg equation on $\delta_0$, whose position is zero, gives $\|X_je^{itH}\delta_0\|\le2|t|$, and hence $M_2(t)\le4\nu t^2$. Finite-range hopping justifies this on truncated position operators followed by the usual monotone moment limit; alternatively the power series of the bounded Hamiltonian provides finite moments at each fixed time and the same integrated commutator identity.
Remaining target.
If the intended disorder is strictly positive, the zero-disorder calculation is a boundary countercheck, not a disproof of that intended assertion. If zero is literally included, it excludes an unrestricted conditional formulation. Neither calculation gives the linear leading term for positive random disorder. The first missing step is a quantitative long-time decorrelation estimate for the velocity, with the required disorder-averaged, almost-sure or energy-restricted meaning fixed. Those meanings are not chosen silently here.
