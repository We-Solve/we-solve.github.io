Math-1261

Seeing a primitive surface class in the generic Jacobian

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's comment asks to make a surface over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ into a curve over ${\mathbb{F}}_q(T)$ by a Lefschetz pencil and to "see" an expected second Tate class beyond the hyperplane class, or the corresponding Jacobian point. For a smooth projective surface, a Tate class is understood in $H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(S_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_q},{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(1))$, $\ell\nmid q$, fixed by Frobenius or by a specified power of it. Those two field-of-definition conventions differ. The source's "typically" two-class/parity remark is not an assumption that every surface has even second Betti number or exactly two classes; an arbitrary extra algebraic class is not supplied.

For the attached aid's conditional construction, take a smooth projective surface $S/{\mathbb{F}}_q$, an ample pencil class $H$, and a blowup $\pi:\widetilde S\to S$ resolving a Lefschetz pencil $\widetilde S\to{\mathbb{P}}^1$, with smooth generic curve $C/K$, $K={\mathbb{F}}_q(T)$, and Jacobian $J_C$. A divisor class $D_0$ is $H$-primitive if $D_0\cdot H=0$, so restriction gives a degree-zero class on $C$. The aid reports that this point is torsion exactly when $\pi^*D_0$ lies in the rational span of geometric fiber-component classes. In particular, when all geometric fibers are integral, every nonzero primitive Néron--Severi class restricts to a non-torsion point, under the pencil hypotheses. If a divisor is defined only after a finite constant-field extension, its resulting point is over that extension of $K$, not automatically over $K$.

For a divisor $D$ defined over the chosen ground field, put \[ D_0=(H^2)D-(D\cdot H)H. \] The aid's numerical certificate is \[ H^2D^2-(D\cdot H)^2<0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad [\mathcal O_C(D_0)]\text{ non-torsion}, \] assuming the integral-geometric-fiber condition. This certifies a point from an already available divisor; it does not construct an unknown Tate class on every surface.

Its explicit specialization is a smooth quartic K3 surface $S\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3$ containing a line $L$, with a suitable plane pencil of axis $R$ skew to $L$. Set $p_t=L\cap\Pi_t$ and $B=S\cap R$, viewed on the generic plane quartic. The aid reports \[ P=[4p_t-B]\in J_C(K) \] as non-torsion when its pencil hypotheses hold. Here $\deg B=4$, $H^2=4$, $L\cdot H=1$, $L^2=-2$, and the certificate is $-9<0$. Ground-field definition of the line/pencil and geometric integrality of every fiber remain required. The source's intended general-surface problem remains unresolved by this conditional example.

Short English statement

Construct the expected extra class or Jacobian point arising from a surface Lefschetz pencil. The attached aid reports a torsion criterion for restrictions of primitive divisor classes and a conditional explicit non-torsion point on a quartic K3 pencil, without providing a new class for every surface.
