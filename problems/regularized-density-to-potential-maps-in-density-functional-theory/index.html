A complete scalar regularization test.
To test the inference from a regularized derivative to a unique physical potential, use the convex function $F(x)=|x|$ on $\mathbb R$. Minimizing $|y|+(x-y)^2/(2\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon>0$, separately on the positive and negative half-lines gives \[ y_\varepsilon(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)(|x|-\varepsilon)_+, \qquad F_\varepsilon(x)=\begin{cases}x^2/(2\varepsilon),&|x|\le\varepsilon,\\|x|-\varepsilon/2,&|x|\ge\varepsilon.\end{cases} \] The two values and their first derivatives agree at $\pm\varepsilon$; hence $F_\varepsilon'$ is the clipped function $x/\varepsilon$ in the central interval and $\operatorname{sgn}(x)$ outside. It is unique everywhere and Lipschitz with constant $1/\varepsilon$.
What this does not identify.
At $x=0$, the unregularized variational problem $\min_x(|x|+vx)$ has zero as a minimizer for every $v\in[-1,1]$, since $|x|+vx\ge(1-|v|)|x|$. The regularized derivative chooses zero there but does not prove uniqueness of an unregularized potential. At the endpoints $v=\pm1$, even the minimizing density is nonunique along a half-line.
