The Lyapin's Notebook — Problem 31

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A semigroup is a nonempty set $S$ with an associative binary operation. It is finite if $|S|<\infty$, and regular if \[ \forall a\in S\;\exists b\in S\qquad aba=a. \] Write $E(S)=\{e\in S:e^2=e\}$ for its set of idempotents. A regular semigroup is orthodox if $E(S)$ is closed under multiplication. A band, or idempotent semigroup, is a semigroup satisfying $x^2=x$. A variety of bands is a class of bands defined by semigroup identities.

Source Problem 31 (S. I. Kublanovsky). Consider the class \[ \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{fin}} =\{S:S\text{ is a finite regular orthodox semigroup}\}. \] Is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{fin}}$ completely solvable?

For a given variety $\mathcal V$ of bands, consider also \[ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal V} =\{S:S\text{ is a finite regular semigroup},\ E(S)\text{ is closed under multiplication},\ E(S)\in\mathcal V\}, \] where $E(S)$ carries the multiplication inherited from $S$. Is $\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal V}$ completely solvable?

Short English statement

Problem 31, attributed to S. I. Kublanovsky, asks whether finite regular orthodox semigroups are completely solvable, and asks the same question when their idempotent subsemigroups lie in a given variety of bands. The meaning of "completely solvable" is absent from the supplied record, so the intended target requires clarification from the source.
