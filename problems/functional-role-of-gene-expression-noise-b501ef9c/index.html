Statement

Functional role of gene-expression noise

Domain. Gene-expression noise and fitness.   Retained status/source context. Open causal noise--fitness mapping programme. Adaptive stochastic switching has experimental support in particular environments; optimizing a known finite switching model is ordinary analysis, while biological calibration and transfer remain research targets.

Definitions and assumptions.
Choose a microbial gene circuit and two controlled environmental states $E_t\in\{1,2\}$ with known switching rates $q_{12},q_{21}$. Cells occupy expression states $i$ with equalized overall mean expression across engineered noise variants, but specified variance and autocorrelation. Let $K_\sigma$ be the measured expression-switching generator and $r_i(e)$ state-dependent growth rate; perturbations changing noise also have measured metabolic cost. Population counts satisfy a branching or mean growth model $\dot n=(K_\sigma^T+\operatorname{diag}r(E_t))n$ in a prescribed environment protocol.
Problem statement.
Estimate causal fitness differences and predict an optimal feasible noise policy: \[ \Lambda(\sigma)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\log\frac{\sum_i n_i(T)}{\sum_i n_i(0)}, \qquad \sigma^*\in\arg\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\{\Lambda(\sigma)-c(\sigma)\}. \] Here $\Sigma$ is a realizable circuit-variant set and $c$ measured growth-equivalent cost. Validate ranking in withheld fluctuation schedules. Explain when extra expression variability improves long-run growth at comparable mean expression.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
State whether all cells share one stationary ergodic environmental path or experience independent environments. Under the declared boundedness and irreducibility assumptions, define $\Lambda$ as the almost-sure top growth exponent of that random linear system; otherwise use finite-horizon growth. Charge the circuit cost once: subtract $c(\sigma)$ only when it is not already included in $r_i(e)$.

Alternative formulations.
(i) In constant environments, compare noise-induced growth penalties or benefits under measured nonlinear growth response. (ii) In fluctuating environments, vary switching time and noise autocorrelation to test bet hedging. Equal variance with different correlation time can produce different fitness, so variance alone is not a complete noise descriptor.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Primary experiments show switching-rate-dependent survival advantages under particular environmental schedules. Noise manipulations can change burden, mean expression or response kinetics; these must be controlled before claiming a causal noise effect. Rare growth episodes and finite experiments complicate long-run rate estimates. The target is a calibrated environmental mechanism, not a universal optimum noise level.

Attempted answer

Proposition: Two expression states switching symmetrically at rate $k$ with fixed growth rates $r_1,r_2$ have population growth $\Lambda=(r_1+r_2-2k+\sqrt{(r_1-r_2)^2+4k^2})/2$. It decreases with $k$ when $r_1\ne r_2$, although isolated stationary expression weights remain equal.
Proof: Compute the larger eigenvalue of the population matrix with diagonals $r_i-k$ and off-diagonals $k$. Its derivative is $-1+2k/\sqrt{(r_1-r_2)^2+4k^2}<0$. ∎

What remains unresolved

This constant-environment calculation does not optimize bet hedging, costs or fluctuating schedules.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
