Math-3462

Decomposability Thresholds for Simplicial Polytopes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A convex polytope is the convex hull of a finite set of points in a real Euclidean space; its dimension is the dimension of its affine hull. A simplicial polytope is a convex polytope whose proper faces are simplices. For a simplicial polytope $P$, let $\partial P$ denote its boundary complex, consisting of the vertex sets of its proper faces, including the empty face. Decomposability of $P$ is interpreted below as decomposability of $\partial P$.

A finite abstract simplicial complex $\Delta$ is a family of subsets of a finite vertex set, containing the empty set and closed under taking subsets. Its facets are its inclusion-maximal faces. It is pure of dimension $r$ if every facet has cardinality $r+1$. A simplex is the complex of all subsets of a finite set; the simplex on the empty set has dimension $-1$. For a nonempty face $\tau\in\Delta$, define \[ \Delta\setminus\tau=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\tau\nsubseteq\sigma\},\qquad \operatorname{lk}_{\Delta}(\tau)=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\cap\tau=\varnothing,\ \sigma\cup\tau\in\Delta\}. \] Here deletion removes all faces containing $\tau$, and $\dim\tau=|\tau|-1$.

Use the following conventional pure-complex definitions, which are not supplied explicitly in the source record and require confirmation against the cited report. For an integer $k\ge0$, a pure $r$-dimensional complex $\Delta$ is weakly $k$-decomposable if it is a simplex, or it has a nonempty face $\tau$ with $\dim\tau\le k$ such that $\Delta\setminus\tau$ is pure of dimension $r$ and weakly $k$-decomposable. It is $k$-decomposable if it is a simplex, or it has such a face $\tau$ for which $\Delta\setminus\tau$ is pure of dimension $r$ and $k$-decomposable, and $\operatorname{lk}_{\Delta}(\tau)$ is pure of dimension $r-|\tau|$ and $k$-decomposable. These definitions are recursive on the number of faces. To interpret the predecessor condition when $k=0$, adopt the explicit convention that a complex is weakly $(-1)$-decomposable, or $(-1)$-decomposable, exactly when it is a simplex.

Let $\mathcal P_d$ be the class of all simplicial convex polytopes of dimension $d$, with no restriction on their number of vertices. On the usual positive-dimensional interpretation $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}$, determine separately the two thresholds \[ \begin{aligned} k_{\mathrm w}(d)&=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}:\ \forall P\in\mathcal P_d,\ \partial P\text{ is weakly }k\text{-decomposable}\},\\ k_{\mathrm s}(d)&=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}:\ \forall P\in\mathcal P_d,\ \partial P\text{ is }k\text{-decomposable}\}. \end{aligned} \] The requested sharpness conditions are, respectively, \[ \begin{aligned} &\exists P_{\mathrm w}\in\mathcal P_d:\ \partial P_{\mathrm w}\text{ is not weakly }(k_{\mathrm w}(d)-1)\text{-decomposable},\\ &\exists P_{\mathrm s}\in\mathcal P_d:\ \partial P_{\mathrm s}\text{ is not }(k_{\mathrm s}(d)-1)\text{-decomposable}. \end{aligned} \] Each minimum is understood only when its defining set is nonempty; no existence proof or value is asserted here. The source does not explicitly specify the range of $d$, the allowed range of $k$, the negative-index convention, or the recursive definitions. Consequently, the conventions above make the intended formulation explicit but remain subject to source review, particularly if dimension zero is intended.

Short English statement

For each fixed dimension $d$, find separately the smallest decomposition parameter that works for the boundary of every simplicial convex $d$-polytope in the weak and ordinary senses. In each case, require a polytope for which the preceding parameter fails. The dimensional and recursive conventions stated above require confirmation against the source.
