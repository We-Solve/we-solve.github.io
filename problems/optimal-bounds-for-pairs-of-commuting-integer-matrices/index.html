Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

$M_n(\mathbb Z)$ is the set of $n\times n$ integer matrices, and $XY=YX$ means the two products agree. The notation $\ll_\varepsilon$ bounds the left side by a constant times the right side, with allowed dependence stated by the subscript.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For $X\in M_n(\mathbb{Z})$, let $|X|$ be the maximum absolute value of its entries, and define $N(T)=\#\{(X,Y)\in M_n(\mathbb{Z})^2:|X|\leq T,\ |Y|\leq T,\ XY=YX\}$. Prove that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $N(T)\ll_\varepsilon T^{n^2+1+\varepsilon}$ for arbitrary $n$. [S2]

Literature and context check

The primary workshop report, printed pp. 2780--2781, states the general counting conjecture and explicitly says it is known for $n\leq3$. Thus the following $n=2$ proof reconstructs known special-case mathematics. The report's finite-field dimension comparison does not itself give an integer height bound. No recent-status claim beyond this consulted report is made.

Research attempt: Primitive-direction counting in dimension two

Put $B=\lfloor T\rfloor\geq1$. For every $n$, taking $X=aI_n$ and arbitrary $Y$ gives exactly $(2B+1)^{n^2+1}$ pairs in the box. Hence the conjectured exponent cannot be reduced.

For $n=2$, write \[ X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad Y=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\g&h\end{pmatrix},\qquad v=(a-d,b,c),\quad w=(e-h,f,g). \] Direct multiplication shows that $XY=YX$ if and only if \[ bg-cf=0,\quad (a-d)f-b(e-h)=0,\quad c(e-h)-(a-d)g=0. \] These are exactly the three two-coordinate minors of $v,w$, up to sign. If $v=0$, $X$ is scalar and this contributes $(2B+1)^5$ pairs.

If $v\ne0$, choose the unique primitive integer vector $u$ on its rational line whose first nonzero coordinate is positive. Then $v=ku$, $k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$. Vanishing minors give $w=\ell u$ with rational $\ell$. Primitivity supplies integers $z_i$ with $\sum z_i u_i=1$, so $\ell=\sum z_iw_i$ is an integer. Let $H=\|u\|_\infty$. Every coordinate of $v,w$ has absolute value at most $2B$, so \[ 1\leq H\leq2B,\qquad 0<|k|\leq2B/H,\qquad |\ell|\leq2B/H. \] The number of possible $u$ of height $H$ is at most the number of all integer vectors on that cube shell, \[ (2H+1)^3-(2H-1)^3=24H^2+2. \] There are at most $(4B/H)(4B/H+1)\leq24B^2/H^2$ pairs $(k,\ell)$, since $H\leq2B$. Once $v,w$ are fixed, choosing $a,e$ determines all other entries, so there are at most $(2B+1)^2$ matrices realizing them. Ignoring incompatible entry bounds only increases this upper count. Consequently \[ N_2(T)\leq (2B+1)^5+(2B+1)^2 \sum_{H=1}^{2B}(24H^2+2)\frac{24B^2}{H^2}=O(B^5). \] Together with the scalar lower bound, this proves $N_2(T)=\Theta(T^5)$ for $T\geq1$.

Where the attempted extension stops.
The essential reduction above was that the nonscalar centralizer modulo scalar matrices has just one rational direction. For a diagonal matrix with $n$ distinct diagonal entries, commutation forces an arbitrary diagonal matrix, whose quotient by scalars has dimension $n-1$. For $n\geq3$ that quotient has more than one direction. A naive replacement by $n-1$ independent height-bounded parameters does not retain the shell cancellation just proved; rational lattice covolumes and degenerations must be controlled. This precise missing estimate is not supplied by dimension counting.

Verification and adversarial audit

All three commutator equations were expanded, including the case $w=0$ and the distinction between rational proportionality and integer coefficients. The scalar stratum is counted separately so the primitive vector is never applied to zero. Bounds allow overcounting and hence require no converse realization. $n=1$ is immediate: all pairs commute and the count is $(2B+1)^2$. No claim is made that the failed single-direction argument rules out a different proof for larger $n$.
Audit corrections.
Counted zero and nonzero first vectors separately; justified integrality by primitivity rather than assuming rational proportionality has integer ratio.
