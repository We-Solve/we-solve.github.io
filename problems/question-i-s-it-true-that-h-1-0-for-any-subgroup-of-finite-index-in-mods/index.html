Question I — s it true that H 1(Γ) = 0 for any subgroup Γ of finite index in ModS?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the surface $S$ intended by the source, ask whether \[ \forall\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Mod}_S, \quad[\operatorname{Mod}_S:\Gamma]<\infty \ \Longrightarrow\ H^1(\Gamma)=0. \] The coefficient group is not printed. In the usual characteristic-zero, trivial-coefficient interpretation, this is $H^1(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Q}})=0$, equivalently vanishing first Betti number. For finitely generated groups it means finite abelianization, not necessarily zero integral first homology.

The source supplies genus-two counterexamples, so the question cannot literally assert this for every surface type. It reports F. Taherkhani's 1999 MSU thesis computations giving finite-index genus-two subgroups with nonzero first cohomology, whereas all examined genus-three subgroups had zero first cohomology; it describes higher-genus calculations as beyond the resources then available. Those finite computations are not a proof for all genus-three subgroups. The intended remaining closed-surface range is suggested to be genus at least $3$, but is not explicit in the first sentence.

It also reports J. D. McCarthy [McC2]: if $S$ is closed of genus $g\geq3$, and a finite-index subgroup $\Gamma$ contains the Torelli group, then $H^1(\Gamma)=0$. The Torelli group is the kernel of the action on integral first homology. The stated ingredients are Johnson's Torelli results, the congruence subgroup problem for $\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})$, and its Kazhdan property (T), in the printed range $g\geq3$. This restricted result is not extended to subgroups that do not contain Torelli. The source also says $H^1(\operatorname{Mod}_S)=0$, subject to the same unstated surface/coefficient context.

Short English statement

Must every finite-index mapping-class subgroup in the intended higher-genus setting have vanishing first Betti number? Keep the genus-two counterexamples, limited genus-three computations and Torelli-containing theorem separate.
