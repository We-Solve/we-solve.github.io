Moment Inequalities from Positive Semidefinite Localization Matrices

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a standard real-polynomial formulation, let $n\geq 1$, let ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, and write $x^\alpha=\prod_{i=1}^n x_i^{\alpha_i}$ and $|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i$ for $\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_0^n$. If $\mu$ is a finite positive Borel measure on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ with all absolute monomial moments finite, define \[ y_\alpha=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^n}x^\alpha\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(x). \] This supplies a conventional setting for the question; the source does not specify a dimension, moment order, measure normalization, or integrability convention. In particular, the condition $y_0=1$ is not imposed.

For any formal real sequence $y=(y_\alpha)_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_0^n}$, define the linear functional $L_y$ on ${\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ by $L_y(x^\alpha)=y_\alpha$. For $r\in{\mathbb{N}}_0$, its moment matrix is \[ M_r(y)=\left(y_{\alpha+\beta}\right)_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq r}. \] For a polynomial $g(x)=\sum_\gamma g_\gamma x^\gamma$, its localization matrix is \[ M_r(g y)=\left(\sum_\gamma g_\gamma y_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}\right)_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq r}. \] Thus $M_r(y)\succeq0$ is equivalent to $L_y(p^2)\geq0$ for every real polynomial $p$ of degree at most $r$, and $M_r(g y)\succeq0$ is equivalent to $L_y(gp^2)\geq0$ for every such $p$.

Given $m\in{\mathbb{N}}_0$ and real polynomials $g_1,\ldots,g_m$, let \[ K=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:g_j(x)\geq0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq m\}. \] A measure concentrated on $K$, meaning $\mu({\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus K)=0$, satisfies \[ M_r(y)\succeq0,\qquad M_r(g_jy)\succeq0 \quad(r\in{\mathbb{N}}_0,\ 1\leq j\leq m). \] Here a sum of squares is a finite sum of squares of real polynomials. At all orders, these matrix conditions are equivalent to \[ L_y\!\left(\sigma_0+\sum_{j=1}^m g_j\sigma_j\right)\geq0 \] for every choice of sums of squares $\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_m$. At a fixed order, the corresponding equivalence uses only squares whose degrees fit the chosen matrix indices. For truncated data, each matrix is defined only when all moments appearing in its entries are supplied. No compactness or Archimedean hypothesis is supplied, and no converse asserting the existence of a representing measure is part of this formulation.

Problem. Characterize the inequalities among moments that can be deduced from positive semidefiniteness of moment matrices. More generally, for measures concentrated on $K$, characterize the additional inequalities deducible from positive semidefiniteness of the localization matrices associated with its defining polynomials.

One precise conditional interpretation is the following: after specifying a family $\mathcal C$ of the matrix constraints above and an admissible class of expressions $F$ depending on a finite index set $A\subset{\mathbb{N}}_0^n$, characterize those $F$ for which \[ \forall y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathbb{N}}_0^n},\qquad \left[y\text{ satisfies every constraint in }\mathcal C\right] \Longrightarrow F\left((y_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}\right)\geq0. \] The supplied record does not specify the admissible expressions, the matrix orders, the meaning of deduction, or whether this quantifier should range over formal sequences or only realizable moment sequences. Consequently, this is an explicit formulation scheme requiring those qualifications to be resolved, rather than a uniquely determined classification problem.

Short English statement

Determine which inequalities between moments are forced by positive semidefinite moment matrices, and which further inequalities follow from localization matrices encoding semialgebraic support constraints. The source leaves the class of inequalities, matrix orders, normalization, and meaning of deduction unspecified; these choices require mathematical review.
