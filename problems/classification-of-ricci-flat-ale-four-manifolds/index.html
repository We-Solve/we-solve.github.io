Math-780

Classification of Ricci-Flat ALE Four-Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For this draft, a Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold means a connected, complete, smooth, noncompact Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ of real dimension $4$, without boundary, with $\operatorname{Ric}_g=0$, satisfying the following conventional asymptotically locally Euclidean condition. There exist a compact set $K\subset M$, a number $R>0$, a finite subgroup $\Gamma\subset O(4)$ acting freely on $S^3$, and a diffeomorphism \[ \Phi:({\mathbb{R}}^4\setminus\overline{B_R(0)})/\Gamma\longrightarrow M\setminus K \] such that, after lifting $\Phi^*g$ to ${\mathbb{R}}^4\setminus\overline{B_R(0)}$, there is some $\tau>0$ for which \[ \partial^\alpha\left((\Phi^*g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\right) =O\left(r^{-\tau-|\alpha|}\right) \qquad(r\to\infty) \] for every multi-index $\alpha$. Here $r$ is the Euclidean radius and $\delta$ is the Euclidean metric. Completeness, the end convention, the decay and differentiability requirements, and the allowed group at infinity are conventional choices made explicit here; the supplied problem does not specify them or explicitly impose orientability.

Let $\mathcal K$ denote the family referred to in the source as Kronheimer gravitational instantons, and let $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{source}}$ denote the family referred to there as their known finite quotients. A smooth finite Riemannian quotient $X/G$ means a quotient by a finite group $G$ acting freely by isometries, equipped with the induced metric. The source does not enumerate $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{source}}$, specify the exact scope of $\mathcal K$, or say whether every smooth finite quotient is intended.

Source question, with the unresolved family qualification retained. Does there exist a Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold $(M,g)$ such that \[ (M,g)\not\cong X \qquad\text{for every }X\in\mathcal K\cup\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{source}}, \] where $\cong$ denotes Riemannian isometry? This expresses the supplied existence question, but it does not define a fully enumerated exclusion class: the phrase "known finite quotients" requires clarification from the source before the target can be made completely precise. The treatment of flat examples and the intended equivalence relation also require confirmation.

Related question in the supplied dated curation. Must every simply connected Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold be hyperkähler? Here hyperkähler means that $M$ admits three parallel, metric-compatible complex structures $I,J,L$ satisfying \[ I^2=J^2=L^2=-\operatorname{id},\qquad IJ=L=-JI. \] The simply connected hypothesis belongs to this related formulation; it is not imposed in the main source question. The supplied evidence does not establish equivalence between these two formulations. The curation describes Kronheimer's classification as covering hyperkähler ALE four-manifolds and mentions results under additional hypotheses, without specifying those hypotheses. Those assertions are retained as supplied background, not as independently verified classification statements.

Short English statement

The source asks whether a complete Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold can exist outside Kronheimer's gravitational instantons and their known finite quotients. The excluded quotient family and the exact ALE conventions are not specified. The supplied curation also asks whether every simply connected example is hyperkähler; this related question is not asserted to be equivalent to the original one. The supplied open status requires independent review.
