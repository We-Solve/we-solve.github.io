Math-15531

Optimality and Practical Efficiency of Proof Search

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal F$ denote the set of finite propositional formulas over Boolean variables, and let \[ \mathrm{TAUT}=\{\varphi\in\mathcal F: v(\varphi)=1\text{ for every Boolean valuation }v\}. \] For terminology, fix an encoding of formulas and finite proof strings. A sound and complete propositional proof relation $R_P(\varphi,\pi)$ satisfies \[ \forall\varphi\in\mathcal F,\qquad \varphi\in\mathrm{TAUT}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists\pi\;R_P(\varphi,\pi). \] Here $\pi$ ranges over finite strings. This supplies terminology only: the record does not specify an encoding, an effective verification requirement, or the class of admissible proof systems. For $\varphi\in\mathrm{TAUT}$, define its shortest proof length in $P$ by \[ \ell_P(\varphi)=\min\{\lvert\pi\rvert:R_P(\varphi,\pi)\}. \] A proof-search algorithm for $P$, on every input $\varphi\in\mathrm{TAUT}$, must terminate and output a string $\pi$ satisfying $R_P(\varphi,\pi)$. This convention imposes no behavior on inputs outside $\mathrm{TAUT}$; whether the intended task instead requires deciding tautologicity, deciding satisfiability, or producing refutations is unresolved.

The supplied problem asks:

  1. How should proof-search algorithms be compared, and is there an optimal way to search for propositional proofs?
  2. Why is searching in stronger proof systems apparently unhelpful?
  3. How can practical satisfiability and automated-theorem-proving algorithms perform well on very long formulas despite exponential lower bounds for associated proof systems?

The record supplies a research agenda rather than a uniquely quantified conjecture. In particular, if an admissible class $\mathcal A$ of algorithms and a comparison preorder $\preceq$ were specified, with $A\preceq B$ meaning that $A$ is at least as efficient as $B$, an optimality question could take the form \[ \exists A_*\in\mathcal A\;\forall A\in\mathcal A,\qquad A_*\preceq A. \] Neither $\mathcal A$ nor $\preceq$ is supplied, so this is a conditional template, not an attributed formal conjecture. It remains unspecified whether the comparison fixes one proof system or ranges across systems, and whether efficiency is measured in input length, output length, shortest proof length, or another parameter.

The meaning of stronger proof systems, the sense in which their search is apparently unhelpful, the relevant exponential lower bounds and formula families, and the practical performance criterion are also unspecified. The record does not identify whether automated theorem proving here is restricted to propositional logic. These qualifications prevent replacing the questions by a single precise theorem or conjecture without additional source evidence.

Short English statement

The problem asks how to compare propositional proof-search methods, whether an optimal method exists, why stronger proof systems appear to offer little practical advantage, and how practical solver performance relates to proof-length lower bounds. The supplied record leaves the comparison criterion, proof systems, computational tasks, and practical input classes unspecified. The formal notation provides terminology and a conditional optimality template; it does not resolve those missing choices.
