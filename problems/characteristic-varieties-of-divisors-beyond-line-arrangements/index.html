Characteristic Varieties of Divisors Beyond Line Arrangements

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the standard complex rank-one interpretation, let $X$ be a smooth connected projective complex surface, let $D\subset X$ be the support of a divisor, and put $U=X\setminus D$. These choices provide a framework for the question; the supplied record does not explicitly specify the ground field, connectedness, or conventions concerning divisor multiplicities. For a base point $u\in U$, define the character space and its cohomology jump loci by \[ \mathbb T(U)=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(U,u),{\mathbb{C}}^*),\qquad \mathcal V^q_r(U)=\{\rho\in\mathbb T(U):\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}H^q(U;{\mathbb{C}}_\rho)\ge r\}, \] where $q\ge0$, $r\ge1$, and ${\mathbb{C}}_\rho$ is the rank-one local system with monodromy $\rho$. An irreducible component means an irreducible algebraic component of the specified jump locus.

Source question. Does the characterization of high-dimensional essential components of the characteristic variety for a union of lines in $\mathbb P^2$ extend to more general divisors on smooth projective surfaces, or is that characterization specific to unions of lines?

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not state the line-arrangement characterization being referenced. It also does not identify the cohomological degree $q$, the depth $r$, the dimension threshold meant by "high-dimensional," or the definition of "essential." Consequently, the displayed jump loci explain a standard interpretation of characteristic varieties but do not identify the precise locus intended by the source. Neither the exact proposed equivalence nor its hypotheses for general divisors can be reconstructed from this record. The question is therefore retained as an extension problem requiring those specifications, rather than asserted as a fully quantified conjecture for every divisor and every surface.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a criterion describing high-dimensional essential components of characteristic varieties of plane line complements also applies to complements of more general divisors on smooth projective surfaces. The supplied record omits the criterion and several defining conventions, so a precise universal assertion remains unresolved.
