Affine tractor factories and Q-like tensor-densities

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 2 asks for natural tensor-densities with the affine conformal transformation law labeled (43) in the source. That preceding formula is not reproduced. The paired aid uses the following convention. On a conformal $n$-manifold, let $\widehat g=e^{2\omega}g$, let $\mathcal E[w]$ denote weight-$w$ densities, and let $\mathcal T[-1]=\mathcal T\otimes\mathcal E[-1]$ be the weighted standard tractor bundle. The scale-associated tractor $I_g$ and associated operator $D$ are normalized so their scale-change law gives the affine construction below; their complete formulas must be checked against (43).

Let $P:\mathcal T[-1]\to\mathcal E[-n]$ be a natural conformally invariant linear differential operator. The aid reports \[ A_g=PI_g,\qquad L=PD,\qquad A_{\widehat g}=A_g+L\omega,\qquad L1=0, \] as an identity of density sections. In scalar representatives this reads \[ e^{n\omega}a_{\widehat g}=a_g+L_g\omega. \] The operator $L$ is conformally invariant in the corresponding density sense. This is a construction of examples, not an exhaustive classification.

For even dimension in a setting with critical $Q$-curvature and GJMS operator $P_n$, use the convention $e^{n\omega}q_{\widehat g}=q_g+(P_n)_g\omega$. If $L$ is formally self-adjoint on a closed manifold, then for any constant $\alpha$, \[ \widetilde q_g=q_g+\alpha a_g,\qquad \widetilde P_g=(P_n)_g+\alpha L_g \] satisfy the same affine law, and the integral of $\widetilde q_g$ is conformally invariant. A general correction need not have zero integral; its integral is invariant. The aid reports the quadratic conformal cocycle \[ S_g(\omega)=\int_M\left( \omega\widetilde q_g+\tfrac12\omega\widetilde P_g\omega \right)dv_g, \] \[ S_g(\omega+\eta)=S_g(\omega)+S_{e^{2\omega}g}(\eta). \] Self-adjointness, annihilation of constants and the boundary-free integration convention are essential here.

The workshop example is $P=\iota(D)|C|^2$, where $C$ is Weyl curvature, $|C|^2$ acts by multiplication and $\iota(D)$ denotes tractor contraction. The source discusses this formal construction in any dimension and singles out dimension six for the integrable weight $-n$. Its six-dimensional scalar representatives, in the workshop sign and normalization convention, are \[ a_g=4\Delta_g|C_g|^2,\qquad L_g=-16\delta_g\left(|C_g|^2d\right), \] \[ e^{6\omega}a_{\widehat g} =4\Delta_g|C_g|^2-16\delta_g\left(|C_g|^2d\omega\right). \] The extracted trailing "21" in the first formula is treated as a page artifact, as the supplied summary reconstructs the expression. The divergence-form operator is formally self-adjoint. On every closed six-manifold, $\int_M a_g\,dv_g=0$, so this example leaves the total $Q$ value unchanged while changing the local prescription operator wherever Weyl curvature is nonzero. It relates $Q_6+\alpha a_g$ to a modification of $P_6$, rather than asserting all the original $Q_6$ properties are identical.

The aid also names later Branson--Gover $Q_k$/Pontrjagin constructions as further scalar families, but supplies no formulas or exhaustive classification. The original record ends mid-sentence, so no missing final assertion is reconstructed.

Short English statement

The supplied tractor construction produces densities with an affine conformal law and a conformally invariant correction operator. Under self-adjointness it yields modified $Q$-curvature and a quadratic cocycle. The six-dimensional Weyl-square example changes the local operator but has zero total correction on closed manifolds. The requested general classification is not supplied.
