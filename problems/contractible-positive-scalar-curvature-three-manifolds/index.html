Math-4911

Contractible Positive-Scalar-Curvature Three-Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Interpret a complete contractible three-manifold with positive scalar curvature as a smooth Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ of dimension $3$, using the usual convention that manifolds have no boundary. This boundary convention is not explicit in the supplied statement and requires confirmation. Completeness means completeness of the Riemannian distance induced by $g$. Contractibility means that there exist $p\in M$ and a continuous map $H:M\times[0,1]\to M$ such that \[ H(x,0)=x,\qquad H(x,1)=p\qquad(x\in M). \] Positive scalar curvature means \[ \operatorname{Scal}_g(x)>0\qquad\text{for every }x\in M; \] no uniform positive lower bound is assumed.

Question. For every such $(M,g)$, must there exist a homeomorphism \[ f:M\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^3? \] The conclusion concerns the topology of $M$. No assumption of trivial fundamental group at infinity, bounded geometry, or curvature decay is included in the question. The supplied dated curation mentions results under additional hypotheses of these kinds, without specifying their precise formulations.

Short English statement

Must every contractible three-manifold admitting a complete Riemannian metric whose scalar curvature is positive at every point be homeomorphic to ${\mathbb{R}}^3$? The formulation uses the usual convention of smooth manifolds without boundary, which the source does not explicitly state. The supplied record labels the unrestricted question open.
