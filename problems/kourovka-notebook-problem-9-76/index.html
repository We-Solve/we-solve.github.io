Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.76

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime, let $k$ be any field of characteristic $p$, and let $r\geq 2$ be an integer. Let \[ F=k\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle_{+} \] be the free associative $k$-algebra without identity consisting of polynomials with zero constant term in the indicated noncommuting variables. Let $I$ be a two-sided ideal of $F$, and put $A=F/I$. Assume that $A$ is nil but not nilpotent: every $a\in A$ satisfies $a^m=0$ for some integer $m\geq 1$ depending on $a$, while for every integer $n\geq 1$ there exist $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A$ with $a_1\cdots a_n\neq 0$.

In the algebra obtained by adjoining an identity to $A$, the adjoint group $1+A$ consists of the elements $1+a$, with multiplication \[ (1+a)(1+b)=1+(a+b+ab). \] Writing $\bar x_i=x_i+I$, define the Golod group associated with these data by \[ G=\langle 1+\bar x_1,\ldots,1+\bar x_r\rangle\leq 1+A. \] Here $r$-generated means generated by the displayed $r$ elements; minimality of this generating set is not assumed.

Statement (Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.76). For every choice of $p,k,r,I$ satisfying these assumptions and every $g\in G$, the centralizer \[ C_G(g)=\{h\in G:hg=gh\} \] is infinite.

The supplied record additionally reports constructions of Golod groups with infinite centre by A. V. Timofeenko and, independently by other methods in the 1990s, by L. Hammoudi. These are background claims about particular constructions, not additional hypotheses on $G$. The record supplies no precise bibliographic reference for Hammoudi's work, and these historical claims have not been independently verified here.

Short English statement

For every Golod group formed from a finitely generated free associative algebra over a field of positive characteristic by taking a nil but non-nilpotent quotient as specified above, every element should commute with infinitely many elements of that group. Proposed by V. P. Shunkov in 1984; the supplied registry labels the problem open. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
