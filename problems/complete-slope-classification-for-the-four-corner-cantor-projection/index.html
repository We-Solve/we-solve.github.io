Math-1382

Complete slope classification for the four-corner Cantor projection

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the normalization explicitly recovered in the supplied source verification: \[ \mathcal C_4=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_j}{4^j}:a_j\in\{0,1\}\right\}\subseteq[0,4/3]. \] For $\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}$ let $E_\alpha=\mathcal C_4+\alpha\mathcal C_4=\{x+\alpha y:x,y\in\mathcal C_4\}$. T. Tao asks to characterize exactly the slopes $\alpha$ for which one-dimensional Lebesgue measure $\mathcal L^1(E_\alpha)$ is zero. The workshop remarks that this is known for Lebesgue-almost every real $\alpha$.

The attached aid reports the complete classification \[ \mathcal L^1(E_\alpha)=0 \iff \begin{cases} \alpha=0,\quad\text{or}\\ \alpha\notin{\mathbb{Q}},\quad\text{or}\\ \alpha=p/q\ne0,\ \gcd(|p|,q)=1,\ q>0, \quad v_2(|p|)-v_2(q)\equiv0\pmod2. \end{cases} \] Here $v_2(a)$ is the exponent of $2$ in a positive integer $a$; the gap may be negative, with parity taken in ${\mathbb{Z}}$. Equivalently, a nonzero reduced rational has positive measure exactly when this valuation gap is odd. The aid further reports that these positive-measure sets contain a nondegenerate interval; irrational slopes have Hausdorff dimension one despite zero measure; and nonzero rational zero-measure cases have an exact self-similar overlap and Hausdorff dimension less than one.

For the normalized positive rational subcase $p=2u>0$ with $u,q$ odd and $\gcd(p,q)=1$, it also records the bound $\mathcal L^1(E_{p/q})\geq4/q$. This uses the displayed index-$0$ convention; changing the starting index rescales the measure. The aid describes reductions by sign, inversion, and multiplication of the slope by $4$, followed by complete-residue and Fourier certificates for rational slopes. No proof or full published classification reference is supplied here, so the characterization and dimensional refinements remain recorded claims requiring independent verification.

Short English statement

Characterize zero-measure projections of the four-corner Cantor product. The supplied classification says all irrational slopes have zero measure, while a nonzero rational has positive measure exactly when its reduced numerator/denominator 2-adic valuation gap is odd. The starting-index normalization is explicit.
