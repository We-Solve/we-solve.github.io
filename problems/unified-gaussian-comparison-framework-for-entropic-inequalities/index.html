Unified Gaussian Comparison Framework for Entropic Inequalities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For interpreting the displayed comparison, let $k,m$ be positive integers, let $X_i$ be random vectors taking values in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n_i}$, and write $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_k)$. Under the conventional coupling interpretation, $\Pi(X_1,\ldots,X_k)$ denotes the collection of joint probability laws whose respective marginals are the laws of $X_1,\ldots,X_k$. The supplied excerpt does not explicitly define $\Pi$, so this interpretation requires confirmation. For a random vector $Y$ with Lebesgue density $f_Y$, its differential entropy, when well-defined and finite, is \[ h(Y)=-\int f_Y(y)\log f_Y(y)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y. \] Let $d_j\geq 0$ and let $B_j:{\mathbb{R}}^{n_1+\cdots+n_k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{r_j}$ be linear maps, for $1\leq j\leq m$. These domains express the dimensional compatibility mentioned in the excerpt; the remaining hypotheses of the cited principle are not supplied.

The source fragment describes a Gaussian comparison in which Gaussian random vectors $Z_1,\ldots,Z_k$ satisfy \[ \dim Z_i=\dim X_i,\qquad h(Z_i)=h(X_i)\quad(1\leq i\leq k), \] and displays the schematic inequality \begin{equation} \max_{X\in\Pi(X_1,\ldots,X_k)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_jh(B_jX) \;\geq\; \max_{Z\in\Pi(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_jh(B_jZ). \tag{1} \end{equation} Here membership in $\Pi$ is interpreted as a condition on the law of the concatenated vector. Gaussian marginal laws do not by themselves require a jointly Gaussian coupling. The source fragment does not specify whether the right-hand optimization has an additional joint Gaussian restriction. The maxima in (1) are retained as supplied; their existence, entropy finiteness, admissible couplings, and any nondegeneracy or regularity hypotheses are unresolved. Consequently, (1) is recorded as source context, not asserted here as a theorem for every choice of the displayed data.

Open research target, as supplied. Find a common Gaussian-comparison framework that encompasses the entropic and geometric inequalities unified by (1) and also proves the additional inequalities referred to in the report as not obviously implied by (1). The supplied record does not identify those additional inequalities or give a precise criterion for what constitutes a common framework. Thus the target remains a research-program question rather than a fully specified quantified conjecture. In particular, "not obviously implied" does not assert that implication from (1) is impossible.

The supplied curation labels the problem partially solved and attributes a broad unifying Gaussian-comparison framework to Aras and Courtade, while reporting incomplete coverage of the additional inequalities. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

Seek a common Gaussian-comparison framework covering both the inequalities unified by the report's comparison principle and the additional inequalities it describes as not obviously implied. The omitted hypotheses of that principle and the unspecified additional inequalities prevent a fully precise conjecture from being extracted from this record.
