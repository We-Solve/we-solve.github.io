Giant Components in Nonlinear Degree-Dependent Random Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite graph $H$, let $V(H)$ be its vertex set and define its largest-component size by \[ L_1(H)=\max\{\lvert V(C)\rvert:C\text{ is a connected component of }H\}, \] with $L_1(H)=0$ when $V(H)=\varnothing$. For a sequence of graphs whose vertex counts tend to infinity, the usual giant-component question concerns components containing a positive proportion of all vertices. In a random-graph formulation, the probability quantifiers and limiting regime must also be specified.

The supplied problem asks: determine the emergence and size of the giant component in the degree-dependent random-graph processes \[ G^f_{n,m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G^{f,*}_{n,m} \] when the attachment function $f$ is genuinely nonlinear. This entails describing the transition and the asymptotic behavior of the largest-component sizes in the intended parameter regime.

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not define either random-graph process. In particular, it does not specify the vertex sets, initial graphs, transition probabilities, the roles and admissible ranges of $n$ and $m$, the treatment of loops and repeated edges, or the distinction denoted by the asterisk. It also does not give the domain, codomain, positivity or growth assumptions on $f$, or a definition of "genuinely nonlinear." The asymptotic scaling and required probability quantifiers are absent. Consequently, the displayed problem is a faithfully retained research target, but a fully quantified mathematical statement cannot be recovered from this record alone; the missing process definitions and hypotheses require source clarification.

Short English statement

Determine when a giant connected component appears, and how large it becomes, in the two supplied degree-dependent random-graph models with genuinely nonlinear attachment. The record omits the model definitions, admissible attachment functions, and limiting regime, so the precise probabilistic statement remains unresolved.
