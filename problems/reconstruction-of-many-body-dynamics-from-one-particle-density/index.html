Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For an $N$-particle state $\Psi(t)$, the one-particle reduced density matrix is obtained by tracing out $N-1$ particles. Its diagonal is the one-particle density $n(x,t)$; for a normalized wave function one convention multiplies this diagonal by $N$ so that $\int n=N$. Bosonic and fermionic wave functions are respectively symmetric and antisymmetric. Reconstruction is relative to fixed interactions and the supplied initial state, with external potentials equivalent up to a purely time-dependent additive scalar when that only changes the global phase.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For time-dependent $N$-boson or $N$-fermion systems with known interactions and initial state, can the full quantum dynamics, and in particular the external potential, be reconstructed from the one-particle density?
Source scope and qualification.
The scalar density is not the entire one-particle density matrix. The appropriate potential class, time regularity and gauge equivalence must be kept in a precise reconstruction theorem; these are not supplied in the extraction.

Short English statement

With the interaction and initial state known, does a particle density determine the driving potential and the resulting many-body evolution?

A reconstructible one-particle case.
Take one particle on the circle $\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z$, with kinetic operator $-\tfrac12\partial_x^2$. Suppose a proposed density $n(x,t)$ is smooth, strictly positive, periodic, and has integral one at every time. Write a wave function as $\psi=\sqrt n\,e^{iS}$, allowing the real phase to have winding $S(L,t)-S(0,t)=2\pi k$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. The fixed nonvanishing initial wave function determines $k$. The imaginary part of the Schrödinger equation is the continuity equation \[ n_t+\partial_x j=0,\qquad j=nS_x. \] Define $F(x,t)=\int_0^x n_t(y,t)\,dy$. Every periodic current satisfying continuity has the form $j(x,t)=J(t)-F(x,t)$, since $F(L,t)=0$. Winding then determines the previously unknown spatially constant current: \[ J(t)=\frac{2\pi k+\int_0^L F(x,t)/n(x,t)\,dx} {\int_0^L 1/n(x,t)\,dx}. \] The denominator is finite and strictly positive. Thus density plus winding fixes $S_x=j/n$. Integrating in $x$ fixes $S$ up to a function of time.
Recovering and checking the potential.
The real part gives the explicit real potential \[ v(x,t)=-S_t(x,t)-\tfrac12 S_x(x,t)^2 +\frac{\partial_x^2\sqrt{n(x,t)}}{2\sqrt{n(x,t)}}. \] Substitution of $\psi=\sqrt n e^{iS}$ verifies both real and imaginary parts of $i\psi_t=-\tfrac12\psi_{xx}+v\psi$. Because the winding $k$ is constant, $S_t$ is periodic, so $v$ is a potential on the circle. Replacing $S$ by $S+a(t)$ replaces $v$ by $v-a'(t)$, exactly the allowed time-dependent scalar gauge.
Compatibility and limitation.
For reconstruction from a specified initial state, require $n(x,0)=|\psi_0(x)|^2$ and require the displayed reconstructed current at $t=0$ to equal $\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\psi_0}\partial_x\psi_0)$. These are genuine representability conditions; an arbitrary smooth density history need not satisfy the second one. With them, a constant phase choice matches $\psi_0$. Within this smooth positive one-particle class, any other solution with the same density and initial state has the same fixed winding, hence the same current and potential modulo gauge. Nodes invalidate division by $n$. For interacting many-particle states, the scalar density does not provide a single phase field with current $nS_x$, and internal stresses and correlations remain undetermined by this computation. Those are the first missing ingredients in the full source problem.
