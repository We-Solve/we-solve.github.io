Global Weak Solutions from Transport Discretizations of Euler Flow

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In standard notation, the isentropic Euler equations for density $\rho\geq 0$ and velocity $u$ are \[ \partial_t\rho+\nabla_x\!\cdot(\rho u)=0, \qquad \partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla_x\!\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla_x p(\rho)=0. \] Here $t\geq0$, $x\in\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$, and $p$ denotes the pressure law. These symbols provide a schematic description: the supplied record does not specify $\Omega$, $d$, or $p$. A weak solution satisfies these equations in the sense of distributions, with the displayed quantities locally integrable, and must also satisfy the intended initial and boundary conditions. Those conditions, the required function spaces, and the treatment of vacuum are not supplied.

Source question. Does the isentropic Euler initial-value problem under the intended hypotheses admit a weak solution for all $t\geq0$? Does the proposed optimal-transport time discretization converge, as its time step tends to zero, to such a solution?

The supplied background qualifies the existence target as global admissible weak solutions for arbitrary finite-energy initial data in the multidimensional setting. Thus the intended existence quantifier is over every initial datum in the intended finite-energy class, with a solution required for all nonnegative times. However, the record does not define that class, the energy, or the admissibility condition. The shorter source question itself says only "weak solution." The background further states that dissipative or measure-valued solutions and results for special initial data do not resolve the intended question.

The discretization is not described: its update rule, interpolants, parameter restrictions, convergence topology, and whether convergence means convergence of the entire family or only of a subsequence are absent. Consequently, the supplied evidence determines the two questions above but does not determine a fully quantified existence or convergence assertion. The missing specifications must be recovered before this schematic statement can be made precise.

Short English statement

The problem asks for global weak solutions of isentropic Euler and convergence of a proposed optimal-transport time discretization to such solutions. The supplied background specifies admissible solutions for arbitrary finite-energy data in several space dimensions. The pressure law, spatial setting, admissibility criterion, and numerical scheme are missing, so a complete mathematical formulation remains unresolved.
