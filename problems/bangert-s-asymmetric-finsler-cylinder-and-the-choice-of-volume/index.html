Math-1446

Bangert's asymmetric Finsler cylinder and the choice of volume

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Bangert's exact question is whether ${\mathbb{R}}\times S^1$ admits an irreversible finite-volume Finsler metric with no nonconstant closed geodesics. A smooth strongly convex Finsler metric $F$ is positively homogeneous on tangent vectors, with positive-definite fiber Hessian of $F^2/2$ off the zero section; irreversible means $F(x,v)\ne F(x,-v)$ for some $(x,v)$. The source's section heading concerns complete finite-volume metrics, although completeness and the volume convention are omitted from the sentence. Forward and backward completeness must be distinguished. The aid reports the intended complete finite-Holmes--Thompson-area question still open, and supplies a different answer for Busemann--Hausdorff area.

Use coordinates $(r,\theta)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})$, and set \[ a(r)=2+\pi^{-1}\arctan r,\qquad \alpha^2={\,\mathrm{d}} r^2+a(r)^2{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta^2, \] \[ f(r)=\sqrt{1+r^2}-\log(1+\sqrt{1+r^2})-1+\log2, \qquad F=\alpha+{\,\mathrm{d}} f. \] This exact Randers metric has \[ f'(r)=\frac{r}{1+\sqrt{1+r^2}},\qquad b(r)=|{\,\mathrm{d}} f|_\alpha<1,\qquad 1-b(r)^2=\frac2{1+\sqrt{1+r^2}}. \] The aid asserts strong convexity, irreversibility, both forward and backward completeness, and no nonconstant closed geodesics. For Randers surfaces the indicated volume densities are \[ {\,\mathrm{d}} A_{\mathrm{BH}}=(1-b^2)^{3/2}{\,\mathrm{d}} A_\alpha, \qquad {\,\mathrm{d}} A_{\mathrm{HT}}={\,\mathrm{d}} A_\alpha, \qquad {\,\mathrm{d}} A_\alpha=a(r)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} r\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta. \] Thus this example has finite BH area and infinite HT area. BH uses Euclidean-normalized tangent unit-ball volume, whereas HT uses normalized symplectic cotangent co-ball volume. The exact perturbation preserves unparametrized geodesic curves, and the aid's conclusion concerns closed curves, not equality of their parametrizations.

The reported complementary obstruction is: every forward-complete exact Randers cylinder $F=\alpha+{\,\mathrm{d}} f$, with $|{\,\mathrm{d}} f|_\alpha<1$ and finite HT area, has infinitely many geometrically distinct nonconstant closed geodesics. The same holds with finite BH area if there is a uniform margin $|{\,\mathrm{d}} f|_\alpha\leq1-\eta$ for some $\eta>0$. This restriction is substantial: the explicit example has $b(r)\to1$ at both ends. The obstruction is for exact Randers metrics, not arbitrary irreversible Finsler metrics.

The merged record also includes the introduction to the next section, on magnetic flows on a closed surface $M$. It describes charged-particle trajectories using the cotangent bundle projection $\pi:T^*M\to M$, the twisted symplectic form \[ \Omega={\,\mathrm{d}} p\wedge{\,\mathrm{d}} x+\pi^*\omega, \] where $\omega$ is a closed, not necessarily exact, two-form, and a kinetic Hamiltonian proportional to $\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}(x)p_i p_j$. Its extraction does not fix the conventional factor $1/2$. This is adjacent background, with no separate magnetic-flow question supplied, and is not a hypothesis of Bangert's cylinder question.

Short English statement

The finite-volume question depends on the Finsler volume convention. The aid gives a complete irreversible exact Randers cylinder with finite BH area and no closed geodesics, but its HT area is infinite. Finite-HT forward-complete exact Randers cylinders instead have infinitely many closed geodesics, while the general intended HT question remains open in the supplied report.
