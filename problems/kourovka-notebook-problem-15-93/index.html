Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.93

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p>2$ be a prime. A pro-$p$ group is a topological group that is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. Automorphisms of pro-$p$ groups are understood here to be continuous. For an automorphism $\varphi$ of a pro-$p$ group $G$, define its fixed-point subgroup by \[ C_G(\varphi)=\{g\in G:\varphi(g)=g\}. \] For a positive integer $d$, let $F_d^{(p)}$ be the free pro-$p$ group on generators $x_1,\ldots,x_d$. Given $w\in F_d^{(p)}$ and $(g_1,\ldots,g_d)\in G^d$, define $w(g_1,\ldots,g_d)$ as the image of $w$ under the unique continuous homomorphism sending each $x_i$ to $g_i$. In this draft, saying that $G$ satisfies a pro-$p$ identity means that \[ \exists d\geq 1\ \exists w\in F_d^{(p)}\setminus\{1\}\ \forall(g_1,\ldots,g_d)\in G^d, \qquad w(g_1,\ldots,g_d)=1. \] The source does not explicitly define "pro-$p$ identity"; this finite-variable interpretation requires confirmation against the original source. The word $w$ is allowed to depend on $G$, and need not be an ordinary abstract group word.

The question is whether, for every prime $p>2$, every pro-$p$ group $G$, and every continuous automorphism $\varphi$ of exact order $2$, the hypothesis that $C_G(\varphi)$ is abelian implies that $G$ satisfies a pro-$p$ identity in the sense above. No finite-generation hypothesis is supplied.

The supplied background says that an affirmative answer would generalize a result attributed to A. N. Zubkov asserting that nonabelian free pro-$p$ groups cannot be represented by $2\times2$ matrices. The coefficient setting and precise meaning of representation in that background assertion are not specified, so it is retained as attributed context rather than a fully formulated auxiliary theorem.

Short English statement

For an odd prime $p$, must every pro-$p$ group admitting an involutory automorphism with abelian fixed-point subgroup satisfy a nontrivial pro-$p$ identity? The draft interprets this as a nonidentity element of a free pro-$p$ group on finitely many generators that evaluates to the identity on every tuple in $G$.
