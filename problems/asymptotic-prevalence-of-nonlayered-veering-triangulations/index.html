Asymptotic Prevalence of Nonlayered Veering Triangulations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "As the number of tetrahedra tends to infinity among veering triangulations, does the proportion of non-layered veering triangulations tend to one?"

The record does not define the admissible veering triangulations, their equivalence relation for counting, or the predicate of being layered. Consequently, the following is a conditional counting formulation, pending specification of those choices.

Fix an admissible class $\mathcal V$ of finite veering triangulations, an equivalence relation $\sim$ preserving tetrahedron count, and a layeredness predicate invariant under $\sim$. For a triangulation $T$, let $t(T)$ denote its number of tetrahedra. For each positive integer $n$, define \[ V_n=\{[T]_{\sim}:T\in\mathcal V,\ t(T)=n\}, \qquad N_n=\{[T]_{\sim}\in V_n:T\text{ is non-layered}\}. \] Here non-layered means that the chosen layeredness predicate fails. Assume that every $V_n$ is finite and that $I=\{n\geq 1:V_n\neq\varnothing\}$ is unbounded. Under counting by exactly $n$ tetrahedra, the question becomes whether \[ \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\in I}} \frac{|N_n|}{|V_n|}=1. \] Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$, does there exist a positive integer $n_0$ such that \[ |N_n|\geq(1-\varepsilon)|V_n| \qquad\text{for every }n\in I\text{ with }n\geq n_0? \] The finiteness and unboundedness assumptions above are requirements for this formulation, not facts established by the supplied record.

The source also leaves unresolved whether counting is instead cumulative. With the same choices and assumptions, that interpretation asks whether \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}|N_k|}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}|V_k|}=1, \] where the denominator is positive for all sufficiently large $n$. The record does not select between these two counting interpretations. Neither formulation is fully specified until the admissible class, counting convention, and meaning of layeredness are fixed.

Short English statement

The question asks whether non-layered veering triangulations account for a proportion tending to one as tetrahedron count grows. A precise counting statement requires conventions for admissible triangulations, equivalence, layeredness, and exact-size versus cumulative counting; these are absent from the supplied record.
