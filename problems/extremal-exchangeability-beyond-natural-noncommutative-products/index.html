Extremal Exchangeability Beyond Natural Noncommutative Products

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An algebraic probability space is a unital $*$-algebra $A$ with a positive normalized state $\phi$. Write $A^{*\,\mathbb N}$ for the unital free product of countably many copies, with canonical embeddings $j_n$. A state on it is exchangeable if invariant under every finite permutation of the copies, and extremal if not a nontrivial convex combination of exchangeable states. For $B,C\subseteq A$, the source's independence requires an extremal exchangeable state $\phi_\infty$ whose restriction to separate copies of $B$ and $C$ agrees with their original joint distribution under $\phi$. The five universal product notions named in the source are tensor, free, Boolean, monotone, and antimonotone independence. In a represented von Neumann setting the tail algebra is the intersection of algebras generated by copies with indices at least $n$ as $n\to\infty$; triviality means that this intersection is only the scalars.
Source scope and qualifications.
The curated statement speaks of replacing extremal shift-invariance by shift-invariance with a trivial tail. The primary item (5) actually says extremal S_infinity-invariance and S_infinity-invariance with trivial tail field, meaning exchangeability, not mere shift invariance. These conditions differ and are recorded separately rather than silently conflated.
Primary-source invariance convention.
In the report's comparison, replace extremality among $S_\infty$-invariant states by $S_\infty$-invariance with trivial tail field. $S_\infty$ acts by finite permutations; the one-sided shift is a weaker symmetry.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the exchangeability-based notion of independence, does extremality hold for the free and Boolean products? Are there examples beyond the tensor, free, Boolean, monotone, and antimonotone universal products, and what changes when extremal shift-invariance is replaced by shift-invariance with trivial tail algebra?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Free and Boolean noncommutative de Finetti theorems resolve related exchangeability and conditional-independence statements, but the broad extremality question listed for the free and Boolean products remains unresolved in this formulation. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

A test separating shift symmetry from exchangeability.
On sequences with entries in $\{0,1\}$, put equal probability on the two alternating sequences $0101\ldots$ and $1010\ldots$. The one-sided shift swaps these atoms, so the law is shift invariant. It is extremal among shift-invariant laws supported there, and indeed among all shift-invariant laws: any decomposition must remain supported on those two atoms, where invariance forces equal weights.
Tail and permutation checks.
The initial bit is recoverable from every tail: from coordinate $n$, parity of $n$ determines coordinate one. Thus the event "initial bit is zero" belongs to the tail sigma-field and has probability $1/2$, so the tail is nontrivial. The law is not exchangeable, since it assigns probability zero to equality of the first two bits, whereas swapping coordinates two and three makes those bits equal with probability one. Therefore extremal shift invariance does not imply a trivial tail, and the difference is already visible classically.
Source-corrected question.
The primary report asks about extremality under finite permutations, not merely shifts. This example deliberately fails that stronger symmetry and hence cannot decide the requested comparison with a trivial tail in the exchangeable class. It also supplies no proof of free or Boolean product extremality and no new universal independence product. The first gap is an argument in the actual permutation-invariant noncommutative state space, controlling tail operators there. All three source requests remain separate.
