Problem 4 — Does the recipe in Theorem 4 give virtually all pA maps?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix the surface $F$ and let $\mathcal P_4(F)$ be the mapping classes produced by the Dehn-twist recipe in the source's Theorem 4. That theorem and its recipe are not supplied. A pseudo-Anosov (pA) class has stretch factor $\lambda(f)>1$ and invariant transverse measured foliations scaled by reciprocal factors.

Problem 4 asks whether \[ \forall f\in\operatorname{Mod}(F)\text{ pseudo-Anosov},\quad \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:\quad f^n\in\mathcal P_4(F). \] Thus "virtually all" means that every pA class has an iterate produced by this particular recipe. It does not mean a density-one assertion, passage to a finite cover of the surface, or merely using some other construction. The registry relates the question to Penner's construction, but this label alone does not supply all the omitted hypotheses and allowed twist words.

The source traces the question to [23,25] and its relationship to the Ehrenpreis Conjecture, identified there as Problem 14, to [10]. It also cites Mosher [42] and joint work of the chapter author with Papadopoulos [45] for other descriptions of pA maps up to iteration; these are described as combinatorial rather than Dehn-twist descriptions and therefore do not automatically answer this question.

The following extracted spectrum discussion defines \[ \Sigma(F)=\{\log\lambda(f):f\text{ is pseudo-Anosov on }F\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}. \] It identifies this with the Teichmüller geodesic length spectrum of $\mathcal M(F)$ and reports discreteness, explaining that dilatations occur as spectral radii of integral-linear Perron--Frobenius symplectomorphisms in dimensions bounded in terms of the topology of $F$. These are supplied background assertions, without the dimensional bounds or surface conventions needed to restate their exact theorems.

Short English statement

Does every pseudo-Anosov mapping class have a positive power arising from the particular Dehn-twist recipe of the omitted Theorem 4? Other combinatorial constructions up to iteration do not answer that exact recipe question.
