Geodesic Zeta Functions and Commensurability

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ and $M'$ be compact negatively curved Riemannian manifolds, in the usual closed connected setting. The source asks whether \[ \zeta_M(s)=\zeta_{M'}(s) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\text{ and }M'\text{ are commensurable}. \] No constant-curvature, arithmetic or dimension-three restriction is imposed in the general question.

The exact geodesic zeta convention is not supplied. For example, a length-based Ruelle convention is \[ \zeta_M(s)=\prod_{\gamma\text{ primitive closed geodesic}} (1-e^{-s\ell(\gamma)})^{-1} \] on a common half-plane of convergence. Orientation counting and multiplicities must be fixed consistently. Selberg-type weighted zeta functions are not silently identified with this product in variable curvature. Equality means equality in the intended common analytic domain (or of the specified continuations if provided), not approximate agreement of finitely many lengths. The data are unmarked unless an identification of fundamental groups is separately supplied.

Under the Riemannian meaning of commensurable, the conclusion seeks a common finite-sheeted Riemannian cover: a manifold $\widetilde M$ and finite covering maps $p:\widetilde M\to M$, $q:\widetilde M\to M'$ with \[ p^*g_M=q^*g_{M'}. \] This is stronger than merely a common topological cover; a rescaling or homothetic variant is another convention. The source does not explicitly define which commensurability category is intended, so this standard metric interpretation needs confirmation.

The supplied literature triage checked 21 August 2026 says the report records a theorem for arithmetic hyperbolic $3$-manifolds, while leaving the broader equal-zeta commensurability question unresolved. It supplies no full theorem statement or extra hypotheses for that special case. The general assertion is not reduced to the arithmetic/three-dimensional setting, and the dated partial-status claim is not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Must compact negatively curved manifolds with the same geodesic zeta function share a finite Riemannian cover? The zeta and commensurability conventions require confirmation. The supplied assessment reports an arithmetic hyperbolic three-dimensional special case, without settling the general question.
