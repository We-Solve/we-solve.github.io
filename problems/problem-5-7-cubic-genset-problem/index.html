Problem 5.7 — (Cubic genset problem).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal I_g$ be the Torelli group in the source's surface convention, the kernel of the mapping-class action on integral first homology. The excerpt does not specify closed versus boundary or marked-point conventions; these matter for sharp generator counts.

Problem 5.7 (cubic generating-set problem). Find a family of generating sets $S_g$ for $\mathcal I_g$ of cardinality $O(g^d)$ for some fixed $d\geq3$, optimally $d=3$. Explicitly, seek a construction and constants $C,g_0$, independent of $g$, with \[ \langle S_g\rangle=\mathcal I_g,\qquad |S_g|\leq Cg^d \quad(g\geq g_0) \] in the intended genus range. The polynomial exponent must not be allowed to depend on $g$.

The source separately quotes Johnson's harder question from [Jo2], $\S5$: for every $g\geq4$, can $\mathcal I_g$ be generated by \[ \frac{4g^3-g}{3} \] elements? It says this would be sharp, with the lower-bound context omitted. It reports Johnson [Jo2], Theorem 3, constructing $35$ explicit generators in genus $3$, attaining the corresponding sharp count. It describes converting his earlier generators to cubic size as unwieldy for $g>3$. The extracted earlier bound reads $O(2g)$; in this context an exponential $O(2^g)$ is a plausible lost-superscript reading, but the typography is unresolved. It is not silently read as a linear-size set, which would already satisfy the polynomial request.

The proposed approach follows Johnson's plan using a simpler generating set for $\operatorname{Mod}_g$. The source credits Brendle and the author [BFa1] with six involutions, and Kassabov [Ka] with four involutions for at least $g\geq7$. It explains that two involutions would give a quotient of the infinite dihedral group and hence a virtually abelian group, precluding that possibility for the intended mapping class groups. It then asks naturally for sharp involution-generator bounds. These are related historical facts and motivations, with source scope, not a claimed present-day optimum or a solution to the Torelli problem.

Short English statement

Construct polynomial-size generating sets for Torelli groups, ideally cubic in genus. Johnson's separate sharp target is $(4g^3-g)/3$ for $g\geq4$, with $35$ generators reported in genus three; surface conventions and one flattened exponential bound require checking.
