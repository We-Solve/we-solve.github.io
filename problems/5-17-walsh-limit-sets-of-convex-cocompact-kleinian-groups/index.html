5.17 (Walsh) — Limit sets of convex-cocompact Kleinian groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Identify $S^2$ with the ideal boundary of $\mathbb H^3$. A Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}_2({\mathbb{C}})$, acting by orientation-preserving isometries of $\mathbb H^3$. Its limit set is \[ \Lambda(\Gamma)=\overline{\Gamma\cdot o}\cap S^2, \] where closure is in $\mathbb H^3\cup S^2$ and $o\in\mathbb H^3$; the result is independent of $o$. In the usual non-elementary setting, $\Gamma$ is convex cocompact when the quotient of the hyperbolic convex hull of $\Lambda(\Gamma)$ by $\Gamma$ is compact.

Walsh's Problem 5.17 asks to characterize the subsets $A\subset S^2$ for which there exists a convex-cocompact Kleinian group with \[ A=\Lambda(\Gamma). \] The source does not specify whether subsets are classified literally in the given sphere, up to ambient homeomorphism, up to a Möbius transformation, or up to quasiconformal equivalence. These are different classification targets. Torsion-free versus orbifold groups and elementary versus non-elementary groups are also not specified.

The supplied summary names Cantor sets, Jordan curves and Sierpiński carpets and links the full question to unresolved carpet questions. It supplies no hypotheses making that list exhaustive or establishing the claimed equivalence. For example, the standard definition also allows cocompact lattice cases with full-sphere limit set unless they are explicitly excluded. Thus the summary's list and equivalence cannot replace the full subset-classification request; they remain qualified context requiring source review.

Short English statement

Characterize the subsets of the sphere at infinity that occur as limit sets of convex-cocompact Kleinian groups. The group class and equivalence relation on subsets need specification. The supplied named topological examples and carpet discussion do not constitute an exhaustive verified classification.
