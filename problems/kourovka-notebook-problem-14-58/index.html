Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.58

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $B$ be an abelian group, written additively, and let $A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(B)$. A group is periodic if each of its elements has finite order. Interpret the source phrase "group of regular automorphisms" as requiring every nonidentity element of $A$ to act without nonzero fixed points: \[ \forall a\in A\setminus\{1\},\qquad \{b\in B:a(b)=b\}=\{0\}. \] The supplied record does not define "regular"; this interpretation requires confirmation against the original source. No finiteness or torsion assumption on $B$ is supplied.
  1. (a) For every prime $p$, if $A$ is periodic, satisfies the displayed fixed-point condition, and has exponent exactly $p$, must $A$ be cyclic? Here exponent exactly $p$ means that $A\neq\{1\}$ and $a^p=1$ for every $a\in A$.
  2. (b) If $A$ is periodic, satisfies the displayed fixed-point condition, and admits a generating set $S\subseteq A$ such that \[ A=\langle S\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{ord}(s)=3\quad\text{for every }s\in S, \] must $A$ be finite? The generating set is not assumed finite.
The supplied record retains part (a) in the main problem section and marks part (b) as archived solved, reporting an affirmative answer attributed to A. Kh. Zhurtov. These are supplied status qualifications, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

For a periodic group acting by fixed-point-free automorphisms on an abelian group, part (a) asks whether prime exponent forces cyclicity, and part (b) asks whether generation by elements of order $3$ forces finiteness. The supplied record labels the overall problem partially solved and reports part (b) as affirmatively solved.
