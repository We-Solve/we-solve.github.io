Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Let $Hu_j=E_ju_j$ and $A_{jk}=\langle u_j,Au_k\rangle$ for a selfadjoint observable with classical symbol $a$. $\{A\}$ is the classical mean on the energy surface, and $C(t)=\{a\circ\Phi_t\,a\}$ is its autocorrelation. The regularized power spectrum is $S(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb R}(C(t)-\{A\}^2)e^{-i\omega t}\,dt$ when the chosen regularization makes this meaningful. For $d$ configuration dimensions the source uses the Weyl density $\rho(E)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-d}\int\delta(E-H_{\rm cl}(p,q))\,dp\,dq$. A quantum variance averages squared matrix elements over the appropriate near-energy ensemble; it is not the value of one chosen entry.
Source scope and qualifications.
The catalogue formula suppresses the averaging bar and can be misread as exact equality for each matrix entry. The primary source calls this a distributional variance conjecture in the semiclassical near-diagonal regime, with j distinct from k and frequency (E_j-E_k)/hbar tending to zero. The Gaussian assertion is an asymptotic distribution claim under the source's chaotic-dynamics setting, not an identity for every observable and Hamiltonian.
Primary-source variance meaning.
With the report's averaging conventions, the proposed off-diagonal variance is $\overline{|A_{jk}|^2}\sim S(0)/(2\pi\hbar\rho(E))$ and the diagonal variance is $\overline{|A_{jj}-\{A\}|^2}\sim gS(0)/(2\pi\hbar\rho(E))$ as $\hbar\downarrow0$. The source's Gaussian and symmetry-factor statements remain part of the target.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For an observable $A$, do the off-diagonal matrix elements satisfy $|A_{jk}|^2=S(0)/(2\pi\hbar\rho(E))$, hence scale as $\hbar^{d-1}$? Are the diagonal matrix elements Gaussian with mean $\{A\}$ and variance $gS(0)/(2\pi\hbar\rho(E))$, where $g=2$ in the presence of time-reversal symmetry and $g=1$ otherwise?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The Feingold--Peres-type variance predictions remain beyond general proofs; rigorous work gives upper bounds and special arithmetic cases, so the stated conjectures are only partially supported. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

An exact spectral correlation identity.
In a finite spectral window with $N$ eigenvectors, let $A$ be a self-adjoint matrix and $A(t)=e^{itH/\hbar}Ae^{-itH/\hbar}$. A direct basis expansion gives \[ C_N(t)=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(A(t)A) =N^{-1}\sum_{j,k}|A_{jk}|^2e^{it(E_j-E_k)/\hbar}. \] For a Schwartz test function $g$ with $\widehat g(\omega)=\int g(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt$, integration yields $\int g(t)C_N(t)dt=N^{-1}\sum_{j,k}|A_{jk}|^2\widehat g((E_k-E_j)/\hbar)$. This is the precise averaged connection between correlations and squared matrix entries; it does not identify a single entry with the classical spectrum.
Why the Gaussian conclusion needs more.
A variance does not determine a distribution. A random variable taking $\pm\sigma$ with equal probability and a centered Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2$ have the same first two moments, but fourth moments $\sigma^4$ and $3\sigma^4$. Thus even proving the proposed second-moment scaling would not establish the diagonal Gaussian law or its symmetry factor. If $\rho(E)$ is of order $\hbar^{-d}$ and $S(0)$ stays finite, the source denominator indeed yields order $\hbar^{d-1}$ by algebra alone.
First missing step.
One needs a controlled quantum-to-classical correlation comparison on time/frequency scales fine enough for the shrinking near-diagonal average, followed by higher-moment or distribution estimates. The source's averaging bars, $j\ne k$ condition and $g=1,2$ assertion are all retained. No exact entrywise equality or general Gaussian theorem is claimed.
