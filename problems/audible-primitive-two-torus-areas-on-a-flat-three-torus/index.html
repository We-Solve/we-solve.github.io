Flat three-torus area and eigenvalue relation.
Let $T^3=\mathbb R^3/\Lambda$ have volume $V$, and let $k$ be a primitive vector in the dual lattice $\Lambda^*$. The map $x\mapsto k\cdot x\pmod1$ has connected flat two-torus fibers. Its gradient has constant length $|k|$. Integrating over one period in the normal direction, whose physical length is $1/|k|$, gives fiber area $A_k=V|k|$. Each fiber is totally geodesic, hence minimal. The Fourier mode $e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}$ has Laplace eigenvalue $\lambda_k=4\pi^2|k|^2$, so \[A_k=\frac{V}{2\pi}\sqrt{\lambda_k}. \] Primitivity matters: a nonprimitive $lk$ defines a disconnected union of $l$ parallel fibers and does not describe one primitive surface.
A counting obstruction in the four-torus clause.
In the product $T^4=T^2\times T^2$, every $T^2\times\{p\}$, with $p$ in the second torus, is a distinct embedded flat minimal torus of genus one representing $[T_1]$. Thus the literal unquotiented count $N(1,0,1)$ is uncountable. Translation equivalence, marked-point conditions or a virtual/moduli count would change the problem and must be declared, rather than silently imposed.
Gap.
The flat relation does not show that a general three-manifold Laplace spectrum determines minimal-surface areas, nor define a suitable area spectrum with multiplicities. The first missing step is a precise object/counting convention stable under the relevant geometry; for the four-torus question it must handle continuous families even in the simplest class. This elementary subcase and obstruction preserve all three source requests without asserting a universal spectral-surface theorem.
