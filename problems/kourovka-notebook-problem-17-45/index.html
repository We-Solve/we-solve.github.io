Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.45

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group. For $H\leq G$ and $g\in G$, write $H^g=g^{-1}Hg$, and write $\langle A,B\rangle$ for the subgroup generated by subgroups $A$ and $B$. A subgroup $H\leq G$ is pronormal if \[ \forall g\in G\quad \exists x\in\langle H,H^g\rangle\quad H^x=H^g. \] It is strongly pronormal if \[ \forall L\leq H\quad\forall g\in G\quad \exists x\in\langle H,L^g\rangle\quad (L^g)^x\leq H. \] For finite $G$, a subgroup $H\leq G$ is a Hall subgroup if \[ \gcd\left(|H|,[G:H]\right)=1. \] Here, having a Sylow tower is interpreted as follows: if $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ are the distinct prime divisors of $|H|$, there is some ordering of these primes and a series \[ 1=H_0\leq H_1\leq\cdots\leq H_k=H, \qquad H_i\trianglelefteq H\quad(0\leq i\leq k), \] such that $|H_i/H_{i-1}|$ is the largest power of $p_i$ dividing $|H|$, for every $1\leq i\leq k$. The trivial group has the empty tower, with $k=0$. The supplied source does not expand the term "Sylow tower" or specify an ordering convention; the interpretation above requires confirmation against the source.

Remaining question, part (c). Is it true that for every finite group $G$ and every Hall subgroup $H\leq G$ having a Sylow tower, $H$ is strongly pronormal in $G$? Explicitly, under these hypotheses, must \[ \forall L\leq H\quad\forall g\in G\quad \exists x\in\langle H,L^g\rangle\quad (L^g)^x\leq H \] hold? No simplicity assumption is imposed on $G$.

Archived questions and supplied qualifications. Part (a) asks whether every Hall subgroup of every finite simple group is pronormal. Part (b) asks whether every Hall subgroup of every finite simple group is strongly pronormal. The supplied Archive of Solved Problems entry reports an affirmative answer to (a), attributed to E. P. Vdovin and D. O. Revin (2012), and a negative answer to (b), attributed to M. N. Nesterov (2017). The source additionally reports that an affirmative answer to (a) for Hall subgroups of odd order was known modulo the classification of finite simple groups (CFSG), citing F. Gross (1987). It also states that some finite nonsimple groups have nonpronormal Hall subgroups, whereas Hall subgroups having a Sylow tower are known to be pronormal. These are preserved source reports, not independently verified mathematical or literature assessments. The registry labels the whole record partially solved and places part (c) among the remaining questions.

Short English statement

Part (c) asks whether every Hall subgroup with a Sylow tower in a finite group is strongly pronormal: for every subgroup $L\leq H$ and every $g\in G$, can $L^g$ be conjugated into $H$ using an element of $\langle H,L^g\rangle$? The supplied record reports parts (a) and (b) as resolved. The precise Sylow tower convention needs source confirmation.
