The Hofer diameter of equators and a bounded graphical chamber

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(S^2,\omega)$ have total area $A>0$, and let $\mathcal L$ be the space of embedded simple closed curves that split it into two regions of area $A/2$. For a Hamiltonian isotopy $L_t=\phi_F^t(L_0)$, $0\le t\le1$, set \[ \ell(L_t)=\int_0^1\left(\max_{L_t}F_t-\min_{L_t}F_t\right){\,\mathrm{d}} t, \qquad d(L_0,L_1)=\inf_{L_t:L_0\leadsto L_1}\ell(L_t). \] The infimum uses Hamiltonian paths of curves, not arbitrary paths without a generating Hamiltonian. Question 1.24, prompted by Polterovich, asks for \[ \operatorname{diam}(\mathcal L,d)=\sup_{L_0,L_1\in\mathcal L}d(L_0,L_1), \] in particular whether it is finite. The area scale is retained rather than normalized away.

The source reports infinite diameter of $\operatorname{Ham}(S^2)$, but does not infer infinite diameter of this Lagrangian orbit. It mentions spinning a neighborhood of the equator to stretch a vertical great circle, while recording skepticism about that candidate. Infinite length of one chosen path would itself not prove unbounded endpoint distance, which is an infimum. It also suggests pair-dependent combinatorial upper bounds using meanders without giving a formula.

The aid's quantitative special case uses action--angle coordinates on the complement of two poles, \[ (q,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\times(-A/2,A/2),\qquad \omega={\,\mathrm{d}} p\wedge{\,\mathrm{d}} q, \] with standard equator $p=0$. For smooth functions $u,v:{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\to(-A/2,A/2)$ of mean zero, their graphs $L_u,L_v$ are equal-area equators. The reported bound is \[ d(L_u,L_v)\le\tfrac12\int_0^1|u(q)-v(q)|{\,\mathrm{d}} q\le A/2. \] Thus the entire fixed graphical chamber has diameter at most $A/2$. For fixed $0<a<A/2$ and integers $N\ge1$, the aid supplies \[ u_N(q)=a\sin(2\pi Nq),\qquad \#(L_{u_N}\cap L_0)=2N\text{ transversely},\qquad d(L_{u_N},L_0)\le\frac{a}{\pi N}\longrightarrow0. \] Raw intersection count therefore gives no divergent lower bound. A family whose distance from a fixed equator tends to infinity must eventually leave this fixed graphical chamber; no statement says the whole orbit is graphical.

The source proposes the analogous diameter for a general symplectic pair $(M,L)$, particularly complex projective two-space with its real projective Lagrangian locus. It also records the graph map \[ \operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega)\longrightarrow \mathcal L(M\times M,\omega\oplus(-\omega)),\qquad \phi\longmapsto\operatorname{graph}(\phi). \] The natural target is the Hamiltonian orbit of the diagonal. The source says this preserves lengths of smooth graph paths but is not an isometry and attributes strong distortion to Ostrover. Length preservation along those paths does not identify the infimum among graph paths with the infimum among all Lagrangian paths. No precise distortion theorem or general-pair diameter is supplied. The aid describes the sphere-equator diameter question as open through primary literature checked in August 2026; that is a dated supplied assessment.

Short English statement

The problem asks for the Hofer diameter of equal-area equators on a symplectic sphere. The aid bounds a fixed graphical chamber and gives curves with arbitrarily many intersections but distance tending to zero. Neither Hamiltonian-group diameter nor length preservation of graph paths answers the full Lagrangian-orbit diameter question.
