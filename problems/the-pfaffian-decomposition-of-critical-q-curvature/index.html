The Pfaffian decomposition of critical Q-curvature

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 21 asks whether $Q$ on a general Riemannian manifold is a multiple of the Pfaffian plus a local conformal invariant plus a divergence. The extraction stops at "See". The aid interprets $Q$ as critical $Q_n$-curvature in even dimension $n=2m\geq2$, with the standard normalization $Q_n=(n-1)!$ on the unit round sphere. A different normalization would change the numerical coefficient below.

Let $E_n$ be the Euler curvature polynomial with positive-round-sphere convention \[ E_n=2^{-m}\delta^{i_1\cdots i_n}_{j_1\cdots j_n} R_{i_1i_2}{}^{j_1j_2}\cdots R_{i_{n-1}i_n}{}^{j_{n-1}j_n}, \] normalized by \[ \int_M E_n\,dv_g=(4\pi)^m m!\,\chi(M) \] on closed manifolds. The aid reports that Alexakis's full proof of the Deser--Schwimmer conjecture applies: critical $Q_n$ is a natural scalar invariant of weight $-n$, and its integral is conformally invariant on closed even-dimensional manifolds. Consequently there exist a pointwise local conformal invariant $W_g$ of weight $-n$ and a natural vector field $T_g$ such that \[ Q_n(g)=\frac1nE_n(g)+W_g+\operatorname{div}_gT_g. \] Here $W_{e^{2\omega}g}=e^{-n\omega}W_g$. If the Pfaffian is instead normalized as a scalar $\operatorname{Pf}_\chi$ satisfying $\int\operatorname{Pf}_\chi\,dv=\chi$, its coefficient is \[ \frac{(4\pi)^m m!}{n}. \] These coefficient statements are equivalent, not competing normalizations.

The aid additionally gives an Euler-coefficient extraction rule. If a weight-$-2m$ natural scalar invariant admits an Alexakis-type decomposition \[ L=aE_{2m}+W+\operatorname{div}T, \] then the unit round sphere determines its coefficient uniquely: \[ a=\frac{L(S^{2m})}{(2m)!} =\frac{\int_{S^{2m}}L\,dv} {(4\pi)^m m!\,\chi(S^{2m})}. \] The first numerator is the constant scalar value on that unit round metric. The local conformal term vanishes there and the divergence integrates to zero. For $L=Q_n$, this gives $(n-1)!/n!=1/n$.

The residual terms need not be unique. Two decompositions with the same Euler coefficient differ precisely by \[ W-W'=\operatorname{div}(T'-T). \] The pointwise identity can be considered locally on a manifold with boundary, but dropping the divergence after integration then requires boundary information. The closed-manifold integral hypothesis in the cited global-invariant theorem is not silently discarded, and no odd-dimensional or differently defined $Q$ assertion is inferred.

Short English statement

Under the standard even-dimensional critical-$Q$ interpretation, the supplied report attributes the desired decomposition to Alexakis's Deser--Schwimmer theorem. The Euler coefficient is $1/n$ in the stated Euler-polynomial normalization and is uniquely determined on the round sphere. The remaining conformal-invariant and divergence terms are not individually unique.
