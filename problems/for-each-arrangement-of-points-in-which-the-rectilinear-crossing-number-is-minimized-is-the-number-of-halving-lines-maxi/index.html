Math-5668

For each arrangement of points in which the rectilinear crossing number is minimized, is the number of halving lines maximized

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual complete-graph interpretation, let $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be an $n$-point set in general position, with no three points collinear. Draw every edge of $K_n$ as its straight segment. Let $c(S)$ count unordered pairs of edges crossing in their relative interiors, and define \[ \overline{\operatorname{cr}}(K_n)=\min_{|T|=n}c(T), \] where the minimum ranges over such planar point sets. Crossing pairs, rather than merely distinct intersection locations, are counted.

For even $n=2m$, a halving line is a line through two points of $S$ leaving exactly $m-1$ other points in each open half-plane. Count each unoriented line once, let $h(S)$ be their number, and put $h_{\max}(n)=\max_{|T|=n}h(T)$ in the same admissible class. The source's extremal question then becomes \[ \forall n\text{ even}\ \forall S,\qquad c(S)=\overline{\operatorname{cr}}(K_n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(S)=h_{\max}(n). \] Thus it asks whether every crossing-minimizing configuration maximizes the number of halving lines, not merely whether some configuration simultaneously optimizes both quantities.

The source does not explicitly specify $K_n$, general position, parity, or the halving-line convention. For odd $n$, equal numbers on the two sides of a point-pair line are impossible; almost-halving pair lines and bisectors through a single point are different extensions. The display is therefore the standard even-cardinality candidate, and an all-$n$ version requires an explicit odd-cardinality definition.

Short English statement

Does every straight-line drawing of $K_n$ with the fewest crossings also have the maximum possible number of halving lines? The standard pair-line formulation uses even-sized point sets in general position.
