Perfection of Pattern-Avoiding Infinite-Word Spaces

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: let $L$ be the set of infinite words over an alphabet $\Sigma$ avoiding a pattern $p$; is $L$ perfect? The record does not specify the alphabet, the pattern, the meaning of pattern avoidance, the topology, or the intended quantification over these parameters. Consequently, the following makes one conventional interpretation precise, conditionally; the supplied evidence does not establish that it is the intended formulation.

Under the one-sided interpretation, put $\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ and regard an infinite word as a function $w:\mathbb N_0\to\Sigma$. Give $\Sigma$ the discrete topology and $\Sigma^{\mathbb N_0}$ the product topology. For a finite word $u\in\Sigma^*$, its prefix cylinder is \[ [u]=\{w\in\Sigma^{\mathbb N_0}:w_0\cdots w_{|u|-1}=u\}. \] Here $\Sigma^*$ denotes the finite words, including the empty word, and $\Sigma^+$ denotes the nonempty finite words.

If a pattern means a nonempty finite word $p\in V^+$ over a variable alphabet $V$, and occurrences mean nonerasing morphic occurrences, define \[ L_{\Sigma,p}=\left\{w\in\Sigma^{\mathbb N_0}:\begin{array}{l} \text{there are no }i\in\mathbb N_0\text{ and monoid morphism }h:V^*\to\Sigma^*\\ \text{with }h(a)\ne\varepsilon\text{ for every }a\in V\text{ and}\\ w_iw_{i+1}\cdots w_{i+|h(p)|-1}=h(p) \end{array}\right\}. \] The symbol $\varepsilon$ denotes the empty word. This definition is conditional: the record does not say whether $p$ is a variable pattern, a literal forbidden word, or another kind of pattern, nor whether erasing substitutions are permitted.

With these conventions, the question is whether $L_{\Sigma,p}$ is perfect, meaning closed in $\Sigma^{\mathbb N_0}$ with no isolated points. Equivalently, the requested property is the conjunction \[ \overline{L_{\Sigma,p}}=L_{\Sigma,p} \quad\text{and}\quad \forall w\in L_{\Sigma,p}\ \forall n\in\mathbb N_0\ \exists v\in L_{\Sigma,p}: \ v\ne w\ \text{ and }\ \forall j<n,\ v_j=w_j. \] Under this definition the empty set is perfect. Whether nonemptiness is intended as an additional requirement is unresolved. The source does not identify a particular $(\Sigma,p)$, state that the question concerns every such pair, or supply restrictions such as finiteness or cardinality of $\Sigma$ or avoidability of $p$. These omissions prevent identification of a unique fully quantified problem.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the space of infinite words avoiding $p$ is perfect. Under a conventional product topology, this means that the space is closed and every allowed word has a different allowed word sharing any prescribed finite prefix. The alphabet, pattern interpretation, topology, and parameter restrictions remain unspecified, so the displayed formulation is conditional and requires mathematical review.
