Math-1336

Exact incidence and complete-intersection interpolation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $H$ be an irreducible component of $\operatorname{Hilb}_{d,g}({\mathbb{P}}_k^3)$, with Hilbert polynomial $dt+1-g$. Interpret general points over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field $k$ as tuples in a dense open subset of $({\mathbb{P}}^3)^r$, rather than merely a finite list of linear-position conditions. Define \[ I_r(H)=\{([C],p_1,\ldots,p_r):[C]\in H,\ p_i\in C, \ p_i\ne p_j\ (i\ne j)\}, \] \[ \iota(H)=\max\{r\ge0:I_r(H)\longrightarrow({\mathbb{P}}^3)^r \text{ is dominant}\}. \] The source asks to determine $\iota(H)$ for fixed $d,g$ and a specified component $H$. It does not specify a field or the exact meaning of general position; the above are explicit interpretation choices. Its remark says the problem is hard but should be treatable for arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay curves, meaning curves whose homogeneous coordinate rings are Cohen--Macaulay.

For a smooth curve $C$ at a smooth Hilbert point and distinct marked smooth points, the supplied aid gives the normal-evaluation criterion \[ H^0(C,N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^3})\longrightarrow \bigoplus_{i=1}^r N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^3}|_{p_i}. \] Surjectivity is the infinitesimal criterion for the incidence projection to be a submersion at that flag. In characteristic zero, dominance on the relevant incidence component is tested at its general smooth flags; dominance is not a claim that evaluation is surjective at every special flag. This criterion applies to components with the necessary smooth-curve locus and is not a complete answer for arbitrary Hilbert components.

The aid reports an exact component-wide answer for the complete-intersection component $H_{a,b}$, the closure of smooth complete intersections of surfaces of degrees $1\le a\le b$. Here \[ d=ab,\qquad g=1+\frac{ab(a+b-4)}2, \] and the reported answer is \[ \iota(H_{a,b})= \begin{cases} \binom{a+3}{3}-1,&a<b,\\ \binom{a+3}{3}-2,&a=b. \end{cases} \] The supplied lower-bound argument uses $N_{C/{\mathbb{P}}^3}\simeq\mathcal O_C(a)\oplus\mathcal O_C(b)$ and normal evaluation. For the upper bound, every member of this component, including non-complete-intersection boundary subschemes, is reported to retain at least one independent degree-$a$ equation if $a<b$, and at least two if $a=b$, by upper semicontinuity. Thus the formula is asserted for the whole component, not only its smooth complete-intersection open locus. The record does not provide a corresponding formula for every arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay component or every pair $(d,g)$.

Short English statement

For a chosen component of space curves, how many general points can some member contain? The supplied aid gives a normal-bundle evaluation test and a sharp formula for complete-intersection components, including a claimed upper bound that also controls their boundary curves.
