Math-785

Bipartizing Triangle-Free Graphs with Few Edge Deletions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph, let $n=|V|$, and assume $G$ is triangle-free, meaning that no three distinct vertices span all three possible edges. Define its edge bipartization number by \[ b(G)=\min\{|F|:F\subseteq E,\ (V,E\setminus F)\text{ is bipartite}\}. \] A graph is bipartite if its vertices can be partitioned into two sets with no edges within either set. The conjecture attributed in the supplied assessment to Erdős asks whether \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \forall G\text{ triangle-free with }|V(G)|=n: \qquad b(G)\le\frac{n^2}{25}. \] Since $b(G)$ is an integer, the exact bound is equivalently $b(G)\le\lfloor n^2/25\rfloor$. Only edges are deleted; all $n$ vertices remain.

Equivalently, writing $\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)$ for the largest number of edges crossing a partition $V=A\sqcup B$, the question is \[ \operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\ge |E(G)|-\frac{n^2}{25}. \] There is no asymptotic error term or restriction to sufficiently large $n$. The finite simple unweighted graph convention is the standard interpretation of the source; loops, parallel edges, and weighted variants are not supplied.

Short English statement

Can every finite triangle-free graph be made bipartite by deleting at most $n^2/25$ edges, with the bound exact for every number of vertices? Equivalently, its largest cut must leave at most that many edges uncut.
