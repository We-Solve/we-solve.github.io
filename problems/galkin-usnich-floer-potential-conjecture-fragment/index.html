Definitions and mathematical statement

For the purpose of explaining the surviving fragment, let $K$ be a field and let $W\in K[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]$ be a nonzero Laurent polynomial. Write \[ W(x,y)=\sum_{(a,b)\in S}c_{a,b}x^ay^b, \qquad S=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2:c_{a,b}\ne0\}. \] Its Newton polygon is \[ \operatorname{Newt}(W)=\operatorname{conv}(S)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2. \] This is a general definition only: the supplied record does not specify a coefficient field, coordinates, or a Laurent-polynomial model for the Floer potentials in question.

The surviving passage refers to a conjecture concerning Floer potentials and to the assertion that their Newton polygons are as predicted. The supplied curation identifies the broader subject as the Galkin--Usnich Floer-potential conjecture. However, the passage begins in the middle of a sentence and supplies neither the full conjecture nor the predicted polygons. Consequently, it does not determine a mathematical assertion with specified objects, hypotheses, and quantifiers. In particular, no equality of potentials or polygons can be stated precisely from this record alone.

The supplied record marks the statement as unrecoverable and its literature status as partially_solved. Its dated assessment attributes to Vianna the predicted Newton-polygon distinction and infinitely many non-Hamiltonian-isotopic tori in unspecified key cases, while reporting that the full Galkin--Usnich Floer-potential conjecture is not established. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical conclusions. The record also reports that Galkin and Mikhalkin suggested a direct tropical translation of Galkin's original construction; it does not describe that construction or translation.

Short English statement

The fragment concerns predicted Newton polygons of Floer potentials associated in the supplied curation with the Galkin--Usnich conjecture. It omits the relevant geometric objects, predictions, and full conjectural claim, so a precise conjecture cannot be recovered.
