Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 16. Computing normalization constants and posterior distributions for continuous random variables involves the computation of integrals involving rational and transcendental funtions. Can algebraic techniques be used to give exact expressions for these integrals or help give approximations for them? 7

A precise integral family for the attempt

The source asks broadly about rational and transcendental integrals and specifies neither a model family nor an accuracy criterion. We investigate one compact exponential family rather than declare an algorithm for all such expressions. Let $w(x)=\sum_{j=0}^d c_jx^j$ be a rational-coefficient polynomial, nonnegative on $[0,1]$, with $M_0=\int_0^1w(x)\,dx>0$. For a specified real $t$, define \[ I_k(t)=\int_0^1x^k e^{tx}\,dx,\qquad Z(t)=\int_0^1w(x)e^{tx}\,dx, \] and the posterior density $w(x)e^{tx}/Z(t)$. These hypotheses include integer-parameter beta polynomial weights, but do not include arbitrary noncompact or singular priors.
Exact expressions with a removable singularity.
Direct integration gives $I_0(t)=(e^t-1)/t$ for $t\ne0$. Integration by parts, using $d(e^{tx})=te^{tx}dx$, gives \[ t I_k(t)=e^t-k I_{k-1}(t)\qquad(k\ge1,\ t\ne0). \] At $t=0$ direct integration gives $I_k(0)=1/(k+1)$. Thus finite recurrence computes every integral and $Z(t)=\sum_{j=0}^d c_jI_j(t)$ exactly in the field generated by $t$ and $e^t$, with the stated special values at zero. The same applies to the numerator of any polynomial posterior moment. The recurrence's subtractions near zero do not themselves supply numerical stability; that is the first gap in an attempted "exact expression implies a good approximation" inference.
A separate error certificate.
Write $M_k=\int_0^1x^kw(x)\,dx=\sum_{j=0}^d c_j/(k+j+1)$, exactly rational. For a nonnegative integer $N$ use \[ Z_N(t)=\sum_{k=0}^N\frac{t^k}{k!}M_k. \] Taylor's integral remainder for $e^u$ has absolute value at most $e^{\max(u,0)}|u|^{N+1}/(N+1)!$. One can verify this directly from $e^u-\sum_{k=0}^Nu^k/k!=u^{N+1}\int_0^1(1-s)^Ne^{su}ds/N!$, obtained by $N+1$ successive integrations by parts. With $u=tx$, nonnegativity of $w$ and $0\le x\le1$ give the rigorous bound \[ |Z-Z_N|\le\delta_N:= \frac{e^{\max(t,0)}|t|^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}M_{N+1}. \] Also $Z\ge e^{\min(t,0)}M_0>0$. Thus the denominator is controlled independently of cancellation. For the posterior moment numerator $A=\int_0^1x^r w(x)e^{tx}dx$, $r\ge0$, replace $M_k$ by $M_{k+r}$ to obtain $A_N$ and a corresponding bound $\eta_N$. Whenever $z_-=Z_N-\delta_N>0$, the quotient certificate is \[ \left|\frac A Z-\frac{A_N}{Z_N}\right| \le \frac{\eta_N}{z_-} +\frac{|A_N|\delta_N}{z_-|Z_N|}. \] Indeed, split the difference as $(A-A_N)/Z+ A_N(Z_N-Z)/(ZZ_N)$ and apply the two interval bounds. For fixed $t$, $M_k\le M_0$, and the factorial bound tends to zero: the ratio of successive $|t|^{N+1}/(N+1)!$ terms is eventually at most $1/2$. Therefore $Z_N\to Z>0$ and eventually $z_->0$. This proves convergence and a usable certificate for this family, including $t=0$ where all remainder terms vanish.

Why the general request is still open in this attempt

Termwise moment expansion cannot simply be exported to unbounded domains. Even integrability can fail: $\int_{\mathbb R}e^x dx$ diverges because $\int_0^R e^x dx=e^R-1\to\infty$. For a proper noncompact posterior, one would still need a tail bound and a justified interchange of series and integration; neither follows from the phrase rational and transcendental functions. The primary AIM Question 16 was inspected. The audit checked compactness, nonnegative weights despite signed coefficients, removable singularities, and division by a certified positive number. No numerical experiment or symbolic package was run. These elementary recurrence and error bounds are known-type special-case methods, with no assertion of a universal integration result or novelty.
