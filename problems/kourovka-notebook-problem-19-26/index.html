Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.26

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be an arbitrary group, and let $A,B\subseteq G$ be finite subsets with $|A|\geq 2$ and $|B|\geq 2$. Define \[ AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\},\qquad r_{A,B}(x)=\left|\{(a,b)\in A\times B:ab=x\}\right| \] for $x\in G$, and put \[ A\mathbin{\cdot_2}B=\{x\in G:r_{A,B}(x)\geq 2\}. \] Thus distinct representations mean distinct ordered pairs in $A\times B$. Define \[ M(A,B)=\max\left(\{2\}\cup \left\{|gH|:H\leq G,\ g\in G,\ gH\subseteq A\mathbin{\cdot_2}B\right\}\right), \] where $gH=\{gh:h\in H\}$ is a left coset. This maximum exists because $A\mathbin{\cdot_2}B$ is finite, so every qualifying coset is finite and its cardinality is bounded. If no coset qualifies, the maximum is $2$.

Is it true that, for every such $G,A,B$, \[ |A|+|B|-\frac12|AB|-\frac12|A\mathbin{\cdot_2}B|\leq M(A,B)? \] The supplied source states that this inequality is proved when $G$ is abelian. That assertion is retained as a source qualification and has not been independently verified.

Short English statement

For two finite subsets of an arbitrary group, each of size at least two, consider both their product set and the products with at least two distinct representations. The question asks whether the displayed expression in their cardinalities is bounded by the larger of $2$ and the size of any left coset contained in the latter set. The supplied source reports the abelian case as proved.
