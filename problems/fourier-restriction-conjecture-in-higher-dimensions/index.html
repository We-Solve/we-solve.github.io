Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For a smooth compact hypersurface with nonzero curvature in $\mathbb R^d$, prove the conjectured sharp $L^p$--$L^q$ Fourier restriction bounds in every dimension $d\geq3$. The restriction operator sends a Schwartz function to the restriction of its Fourier transform to the hypersurface; the requested range is the one dictated by the standard scaling and concentration obstructions.

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

Fourier Restriction Conjecture in Higher Dimensions

OpenAI's claim

Extension from curved surfaces: For a compact smooth surface in $\mathbb R^3$ with positive curvature, the paper claims diagonal Fourier extension boundedness for every exponent $p>3$; it includes smooth surface boundaries and draws a two-dimensional Schrödinger smoothing consequence from the paraboloid case.

Sphere extension and packet capacity: For bounded data on the two-sphere, the announced extension bound has every output exponent larger than three; the threshold three is also asserted to be the sharp endpoint of this open range.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a compact smooth surface with positive curvature, allowing smooth boundary, and use surface area $\sigma$. For $E_\Sigma f(x)=\int_\Sigma e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\,d\sigma(\xi)$, the claim is \[\|E_\Sigma f\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^3)}\le C_{\Sigma,p}\|f\|_{L^p(\Sigma,\sigma)}\quad(3<p<\infty),\] for all complex-valued $f\in L^p(\Sigma)$. It concerns this diagonal range in three dimensions, with a surface-dependent constant.
Mathematical claim 2.
For surface area $\sigma$ on $S^2$ and $Eg(x)=\int_{S^2}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}g(\omega)\,d\sigma(\omega)$, every bounded measurable complex $g$ satisfies \[\|Eg\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^3)}\le C_p\|g\|_{L^\infty(S^2,\sigma)}\quad(p>3).\] The constant depends only on $p$; the endpoint $p=3$ and other ambient dimensions are outside this theorem.

Outline of the argument

Extension from curved surfaces: Graph patches are extended to phases compatible with an elliptic-capacity propagation theorem. That theorem is combined with weighted Kakeya control to sum packet coefficients by their capacity. A joint estimate for widely separated balls removes the local spatial loss, and chart assembly transfers the estimate to the original surface.

Sphere extension and packet capacity: Joint tests of position and velocity use ellipse weights that penalize eccentricity. A geometric propagation estimate controls how this capacity evolves along lines, using overlap savings from differently directed tests. Exact packet analysis and synthesis retain the oscillatory information needed to convert the positive capacity estimate into Fourier extension bounds.
