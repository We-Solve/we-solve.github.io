Determinacy Methods in LOCAL and Borel Coloring

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Write $[\mathbb N]^{\mathbb N}$ for the infinite subsets of $\mathbb N$ with their standard Borel structure. The shift map removes the least element; the undirected shift graph $G_S$ joins $x$ to $x\setminus\{\min x\}$. A proper coloring gives distinct colors to adjacent vertices; its Borel version must be Borel measurable. The chromatic number is the least number of colors, while the Borel chromatic number restricts to Borel colorings. The Axiom of Determinacy (AD) says every length-$\omega$ perfect-information game on natural numbers has a winning strategy for one player. The LOCAL model counts synchronous graph-communication rounds.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Can shift-graph and Galvin–Prikry methods be used in the LOCAL model? Can determinacy arguments construct functions of large Borel chromatic number, and does the Axiom of Determinacy imply that the shift graph $G_S$ has infinite chromatic number? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The determinacy question is over ZF as in the source. Ordinary and Borel chromatic numbers are distinct; the final question concerns the ordinary chromatic number under AD.

Literature and context check

I read the shift-graph discussion on pages 451--453 of the primary report. It invokes Galvin--Prikry for Borel colorings and expressly says that the implication from AD to the Ramsey property is not established there. The calculations below therefore do not assume that implication. They examine an exact finite shift-coloring problem and the conditional Ramsey argument, then identify why neither gives the original determinacy result.

Research attempt: Finite shift-color bounds and the choice-sensitive infinite obstruction

An exact finite combinatorial calculation.
For $N\geq2$, let $H_N$ have vertices $(i,j)$ with $0\leq i<j<N$ and edges between $(i,j)$ and $(j,k)$ whenever $i<j<k$. If a proper coloring uses $q$ colors, put $S_j=\{c(i,j):i<j\}$, including $S_0=\varnothing$. For every $j<k$, the color $c(j,k)$ belongs to $S_k$ but not $S_j$: any occurrence $c(i,j)=c(j,k)$ would violate the edge constraint. Thus the $N$ subsets $S_0,\ldots,S_{N-1}$ are distinct, proving $N\leq2^q$ and $q\geq\lceil\log_2N\rceil$. For the matching upper bound, write the integers in $\lceil\log_2N\rceil$ bits and color $(i,j)$ by the most significant position at which they differ. Since $i<j$, the bit there is zero in $i$ and one in $j$. If $(i,j)$ and $(j,k)$ had the same position, the bit of $j$ would be both one and zero, impossible. Hence \[ \chi(H_N)=\lceil\log_2N\rceil. \] This is a finite constraint on color rules for consecutive pairs of identifiers. It is not a bounded-degree tree LOCAL lower bound: these finite graphs have growing degree and are prefix-state constructions, not asserted to be finite subgraphs of the infinite functional shift forest.
The exact Ramsey hypothesis that would suffice.
Suppose a finite coloring of $[\mathbb N]^{\mathbb N}$ has an infinite set $A$ on which all infinite subsets have the same color. Choose any infinite $x\subseteq A$. Then both $x$ and $Sx=x\setminus\{\min x\}$ are infinite subsets of $A$, so the coloring is not proper for $G_S$. Thus a Ramsey property for every finite coloring would imply $\chi(G_S)=\infty$. The primary report applies the external Galvin--Prikry theorem to Borel colorings for this reason. That theorem is context, not re-proved here. The same reasoning cannot be applied to arbitrary color classes under AD without a theorem establishing the corresponding Ramsey conclusion.
Why an ordinary two-coloring under choice is consistent.
Orient every edge $x\to Sx$. Every vertex has exactly one outgoing edge and there are no directed cycles, since the minimum strictly increases at each forward step. There is no finite undirected cycle either: on such a cycle, the number of edges equals the number of vertices, while each vertex can supply at most one outgoing cycle edge. Equality forces exactly one outgoing edge at every cycle vertex, giving a directed cycle, a contradiction. Thus each component is a tree. Under the axiom of choice select one vertex in each component and color vertices by parity of their finite graph distance from that chosen vertex. Unique paths in a tree make this a proper two-coloring. This selection need not be Borel and is not an operation justified by ZF+AD. Consequently an argument that silently chooses component roots would discard precisely the set-theoretic issue in the source question.

Verification and adversarial audit

The finite proof includes $N=2$, where the single vertex needs one color. In the infinite graph, the lack of finite cycles is proved from the orientation, and the use of choice in selecting component representatives is explicit. The Ramsey implication distinguishes one homogeneous infinite family from an ordinary infinite subset of vertices. AD is never used as an unproved substitute for that property.

Did not assume AD implies the Ramsey property. Distinguished the finite pair graph from the infinite shift forest and marked exactly where the ordinary two-coloring invokes component-wise choice.
