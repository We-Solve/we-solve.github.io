Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.18

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a prime $p$ and an integer $k\geq 2$, let \[ R_{p^k}=\{G: G\models \forall x\,x^{p^k}=1\text{ and }\forall x,y,z\,[[x,y],z]=1\}, \] where $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. Thus $R_{p^k}$ is the variety of groups of nilpotency class at most $2$ and exponent dividing $p^k$. These bounds are the conventional interpretation of the source's phrase "variety of class $2$ nilpotent groups of exponent $p^k$." Write $G' = \langle [x,y]:x,y\in G\rangle$ for the derived subgroup. For a finite group $H$, $\exp(H)$ is the least positive integer $e$ such that $h^e=1$ for every $h\in H$.

A group quasi-identity is a universally quantified implication \[ \forall x_1,\ldots,x_n\;\left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}u_j=v_j\ \Longrightarrow\ u=v\right), \] where all displayed expressions are group terms and the conjunction may be empty. A quasivariety is a class of groups axiomatized by quasi-identities. Let $\mathsf Q(F)$ denote the smallest quasivariety containing a group $F$, equivalently the class of groups satisfying every quasi-identity valid in $F$.

A basis of quasi-identities for a quasivariety $\mathcal K$ is a set $B$ whose group models are exactly $\mathcal K$. It is independent if, for every $\varphi\in B$, there exists a group satisfying $B\setminus\{\varphi\}$ and failing $\varphi$. This defines independence over the group axioms. A relative basis within $R_{p^k}$ instead has $\mathcal K=\{G\in R_{p^k}:G\models B\}$, and relative independence requires each witnessing group to belong to $R_{p^k}$. The supplied record does not specify which meaning of basis and independence is intended; that qualification remains unresolved.

Question (A. I. Budkin, 2022). Is it true that, for every prime $p$ and every integer $k\geq 2$, there exist infinitely many pairwise distinct subquasivarieties $\mathcal K_i\subseteq R_{p^k}$, indexed by $i\geq 1$, and finite groups $F_i\in R_{p^k}$ such that \[ \mathcal K_i=\mathsf Q(F_i),\qquad \exp(F_i')=p^k, \] and each $\mathcal K_i$ has no independent basis of quasi-identities? The basis convention must be resolved as described above. The equality $\exp(F_i')=p^k$ retains the source's stated exponent requirement as an exact exponent. The universal quantifier includes $p=2$; the source supplies no exception for that prime.

Short English statement

For every prime $p$ and integer $k\geq 2$, Budkin asks whether there are infinitely many distinct quasivarieties within $R_{p^k}$, each generated by a finite group whose derived subgroup has exponent exactly $p^k$, and each lacking an independent basis of quasi-identities. The source leaves unresolved whether the basis and independence are meant over all groups or relative to $R_{p^k}$.
