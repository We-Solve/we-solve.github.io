Erdős Problem #243

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

I am pursuing (C) obstruction/correction. Round-1 produced only the very soft inequality \[ a_{n+1}\le 2q\,L_n \] where $L_n=\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ and $\sum 1/a_n=p/q$. The natural Round-2 objective is to sharpen this to the correct first-order scale and then combine it with the classical Erdős--Straus criterion to isolate a strong obstruction that every non-Sylvester counterexample would have to satisfy.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 results.
  1. If $S=\sum_{m\ge 1}1/a_m=p/q$ in lowest terms and \[ T_n:=\sum_{m\ge n+1}\frac{1}{a_m}, \qquad L_n:=\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n), \] then $T_n$ is a positive rational number whose denominator divides $qL_n$.
  2. In particular, \[ T_n\ge \frac{1}{qL_n}. \]
  3. Sylvester's recurrence \[ a_{n+1}=a_n^2-a_n+1 \] implies a telescoping identity and hence rationality of the reciprocal sum.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

There are two new ingredients.
  1. A refined tail asymptotic: \[ T_n=\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}+\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}. \] This upgrades the Round-1 crude estimate $T_n\le 2/a_{n+1}$.
  2. An external theorem of Erdős and Strauss (quoted explicitly in Duverney's 2001 paper): if $L_n/a_{n+1}$ is bounded and $a_{n+1}/a_n^2$ has limsup at least $1$, then rationality already forces eventual Sylvester recurrence.
Combining these yields a strong new obstruction: any non-Sylvester counterexample must have \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{qL_n}{a_{n+1}}\ge 1 \qquad\mbox{and}\qquad \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_n}{a_{n+1}}=\infty. \] So the lcm-ratio can neither go to $0$ nor stay bounded.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

The exact Round-1 gap was the factor $2$ in $a_{n+1}\le 2qL_n$, which was too weak to interact sharply with the classical lcm criterion. I will:
  1. prove the tail asymptotic to first nontrivial order;
  2. use rational approximation to sharpen $a_{n+1}\le 2qL_n$ to \[ a_{n+1}\le (1+o(1))qL_n; \]
  3. import the Erdős--Straus/ Duverney theorem for the bounded-lcm-ratio regime;
  4. conclude that any genuine counterexample must force $L_n/a_{n+1}$ to be unbounded above.
This rules out an entire class of possible counterexamples, namely those with $L_n=O(a_{n+1})$.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Let \[ S:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_m}=\frac{p}{q} \] with $p,q$ coprime positive integers, and let \[ T_n:=\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_m}, \qquad L_n:=\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n). \]
Lemma 5.1 (refined tail asymptotic).
Under the hypothesis \[ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 1, \] one has \[ T_n-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}=\sum_{m=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_m}=\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}. \] Hence \[ T_n=\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}+\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}. \]

Proof. Write \[ R_n:=\sum_{m=n+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_m}. \] Since $a_{n+2}/a_{n+1}^2\to 1$, we already have \[ \frac{1}{a_{n+2}}=\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}. \] Thus it suffices to show that the tail beyond $a_{n+2}$ is negligible compared to $1/a_{n+2}$.

Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$. For all sufficiently large $m$, \[ a_{m+1}\ge (1-\varepsilon)a_m^2. \] Applying this repeatedly gives, for $j\ge 1$, \[ a_{n+2+j}\ge (1-\varepsilon)^{2^j-1}a_{n+2}^{2^j}. \] Therefore \[ \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_{n+2+j}} \le \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{-(2^j-1)}}{a_{n+2}^{2^j}} =O\!\left(\frac{1}{a_{n+2}^2}\right), \] which is $o(1/a_{n+2})$. Hence \[ R_n=\frac{1}{a_{n+2}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{a_{n+2}}\right)=\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+2}}. \] Using $a_{n+2}=(1+o(1))a_{n+1}^2$ yields \[ R_n=\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}. \] This proves the lemma.

Proposition 5.2 (sharp first-order denominator obstruction).
One has \[ a_{n+1}\le (1+o(1))qL_n. \] Equivalently, \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{qL_n}{a_{n+1}}\ge 1. \]

Proof. Set \[ D_n:=qL_n. \] By Round-1, $T_n$ is a positive rational number whose denominator divides $D_n$.

Fix $\delta>0$ and suppose, for contradiction, that along an infinite subsequence, \[ a_{n+1}\ge (1+\delta)D_n. \] For such $n$ we certainly have $a_{n+1}>D_n$, hence $a_{n+1}$ does not divide $D_n$. Therefore $1/a_{n+1}$ is not a rational whose denominator divides $D_n$.

Now let $r=u/v$ be any rational number with $v\mid D_n$ and $r\ne 1/a_{n+1}$. Then \[ \left|r-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\right| =\frac{|a_{n+1}u-v|}{a_{n+1}v} \ge \frac{1}{a_{n+1}D_n}, \] because $a_{n+1}u-v$ is a nonzero integer and $v\le D_n$.

Applying this to $r=T_n$ gives \[ T_n-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\ge \frac{1}{a_{n+1}D_n} \ge \frac{1+\delta}{a_{n+1}^2}. \] But by Lemma 5.1, \[ T_n-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}=\frac{1+o(1)}{a_{n+1}^2}, \] which is $< (1+\delta)/a_{n+1}^2$ for all sufficiently large $n$. This contradiction proves that no fixed $\delta>0$ can occur infinitely often. Hence \[ a_{n+1}\le (1+o(1))D_n=(1+o(1))qL_n. \] The liminf formulation is just a rearrangement.

External theorem 5.3 (Erdős--Straus criterion; quoted in Duverney).
Let $(u_n)$ be an increasing sequence of positive integers such that \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n^2}\ge 1 \] and \[ \frac{\mathrm{lcm}(u_1,\dots,u_n)}{u_{n+1}} \] is bounded. Then \[ \sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{1}{u_n}\in \mathbf{Q} \] if and only if \[ u_{n+1}=u_n^2-u_n+1 \] for all sufficiently large $n$.
Corollary 5.4 (new obstruction for any non-Sylvester counterexample).
Assume the hypotheses of Problem #243 and suppose the sequence is not eventually Sylvester. Then \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_n}{a_{n+1}}=\infty. \] Combined with Proposition 5.2, such a counterexample would have to satisfy both \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{qL_n}{a_{n+1}}\ge 1 \qquad\mbox{and}\qquad \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_n}{a_{n+1}}=\infty. \]

Proof. If $L_n/a_{n+1}$ were bounded, then External Theorem 5.3 would apply with $u_n=a_n$, because our problem hypothesis gives \[ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 1, \] so in particular the limsup is at least $1$, and the reciprocal sum is rational by assumption. The theorem would then force eventual Sylvester recurrence, contrary to hypothesis. Therefore $L_n/a_{n+1}$ is unbounded, which is exactly the displayed limsup statement. The liminf statement is Proposition 5.2.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  1. Is Lemma 5.1 really sharp enough? Yes. The first omitted term is $1/a_{n+2}=(1+o(1))/a_{n+1}^2$, and the remaining tail is smaller by another factor of order $1/a_{n+2}$.
  2. Did Proposition 5.2 misuse the denominator-divisibility argument? No. The lower bound \[ \left|r-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\right|\ge \frac{1}{a_{n+1}D_n} \] was applied only in the regime $a_{n+1}>D_n$, where $1/a_{n+1}$ definitely does not itself have denominator dividing $D_n$.
  3. Is the imported theorem applicable with our indexing? Yes. The theorem compares $\mathrm{lcm}(u_1,\dots,u_n)$ to $u_{n+1}$; this matches exactly our $L_n$ and $a_{n+1}$ after the harmless shift from Duverney's $u_0,u_1,\dots$ notation to $a_1,a_2,\dots$.
  4. What class of counterexamples has been ruled out? All sequences with \[ L_n=O(a_{n+1}). \] Any counterexample must have $L_n/a_{n+1}$ unbounded above, so the lcm must sometimes be far larger than the next term.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round-2 sharpens the Round-1 denominator obstruction from \[ a_{n+1}\le 2qL_n \] to the asymptotically sharp first-order statement \[ a_{n+1}\le (1+o(1))qL_n. \] More importantly, after combining this with the classical Erdős--Straus criterion, we obtain a new necessary condition for any genuine counterexample: \[ \frac{L_n}{a_{n+1}} \] cannot stay bounded. So any non-Sylvester rational example would have to lie outside the entire regime $L_n=O(a_{n+1})$.

Erdős Problem #243: independent continuation

Date: 23 September 2026. Source versions read in full: 243.tex in gpt_pro_5.4.

1) OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE AUDIT

Suppose increasing integers $a_n$ satisfy $a_{n+1}/a_n^2\to1$ and $\sum1/a_n\in\mathbb Q$. Must the sequence eventually obey $a_{n+1}=a_n^2-a_n+1$? The supplied file proves a tail asymptotic and invokes an external bounded-lcm theorem. I give a direct arithmetic criterion in terms of reduced tail numerators, without using that theorem.

2) WORK: RECIPROCAL TAIL RIGIDITY

Put $T_n=\sum_{j>n}1/a_j=b_n/c_n$ in lowest positive terms, and $r_n=1/T_n=c_n/b_n$. Write $a=a_{n+1}$, $b=a_{n+2}$ (the temporary $b$ here is a sequence term, not the tail numerator $b_n$). Quadratic growth gives \[ T_n=1/a+1/b+O(b^{-2}),\qquad r_n=a-\frac{a^2}{b}+O(a^{-1}). \] The tail bound follows since eventually each new term is at least half the square of the previous one; the terms after $b$ contribute $O(b^{-2})$. Expanding the reciprocal, with $b\sim a^2$, proves the second formula. In particular $r_n-(a-1)\to0$.

If the reduced numerator $b_n$ is bounded for all sufficiently large $n$, the integer $c_n-b_n(a_{n+1}-1)=b_n(r_n-a_{n+1}+1)$ tends to zero and therefore eventually vanishes. Hence $r_n=a_{n+1}-1$ exactly. The identity \[ T_{n+1}=T_n-1/a_{n+1} \] now gives $r_{n+1}=a_{n+1}(a_{n+1}-1)$. Comparing it with $r_{n+1}=a_{n+2}-1$ proves the desired Sylvester recurrence.

The same proof permits growing numerators under the explicit condition \[ b_n\left(\left|\frac{a_{n+2}}{a_{n+1}^2}-1\right| +\frac1{a_{n+1}}\right)\longrightarrow0. \tag{1} \] Indeed the reciprocal expansion bounds $|r_n-(a_{n+1}-1)|$ by a constant times the expression inside parentheses. Multiplication by $b_n$ again forces the integer difference to be zero eventually.

3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

The assumption that the reciprocal sum is rational makes every $T_n$ rational, but does not bound its reduced numerator. Approaching an integer does not imply eventual equality for rationals with growing denominators. Condition $1$ explicitly supplies that missing discrete separation. Bounded numerator only along a subsequence would not justify the two consecutive exact identities used in the recurrence argument.

7) FINAL AND REFERENCE

UNRESOLVED. A self-contained sufficient condition is proved, along with a quantitative description of what prevents the rounding argument. No argument establishing $1$ from the original hypotheses is available here. The full target was checked in the indexed primary history: https://www.erdosproblems.com/history/243.

Problem #243 (Rational reciprocal sum and Sylvester recurrence)

FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Let $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be a strictly increasing sequence of (nonzero) integers such that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{a_{n-1}^2}=1 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}\in\mathbb{Q}. \] The conjectured conclusion is that there exists $n_0$ such that for all $n\ge n_0$, \[ a_n=a_{n-1}^2-a_{n-1}+1. \]

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

The prompt records the Erdős--Straus $1964$ result: under the same hypotheses, if the recurrence fails infinitely often, then \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{[a_1,\dots,a_n]}{a_{n+1}}\left(\frac{a_n^2}{a_{n+1}}-1\right)>0, \] where $[\cdot]$ denotes least common multiple. Sequences satisfying $a_n=a_{n-1}^2-a_{n-1}+1$ are (a shift of) Sylvester-type sequences.

ATTACK PLAN

Proof track.
  • Use rationality of the total sum to force strong divisibility constraints on denominators of tails.
  • Combine with the near-squaring growth to show the tail is "as small as possible," potentially forcing a deterministic recurrence.
  • Compare with the exact telescoping identity satisfied by the Sylvester recurrence.
Disproof track.
  • Attempt to construct a non-Sylvester sequence with $a_{n+1}=a_n^2+o(a_n^2)$ but with a carefully tuned rational reciprocal sum.
  • Show that any such perturbation would typically change the sum by an irrational amount (hard), or else violates necessary lcm/tail bounds.

WORK

Lemma (Telescoping identity for the Sylvester recurrence): Assume $a_{n+1}=a_n^2-a_n+1$ for all $n\ge 1$ and $a_1\ge 2$. Then for every $n\ge 1$, \[ \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{a_n-1}-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}-1}. \] Consequently, the series converges and \[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{a_1-1}\in\mathbb{Q}. \]
Proof: From $a_{n+1}-1=a_n^2-a_n=a_n(a_n-1)$ we have \[ \frac{1}{a_n-1}-\frac{1}{a_{n+1}-1} =\frac{1}{a_n-1}-\frac{1}{a_n(a_n-1)} =\frac{a_n-1}{a_n(a_n-1)} =\frac{1}{a_n}. \] Summing from $n=1$ to $N$ telescopes: \[ \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{a_n} =\frac{1}{a_1-1}-\frac{1}{a_{N+1}-1}. \] Since $a_{N+1}\to\infty$, the last term tends to $0$ and the sum tends to $1/(a_1-1)$. ∎
Lemma (A general "tail lower bound" from rationality): Let $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be positive integers and suppose \[ S:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{p}{q}\in\mathbb{Q} \qquad(p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\ \gcd(p,q)=1,\ q\ge 1). \] Let $L_n:=\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ and $s_n:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_k}$. Then the tail $t_n:=S-s_n$ satisfies \[ t_n>0\quad\text{and}\quad t_n\ge \frac{1}{qL_n}\qquad\text{for all }n\ge 1. \]
Proof: Positivity: since the $a_n$ are positive and the series converges, $t_n=\sum_{k\ge n+1}1/a_k>0$.

Denominator bound: each $1/a_k$ has denominator dividing $L_n$ for $k\le n$, hence $s_n$ is a rational number with denominator dividing $L_n$. Therefore $t_n=S-s_n$ is a rational number with denominator dividing $qL_n$. Any positive rational with denominator dividing $qL_n$ is at least $1/(qL_n)$. ∎

Lemma (A crude tail upper bound under near-squaring): Assume $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ are positive integers and \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}=1. \] Then for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there exists $n_0$ such that for all $n\ge n_0$, \[ \sum_{k\ge n+1}\frac{1}{a_k}\le \frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\left(1+\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)a_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^3 a_{n+1}^3}+\cdots\right) \le \frac{2}{a_{n+1}}. \]
Proof: Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. By the limit hypothesis there is $n_0$ such that for all $m\ge n_0$, \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6e-65-61-72-2d-73-71-75-61-72-65} a_{m+1}\ge (1-\varepsilon)a_m^2. \end{equation} Iterating (eq:near-square) gives for $j\ge 1$, \[ a_{n+1+j} \ge (1-\varepsilon)^{1+2+\cdots+2^{j-1}}\, a_{n+1}^{2^{j}}. \] (Indeed, each squaring step doubles exponents and multiplies by an extra $(1-\varepsilon)$.) Hence \[ \frac{1}{a_{n+1+j}} \le \frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{1+2+\cdots+2^{j-1}}\, a_{n+1}^{2^{j}}}. \] Therefore \[ \sum_{k\ge n+1}\frac{1}{a_k} =\frac{1}{a_{n+1}}+\sum_{j\ge 1}\frac{1}{a_{n+1+j}} \le \frac{1}{a_{n+1}}\left(1+\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)a_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^3 a_{n+1}^3}+\cdots\right). \] For fixed $\varepsilon$, the bracketed series is dominated by $ 1+\sum_{j\ge 1}\frac{1}{a_{n+1}^{2^j-1}} \le 1+\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{a_{n+1}^{m}}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}-1}, $ once $a_{n+1}$ is large, and in particular is $<2$ for all $a_{n+1}\ge 2$. Thus for all sufficiently large $n$ the total tail is at most $2/a_{n+1}$. ∎
Corollary (A necessary lcm growth condition): Under the hypotheses of Lemmas 7 and 8, writing $S=p/q$ in lowest terms, there exists $n_1$ such that for all $n\ge n_1$, \[ a_{n+1}\le 2q\,\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n). \] In particular, \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(a_1,\dots,a_n)}{a_{n+1}}\ge \frac{1}{2q}>0. \]
Proof: Combine the lower bound $t_n\ge 1/(qL_n)$ from Lemma 7 with the upper bound $t_n\le 2/a_{n+1}$ from Lemma 8, valid for all sufficiently large $n$. ∎

VERIFICATION

  • Lemma 6 is a direct algebraic telescoping identity and is exact.
  • Lemma 7 uses only that $s_n$ has denominator dividing $L_n$ and that a positive rational with denominator $\le qL_n$ is at least $1/(qL_n)$.
  • Lemma 8: the only delicate point is the domination of the tail by a geometric series; the iterated bound shows successive reciprocals decay at least like $a_{n+1}^{-2^j}$ up to a fixed $(1-\varepsilon)$-factor, so the tail is indeed $O(1/a_{n+1})$.

FINAL

UNRESOLVED.
  • (i) Partial results obtained:
    • Proved the exact telescoping identity for the Sylvester recurrence, yielding a rational reciprocal sum.
    • Derived a general necessary condition for rationality under near-squaring growth: the lcm $[a_1,\dots,a_n]$ must be within a constant factor of $a_{n+1}$ infinitely often (Corollary 9).
  • (ii) Strongest obstruction / first gap: Corollary 9 is far weaker than forcing the exact recurrence $a_{n+1}=a_n^2-a_n+1$. Bridging this requires a mechanism turning approximate tail/lcm constraints into an exact deterministic recurrence.
  • (iii) What would likely finish: Strengthening the lcm/tail comparison to an equality-type statement (as happens in Lemma 6) or leveraging the Erdős--Straus limsup criterion to deduce eventual integrality constraints that uniquely select the Sylvester recurrence.
  • (iv) Confidence assessment: High confidence in Lemmas 6--8 and Corollary 9; no claim about the conjectured eventual recurrence.

COMPLETION ESTIMATE
