Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.53

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $m$ denote the rank parameter in the source, and let $F_m$ be the free group on $m$ generators. For a positive integer $n$, define the free Burnside group by \[ B(m,n)=F_m/\langle\!\langle w^n : w\in F_m\rangle\!\rangle, \] where the denominator is the normal subgroup generated by all $n$-th powers. Thus $B(m,n)$ is the free group in the class of groups satisfying $x^n=1$ for every element $x$. Write $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ for the group of bijective homomorphisms from $G$ to itself, with composition as its operation.

(a) Main question. For sufficiently large odd positive integers $n$, describe \[ \operatorname{Aut}(B(m,n)). \] The source does not specify the permitted range of $m$, a lower bound on $n$, whether such a bound must be uniform in $m$, or what form of description is required. These qualifications remain unresolved; the request is a structural description problem rather than a specified proposition to prove.

(b) Archived question. For sufficiently large odd positive integers $n$, is the following assertion true? \[ \forall H\leq B(m,n),\qquad H\text{ noncyclic}\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists K\leq H\text{ such that }K\cong B(2,n). \] Here a group is cyclic if it is generated by one element. The rank range and the meaning of sufficiently large are again unspecified in the original question. The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer to part (b) for odd $n\geq 1003$, attributed to V. S. Atabekyan. This is a supplied report of a solved part, not an independently verified theorem statement or an answer to part (a).

Short English statement

For sufficiently large odd exponents, describe the automorphisms of the free Burnside group $B(m,n)$. A separate archived part asks whether every noncyclic subgroup contains a copy of $B(2,n)$; the supplied record reports that part as solved affirmatively for odd $n\geq 1003$. The rank range, the threshold for the main question, and the intended form of description require clarification.
