Math-1365

Exact identities and rounding loss for induced directed paths

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite simple oriented graph on $n$ vertices. Let $\tau$ count three-vertex sets inducing exactly a directed two-edge path, $\eta$ count three-sets inducing one arc and one isolated vertex, and $\theta$ count cyclic triangles. In particular $\eta$ is not merely the number of arcs; the source's phrase "induced edges" needs this three-vertex interpretation.

Thomassé conjecture. The maximum possible $\tau$ is \[ \frac{n^3}{15}+O(n^2). \] The extracted conjecture number is damaged. The source notes iterated substitution of directed $C_4$ as the lower construction, and records historical bounds attributed to Füredi, $\tau\leq n^3/12+O(n^2)$, and Bondy, $\tau\leq2n^3/25$. These are source-era literature statements, not independently updated assessments.

Reported exact degree identity. With out- and indegrees $d^+(v),d^-(v)$, the aid proves the source's expression is exactly \[ \tau+\frac\eta3+2\theta=\frac{n^3}{12} -\frac16\sum_v\left[(d^+(v)-d^-(v))^2+ (n/2-d^+(v))^2+(n/2-d^-(v))^2\right]. \] Under the stated counting convention there is no asymptotic error in this identity. It does not itself yield the conjectured $1/15$ general upper bound.

Reported balanced recursive construction. Define $B_0,B_1$ trivially. For $n\geq2$, partition into four classes of sizes $n_0,\ldots,n_3$ differing by at most one; put all arcs from class $i$ to $i+1$ modulo four, no arcs between opposite classes, and recursively place $B_{n_i}$ inside each class. If $P(n)$ is its induced directed-path count, the exact recurrence is \[ P(n)=\sum_{i=0}^3P(n_i)+\sum_{i=0}^3n_i n_{i+1}n_{i+2},\qquad P(0)=P(1)=0, \] with indices modulo four. Writing $\Delta(n)=(n^3-n)/15-P(n)$ gives the equivalent exact defect recurrence \[ \Delta(n)=\sum_i\Delta(n_i)+\frac{n^3-\sum_i n_i^3}{15} -\sum_i n_i n_{i+1}n_{i+2}. \] The aid proves, for $n\geq1$, \[ 0\leq\Delta(n)\leq\frac n5\lceil\log_4n\rceil. \] Hence this construction has $n^3/15-O(n\log n)$ copies for every $n$, and exactly $(n^3-n)/15$ when $n$ is a power of four. This is a quantitative lower construction; it does not prove optimality for all oriented graphs.

Appended majority-digraph context. The record then defines a graph using $\beta$ linear orders, with arc $u\to v$ if $u$ precedes $v$ in at least $\alpha$ orders. Its terminal majority condition is corrupted as "$\alpha>\beta>2/3$"; the natural ratio reading is $\alpha/\beta>2/3$ with $1\leq\alpha\leq\beta$, but the missing fraction must be source-checked. This belongs to following context and is not a hypothesis of the induced-path theorem or an additional resolved conjecture.

Short English statement

The aid makes the source's three-vertex degree identity exact and sharpens the rounding analysis of the iterated directed-$C_4$ lower construction. It gives a recurrence and $O(n\log n)$ defect, with exact values at powers of four. The general upper conjecture and the corrupted appended majority-digraph definition remain separate.
