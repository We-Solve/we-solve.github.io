Determinant of a Longest-Increasing-Subsequence Matrix

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $n$, let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $S_n$ denote the permutations of $[n]$, regarded as functions on positions. Define \[ \operatorname{LIS}(\sigma)=\max\left\{k:\text{there exist }1\le i_1<\cdots<i_k\le n\text{ with }\sigma(i_1)<\cdots<\sigma(i_k)\right\}. \] The supplied extract asks that one choose a longest increasing subsequence $S(\sigma)$ for each relevant $\sigma$. It uses a parameter $t$, without specifying its domain or range, and gives the column index set \[ C_{n,t}=\{\sigma\in S_n:\operatorname{LIS}(\sigma)\ge n-t\}. \] Under the possible interpretation that $S(\sigma)$ means the set $I_\sigma\subseteq[n]$ of positions of the chosen subsequence, the stated entry rule would be \[ A=(A_{\pi,\sigma})_{\pi\in S_n,\,\sigma\in C_{n,t}},\qquad A_{\pi,\sigma}=\begin{cases} 1,&\pi(i)=\sigma(i)\text{ for every }i\in[n]\setminus I_\sigma,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \] This interpretation of the subsequence as a set of positions is conditional: the extract does not explicitly distinguish positions from values. It also gives no rule for selecting among multiple longest increasing subsequences and does not state whether the requested quantity should be independent of those choices.

The requested quantity is $\det(A)$. However, the displayed indexing gives $n!$ rows and $|C_{n,t}|$ columns, with no hypothesis ensuring that these numbers agree. Thus the extract does not unambiguously define a square matrix whose determinant can be requested. It also supplies no ordering of the row and column indices for a numerical determinant. The supplied statement-verification note additionally reports inconsistent use of the matrix names $M$ and $A$ in the official problem session. The admissible parameters, intended index sets, subsequence convention, and any necessary ordering or choice conventions must be recovered before a precise determinant problem can be stated. This is an unresolved reconstruction of the supplied question, not a corrected formulation.

Short English statement

The extract asks for the determinant of a matrix defined using chosen longest increasing subsequences of permutations. Its row and column sets need not have equal size, and parameter and subsequence conventions are missing. Consequently, the intended determinant problem remains unrecoverable from the supplied record.
