Math-3706

Convergence and Curvature of Homogeneous Ricci Flows

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record contains two questions, with unresolved choices described below.

For the first question, let $\mathfrak{s}$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space with an inner product, and let $\mu_0\in\Lambda^2\mathfrak{s}^*\otimes\mathfrak{s}$ be a solvable Lie bracket representing a left-invariant Riemannian metric. Write $\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu}$ for the Ricci endomorphism determined by the bracket and inner product. For an endomorphism $A$, put \[ (\pi(A)\mu)(u,v)=A\mu(u,v)-\mu(Au,v)-\mu(u,Av). \] The unnormalized bracket flow is \[ \frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}=-\pi(\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu})\mu, \qquad \mu(0)=\mu_0. \] A Riemannian metric $g$ is a Ricci soliton if there are a smooth vector field $X$ and a constant $\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ \operatorname{Ric}(g)+\tfrac12\mathcal L_Xg=\lambda g. \] Question 1. For every initial metric on a general solvable Lie group, does a suitably normalized bracket flow converge to a bracket representing a Ricci soliton, and is the limit unique?

The supplied source does not specify the normalization, time reparametrization, convergence topology, or equivalence relation for uniqueness. In particular, it does not say whether uniqueness concerns a fixed initial metric or all initial metrics on the same group, or whether limits are identified under isometry, scaling, or changes of basis. It also does not require the limiting bracket to represent the original Lie group rather than a degeneration. Thus this question remains a formulation requiring those specifications; no particular normalization or uniqueness assertion is imposed here.

For the second question, a homogeneous Riemannian metric is one whose isometry group acts transitively. Its scalar curvature is spatially constant. A homogeneous Ricci-flow solution is a family of homogeneous metrics satisfying \[ \frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)). \] Question 2. Suppose a homogeneous space $M$ admits a homogeneous Riemannian metric $h$ with $\operatorname{scal}(h)>0$. Must every homogeneous Ricci-flow solution on $M$ eventually have positive scalar curvature?

For a solution considered on its maximal forward interval $[0,T)$, where $0<T\leq\infty$, a precise possible meaning of "eventually" is \[ \exists\,t_0\in[0,T)\quad\forall\,t\in[t_0,T): \operatorname{scal}(g(t))>0. \] This includes finite maximal existence times. The source does not explicitly prescribe this time convention. It also does not specify whether all metrics must be invariant under one fixed transitive group action on $M=G/H$, or whether their transitive isometry groups may differ. These choices remain unresolved.

Short English statement

For arbitrary solvable Lie groups, ask whether suitably normalized bracket flow converges to a Ricci soliton with a unique limit. Separately, ask whether admitting a homogeneous metric of positive scalar curvature forces every homogeneous Ricci flow on the space eventually to have positive scalar curvature. The normalization, meaning of uniqueness, symmetry convention, and forward-time interpretation require clarification.
