Strong Lefschetz Property of the Zeroth Jordan Component

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks whether there exists an Artinian Gorenstein algebra $A$ such that \[ \operatorname{SL}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad\neg\operatorname{SL}(Q(0)), \] where $\operatorname{SL}$ denotes the strong Lefschetz property and $Q(0)$ is the component designated by the source. This is an existence question, not an asserted example or theorem.

The record does not specify the ground field, its characteristic, whether $A$ is local or graded, or the construction of $Q(0)$. Its edited statement calls the decomposition a "Jordan decomposition," whereas its original statement says only "decomposition." No definition identifying $Q(0)$ is supplied. Consequently, the displayed question preserves the intended logical form but is not yet a fully specified mathematical proposition.

For clarity, one conventional graded definition is as follows; its hypotheses are conditional and are not supplied hypotheses of this problem. Let $K$ be a field and let $B=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}B_i$ be a finite-dimensional commutative graded $K$-algebra with $B_0=K$, generated by $B_1$. Then $B$ has the strong Lefschetz property if there exists $\ell\in B_1$ such that, for every pair of integers $i,d\geq 0$, multiplication induces a map \[ \times\ell^d:B_i\longrightarrow B_{i+d} \] with \[ \operatorname{rank}_K(\times\ell^d)=\min\{\dim_K B_i,\dim_K B_{i+d}\}. \] Under these same hypotheses, failure of this property means that for every $\ell\in B_1$ there exist $i,d\geq 0$ for which the rank is strictly smaller than this minimum. In this connected graded setting, the Gorenstein condition can be expressed by \[ \dim_K\operatorname{Soc}(B)=1,\qquad \operatorname{Soc}(B)=\{b\in B:B_+b=0\},\qquad B_+=\bigoplus_{i>0}B_i. \] These definitions do not determine whether this graded setting is intended, whether $Q(0)$ is an algebra or a module, or which grading and multiplication operators should be used for it. Those choices and the defining decomposition must be recovered from the cited source before a precise specialization of the existence question can be made.

Short English statement

The question asks whether an Artinian Gorenstein algebra can have the strong Lefschetz property while its designated component $Q(0)$ fails that property. The supplied record leaves the decomposition, the ground field, and the local or graded setting unspecified, so the statement requires clarification and human mathematical review.
