Math-424

Is the theory of the field of Laurent series over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ decidable? of the field of polynomials over $\mathbb{C}$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a structure $A$ in a specified effective first-order language, $\operatorname{Th}(A)$ is decidable if an algorithm determines, for every sentence in that language, whether $A$ satisfies it. The conventional field/ring language is $\{0,1,+,-,\cdot\}$; naming a parameter such as $t$, or additional constants or a valuation, changes the formal question.

The first source question concerns "the field of Laurent series over $\mathbb Z_p$". For a coefficient ring $A$, \[ A((t))=\left\{\sum_{n\ge N}a_nt^n:N\in\mathbb Z,\ a_n\in A\right\}. \] If $\mathbb Z_p$ means $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z=\mathbb F_p$ for a prime $p$, this is the field $\mathbb F_p((t))$. If it means the ring of $p$-adic integers, $\mathbb Z_p((t))$ is a ring rather than a field. Replacing its coefficients by $\mathbb Q_p$ would give a different field and is not done here. The notation and quantification over $p$ must therefore be resolved before a single decidability problem is identified.

The second question says "field of polynomials over $\mathbb C$". The polynomial object $\mathbb C[t]$ is not a field; its fraction field is $\mathbb C(t)$. The supplied summary explicitly interprets this part as asking whether \[ \operatorname{Th}(\mathbb C(t))\text{ is decidable}. \] That interpretation is retained conditionally, not equated with decidability of $\mathbb C[t]$ or the Laurent-series field $\mathbb C((t))$.

The summary quotes Scanlon describing the $\mathbb C(t)$ question as long-standing open, mentions an unfinished Pheidas undecidability strategy, and attributes decidability of $\mathbb C((t))$ to Ax--Kochen in connection with R. Robinson's question. It leaves the first part unverified and ends mid-sentence. No exact language or complete bibliographic details are supplied. These source reports do not resolve the notation or establish independently verified current status; primary-source review remains outstanding.

Short English statement

Ask for decidability of the intended Laurent-series theory and, on the summary's reading, of $\mathbb C(t)$. The original wording confuses coefficient-ring notation and polynomial rings with fields, and the language and prime quantifiers must be specified.
