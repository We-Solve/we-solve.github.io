Analysis of Boolean Functions — Uncertainty Principle for Quadratic Fourier Analysis

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer and let ${\mathbb{F}}_2=\{0,1\}$ be the field with two elements. Write $\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$. A polynomial function of degree at most $2$ means a function admitting an expression \[ q(x)=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^n a_jx_j+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}b_{jk}x_jx_k, \qquad a_0,a_j,b_{jk}\in{\mathbb{F}}_2, \] with arithmetic in ${\mathbb{F}}_2$. Its associated real-valued quadratic phase is $x\mapsto(-1)^{q(x)}$.

The source calls its target the indicator of $\mathbf{1}$, but specifies the range $\{-1,1\}$. These descriptions require a convention that the record does not supply. Define the ordinary indicator by \[ D_n(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x=\mathbf{1},\\0,&x\ne\mathbf{1},\end{cases} \] and its two signed encodings by \[ A_{n,\sigma}(x)=\sigma\left(2D_n(x)-1\right),\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}. \] The two signed encodings differ by a global sign, which can be absorbed into the real coefficients below. The ordinary indicator $D_n$ is a distinct target; the source does not unambiguously reconcile its indicator terminology with its specified range.

Problem, under the stated signed-range interpretation. Fix either $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$. Suppose $m\geq1$ is an integer, $q_1,\ldots,q_m:{\mathbb{F}}_2^n\to{\mathbb{F}}_2$ have degree at most $2$, and $c_1,\ldots,c_m\in{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfy the exact pointwise identity \[ A_{n,\sigma}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i(-1)^{q_i(x)} \qquad\text{for every }x\in{\mathbb{F}}_2^n. \] Determine a lower bound on $m$ as a function of $n$, valid for every such representation. No distinctness condition on the $q_i$, or restriction on the real coefficients, is supplied.

The background attributes to Hamed Hatami the conjecture that an exponential lower bound is necessary. In quantified form, this asks whether there exist constants $a>0$ and an integer $n_0\geq1$ such that, for every integer $n\geq n_0$, every representation above satisfies \[ m\geq 2^{an}. \]

As supplied background, the source reports that Hatami can show $m\geq n$, and states that if every $q_i$ has degree at most $1$, then $m=2^n$ is necessary and sufficient, understood as a claim about the minimum number of terms. These are attributed source statements, not independently verified results here. The degree-one comparison requires a boundary qualification at $n=1$ under the signed interpretation; the source supplies none. The record also describes the Constant-Degree Hypothesis of Barrington, Straubing, and Thérien, attributed to 1990 and concerning finite fields, as a similar conjecture. Its formulation is not supplied, so no equivalence is asserted.

Short English statement

How many quadratic phases $(-1)^{q_i(x)}$, with arbitrary real coefficients, are needed to represent exactly the signed Boolean function that singles out $(1,\ldots,1)$? The supplied source attributes to Hatami the conjecture that exponentially many terms are necessary. The indicator convention and a boundary qualification in the linear-phase comparison require review. The registry labels this problem open; that status is supplied, not independently verified.
