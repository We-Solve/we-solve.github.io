Math-14469

Canonical Endpoint Normalizations for Singular Sturm–Liouville Problems

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A fundamental system for a specified scalar second-order linear differential equation is a pair of linearly independent solutions, so that every solution is their linear combination. A normalization prescribes additional conditions selecting such a pair; the source does not specify the conditions that would make a normalization satisfactory.

For terminology only, consider the conventional real scalar Sturm--Liouville setting \[ -(p u')'+q u=z w u \] on an interval $I=(a,b)$, where $p>0$ and $w>0$ almost everywhere, $q$ is real-valued, and $p^{-1},q,w\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)$. For a fixed $z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}$, an endpoint is of limit-circle type if every solution is square-integrable near that endpoint with respect to $w(x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x$; it is of limit-point type if the space of such square-integrable solutions has dimension at most one. These are conditional definitions explaining the terminology, not additional hypotheses supplied by the record: the record does not specify its differential expression, coefficient class, interval, or weight.

Source problem. Formulate satisfactory normalizations of a fundamental system of solutions near endpoints of limit-point or limit-circle type which are not regular singular points. The requested formulation should include examples such as the Lennard--Jones potential \[ q(x)=4\epsilon\left(\left(\frac{\sigma}{x}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{x}\right)^6\right), \qquad \epsilon>0,\quad \sigma>0. \] As a real-variable formula this is defined for $x\ne0$; the source does not specify the interval, the side of approach to an endpoint, or whether the intended differential equation is specifically $-u''+q u=z u$.

The request remains a formulation problem rather than a fully specified existence assertion. In particular, the source gives no precise requirements for uniqueness, endpoint asymptotics, spectral-parameter dependence, or admissible changes of basis, and it does not specify the coefficient regularity or convention underlying the exclusion of regular singular points. These unresolved choices must be supplied before a precise normalization theorem can be stated.

Short English statement

Seek satisfactory ways to normalize a basis of solutions near singular Sturm--Liouville endpoints of limit-point or limit-circle type, including the stated Lennard--Jones example with $\epsilon,\sigma>0$. The equation, domain, and meaning of satisfactory normalization are insufficiently specified for a definitive theorem statement. The supplied open status requires independent review.
