Banach Spaces and Ramsey Theory — Problem 3

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb K$ denote a scalar field, either ${\mathbb{R}}$ or ${\mathbb{C}}$. The supplied record does not specify the scalar field; the formulation below is relative to a fixed choice of $\mathbb K$, and the intended field requires source verification.

A Schauder basis $(x_n)_{n\geq 1}$ of a Banach space $X$ is a sequence such that every $x\in X$ has a unique expansion $x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nx_n$ converging in norm, with $a_n\in\mathbb K$. Two basic sequences $(u_n)$ and $(v_n)$ are equivalent if there exist constants $0<A\leq B<\infty$ such that, for every $m\geq 1$ and every $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in\mathbb K$, \[ A\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_nu_n\right\| \leq \left\|\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_nv_n\right\| \leq B\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_nu_n\right\|. \] A normalized block sequence of $(x_n)$ is a sequence $(y_k)_{k\geq 1}$ of the form \[ y_k=\sum_{n=r_k}^{s_k}b_n^{(k)}x_n, \qquad 1\leq r_k\leq s_k<r_{k+1}, \qquad \|y_k\|=1, \] where all coefficients belong to $\mathbb K$. Use the standard block-sequence interpretation of a Rosenthal basis: every normalized block sequence $(y_k)$ has a subsequence $(y_{k_j})_{j\geq 1}$ equivalent to $(x_j)_{j\geq 1}$. The supplied record uses the term without defining it, so this interpretation requires confirmation against the cited source.

Define \[ c_0(\mathbb K)=\{(a_n)_{n\geq1}:a_n\in\mathbb K,\ a_n\to0\}, \qquad \|(a_n)\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n\geq1}|a_n|, \] and, for $1\leq p<\infty$, \[ \ell_p(\mathbb K)=\left\{(a_n)_{n\geq1}:a_n\in\mathbb K,\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n|^p<\infty\right\}, \qquad \|(a_n)\|_p=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n|^p\right)^{1/p}. \] Their standard unit vectors are $e_n=(\delta_{nj})_{j\geq1}$.

Rosenthal basis problem (H. P. Rosenthal; supplied reference label [FPR]). Is it true that, for every Banach space $X$ over the chosen field $\mathbb K$ and every Rosenthal basis $(x_n)$ of $X$, either $(x_n)$ is equivalent to the standard unit vector basis of $c_0(\mathbb K)$, or there exists a real number $p$ with $1\leq p<\infty$ such that $(x_n)$ is equivalent to the standard unit vector basis of $\ell_p(\mathbb K)$? The choice of alternative and, when applicable, $p$, may depend on the basis.

Short English statement

Must every Rosenthal basis be equivalent to the usual unit vectors of $c_0$ or of $\ell_p$ for some $1\leq p<\infty$? Here the Rosenthal property means that every normalized block sequence contains a subsequence equivalent to the original basis. The source record leaves this definition and the scalar field implicit; both require confirmation. The registry labels the problem open, without independent verification in this draft.
