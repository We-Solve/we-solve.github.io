A strongly regular graph with parameters $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$ is a simple $k$-regular graph on $v$ vertices in which adjacent pairs have $\lambda$ common neighbours and nonadjacent pairs have $\mu$. A conference graph has parameters $(v,(v-1)/2,(v-5)/4,(v-1)/4)$. The proposed existence is for every $v>1$ satisfying $v\equiv1\pmod4$ and $v=a^2+b^2$ with integers $a,b$. [S1] [S2]

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does there exist a conference graph for every number of vertices $v > 1$ where $v \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ and v is an odd sum of two squares? [S1]
Residual task reported by the archive.

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Either construct conference graphs at all arithmetically admissible orders or identify an admissible order for which they cannot exist. [S1]

Short English statement

Do conference graphs exist for every order meeting the stated congruence and sum-of-two-squares conditions?
