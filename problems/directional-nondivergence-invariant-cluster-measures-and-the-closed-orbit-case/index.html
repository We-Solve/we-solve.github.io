Directional nondivergence, invariant cluster measures, and the closed-orbit case

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 50 (B. Weiss). Let $X=G/\Gamma$, let $A$ be the torus appearing in the missing preceding context, with $\dim A=r\ge2$, and take $x\in X$. Suppose that for every nontrivial one-parameter subgroup $D\le A$, the orbit $Dx$ is not divergent. Does there exist a compact subset $K\subset X$ such that\[\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\lambda(\{a\in A_T:ax\in K\})}{\lambda(A_T)}>0?\] Here $\lambda$ is Haar measure on $A$. The excerpt omits the preceding definition of $A_T$ and the precise ambient-group hypotheses. The aid uses the norm-ball interpretation: identify the connected real split torus $A$ with $({\mathbb{R}}^r,+)$ by its logarithm, fix a norm, and set $A_T=\{\exp(v):\|v\|\le T\}$. Divergence of a one-parameter orbit means properness of its orbit map, equivalently eventual departure from every compact set as the parameter leaves compact subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}$. Nondivergence is not the assertion that the whole orbit is relatively compact. These conventions are explicit reconstructions, pending the missing source context.

The attached aid supplies three boundary statements, rather than a solution in the general homogeneous setting. For a continuous $A\cong{\mathbb{R}}^r$ action on a locally compact second-countable Hausdorff space, put\[\eta_T=\frac1{\lambda(A_T)}\int_{A_T}\delta_{ax}\,d\lambda(a).\] The existence of a compact $K$ with positive upper return proportion is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero vague subsequential limit of $\eta_T$, where vague convergence tests $C_c(X)$. Every such limit is an $A$-invariant finite Radon measure with total mass at most one. Nonzero mass does not assert mass one or full equidistribution. In the homogeneous case with $\Gamma$ discrete, the stabilizer $H=\{a\in A:ax=x\}$ is discrete. If $Ax$ is closed, so that the orbit is identified with $A/H$, nondivergence of every one-parameter subgroup forces $H$ to have rank $r$; hence $Ax$ is compact and choosing $K=Ax$ gives return proportion one. Finally, the aid reports a nonhomogeneous translation-hull example of a locally compact ${\mathbb{R}}^2$-action and a point for which every line returns at unbounded parameter values to one common compact target, although every compact target has norm-ball return proportion tending to zero. The actual hull construction is not supplied; this example is a reported obstruction to inferring density from purely directional recurrence in arbitrary actions, not a homogeneous counterexample to Weiss's question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether nondivergence in every one-dimensional direction forces a positive fraction of a large torus ball to return to some compact set. The aid establishes the closed-orbit case and characterizes the desired conclusion by nonzero invariant vague limits. Its general-action obstruction does not answer the homogeneous question. Appended Shimura-variety background belongs to the next section.
