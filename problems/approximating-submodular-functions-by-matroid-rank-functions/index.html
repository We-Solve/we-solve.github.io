Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Is there a universal constant $\alpha$ such that every normalized nonnegative monotone submodular function $f:2^V\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ with $f(\{i\})=1$ for all $i\in V$ admits a matroid rank function $r$ satisfying $r(S)\leq f(S)\leq\alpha r(S)$ for every $S\subseteq V$?

The two formulations and the result

The preserved catalogue requires $r(S)\le f(S)\le\alpha r(S)$. The primary report, printed page 2946, instead displays \[ \frac{r(S)}\alpha\le f(S)\le\alpha r(S)\qquad(S\subseteq V). \] The missing denominator was already flagged in the preserved definition. The primary PDF page was inspected visually as well as through its text extraction. Both displays refer to an ordinary, unweighted matroid rank on the same ground set. The following elementary counterexample disproves existence of a universal constant in either display. It makes no novelty claim and does not purport to settle any different intended formulation involving a rescaled, weighted, or otherwise transformed rank function.
The counterexample family.
For each integer $n\ge1$, let $V$ be an $n$-element set and define $f(S)=\sqrt{|S|}$ for every $S\subseteq V$. It is nonnegative, normalized at the empty set, monotone under inclusion, and has $f(\{i\})=1$. Here is a direct proof of submodularity, including intersecting sets. Set $d_j=\sqrt{j+1}-\sqrt j=1/(\sqrt{j+1}+\sqrt j)$ for $j\ge0$. This is positive and decreases with $j$. Given $A,B$, let $a=|A\cap B|$, $b=|A\setminus B|$, and $c=|B\setminus A|$. Then \[ \sqrt{a+b}-\sqrt a=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}d_{a+j} \ge\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}d_{a+c+j} =\sqrt{a+b+c}-\sqrt{a+c}. \] For $b=0$ both sums are empty and equality holds. Rearranging is precisely $f(A)+f(B)\ge f(A\cap B)+f(A\cup B)$. Thus every member of this family satisfies every function hypothesis in both formulations.
Disproof of the primary two-sided version.
Suppose a matroid $M$ on $V$, with rank function $r$, satisfies the primary display for some $\alpha>0$. Every singleton has $1=f(\{i\})\le\alpha r(\{i\})$, so it is not a loop: its integer rank is one. In particular $R=r(V)>0$, and the singleton inequality implies $\alpha\ge1$. By the definition of rank as maximum independent-set cardinality, there is an independent set $B$ with $|B|=R$ and $r(B)=R$. Applying the lower approximation inequality to $B$ gives \[ \frac R\alpha\le\sqrt R, \quad\hbox{hence}\quad R\le\alpha^2. \] Applying the upper inequality to $V$ gives $\sqrt n\le\alpha R\le\alpha^3$. Consequently \[ \alpha\ge n^{1/6}. \] For any proposed finite universal constant $\alpha>0$, choose an integer $n>\alpha^6$. The preceding inequalities are then impossible for every matroid on this ground set. This negates the complete quantifier "there exists a constant for every function" in the primary displayed question. The argument chooses a basis of the matroid proposed by the approximation, so it does not assume that this matroid is uniform or symmetric.
Disproof of the catalogue's stronger version.
If the catalogue's lower inequality $r(S)\le f(S)$ is required, the same basis gives $R\le\sqrt R$. Since $R$ is a positive integer this forces $R=1$. The upper inequality on $V$ then requires $\alpha\ge\sqrt n$. Conversely the uniform rank-one matroid, in which the independent sets have size at most one, satisfies $r(S)=1$ for every nonempty $S$, so $r(S)\le\sqrt{|S|}\le\sqrt n\,r(S)$ for all sets, including the empty set by equality. Thus $\sqrt n$ is exactly the least factor for this example in the catalogue formulation.
A sharpness check for the primary lower-bound mechanism.
For any $1\le R\le n$, the uniform matroid of rank $R$ has rank $r(S)=\min\{|S|,R\}$. Its independent sets are all sets of size at most $R$: heredity is immediate, and if two independent sets have different sizes, any element of the larger not in the smaller can be added to the smaller without exceeding $R$, proving the exchange axiom. For a nonempty set of size $s$, the maximum of $r(S)/\sqrt s$ occurs at $s=R$ and equals $\sqrt R$: for $s\le R$ it is $\sqrt s$, and for $s\ge R$ it is $R/\sqrt s$. The maximum of $\sqrt s/r(S)$ is $\max\{1,\sqrt n/R\}$. Hence this matroid satisfies the primary inequalities with \[ \alpha_R=\max\{\sqrt R,\sqrt n/R\}. \] Every other rank-$R$ matroid has factor at least this large by the basis and ground-set arguments just proved. Therefore the exact best factor for this family is $\min_{1\le R\le n}\max\{\sqrt R,\sqrt n/R\}$. Taking $R=\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil$ gives a factor at most $\sqrt2\,n^{1/6}$, because $R\le2n^{1/3}$ and $\sqrt n/R\le n^{1/6}$. This confirms that the divergent lower bound is a real rank-approximation obstruction, not an artifact of a needlessly weak estimate. No sharp constant is needed for the negative answer.
