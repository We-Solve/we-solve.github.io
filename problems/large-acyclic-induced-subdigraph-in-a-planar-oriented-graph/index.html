Math-8519

Large acyclic induced subdigraph in a planar oriented graph.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D=(V,A)$ be a finite oriented graph whose underlying simple undirected graph is planar. Thus there are no loops, repeated arcs, or opposite pairs of arcs. Put $n=|V|$. For $U\subseteq V$, the induced subdigraph $D[U]$ contains all arcs of $D$ having both ends in $U$. It is acyclic when it has no directed cycle, equivalently when its vertices admit a linear ordering in which every arc points forward.

The conjecture asks whether one can always find $U\subseteq V$ with \[ |U|\ge\frac35n \quad\text{and}\quad D[U]\text{ acyclic}. \] Since $|U|$ is an integer, the size condition means $|U|\ge\lceil3n/5\rceil$. The equivalent deletion formulation asks for a set $S\subseteq V$ of at most $\lfloor2n/5\rfloor$ vertices meeting every directed cycle; then $U=V\setminus S$. Such an $S$ is a directed feedback vertex set.

Deleting arcs is not allowed when testing whether $D[U]$ is acyclic: all arcs between retained vertices remain. On the other hand, undirected cycles are allowed if their orientations do not form directed cycles. This distinguishes the target from an induced-forest problem for the underlying graph. It also asks for one large acyclic set, not a partition of all vertices into a fixed number of acyclic classes. The graph may be disconnected, and the orientation is arbitrary subject to planarity of the underlying graph.

Short English statement

Can at least three fifths of the vertices of every planar oriented graph be retained without retaining any directed cycle?
