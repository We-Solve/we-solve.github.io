Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.36

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the first-order language of groups $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$. A group quasi-identity is a sentence of the form \[ \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}u_i(\mathbf x)=v_i(\mathbf x)\;\Longrightarrow\;u(\mathbf x)=v(\mathbf x)\right), \] where all displayed terms are group terms and $m\geq 0$, with the empty conjunction interpreted as true. For a finite group $F$, let $\mathcal Q(F)$ be the quasivariety generated by $F$, namely the class of all groups satisfying every group quasi-identity valid in $F$.

For a group $G$ and $a\in G$, define \[ a^G=\{g^{-1}ag:g\in G\},\qquad \langle a^G\rangle=\langle g^{-1}ag:g\in G\rangle. \] Thus $\langle a^G\rangle$ is the smallest normal subgroup of $G$ containing $a$. For any class $M$ of groups, put \[ L(M)=\{G:\text{for every }a\in G,\ \langle a^G\rangle\in M\}. \] Question. For every finite group $F$, is $L(\mathcal Q(F))$ finitely axiomatizable?

Under the usual first-order interpretation of finite axiomatizability, this asks whether, for every finite group $F$, there exists a finite set $\Sigma_F$ of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}$-sentences such that, for every group $G$, \[ G\models\Sigma_F \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall a\in G\;\left(\langle a^G\rangle\in\mathcal Q(F)\right). \] The finite set may depend on $F$, and $G$ is not assumed finite. The supplied source does not specify whether the axioms must instead be quasi-identities. Under that more restrictive reading, the same question requires $\Sigma_F$ to consist of quasi-identities; this qualification remains unresolved.

Short English statement

For each finite group $F$, consider all groups in which the normal closure of every element satisfies every quasi-identity valid in $F$. Must this class admit a finite axiomatization? The source leaves the permitted form of the axioms unspecified.
