Minimum Volume of Bodies with Unit Width

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d$ be a positive integer, and equip ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ with its Euclidean inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. The source does not explicitly specify the range of the dimension parameter. Write \[ S^{d-1}=\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\|u\|_2=1\}. \] For a nonempty convex set $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$, define its directional width by \[ w_K(u)=\sup\{\langle x-y,u\rangle:x,y\in K\}\in[0,+\infty],\qquad u\in S^{d-1}. \] For compact $K$, this is the distance between the two supporting hyperplanes perpendicular to $u$. Let $\operatorname{vol}_d(K)$ denote its $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Define \[ \mathcal C_d=\{K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d:K\ne\varnothing,\ K\text{ is convex, and }w_K(u)\ge1\text{ for every }u\in S^{d-1}\}. \] The problem asks for the minimum volume in this class. Its numerical optimization target is \[ m_d=\inf_{K\in\mathcal C_d}\operatorname{vol}_d(K). \] Determine $m_d$ as a function of $d$. The source uses the word "minimum"; the infimum notation here does not assert that it is attained. The condition requires width at least $1$ in every direction, not constant width $1$.

The supplied wording says "all convex sets" and does not impose compactness, closedness, or boundedness. The definition above follows that literal wording and allows infinite widths and volumes. Whether the intended formulation instead restricts to convex bodies, meaning compact convex sets with nonempty interior, requires source confirmation.

The supplied dated literature assessment states that the planar problem is solved by the equilateral triangle and that the exact higher-dimensional problem remains open. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

For each positive integer dimension $d$, determine the least possible $d$-dimensional volume of a convex set whose width is at least $1$ in every direction. The source leaves compactness and attainment implicit. Its supplied dated assessment describes the planar case as solved and the higher-dimensional problem as open.
