Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 8

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The Continuum Hypothesis is the assertion \[ \mathrm{CH}:\quad 2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1. \] For families $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal D$ of collections of subsets of a set $X$, the selection principle $\mathsf S_1(\mathcal A,\mathcal D)$ means that for every sequence $(\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of members of $\mathcal A$, there are choices $U_n\in\mathcal U_n$ such that $\{U_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\in\mathcal D$. The principle $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal A,\mathcal D)$ means that for every such sequence there are finite subfamilies $\mathcal F_n\subseteq\mathcal U_n$ such that $\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal F_n\in\mathcal D$. These abstract definitions do not specify the particular families denoted by $\mathrm B$, $\mathrm B_\Gamma$, and $\mathrm B_\Omega$ in the source; their definitions and cover conventions are absent from the supplied record.

The question attributed to Tsaban (2006), Problem 8, is whether $\mathrm{CH}$ implies that each of Settings $(d),(e),(g),(j),(l)$ can be realized. Its logical form can only be recorded schematically as \[ \mathrm{CH}\ \Longrightarrow\ \bigwedge_{s\in\{d,e,g,j,l\}}\exists X_s\;\operatorname{Realizes}(X_s,s). \] Here $\operatorname{Realizes}$ is an unresolved placeholder: the supplied extraction does not define any of the named settings or the domain of their realizations. Thus this display records the requested separate realizations, but is not a complete mathematical formulation. In particular, it does not require one object to realize all five settings simultaneously.

The source supplies the following context under $\mathrm{CH}$. It reports realizations of Settings $(c),(h),(i)$ in [coc2], of Setting $(k)$ in [o-bdd], and of Setting $(n)$ in [Rec94, Brendle96, MilNonGamma]. For Setting $(b)$, it proposes taking $L$ as in Setting $(c)$, $S$ as in Setting $(i)$, and $X=L\cup S$. It states that additivity of $\mathsf S_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm B,\mathrm B)$ gives this property for $X$, whereas heredity for subsets of $\mathsf S_1(\mathrm B_\Gamma,\mathrm B_\Omega)$ and $\mathsf S_1(\mathrm B,\mathrm B)$, cited to [ideals], shows that $X$ satisfies neither of these latter properties. The missing definitions of Settings $(c)$ and $(i)$ prevent reconstruction of the hypotheses on $L$ and $S$. Finally, the source tentatively suggests that forcing arguments similar to those of [Brendle96] could realize Settings $(f)$ and $(m)$; this suggestion is not presented as an established realization.

Short English statement

Assuming the Continuum Hypothesis, must each of the five configurations labeled $(d),(e),(g),(j),(l)$ have a realization?
