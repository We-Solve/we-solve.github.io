Finite-Area Infinite-Genus Surfaces with Lattice Veech Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A translation structure on a connected oriented surface is a Euclidean coordinate atlas on its regular locus whose transition maps are translations $z\mapsto z+c$. It induces a flat area measure, denoted by $\operatorname{Area}(S)$. The surface has infinite genus if, for every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$, it contains a compact connected subsurface of genus at least $n$.

Using the orientation-preserving convention, let $\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(S)$ be the group of self-homeomorphisms preserving the translation structure affinely: in translation coordinates each map has the form $z\mapsto Az+b$, with a constant matrix $A\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$. Its derivative group is the Veech group \[ \Gamma(S)=\{Df:f\in\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(S)\}. \] When $0<\operatorname{Area}(S)<\infty$, area scaling implies $\det Df=1$, so $\Gamma(S)\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$. A subgroup $\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ is a lattice if it is discrete and $\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})/\Gamma$ has finite invariant Haar volume.

Existence question. Does there exist a translation surface $S$ such that \[ \operatorname{genus}(S)=\infty,\qquad 0<\operatorname{Area}(S)<\infty,\qquad \Gamma(S)\text{ is a lattice in }\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})? \]

Unresolved source qualifications. The supplied record does not specify the permitted singularities, the treatment of points in the metric completion, completeness or boundary conventions, or the admissible affine maps at singularities. These choices must be checked against the cited report before the class of admissible surfaces is fixed precisely. The definition above states the orientation-preserving convention explicitly; that convention also requires source confirmation. The supplied background singles out Jacob's ladder as a particular topology of interest, but the original and cleaned questions impose only infinite genus and finite area. Accordingly, no Jacob's ladder topology is imposed in the displayed question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a translation surface can have infinite genus and finite positive flat area while its Veech group is discrete and has finite covolume in $\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$. The supplied background also highlights Jacob's ladder. Conventions concerning singularities and completion remain unspecified and require mathematical review.
