Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The source model is the self-dual noncommutative $\phi^4_4$ theory in its planar sector. Its renormalized connected two-point function is expanded formally in the coupling $\lambda$, $G_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda^nG^{(n)}_{\alpha\beta}$, with $0\le\alpha,\beta<1$. Write $A=(1-\alpha)/(1-\alpha\beta)$ and $B=(1-\beta)/(1-\alpha\beta)$. Rooted trees index nested integrals arising in the source's integral-equation recursion. The first examples are $I(z)=\int_0^1 z(1-zx)^{-1}\,dx=-\log(1-z)$ and $I_\bullet(z)=\int_0^1 zI(x)(1-zx)^{-1}\,dx$. Polynomiality at every order means that each coefficient $G^{(n)}$ lies in the algebra over $\mathbb Q$ generated by the specified variables and source iterated integrals.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source supplies the first integral examples and checks low orders; it does not give a complete recursive definition for every allowed tree integral in this brief passage. Any all-order proof must fix that source family, including permitted endpoint constants, rather than allow arbitrary integrals that trivialize the assertion. The same conjecture recurs at ID 30001596.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
At every perturbative order, is the renormalized planar connected two-point function $G_{\alpha,\beta}$ a polynomial with rational coefficients in $\alpha$, $\beta$, $A$, $B$, and iterated integrals indexed by rooted trees?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The cited work derives the Schwinger-Dyson equation and verifies the rooted-tree form only through low perturbative orders; the all-order assertion remains a conjecture. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

An exact first nested integral.
For $0\leq z<1$, the source gives $I(z)=-\log(1-z)$ and \[ I_\bullet(z)=\int_0^1\frac{z[-\log(1-x)]}{1-zx}\,dx. \] Expanding the nonnegative geometric series and integrating termwise gives $I_\bullet(z)=\sum_{m\geq1}H_mz^m/m$, where $H_m=\sum_{k=1}^m1/k$. To verify the coefficient, write $-\log(1-x)=\sum_{k\geq1}x^k/k$ and use $\sum_{k\geq1}1/[k(k+m)]=H_m/m$, a telescoping difference.
Reduction to familiar functions.
Define $\operatorname{Li}_2(z)=\sum_{m\geq1}z^m/m^2$. Differentiating convergent series on compact subsets of $[0,1)$ gives \[ \frac d{dz}\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{H_m}{m}z^m =\frac{-\log(1-z)}{z(1-z)}. \] The same derivative is obtained from $\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\tfrac12\log^2(1-z)$, and both vanish at zero. Thus $I_\bullet(z)=\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\tfrac12\log^2(1-z)$. The logarithmic divergence at $z\uparrow1$ shows that endpoint substitutions need explicit renormalized constants rather than unqualified evaluation.
All-order obstruction.
This calculation verifies a supplied generator, not an all-order coefficient of $G_{\alpha\beta}$. The exact permitted rooted-tree family and the Schwinger--Dyson recursion must be fixed before proving closure under multiplication, subtraction and endpoint operations. Allowing every new integral as a new generator would trivialize the conjecture. That closure proof is the first gap, and the repeated target at 30001596 receives no new resolution from this low-order identity.
