Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.19

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be a nonempty finite set and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ act transitively. Write \[ \binom{\Omega}{2}=\{\{\alpha,\beta\}:\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\ \alpha\ne\beta\}. \] The induced action on this set is $g\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{g\alpha,g\beta\}$. For an orbit $O\subseteq\binom{\Omega}{2}$, a proper block of imprimitivity is a nonempty subset $B\subsetneq O$ such that, for every $g\in G$, either $gB=B$ or $gB\cap B=\varnothing$. In particular, singleton blocks are included. Define the undirected graph \[ \Gamma_{O,B}=(\Omega,O\setminus B). \] The group $G$, in its specified action on $\Omega$, has the road closure property if $\Gamma_{O,B}$ is connected for every such orbit $O$ and every such proper block $B$. Connectivity means that every two vertices can be joined by a finite path; paths of length zero are allowed. No lower bound on $|\Omega|$ is supplied. If there are no applicable orbits or proper blocks, the defining universal condition is vacuous.

A transitive action is primitive if its only invariant partitions are the partition into singletons and the one-part partition. Following the source's description, call the action basic if it is not contained, up to identification of the underlying sets, in a wreath product in a nontrivial product action. Explicitly, this excludes an identification $\Omega\cong\Delta^k$, with $|\Delta|\geq2$ and $k\geq2$, under which $G$ is contained in $\operatorname{Sym}(\Delta)\wr\operatorname{Sym}(k)$ acting by coordinate permutations and independent permutations within coordinates.

The classification problem is to determine all finite basic primitive permutation groups $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ for which there exist an orbit $O$ on $\binom{\Omega}{2}$ and a nonempty proper block $B$ for the action on $O$ such that \[ (\Omega,O\setminus B)\quad\text{is disconnected}. \]

The source further asks whether every group in this classification either has a subgroup of index at most $3$ or is almost simple. Here almost simple means that, for some finite nonabelian simple group $S$, one has $S\leq G\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)$, up to group isomorphism. The subgroup clause has an unresolved qualification: as literally written, $\exists H\leq G$ with $[G:H]\leq3$ allows $H=G$, so it is automatic. If a proper subgroup is intended, the substantive question is whether every group in the classification satisfies \[ \left(\exists H<G:\ [G:H]\in\{2,3\}\right) \quad\text{or}\quad \left(G\text{ is almost simple}\right). \] Properness is not stated in the supplied record, so this latter formulation is recorded as a possible intended reading, not an unqualified replacement.

As background, the source states that a group with the road closure property must be primitive and basic and cannot have an imprimitive subgroup of index $2$. It also excludes permutation groups arising from triality whose socle is denoted $D_4(q)$ and whose intersection with a point stabiliser is the parabolic subgroup corresponding to the three leaves of the Coxeter--Dynkin diagram for $D_4$. These are supplied background assertions, not results established here. The record does not specify the parameter restrictions or a complete construction of this triality family; the parabolic description is retained without choosing an additional indexing convention.

Short English statement

Classify the finite basic primitive permutation groups for which deleting a proper block from some orbit on unordered pairs leaves a disconnected graph on the original point set. The source also asks whether every such group has a subgroup of index at most $3$ or is almost simple; whether the subgroup must be proper is unresolved. The supplied registry labels the problem open, without independent verification here.
