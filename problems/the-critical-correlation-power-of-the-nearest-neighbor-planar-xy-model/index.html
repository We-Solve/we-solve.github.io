The critical correlation power of the nearest-neighbor planar XY model

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: At the ordinary XY critical point, the proposed infinite-volume two-point law has exponent $1/4$, with the free-box limit taken first; The critical XY correlation is claimed to be $B r^{-1/4}(\log r)^{1/8}(1+o(1))$ with $B>0$. Together they give a claimed affirmative answer in the matching scope.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For angles $\theta_x\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ on $\Lambda_R=[-R,R]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2$, use the free-boundary cosine law proportional to $\exp[b\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_R,\,|x-y|=1}\cos(\theta_x-\theta_y)]$, relative to product uniform angles. Count each edge once and omit exterior edges. Define \[ C_b(r)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb E_{R,b}^{\mathrm{free}}\cos(\theta_0-\theta_{re_1}),\quad m(b)=\lim_{r\to\infty}-r^{-1}\log C_b(r),\quad b_c=\inf\{b>0:m(b)=0\}. \] The thermodynamic limit precedes the separation limit. At the critical point, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log C_{b_c}(n)}{\log n}=-\frac14. \] Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large integers $n$, $n^{-1/4-\varepsilon}\le C_{b_c}(n)\le n^{-1/4+\varepsilon}$. This paper determines the power exponent; a separate selected manuscript supplies the logarithmic factor.
Result 2.
For angles $\theta_x\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ on $\Lambda_R=[-R,R]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2$, use the free-boundary cosine law proportional to $\exp[b\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_R,\,|x-y|=1}\cos(\theta_x-\theta_y)]$, relative to product uniform angles. Count each edge once and omit exterior edges. Define \[ C_b(r)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb E_{R,b}^{\mathrm{free}}\cos(\theta_0-\theta_{re_1}),\quad m(b)=\lim_{r\to\infty}-r^{-1}\log C_b(r),\quad b_c=\inf\{b>0:m(b)=0\}. \] The thermodynamic limit precedes the separation limit. There exists a finite positive $B_{\mathrm{XY}}$ satisfying \[ C_{b_c}(r)=B_{\mathrm{XY}}r^{-1/4}(\log r)^{1/8}(1+o(1))\quad(r\to\infty\text{ through positive integers}). \] This fixes the logarithmic multiplier as well as the critical power.

Outline of the argument

The dual Bessel-height model identifies the physical mass threshold and its critical stiffness. Multiscale height estimates, combined with the exact spin--height correspondence, translate that stiffness into the logarithmic slope of the spin correlation.

The companion critical-height input fixes the leading stiffness, and an analytic activity map tracks its marginal running correction. Summing the renormalization increments gives the logarithmic factor and controls the remaining amplitude in the thermodynamic two-point function.
