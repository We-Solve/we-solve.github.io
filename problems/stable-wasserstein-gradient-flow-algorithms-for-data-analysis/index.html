Stable Wasserstein Gradient Flow Algorithms for Data Analysis

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For terminology, let $(X,d)$ be a complete separable metric space, let $p\in[1,\infty)$, and fix $x_0\in X$. Define the space of Borel probability measures with finite $p$-th moment by \[ \mathcal P_p(X)=\left\{\mu:\int_X d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm d\mu(x)<\infty\right\}. \] For $\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_p(X)$, let $\Pi(\mu,\nu)$ denote their probability couplings and define \[ W_p(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/p}. \] These definitions provide a possible framework for the terminology; the source does not specify $X$, $d$, or $p$, and does not impose the hypotheses above as part of its question. A Wasserstein gradient-flow formulation would require a specified energy on a specified space of measures and a specified notion of gradient flow with respect to the chosen Wasserstein metric. None of these choices is supplied.

Research question. Which clustering or classification algorithms admit a formulation as Wasserstein gradient flows, and can one design such algorithms that remain stable as the sample size $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ tends to infinity?

Short English statement

Identify clustering or classification algorithms that can be represented as Wasserstein gradient flows, and investigate designs whose stability persists as the sample size $n\to\infty$. The source leaves the Wasserstein setting, algorithmic representation, sampling assumptions, and meaning of stability unspecified.
