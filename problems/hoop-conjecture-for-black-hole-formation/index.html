Definitions and conventions.
Use geometrized units $G=c=1$, so a mass $M$ has units of length. An event horizon is the boundary separating points that can send causal signals to future null infinity from the black-hole region. The hoop conjecture proposes a relation between compactness, a notion of mass, and circumferences in all directions. In time-symmetric initial data an apparent horizon corresponds to an appropriate closed minimal surface, which is a more specific geometric setting discussed in the report.
Source scope and qualifications.
The primary report explicitly says that mass and circumference are not clearly defined in this formulation. Event and apparent horizons are different notions. No arbitrary definition of a hoop, quasi-local mass, or horizon is imposed here, and the time-symmetric results do not resolve every interpretation.

The spherical equality check.
On the time-symmetric Schwarzschild exterior of mass $M>0$, use areal radius $r>2M$ and metric \[ g=(1-2M/r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2. \] The outward unit normal to a coordinate sphere is $\nu=\sqrt{1-2M/r}\,\partial_r$. Its area is $4\pi r^2$, so its mean curvature, the normal logarithmic area derivative, is \[ H=\nu\left(\log(4\pi r^2)\right)=\frac2r\sqrt{1-2M/r}. \] It tends to zero at $r=2M$. In the smooth doubled spatial geometry this limiting sphere is the throat minimal surface. Every great circle there has length $2\pi r=4\pi M$, reproducing the proposed constant in this symmetric apparent-horizon example.
Why circumference alone does not generalize.
The calculation uses the special facts that all great-circle circumferences agree and that $r$ is geometrically fixed by area. Away from symmetry one cannot replace this by an arbitrary coordinate circumference. Even a fixed round sphere has closed curves of arbitrarily large length by repeated winding, so "every direction" cannot mean "every closed curve". Nor does the local equation $H=0$ identify an event horizon, which uses the entire spacetime future.
Remaining gap.
This is a consistency test of the normalization, not an if-and-only-if formation theorem. The first unresolved issue is the source's own missing definitions: which quasi-local mass, which family of hoops, and which horizon notion are intended. After fixing them, both necessity and sufficiency for nonspherical dynamical data still require proof. At $M=0$ the displayed exterior becomes Euclidean space and has no positive-radius coordinate minimal sphere, consistent with the degenerate limit.
