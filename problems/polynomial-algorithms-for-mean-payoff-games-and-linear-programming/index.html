CS-114: Polynomial Algorithms for Mean-Payoff Games and Linear Programming

prb_fcffd5b9cd1f417a9736ac25936b387f: Polynomial Algorithms for Mean-Payoff Games and Linear Programming

Claim type: **Partial result**.

Exact scope: One-player finite mean-payoff threshold games with arbitrary binary signed rewards and binary rational thresholds are decidable deterministically in polynomial bit time, including equality at the threshold, via a strict positive-cycle test.

Fix a directed finite graph with at least one outgoing edge at each vertex, a starting vertex s, and one controller who chooses every move and maximizes liminf average reward. Edge rewards w_e are signed binary integers. Let the rational threshold be r=p/q with q>0. Remove vertices not reachable from s, and let N be the remaining vertex count. Every finite walk decomposes into simple directed cycles plus a residual path of fewer than N edges.

There exists an infinite play with liminf average at least r if and only if a reachable simple cycle has mean at least r. One direction follows by taking a path to that cycle and repeating it: the finite prefix disappears in the average. Conversely, if all simple cycles have mean below r, their integer transformed sums q sum w-p length are at most -1 and their lengths are at most N. Thus each cycle's original mean is at most r-1/(qN). Deleting simple cycles from a finite walk leaves fewer than N residual edges; their total reward is bounded independently of the walk length. Taking the limiting upper average therefore gives at most r-1/(qN), excluding even a limsup of r.

To distinguish zero transformed cycles without numerical approximation, define
\[
z_e=(N+1)(q w_e-p)+1.
\]
For a simple cycle C of length ell<=N, if q sum_C w-p ell>=0 then sum_C z>=ell>0. If that integer is at most -1, then sum_C z<=-(N+1)+ell<0. Therefore the required threshold cycle exists exactly when the reachable z-weighted graph has a strictly positive cycle. This handles equality correctly; merely searching for a strictly positive cycle under q w-p would miss zero-threshold wins.

Initialize h_0(v)=0 for all reachable vertices, and for t=1,...,N compute synchronously
\[
h_t(v)=\max\{h_{t-1}(v),\max_{u\to v}(h_{t-1}(u)+z_{uv})\}.
\]
h_t is the largest weight of a walk of at most t edges ending at v, with any starting vertex. If there are no positive cycles, remove nonpositive cycles from a maximizing walk without decreasing weight, so some maximizing walk has at most N-1 edges and h_N=h_{N-1}. Conversely, if h_N=h_{N-1}, that common vector satisfies h(v)>=h(u)+z_uv on every edge. Summing around a cycle proves it cannot be positive. Thus any coordinate increase in the Nth round is equivalent to YES. This supplies a direct decision algorithm with no cycle-enumeration oracle.

There are N rounds and O(N+m) work per round. The z weights have bit length O(B+log N), where B counts the threshold and reward encodings, and h_t sums at most N such weights. Ordinary exact signed arithmetic gives polynomial bit cost, with O(N) numeric workspace using two rows. Thresholds do not require a pseudopolynomial loop through reward magnitudes. Predecessor information may be retained if a positive-cycle witness is desired; the stated algorithm only claims the threshold decision.

The checker compares this test at rational thresholds with independent enumeration of all simple reachable cycles on small digraphs, including negative rewards, self-loops, zero means, inaccessible positive cycles and large binary rewards. The proof covers only one controlling player; it does not solve two-player games or strongly polynomial general linear programming, the other question in the original prompt. The connection with classical minimum/maximum cycle-mean algorithms is credited to Karp; no new unrestricted result is asserted.

Comparison with the earlier attempt: The earlier item reports broad 2026 quasipolynomial manuscript claims. Here a self-contained polynomial algorithm and explicit arithmetic separation are proved for the genuine one-player subclass; none of those manuscripts is assumed correct.

Polynomial Algorithms for Mean-Payoff Games and Linear Programming

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 104; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that ordinary finite mean-payoff games have a deterministic quasipolynomial-bit-time solver for all zero-threshold vertices, exact values, and optimal positional strategies; In manuscript 2 (family 104; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that mean-payoff parity games with arbitrary signed binary rewards and priorities have deterministic quasipolynomial-time winning-set computation; In manuscript 3 (family 104; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that ordinary mean-payoff games have a quasipolynomial-time randomized zero-threshold solver with success probability at least $7/8$ on every input and a polynomial certificate check; In manuscript 4 (family 104; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that finite stochastic games in which players move in turn admit deterministic quasipolynomial-bit-time zero-threshold solution for signed binary rewards and binary rational probabilities.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
A finite directed arena, with each vertex assigned to Max or Min and binary integer edge weights, defines payoff $\operatorname{MP}_w(\pi)=\liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\sum_{t<T}w(e_t)$. Every vertex has a successor. For total binary encoding length $L$, the claimed uniform deterministic algorithm exactly returns \[ U=\{v:\exists\sigma\ \forall\tau:\operatorname{MP}_w(\pi_{v,\sigma,\tau})\ge0\} \] within $2^{C(\log_2(L+2))^2}$ bit operations for an absolute $C$. All preprocessing, exact arithmetic, recursion and output costs are counted. This is quasipolynomial, rather than polynomial, time.
Manuscript 2.
A finite directed arena has an outgoing edge at every vertex, a Max/Min ownership partition, binary integer vertex rewards $r(v)$ and nonnegative integer priorities $p(v)$. For pure strategies Max's objective on $\pi=v_0v_1\cdots$ is \[ \min\{p(v):v\text{ recurs infinitely often}\}\text{ even},\qquad \liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\sum_{t<T}r(v_t)\ge0. \] The claimed deterministic, uniform algorithm decides for each vertex whether Max has a strategy forcing both conditions against every strategy of Min in at most $2^{C(\log_2(L+2))^2}$ bit operations, with absolute $C$ and full binary input length $L$. Priority count and reward magnitudes are unrestricted.
Manuscript 3.
A finite directed Max/Min arena has at least one outgoing edge per vertex and binary integer edge weights. Let $\operatorname{MP}_w(\pi)=\liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\sum_{t<T}w(e_t)$. The claimed uniform randomized algorithm returns exactly \[ U=\{v:\exists\sigma\ \forall\tau:\operatorname{MP}_w(\pi_{v,\sigma,\tau})\ge0\} \] with probability at least $7/8$, using at most $2^{C(\log_2(L+2))^2}$ bit operations on every random tape, for an absolute $C$ and full binary input length $L$. The bound includes all preprocessing, recursion, arithmetic and random-bit generation. It is a randomized quasipolynomial algorithm, rather than a deterministic polynomial one.
Manuscript 4.
Consider a finite explicitly encoded directed multigraph where every vertex has positive outdegree, integer edge rewards and vertices controlled by Max, Min or chance. Chance probabilities are binary rational numbers, and parallel edges, loops and zero probabilities are allowed. For behavioral strategies using the full history, define \[ \operatorname{MP}=\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\sum_{t<T}w(e_t),\qquad \operatorname{val}(i)=\sup_{\sigma}\inf_{\tau}\mathbb E_i^{\sigma,\tau}[\operatorname{MP}], \qquad U=\{i:\operatorname{val}(i)\ge0\}. \] The expectation follows the pathwise lower limit. The claim supplies a single deterministic Turing machine and absolute $C$ outputting exactly $U$ in \[ 2^{C(\log_2(L+2))^2} \] bit operations, where $L$ is the full binary input length, including exact rational arithmetic and output. Value-zero vertices are included.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 104; 2026-09-25), potential comparisons are encoded as simultaneous obligations on finite integer boxes. Recursion decreases both width and weighted support mass while protecting witnesses at moving boundaries; polynomially many threshold queries recover exact values and strategies. For manuscript 2 (family 104; 2026-10-05), the ordinary mean-payoff solver is coupled to attractor and bounded-dominion recursion. Uniform prefix-payoff bounds permit strategies to be combined across attractors, and a decreasing recursion budget bounds the full bit complexity.

For manuscript 3 (family 104; 2026-09-25), weighted potential supports are repeatedly transported and recentered in integer boxes. Random directions narrow the comparison bands while a decreasing rank ensures termination on every tape; verified positional strategies permit repetition with no erroneous accepted answer. For manuscript 4 (family 104; 2026-10-05), rational gain separation reduces sign decisions to one discounted monotone fixed point. Simultaneous boundary labels move two nearby integer comparison vectors through shrinking boxes, reaching enough precision to include zero-valued vertices correctly.
