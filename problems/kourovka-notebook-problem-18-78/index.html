Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.78

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be an arbitrary field, let $G$ be a finite group with identity $1$, and let $\alpha\colon G\times G\to K^{\times}$ be a normalized scalar $2$-cocycle, meaning that \[ \alpha(g,h)\alpha(gh,k)=\alpha(h,k)\alpha(g,hk),\qquad \alpha(1,g)=\alpha(g,1)=1 \quad(g,h,k\in G). \] Under the standard scalar-cocycle interpretation of the source notation, the twisted group algebra $K^tG=K^{\alpha}G$ is the $K$-vector space with basis $\{u_g:g\in G\}$ and multiplication given by \[ u_gu_h=\alpha(g,h)u_{gh}, \] extended $K$-bilinearly. It is simple if it has no nonzero proper two-sided ideals. Define the derived series by $G^{(0)}=G$ and \[ G^{(n+1)}=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G^{(n)}\rangle. \] The group $G$ is solvable if $G^{(m)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $m\geq 0$.

The question is whether, for every field $K$, every finite group $G$, and every normalized scalar $2$-cocycle $\alpha$, \[ K^{\alpha}G\text{ is simple}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ is solvable}. \] The supplied statement imposes no restriction on $\operatorname{char}K$. It further reports that the implication is known when $K^{\alpha}G$ is central simple, meaning that it is simple and its center is exactly $K u_1$. This special-case assertion is supplied background, not an independently verified result of this draft.

Short English statement

Must a finite group be solvable whenever a twisted group algebra of it over an arbitrary field is simple? The supplied source reports an affirmative answer when the algebra is also central over that field.
