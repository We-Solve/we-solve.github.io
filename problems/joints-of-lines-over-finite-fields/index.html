Math-1376

Joints of lines over finite fields

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This source says "Same problem, but over finite fields." The immediately preceding problem 3.13 defines a joint of lines in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ as a point incident with three noncoplanar lines. Thus the recovered target is: given a prime power $q$ and $L$ distinct affine lines $\mathcal L$ in ${\mathbb{F}}_q^3$, determine the largest possible number of joints. A point is a joint if three incident lines have direction vectors spanning ${\mathbb{F}}_q^3$. Count points, not line triples or intersection multiplicities.

The supplied aid records a characteristic-independent bound of order $L^{3/2}$ and, attributing the sharper arbitrary-field result to Chao and Yu, the envelope \[ J(\mathcal L)\leq \min\left\{q^3,\ \frac{qL}{3},\ \frac{L(\sqrt{8L+1}-3)}6\right\}. \] The first two terms retain the field-size and incidence ceilings. There is no field-size or characteristic restriction for this upper bound. Sharp $L^{3/2}$ order means that suitable configurations attain that order in a sufficiently large field; it is not a lower bound for every $L$ in a fixed small field.

The aid supplies two equality families. All axis-parallel lines have $L=3q^2$ and every one of the $q^3$ points is a joint, attaining the first two ceilings. For an integer $3\leq M\leq q$, take distinct $t\in{\mathbb{F}}_q$ and the $M$ planes \[ x+t y+t^2z=t^3. \] Their pairwise intersection lines number $L=\binom M2$, and their triple intersection points number $J=\binom M3$, attaining the third expression. This is an explicit interpretation of the aid's Vandermonde-plane family; the parameter restriction is essential.

The aid also reports a self-contained polynomial/deletion proof of $J\leq(6^{1/3}+1)^{3/2}L^{3/2}$, addressing the positive-characteristic zero-derivative case by taking a $p$-th root over the perfect field ${\mathbb{F}}_q$. The quoted sharp theorem and the construction claims remain supplied results requiring independent verification; no characteristic-zero derivative argument is silently transferred to finite characteristic.

Short English statement

Count joints of distinct lines in finite three-dimensional space. The preceding real-space problem supplies the missing definition. The supplied aid gives a uniform sharp-type upper bound together with finite-field capacity ceilings and equality constructions in their stated parameter ranges.
