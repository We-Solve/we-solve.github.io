Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.14

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\gamma$ be an oriented $C^1$ plane curve and let $f:\gamma\to{\mathbb{C}}$ be continuous. Wherever the integral is well defined, set \[ F(z)=\int_\gamma\frac{f(t)}{t-z}\,dt\qquad(z\notin\gamma). \] For example, compact rectifiable curves and continuous densities give the ordinary line integral off the curve. The source does not explicitly state compactness, regularity of parametrization, or convergence assumptions for a noncompact curve.

Boundary-limit question (A. Baernstein). Does $F$ have finite non-tangential boundary values at almost every point of $\gamma$, with almost everywhere understood with respect to arclength? A usual non-tangential approach from a complementary domain $\Omega$ requires $z\to\xi\in\partial\Omega$ with $|z-\xi|\leq A\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)$ for a fixed $A>1$. For a regular Jordan curve this gives the respective interior and exterior questions; the source does not specify sides or how to treat self-intersections in its first part.

Function-class question. Suppose $\gamma$ is a Jordan curve, let $\Omega$ be its bounded interior, and let $\phi:\mathbb D\to\Omega$ be conformal. For $h=F\circ\phi$, does there exist $0<p<1$ such that \[ \sup_{0<r<1}\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|h(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta<\infty, \] that is, $h\in H^p$? If this Hardy-class conclusion is unavailable, does at least $h\in N$, the analytic class of bounded Nevanlinna characteristic, hold, meaning \[ \sup_{0<r<1}\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log^+|h(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta<\infty? \] The natural reading allows $p$ to depend on the curve and density, but the source does not specify whether a universal exponent is sought. It is also unclear whether the original $C^1$ hypothesis is retained in this Jordan-curve part; for an arbitrary nonrectifiable Jordan curve the meaning of the original line integral itself requires an extra convention.

The source describes a long-studied old problem, reports affirmative answers under slightly stronger smoothness assumptions on either $\gamma$ or $f$, and highlights Havin's contribution without stating its hypotheses. The supplied summary says the question was open in the 2018 edition and no definitive resolution was located. These are historical/supplied status claims, not an independent present-day literature assessment.

Short English statement

For a continuous density on a $C^1$ curve, ask whether its Cauchy integral has non-tangential limits almost everywhere. On a Jordan-curve interior, ask whether conformal pullback lies in some $H^p$, $0<p<1$, or at least has bounded Nevanlinna characteristic. Curve scope and integral conventions need clarification.
