Math-5345

Effective semilinearity for polynomial zeros at powers of two

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 8 (Pheidas) asks for an algorithm which, given $P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X_1,\ldots,X_m]$, decides whether \[ \exists(n_1,\ldots,n_m)\in{\mathbb{N}}^m\qquad P(2^{n_1},\ldots,2^{n_m})=0. \] The supplied source answers yes and relates this to Mordell--Lang for tori. The extracted superscripts have been lost; the supplied verification identifies the intended inputs as powers of two. Since the convention for ${\mathbb{N}}$ is unspecified, distinguish ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,\ldots\}$ from ${\mathbb{N}}_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the aid asserts decidability for either convention.

The stronger supplied claim is that \[ S_P=\{\bar n\in{\mathbb{N}}_0^m:P(2^{n_1},\ldots,2^{n_m})=0\} \] is effectively semilinear: one can compute a finite union of sets $\bar a+{\mathbb{N}}_0\bar b_1+\cdots+{\mathbb{N}}_0\bar b_r$ with vectors in ${\mathbb{N}}_0^m$, equivalently a Presburger definition of $S_P$. This in particular decides emptiness. For positive exponents one restricts the same description by $n_i\geq1$, or shifts the variables.

The aid describes the following finite valuation-pair elimination scheme. Write the nonzero monomial contributions as \[ P(2^{n_1},\ldots,2^{n_m})=\sum_{j=1}^s u_j2^{L_j(\bar n)}, \] where $u_j$ is an odd signed integer and $L_j$ is an integer affine expression obtained from the monomial exponent and the $2$-adic valuation of its coefficient. At each branch choose two terms with a common minimum exponent, adding the linear conditions \[ L_i=L_j\leq L_k\qquad\text{for every current term }k. \] If $u_i+u_j=0$, delete the pair. Otherwise write $u_i+u_j=2^v u$ with $u$ odd and replace the pair by $u2^{L_i+v}$. Every step strictly reduces the number of current terms; branch constraints retain all previous linear equalities and inequalities. Accept a zero-term leaf, reject a nonzero one-term leaf, and take the union of accepting branch constraints over ${\mathbb{N}}_0^m$. The supplied claim is that this produces exactly $S_P$, including complete cancellations and the identically zero polynomial. This is a reported algorithmic argument, requiring independent mathematical review.

The source's abstract toric Mordell--Lang connection and the aid's effective construction are distinct forms of support: an abstract coset description alone does not provide an algorithm unless its effectiveness is established. The aid additionally cites Semenov's theorem and a 2023/2026 existential Presburger-with-power algorithm, without giving precise theorem statements or bibliographic data.

Short English statement

The question asks whether an integer polynomial vanishes when all its arguments are powers of two. The source reports a positive answer. The aid strengthens this to an effective semilinear description of all exponent tuples, using finite branching on minimal $2$-adic valuations; both zero-based and positive exponent conventions are covered.
