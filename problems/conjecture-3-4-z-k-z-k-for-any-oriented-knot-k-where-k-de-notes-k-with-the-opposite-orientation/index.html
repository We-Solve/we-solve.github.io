Conjecture 3.4 — Z(K) = Z(−K) for any oriented knot K, where −K de- notes K with the opposite orientation.

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K\subset S^3$ be an oriented knot, and let $-K$ be the same knot with its orientation reversed. This is not a mirror-image operation. Let $\mathcal A(S^1)$ be the source's graded algebra of Jacobi diagrams on an oriented circle, with product given by connected sum along the circle, and let $\widehat{\mathcal A}(S^1)$ be its degree completion. Write $Z(K)$ for the Kontsevich invariant with the source's fixed diagram/framing normalization.

Prove that every oriented knot satisfies \[ Z(K)=Z(-K)\quad\text{in }\widehat{\mathcal A}(S^1). \] The equality is in every degree, not just a fixed truncation. The source cross-refers to Conjecture 2.7 for an equivalent assertion, whose statement is not included and is not reconstructed.

The following text in the extract is background from Section 3.3: $Z(K)$ is group-like, so with its unit constant term the formal logarithm \[ \log Z(K)=\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{r+1}}r(Z(K)-1)^r \] lies in the completed connected-diagram subspace, spanned by diagrams whose uni-trivalent part is connected. This structural fact is not an additional request to characterize all possible Kontsevich invariants.

Short English statement

Show that the Kontsevich invariant cannot distinguish any oriented knot from the same knot with reversed orientation, in every diagram degree.
