Math-4713

Fujiki-Class Limits of Kähler Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Delta\subset{\mathbb{C}}$ be an open disc centered at $0$, and put $\Delta^*=\Delta\setminus\{0\}$. Interpret a complex-analytic family of compact complex manifolds in the usual smooth sense: a proper surjective holomorphic submersion $\pi:X\to\Delta$ from a complex manifold, with fibers $X_t=\pi^{-1}(t)$. This interpretation makes the family hypotheses precise; the supplied record does not explicitly specify properness or the submersion condition, so their intended inclusion requires source review.

A compact complex manifold $M$ is Kähler if it admits a smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\omega$ such that ${\,\mathrm{d}}\omega=0$ and $\omega$ is positive definite. It belongs to Fujiki class $\mathcal C$ if it is bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold. Here bimeromorphic means that there is a meromorphic map admitting a meromorphic inverse.

Question. For every family $\pi:X\to\Delta$ as above, does \[ \left(\forall t\in\Delta^*,\;X_t\text{ is K\"ahler}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X_0\in\mathcal C \] hold?

The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and reports that a projective or integral-class version is proved, while the general question remains open and is related to transcendental Morse inequalities. The precise hypotheses of those special versions are not supplied, so this contextual assessment is not formulated here as a separate theorem or an additional assumption.

Short English statement

If every noncentral fiber of a smooth proper holomorphic family of compact complex manifolds over a disc is Kähler, must the central fiber be bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold? The smooth proper interpretation of "family" requires confirmation from the source. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation, not an independently verified status.
