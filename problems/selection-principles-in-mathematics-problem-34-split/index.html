Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 34 ([split])

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a topological space $X$, a property $P$ is hereditary if every subspace of every space having $P$ also has $P$. More explicitly, relative to an intended class $\mathcal C$ of spaces, write \[ H_{\mathcal C}(P):\quad \forall X\in\mathcal C\;\forall Y\subseteq X, \qquad P(X)\Longrightarrow P(Y), \] where $Y$ carries the subspace topology.

The four properties named in the supplied record are \[ \begin{aligned} P_1&=\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm{B}_{\Omega},\mathrm{B}_{\Lambda}),\\ P_2&=\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm{B}_{\Omega},\mathrm{B}_{\Omega}),\\ P_3&=\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{T}}),\\ P_4&=\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{B}_{\Gamma}). \end{aligned} \] The question is whether \[ \bigvee_{i=1}^{4}H_{\mathcal C}(P_i) \] holds: is at least one of these four properties hereditary?

The supplied record does not specify $\mathcal C$ or define the cover classes and splitting operator. The following supplies a conditional interpretation using customary cover terminology; these conventions require confirmation against the cited source. For a space $X$, consider countable families $\mathcal U$ of Borel subsets of $X$ such that $\bigcup\mathcal U=X$ and $X\notin\mathcal U$. Such a cover is large if every point belongs to infinitely many members; it is an $\omega$-cover if every finite subset of $X$ is contained in some member; and it is a $\gamma$-cover if $\mathcal U$ is infinite and each point belongs to all but finitely many members. A $\tau$-cover is a large cover satisfying, for every $x,y\in X$, \[ \left|\{U\in\mathcal U:x\in U,\ y\notin U\}\right|<\infty \quad\text{or}\quad \left|\{U\in\mathcal U:y\in U,\ x\notin U\}\right|<\infty. \] Under this interpretation, $\mathrm{B}_{\Lambda}$, $\mathrm{B}_{\Omega}$, $\mathrm{B}_{\Gamma}$, and $\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{T}}$ denote, respectively, these Borel large, $\omega$-, $\gamma$-, and $\tau$-covers.

One possible precise splitting convention is that $X$ satisfies $\mathsf{Split}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ when \[ \forall\mathcal U\in\mathcal A(X)\;\exists\mathcal V_0,\mathcal V_1\subseteq\mathcal U, \qquad \mathcal V_0\cap\mathcal V_1=\varnothing, \quad \mathcal V_0,\mathcal V_1\in\mathcal B(X). \] A partition convention additionally requires $\mathcal V_0\cup\mathcal V_1=\mathcal U$. The supplied record does not resolve which convention is intended. Thus the displayed heredity question preserves the source question, while its fully instantiated topological formulation remains conditional on the ambient class and these definitions. No mathematical resolution is asserted.

Short English statement

Is at least one of the four listed Borel cover splitting properties inherited by every subspace? The intended class of spaces and exact cover and splitting conventions need confirmation from the cited source.
