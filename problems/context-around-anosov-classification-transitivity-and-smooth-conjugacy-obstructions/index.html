Context around Anosov classification: transitivity and smooth-conjugacy obstructions

Definitions and mathematical statement

This record is contextual prose, not a standalone statement of Conjecture 3. The aid reconstructs its opening as "the conjugation map in Conjecture 3 is not necessarily smooth." The actual conjecture and preceding sentence are absent. Its concluding fragment, "2.2. The historical context reports Anosov diffeomorphisms on manifolds homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to a torus [54], non-topologically-transitive Anosov flows [63], and a classification conjecture in the codimension-one stable or unstable case [195, 70]. Here Anosov means an invariant tangent splitting with uniformly contracting and expanding directions, together with the flow direction for a flow. These reports distinguish topological conjugacy, smooth conjugacy and orbit equivalence; none may be interchanged merely from hyperbolicity.

The aid's first independent audit is the following exact statement. Let $T:X\to X$ be a homeomorphism of a compact metric space and $r:X\to(0,\infty)$ continuous. Form the suspension \[ X^r=\{(x,s):0\leq s\leq r(x)\}/((x,r(x))\sim(Tx,0)). \] The suspension flow has a dense full two-sided orbit if and only if $T$ has a dense full ${\mathbb{Z}}$-orbit. Positivity and compactness bound the roof away from zero and make the full flow well defined. This uses two-sided transitivity, without silently substituting a different forward-orbit convention.

The second audit concerns differentiable conjugacy. If a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $h$ conjugates smooth maps $f,g$ and $x$ has period $k$, then \[ Dg^k_{h(x)}=Dh_x\,Df^k_x\,(Dh_x)^{-1}. \] Thus periodic derivative spectra and determinants must agree. For a concrete version of the aid example, take the cat map on ${\mathbb{R}}^2/{\mathbb{Z}}^2$ induced by $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, whose fixed point is unique. A sufficiently small smooth perturbation $f_\varepsilon=\psi_\varepsilon\circ A$, supported in local coordinates near that fixed point, may fix it and have $D\psi_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1+\varepsilon,1)$ there. For small nonzero $\varepsilon$, the aid invokes Anosov structural stability to retain topological conjugacy, while \[ \det Df_\varepsilon=1+\varepsilon\ne1=\det A \] at the corresponding fixed points excludes a $C^1$ diffeomorphic conjugacy. This example is on the standard torus and is separate from the exotic-torus historical claim.

The aid additionally reports a May 2026 primary preprint claiming four-dimensional codimension-one Anosov flows not orbit equivalent to suspensions, described there as refuting the broad Verjovsky suspension conjecture. No authors, title, identifier or full hypotheses are supplied. This is a dated unverified preprint report, not independently confirmed literature status and not the missing statement of Conjecture 3. The paired audits and historical qualifications do not constitute a classification of general Anosov flows.
