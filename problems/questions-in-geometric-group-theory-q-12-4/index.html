Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 12.4

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a connected orientable finite-type surface $S=S_{g,n}$ with $2g-2+n>0$, let $\mathcal T(S)$ be its Teichmüller space of marked complete finite-area hyperbolic structures, modulo the usual marking-preserving equivalence, and let $d_{\mathrm{WP}}$ be the path metric of the Weil--Petersson Riemannian metric, defined by the hyperbolic $L^2$ pairing on harmonic Beltrami differentials. Its complex dimension is $\xi(S)=3g-3+n$.

For $\xi(S)\geq1$, use the graph metric on the one-skeleton $\mathcal C(S)$ of the curve complex: vertices are isotopy classes of essential nonperipheral simple closed curves, with edges for disjoint representatives. For $S_{1,1}$ and $S_{0,4}$ use the usual modified adjacency of minimal intersection $1$ and $2$, respectively, giving the Farey graph. A geodesic metric space is Gromov hyperbolic if some $\delta\geq0$ makes all its geodesic triangles $\delta$-thin. Metric spaces $X,Y$ are quasi-isometric if there exist $f:X\to Y$, $A\geq1$, and $B\geq0$ such that \[ A^{-1}d_X(x,x')-B\leq d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\leq A d_X(x,x')+B \] for all $x,x'\in X$, and every point of $Y$ is at distance at most $B$ from $f(X)$.

Questions (Bowditch). For which surfaces $S$ is $(\mathcal T(S),d_{\mathrm{WP}})$ Gromov hyperbolic? For which $S$ is it quasi-isometric to $\mathcal C(S)$? The original source does not specify the class of surfaces, so the conventional finite-type setting above is explicit rather than an assertion about all possible Teichmüller spaces.

The supplied summary reports negative answers to both questions when $\xi(S)\geq3$, and affirmative answers for $S_{1,1}$ and $S_{0,4}$ using the Farey graph. It attributes the negative hyperbolicity result to Brock--Farb and hyperbolicity of the curve complex to Masur--Minsky. Although called a clean dichotomy there, these cases omit $\xi(S)=2$ and do not settle that part from the supplied evidence. The summary identifies the pants graph as a quasi-isometric model for the Weil--Petersson metric, attributed to Brock.

Short English statement

Determine when Weil--Petersson Teichmüller space is Gromov hyperbolic and when its large-scale metric agrees with the curve graph. The supplied status summary distinguishes higher complexity from two Farey-graph cases but omits complexity two.
