Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Conjecture 3 (Long)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a smooth, strongly convex Finsler metric on $S^2$; reversibility is not assumed. Thus $F:TS^2\to[0,\infty)$ is smooth off the zero section, positive on nonzero vectors, positively homogeneous of degree one, and the fibrewise Hessian of $\tfrac12F^2$ is positive definite off the zero section. A closed geodesic is a nonconstant periodic geodesic, parametrized with unit speed. It is prime if it is not an iterate of a closed geodesic of smaller period. Write $\mathcal P(F)$ for the set of prime closed geodesics modulo translation of the unit-speed parameter.

For $c\in\mathcal P(F)$, let $P_c$ be the linearized Poincaré return map on a two-dimensional transverse section in the unit tangent bundle. Call $c$ hyperbolic if \[ \operatorname{spec}(P_c)\cap\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|=1\}=\varnothing. \] Under the non-strict spectral convention, call $c$ elliptic if \[ \operatorname{spec}(P_c)\subseteq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|=1\}. \] This convention permits eigenvalues $1$ and $-1$, including degenerate cases. The supplied source does not specify whether these cases are intended to count as elliptic; this qualification remains unresolved.

The supplied record contains the following two conjectural assertions, with the displayed conjecture attributed to Long:

  1. For every Finsler metric $F$ on $S^2$, \[ \left(\exists c\in\mathcal P(F)\;\;c\text{ is hyperbolic}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \#\mathcal P(F)=\infty. \]
  2. Every Finsler metric $F$ on $S^2$ has an elliptic prime closed geodesic: \[ \forall F\;\exists c\in\mathcal P(F)\quad c\text{ is elliptic}. \]
The second assertion uses the ellipticity convention stated above, subject to the unresolved source qualification. The source also does not define "distinct": the first assertion is written using periodic geodesics modulo parameter translation, while the intended alternative of distinct images on $S^2$ is not explicitly resolved by the supplied excerpt. No additional nondegeneracy or curvature hypothesis is supplied.

Short English statement

The record presents two conjectural claims about Finsler metrics on $S^2$: one hyperbolic prime closed geodesic forces infinitely many distinct prime closed geodesics, and every such metric has an elliptic prime closed geodesic. The meaning of elliptic at eigenvalues $\pm1$, and the intended equivalence defining distinct geodesics, require clarification.
