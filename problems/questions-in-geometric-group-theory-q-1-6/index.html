Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of a group $G$, its Cayley graph has vertices $G$ and unit-length edges from $g$ to $gs$ for $s\in S$. The associated path metric is the word metric. A finitely generated group is word-hyperbolic if some $\delta\geq0$ makes every geodesic triangle in this graph $\delta$-thin: each side lies in the $\delta$-neighborhood of the other two. This property is independent of the finite generating set.

The group is one-ended if its Cayley graph is infinite and deleting any finite set of vertices leaves exactly one infinite connected component. Let $\Sigma_g$ denote the closed connected orientable surface of genus $g$. Its fundamental group has presentation \[ \pi_1(\Sigma_g)=\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\mid [a_1,b_1]\cdots[a_g,b_g]=1\rangle, \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}. \] Does every one-ended word-hyperbolic group $G$ admit an injective homomorphism $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)\to G$ for some integer $g\geq2$?

The genus may depend on $G$, and no bound on it is requested. The surface subgroup is not required to have finite index or to be quasiconvex. Allowing nonorientable closed hyperbolic surfaces gives the same existence question, since their orientation double covers supply orientable surface subgroups. The assumption of one end excludes free groups, whose subgroups are free and therefore cannot be closed hyperbolic surface groups. This is a subgroup-existence question, rather than the construction of a quotient onto a surface group.

Short English statement

Must every one-ended hyperbolic group contain the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus at least two?

Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.6

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a mathematical result relevant to this question in the selected manuscript. A hyperbolic group with two-sphere boundary is claimed to act geometrically on hyperbolic three-space with finite kernel.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Let $G$ be word-hyperbolic with Gromov boundary $\partial G\cong S^2$. The source claims a homomorphism \[ \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H^3) \] whose action on hyperbolic three-space is proper and cocompact, and whose kernel is finite. With the additional assumption that $G$ is torsion-free, the corollaries identify $G\cong\pi_1(M)$, where $M$ is closed and has a hyperbolic metric in dimension three, and supply a finite-index subgroup \[ H\cong\pi_1(\Sigma)\rtimes\mathbb Z, \] where $\Sigma$ is a closed connected orientable surface. They also assert residual finiteness, virtual compact specialness, and positive virtual first Betti number in that torsion-free case.

Outline of the argument

The argument applies a boundary modulus criterion. Unbounded modulus produces scalar limits analyzed by exponential records or subexponential probability flows; comparing same-depth expansions forces the required measure control and the three-dimensional hyperbolic action.
