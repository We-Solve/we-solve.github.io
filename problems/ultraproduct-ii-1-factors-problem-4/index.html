Ultraproduct II_1 Factors — Problem 4

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a discrete group $G$, let $\lambda_G$ be its left regular representation on $\ell^2(G)$, given by $\lambda_G(g)\delta_h=\delta_{gh}$. Its group von Neumann algebra is \[ L(G)=\{\lambda_G(g):g\in G\}'', \] with canonical trace $\tau_G(x)=\langle x\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle$. An infinite group $G$ is icc if every conjugacy class other than $\{e\}$ is infinite; in this case $L(G)$ is a type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor. For an icc group, inner amenability means the existence of a conjugation-invariant finitely additive probability measure on all subsets of $G\setminus\{e\}$. Non-inner amenability means that no such measure exists.

The supplied record does not define pseudocompact. Write $\mathcal P(M)$ solely as a placeholder for the source's intended assertion that the von Neumann algebra $M$ is pseudocompact. This notation does not resolve the missing definition. With this qualification, the two questions attributed to Elayavalli are:

  1. Does there exist a group $G$ such that $\mathcal P(L(G))$?
  2. Does there exist an infinite icc group $G$ that is not inner amenable and satisfies $\neg\mathcal P(L(G))$?
The group von Neumann algebra notation is interpreted here in its usual discrete-group setting. The record supplies no countability hypothesis. Although the source title concerns type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors, the first question does not explicitly require $G$ to be icc or $L(G)$ to be a type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor; this intended restriction remains unresolved. Consequently, the missing definition and domain qualifications must be checked before these questions become fully specified mathematical statements.

Short English statement

The source asks whether a group von Neumann algebra can be pseudocompact, and whether an icc group that is not inner amenable can have a group von Neumann algebra that is not pseudocompact. The supplied record reports the second question solved by J. Peterson. The meaning of pseudocompact and the intended restrictions on groups in the first question require clarification.
