Kourovka Notebook Problem 12.67

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied source asks for a structural description of two classes of locally compact soluble groups. The following formulation uses the usual conventions that locally compact groups are Hausdorff and that solubility refers to the underlying abstract group. These conventions are not explicitly specified in the supplied record and require confirmation against the source.

Let $G$ be a Hausdorff locally compact topological group. Define its abstract derived series by \[ G^{(0)}=G,\qquad G^{(j+1)}=[G^{(j)},G^{(j)}], \] where $[H,H]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in H$, without taking topological closure. Call $G$ soluble if $G^{(r)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $r\geq 0$.

Write \[ \mathcal{S}(G)=\{H\leq G: H\text{ is closed in }G\text{ and is noncompact in its subspace topology}\}. \] The maximum condition for closed noncompact subgroups means that every ascending sequence \[ H_1\leq H_2\leq\cdots,\qquad H_n\in\mathcal{S}(G), \] is eventually constant: there exists $N\geq 1$ such that $H_n=H_N$ for every $n\geq N$. The minimum condition for closed noncompact subgroups means the corresponding statement for every descending sequence \[ H_1\geq H_2\geq\cdots,\qquad H_n\in\mathcal{S}(G). \] These conditions concern all closed noncompact subgroups, without requiring them to be proper or normal.

Problem (V. M. Poletskikh, 1992). Give structural characterizations, separately, of

  1. the locally compact soluble groups satisfying the maximum condition for closed noncompact subgroups;
  2. the locally compact soluble groups satisfying the minimum condition for closed noncompact subgroups.
No noncompactness assumption on $G$ itself is stated. In particular, compact groups satisfy both chain conditions vacuously. The parenthetical wording in the source is interpreted as two separate classification questions. If the source uses a different separation convention or a notion of solubility involving closures of derived subgroups, the corresponding classification targets must be adjusted; the supplied record does not resolve these conventions.

Short English statement

Describe, separately, the structure of locally compact soluble groups in which every ascending, respectively descending, chain of closed noncompact subgroups eventually stabilizes. Compact ambient groups are included. The draft explicitly uses Hausdorffness and abstract solubility, conventions that require source confirmation.
