Data-Driven Estimation of Maximal Time Averages

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns estimation from trajectory data for an unknown deterministic dynamical system. Its state space, regularity, invariant region, and sampling model are not specified. The following notation describes a conditional formulation; these hypotheses require clarification before a convergence theorem can be stated.

Let $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be a state space carrying a forward flow $\phi_t)_{t\geq0}$, and let $g:X\to{\mathbb{R}}$ be an observable for which the integrals below are defined. A possible convention for the long-time average and its maximal value is \[ \overline g(x_0):=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T g(\phi_t(x_0))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t, \qquad M:=\sup_{x_0\in X}\overline g(x_0). \] The record does not specify whether an ordinary limit exists, whether a limsup is intended, whether $M$ is finite, or whether the supremum is attained. Thus this convention does not resolve the source's use of a maximum.

For a test function $v$, the Koopman operators and their generator, whenever defined, are \[ (K_t v)(x):=v(\phi_t(x)), \qquad (\mathcal L v)(x):=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{v(\phi_h(x))-v(x)}{h}. \] For an ordinary differential equation $\dot x=f(x)$ and differentiable $v$, this generator has the expression $\mathcal L v=f\cdot\nabla v$. The vector field $f$ is unavailable explicitly in the problem under consideration.

The original excerpt presents the auxiliary-function optimization expression \[ B:=\inf\left\{U\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ \exists v\in C^1(X)\ \text{such that}\ U-g(x)-(\mathcal L v)(x)\geq0\quad\text{for every }x\in X\right\}, \] and identifies it with the maximum long-time average. Here $v$ is an auxiliary function and $U$ is a candidate upper bound. The meaning of $C^1(X)$ for the unspecified set $X$, and the hypotheses supporting this identification, are absent from the excerpt. In particular, admissibility of auxiliary functions must address their behavior along trajectories, including the boundary term \[ \frac{v(\phi_T(x_0))-v(x_0)}{T}. \] The equality with $B$ is retained as a source assertion requiring its original hypotheses, rather than asserted here as an unconditional identity.

Requested research statement. Given sampled trajectory observations, with no explicit formula for $f$, develop an estimator of the maximal long-time average by approximating $\mathcal L$ on dictionaries of observables. Establish a convergence theory for a stated class of systems extending beyond polynomial ordinary differential equations, and characterize the required assumptions on the dynamics, observable, data, dictionaries, and sampling. Schematically, for data collections $D_k$, dictionaries $\mathcal V_k$, and generator approximations $\widehat{\mathcal L}_k$ constructed from those data, seek estimates satisfying \[ \widehat M_k=\widehat M(D_k,\mathcal V_k,\widehat{\mathcal L}_k)\longrightarrow M. \] The admissible class, data model, refinement regime, mode of convergence, and interpretation of $M$ must be specified as part of the formulation. No particular dictionary, sampling rule, noise model, compactness assumption, or certification property is supplied. The excerpt's phrase "in this case" also lacks its antecedent in the supplied original quotation.

Short English statement

Estimate the largest long-time average of an observable using sampled trajectories of an unknown dynamical system and an approximation of its Koopman generator. The requested advance is a convergence theory beyond polynomial ordinary differential equations, with explicit data, dictionary, and sampling assumptions. The supplied record leaves essential hypotheses and the precise meaning of the maximal average unresolved; this is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
