Non-Desarguesian Plane Lattices as Bricks

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A projective plane is an incidence structure $\Pi=(P,\mathcal L,I)$ in which any two distinct points lie on exactly one common line, any two distinct lines meet in exactly one point, and there exist four points no three of which are collinear. Its subspace lattice $L(\Pi)$ consists of the empty subspace, the points, the lines, and the whole plane, ordered by incidence and inclusion. The meet is intersection and the join is the smallest subspace containing both arguments.

For a division ring $D$, let $\operatorname{PG}(2,D)$ be the incidence plane of one-dimensional and two-dimensional right $D$-linear subspaces of $D^3$, with incidence given by inclusion. A projective plane is Desarguesian if it is isomorphic to $\operatorname{PG}(2,D)$ for some division ring $D$, and non-Desarguesian otherwise.

The supplied question asks whether there exist a non-Desarguesian projective plane $\Pi$ and a brick $B$ whose underlying lattice is isomorphic to $L(\Pi)$. Schematically, writing $U(B)$ for that underlying lattice, the question is whether \[ \exists\,\Pi\;\exists\,B\quad \left(\Pi\text{ is a non-Desarguesian projective plane}\right) \ \land\ \left(B\text{ is a brick}\right) \ \land\ U(B)\cong L(\Pi). \] Here the isomorphism is a lattice isomorphism. The supplied record does not define a brick or its underlying-lattice construction. Consequently, this display records the intended existence question but is not a complete axiomatic formulation: the predicate defining bricks and the construction $U$ must be recovered from the source before mathematical review. No restriction to finite planes is stated in the supplied question; whether an ambient finiteness convention applies remains unresolved.
