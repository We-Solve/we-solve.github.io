Definitions and mathematical statement

For each integer $n\geq 1$, let $p_n$ denote the $n$-th prime and let \[ \pi(n)=\#\{p\leq n:p\text{ is prime}\}. \] For terminology, let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $F(z)\in K[[z]]$. The series $F$ is differentially transcendental over $K(z)$ if, for every integer $r\geq 0$ and every nonzero polynomial $P\in K(z)[Y_0,\ldots,Y_r]$, \[ P\left(F(z),F'(z),\ldots,F^{(r)}(z)\right)\neq 0. \] Here derivatives are formal derivatives and the expression is interpreted in $K((z))$. The series is D-finite over $K(z)$ if there exist an integer $r\geq 0$ and polynomials $a_0(z),\ldots,a_r(z)\in K[z]$, not all zero, such that \[ \sum_{j=0}^{r}a_j(z)F^{(j)}(z)=0. \] These definitions explain the terminology; the supplied record does not specify the coefficient field for its intended assertion.
