The Kakeya Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every integer $n\geq2$, let $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ contain a line segment of length one parallel to each direction. Must its Hausdorff dimension be $n$? A set with this segment property is called a Kakeya set.

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

Short English statement

For every integer $n\geq2$, let $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ contain a line segment of length one parallel to each direction. Must its Hausdorff dimension be $n$? A set with this segment property is called a Kakeya set.

The Kakeya Conjecture

OpenAI's claim

Four-dimensional Kakeya sets: The manuscript claims that a set in $\mathbb R^4$ containing a segment of length one in every direction has Hausdorff dimension four; it permits arbitrary sets and arbitrary choices of witnessing segments.

Three-dimensional Kakeya maxima: The claimed tube maximal estimate in three dimensions has $L^3$ input and output and loses only an arbitrarily small power of the tube radius; in particular, the density exponent in its corresponding shaded-tube estimate is three.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
If $K\subset\mathbb R^4$ and for each unit direction $e\in S^3$ some $a_e\in\mathbb R^4$ satisfies $\{a_e+te:0\le t\le1\}\subset K$, then $\dim_H K=4$. No measurability assumption on $K$ or regularity assumption on the choice $e\mapsto a_e$ is imposed. This theorem treats dimension four, rather than all higher dimensions.
Mathematical claim 2.
For $0<\delta<1$, let $T_\delta(a,\omega)=\{a+t\omega+u:|t|\le1/2,\ u\cdot\omega=0,\ |u|\le\delta\}$ and $K_\delta f(\omega)=\sup_a(\pi\delta^2)^{-1}\int_{T_\delta(a,\omega)}|f|$. With ordinary surface measure on $S^2$, the asserted estimate is \[\|K_\delta f\|_{L^3(S^2)}\le C_\epsilon\delta^{-\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^3(\mathbb R^3)}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ f\in L^3(\mathbb R^3)),\] where $C_\epsilon$ does not depend on $\delta$ or $f$.

Outline of the argument

Four-dimensional Kakeya sets: A hypothetical dimension loss is converted into a weighted incidence model for lines. Nested boxes and quadratic fits describe persistent concentration; each fit must retain enough incidence mass to contribute. Scalar comparison and planar rigidity then contradict the simultaneous density and time-length requirements forced on substantial terminal chart systems.

Three-dimensional Kakeya maxima: The proof keeps the fraction of each tube that is actually observed throughout a multiscale concentration model. Near-extremal indexed configurations are made stationary at critical exponents, while their position and direction information are retained jointly. The geometric argument excludes a positive critical loss, and the final reduction passes from tube shadings to general functions without worsening the cubic density dependence.
