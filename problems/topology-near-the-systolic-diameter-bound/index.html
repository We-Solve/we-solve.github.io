Math-14728

Topology Near the Systolic-Diameter Bound

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a connected Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, its diameter is \[ \operatorname{diam}_g(M)=\sup\{d_g(x,y):x,y\in M\}. \] One possible meaning of the systole is the homotopy systole \[ \operatorname{sys}_{\pi_1}(M,g)=\inf\{\operatorname{Length}_g(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a piecewise smooth closed curve in }M\text{ that is not null-homotopic}\}. \] The supplied record does not specify whether this is the intended systole, rather than a homological systole or another convention. It also does not specify how manifolds with no noncontractible closed curves are treated. No convention for that case is imposed here.

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the intended class of Riemannian manifolds, which the supplied record does not fully specify. Once $\mathcal C$, the systole convention, and the meaning of topological type have been fixed so that the ratio below is defined, the supplied question has the following form: \[ \text{For every }\varepsilon>0,\qquad \#\left\{[M]_{\mathrm{homeo}}:\exists g\text{ with }(M,g)\in\mathcal C,\quad \frac{\operatorname{sys}_g(M)}{\operatorname{diam}_g(M)}\geq 2-\varepsilon\right\}<\infty\;? \] Here $[M]_{\mathrm{homeo}}$ denotes the homeomorphism class of the underlying manifold; this makes explicit the usual interpretation of "topological type," which still requires confirmation from the source. The metric is allowed to vary, and any proposed finite bound may depend on $\varepsilon$ and on the fixed parameters defining $\mathcal C$.

Unresolved qualifications. The record does not state whether dimension is fixed, whether manifolds are connected, compact, closed, complete, or permitted to have boundary, or whether curvature, fundamental-group, or other restrictions apply. For an ordinary real-valued ratio, the diameter must be positive and finite and the systole must have a specified finite value; treatment of other cases is missing. These are unresolved domain issues, not additional hypotheses supplied by this draft. The quantifier is exactly $\varepsilon>0$, with no stated upper bound. In particular, for $\varepsilon\geq2$ the displayed inequality imposes no restriction whenever the ratio is defined and nonnegative.

The corrupted original extract mentions a systolic bound involving $2$ and diameter and attributes to Gromov an equality characterization involving real projective space $\mathbb{RP}^{n}$. Its notation and hypotheses are incomplete, so neither the precise bound nor that characterization is asserted here as a verified theorem. The extract then runs into unrelated questions concerning Tverberg points, drawings of hypercube graphs, and orthogonal vectors; those passages do not specify the intended manifold class. Thus the displayed question is a conditional formalization of the supplied cleaned statement, pending resolution of its missing hypotheses.

Short English statement

For each $\varepsilon>0$, the supplied question asks whether only finitely many homeomorphism types admit a Riemannian metric whose systole-to-diameter ratio is at least $2-\varepsilon$. The intended manifold class, dimension restrictions, systole convention, and treatment of boundary or infinite quantities are unspecified, so a fully determined mathematical statement requires source review.
