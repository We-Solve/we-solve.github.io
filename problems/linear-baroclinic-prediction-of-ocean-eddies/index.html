An amplitude-identifiability test.
Suppose a particular linearly unstable eddy mode has complex amplitude $A$ and growth rate $\sigma>0$. Every nonlinear model in the family \[ \dot A=\sigma A-g|A|^2A,\qquad g>0, \] has the identical linearization $\dot A=\sigma A$ at zero. Writing $R=|A|^2$ gives $\dot R=2\sigma R-2gR^2$, whose solution for $R(0)=R_0>0$ is \[ R(t)=\frac{\sigma/g}{1+(\sigma/(gR_0)-1)e^{-2\sigma t}}. \] Direct substitution verifies the expression, and $R(t)\to\sigma/g$. Varying $g$ leaves all linear mode shapes and growth rates unchanged while changing the saturated amplitude arbitrarily.
What linear predictions can still test.
At early times with $gR_0e^{2\sigma t}\ll\sigma$, the solution is close to $R_0e^{2\sigma t}$. The regime ends on a time scale comparable to $(2\sigma)^{-1}\log(\sigma/(gR_0))$ when the logarithm is positive. Thus an observed scale or initial growth can be compared with a linear instability, but mature amplitude is not determined by the linearization alone. At $R_0=0$ the solution stays zero, another reminder that excitation must be specified.
Source-scope gap.
This is an exactly solved mode model, not a fitted ocean model or a counterexample to the report's broad null hypothesis. Whether observed eddy shapes and scales are "largely" predicted requires a chosen mean state, data, error criterion and nonlinear saturation information. None is supplied here. The first obstacle to an amplitude prediction is identifying the analogue of $g$ or proving it is irrelevant to the chosen observable.
