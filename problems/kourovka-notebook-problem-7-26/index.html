Kourovka Notebook Problem 7.26

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A variety of groups is the class of all groups satisfying some fixed collection of group identities, each universally quantified over its variables. A subvariety of a variety $\mathcal V$ is a variety $\mathcal W$ with $\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal V$; it is proper if $\mathcal W\ne\mathcal V$. Write $\operatorname{Sub}(\mathcal V)$ for the set of subvarieties of $\mathcal V$.

Define the ordinal height of $\mathcal V$ by \[ h(\mathcal V)=\sup\left\{\operatorname{otp}(C,\subseteq): C\subseteq\operatorname{Sub}(\mathcal V)\setminus\{\mathcal V\},\ (C,\subseteq)\text{ is well-ordered}\right\}. \] Here $\operatorname{otp}$ denotes order type, and the order is increasing inclusion. Equivalently, the ordinals in this supremum are those $\alpha$ for which there is a family $(\mathcal V_\beta)_{\beta<\alpha}$ of proper subvarieties of $\mathcal V$ satisfying \[ \mathcal V_\beta\subsetneq\mathcal V_\gamma\qquad(\beta<\gamma<\alpha). \] The empty chain is allowed and has order type $0$; no continuity condition at limit indices is imposed.

The problem asks whether \[ \forall\alpha<\omega_1\quad\exists\text{ a variety of groups }\mathcal V\quad h(\mathcal V)=\alpha, \] where $\omega_1$ is the first uncountable ordinal, so the quantifier includes every finite ordinal and $0$.

As contextual background, the supplied source also states that the cardinality of an ordinal height is finite, countably infinite, or equal to the cardinality of $\omega_1$. This assertion is retained as supplied source context, without independent verification.

Short English statement

Does every countable ordinal occur as the supremum of the order types of all chains of proper subvarieties of some group variety that are well-ordered by increasing inclusion? The question is attributed to A. V. Kuznetsov (1980).
