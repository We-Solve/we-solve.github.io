Math-1208

Expected-dimension generality of binary curves and two explicit boundary diagnostics

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Brian Osserman asks whether a curve $X$ formed by gluing two rational curves at $g+1$ generic pairs of points is Brill--Noether general. Concretely, take $C_1\simeq C_2\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^1$ with ordered pairwise distinct points $p_0,\ldots,p_g$ and $q_0,\ldots,q_g$, and identify $p_i\sim q_i$ transversely. The resulting connected binary nodal curve has arithmetic genus $g$. "Generic" must mean independently general labeled configurations on the two components, rather than configurations related by one projectivity.

For a multidegree $(a,b)$, let $G^r_{(a,b)}(X)$ parametrize line bundles $L$ with component degrees $a,b$ together with $(r+1)$-dimensional subspaces $V\subset H^0(X,L)$, and put \[ d=a+b,\qquad \rho(g,r,d)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r). \] An expected-dimension interpretation asks that the chosen linear-series spaces have dimension $\rho$ when nonempty and be empty for $\rho<0$. On a reducible curve one must specify which multidegrees, balancedness condition, compactification or limit-linear-series theory, and whether nonemptiness for $\rho\ge0$ is part of the assertion. The source does not choose these conventions or a field and genus range.

The attached unreviewed aid attributes an affirmative intended expected-dimension answer for general binary curves to Xiang He's all-rank theorem, including every potentially nonempty balanced multidegree and Osserman limit linear series. Its exact hypotheses and full reference are not supplied. It cautions that the unqualified question is definition-dependent.

Its separate boundary calculation assumes $a<0$. For every binary curve it reports $G^r_{(a,b)}(X)=\varnothing$ if $b<g+r+1$; otherwise it is a Grassmann bundle over the multidegree Picard torsor, with \[ \dim G^r_{(a,b)}=g+(r+1)(b-g-r-1),\qquad \dim G^r_{(a,b)}-\rho=(r+1)(-a-1). \] It reports balanced examples at $a=-1$ with $\rho\ge0$ but empty $G^0$, and positive dimension excess below $a=-1$ when nonempty. Finally it claims that a $(1,1)$-pencil exists exactly when the two labeled node configurations are projectively equivalent; for $g\ge2$ this diagonal in their configuration moduli has codimension $g-2$. The genus restriction is necessary for that codimension expression, and the pencil convention must be fixed. These diagnostics do not justify an unrestricted all-multidegree generality assertion.

Short English statement

Ask whether independently general gluing of two rational components produces a Brill--Noether general binary curve. The answer depends on the chosen multidegrees and linear-series theory; the aid reports an affirmative theorem in the intended balanced setting and explicit boundary exceptions to stronger readings.
