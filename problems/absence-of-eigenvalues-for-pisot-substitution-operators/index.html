Math-4702

Absence of Eigenvalues for Pisot Substitution Operators

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a finite nonempty alphabet and let $\sigma:A\to A^+$ be a substitution, extended to finite words by concatenation. Its incidence matrix is $M_\sigma=(m_{ab})_{a,b\in A}$, where $m_{ab}$ counts occurrences of $a$ in $\sigma(b)$. The substitution is primitive if some positive power of $M_\sigma$ has every entry positive. Using the usual substitution-language convention, its two-sided hull is \[ \Omega=\{\omega\in A^{{\mathbb{Z}}}:\text{every finite contiguous block of }\omega\text{ occurs in some }\sigma^k(a),\ a\in A,\ k\geq0\}. \] Let $\ell^2({\mathbb{Z}})=\{\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}:\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\psi(n)|^2<\infty\}$. For an arbitrary injective map $f:A\to{\mathbb{R}}$ and each $\omega\in\Omega$, define the bounded self-adjoint operator $H_{\omega,f}$ on all of $\ell^2({\mathbb{Z}})$ by \[ (H_{\omega,f}\psi)(n)=\psi(n+1)+\psi(n-1)+f(\omega_n)\psi(n),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] These are operators on the whole integer line; no finite-endpoint boundary conditions are imposed. Define \[ \Omega_c(f)=\{\omega\in\Omega:H_{\omega,f}\text{ has no eigenvalues}\}. \] Here absence of eigenvalues means \[ \forall E\in{\mathbb{R}}\quad\forall\psi\in\ell^2({\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad H_{\omega,f}\psi=E\psi\ \Longrightarrow\ \psi=0. \] A Pisot number is a real algebraic integer greater than $1$ whose other algebraic conjugates all have modulus less than $1$. However, the supplied record does not define the phrase "Pisot substitution." Requiring the Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue of $M_\sigma$ to be a Pisot number does not specify the same condition as requiring every other eigenvalue of $M_\sigma$ to have modulus less than $1$; additional irreducibility conventions may also matter. No convention is selected here.

Question, subject to this unresolved qualification. If $\sigma$ is a primitive Pisot substitution in the intended source sense, is it true that, for every injective $f:A\to{\mathbb{R}}$, \[ \Omega_c(f)=\Omega? \] Equivalently, must every $\omega\in\Omega$, every $E\in{\mathbb{R}}$, and every $\psi\in\ell^2({\mathbb{Z}})$ satisfy \[ \left[\ \psi(n+1)+\psi(n-1)+f(\omega_n)\psi(n)=E\psi(n)\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ \right]\ \Longrightarrow\ \psi=0? \] The supplied statement includes no explicit aperiodicity, irreducibility, or unimodularity hypothesis. Whether any such restriction belongs to the intended meaning of "Pisot" requires source clarification; none is silently added.

Short English statement

For a primitive substitution described by the source as Pisot, assign distinct real potential values to its letters. Must every resulting whole-line Schrödinger operator, for every sequence in the substitution hull, have no nonzero square-summable eigenfunction? The intended definition of a Pisot substitution remains unresolved.
