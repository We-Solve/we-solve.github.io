Literature and context check

The primary report, printed pages 85--86, describes the transmit-power equation and asks qualitatively about ten-percent traffic errors. It does not select a probability metric or a loss scale. The following explicitly chooses two auxiliary models: total-variation misspecification of a finite traffic distribution, and an operator-norm perturbation of the reported linear interference equation. They yield different, concrete guarantees; neither silently defines the report's unspecified ten-percent criterion.

Research attempt: Distributional error and an interference-margin estimate

Bounded loss under a specified probability distance
Let $\Omega$ and a nonempty design set $\mathcal A$ be finite, let $0\leq\ell(a,\omega)\leq L$, and let nominal and actual distributions $p,q$ satisfy \[ \tfrac12\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|p_\omega-q_\omega|\leq\varepsilon. \] Since the positive and negative parts of $p-q$ have equal total mass at most $\varepsilon$, multiplying by numbers in $[0,L]$ shows \[ |\mathbb E_p\ell(a,\omega)-\mathbb E_q\ell(a,\omega)|\leq L\varepsilon \tag{1} \] for every design. If $a_p$ minimizes nominal loss and $a_q$ minimizes actual loss, inserting and subtracting the two nominal losses gives \[ \mathbb E_q\ell(a_p,\omega)-\mathbb E_q\ell(a_q,\omega)\leq2L\varepsilon. \] For violation indicators the same proof gives a chance-constraint certificate: nominal violation probability at most $\tau-\varepsilon$ implies actual violation probability at most $\tau$.

There is also a finite exact robust optimization route. For each design maximize $\sum_\omega q_\omega\ell(a,\omega)$ over \[ q\geq0,\quad\sum_\omega q_\omega=1,\quad -u_\omega\leq q_\omega-p_\omega\leq u_\omega,\quad u\geq0,\quad\sum_\omega u_\omega\leq2\varepsilon. \] This is a linear program describing exactly the chosen total-variation ball: feasible $u$ implies the absolute-value bound, and conversely $u=|q-p|$ works. Minimize these finitely many optimum values over $\mathcal A$. This proves an exact finite method in the auxiliary scenario model, without a scalability claim or an empirical superiority claim for radio planning.

The loss bound is substantive. With two outcomes, nominal mass one on a safe outcome, and actual mass $\varepsilon$ on a bad outcome with loss $M$, the expectation changes by $\varepsilon M$ at total-variation distance $\varepsilon$. Letting $M$ grow precludes any scale-free performance bound based on this distance alone.

Perturbing the source's power equation
Write that equation as $z=(I-C)^{-1}\eta$, incorporating the fixed noise and overhead vector in $\eta$. Use the induced infinity norm and assume \[ \lVert C\rVert_\infty\leq1-\gamma,\quad 0<\gamma\leq1,\qquad \lVert\Delta\rVert_\infty\leq\gamma/2. \] For a matrix of norm $r<1$, the partial geometric sums satisfy $(I-C)\sum_{j=0}^mC^j=I-C^{m+1}$; the remainder tends to zero in norm. Thus the inverse exists and its norm is at most $1/(1-r)$. Applying this twice gives \[ \lVert(I-C)^{-1}\rVert_\infty\leq1/\gamma,\qquad \lVert(I-C-\Delta)^{-1}\rVert_\infty\leq2/\gamma. \] With $z'=(I-C-\Delta)^{-1}(\eta+\delta)$, subtracting the defining equations yields \[ z'-z=(I-C-\Delta)^{-1}(\delta+\Delta z), \] and therefore \[ \lVert z'-z\rVert_\infty \leq\frac2\gamma\left(\lVert\delta\rVert_\infty+ \frac{\lVert\Delta\rVert_\infty\lVert\eta\rVert_\infty}{\gamma}\right). \] This displays the needed interference margin. If the coupling matrices and forcing are nonnegative, their geometric inverses also give nonnegative powers. Without a margin, even the scalar case is unstable in the planning sense: $z=0.95z+\eta$ with $\eta>0$ has positive solution $20\eta$, whereas a ten-percent relative increase in that coefficient gives $z=1.045z+\eta$, whose sole algebraic solution is negative. This is a coefficient perturbation, not a ten-percent total-variation perturbation of a distribution; the two must not be conflated.

Verification and adversarial audit

The expectation bound is proved with the convention that total variation is half the $\ell_1$ distance. The regret bound applies to finite design sets with attained minima; no optimizer-existence assumption is hidden. The matrix estimate separately states a quantitative margin and proves the inverse bounds by geometric series. The scalar counterexample checks physical nonnegativity, rather than incorrectly claiming the algebraic inverse ceases to exist.
