Occupational Measures for Tonelli Hamilton–Jacobi Equations

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for an extension of a theory, but does not specify the results to be extended. Consequently, it determines a research problem rather than a fully quantified conjecture.

For interpreting the displayed equations, write $\mathbb T^d={\mathbb{R}}^d/{\mathbb{Z}}^d$, with $d\geq 1$, and interpret $c\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$ as a constant covector. The customary autonomous Tonelli framework takes a Hamiltonian $H\colon\mathbb T^d\times{\mathbb{R}}^d\to{\mathbb{R}}$ of class $C^2$, with positive definite momentum Hessian $D^2_{pp}H(x,p)$, and uniform superlinearity: \[ \lim_{R\to\infty}\ \inf_{\substack{x\in\mathbb T^d\\ |p|\geq R}} \frac{H(x,p)}{|p|}=+\infty. \] These are explicit conventional interpretations of the notation and the word Tonelli; the supplied excerpt does not state the precise regularity and convexity conventions intended by the report.

The mechanical equation appearing in the record is \[ H_{\mathrm{mech}}(x,Du(x)+c) =\frac12|Du(x)+c|^2+V(x)=\alpha[c], \qquad x\in\mathbb T^d, \] and the proposed generalization is \[ H(x,Du(x)+c)=\alpha[c], \qquad x\in\mathbb T^d. \] Here $u$ is an unknown function on the torus, $V$ is the potential in the mechanical case, and $\alpha[c]$ denotes the scalar associated with $c$ in the source notation. The record does not define $\alpha$, specify the admissible potentials, or state the solution concept and regularity required of $u$. In particular, interpreting $u$ as a viscosity solution requires confirmation from the underlying report. Working on the torus expresses periodicity; no additional boundary condition is supplied.

For a locally Lipschitz function $u$, its singular set can conventionally be written as \[ \operatorname{Sing}(u)=\{x\in\mathbb T^d: u\text{ is not differentiable at }x\}. \] For a measurable trajectory $\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb T^d$, its time-averaged occupation probability measure is \[ \mu_T=\frac1T\int_0^T\delta_{\gamma(t)}\,\mathrm dt, \qquad T>0. \] A limiting occupation measure in this position-space convention is a probability measure $\mu$ such that, for some sequence $T_j\to\infty$, \[ \int_{\mathbb T^d}f\,\mathrm d\mu_{T_j} \longrightarrow \int_{\mathbb T^d}f\,\mathrm d\mu \quad\text{for every }f\in C(\mathbb T^d). \] This definition explains the terminology but does not identify the dynamics intended in the source. The record does not specify the trajectories, whether occupation measures instead live in phase space, or the definition of the critical set, particularly at singular points.

Requested extension. Extend the relationships between critical sets, singular sets, and limiting occupation measures known for the displayed mechanical equation on $\mathbb T^d$ to the displayed equation for general Tonelli Hamiltonians. The exact relationships, their hypotheses, the relevant dynamics, and their quantifiers are absent from the supplied record. A precise theorem or conjecture cannot therefore be stated without additional source information; the conventions above do not resolve those omissions.

Short English statement

The problem asks to extend relationships among critical points, singularities of solutions, and long-time occupation measures from mechanical Hamilton--Jacobi equations on a torus to general Tonelli Hamiltonians. The supplied record omits the specific relationships and several necessary definitions, so this is a statement-only research-program draft requiring mathematical review.
