Maulik–Nekrasov–Okounkov–Pandharipande conjecture on an equivalence between Gromov–Witten theory and Donaldson–Thomas theory

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The record names the Maulik--Nekrasov--Okounkov--Pandharipande correspondence between Gromov--Witten and Donaldson--Thomas theory, without giving a precise version. A standard unmarked Calabi--Yau threefold formulation illustrates the required data, rather than defining every version of the named conjecture.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective complex threefold with trivial canonical bundle. For a nonzero effective curve class $\beta$, let $N_{g,\beta}$ be the zero-point genus-$g$ Gromov--Witten invariant, the degree of the virtual class of stable maps in class $\beta$. Let $I_{n,\beta}$ be the Donaldson--Thomas invariant of ideal sheaves of subschemes $Z\subset X$ with one-dimensional class $[Z]=\beta$ and Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathcal O_Z)=n$. These virtual classes have dimension zero in this setting. With $Q^\beta$ formal curve-class variables, form \[ Z'_{\mathrm{GW}}(u,Q)=\exp\left(\sum_{\beta\ne0}\sum_{g\geq0} N_{g,\beta}u^{2g-2}Q^\beta\right), \] \[ Z'_{\mathrm{DT}}(q,Q)= \frac{\sum_{\beta,n}I_{n,\beta}q^nQ^\beta} {\sum_n I_{n,0}q^n}. \] The first expression omits degree-zero contributions; the second divides them out. The conventional correspondence requires appropriate rationality in $q$ of fixed-curve-class reduced DT series and identifies their expansion under \[ q=-e^{iu},\qquad Z'_{\mathrm{GW}}(u,Q)=Z'_{\mathrm{DT}}(-e^{iu},Q). \] The change of variable uses the rational functions and their prescribed expansion, not an unjustified substitution into an arbitrary $q$-adic series. Curve variables use a suitable formal/Novikov completion; no analytic convergence is asserted.

This is one usual precise candidate suggested by the summary's Calabi--Yau-threefold context. The source does not specify projectivity, smoothness, insertions/descendants, equivariant or noncompact variants, connected versus disconnected conventions, rationality, degree-zero normalization, or the variable change. General threefold and inserted correspondences require different data; they are not automatically covered by the displayed unmarked Calabi--Yau version. A source-faithful full formalization must first identify the intended version.

Short English statement

The MNOP conjecture relates suitably normalized curve-counting partition functions in Gromov--Witten and ideal-sheaf Donaldson--Thomas theory after a change of variable. The record gives only its name, so the displayed Calabi--Yau version is an explicit conventional interpretation requiring primary-source review.
