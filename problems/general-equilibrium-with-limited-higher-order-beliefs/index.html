Econ-477

General equilibrium with limited higher-order beliefs

LLM status Open unverified

D84 — Expectations - Speculations Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra ultra 65% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

Original subject group: Economic theory, equilibrium and social choice

Audit classification: Published research agenda. In §6.2, printed p.27, the chapter proposes surveys eliciting subjective beliefs to distinguish two specifications of level-1 beliefs in a fiscal-policy application. The treatment design and identification problem here are editorial; attenuation is already a source result.

Definitions and precise research target

Model and research target. Let a population of agents $i\in[0,1]$ choose scalar actions in a linear coordination economy, \[ a_i=\theta_i+\alpha E_i[\bar a],\qquad \bar a=\int_0^1a_i\,di,\quad0<\alpha<1. \] Here $\theta_i$ is the payoff-relevant fundamental and $E_i$ denotes the agent's subjective conditional expectation. Under full information and rational-expectations equilibrium, with common knowledge of the aggregate fundamental and equilibrium strategies, a unit additive shift to every fundamental has aggregate multiplier $M_0=(1-\alpha)^{-1}$. Let $Z$ be a randomized public-information treatment about such a shock, and let $b_i^{(k)}$ denote latent order-$k$ beliefs defined by the recursion below, distinguished from their elicited reports. Define $M_Z=\partial E[\bar a\mid do(\theta+\eta),Z]/\partial\eta$ at $\eta=0$. The research target is to identify how $M_Z/M_0$ varies with belief precision, reasoning depth, and their population distributions. Establish conditions under which a finite collection of action observations and incentivized belief elicitations separately identifies incomplete information and truncated reasoning, or derive sharp observational equivalence sets when it cannot. Estimate the multiplier and its uncertainty under randomized information and stable structural payoffs, and evaluate predictions on untreated economies.

Definitions and maintained assumptions (editorial unless a source definition is named). Use a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, jointly measurable agents $i\in[0,1]$, information sigma-fields $\mathcal I_i$, and square-integrable fundamentals. Let $E_i$ be conditional expectation under a specified subjective probability $\mathbb P_i$; state absolute-continuity and integrability restrictions so these expectations and their cross-agent integrals exist. The equation $a_i=\theta_i+\alpha E_i[\bar a]$ defines an equilibrium only after the information and belief operators have been fixed. Common-prior Bayesian behavior and finite-level reasoning are competing model classes, not identical interpretations of that equation. Define recursively $b_i^{(1)}=E_i[\theta]$ for a common shock and $b_i^{(k+1)}=E_i[\int_0^1 b_j^{(k)}\,dj]$ for positive integers $k$; these are beliefs about the common fundamental and population averages of lower-order beliefs, not forecasts of actions. Measured reports additionally pass through a specified error kernel. Replace the informal intervention symbol by an experimentally specified family $\mathbb P_{\eta,Z}$, holding payoffs fixed, and set $M_Z=\left.\frac{d}{d\eta}\mathbb E_{\eta,Z}[\bar a]\right|_{\eta=0}$, when the derivative exists. Point identification of the multiplier means any two maintained models with the same joint law of actions and reports yield the same multiplier. This does not by itself identify information frictions or reasoning depth separately; those require a declared structural target and its identified set under the common model convention below.

Variants and assumption changes

  1. Finite levels (editorial). Fix a maximum level $K$, a specified level-zero forecast, and a distribution on $\{0,\ldots,K\}$. Ask which randomized signals and elicited orders identify the level distribution separately from report error and fundamental uncertainty.
  2. Bayesian information (editorial). Fix Gaussian common-prior fundamentals and a family of signal precisions, preserving unlimited reasoning. Compare its observable laws with the finite-level class, reporting the intersection and the multiplier range on that intersection rather than assuming the two mechanisms are distinguishable.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

These conventions apply to each entry unless explicitly replaced.

Models and identification. A primitive model $m$ specifies all probability laws, feasible choices, information, equilibrium and measurement rules used by its target. The admissible class $\mathcal M$ is fixed independently of outcomes. If $P_O(m)$ is its observable probability law and $\tau(m)$ the target, its identified set at observable law $P$ is \[ \mathcal I_\tau(P)=\{\tau(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_O(m)=P\}. \] Point identification means that this set is a singleton. An empty set signals incompatibility of data and assumptions. Coordinate bounds need not describe the joint set. Bounds are sharp only if every retained target is attainable or arbitrarily approachable by compatible models. Finite bounds require explicit restrictions on unobserved quantities; unrestricted confounding, hidden wealth, extrapolation or arbitrary equilibrium selection can yield uninformative sets.

Causal interventions. A potential outcome $Y_i(p)$ is the outcome under a complete policy regime $p$, including timing, financing, information and market spillovers. The target population and units are fixed before treatment. With interference, use the complete assignment vector or a justified exposure mapping; individual no-interference notation alone cannot identify an economy-wide intervention. A difference of mean potential outcomes does not require the cross-world joint distribution. Conditioning on post-treatment migration, survival, enrollment or employment changes the estimand and needs separate justification.

Statistical statements. An estimator, confidence set, forecast or test specifies a sampling law, model class and loss/coverage criterion. Uniform $(1-\alpha)$-coverage means $\inf_{m\in\mathcal M}\Pr_m\{\tau(m)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha$, or an explicitly asymptotic version. The whole parameter space trivially has coverage, so informativeness requires a stated width, risk or power objective. A forecasting improvement is relative to a named benchmark, information set and evaluation population.

Equilibrium and optimization. An equilibrium comprises feasible plans, optimality, beliefs where needed, budget constraints and all market/resource clearing. The primitives or a stated benchmark define each correspondence $\mathcal E(p;m)$. Nonemptiness is assumed or proved, never inferred just from compact policy sets. A selected-equilibrium target uses a declared selection rule; a robust target quantifies over all permitted equilibria. Use suprema or infima unless attainment is established. An $\varepsilon$-optimal policy is feasible and within $\varepsilon$ of the finite optimum value. Report an empty feasible set separately.

Welfare and accounting. Normative utility, interpersonal normalization, social weights, numeraire, discounting and the use of public receipts are primitives. Behavioral utility need not coincide with the welfare criterion. Household welfare includes ownership income and rents once; adding profits again to compensating variation generally double-counts them. A monetary equivalent is defined by an explicit transfer and price convention, with monotonicity and range conditions. Average effects, mechanism contributions, transfers and welfare losses are different targets. Infinite sums require convergence and dynamic feasible plans require terminal or no-Ponzi conditions.

Source versions. A working-paper date, revision date and journal publication year are distinguished. A DOI for a journal version is not automatically the DOI of an earlier manuscript. Locators refer to the stated version and printed pages where specified. A metadata-only check does not verify a claim about a full-text conclusion. Every entry's source subsection narrows the provenance claim accordingly.
