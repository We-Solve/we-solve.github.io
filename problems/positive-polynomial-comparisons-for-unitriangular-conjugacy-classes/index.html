Math-4365

Positive Polynomial Comparisons for Unitriangular Conjugacy Classes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a prime power $q$ and an integer $n\geq 1$, let \[ U_n({\mathbb{F}}_q)=\{(u_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{F}}_q):u_{ii}=1\text{ for all }i,\ u_{ij}=0\text{ whenever }i>j\} \] be the upper unitriangular group. Let $k(U_n({\mathbb{F}}_q))$ denote its number of conjugacy classes.

Let $T$ and $x$ be formal indeterminates. Define $A_n(T)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[T]$, for $n\geq0$, by \[ \sum_{n\geq0}A_n(T)\frac{x^n}{n!} =(1-\sin(Tx))^{-1/T} =\exp\!\left(-\frac{\log(1-\sin(Tx))}{T}\right). \] The last expression specifies the formal power series interpretation: the numerator is divisible by $T$, so the apparent division by $T$ has a removable singularity. In particular, $A_0(T)=1$.

Conditional comparison question. Fix $n\geq1$. Suppose that $S$ is an infinite family of prime powers and that a single polynomial $P_{n,S}(X)$ satisfies \[ P_{n,S}(q)=k(U_n({\mathbb{F}}_q))\qquad\text{for every }q\in S. \] Is it necessarily the case that \[ P_{n,S}(T+1)-A_n(T)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}[T]? \] Here membership in ${\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}[T]$ means that every coefficient is a nonnegative integer, including the possibility of zero. The polynomial $P_{n,S}$, if it exists, is uniquely determined by its values on $S$. The source notation $k(U_n({\mathbb{F}}_{T+1}))$ is understood as the polynomial substitution $P_{n,S}(T+1)$, not as a finite field with an indeterminate number of elements.

Unresolved source qualifications. The original statement restricts $S$ to primes, whereas the clean and displayed statements allow prime powers. These are distinct formulations and neither restriction is selected here as authoritative. The source also leaves unclear whether one family $S$ is to work simultaneously for every $n$, whether a separate family may be chosen for each $n$, and whether the assertion concerns every qualifying family or a particular intended family. The displayed question gives the conditional comparison for a specified $n$ and $S$; its full intended quantifier scope requires review. The source does not specify the coefficient ring of the representing polynomial.

Short English statement

The question compares the conjugacy-class counting polynomial of an upper unitriangular group, expressed in $T=q-1$, with polynomials defined by a formal exponential generating function. It asks whether their difference has nonnegative integer coefficients whenever the counting function agrees with a polynomial on an infinite family. The supplied versions disagree on primes versus prime powers and leave the scope of the family and dimension quantifiers unresolved.
