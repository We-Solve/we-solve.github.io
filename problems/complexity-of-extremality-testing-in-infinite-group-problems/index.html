Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Is the pseudo-polynomial running time of the known algorithms for testing extremality of minimal valid functions in the single-row infinite group problem best possible?

Source scope and attempted finite reduction

Koppe's report, printed pages 2898--2900, discusses rational piecewise-linear functions and the common denominator $q$. It states an oversampling theorem for continuous minimal functions with breakpoints and right-hand side in $q^{-1}\mathbb Z$: fixed oversampling factor at least three relates infinite to finite extremality. We do not prove or assume this theorem as a completed algorithm here. Even granting that bridge, the resulting grid has order $q$ points, whereas the rational breakpoints can have only order $\log q$ bits. Discontinuous and irrational representations require their own hypotheses.

Here is the exact finite linear-algebra step, stated in its explicit scope. On the cyclic group $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, fix $b\ne0$ and consider the polytope $P$ of vectors $p=(p_r)$ satisfying \[ p_0=0,\quad p_b=1,\quad p_r\ge0,\quad p_r+p_{b-r}=1,\quad p_r+p_s-p_{r+s}\ge0\quad(r,s\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z). \] All subscripts are taken modulo $N$. This system implies $0\le p_r\le1$. Its members yield valid inequalities for every finitely supported nonnegative integer configuration of residue sum $b$: repeated subadditivity bounds its cost below by $p_b=1$. The symmetry equations make each such vector minimal among nonnegative valid vectors below it. Indeed the configuration containing $r$ and $b-r$ has cost at least one under any dominated valid vector, while its cost under $p$ is one; both coordinate deficits must vanish. This argument also covers $r=b-r$ with a repeated element.

For a supplied rational $p\in P$, form homogeneous equations for all displayed equalities active at $p$, together with the homogeneous versions of all permanent equalities. Then $p$ is a vertex of this explicitly defined $P$ if and only if their only solution $h$ is zero. If $h\ne0$, all inactive inequalities have positive slack; because there are finitely many of them, choose $\delta>0$ smaller than slack divided by $|a^Th|$ for each nonzero inactive directional change. The two points $p\pm\delta h$ lie in $P$ and are distinct, so $p$ is not a vertex. Conversely, if $p$ is the midpoint of distinct $u,v\in P$, every active linear inequality at $p$ must be active at both endpoints, since two nonnegative slacks cannot average to zero. Thus $h=u-v\ne0$ solves all homogeneous equations. A nontrivial convex combination can also be converted to a midpoint of a shorter segment, so this is exactly the vertex criterion. There are $N$ unknowns and $O(N^2)$ rows. Exact elimination is polynomial in their explicit rational encoding: minors are sums of at most $N!$ products, so their bit lengths grow by at most a polynomial in $N$ and input bit length. This does not remove dependence on $N$, nor establish the unproved infinite-to-finite correspondence for every source representation.

Large denominators do not themselves force slow decisions

We give a complete known two-slope extremality proof to test the possible inference that a denominator $q$ alone implies an $\Omega(q)$ lower bound. For any $0<f<1$, let $\pi_f$ be one-periodic, with \[ \pi_f(t)=\begin{cases}t/f,&0\le t\le f,\$1-t)/(1-f),&f\le t\le1. \end{cases} \] A valid function here is nonnegative and gives total cost at least one for every finite multiset of reals whose sum is congruent to $f$ modulo one. The definition matches the normalized nonnegative infinite-group setting being used in this auxiliary proof.

First, $\pi_f$ is valid. Define $\psi(t)=\max(t/f,-t/(1-f))$. As a maximum of two linear forms it satisfies $\psi(u+v)\le\psi(u)+\psi(v)$. For $0\le t<1$, the minimum $\min_{z\in\mathbb Z}\psi(t+z)$ is the lesser of $t/f$ and $(1-t)/(1-f)$: other positive lifts are larger than $t$, and other negative lifts have larger absolute value than $t-1$. These two values cross at $f$. Thus this minimum is exactly $\pi_f(t)$, and choosing minimizing lifts for $u,v$ proves periodic subadditivity. Since $\pi_f(f)=1$, repeated subadditivity proves validity. Directly from its two pieces, $\pi_f(t)+\pi_f(f-t)=1$ modulo one, which also proves minimality by the two-element argument above.

Suppose $\pi_f=(\rho_1+\rho_2)/2$ with nonnegative valid functions $\rho_j$. They need not be assumed continuous, piecewise linear or subadditive. For any feasible multiset on which $\pi_f$ has total cost exactly one, validity and averaging force both $\rho_j$ costs to be exactly one. The tight pairs $(r,f-r)$ and $(r,f-r-k)$ for every integer $k$ also force $\rho_j(f-r)=\rho_j(f-r-k)$, so the competitors are one-periodic without assuming it. In particular $\rho_j(f)=1$ and $\rho_j(0)=0$ (the latter also follows from nonnegativity). For $x,y\ge0$, $x+y\le f$, both multisets $(x,y,f-x-y)$ and $(x+y,f-x-y)$ have $\pi_f$-cost one. Subtracting their forced equalities yields $\rho_j(x)+\rho_j(y)=\rho_j(x+y)$. Nonnegativity makes $\rho_j$ monotone on $[0,f]$ under this truncated additivity. Integer subdivision gives $\rho_j(af/b)=a/b$ for $0\le a\le b$. Approximating any $x/f$ above and below by rational numbers and using monotonicity squeezes $\rho_j(x)=x/f$.

For the other interval set $h_j(t)=\rho_j(-t\bmod1)$, $0\le t\le1-f$. Then $h_j(1-f)=1$. If $t,u\ge0$ and $t+u\le1-f$, the multisets $(-t,-u,f+t+u)$ and $(-(t+u),f+t+u)$ both have residue sum $f$ and $\pi_f$-cost one. Their equalities give truncated additivity of $h_j$. The same nonnegative rational-subdivision argument gives $h_j(t)=t/(1-f)$. The two intervals cover the group, so $\rho_1=\rho_2=\pi_f$. This proves extremality against all nonnegative valid functions in this normalized setting.

For $f=1/q$, $q\ge2$, the two pieces and slopes $q,-q/(q-1)$ have $O(\log q)$-bit descriptions. Membership in this explicit family can be checked by rational equality and order tests in polynomial bit time, and the proof immediately certifies extremality. Thus enumerating the full $q$-grid is unnecessary on this family. No inference about arbitrary piecewise-linear inputs follows.
