Minimal Ordered Structures Beyond Almost Linearity

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In standard model-theoretic terminology, an infinite structure $\mathcal M$ with universe $M$ is called minimal if every subset of $M$ definable in $\mathcal M$, with parameters from $M$, is finite or cofinite. Here cofinite means that its complement in $M$ is finite. The supplied record does not explicitly specify its convention for minimality, so this standard convention requires confirmation against the cited report.

The supplied existence question is: does there exist a minimal ordered structure that is not almost linear? The distinguished group case asks whether such a structure can be a minimal ordered group.

More formally, the intended questions have the schematic forms \[ \exists\mathcal M\;\left(\mathcal M\text{ is minimal and ordered in the source's sense, and is not almost linear in the source's sense}\right) \] and, for the group case, \[ \exists\mathcal G\;\left(\mathcal G\text{ is a minimal ordered group in the source's sense, and is not almost linear in the source's sense}\right). \] These are existential questions; the group case imposes an additional requirement on the witness. The supplied record gives no definition of "ordered" or "almost linear." In particular, it does not specify the order axioms, whether the order is named or definable with parameters, its compatibility with a group operation, or the precise almost-linearity condition. These source-dependent predicates remain unresolved, so the displays are a faithful statement schema rather than a complete self-contained formalization.

Short English statement

Does a minimal ordered structure exist that is not almost linear? Can such an example be an ordered group? The source-specific meanings of "ordered" and "almost linear," and the convention for minimality, require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that status.
