Definitions and notation.
A classical system has a phase-space evolution and a stationary probability measure, invariant under that evolution. A nonconservative external force performs work not represented by a scalar potential; thermostat forces remove energy so that stationary averages can exist. An entropy creation rate would be an observable or measurable functional of the nonequilibrium stationary state. Give a fundamental and experimentally accessible definition of the entropy creation rate for general classical systems in stationary nonequilibrium states under external nonconservative forces balanced on average by thermostat forces, without restricting to systems close to equilibrium.

A stationary jump-process test of an entropy definition.
As a limited analogue of the classical thermostatted problem, consider a finite irreducible continuous-time Markov chain with stationary probabilities $\pi_i>0$ and transition rates $k_{ij}$, with $k_{ij}>0$ exactly when $k_{ji}>0$. Define $a_{ij}=\pi_i k_{ij}$. The candidate stationary production rate is \[\sigma=\frac12\sum_{i\ne j}(a_{ij}-a_{ji})\log\frac{a_{ij}}{a_{ji}}\ge0.\] Each unordered-pair contribution is nonnegative because logarithm is increasing; equality holds precisely when every pair has equal stationary flow. Zero-rate pairs are omitted. This supplies a fully measurable formula if transition counts and stationary frequencies can be estimated, under the explicit Markov assumptions.
The role of stationary averaging.
Expanding the logarithm, the contribution containing $\log\pi_i-\log\pi_j$ vanishes. Indeed stationarity says $\sum_j(a_{ij}-a_{ji})=0$ for every $i$, and summation by parts gives \[\sigma=\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}\log(k_{ij}/k_{ji}).\] For a three-state ring with clockwise rate $a>0$ and counterclockwise rate $b>0$, the stationary distribution is uniform, and $\sigma=(a-b)\log(a/b)$. Thus nonzero circulation is detected without a near-equilibrium expansion. When $a=b$, detailed balance holds and the rate vanishes.
Why this is not a general thermostat definition.
The identification of $\log(k_{ij}/k_{ji})$ with measured heat divided by temperature requires an additional physical relation between rates and reservoirs. The chain calculation does not establish that relation, and the source explicitly includes regimes with no established temperature. A deterministic system also need not possess an exact finite-state Markov description; coarse observation can hide circulation. Deriving an invariant and experimentally meaningful entropy rate for the full classical model class, together with relations among stationary dynamical averages, remains the first and larger gap. This reconstructs a standard finite-state benchmark only.
