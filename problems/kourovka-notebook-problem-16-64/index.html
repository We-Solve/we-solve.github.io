Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.64

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group variety is a class of groups defined by a collection of group identities. A variety $\mathcal V$ is non-abelian if it contains a non-abelian group. A non-abelian variety $\mathcal V$ is called pseudo-abelian if every finite group belonging to $\mathcal V$ is abelian.

Write $H\trianglelefteq G$ when $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$. A group variety $\mathcal V$ is a $t$-variety if normality is transitive within every group in $\mathcal V$; explicitly, for every $G\in\mathcal V$ and all subgroups $K\leq H\leq G$, \[ K\trianglelefteq H\ \text{ and }\ H\trianglelefteq G \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\trianglelefteq G. \]

Question (O. Macedońska, 2006). Is every pseudo-abelian group variety a $t$-variety? Equivalently, if a group variety $\mathcal V$ contains a non-abelian group and all its finite groups are abelian, must the displayed implication hold for every $G\in\mathcal V$ and every subgroup chain $K\leq H\leq G$?

The supplied source reports that every non-abelian $t$-variety is pseudo-abelian, citing O. Macedońska and A. Storozhev (1997), and that the pseudo-abelian varieties constructed by A. Yu. Olshanskii (1986) are $t$-varieties. These background assertions are retained as source reports and have not been independently verified. The supplied registry labels the question open; this label is not an independent assessment of its current status.

Short English statement

A pseudo-abelian variety contains a non-abelian group, but every finite group in it is abelian. Must every group in such a variety have transitive normality: whenever $K$ is normal in $H$ and $H$ is normal in $G$, is $K$ normal in $G$?
