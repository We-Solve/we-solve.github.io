Definitions and notation.
The geometric Langlands programme relates geometric objects associated with a reductive group and its Langlands dual, such as local systems and sheaf-theoretic data on moduli spaces of bundles over a curve. In quantum field theory a duality relates descriptions of physical observables or state spaces. The challenge is to make connections between such mathematical correspondences and fundamental physical symmetries precise.

An abelian lattice calculation.
A modest place to test a symmetry interpretation is a torus $T=(S^1)^r$. Its character lattice $X^*(T)$ consists of continuous homomorphisms $T\to S^1$, and its cocharacter lattice $X_*(T)$ consists of homomorphisms $S^1\to T$; both identify with $\mathbb Z^r$. Their intrinsic pairing is composition: a character applied to a cocharacter gives $z\mapsto z^{\langle m,n\rangle}$. The dual torus interchanges these two lattices.
A compatible charge symmetry.
For electric and magnetic labels $(m,n)\in X^*(T)\oplus X_*(T)$, define the alternating pairing \[\Omega((m,n),(m',n'))=\langle m,n'\rangle-\langle m',n\rangle.\] It is integral, and the transformation $S(m,n)=(n,-m)$, interpreted on the dual torus, preserves it: substitution gives $\langle n,-m'\rangle-\langle n',-m\rangle=\Omega$. Moreover $S^2=-I$. This verifies the algebraic electric-magnetic exchange at the lattice level without assuming a quantum field theory. For rank one and a complex parameter $\tau$ with $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$, the positive quadratic expression \[Q_\tau(m,n)=\frac{|m+\tau n|^2}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\] is invariant under $\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$, $(m,n)\mapsto(n,-m)$: the numerator becomes $|m+\tau n|^2/|\tau|^2$ and the denominator becomes $\operatorname{Im}\tau/|\tau|^2$. The positivity assumption excludes division by a vanishing imaginary part.
Missing bridge.
Preservation of a charge pairing and a quadratic form does not construct state spaces, observables, a path integral or a sheaf-theoretic correspondence. Establishing that a specified quantum theory realizes the exchange, and that its mathematical objects produce the relevant geometric Langlands equivalence, is the first missing bridge.
