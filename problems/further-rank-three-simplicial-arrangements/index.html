Further Rank-Three Simplicial Arrangements

Definitions and mathematical statement

The principal question in the supplied cleaned record concerns further rank-three simplicial arrangements. The record does not specify the underlying field, the equivalence relation on arrangements, or the collection of already known examples. The following gives a conventional real, central interpretation, conditional on these choices being intended.

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space and let $\mathcal A$ be a finite set of linear hyperplanes in $V$. Set \[ T=\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal A}H, \qquad \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal A)=\dim(V/T). \] For rank three, write $W=V/T$ and $\overline{\mathcal A}=\{H/T:H\in\mathcal A\}$. Thus $\dim W=3$. The arrangement is called simplicial if every connected component $C$ of \[ W\setminus\bigcup_{\overline H\in\overline{\mathcal A}}\overline H \] is an open simplicial cone: for some basis $v_1,v_2,v_3$ of $W$, depending on $C$, \[ C=\{t_1v_1+t_2v_2+t_3v_3:t_1,t_2,t_3>0\}. \] Principal question. Are there further rank-three simplicial arrangements, or further infinite series of such arrangements, beyond the examples and series already known in the intended source classification?

To express the first alternative precisely, one must specify an equivalence relation $\sim$ and a reference collection $\mathscr K$ of known equivalence classes. With those choices, the question becomes whether there exists a rank-three simplicial arrangement $\mathcal A$ such that $[\mathcal A]_{\sim}\notin\mathscr K$. Neither choice is supplied. The meaning of a further infinite series also requires clarification: the source does not specify its parameter set, conditions for distinct members, or how novelty relative to known series is determined. Consequently, these missing data prevent a fully specified classification question.

Additional target in the original statement. The original record also asks whether Terao's freeness conjecture holds over a fixed field. This target is absent from the cleaned statement; its intended inclusion remains unresolved. In the conventional finite central setting, fix a field $K$, let $S=K[x_1,\ldots,x_\ell]$, and write each hyperplane $H$ of an arrangement $\mathcal B$ in $K^\ell$ as $H=\ker\alpha_H$, where $\alpha_H$ is a nonzero linear form. Define \[ D(\mathcal B)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Der}_K(S):\theta(\alpha_H)\in\alpha_H S\text{ for every }H\in\mathcal B\}. \] The arrangement is free when $D(\mathcal B)$ is a free $S$-module. Its intersection lattice $L(\mathcal B)$ consists of intersections of subfamilies of $\mathcal B$, including the empty intersection $K^\ell$, ordered by reverse inclusion. The corresponding conjectural statement is that, for every fixed field $K$, every positive integer $\ell$, and all finite central arrangements $\mathcal B,\mathcal B'$ in $K^\ell$, \[ L(\mathcal B)\cong L(\mathcal B') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\mathcal B\text{ is free}\iff\mathcal B'\text{ is free}\right). \] The supplied wording imposes no characteristic restriction on the fixed field. The central setting used in this formulation, and the real setting used for simpliciality above, are explicit conventional interpretations requiring confirmation rather than additional source hypotheses.
