Rigid Subgraphs of the Curve Complex

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied registry asks: Which subgraphs of the curve complex are rigid? The surface, the meaning of rigidity, and even the antecedent of "the complex" in the extracted passage are unresolved.

For a conventional interpretation, let $S$ be a connected orientable surface of finite type. An essential, nonperipheral simple closed curve is an embedded circle that bounds neither a disk nor a once-punctured disk and is not isotopic to a boundary component. The usual disjointness curve graph $\mathcal C^1(S)$ has isotopy classes of such curves as vertices; distinct vertices are adjacent when they admit disjoint representatives. The corresponding curve complex has simplices given by collections admitting pairwise disjoint representatives. These are conditional conventions: the record specifies neither this surface class nor whether modified adjacency conventions for surfaces of low complexity are intended.

One possible precise framework for the requested classification is the following. Fix $S$, a collection $\mathscr H$ of subgraphs of $\mathcal C^1(S)$, a group $G$ acting on $\mathcal C^1(S)$, and, for each $H\in\mathscr H$, a specified class $\mathscr M(H,\mathcal C^1(S))$ of admissible maps. Define the extension property by \[ \operatorname{Ext}_{G,\mathscr M}(H) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall f\in\mathscr M(H,\mathcal C^1(S))\; \exists g\in G\; \forall v\in V(H),\quad f(v)=g\cdot v. \] Under an extension-based interpretation of rigidity, the problem is to characterize \[ \left\{H\in\mathscr H:\operatorname{Ext}_{G,\mathscr M}(H)\right\}. \] The supplied record does not determine $\mathscr H$, $G$, or $\mathscr M$, or whether uniqueness of an extension is required. In particular, it does not specify finite versus arbitrary subgraphs, induced versus arbitrary subgraphs, locally injective simplicial maps versus other maps, or graph automorphisms versus homeomorphism-induced actions. Consequently, this is a conditional formalization schema, not an identification of the intended theorem. The source context must be recovered before a unique mathematical statement can be fixed.
