Circuits and Thresholds in Rank-Two Matrix Completion

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record contains two requests concerning low-rank matrix completion. It does not specify the ground field, the graph model, or the meaning of "completable." The following gives a conditional algebraic formulation and identifies the choices that remain unresolved.

For a chosen algebraically closed field $k$, let $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and let \[ X_{N,n}^{(2)}=\{A\in k^{N\times n}:\operatorname{rank}(A)\leq 2\}. \] Write $x_{ij}$ for its coordinate functions and $k(X_{N,n}^{(2)})$ for its function field. The associated algebraic matroid has ground set $[N]\times[n]$; a subset $S$ is independent if the functions $x_{ij}$, $(i,j)\in S$, are algebraically independent over $k$. A circuit is a dependent subset all of whose proper subsets are independent. For a circuit $C$, a circuit polynomial is an irreducible generator, unique up to multiplication by a nonzero element of $k$, of \[ \ker\!\left(k[t_{ij}:(i,j)\in C]\longrightarrow k(X_{N,n}^{(2)}),\quad t_{ij}\longmapsto x_{ij}\right). \] Under this interpretation, the first request is to characterize all circuits of these rank-two determinantal matroids and their circuit polynomials as the matrix dimensions vary. The choice of $k$, including its characteristic, is not supplied and requires confirmation. Rank at most two is the algebraic variety used here; the source says rank two without specifying whether only matrices of rank exactly two are intended.

For an observed set $E\subseteq[N]\times[n]$, put $k(x_E)=k(x_{ij}:(i,j)\in E)$. An entry $(a,b)$ is generically finitely completable in this algebraic interpretation if $x_{ab}$ is algebraic over $k(x_E)$. Thus generic finite completability of every entry means \[ \forall(a,b)\in[N]\times[n],\qquad x_{ab}\text{ is algebraic over }k(x_E). \] This differs from existence of a completion and from generic unique completion. For observed values $y\in k^E$, existence means that the fiber of the coordinate projection $\pi_E:X_{N,n}^{(2)}\to k^E$ over $y$ is nonempty; uniqueness means that this fiber consists of one matrix. Any generic qualification must specify the relevant dense open subset of the observation image. The source does not choose among these meanings or specify generic observed values.

The second request sets $N=n$ and takes observed positions from a uniformly random $2r$-regular graph. To illustrate one possible precise interpretation, fix a positive integer $r$, and, for each $n\geq\max\{2,2r\}$, let $G_n$ be uniformly distributed among simple bipartite graphs with labeled vertex classes $[n]_{\mathrm{row}}$ and $[n]_{\mathrm{column}}$, every vertex having degree $2r$. Let $E(G_n)\subseteq[n]\times[n]$ be its edge set. If "completable" means generically finitely completable, the requested asymptotic question becomes whether \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left( \forall(a,b)\in[n]\times[n],\quad x_{ab}\text{ is algebraic over }k(x_{E(G_n)}) \right)=1. \] This displayed question is conditional on the stated graph and completion conventions. The supplied record does not establish those conventions, say whether $r$ is fixed or varies with $n$, or explain its relationship to the specified rank two. In particular, setting $r=2$ requires confirmation. An ordinary undirected graph would require a different rule associating edges with matrix entries, including a convention for symmetry and diagonal entries. Consequently, the record does not determine a single unqualified probability statement.

Short English statement

Characterize the minimal algebraic dependencies among entries of rank-two matrices and the polynomial relation attached to each such dependency. Also determine whether observations indexed by a uniformly random $2r$-regular graph determine all entries, in the intended completion sense, with probability tending to one as the square matrix size tends to infinity. The field, graph model, role of $r$, genericity assumptions, and distinction between existence, finite completion, and unique completion remain unspecified.
