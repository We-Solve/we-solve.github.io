Math-1218

Euler--Chow--Witt comparison, real realization, and a sphere test

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Determine the connection between Euler class groups and Chow--Witt groups. In a smooth affine $d$-dimensional setting $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, the top Euler class group $E^d(A)$ records oriented codimension-$d$ local complete intersections modulo global complete-intersection relations. Chow--Witt groups retain quadratic orientation data; with a line-bundle twist $L$, their usual smooth characteristic-not-two description is $\widetilde{\operatorname{CH}}^r(X,L)=H^r(X,\mathbf K_r^{MW}(L))$. The original does not fix smoothness, affine hypotheses, dimension, field or twists.

Jean Fasel's accompanying question additionally asks for a product-compatible integral real cycle map lifting the mod-two cycle map. The source recalls product-compatible maps $\operatorname{CH}^r(X)\to H^{2r}(X({\mathbb{C}}),{\mathbb{Z}})$ over ${\mathbb{C}}$ and $\operatorname{CH}^r(X)\to H^r(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/2)$ over ${\mathbb{R}}$. It asks for a map from $\widetilde{\operatorname{CH}}^r(X,\omega_{X/{\mathbb{R}}})$ to $H^r(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}})$ commuting with forgetful Chow and mod-two reduction maps.

The attached unreviewed aid reports an isomorphism \[ E^d(A)\simeq\widetilde{\operatorname{CH}}^d(\operatorname{Spec}A) \] for smooth affine $d$-folds over infinite fields of characteristic not two. It reports that on a smooth quasi-projective real variety the natural integral realization with twist $L$ instead has target \[ H^r(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}(L)), \] where ${\mathbb{Z}}(L)$ is the sign local system of the real line bundle $L$. For $L=\omega_{X/{\mathbb{R}}}$ this is the orientation local system, so a constant-${\mathbb{Z}}$ formulation needs an orientation trivialization. Products naturally multiply twists: the $L,M$ groups map to the $L\otimes M$ group. Thus product compatibility should be stated for a family of twists; two $\omega$-twisted inputs do not automatically have an $\omega$-twisted product. The precise realization normalization and comparison theorem hypotheses are not supplied.

As a test family the aid takes $A_d={\mathbb{R}}[x_0,\ldots,x_d]/(\sum x_i^2-1)$ and its tangent module $P=\ker(A_d^{d+1}\to A_d)$, where $(v_i)\mapsto\sum x_iv_i$, with determinant orientation. It claims real Euler image $\pm\chi(S^d)$; for even $d\ge2$ this detects an infinite-order element in $\ker(E^d(A_d)\to E_0^d(A_d))$, where $E_0^d$ forgets orientation. For odd $d$ it reports an algebraic unit vector splitting $P$ and vanishing Euler class, but the vector is not given. These are aid claims, not a proof or unrestricted comparison theorem in this draft.

Short English statement

Compare Euler classes with quadratic Chow--Witt classes and construct the real integral cycle map asked for by Jean Fasel. Orientation local systems and multiplication of line-bundle twists are necessary to make the original diagram and product compatibility precise.
