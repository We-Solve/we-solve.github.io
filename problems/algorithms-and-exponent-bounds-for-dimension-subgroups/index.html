Math-4173

Algorithms and Exponent Bounds for Dimension Subgroups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finitely presented group, and let $n\geq 1$ be an integer. Define its lower central series by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{m+1}(G)=[\gamma_m(G),G]$, where $[H,G]$ denotes the subgroup generated by commutators $h^{-1}g^{-1}hg$. For the coefficient rings $k={\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}$, or ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ with $p$ prime, let \[ \varepsilon_k:kG\longrightarrow k,\qquad \varepsilon_k\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)=\sum_{g\in G}a_g, \qquad \Delta(kG)=\ker\varepsilon_k. \] The sums defining elements of $kG$ have finite support. Define the dimension subgroup \[ D_n^k(G)=\gamma_{n,k}(G)=\{g\in G:g-1\in\Delta(kG)^n\}. \] For a subgroup $H\leq G$, write $H^r=\langle h^r:h\in H\rangle$ for $r\geq1$. The corrected source formulation supplies the identities \[ D_n^{{\mathbb{F}}_p}(G) =\left\langle\gamma_m(G)^{p^j}:m\geq1,\ j\geq0,\ mp^j\geq n\right\rangle, \qquad D_n^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)=\sqrt{\gamma_n(G)}, \] where \[ \sqrt{\gamma_n(G)}=\{g\in G:g^r\in\gamma_n(G)\text{ for some integer }r\geq1\}. \] The generated subgroup in the first identity is the meaning of the source's product $\prod_{mp^j\geq n}\gamma_m(G)^{p^j}$.

The supplied record asks the following questions.

  1. Given a finite presentation of $G$, an integer $n\geq1$, and a prime $p$, how can one efficiently compute $G/D_n^{{\mathbb{F}}_p}(G)$? Given a finite presentation of $G$ and $n$, how can one efficiently compute $D_n^{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G)$? The source does not specify an output representation for these computations or a complexity criterion for efficiency.
  2. Does every finitely presented group $G$ satisfy, for every $n\geq1$, \[ D_n^{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G)=\bigcap_{p\text{ prime}}D_n^{{\mathbb{F}}_p}(G)? \]
  3. Is $2$ a universal exponent bound for the dimension quotients $D_n^{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G)/\gamma_n(G)$? In explicit quantifiers, the exponent-two interpretation is \[ \text{for every finitely presented }G,\quad \text{every }n\geq1,\quad \text{and every }x\in D_n^{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G),\qquad x^2\in\gamma_n(G). \] The record writes this question as $c_n=2$, where $c_n$ bounds the exponent of $D_n^{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G)/\gamma_n(G)$. It does not define $c_n$ precisely. The displayed formulation treats $2$ as an annihilating exponent, permitting trivial quotients; it does not require every quotient to have exponent exactly $2$, or assert that the least universal bound equals $2$ for every $n$.

There is a supplied textual discrepancy: the original prose states $D_n^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)=\gamma_n(G)$, whereas the clean and corrected statements give the isolator formula above. This draft follows the explicitly designated corrected formulation and retains that discrepancy for review. The record labels the statement corrected_verified and the problem partially solved; those are supplied curation labels, not independent verification by this draft.

Short English statement

For finitely presented groups, seek efficient methods to compute dimension subgroups over ${\mathbb{Z}}$ and the corresponding quotients over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$. The record also asks whether the integral dimension subgroup equals the intersection of its counterparts over all prime fields, and whether every integral dimension quotient has exponent dividing $2$. The meaning of efficiency and the precise definition of $c_n$ remain unspecified; the supplied rational formulas also require reconciliation.
