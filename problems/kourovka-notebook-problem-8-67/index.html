Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.67

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the conventional interpretation that a nil-ring is an associative ring $R$, not necessarily with an identity, such that for every $x\in R$ there exists an integer $n\geq 1$ with $x^n=0$. Its adjoint group $R^\circ$ has underlying set $R$ and operation \[ x\circ y=x+y+xy, \] with identity $0$. Following the supplied source, a Golod group means a finitely generated, non-nilpotent subgroup $G\leq R^\circ$ for some nil-ring $R$. Here non-nilpotent means that the lower central series $\gamma_1(G)=G$, $\gamma_{j+1}(G)=[\gamma_j(G),G]$ never reaches the trivial subgroup.

Interpret finite rank as finite Prüfer rank: an abelian group $A$ has finite rank if there exists an integer $r_A\geq 0$ such that every finitely generated subgroup of $A$ can be generated by at most $r_A$ elements.

Under these conventions, the question is whether there exist a nil-ring $R$ and a finitely generated, non-nilpotent subgroup $G\leq R^\circ$ such that \[ \forall A\leq G\quad \left(A\text{ abelian}\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists r_A\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\ \forall H\leq A\, (H\text{ finitely generated}\ \Longrightarrow\ d(H)\leq r_A)\right), \] where $d(H)$ denotes the least number of generators of the finitely generated group $H$. The bound may depend on $A$; no common bound for all abelian subgroups is requested.

The supplied record does not explicitly define rank or the ring conventions, so the interpretation above requires confirmation against the original source. It also reports a negative answer for the whole adjoint group of a nil-ring, attributing this to Ya. P. Sysak. The exact hypotheses of that reported result are not supplied; this qualification is retained as a source report rather than expanded into an independently verified theorem.

Short English statement

Does some nil-ring have an adjoint group containing a finitely generated, non-nilpotent subgroup all of whose abelian subgroups have finite rank? Under the stated interpretation, rank means Prüfer rank, with a separate finite bound allowed for each abelian subgroup. The supplied source reports a negative result for whole adjoint groups.
