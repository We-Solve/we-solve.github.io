Statement

For integers $m\ge 0$ and $k\ge 1$ define the rising factorial block \[ P_k(m) \;:=\; \prod_{i=1}^{k}(m+i) \;=\; \frac{(m+k)!}{m!}. \] The problem asks about integer solutions to \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-50-33-38-38-2d-6d-61-69-6e} P_{k_1}(m_1)=P_{k_2}(m_2) \end{equation} under the constraints \[ k_1,k_2>3,\qquad m_1,m_2\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0},\qquad m_1+k_1\le m_2. \] The wording "Are there only finitely many solutions?" is ambiguous:

  • (A) Fixed lengths: for each fixed pair $(k_1,k_2)$ with $k_1,k_2>3$, are there only finitely many pairs $(m_1,m_2)$ satisfying (eq:P388-main) and $m_1+k_1\le m_2$?
  • (B) All parameters: are there only finitely many quadruples $(m_1,k_1,m_2,k_2)$ with $k_1,k_2>3$ and $m_1+k_1\le m_2$ satisfying (eq:P388-main)?
The statement "classify all solutions" suggests (B), while the appearance of fixed $a,b$ in the generalization suggests (A) as a natural first step.

A more general variant mentioned is \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-50-33-38-38-2d-61-62} a\,P_{k_1}(m_1)=b\,P_{k_2}(m_2), \end{equation} with fixed nonzero integers $a,b$ and $k_1>2$.
