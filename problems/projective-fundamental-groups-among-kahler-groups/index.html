Math-847

Projective Fundamental Groups Among Kähler Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A Kähler group is a group isomorphic to $\pi_1(X,x)$ for some connected compact Kähler manifold $X$ and base point $x\in X$. Here a Kähler manifold is a complex manifold admitting a Hermitian metric whose associated real $(1,1)$-form is closed. A smooth complex projective variety means a smooth connected complex algebraic variety admitting a closed algebraic embedding into ${\mathbb{P}}^n_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ for some finite integer $n\geq 0$. Fundamental groups are taken in the complex analytic topology and compared up to abstract group isomorphism; their isomorphism classes do not depend on the chosen base points.

Question. Does there exist a connected compact Kähler manifold $X$ such that \[ \forall\,Y\text{ a smooth connected complex projective variety},\qquad \pi_1(X,x)\not\cong\pi_1(Y,y), \] where $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$ are arbitrary base points? There is no prescribed dimension for $X$ or $Y$.

Equivalently, let $\mathcal P$ be the class of isomorphism classes of fundamental groups of smooth connected complex projective varieties, and let $\mathcal K$ be the class of Kähler groups up to isomorphism. Is the inclusion $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal K$ strict?

The compactness and connectedness conventions above make explicit the standard meaning of "Kähler group" used in this formulation. The supplied record labels the problem open and its statement corrected_verified; these are supplied curation labels, not independent verification by this draft.

Short English statement

Does some compact connected Kähler manifold have a fundamental group that cannot occur as the fundamental group of any smooth connected complex projective variety, in any dimension? The supplied record describes this question as open; this statement-only draft requires human mathematical review.
