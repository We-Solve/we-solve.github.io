Uniform Cartier-section thresholds for Fano-type contractions

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every $d>0$ and real $\varepsilon >0$, does $\tau (d,\varepsilon )>0$ exist such that each $\varepsilon -lc$ rational d-dimensional pair (X,B), Fano-type contraction $f:X\to Z$ of normal integral quasi-projective varieties with dim $Z>0$ and relatively nef $-(K_X+B)$, and closed $z\in Z$ admits a neighborhood U and nonzero effective Cartier D on U through z with $(f^-1(U),B+\tau $ f*D) lc? Fields are algebraically closed of characteristic zero.

Uniform Cartier-section thresholds for Fano-type contractions

OpenAI's claim

For the stated Fano-type contractions and any chosen base point, a Cartier divisor containing that point is claimed to have a pullback threshold bounded below uniformly in dimension and discrepancy gap. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Over a field that has characteristic zero and is algebraically closed, fix $d>0$ and real $\varepsilon>0$. Let $(X,B)$ be an effective rational pair of dimension $d$ with all log discrepancies at least $\varepsilon$ and $-(K_X+B)$ nef over a Fano-type contraction $f:X\to Z$, with $X$ and $Z$ normal, integral and quasi-projective, $\dim Z>0$. Fano type means that some effective rational auxiliary $C$ makes $(X,C)$ klt and $-(K_X+C)$ relatively ample. OpenAI claims a uniform $\tau(d,\varepsilon)>0$ such that for every closed $z\in Z$ there is a neighborhood $U$ and nonzero effective Cartier $D$ on $U$ through $z$ with \[ (f^{-1}(U),B|_{f^{-1}(U)}+\tau f^*D)\text{ log canonical}. \] The threshold controls the whole inverse image of the neighborhood, independent of indices and boundary denominators; $D$ and $U$ may depend on $z$ and the contraction.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 066, 2026-09-25): Ratios of opposite character orders impose exact invariant relations on a constrained valuation slice. Finitely generated central algebras retain another independent variable unless they produce a quotient prime whose discrepancy tends to zero. Finite ambient dimension prevents unlimited variable growth, while the positive discrepancy gap excludes the alternative.
