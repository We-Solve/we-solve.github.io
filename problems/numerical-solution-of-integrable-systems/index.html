Definitions and notation.
The inverse-scattering forward map sends initial data to a reflection coefficient and, where present, discrete eigenvalues and norming constants. The inverse map is a matrix Riemann--Hilbert problem. Small dispersion introduces $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ in the forward spectral problem. The Airy operator on $L^2(s,\infty)$ has kernel $K(\lambda,\mu)=[\operatorname{Ai}(\lambda)\operatorname{Ai}'(\mu)-\operatorname{Ai}'(\lambda)\operatorname{Ai}(\mu)]/(\lambda-\mu)$, with diagonal defined by continuity.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Apply and extend the Olver--Deconinck--Trogdon numerical Riemann--Hilbert framework to integrable systems, addressing both forward scattering and inverse reconstruction. In particular, solve the non-self-adjoint forward problem for focusing NLS with general smooth initial data and reconstruct the solution in the small-dispersion limit $\varepsilon=\hbar\downarrow0$. Analyze Airy-operator eigenfunctions as $s\to\infty$ and compute them reliably for finite $s$. Continue the Painlevé resource project collecting algebraic, analytic and numerical information, with reliable computation of representative solutions.

A positive representation of the Airy kernel.
Let $a=\operatorname{Ai}$, satisfying $a''(x)=xa(x)$ and decaying at positive infinity. Define $W(t)=a(x+t)a'(y+t)-a'(x+t)a(y+t)$. Then $W'(t)=(y-x)a(x+t)a(y+t)$. Integration from zero to infinity, using the decay of $a,a'$, gives \[K(x,y)=\frac{a(x)a'(y)-a'(x)a(y)}{x-y}=\int_0^\infty a(x+t)a(y+t)\,dt. \] Consequently for compactly supported $f$, \[\langle f,Kf\rangle=\int_0^\infty\left|\int_s^\infty a(x+t)f(x)dx\right|^2dt\ge0. \] The diagonal is $K(x,x)=a'(x)^2-xa(x)^2$, obtained by differentiating numerator and denominator with respect to $y$, including the minus sign in the denominator derivative.
What this offers for computation.
If an integral quadrature with positive weights approximates the displayed factorization by a finite matrix $B$, the approximation $BB^*$ is positive semidefinite automatically. This preserves positivity but supplies no error bound. Small operator norm also does not by itself identify a normalized eigenfunction when adjacent eigenvalues are nearly equal; the needed perturbation control must be small relative to their gap.
