Math-1529

Generating families, augmentations, and the missing quantifiers

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Ekholm's Question 1.4 has two tentative parts: (a) compare linearized contact homology with generating-function homology for a possibly compact Legendrian $L\subset J^1({\mathbb{R}}^n)$, with the generating-function definition left in brackets; (b) ask whether a generating function exists if and only if linearized contact homology is "(uniquely) defined." Part (b) literally prints $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, which does not specify a contact manifold or Legendrian dimension. The supplied source report says this is genuine source wording, not an OCR error; the likely $J^1({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ interpretation remains explicit rather than silent.

The aid's precise partial answer concerns closed chord-generic Legendrian knots \[ L\subset J^1({\mathbb{R}})=T^*{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_z, \qquad\alpha={\,\mathrm{d}} z-p\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] with an integer Maslov grading, in particular the rotation-zero grading regime, and coefficients ${\mathbb{F}}_2$. Let $(\mathcal A_L,\partial)$ be the Chekanov--Eliashberg DGA. A graded augmentation is a unital graded DGA map $\epsilon:(\mathcal A_L,\partial)\to({\mathbb{F}}_2,0)$. Linearizing after this augmentation gives a chain complex generated by Reeb chords and homology $LCH_*^\epsilon(L)$. The augmentation is part of the datum, not automatically unique.

An admissible generating family $F:{\mathbb{R}}_x\times{\mathbb{R}}_\eta^N\to{\mathbb{R}}$ has regular fibre-critical locus $\{\partial_\eta F=0\}$ parametrizing $L$ by \[ (x,\eta)\longmapsto(x,\partial_xF,F). \] Use the linear-at-infinity class stipulated by the aid, with its required tameness/stabilization conventions. For the difference function $\delta_F(x,\eta,\eta')=F(x,\eta')-F(x,\eta)$, a standard grading convention is \[ GH_k(F)=H_{k+N+1}(\delta_F^{\leq\Omega}, \delta_F^{\leq\varepsilon};{\mathbb{F}}_2), \] where $\varepsilon>0$ is below all positive critical values and $\Omega$ is above them. The exact class at infinity and grading conventions must be matched to the intended comparison; the original brackets do not define them.

In this knot setting the aid reports the equivalence \[ L\text{ admits an admissible generating family} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\text{ has at least one graded augmentation}. \] It attributes the route to normal-ruling equivalences. Furthermore, for each such family $F$ it reports a compatible augmentation $\epsilon_F$ with \[ GH_*(F)\simeq LCH_*^{\epsilon_F}(L) \] under matched grading conventions. This has the quantifier $\forall F\ \exists\epsilon_F$; it is not an assertion that every augmentation and every family give the same invariant, nor a bijection or uniqueness theorem.

The aid reports the Melvin--Shrestha Legendrian representative of the mirror knot $m(8_{21})$ as a counterexample to the uniqueness reading. It admits a generating family but has graded augmentations $\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1$ with distinct polynomials \[ P_{\epsilon_i}(t)=\sum_k \dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_2}LCH_k^{\epsilon_i}(L)\,t^k, \qquad P_{\epsilon_0}\ne P_{\epsilon_1}. \] The front diagram, DGA and numerical polynomials are not supplied, so this example is preserved as a named literature report rather than reconstructed data. It refutes uniqueness of augmentation-dependent graded homology, not the existence equivalence. The unrestricted higher-dimensional equality in (a) is not resolved here. The source's reported evidence for a Legendrian formed as the double of a compact $n$-manifold with boundary $M\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ lacks its construction and comparison hypotheses; the trailing 4 is a footnote marker, not an exponent.

Theme 1.5 is adjoining research context, explicitly not a question: it mentions Fukaya--Oh/Witten degeneration of holomorphic discs to gradient trees; work of Lenny Ng and Zhu Ke on relative contact homology; Mikhalkin's amoeba/fibre-shrinking perspective in a Lagrangian fibration; and comparisons involving the Chas--Sullivan loop-space product and Floer cup product. None of these remarks supplies a further theorem or resolves the missing hypotheses of Question 1.4.

Short English statement

In the stated knot, grading and coefficient setting, generating families correspond to existence of an augmentation and select a matching linearized homology. They do not force uniqueness: the aid reports a generating-family knot with two different linearized homologies. Higher-dimensional and function-space qualifications remain open in this record.
