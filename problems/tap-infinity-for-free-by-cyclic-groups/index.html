TAP Infinity for Free-by-Cyclic Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A finitely generated free group is a group isomorphic to the free group $F_n$ on a finite basis of size $n$. The original source describes the groups under consideration as $\{\text{finitely generated free}\}$-by-cyclic groups. In extension notation, this means groups $G$ admitting an exact sequence \[ 1\longrightarrow F_n\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 1, \] where $F_n$ is finitely generated and free and $C$ is cyclic. The supplied excerpt does not specify whether "cyclic" means infinite cyclic or also permits finite cyclic quotients; the intended range of $C$ must therefore be checked before this becomes a fully specified quantified statement.

Conjecture 6 (as supplied). Does every $\{\text{finitely generated free}\}$-by-cyclic group $G$ belong to \[ \operatorname{TAP}_{\infty}(R)? \] The supplied record does not define $\operatorname{TAP}_{\infty}(R)$, specify the nature or hypotheses of the parameter $R$, or state its quantifier. Membership in this class is consequently retained as source notation, without an invented definition. The question is a statement schema pending recovery of those definitions and conventions. In particular, the original wording requires the free normal subgroup to be finitely generated; this requirement must not be replaced merely by finite generation of $G$.

Short English statement

The supplied Conjecture 6 asks whether every group with a finitely generated free normal subgroup and cyclic quotient lies in $\operatorname{TAP}_{\infty}(R)$. A complete formulation requires the source definition of this class, the assumptions and quantifier on $R$, and the convention for cyclic quotients. The registry labels the problem open; this is supplied curation, not an independently verified status.
