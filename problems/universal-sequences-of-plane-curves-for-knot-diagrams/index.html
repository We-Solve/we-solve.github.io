Math-6765

Universal Sequences of Plane Curves for Knot Diagrams

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A generic plane immersion is understood here as a smooth immersion $\gamma:S^1\to{\mathbb{R}}^2$ whose only multiple points are finitely many transverse double points. A crossing assignment chooses, at each double point, which of the two branches passes over the other. This produces a knot diagram; it does not mean smoothing the crossing.

For a specified collection $\mathscr K$ of knot types, let \[ \mathcal D(\gamma)=\{K\in\mathscr K: \text{some crossing assignment on }\gamma\text{ gives a diagram of }K\}. \] The usual interpretation takes $\mathscr K$ to be the ambient isotopy classes of tame, unoriented knots in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$. This convention, as well as the smoothness convention above, is not expressly specified in the supplied record and requires confirmation.

Let $(\gamma_n)_{n\geq 1}$ be a sequence of generic plane immersions of $S^1$. Under the eventual-tail interpretation of "eventually carries every knot," the requested problem is to find necessary and sufficient conditions on this sequence for \[ \forall K\in\mathscr K\;\exists N_K\geq 1\;\forall n\geq N_K, \qquad K\in\mathcal D(\gamma_n). \tag{1} \] The crossing assignment may depend on both $K$ and $n$.

Unresolved quantifier qualification. The supplied statement does not explicitly distinguish (1) from the weaker occurrence property \[ \forall K\in\mathscr K\;\exists n\geq 1, \qquad K\in\mathcal D(\gamma_n). \tag{2} \] No nesting or monotonicity hypothesis on the sequence is supplied, so these formulations must not be identified. A definitive characterization problem requires confirmation of the intended quantifiers. The supplied record labels the characterization problem open; this label is not an independently verified mathematical or literature conclusion.

Short English statement

Characterize sequences of immersed plane circles whose crossings can be assigned over/under information to represent every knot. The source leaves unclear whether each knot must occur on every sufficiently late curve or merely on some curve. The knot category and regularity conventions also require confirmation.
