Asymptotic Minimal Quadratic Growth of Gorenstein Algebras

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied statement uses graded pieces but does not specify a ground field or its characteristic. To make the usual graded interpretation explicit, fix a field $k$. Consider commutative standard graded algebras \[ A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]/I=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_i, \] where each variable has degree $1$, $I$ is a homogeneous ideal generated by quadratic forms, and $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$. The codimension is $\dim_k A_1=r$. Write $\mathfrak m=\bigoplus_{i>0}A_i$ and define \[ \operatorname{Soc}(A)=\{a\in A:\mathfrak m a=0\}. \] In this setting, the Artinian Gorenstein condition with socle degree $4$ means \[ \operatorname{Soc}(A)=A_4,\qquad \dim_k A_4=1,\qquad A_i=0\quad(i>4). \] Let $QG_k(r)$ denote the class of these algebras. Whenever this class is nonempty, define \[ \nu_k(r)=\min\{\dim_k A_2:A\in QG_k(r)\}. \] The supplied source writes these objects as $QG(r)$ and $\nu(r)$, without specifying $k$. Its question is whether \[ \lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\nu(r)}{r^{2/3}}=6. \] Under the fixed-field interpretation above, this asks whether, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists an integer $R\geq 1$ such that for every integer $r\geq R$, \[ \left|\frac{\nu_k(r)}{r^{2/3}}-6\right|<\varepsilon. \] This formulation requires $QG_k(r)$ to be nonempty for all sufficiently large integers $r$. Neither that requirement nor the intended field hypotheses are supplied. The fixed-field notation clarifies a possible interpretation; it does not assert that the question is intended for every field or resolve the missing hypotheses.

Short English statement

Among quadratic-presented Artinian Gorenstein algebras of codimension $r$ and socle degree $4$, let $\nu(r)$ be the smallest dimension of the degree-two component. The question asks whether $\nu(r)/r^{2/3}$ tends to $6$. The ground-field hypotheses and the range of codimensions for which the minimum is defined require clarification.
