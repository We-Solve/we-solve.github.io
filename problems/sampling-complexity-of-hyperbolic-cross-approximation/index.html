It approximates on an irregular domain using polynomials orthogonal on an enclosing tensor-product domain, regularizes small singular values, and asks for improved sampling and nonrectangular-domain bounds. Research attempt: Leverage sampling on a triangle and the frame-conditioning gap

A finite-space sampling calculation.
Let $\mu$ be a probability measure and let real functions $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$, $N\ge1$, be orthonormal in $L^2(\mu)$. Assume they can be evaluated, and define \[ k(x)=\sum_{j=1}^N\phi_j(x)^2,\qquad \rho(x)=k(x)/N. \] Then $\int\rho\,d\mu=1$. Sample $X$ from $\rho\,d\mu$ and put $v(X)=\phi(X)/\sqrt{\rho(X)}$ where $\rho>0$. The zero-density set has sampling probability zero; all $\phi_j$ vanish there. Therefore \[ \mathbb E[vv^T]=I_N,\qquad \|v\|_2^2=N. \] For $m$ independent samples set $G=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m v_i v_i^T$. Entrywise independence and zero mean of $vv^T-I$ give \[ \mathbb E\|G-I\|_F^2 =\frac1m\mathbb E\|vv^T-I\|_F^2 =\frac{N^2-N}{m}. \] Here $\operatorname{tr}((vv^T)^2)=\|v\|^4=N^2$ and $\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(vv^T)=N$. Since $\|A\|_2\le\|A\|_F$, Markov's inequality implies \[ \Pr(\|G-I\|_2>\epsilon)\le\frac{N^2-N}{m\epsilon^2}. \] Thus $m\ge(N^2-N)/(\delta\epsilon^2)$ gives the two-sided norm comparison $(1-\epsilon)\|c\|^2\le c^TGc\le(1+\epsilon)\|c\|^2$ with probability at least $1-\delta$, for $0<\epsilon,\delta<1$. For $N=1$, the Gram matrix is identically $1$ for any $m\ge1$. This elementary bound is quadratic, not the near-linear estimate sought in the source.

Among arbitrary sampling densities $\eta$ positive wherever $k>0$, the worst squared row norm is $\operatorname*{ess\,sup}k/\eta$, necessarily at least $N$ because $\int k=N$ and $\int\eta=1$. Equality is attained by $\rho$. Likewise Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\int k^2/\eta\,d\mu\ge(\int k\,d\mu)^2=N^2$. Hence the proposed density minimizes these two particular row statistics. This is not a proof that it minimizes every sample-complexity or regularization criterion.

A nonrectangular example worked explicitly.
Let $\Omega=\{(x,y):x,y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\}$ with uniform probability measure $d\mu=2\,dx\,dy$. Direct integration gives $\mathbb Ex=\mathbb Ey=1/3$, $\mathbb Ex^2=\mathbb Ey^2=1/6$, and $\mathbb Exy=1/12$. An orthonormal basis of the affine polynomials is \[ 1,\qquad \sqrt{18}(x-1/3),\qquad \sqrt{24}(y+x/2-1/2). \] Indeed the last two have mean zero, variances one and covariance zero by the displayed moments. Here \[ k(x,y)=1+18(x-1/3)^2+24(y+x/2-1/2)^2. \] Its minimum is $1$ at the centroid, and its maximum is $9$: it is convex and every triangle point is a convex combination of vertices, where its value is $9$. Thus uniform sampling has worst squared row norm $9$, whereas sampling with density $k/3$ and the corresponding reciprocal-square-root weights makes it exactly $3$. This is a concrete distributional improvement on a triangle, though only for this three-dimensional space and these row statistics.
The failed transfer to the full frame problem.
For an arbitrary hyperbolic-cross restriction one could try to form its physical-domain Gram matrix and whiten it into an orthonormal basis. But that construction requires the very integrals and stable inverse square root not supplied by the enclosing-box model, and small eigenvalues can make the coefficient transform enormous. On a sphere, an ambient polynomial system containing $1,x_1^2,\ldots,x_d^2$ is even exactly dependent under restriction because $\sum x_i^2-1=0$; an inverse square root does not exist until the nullspace is removed. The physical $L^2$ comparison proved above consequently does not establish the original SVD-truncated ambient-coefficient estimates, a practical sampler on every implicit domain, or near-linear hyperbolic-cross complexity.
