Math-2118

Bernoulli factors of group shifts

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group, $d\ge1$, and $X\le G^{\mathbb Z^d}$ a closed subgroup invariant under all shifts $(\sigma^v x)_u=x_{u+v}$. Equip $X$ with the product topology and normalized Haar measure. This is a group shift; no commutativity assumption is imposed on $G$ or $X$. A Bernoulli group shift is $H^{\mathbb Z^d}$ with its shift action, where $H$ is a finite group.

An algebraic factor map is a continuous surjective group homomorphism $\pi:X\to H^{\mathbb Z^d}$ satisfying $\pi\sigma^v=\sigma^v\pi$ for every $v$. The substantive source question asks whether every nonabelian group shift has such a factor of equal entropy: \[ h_{\rm top}(X)=\log|H|. \] Here entropy is the asymptotic logarithmic growth of allowed patterns per lattice site, computed on increasingly large cubes. The full group shift on $H$ has entropy $\log|H|$.

The equal-entropy requirement is supplied by the original Boyle–Schraudner results underlying the survey. Without it the map to the one-element group would make the literal existence question vacuous. Their theorem already gives an equal-entropy topological Bernoulli factor for every group shift, and an algebraic such factor in the abelian case. The target is therefore to preserve multiplication in the nonabelian case. Neither a merely measurable factor nor a continuous factor that fails to be a homomorphism meets that requirement.

Short English statement

Does every nonabelian group shift have an equal-entropy Bernoulli factor through a continuous group homomorphism?
