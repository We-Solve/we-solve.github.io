Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.72

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 2$ be an integer, let $F_n$ be a free group of rank $n$, and let $H\leq F_n$. Interpret existentially closed in $F_n$ in the usual relative sense: every existential first-order formula in the language of groups, with parameters from $H$, that holds in $F_n$ also holds in $H$. Equivalently, every finite system of equations and inequations \[ w_i(x_1,\ldots,x_m;\mathbf h)=1\quad(1\leq i\leq r),\qquad v_j(x_1,\ldots,x_m;\mathbf h)\neq 1\quad(1\leq j\leq s), \] where $m,r,s$ are nonnegative integers, $\mathbf h$ is a finite tuple from $H$, and the $w_i,v_j$ are group words with these parameters, has a solution in $H^m$ whenever it has a solution in $F_n^m$.

A subgroup $H$ is a free factor of $F_n$ if there exists a subgroup $K\leq F_n$ such that the homomorphism $H*K\to F_n$ induced by the inclusions is an isomorphism. The subgroup $K$ may be trivial.

Question (A. G. Myasnikov and V. A. Roman'kov, Kourovka Notebook 18.72). For every integer $n\geq 2$ and every subgroup $H\leq F_n$, does existential closedness of $H$ in $F_n$ imply that $H$ is nonabelian and is a free factor of $F_n$? No finite generation assumption is imposed on $H$.

The supplied record does not define existential closedness explicitly; the formulation above uses full existential formulas with parameters from the subgroup, including inequations, relative to the given ambient group.

Short English statement

Must every subgroup that is existentially closed in a nonabelian free group of finite rank be a nonabelian free factor of that group? Here existential closedness means that every finite system of group equations and inequations with parameters in the subgroup that is solvable in the ambient group is already solvable in the subgroup.
