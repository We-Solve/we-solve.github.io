Math-3073

Mahler's Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

A convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. In the symmetric formulation, its center is moved to the origin, so $K=-K$. Its polar body is \[ K^\circ=\{y\in\mathbb R^n:\langle x,y\rangle\le1 \text{ for every }x\in K\}, \] where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the Euclidean inner product. Write $|K|$ for its $n$-dimensional volume. The cube $[-1,1]^n$ and its polar, the cross-polytope $\{x:\sum_i|x_i|\le1\}$, have volume product $4^n/n!$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every $n\ge1$ and every origin-symmetric convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$, must \[ |K|\,|K^\circ|\ge\frac{4^n}{n!}? \] Equivalently, do the cube and the cross-polytope attain the smallest possible volume product in the centrally symmetric class? [S2]

Short English statement

Does every centrally symmetric convex body have volume product at least that of a cube, equivalently that of its polar cross-polytope?
