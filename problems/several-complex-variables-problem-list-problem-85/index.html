Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Delta=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\zeta|<1\}$, with Poincaré distance \[ \rho_\Delta(a,b)=\operatorname{arctanh}\left|\frac{a-b}{1-\overline a b}\right|. \] In the usual domain interpretation, $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ is a connected open set, where $n\geq1$. For $z\in\Omega$ and $v\in{\mathbb{C}}^n$, define the infinitesimal Kobayashi pseudometric by \[ \kappa_\Omega(z;v)=\inf\{ |\alpha|:\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}},\ f:\Delta\to\Omega\text{ holomorphic},\ f(0)=z,\ \alpha f'(0)=v\}. \] Its integrated pseudodistance is \[ d_\Omega^{\mathrm{int}}(p,q)=\inf_\gamma\int_0^1\kappa_\Omega\left(\gamma(t);\gamma'(t)\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t, \] where the infimum is over piecewise $C^1$ paths $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega$ with $\gamma(0)=p$ and $\gamma(1)=q$. The source calls this the integrated Kobayashi distance.

A Kobayashi chain of $m$ discs from $p$ to $q$ consists of holomorphic maps $f_j:\Delta\to\Omega$ and points $a_j,b_j\in\Delta$, for $1\leq j\leq m$, such that \[ f_1(a_1)=p,\qquad f_j(b_j)=f_{j+1}(a_{j+1})\quad(1\leq j<m),\qquad f_m(b_m)=q. \] Its length is $\sum_{j=1}^m\rho_\Delta(a_j,b_j)$.

Under the usual $C^2$ boundary convention, strong pseudoconvexity means that near every boundary point there is a real $C^2$ defining function $r$, with $\Omega=\{r<0\}$ locally and ${\,\mathrm{d}} r\ne0$ on the boundary, whose Levi form satisfies \[ \sum_{j,k=1}^n\frac{\partial^2 r}{\partial z_j\partial\overline z_k}(x)v_j\overline v_k>0 \] for every nonzero $v\in{\mathbb{C}}^n$ satisfying $\sum_{j=1}^n r_{z_j}(x)v_j=0$, at each boundary point $x$. This convention explains the terminology; the supplied source does not specify boundary regularity, boundedness, or ambient dimension.

Requested assertion, subject to the unresolved scope qualifications below. Prove that strong pseudoconvexity guarantees an absolute integer $K\geq1$ such that for every $p,q\in\Omega$ there is a Kobayashi chain of $m\leq K$ discs from $p$ to $q$ satisfying the exact equality \[ d_\Omega^{\mathrm{int}}(p,q)=\sum_{j=1}^m\rho_\Delta(a_j,b_j). \] Here $K$ must be independent of $p,q$. The word "absolute" suggests further uniformity, but the source does not resolve whether $K$ is independent of $\Omega$, of $n$, or both. The displayed assertion therefore requires a human determination of its intended domain class and quantifier scope. Exact realization is the literal reading of "realized"; replacing equality by arbitrarily close approximation would be a different formulation. Using at most $K$ discs expresses the bounded chain requirement; chains can be padded to exactly $K$ discs using constant maps. The source also states parenthetically that on a convex domain one can take $K=1$, without specifying additional hypotheses for that comparison.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether strong pseudoconvexity forces every integrated Kobayashi distance to be attained by a chain with a uniformly bounded number of holomorphic discs. The source leaves the precise domain hypotheses and the scope of the proposed absolute bound unresolved. Its convex-domain comparison states $K=1$. This is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
