Math-3886

Growth of Triangulated Homology Three-Spheres

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied problem concerns the sequence \[ t_N=\#\{\text{combinatorial isomorphism classes of triangulations of homology three-spheres with }N\text{ facets}\},\qquad N\geq 1. \] For a finite abstract simplicial complex of dimension three, a facet is a maximal simplex; in a pure three-dimensional triangulation every facet is a tetrahedron. Two complexes are combinatorially isomorphic if a bijection of their vertex sets carries simplices to simplices in both directions.

The source record does not specify homology coefficients or explicitly define the permitted triangulations. Under the possible convention that a triangulation is a finite simplicial complex whose realization is a closed connected topological three-manifold, an integral homology three-sphere would satisfy \[ H_i(|K|;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong \begin{cases} {\mathbb{Z}},&i=0,3,\\ 0,&i\ne 0,3. \end{cases} \] If homology over a field is intended, the corresponding condition instead uses that field as coefficients. These conventions are conditional explanations, not hypotheses established by the supplied record. It is also unresolved whether the intended class requires the manifold condition or includes complexes defined by homological conditions alone.

Question. With the intended class of homology three-sphere triangulations fixed, determine whether $t_N$ grows exponentially or superexponentially as $N$ increases.

A precise upper-bound interpretation asks whether \[ \exists C>1\;\exists N_0\in{\mathbb{N}}\;\forall N\geq N_0: \quad t_N\leq C^N, \] or instead \[ \forall C>1\;\forall N_0\in{\mathbb{N}}\;\exists N\geq N_0: \quad t_N>C^N. \] These are complementary alternatives, but the source does not specify that these are its intended meanings. In particular, the second alternative does not assert $t_N^{1/N}\to\infty$, and the first does not assert a matching exponential lower bound. Any stronger intended growth requirement, and any restriction to attainable facet counts, require clarification.

Short English statement

Count triangulations of homology three-spheres with exactly $N$ tetrahedral facets, identifying triangulations that are combinatorially isomorphic. Determine whether this count admits an exponential upper bound or has superexponential growth in the intended sense. The homology coefficients, permitted triangulations, and precise growth convention remain unresolved in the supplied record.
