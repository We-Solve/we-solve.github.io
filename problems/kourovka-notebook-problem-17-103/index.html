Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.103

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal P$ denote the set of all primes. For $\pi\subseteq\mathcal P$, a finite group is a $\pi$-group if every prime divisor of its order belongs to $\pi$; the trivial group is included. A subgroup $H\leq G$ of a finite group is a Hall $\pi$-subgroup if every prime divisor of $|H|$ belongs to $\pi$ and no prime divisor of $[G:H]$ belongs to $\pi$.

Using the standard meaning of $D_\pi$, a finite group $G$ is a $D_\pi$-group if it has a Hall $\pi$-subgroup, any two Hall $\pi$-subgroups of $G$ are conjugate in $G$, and every $\pi$-subgroup of $G$ is contained in a Hall $\pi$-subgroup of $G$.

Define \[ \mathcal S=\left\{\pi\subseteq\mathcal P:\ \text{for every finite group }G,\quad \left(G\text{ has a Hall }\pi\text{-subgroup}\right) \Longrightarrow \left(G\text{ is a }D_\pi\text{-group}\right) \right\}. \] The question proposed by D. O. Revin in 2010 is whether \[ |\mathcal S|=2^{\aleph_0}. \] Here a continuum of sets means continuum many distinct subsets of $\mathcal P$.

The supplied Comment of 2025 reports the following established special case, attributed to K. A. Ilenko and N. V. Maslova: for every integer $x\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, the set \[ \pi_x=\{p\in\mathcal P:p>x\} \] belongs to $\mathcal S$. This reported result and the registry's open status are supplied source assessments, not independently verified here. The source does not explicitly define $D_\pi$; the formulation above uses its standard group-theoretic definition and requires review against the cited notebook.

Short English statement

Are there continuum many sets $\pi$ of primes such that every finite group with a Hall $\pi$-subgroup satisfies the full Sylow-type existence, conjugacy, and containment properties for $\pi$-subgroups? The supplied 2025 comment reports that every set of primes greater than an integer threshold has this property.
