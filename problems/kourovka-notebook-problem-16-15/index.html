Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.15

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group with identity $1$. Use the commutator convention \[ [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy,\qquad [h,{}_1g]=[h,g],\qquad [h,{}_{n+1}g]=[[h,{}_ng],g]\quad(n\geq1). \] An element $g\in G$ is an Engel element if \[ \forall h\in G\ \exists k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:\ [h,{}_kg]=1. \] It is boundedly Engel if \[ \exists k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall h\in G:\ [h,{}_kg]=1. \] The bound may depend on $g$ and $G$. Write \[ B(G)=\{g\in G:g\text{ is boundedly Engel}\}. \] A group is torsion-free if $x^n=1$, for $x\in G$ and $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}$, implies $x=1$. A group is right-ordered if it admits a strict total order $<$ such that $x<y$ implies $xz<yz$ for all $x,y,z\in G$. Here linearly ordered group is interpreted in the usual group-theoretic sense of a group admitting a strict total order invariant under both left and right multiplication: $x<y$ implies $axb<ayb$ for all $a,b,x,y\in G$. The supplied source does not explicitly specify its order or commutator conventions; these conventions make the draft precise and require confirmation against the source.

The remaining questions of Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.15 are:

  1. (b) Is $B(G)$ a subgroup of $G$ for every torsion-free group $G$?
  2. (c) Is $B(G)$ a subgroup of $G$ for every right-ordered group $G$?
  3. (d) Is $B(G)$ a subgroup of $G$ for every linearly ordered group $G$, with the interpretation specified above?
The archived part (a), attributed specifically to B. I. Plotkin, asks whether $B(G)$ is a subgroup for every group $G$, without additional hypotheses. The supplied archive records a negative answer to (a), attributed to A. I. Sozutov. This is a supplied archival assessment, not an independently verified result here. The registry labels the overall problem partially solved and retains (b)--(d) as the remaining questions.

Short English statement

For each of three classes of groups---torsion-free groups, right-ordered groups, and groups with an order invariant under multiplication on both sides---ask whether the boundedly Engel elements form a subgroup. Each element may have its own Engel bound, but that bound must work for every other element of the group. The supplied archive reports that the unrestricted question (a) has a negative answer; the registry retains (b)--(d) as unresolved.
