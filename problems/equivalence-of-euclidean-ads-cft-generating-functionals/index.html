Equivalence of Euclidean AdS/CFT Generating Functionals

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Euclidean anti-de Sitter space is hyperbolic space with a conformal boundary. A generating functional assigns a partition-function ratio to boundary data or a boundary source $h$. An exponential interaction modifies the bulk field measure, and an infrared cutoff restricts its spatial extent. Equivalence up to $h\mapsto ch$ compares the two specified functionals for all admissible sources, allowing one fixed positive normalization $c$. The thermodynamic limit removes the infrared cutoff. In the report's model, the free action is $S_0(\phi)=\frac12\int_{H^d}(|\nabla\phi|^2+m^2\phi^2)\,dg$ and the cutoff interaction is $V_\Lambda(\phi)=\int_\Lambda:\!e^{\alpha\phi}\!:\,dg$. The two normalized functionals impose boundary data by a delta constraint, or pair the boundary field with a source inside an exponential, using their respective Gaussian boundary conditions.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the Euclidean AdS/CFT model on hyperbolic space with exponential interaction and an infrared cutoff, is the boundary-value generating functional equivalent, up to a rescaling $h\mapsto ch$ for some $c>0$, to the ordinary quantum-field-theoretic generating functional with a boundary source term? What happens to this equivalence in the thermodynamic limit?
Source scope and qualification.
The primary report (PDF pages 15--16) explicitly says the finite-cutoff equivalence was established for $d=2$. It also reports triviality along some cutoff sequences in the massive exponential-interaction model; possible remedies such as holographic renormalization are left for future work. Thus the inherited sentence includes a known finite-cutoff comparison and an unresolved limiting/renormalization programme, rather than two newly verified open assertions.

Short English statement

Are the boundary-value and source-based Euclidean AdS/CFT generating functionals equivalent after rescaling, and does that survive removal of the cutoff?

A finite-cutoff Gaussian identity.
The full supplied definition says that finite-cutoff equivalence in $d=2$ was already proved and that some cutoff removals give triviality. We do not reclassify that known part as a new open result. To isolate an algebraic ingredient, let $X\in\mathbb R^N$ be centered Gaussian with positive-definite covariance $C$, let $J\in\mathbb R^N$, and let $V_\Lambda$ be a nonnegative finite-cutoff interaction. Completing the square in its Gaussian density gives \[ -\tfrac12x^TC^{-1}x+J^Tx =-\tfrac12(x-CJ)^TC^{-1}(x-CJ)+\tfrac12J^TCJ. \] Translation of the finite-dimensional integral therefore proves \[ \mathbb E[e^{J^TX}e^{-V_\Lambda(X)}] =e^{J^TCJ/2}\mathbb E[e^{-V_\Lambda(X+CJ)}]. \] Both sides are finite because $0<e^{-V_\Lambda}\leq1$ and Gaussian exponential moments are finite. Dividing by the positive zero-source integral yields \[ \frac{Z_\Lambda(J)}{Z_\Lambda(0)} =e^{J^TCJ/2} \frac{\mathbb E[e^{-V_\Lambda(X+CJ)}]} {\mathbb E[e^{-V_\Lambda(X)}]}. \] Thus an exponential source becomes a deterministic shift, together with a Gaussian prefactor. This explains why a comparison of boundary prescriptions must track both the source normalization and the prefactor; equality of the interaction shifts alone is insufficient.
Why pointwise control before normalization fails.
Even if $e^{-V_\Lambda}\leq1$, the denominator may tend to zero. For a scalar demonstration, take $X$ standard Gaussian and $V_a(x)=a x^2/2$, $a\to\infty$. Direct Gaussian integration gives \[ \mathbb E[e^{-V_a(X)}]=(1+a)^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0, \qquad \frac{\mathbb E[e^{JX-V_a(X)}]}{\mathbb E[e^{-V_a(X)}]} =\exp\left(\frac{J^2}{2(1+a)}\right)\longrightarrow1. \] The normalized limit has no source dependence although every finite-$a$ functional does. This quadratic example is not a replacement for the source's Wick exponential interaction; it is a countercheck on inferring nontriviality from a finite-cutoff identity.
Gap for the AdS/CFT problem.
A comparison of the actual hyperbolic covariances, the boundary-to-bulk map and a fixed positive source multiplier is still needed to identify the two prescriptions. The Gaussian calculation supplies none of the infinite-dimensional ultraviolet or volume estimates. If two normalized functionals are exactly equal after a fixed source rescaling at every cutoff and both pointwise limits exist, equality passes to those limits; nontriviality does not follow. Constructing the appropriate renormalized limiting functionals and controlling the source dependence is the first missing analytical step. The reported finite-$d=2$ result and trivial cutoff sequences remain part of the source scope.
