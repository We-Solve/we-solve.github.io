A multiplicity- and cusp-safe QUE certificate

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's Conjecture 38 (quantum unique ergodicity) states that negative sectional curvature of $X$ should imply $\mu_i\to dV$. The excerpt does not supply the preceding definitions of $\mu_i$, the normalization of $dV$, compactness or finite-volume hypotheses, or the eigenvalue enumeration. A standard configuration-space interpretation, conditional on these missing conventions, is the following. Let $X$ be a complete Riemannian manifold of finite positive volume, put $dV=d\operatorname{vol}_X/\operatorname{vol}(X)$, and let $\varphi_i$ be normalized $L^2(X,dV)$ Laplace eigenfunctions with eigenvalues tending to infinity. Define $d\mu_i=|\varphi_i|^2dV$. Under the source's negative-sectional-curvature hypothesis, the conjectured conclusion is $\int_X a\,d\mu_i\to\int_X a\,dV$ for every bounded continuous test function $a$. On a noncompact manifold this formulation includes absence of escape of mass. The source does not determine whether its intended class is compact, finite-volume noncompact, or otherwise; an infinite-volume space has no normalized Riemannian probability measure of this form. No assertion about continuous-spectrum generalized eigenfunctions or microlocal lifts is specified here.

The arithmetic specialization in the source takes $X=\Gamma\backslash\widetilde X$, with $\widetilde X$ a symmetric space of noncompact type and $\Gamma$ an arithmetic lattice, and requires joint eigenfunctions of the invariant differential operators and Hecke operators. It calls weak-* limits of the associated $\mu_i$ arithmetic quantum limits. The source describes the Laplacian as the relevant invariant differential operator in rank one and cites [32] for the belief that $\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\mathbb H^2$ has simple cuspidal Laplace spectrum, which would make the Hecke eigenfunction condition automatic on those eigenspaces. This belief is a supplied source qualification, not a verified present-day status claim. The broader symmetric-space discussion is not itself a claim that every such space has strictly negative sectional curvature.

The supplied research aid proposes a conditional, basis-independent certificate. For a finite-dimensional Laplace eigenspace $E_\lambda\subset L^2(X,dV)$, let $m_\lambda=\dim E_\lambda$, let $P_\lambda$ be its orthogonal projection, and take a real bounded observable $a$. Write $\bar a=\int_X a\,dV$, $M_b f=bf$, and\[T_\lambda(a)=P_\lambda M_{a-\bar a}P_\lambda\big|_{E_\lambda},\qquad D_\lambda(a)=\sup_{\substack{\varphi\in E_\lambda\\\|\varphi\|_2=1}}\left|\int_X a|\varphi|^2dV-\bar a\right|,\qquad V_\lambda(a)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(T_\lambda(a)^2)}{m_\lambda}.\] Its claimed identities and sharp multiplicity bounds are\[D_\lambda(a)=\|T_\lambda(a)\|_{\mathrm{op}},\qquad \sqrt{V_\lambda(a)}\le D_\lambda(a)\le\sqrt{m_\lambda V_\lambda(a)}.\] Thus $m_\lambda V_\lambda(a)\to0$ on a countable uniformly dense family of real continuous observables is a sufficient all-unit-eigenvector QUE criterion, with the compact case using $C(X,{\mathbb{R}})$ and the locally compact, second-countable noncompact case using $C_0(X,{\mathbb{R}})$. The latter uses the fact that probability measures converging on a uniformly dense subset of $C_0(X)$ to the integrals against a specified probability measure converge weakly to that probability measure and have no escape of mass. A local limit proportional to volume with an unspecified coefficient does not provide that conclusion. The variance here includes all matrix entries: vanishing of only the diagonal variance in one chosen basis does not control $D_\lambda$, as the abstract self-adjoint matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ illustrates. This matrix illustration is not a constructed counterexample to QUE. These conditional certificates do not establish the conjecture's required estimates for negatively curved manifolds.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks for equidistribution of every high-energy eigenfunction's probability density. The supplied aid gives a criterion that controls all vectors in a multiple eigenspace and handles loss of mass in a cusp. Missing normalization and geometric hypotheses remain explicit; neither the criterion nor the recorded progress labels constitute a solution of QUE.
