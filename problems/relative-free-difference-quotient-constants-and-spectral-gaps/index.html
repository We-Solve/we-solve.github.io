Math-1644

Relative free-difference-quotient constants and spectral gaps

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,\tau)$ be a von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state, let $1\in B\subset M$ be an infinite-dimensional von Neumann subalgebra, and let $X=X^*\in M$ with $M=W^*(B,X)$. The source assumes $B$ and $X$ are free in the algebraic sense, meaning that the polynomial algebra $B\langle X\rangle$ has no extra algebraic relations obstructing the derivation below. This does not by itself assert scalar freeness with respect to $\tau$.

The coarse free difference quotient $\partial_{X:B}$ is the derivation on $B\langle X\rangle$ satisfying \[ \partial_{X:B}b=0\ (b\in B),\qquad \partial_{X:B}X=1\otimes1, \] with the Leibniz rule and target the coarse Hilbert bimodule $L^2(M,\tau)\otimes L^2(M,\tau)$, equivalently the product-trace $L^2$ space with the right factor represented oppositely. The tensor product is not balanced over $B$. Assume this densely defined operator is closable and denote its closure by $D$. Let $E_B:M\to B$ be the trace-preserving conditional expectation, also the orthogonal projection from $L^2(M)$ to $L^2(B)$.

Questions, attributed to Voiculescu. Under what additional conditions does \[ \ker D=L^2(B,\tau) \] hold? Equivalently, when must every $u\in\operatorname{Dom}D$ with $Du=0$ belong to $L^2(B)$? Under what stronger conditions is there $C<\infty$, independent of $\xi$, such that \[ \|\xi-E_B\xi\|_2\le C\|\partial_{X:B}\xi\|_2 \qquad(\xi\in B\langle X\rangle)? \] By closure the same estimate then applies on $\operatorname{Dom}D$. The source gives as a closability example $X=X_0+\varepsilon S$, $\varepsilon\ne0$, with a semicircular $S$ scalar-free from $W^*(X_0,B)$; it does not assert the desired kernel or inequality merely from that example.

The supplied aid records the operator-theoretic distinction: for the nonnegative self-adjoint operator $A=D^*D$, the inequality is the form estimate \[ \langle A\xi,\xi\rangle\ge C^{-2}\|\xi-E_B\xi\|_2^2 \] on its appropriate form domain, or equivalently a spectral lower bound $C^{-2}>0$ on $L^2(B)^\perp$ with no additional zero modes. Kernel equality alone does not provide this bound; positive spectrum could accumulate at zero. This is a structural distinction, not a supplied counterexample within every free-quotient class.

Semicircular special case reported in the aid. Let $S$ be a variance-one centered semicircular element scalar-free from an arbitrary tracial $B$, and take \[ X=a+\sigma S,\qquad a=a^*\in B,\quad\sigma>0. \] Here $a$ need not be scalar, so $X$ need not be scalar-free from $B$; algebraic freeness and the quotient remain meaningful. In the orthogonal reduced-word Chebyshev decomposition \[ L^2(W^*(B,S))=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}K_d,\qquad K_0=L^2(B), \] $d$ is total semicircular polynomial degree in reduced free-product words, using the orthogonal Chebyshev polynomials for variance-one $S$. In particular $K_1$ is the $L^2$-closed span of $BSB$. For $\xi=\sum_d\xi_d$, $\xi_d\in K_d$, the aid reports \[ \operatorname{Dom}D=\left\{\xi:\sum_{d\ge1}d\|\xi_d\|_2^2<\infty\right\},\qquad \|D\xi\|_2^2=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{d\ge1}d\|\xi_d\|_2^2. \] Consequently its kernel is exactly $L^2(B)$, the optimal constant in the unsquared-norm inequality is $C=\sigma$, and \[ \operatorname{spec}(D^*D)=\{d/\sigma^2:d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}\}, \qquad (D^*D)|_{K_d}=d/\sigma^2. \] Equality in the optimal inequality occurs precisely for $\xi\in K_0\oplus K_1$, including the trivial zero-energy equality cases in $L^2(B)$. This special exact decomposition is not asserted for a general perturbation $X_0+\varepsilon S$ with $X_0\notin B$.

The trailing heading "Large Deviations", with Guionnet, Hiai and Cabanal-Duvillard, is adjacent workshop context and supplies no further mathematical question in this record.

Short English statement

The question asks when the closed relative free difference quotient has only $B$-valued constants, and when a quantitative Poincaré inequality holds. The aid distinguishes kernel equality from a spectral gap and reports an exact semicircular translate-and-scale model with optimal constant $\sigma$. Algebraic freeness, trace freeness, and general semicircular perturbations have different scopes.
