OpenAI claim summary for Math-2224

The question is read as a set of distinct positive integers. The manuscript claims that divergent reciprocal sum forces a nonconstant progression of every finite length, covering that entire assertion.

Suppose I have a sequence of positive integers whose reciprocals sum to infinity

OpenAI's claim

Progression-free density and reciprocal sums: For each fixed length $k\ge3$, the claimed progression-free density decays as $\exp(-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k})$ up to a fixed factor. This quantitative estimate is used to deduce arbitrarily long progressions from a divergent reciprocal sum.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For each fixed integer $k\ge3$, let $r_k(N)$ denote maximal cardinality among subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ containing no arithmetic progression $a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d$ with $d\ne0$. The claim gives constants $C_k,c_k,\epsilon_k>0$ with \[r_k(N)\le C_kN\exp\{-c_k(\log N)^{\epsilon_k}\}\quad(N\ge2).\] Its divergent-reciprocal-sums corollary asserts \[A\subset\mathbb N,\ \sum_{a\in A}a^{-1}=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\text{ contains a nonconstant progression of every finite length}.\] This concerns sets of distinct positive integers. It does not settle a separate finite-field progression clause.

Outline of the argument

Progression-free density and reciprocal sums: Density increments take place inside triangular polynomial cells rather than repeatedly discarding inherited structure. Integer coordinate blocks and exact substitution retain old constraints when new ones are added. A hierarchy of precision costs bounds the total logarithmic length loss by a polynomial in the logarithmic density parameter, enabling the quasipolynomial estimate and the reciprocal-sum consequence.
