Normal Hessian blocks, an exact four-body index jump, and stability obstructions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. A central configuration has $\nabla_{q_i}U=-\lambda m_iq_i$ in center-of-mass coordinates for a common scalar $\lambda$. After fixing $I=1$, it is a critical point of $U$ on the collision-free inertia ellipsoid. Congruent configurations and scales are factored out for a finiteness count. A relative equilibrium is a rigidly rotating solution with time-independent shape. The Morse index of a constrained critical point is the number of negative Hessian directions, with multiplicity, after imposing the normalization and accounting for symmetry degeneracies. A planar configuration can be embedded in the spatial configuration space and acquire extra normal directions. Linear stability concerns the variational equations of the corresponding rotating motion.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Give a sharp upper bound for the Morse index of a nonplanar central configuration as a critical point of the positive Newtonian potential on normalized configuration space. Give a sharp lower bound for the index of a planar central configuration when viewed in the nonplanar configuration space. Determine the relation to stability of rigidly rotating periodic solutions; in particular, must a linearly stable relative equilibrium come from a minimum of that potential?

The normal Hessian block at a planar configuration.
Let $q_i=(x_i,y_i,0)$ be a planar central configuration with multiplier $\lambda>0$. An out-of-plane variation is $\delta q_i=(0,0,z_i)$, satisfying center-of-mass constraint $\sum m_iz_i=0$. It is automatically tangent to the inertia sphere. For each pair, differentiating $(r_{ij}^2+t^2(z_i-z_j)^2)^{-1/2}$ twice at zero gives $-(z_i-z_j)^2/r_{ij}^3$. Thus the constrained Hessian is \[Q_\perp(z)=\lambda\sum_im_iz_i^2-\sum_{i<j}\frac{m_im_j}{r_{ij}^3}(z_i-z_j)^2. \] Here the positive $\lambda$ term comes from the Lagrange function $U+\lambda I/2$, fixing the source's positive-potential convention.
Rotation zero modes and what can be counted.
Infinitesimal rotations tilting the plane give $z_i=x_i$ and $z_i=y_i$, both in the nullspace. This can also be checked directly: the central-configuration equations imply that the weighted graph Laplacian with edge weights $m_im_j/r_{ij}^3$ sends each coordinate vector to $\lambda\operatorname{diag}(m_i)$ times that vector. The normal Morse index is therefore the number of its generalized eigenvalues above $\lambda$, after removing translation and the rotation zero modes. This is a computable block criterion once the configuration is known.
Gap.
No sharp uniform upper/lower index bound follows without information on those graph eigenvalues across all configurations. Dynamical stability also involves velocity/Coriolis terms in the rotating variational equation, not only the constrained potential Hessian. Consequently the stable-relative-equilibrium/minimum question is not answered by counting normal negative directions alone. Both sharp index questions and the stability implication remain unresolved; the first missing step is a global spectral bound for the relevant Hessian blocks and a separate reduced dynamical analysis.
