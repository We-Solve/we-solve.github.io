Shared notation.

The adjoint $A^*$ satisfies $\langle Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,A^*y\rangle$. A forward sketch queries $A$ on selected vectors; adjoint-free access forbids corresponding queries to $A^*$. Low-rank approximation minimizes the stated matrix or operator error.

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. Matrix completion reconstructs a low-rank matrix from a subset of its entries; matrix sensing reconstructs it from specified linear measurements. Tensor decomposition represents a multiway array as a sum of simple outer products.

Deterministic forward queries require the full domain in the worst case

Let an unknown real $m\times n$ matrix be accessed by exact forward queries $x\mapsto Ax$, where $m,n\ge1$. Algorithms may adapt queries to previous answers but are deterministic.
Proposition: After $k<n$ queries, no such algorithm can guarantee spectral-norm error strictly below one for every rank-one norm-one matrix. In particular, worst-case exact recovery of all matrices of rank at most one requires $n$ queries; $n$ suffice.
Proof: Run the algorithm against answers all equal to zero. Its resulting query vectors span a subspace $Q$ of dimension at most $k<n$. Choose unit vectors $v\in Q^\perp$ and $u\in\mathbb R^m$. The two matrices $A_+=uv^T$ and $A_-=-uv^T$ both have rank one, norm one, and give zero for every query on this history. By induction over queries, the deterministic algorithm follows this same history on both matrices, so its output $B$ is identical. Since $\|A_+-A_-\|_2=2$, the triangle inequality implies $\max(\|A_+-B\|_2,\|A_--B\|_2)\ge1$. Querying the $n$ standard basis vectors returns all columns and proves sufficiency. ∎
Proposition: If a known matrix $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times s}$ has orthonormal columns and $\operatorname{range}(A^T)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(V)$, then $s$ forward queries recover $A$ exactly as $(AV)V^T$. Without this containment the same formula has error exactly $\|A(I-VV^T)\|_2$.
Proof: Under containment, every row of $A$ is in the column space of $V$, so $A=AVV^T$. Each column of $AV$ is one forward answer. In general subtracting $AVV^T$ from $A$ gives the claimed error formula. For $s<n$, the preceding $uv^T$ with $v\perp V$ shows that the observed answers cannot by themselves certify a small omitted tail. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

Finite matrix checks verify two indistinguishable rank-one operators and recovery with a correct right-space prior. The adversarial matrix in the lower bound is selected after the deterministic zero history; choosing it separately for every random seed would not establish a randomized per-input lower bound. No such extension is claimed. Structured near-symmetric and PDE classes, randomized guarantees and useful tail priors remain outside this elementary result.
