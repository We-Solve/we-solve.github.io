Math-1639

Aggregate singular sets of minimizing laminations and the codimension-one stable norm

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The canonical extract concatenates two questions. The first, attributed to Franz Auer, conjectures a Hausdorff-dimension bound $n-7$ for the singularity set of a minimizing closed codimension-one current. The second defines a stable norm on $H_q(M;{\mathbb{R}})$ by minimum mass and asks for convexity and differentiability properties of its unit ball, comparing it with Mather's $\beta$-function. Both targets are retained.

For the first question, the aid uses ambient dimension $N$ and real codimension-one currents $T$ on a smooth Riemannian $N$-manifold. "Closed" means $\partial T=0$; the precise local or homological mass-minimizing class and the definition of singular set must be fixed. The original intended assertion is \[ \dim_H\operatorname{sing}(T)\le N-7. \] The aid reports a stronger, specifically Auer--Bangert aggregate-singular-set assertion \[ \dim_H\operatorname{sing}_{\mathrm{AB}}(T)\le N-8. \] Here the aggregate is associated with the measured-leaf decomposition of a minimizing real current, rather than an unspecified notion of singular support. The supplied record does not give its exact definition or all decomposition hypotheses. It attributes the claimed bound to a disjoint-family Hausdorff-content argument of Chodosh--Mantoulidis--Schulze, a $C^2$ variable-metric clustering extension, the Auer--Bangert decomposition, and the strict maximum principle. These are reported ingredients, not supplied proofs. With the convention that the empty set has dimension $-\infty$, the stronger bound would require this aggregate singular set to be empty for $N\le7$.

For the second question, in the standard compact Riemannian interpretation let $M$ be closed and $0\le q\le\dim M$. For real homology $h\in H_q(M;{\mathbb{R}})$ put \[ \|h\|_{\mathrm{st}}= \inf\{\mathbf M(T):\partial T=0,\ [T]=h\},\qquad B_{\mathrm{st}}=\{h:\|h\|_{\mathrm{st}}\le1\}, \] where the infimum is over the appropriate finite-mass real currents and $\mathbf M$ is Riemannian mass, dual to the comass bound on test forms. The current class and compactness assumptions are not specified in the extract. In the usual stable-norm setting the aid records positivity, absolute homogeneity and the triangle inequality, so $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ is convex, centrally symmetric, and compact in the finite-dimensional homology space. The broad question concerns its finer geometry and regularity; elementary convexity alone does not settle it. At a nonzero class, differentiability of the norm can equivalently be expressed as uniqueness of its supporting dual functional. The aid also names a known Auer--Bangert codimension-one differentiability theorem, but gives no exact hypotheses or conclusion from which differentiability at every class could be asserted. The general-$q$ program is left open in its assessment.

Short English statement

Two concatenated questions concern singular sets of minimizing currents and the geometry of stable-norm balls. The aid reports a stronger aggregate singular-set bound and elementary stable-ball properties, while its precise singular-set definition and cited differentiability theorem remain insufficiently specified for independent verification.
