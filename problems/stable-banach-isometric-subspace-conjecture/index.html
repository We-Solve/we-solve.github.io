Stable Banach Isometric Subspace Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}$. Dimensions and linear maps are taken over $\mathbb K$. For two normed spaces $X,Y$ of the same finite dimension over $\mathbb K$, define their Banach--Mazur distance by \[ d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,Y)=\inf\left\{\|T\|\,\|T^{-1}\|:T:X\longrightarrow Y\text{ is a bijective }\mathbb K\text{-linear map}\right\}, \] where the norms of maps are operator norms. Write $\ell_2^n(\mathbb K)$ for $\mathbb K^n$ with norm $\|(x_i)_{i=1}^n\|_2=(\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|^2)^{1/2}$. Every subspace is equipped with the norm inherited from its ambient space.

Requested stability statement. For every pair of integers $n,k$ with $2\leq k<n$, there exists a function \[ \varepsilon_{n,k}:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty), \qquad \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\varepsilon_{n,k}(\delta)=0, \] such that, for every $\mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}$, every $n$-dimensional normed space $V$ over $\mathbb K$, and every $\delta>0$, the implication \[ \left[\ d_{\mathrm{BM}}(E,E')<1+\delta \quad\text{for every pair of }k\text{-dimensional subspaces }E,E'\subset V\ \right] \ \Longrightarrow\ d_{\mathrm{BM}}\left(V,\ell_2^n(\mathbb K)\right)<1+\varepsilon_{n,k}(\delta) \] holds. The function must be independent of $V$. A common function for the two scalar fields expresses the supplied notation $\varepsilon(n,k,\delta)$; allowing separate functions for the two fields gives an equivalent formulation by taking their maximum.

Short English statement

For fixed $n$ and $k$ with $2\leq k<n$, the requested assertion says that if every pair of $k$-dimensional subspaces of a real or complex normed space is sufficiently close in Banach--Mazur distance, then the whole space is close to the corresponding Euclidean or Hermitian space, with an error tending to zero uniformly over the ambient spaces. The supplied original excerpt does not itself specify this quantitative formulation, which requires human mathematical review.
