Math-1273

A necessary Bockstein fingerprint for a bounded Weil–étale topology on Spec Z

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Original question (M. Flach). Find a natural definition of Weil--étale topology for which integral cohomology vanishes in degrees greater than three. Flach comments that the then-current definition has the unwanted pattern of vanishing in odd degrees and infinitely generated groups in even degrees, and that it is unclear whether a better definition is near or truncation of the cohomology complex is the only solution. The low-degree range and underlying arithmetic object are not specified in the extracted sentence; the attached aid specializes to $\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{Z}}$. The historical odd-degree comment is not asserted as a newly checked statement about every degree or every modern theory.

Attached unreviewed necessary test. Let a proposed compactified Grothendieck topos for $\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{Z}}$ have ordinary and compactly supported derived sections $R\Gamma$ and $R\Gamma_c$. The aid imposes the following explicit hypotheses: (H1) retain the indicated Lichtenbaum integral groups in degrees zero through three, namely ordinary $H^0={\mathbb{Z}}$, $H^1=H^2=0$, $H^3={\mathbb{Z}}/2$, and compactly supported $H_c^0=H_c^1=H_c^2=0$, $H_c^3={\mathbb{Z}}/2$; (H2) both theories vanish in negative degrees and degrees greater than three; (H3) both derived section theories respect the constant-sheaf coefficient sequence \[0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{m}{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/m\longrightarrow0\qquad(m\ge1).\] The compactification, support functor and claimed historical verification of these low groups require independent review.

Under (H1)--(H3), the reported necessary derived forms are, in $D({\mathbb{Z}})$, \[R\Gamma({\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}/2)[-3],\qquad R\Gamma_c({\mathbb{Z}})\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/2)[-3].\] These are derived isomorphism statements, not an asserted canonical splitting or construction of a topology. Here a group shifted by $[-3]$ lies in cohomological degree three.

The claimed finite-coefficient fingerprint is: for every $m\ge1$, if $m$ is even then \[H_c^i({\mathbb{Z}}/m)\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}/2&i=2,3,\\0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\] and if $m$ is odd all $H_c^i({\mathbb{Z}}/m)$ vanish. Ordinary cohomology has the same degree-two and degree-three parity pattern and additionally $H^0({\mathbb{Z}}/m)={\mathbb{Z}}/m$, with all other degrees zero. The degree-two term is the coefficient-sequence/Bockstein effect and cannot be omitted merely because integral $H^2$ vanishes.

The aid describes the requested bounded integral topology as still open. Its parity test is only a necessary falsification criterion for theories satisfying the stated low-degree and coefficient hypotheses; it neither supplies the missing topology nor proves those hypotheses are the unique desirable axioms.

Short English statement

Flach asks for a natural Weil--étale topology with integral cohomology bounded above by degree three. The aid supplies only a necessary test on $\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{Z}}$: preserving specified low groups and coefficient exactness forces finite-coefficient groups in degrees two and three precisely for even moduli. It does not construct the topology.
