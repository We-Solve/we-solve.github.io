Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.108

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group, and let $\operatorname{Sym}(G)$ denote the group of all permutations of its underlying set. For $g\in G$, define the left translation $\lambda_g$ by $\lambda_g(x)=gx$, and put $\lambda(G)=\{\lambda_g:g\in G\}$. For a subgroup $H\leq K$, write \[ N_K(H)=\{k\in K:kHk^{-1}=H\}. \] Define \[ \operatorname{Hol}(G)=N_{\operatorname{Sym}(G)}(\lambda(G)),\qquad \operatorname{NHol}(G)=N_{\operatorname{Sym}(G)}(\operatorname{Hol}(G)),\qquad T(G)=\operatorname{NHol}(G)/\operatorname{Hol}(G). \] The quotient is defined because $\operatorname{Hol}(G)$ is normal in its normalizer. The center of $G$ is \[ Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}, \] and $G$ is centerless if $Z(G)=\{1\}$. An elementary abelian $2$-group means an abelian group $E$ such that $e^2=1$ for every $e\in E$; the trivial group is included, and no finiteness restriction is imposed on $E$.
  1. (a) Does there exist a group $G$, with no finiteness assumption, such that $Z(G)=\{1\}$ and $T(G)$ is not an elementary abelian $2$-group?
  2. (b) Does there exist a finite group $G$ such that $Z(G)=\{1\}$ and $T(G)$ is not an elementary abelian $2$-group?
  3. (c) Does there exist a prime $p$, a finite group $G$ of order $p^n$ for some integer $n\geq 0$, and a prime $q$ such that \[ q\mid |T(G)|\qquad\text{and}\qquad q\nmid p(p-1)? \] No centerlessness assumption is imposed in part (c), which is attributed to A. Caranti.

These are the original existence questions. The supplied record reports affirmative resolutions of parts (a) and (b) and labels the overall problem partially solved. It supplies no resolution of part (c). These status reports are supplied curation, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

For a group $G$, compare the normalizer of its holomorph with the holomorph itself through the quotient $T(G)$. Parts (a) and (b) ask whether this quotient can fail to be elementary abelian of exponent $2$ for a centerless group, respectively without and with a finiteness restriction. Part (c) asks whether a finite $p$-group can have $|T(G)|$ divisible by a prime outside the prime divisors of $p(p-1)$. The supplied source reports affirmative answers to (a) and (b).
