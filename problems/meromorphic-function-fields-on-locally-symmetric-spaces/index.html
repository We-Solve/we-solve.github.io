Math-3341

Meromorphic Function Fields on Locally Symmetric Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $Z=\Gamma\backslash X$ be the noncompact Hermitian locally symmetric space intended by the source, with $X$ a connected Hermitian symmetric space and $\Gamma$ a discrete group acting by holomorphic isometries. For a smooth quotient one ordinarily requires a free properly discontinuous action; any orbifold interpretation must be specified if torsion is allowed. Let $\mathcal M(Z)$ be its field of global meromorphic functions, over the constant field ${\mathbb{C}}$.

Determine \[ \operatorname{trdeg}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathcal M(\Gamma\backslash X). \] Here the transcendence degree is the cardinality of a transcendence basis, or equivalently the maximal size of an algebraically independent family of global meromorphic functions over ${\mathbb{C}}$. A finite family $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ is algebraically independent when \[ P(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\not\equiv0 \quad\text{for every nonzero }P\in{\mathbb{C}}[t_1,\ldots,t_r]. \] The request is to determine this invariant for the intended class of pairs $(X,\Gamma)$; the extraction supplies no proposed value or criterion.

The source does not specify finite covolume, arithmeticity, rank, irreducibility, torsion conditions, or compactification data. It also does not impose automorphic growth conditions on meromorphic functions. Such restrictions cannot be added silently. In particular, noncompactness alone does not justify importing a compact-complex-space bound on transcendence degree, so neither finiteness nor equality with $\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}Z$ is asserted here.

The literature assessment additionally discusses a Helgason Poisson-transform conjecture and says it was proved by Kashiwara and collaborators. That conjecture is not stated in the supplied mathematical question. Its alleged resolution must not be treated as a solution of the separate meromorphic-function-field problem, which the assessment calls the broader Siegel transcendence-degree problem.

Short English statement

The question asks how many algebraically independent global meromorphic functions exist on the specified noncompact Hermitian locally symmetric quotient. The quotient class and a proposed formula are missing; a separate solved Poisson-transform question mentioned in the metadata does not answer this target.
