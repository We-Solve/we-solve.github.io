Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.39

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in ZFC, including the Axiom of Choice; the Continuum Hypothesis is not assumed. Let $\Omega$ be a countable set, and let $\mathfrak m$ be a cardinal satisfying \[ \aleph_0\leq\mathfrak m\leq 2^{\aleph_0}. \] Write $\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ for the group of all bijections of $\Omega$, and $\mathfrak P(\Omega)$ for its power set. A permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ acts on $\mathfrak P(\Omega)$ by \[ g\cdot A=\{g(a):a\in A\}. \] Thus $A,B\subseteq\Omega$ belong to the same orbit if and only if $B=g\cdot A$ for some $g\in G$.

The source asks whether, for every cardinal $\mathfrak m$ in the stated interval, there exists such a group $G$ with \[ \left|\mathfrak P(\Omega)/G\right|=\mathfrak m. \] Here $\mathfrak P(\Omega)/G$ denotes the set of orbits. The source says only "countable," without explicitly saying "countably infinite." The prescribed infinite number of orbits requires $\Omega$ to be infinite, so the intended domain appears to be $|\Omega|=\aleph_0$. This restriction is an unresolved wording qualification, rather than an explicitly supplied hypothesis.

The supplied comment dated 2001 reports that the answer is affirmative under Martin's Axiom, citing S. Shelah and S. Thomas (1988), and describes the question as still open in ZFC. These are supplied historical assessments, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

For a countably infinite set, can every cardinal between $\aleph_0$ and $2^{\aleph_0}$, inclusive, occur as the number of orbits of a permutation group acting on its subsets? The source's use of "countable" needs clarification. Its 2001 comment reports a positive answer under Martin's Axiom and an open status in ZFC.
