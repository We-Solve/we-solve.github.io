Incomplete Self-Similar-Tree DtN Uniqueness Claim

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns an unknown scalar Dirichlet-to-Neumann function $\Lambda$ associated with a self-similar tree and displays the functional equation \[ \Lambda(\omega)\cos\omega-\omega\sin\omega = \sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{\mu_i}{\alpha_i} \left(\cos\omega+\frac{\sin\omega}{\omega}\Lambda(\alpha_i\omega)\right). \] Here $\omega$ is the frequency variable, and $p,\mu_i,\alpha_i$ are parameters whose admissible values and geometric meanings are not specified in the supplied statement. The frequency domain, scalar field, tree construction, boundary conditions, and regularity requirements on $\Lambda$ are also unspecified. The displayed expressions require nonzero denominators wherever they are evaluated; no interpretation at $\omega=0$ is supplied.

The requested assertion is that there are at most two "homeomorphic solutions" and that $\Lambda(0)$ uniquely determines a solution. To express only its quantifier structure, let $\mathcal S$ stand for the intended, presently unspecified solution class for fixed admissible tree data and parameters. Provided evaluation at zero is defined for every member, the proposed conclusions would be \[ |\mathcal S|\leq 2, \qquad \forall\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2\in\mathcal S,\quad \Lambda_1(0)=\Lambda_2(0)\Longrightarrow\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2. \] The task is to prove or disprove these conclusions once the missing specifications are recovered. This notation does not define the phrase "homeomorphic solutions": the source gives neither spaces between which the solutions should be homeomorphisms nor another precise meaning for that phrase. Consequently, these formulas record the intended claim but do not constitute a recovered, well-posed conjecture. The supplied statement-verification record marks the formulation as unrecoverable and supplies no clean replacement.

Short English statement

The source asks whether the displayed self-similar-tree Dirichlet-to-Neumann equation has at most two solutions in a class called "homeomorphic," with each solution uniquely determined by its value at zero. That class, the parameter assumptions, the frequency domain, and the interpretation at zero are missing, so a precise conjecture cannot yet be recovered.
