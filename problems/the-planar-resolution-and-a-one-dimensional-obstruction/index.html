Math-1405

The planar resolution and a one-dimensional obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix a dimension $d$ and a convex body $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ (compact, convex, with nonempty interior). Let $x_1,x_2,\ldots$ be independent uniform points in $K$, and set \[ P_n=\operatorname{conv}(x_1,\ldots,x_n),\quad V_n=\operatorname{vol}_d(P_n),\quad Z_n=\frac{V_n-{\mathbb{E}} V_n}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(V_n)}}. \] Van Vu asks whether, for every fixed $K$ in the intended dimensions $d\geq2$, $Z_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)$ as $n\to\infty$. This is a fixed-body binomial model, not a growing-dimension or uniform-over-all-bodies assertion. The source's 2007 remark says smooth-body and polytope cases were known, but the general planar case was then open; it does not specify the exact smoothness assumptions.

The attached aid reports that Pardon's 2011 Poisson theorem and 2012 de-Poissonization theorem establish this binomial CLT for every planar convex body. It says no unrestricted fixed-dimensional theorem for arbitrary $K$ in $d\geq3$ was located, rather than claiming a complete verified current literature classification.

The literal source does not exclude $d=1$, where the aid gives a counterexample. If $K$ is an interval of length $\ell>0$ and $n\geq2$, its missing-length fraction \[ Y_n=\frac{\ell-V_n}{\ell} \] has distribution $\operatorname{Beta}(2,n-1)$. Thus the supplied standardized limit is \[ Z_n\Rightarrow\frac{2-G}{\sqrt2},\qquad G\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1), \] where the gamma density is $t e^{-t}$ on $t\geq0$. This is not Gaussian. If $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function and $d_K$ is Kolmogorov distance between distributions, the aid records \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}d_K(\mathcal L(Z_n),N(0,1))\geq1-\Phi(\sqrt2). \] Consequently excluding dimension one is material, not a cosmetic convention.

For all dimensions, missing volume $M_n=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)-V_n$ has standardized variable exactly $-Z_n$, so the corresponding Gaussian CLT is equivalent. The aid also records Efron's first-moment identity \[ \frac{{\mathbb{E}} M_n}{\operatorname{vol}_d(K)} =\frac{{\mathbb{E}} f_0(P_{n+1})}{n+1}, \] where $f_0$ counts vertices. This identity alone supplies neither a variance comparison nor a CLT. The literature and exact-law statements above are supplied claims requiring independent verification.

Short English statement

Ask whether variance-standardized random-polytope volume is asymptotically Gaussian for every fixed convex body. The supplied aid reports the planar problem solved, retains uncertainty in higher dimensions, and gives a reflected-gamma obstruction in the literal one-dimensional case.
