Unspecified Log-Concave Measure Conjecture

Definitions and mathematical statement

Recoverable definition fragment. The supplied record describes a measure $\mu$ as being of the form $(\star)$ when it has density \[ e^{-w(|x|)},\qquad w:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}, \] where $w$ is increasing, followed by the unfinished condition "such that $t\mapsto w(e^t)$." The condition on this map is missing. Consequently, these data do not give a complete definition of $(\star)$. The fragment does not explicitly specify the ambient space or reference measure for this density, define $|x|$, distinguish strictly increasing from nondecreasing, or state normalization or integrability requirements. Since $w$ is defined only on $(0,\infty)$, the displayed density also leaves its value at the origin unspecified.

Mathematical statement unavailable. The excerpt begins with the isolated word "conjecture" and supplies no complete conjectural proposition. The properties $(B)$ and $(GZ)$, and the measure $\gamma$, are not defined. No assertion with recoverable hypotheses, conclusion, and quantifiers can therefore be stated from this record.

Supplied contextual claims. The excerpt historically reports few examples satisfying either $(B)$ or $(GZ)$ apart from $\gamma$. It lists Lebesgue measure restricted to a convex body $T$ as an example for $(GZ)$, and says no other examples for that property had previously been known. It attributes to Livshyts, reference [12], a version of $(GZ)$ for every even log-concave measure with a weakened exponent; the extracted exponents are corrupted and cannot be stated reliably. It attributes to Eskenazis, Nayar and Tkocz, reference [6], the property $(B)$ for certain Gaussian mixtures, including a displayed measure with parameter $0<p\leq1$; that measure's formula is also corrupted. The excerpt further attributes to references [14] and [13] the implication that the log-Brunn--Minkowski conjecture in dimension $n$ would imply both $(B)$ and $(GZ)$ for every even log-concave measure $\mu$ on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, and cites [2] for the case $n=2$. The incomplete definition of $(\star)$ is associated with the unidentified work [4]. These are supplied contextual reports, not independently verified results or a substitute for the missing problem statement.

The supplied registry labels the record open, but its statement status is unrecoverable. Its supplied verification note says the official report places this fragment among proved results on log-concave measures and states no open problem. The open label thus does not establish the existence of an unresolved conjecture.
