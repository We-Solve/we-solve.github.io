Math-15243

Superexpansion and Coarse Classification of Expanders

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

One standard framework for the question is the following; the supplied record does not explicitly fix these conventions. An expander sequence is a sequence $(G_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of finite connected undirected simple graphs, with $|V(G_n)|\geq 2$ and $|V(G_n)|\to\infty$, for which there exist constants $D<\infty$ and $h>0$ such that, for every $n$, \[ \max_{v\in V(G_n)}\deg(v)\leq D, \qquad \min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V(G_n)\\ |A|\leq |V(G_n)|/2}} \frac{|\partial_E A|}{|A|}\geq h. \] Here $\partial_E A$ is the set of edges with exactly one endpoint in $A$, and each graph carries its shortest-path metric. These are conventional working definitions, not hypotheses explicitly documented in the supplied problem statement.

For metric spaces $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$, a map $f:X\to Y$ is bornologous if \[ \forall R\geq 0\ \exists S\geq 0\ \forall x,x'\in X, \qquad d_X(x,x')\leq R\ \Longrightarrow\ d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\leq S. \] A standard meaning of coarse equivalence is the existence of bornologous maps $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to X$ and a constant $C<\infty$ satisfying \[ d_X(g(f(x)),x)\leq C\quad(x\in X), \qquad d_Y(f(g(y)),y)\leq C\quad(y\in Y). \] This supplies a reference definition, but the source asks which notion is appropriate and does not prescribe this relation as the desired classification relation.

Research question. Identify and justify a notion of coarse equivalence or coarse distinction that gives a useful classification of expander sequences. The supplied record does not specify whether the objects compared are coarse disjoint unions, sequences compared through uniformly controlled maps between components, or another construction. It also does not specify the meaning of a useful classification, the permitted invariants, or the required completeness of such invariants. Accordingly, the source supports an open-ended classification problem, not a uniquely determined conjecture with fixed quantifiers and a specified conclusion.

The original wording also asked whether all expanders are superexpanders. The supplied correction explicitly removes that clause because it duplicates source problem OWR-16160-001; it is not an additional target of this entry.

Short English statement

Find an appropriate way to compare or distinguish expander sequences by their large-scale geometry and use it to organize them into meaningful classes. The source leaves the comparison relation and the requirements for a classification unspecified. Its corrected formulation excludes the separate question of whether every expander is a superexpander.
