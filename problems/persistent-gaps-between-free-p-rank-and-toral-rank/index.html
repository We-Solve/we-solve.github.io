Math-7349

Persistent Gaps between Free $p$-Rank and Toral Rank

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a finite connected complex with base point $x$. Require that $\pi_1(X,x)$ be abelian and that its natural action on every higher homotopy group be trivial: \[ \gamma\cdot\alpha=\alpha \qquad\text{for every }n\geq 2,\ \gamma\in\pi_1(X,x),\ \alpha\in\pi_n(X,x). \] The finite-dimensional rational homotopy hypothesis is expressed as \[ \dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\left(\bigoplus_{n\geq 2}\pi_n(X,x)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)<\infty. \] Under the usual finite CW complex interpretation, the abelian group $\pi_1(X,x)$ is finitely generated, so including its rationalization in this direct sum does not change the finiteness condition.

Retain the source notation $\operatorname{rk}_p(X)$ for free $p$-rank and $\operatorname{rk}_T(X)$ for free toral rank. The supplied record does not define these invariants. In particular, it does not specify whether actions must occur on $X$ itself or on a space in its homotopy type, which category of actions is allowed, or the precise freeness condition. These conventions must be recovered from the cited report before the statement is fully unambiguous; no definition of either invariant is imposed here. The precise category intended by "finite complex" is also unspecified in the supplied record.

Subject to those unresolved source conventions, the question is whether there exists a single such $X$ for which \[ \left\{p\in{\mathbb{N}} : p\text{ is prime and }\operatorname{rk}_p(X)>\operatorname{rk}_T(X)\right\} \] is infinite. Thus the same finite complex must satisfy the strict inequality for infinitely many distinct primes.

Short English statement

Does one finite connected complex, with abelian fundamental group acting trivially on all higher homotopy groups and finite-dimensional rational homotopy, have free $p$-rank strictly greater than free toral rank for infinitely many primes? The definitions of the two ranks and the intended category of complexes require clarification from the source.
