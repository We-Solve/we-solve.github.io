Math-6676

Veech Groups with Prescribed End Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $C$ be the Cantor set and let $\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,{\mathbb{R}})=\{A\in M_2({\mathbb{R}}):\det A>0\}$. A translation surface is understood here as a connected, oriented surface without boundary, equipped away from its singularities with Euclidean coordinate charts whose transition maps are translations. Its translation structure induces a flat metric. The usual tameness convention is that points of the metric completion have local models given by regular Euclidean disks, finite-angle Euclidean cones of angle $2\pi k$, $k\geq 1$, or infinite-angle cones. The supplied record does not specify its precise convention for tameness; this local-model formulation requires confirmation against the source.

An orientation-preserving affine automorphism of $S$ is a self-homeomorphism that, in translation coordinates, has the form $z\mapsto Az+b$, with a constant derivative $A\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$. Its Veech group is \[ \operatorname{Veech}(S)=\{D f:f\in\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(S)\}\leq\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,{\mathbb{R}}). \] The realization requested below is equality with the prescribed matrix subgroup $G$, rather than merely an abstract group isomorphism.

Write $\operatorname{Ends}(S)$ for the topological end space, defined by compatible choices of components escaping compact subsets of $S$, with its usual end topology. An end is planar if it has a neighborhood of genus zero. Thus having no planar ends means that every end is accumulated by genus. Infinite genus means that the genera of compact subsurfaces of $S$ are unbounded.

Supplied realization question. For every closed subspace $X\subseteq C$ and every countable subgroup $G\leq\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ without contracting elements, does there exist a tame translation surface $S$ of infinite genus, without planar ends, such that \[ \operatorname{Veech}(S)=G \qquad\text{and}\qquad \operatorname{Ends}(S)\cong X, \] where $\cong$ denotes homeomorphism of topological spaces?

Unresolved qualifications. The record does not define "contracting." A conventional metric interpretation is that $A$ is contracting when \[ \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}:=\sup_{\|v\|_2=1}\|Av\|_2<1, \] so that the hypothesis becomes $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq1$ for every $A\in G$. This interpretation is provisional and must be checked against the cited report. Also, the stated condition that $X$ be closed permits $X=\varnothing$. Under the usual surface conventions, a connected surface of infinite genus is noncompact and has a nonempty end space. Consequently, the empty case is incompatible with the requested realization. The supplied record does not state whether $X\neq\varnothing$ was intended; that restriction has not been silently inserted.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every countable matrix group satisfying the source's noncontraction condition occurs as the Veech group of a tame surface of infinite genus whose end space is prescribed and whose ends are all accumulated by genus. The empty end-space case and the precise contraction and tameness conventions require clarification. The supplied status is partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
