Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record is an incomplete preamble: it ends with "So, it may be reasonable to ask:" before the actual question. Consequently, no complete conjecture or research question can be stated from this record alone. The assertions below are retained as source-reported background, requiring mathematical review.

The source uses the abbreviation SCY3 for a strict Calabi--Yau threefold, but does not supply its precise definition, ground field, or complete geometric hypotheses. These hypotheses are left unresolved. Write $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ for the automorphism group of $X$, and $c_2(X)$ for its second Chern class whenever defined under the intended hypotheses. For a projective variety $Y$, let $N^1(Y)_{{\mathbb{R}}}$ denote the real vector space of Cartier divisor classes modulo numerical equivalence. For divisor classes on $X$, set \[ c_2(X)^{\perp}=\{\alpha\in N^1(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}:\langle c_2(X),\alpha\rangle=0\}. \] The pairing here is the intersection pairing intended by the source.

The excerpt defines a $c_2$-contraction to be a surjective morphism $\varphi:X\to B$, where $X$ is a SCY3 and $B$ is a normal projective variety with $\dim B>0$, having connected fibers and satisfying \[ \langle c_2(X),\varphi^*[H_B]\rangle=0 \] for an ample Cartier divisor $H_B$ on $B$. The source states that, by the pseudo-effectivity of $c_2(X)$, this is equivalent to \[ \langle c_2(X),\varphi^*\beta\rangle=0 \qquad\text{for every }\beta\in N^1(B)_{{\mathbb{R}}}. \] It further states that such a morphism is never an isomorphism, because simple connectedness of $X$ implies that $c_2(X)$ is not the zero functional on $N^1(X)$. These are supplied assertions, with their intended hypotheses unresolved.

According to the literal factorization direction in Definition 6 of the excerpt, a $c_2$-contraction $\varphi_0:X\to B_0$ is maximal if, for every $c_2$-contraction $\varphi:X\to B$, there exists a holomorphic map $g:B\to B_0$ such that \[ \varphi_0=g\circ\varphi. \] This direction is retained as supplied and requires checking against the full definition in the report.

Theorem 5 of the excerpt asserts that each of two rigid SCY3 varieties $X_k$, $k\in\{3,7\}$, admits a primitive automorphism $f_k\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_k)$ of positive entropy. The explicit definitions of $X_k$ are deferred to reference [10], whose bibliographic entry is absent. The meanings of rigidity, primitivity, and entropy are not specified in the supplied passage. The source cites [11] for $X_3$ and [9, Lemma 4.6] for $X_7$, and adds the comparison $d_2(f)>d_1(f)$ for an automorphism of $X_7$ discussed there. Neither the definitions of $d_i$ nor the precise scope of that comparison are supplied. The opening claim that there are only two "such" SCY3 examples has an antecedent missing from this record.

Theorem 7 of the excerpt asserts that every SCY3 admitting a $c_2$-contraction admits a maximal one, and that a SCY3 admits a birational $c_2$-contraction if and only if it is isomorphic to $X_3$ or $X_7$. It also asserts that the birational $c_2$-contraction is maximal, referring to [10] for proof and details.

For $K\in\{{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}}\}$, retain $\operatorname{Amp}(X)(K)$ as the notation appearing in the extraction. It is unresolved whether this denotes ample classes or whether a closure or another cone notation was lost during extraction. Proposition 8, attributed in the source to Wilson [14], is reported as \[ |\operatorname{Aut}(X)|=\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(c_2(X)^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{Amp}(X)({\mathbb{R}})\right)\setminus\{0\}\ne\varnothing \] for a SCY3 $X$. The source then gives the speaker's qualified knowledge statement that no example is known to them satisfying both \[ \left(c_2(X)^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{Amp}(X)({\mathbb{R}})\right)\setminus\{0\}\ne\varnothing, \qquad \left(c_2(X)^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{Amp}(X)({\mathbb{Q}})\right)\setminus\{0\}=\varnothing. \] This is a source-specific knowledge qualification, not a verified nonexistence assertion. The record does not state the question that follows, and these displayed conditions do not determine its intended hypotheses or quantifiers.
