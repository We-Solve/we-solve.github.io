Open Problems in Wavelet Theory — Problem 10

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$, and write $A^T$ for the transpose of a real $n\times n$ matrix $A$. The negative powers in the displayed formula require $A$ to be invertible. Let $\widehat\psi$ denote the Fourier transform of $\psi\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$, with the Fourier normalization left unresolved because it is not specified in the supplied record.

For clarity, under the conventional affine interpretation, the wavelet family associated with $(A,\Gamma)$ would be \[ \mathcal W(\psi;A,\Gamma) =\{\psi_{j,\gamma}:j\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}, \qquad \psi_{j,\gamma}(x)=|\det A|^{j/2}\psi(A^j x-\gamma). \] This interpretation of the associated family requires verification against the source context. An orthonormal wavelet means that this family is an orthonormal basis of $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n)$. A Parseval wavelet means that it is a Parseval frame, namely \[ \|f\|_{L^2}^2 =\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} |\langle f,\psi_{j,\gamma}\rangle|^2 \qquad\text{for every }f\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^n), \] when the source uses this discrete affine interpretation.

Question (Bownik and Rzeszotnik, Problem 10, 2019). For every admissible pair $(A,\Gamma)$ in the intended source setting, does every orthonormal wavelet $\psi$ associated with $(A,\Gamma)$ satisfy Calderón's formula \[ \sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}} \left|\widehat\psi((A^T)^j\xi)\right|^2=1 \qquad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^n? \] Does the same conclusion hold for every Parseval wavelet associated with $(A,\Gamma)$? The nonnegative series is interpreted as \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=-N}^{N} \left|\widehat\psi((A^T)^j\xi)\right|^2. \] Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not specify the admissible class of $A$, the nature or normalization of $\Gamma$, the Fourier convention, or the precise associated affine system. In particular, no expansiveness hypothesis, lattice hypothesis, or lattice covolume normalization is supplied. These omissions matter to the scope of the quantifiers and potentially to the constant on the right-hand side. The question above preserves the supplied target $1$; it does not assert that this target is appropriate for every possible convention or parameter class.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every orthonormal wavelet, and also every Parseval wavelet, satisfies the displayed Calderón identity almost everywhere. The source record labels the problem open. Its admissible dilation matrices, translation structures, and normalization conventions are missing, so the precise scope requires human review.
