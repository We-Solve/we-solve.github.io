Optimal Flux for the Quarter-Filled Band

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let $\Lambda$ be a finite periodic square lattice with $N=|\Lambda|$ divisible by four. Give each oriented nearest-neighbour bond a phase $\theta_{xy}\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$ with $\theta_{yx}=-\theta_{xy}$. The Hermitian hopping matrix has $T_{xy}=e^{i\theta_{xy}}$ on these bonds and zero otherwise. Write its eigenvalues as $\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_N$ and define the quarter-filled energy $E(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{N/4}\lambda_j$. For each elementary square, its flux $\Phi_p$ is the sum of the phases along a consistently oriented boundary, modulo $2\pi$; bond phases also allow global twists around the torus.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Is the minimum of $E(\theta)$ over all bond phases attained by a configuration with $\Phi_p=\pi/2$ on every elementary square? The optimization retains all allowed global twists as part of the bond-phase data. [S2]

Short English statement

For noninteracting electrons filling the lowest quarter of a periodic square-lattice band, does one quarter of a flux quantum through every elementary square minimize the total energy?

A spectral bound with explicit scope.
Restrict this test to an even-by-even periodic square lattice with distinct nearest neighbours, so that the graph is bipartite and every vertex has degree four. Let $U$ equal $+1$ on one sublattice and $-1$ on the other. Then $UTU=-T$, independently of phases and global twists. Eigenvalues therefore occur as $\pm s_j$, $s_j\ge0$, $1\le j\le N/2$. Since $(T^2)_{xx}=4$, \[\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}s_j^2=2N,\qquad E=-\sum_{j=1}^{N/4}s_j^{\downarrow}\ge-\sqrt{(N/4)2N}=-N/\sqrt2 .\] Here the downward arrow orders the nonnegative eigenvalues decreasingly. The inequality follows by Cauchy--Schwarz and by bounding a partial sum of squares by the full sum. Equality would require all selected $s_j$ to equal $2\sqrt2$, and all remaining $s_j$ to vanish; this strong flat-band condition is not established for the proposed flux.
Trying a local optimality route.
If there is a spectral gap after $\lambda_{N/4}$, let $P$ be the projection onto the lowest quarter. Perturb the phase on a single edge $x\to y$ by $h$. Differentiating the finite-dimensional eigenvalue sum, or the trace of its separated spectral block, gives \[\frac{dE}{dh}\bigg|_{0}=iP_{yx}e^{i\theta_{xy}}-iP_{xy}e^{-i\theta_{xy}}=-2\operatorname{Im}(P_{yx}e^{i\theta_{xy}}).\] Thus a differentiable minimum has zero bond current on every independently varied edge. Gauge variations produce dependent equations, but the necessity remains valid for each edge. At a crossing, differentiability can fail; this test cannot be used without a gap.
Obstruction.
Neither this trace bound nor current cancellation compares all flux arrangements with uniform $\pi/2$. In particular an energy lower bound need not be attained, and stationarity need not imply minimality. Odd torus circumferences can also destroy the bipartition despite $4\mid N$, so the displayed bound has deliberately narrower scope than the original lattice class. Global twists have not been fixed or discarded. The required flux minimization remains open in this attempt.
