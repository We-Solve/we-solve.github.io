A random-code identifiability calculation

To separate sample information from computational hardness, specify one model: $Q\in\mathbb F_q^{m\times n}$ has independent uniform entries, $s\in\mathbb F_q^n$ is fixed, and $b=Qs+e$, where the errors are independent of $Q$, independent across coordinates, and have common zero probability $\theta>1/q$. Their distribution on nonzero symbols is otherwise arbitrary. We examine exhaustive nearest-codeword recovery of $s$.
Lemma: If independent variables $X_i\in[0,1]$ have sum mean $\mu$, then for $a>0$, either tail at distance $ma$ from $\mu$ has probability at most $e^{-2ma^2}$.
Proof: For one such variable let $g(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{tX}$. The second derivative is the variance under the tilted probability law. Since $\mathbb E(X-1/2)^2\le1/4$ under every law supported on $[0,1]$, this variance is at most $1/4$. With $g(0)=0$ and $g'(0)=\mathbb EX$, twice integrating gives $\log\mathbb E e^{t(X-\mathbb EX)}\le t^2/8$ for every real $t$. Independence and exponential Markov give an upper-tail bound $\exp(-tma+mt^2/8)$ for $t>0$. Choosing $t=4a$ proves the result; applying the same argument to $-\sum(X_i-\mathbb EX_i)$ proves the lower tail. ∎
Proposition: Except with probability at most $q^n\exp[-m(\theta-1/q)^2/2]$, the true $s$ is the unique message minimizing the Hamming discrepancy between $b$ and $Qs'$.
Proof: The true discrepancy is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables of mean $1-\theta$. For each fixed $s'\ne s$, the nonzero linear form given by each row of $Q(s'-s)$ is uniform over $\mathbb F_q$, independently across rows and independently of the errors. Thus the wrong discrepancy is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables of mean $1-1/q$. Use the intermediate threshold \[ \tau=m\left[1-(\theta+1/q)/2\right]. \] The probability that the true discrepancy is at least $\tau$, or that a particular wrong discrepancy is at most $\tau$, is bounded by $\exp[-m(\theta-1/q)^2/2]$ by the lemma. A union bound over one true and $q^n-1$ wrong events proves the assertion. The strict inequalities outside these events also exclude ties, whether or not $\tau$ is integral. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

A seeded instance with $q=3,n=2,m=100,\theta=3/4$ was checked against all nine messages; the theoretical union bound was evaluated as well. This is a polynomial sample bound when the parameters permit a fixed positive gap $\theta-1/q$, but the described search enumerates $q^n$ candidates. The attempted inference from statistical distinguishability to efficient decoding stops at that enumeration. Conversely, worst-case hardness of a decoding problem does not by itself imply average-case hardness under this independent random-code/noise distribution. No such reduction, efficient decoder or general hardness classification is proved. This is an auxiliary known-style calculation for a specified ensemble, not completion of the report's broad decoding-complexity question.
