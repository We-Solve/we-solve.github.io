Math-5038

Cocompact Amenable Subgroups of Type-I Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff topological group. A unitary representation is understood to be strongly continuous. The group $G$ is type I if, for every unitary representation $\pi$ for which the von Neumann algebra $\pi(G)''$ is a factor, that factor is of type I, that is, isomorphic to $B(\mathcal K)$ for some Hilbert space $\mathcal K$.

A locally compact group $H$ is amenable if there is a positive linear functional $m:L^\infty(H)\to{\mathbb{C}}$, with respect to a left Haar measure, such that $m(1)=1$ and $m(f\circ L_h)=m(f)$ for all $h\in H$ and $f\in L^\infty(H)$, where $L_h(x)=hx$. A closed subgroup $H\leq G$ is cocompact if the quotient space $G/H$ is compact. We use the usual interpretation that the sought subgroup is closed and carries its subspace topology; the supplied statement does not explicitly include the word "closed."

Main conjecture (Conjecture 1 in the supplied excerpt). For every second-countable locally compact group $G$, \[ G\text{ is type I} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\,H\leq G\;\left(H\text{ is closed and amenable, and }G/H\text{ is compact}\right). \] No nonamenability assumption is imposed. If $G$ is amenable, the conclusion holds by taking $H=G$; this explains the source's description of the statement as "meaningless" in that case.

Qualification concerning a converse. The excerpt observes that amenable groups need not be type I, so the unrestricted converse fails. It only suggests that a converse might hold under the additional assumption that $G$ has trivial amenable radical, meaning that its only amenable closed normal subgroup is $\{e\}$. In precise conditional form, the suggested question is whether, for a second-countable locally compact $G$ with this property, the existence of a closed cocompact amenable subgroup implies that $G$ is type I. This suggestion is separate from the main conjecture.

Additional tree context in the supplied excerpt. Let $T$ be a locally finite tree in which every vertex has degree at least $3$. Its end space $\partial T$, consisting of equivalence classes of infinite rays that eventually have a common tail, has its usual compact Hausdorff topology. Let $G$ be a noncompact closed subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(T)$, with the topology of pointwise convergence on vertices, and suppose that its action on $\partial T$ is minimal, meaning that every orbit is dense. A group is liminal if every irreducible unitary representation $\pi$ maps its full group $C^*$-algebra onto the compact operators on the representation space. The separately stated Conjecture 2, attributed in the excerpt to Nebbia [8] and Houdayer--Raum [7], asserts the equivalence of \[ \begin{aligned} \text{(i)}\;&G\text{ is liminal},\\ \text{(ii)}\;&G\text{ is type I},\\ \text{(iii)}\;&G\text{ acts transitively on ordered pairs of distinct points of }\partial T. \end{aligned} \] The excerpt reports that, under these tree hypotheses, (iii) is equivalent to the existence of a cocompact amenable subgroup, citing its reference [3]. It states that (i) implies (ii) in general, attributes the conjectural implication (iii) to (i) to Nebbia [8], and attributes the conjectural implication (ii) to (iii) to Houdayer--Raum [7]. These are contextual source assertions, not additional hypotheses of the main conjecture.

The excerpt also reports the following theorem of Houdayer--Raum [7]: for $G$ and $T$ as above, if the group von Neumann algebra $L(G)=\lambda_G(G)''$, generated by the left regular representation on $L^2(G)$, is amenable as a von Neumann algebra, then every vertex stabilizer $G_v$ acts $2$-transitively on the neighbors of $v$. It gives the type I property of $L(G)$ as a sufficient condition for this operator-algebraic amenability. The excerpt further states that boundary $2$-transitivity implies this local $2$-transitivity and that the converse need not hold. The numbered references underlying these contextual claims are not identified bibliographically in the supplied record. A subsequent mention of a conjectural description of nondiscrete type I groups in reference [4] supplies no formulation and therefore cannot be reconstructed here.

Supplied status qualification. The registry labels the problem partially solved. Its literature assessment dated 2026-08-22 says that the conjecture is proved in important hyperbolic and unimodular cases and remains open in general. Short English statement

The main question asks whether every second-countable locally compact type I group contains a closed amenable subgroup with compact quotient. For amenable groups, choosing the whole group already gives such a subgroup. The supplied excerpt also discusses a possible converse under a trivial amenable radical and a separate conjecture about groups acting on trees. Its partially solved label and claims about known cases are supplied dated assessments requiring review.
