Math-1238

Virtual classes for KSBA stable-surface moduli

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Hassett's AIM question asks whether the moduli space of stable surfaces has a virtual fundamental class. In the KSBA interpretation, stable surfaces are projective semi-log-canonical surfaces with ample ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier canonical class. A precise question must fix invariants and distinguish a moduli stack $\mathcal M$ from its coarse space, as well as the deformation theory, expected dimension, and coefficient theory for a virtual cycle. For example a Behrend--Fantechi perfect obstruction theory is a morphism $E^\bullet\to L_{\mathcal M}$, with $E^\bullet$ perfect of amplitude $[-1,0]$, inducing an isomorphism on $h^0$ and a surjection on $h^{-1}$; it yields a virtual class of its virtual dimension. The original does not fix these data.

The supplied report says Jiang's arXiv:2206.00575v7 claims a global rational Chow class on a fixed-invariant KSBA stack, by pushing forward the virtual fundamental class of a proper auxiliary stack of lci covers. It explicitly distinguishes this from a perfect obstruction theory on the full KSBA stack, whose natural index-one deformation complex can have higher obstructions. This records the cited version's claim, not an independently verified existence theorem.

The report separately asserts that a universal proper flat lci and Gorenstein family of stable stacky surfaces with finite reduced automorphism groups has a two-term Behrend--Fantechi perfect obstruction theory. Precise base, stack, and universal-family hypotheses are not supplied; this is a restricted lci assertion, not a perfectness statement across the entire non-lci boundary.

Its candidate comparison criterion is the following. Let $U\subset\mathcal M$ be the intrinsic lci open locus and $i:B=\mathcal M\setminus U\hookrightarrow\mathcal M$ its closed complement. Suppose two proper auxiliary constructions give classes \[ \alpha_j=(f_j)_*[\mathcal N_j]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_v(\mathcal M)_{{\mathbb{Q}}} \quad(j=1,2) \] of the same virtual dimension $v$, and their restrictions agree on $U$. Then the report asserts \[ \alpha_1-\alpha_2\in \operatorname{im}\left(i_*:A_v(B)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to A_v(\mathcal M)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\right). \] Consequently $\alpha_1=\alpha_2$ if $A_v(B)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=0$, in particular if $\dim B<v$. This is presentation-independence among the compatible constructions agreeing on the lci locus. It does not establish that every proposed auxiliary construction has that agreement, nor does it produce a full-stack obstruction theory. The report requires the rational Chow localization framework in which these restrictions and proper pushforwards are defined.

Short English statement

Ask for virtual classes on stable-surface moduli. The report distinguishes an auxiliary pushforward class from a full perfect obstruction theory and proposes that two constructions agreeing on the lci locus can differ only by a non-lci boundary class.
