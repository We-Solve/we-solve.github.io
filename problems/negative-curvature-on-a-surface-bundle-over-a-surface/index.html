Negative curvature on a surface bundle over a surface

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For fixed genera $g,h$ with $h\ge2$, the question of Dieter Kotschick asks for a locally trivial surface bundle \[ F_h\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow B_g \] whose total space admits a Riemannian metric of strictly negative sectional curvature. Use closed connected oriented surfaces in the reduction below. The metric need not make the bundle map a Riemannian submersion. The following heading "4 Invariants of representations" is not part of the question.

For $g,h\ge2$, let $\rho:\pi_1(B_g)\to\operatorname{Mod}(F_h)$ be monodromy and \[ 1\to\pi_1(F_h)\to\pi_1(X)\to\pi_1(B_g)\to1 \] the associated group extension. Faithful purely pseudo-Anosov means $\rho$ is injective and sends every nontrivial element to a pseudo-Anosov mapping class; it is stronger than saying merely that the image has no reducible nontrivial element. Convex-cocompact monodromy means the image has a quasiconvex orbit in fiber Teichmüller space, in the convention of the cited criterion. The supplied aid reports the chain \[ \sec_X<0\ \Longrightarrow\ \pi_1(X)\text{ word-hyperbolic} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \rho\text{ faithful convex-cocompact} \ \Longrightarrow\ \rho\text{ faithful purely pseudo-Anosov} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ {\mathbb{Z}}^2\not\hookrightarrow\pi_1(X). \] The middle equivalence is attributed to Hamenstädt/Farb--Mosher. The last equivalence includes the kernel case: if a nontrivial base element has trivial outer monodromy, a suitable lift centralizes a nontrivial fiber element, producing ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$. Reducible or periodic monodromy likewise produces such a subgroup after passing to a suitable power. Conversely the centralizing relation from a ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$ subgroup is incompatible with faithful purely pseudo-Anosov monodromy.

There is no reverse implication from absence of ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$ to word hyperbolicity asserted here, nor from word-hyperbolic extension to a smooth strictly negatively curved metric on $X$. The latter metric-realization step remains unresolved in the supplied report. The aid invokes Bowditch finiteness for fixed-genus candidate monodromies but does not supply the exact finiteness class, equivalence relation or theorem hypotheses; it cannot be substituted for existence of a candidate or a metric.

One existing example would propagate in base genus: a degree-$d$ connected cover of $B_g$ gives a pullback bundle and pulled-back negatively curved metric with genera \[ (g',h)=(1+d(g-1),h),\qquad d\ge1. \] This does not supply an initial example for any prescribed pair. The original wording states only $h\ge2$; the supplied group-theoretic reduction is explicitly for $g\ge2$ and does not analyze the other base genera.

Kotschick's accompanying comment says pseudo-Anosov monodromy is necessary and that constant negative curvature forces signature zero, hence vanishing of the associated Toledo invariant. The precise representation and normalization defining that Toledo invariant are not supplied. The strengthened faithful condition and the distinction between variable and constant curvature are retained; no constant-curvature construction is asserted.

Short English statement

For fixed genera, seek a surface bundle admitting strictly negative sectional curvature. The supplied reduction separates faithful convex-cocompact monodromy, the weaker no-${\mathbb{Z}}^2$ obstruction and the unresolved metric-realization step, while retaining the source's constant-curvature signature/Toledo qualification.
