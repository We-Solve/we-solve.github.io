The Riemann Hypothesis

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems openaiunknowngpt 6 astra progpt 6 astra ultraunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $s\in{\mathbb{C}}$ with $\Re s>1$, define \[ \zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}. \] Use its meromorphic continuation to ${\mathbb{C}}$, whose only pole is at $s=1$. The assertion is \[ \forall\rho\in{\mathbb{C}},\qquad \left(0<\Re\rho<1\ \text{and}\ \zeta(\rho)=0\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ \Re\rho=\tfrac12. \] The open critical strip excludes the negative even integer zeros. The assertion concerns locations, not multiplicities, of zeros.

Formulation and concept references: [S1].

Short English statement

Are all zeta zeros between the vertical lines zero and one located on the halfway line?

OpenAI claim summary for Math-2

This question concerns the Riemann zeta function alone and requires every zero in the open critical strip to have real part $1/2$. The selected papers exclude zeros only to the right of $11/12$ or $7/8$. Zeta zeros in the remaining strip are not placed on the critical line, so these are partial claims toward the displayed question.

The Riemann Hypothesis

OpenAI's claim

The two selected manuscripts claim zero-free half-planes for the Riemann zeta function, together with broader Dirichlet and finite-order Hecke families. Manuscript 1 gives the boundary $11/12$, and manuscript 2 gives $7/8$. Neither boundary is the critical line $1/2$ required by this problem. Manuscript numbers follow the order in Sources.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and every finite-order Hecke character $\chi$ over $K$, the claimed zero-free assertion, with meromorphic poles excluded from evaluation, is \[ \operatorname{Re}s>11/12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_K(s,\chi)\ne0. \] It includes every Dirichlet $L$-function and the Riemann zeta function. The accompanying prime-progression claim uses $\pi(x;q,a)$ and asserts, with an absolute effective constant, \[ \sup_{\substack{1\le q\le x\$a,q)=1}} \left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\int_2^x\frac{dt}{\log t}\right| \ll x^{11/12}\log x\quad(x\ge2). \] The critical-line requirement $\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2$ is not a consequence of this half-plane claim.
Manuscript 2.
For every finite-order Hecke character of $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, every Dirichlet character and the zeta function, the principal claim is \[ \operatorname{Re}s>7/8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(s)\ne0, \] where a principal character's pole at $s=1$ is allowed. The weaker displayed $11/12$ theorem follows within this scope. The cited arithmetic consequence gives absolute $A,C>0$ such that every odd prime $p$ has least positive quadratic nonresidue \[ n(p)\le C(\log p)^A. \] Thus $n(p)\ll_{\delta} p^{\delta}$ for each fixed $\delta>0$. Given binary $p$ and $a\in\mathbb F_p$, a deterministic algorithm of time polynomial in $\log p$ is further claimed to return a square root of $a$ or correctly declare it nonexistent. The half-plane assertion leaves zeros between the critical line and $7/8$ untreated.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05), twisted Möbius sums are enlarged to a family before Poisson summation. A recursive estimate for the dual mean square, followed by inversion of cube factors, supplies the saving used to exclude a reciprocal-function singularity.

For manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), smoothed character sums are estimated in two ways: directly by large-sieve and recursive moment bounds, and through a Mellin transform involving the reciprocal of an $L$-function. Poisson summation and sextic residue characters align the two expressions so that their savings force continuation of the reciprocal.

Formal verification

For manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), OpenAI lists Lean statements for the $7/8$ zero-free claims for zeta, Dirichlet functions and finite-order Hecke functions over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. This covers the asserted half-plane statements and does not establish the critical-line conclusion asked here. No Lean formalization is listed at this snapshot for manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05).
