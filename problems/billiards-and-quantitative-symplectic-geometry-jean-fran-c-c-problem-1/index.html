Billiards and Quantitative Symplectic Geometry — Jean-Fran\c{c — Problem 1

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A closed manifold means a compact smooth manifold without boundary. For a connected manifold, being a $K(\pi,1)$ means having fundamental group $\pi$ and satisfying $\pi_k(M)=0$ for every $k\geq 2$. The source does not explicitly state connectedness of $M$; its formulation for disconnected manifolds is unresolved. The statement below uses the usual connected interpretation.

Let $p:T^*M\to M$ be the cotangent projection. The tautological one-form is $\lambda_{(q,\xi)}(v)=\xi({\,\mathrm{d}} p(v))$, and we use the standard symplectic form $\omega=-{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda$. For a smooth autonomous Hamiltonian $H:T^*M\to{\mathbb{R}}$, define its Hamiltonian vector field by $\iota_{X_H}\omega=-{\,\mathrm{d}} H$. A contractible periodic orbit on $H^{-1}(e)$ means a nonconstant smooth map $x:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to T^*M$, for some $T>0$, satisfying \[ \dot x(t)=X_H(x(t)),\qquad H(x(t))=e, \] whose underlying loop is null-homotopic in $T^*M$. Equivalently, its projection to $M$ is null-homotopic. Contractibility is understood in the ambient cotangent bundle, rather than necessarily within the energy hypersurface.

Conjecture, in the usual connected interpretation. Let $M$ be a closed connected smooth manifold that is not a $K(\pi_1(M),1)$. For every closed connected smooth embedded hypersurface $\Sigma\subset T^*M$, every smooth autonomous Hamiltonian $H:T^*M\to{\mathbb{R}}$, and every $c\in{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ \Sigma=H^{-1}(c),\qquad {\,\mathrm{d}} H_z\neq 0\quad\text{for every }z\in\Sigma, \] there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that, for Lebesgue-almost every $e\in(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon)$, the level $H^{-1}(e)$ carries a contractible periodic orbit of $H$. The equality with $H^{-1}(c)$ concerns the entire level. The source imposes no contact-type condition, no requirement that $\Sigma$ enclose the zero section, and no properness assumption on $H$ in this conjecture. Smoothness, nonconstancy, Lebesgue measure, and ambient contractibility are the standard interpretations used here.

Supplied context and qualifications. The following are reports from the supplied source, not independently verified literature conclusions. For contact-type hypersurfaces, the source recalls the Weinstein conjecture [Weinstein79] and credits Viterbo with its first proof in ${\mathbb{R}}^{2n}$ [Viterbo-WeinsteinR2n]. Its general discussion of the Hamiltonian Seifert question assumes a proper autonomous smooth Hamiltonian on a symplectic manifold. It reports counterexamples in ${\mathbb{R}}^{2n}$ for $2n\geq6$, credited to Ginzburg [Ginzburg-Ham-Seifert1, Ginzburg-Ham-Seifert2] and Herman [Herman], and $\mathcal C^2$ counterexamples for $2n=4$, credited to Ginzburg and Gürel [Ginzburg-Gurel1, Ginzburg-Gurel2]. It credits Hofer--Zehnder [HZ] and Struwe [Struwe] with almost existence on almost all regular levels of smooth proper Hamiltonians on standard ${\mathbb{R}}^{2n}$.

The supplied account asserts that a closed connected hypersurface in a cotangent bundle separates it into a bounded and an unbounded component; the precise hypotheses and meaning of boundedness in that contextual assertion are not supplied. It credits Hofer--Viterbo [HV88] with almost existence for hypersurfaces enclosing the zero section, without a contractibility conclusion. It credits Albers--Frauenfelder--Oancea [AFO17] with the contractible almost-existence property when the Hurewicz map $\pi_2(M)\to H_2(M)$ is nonzero, using multiplicative symplectic homology with suitable rank-one local coefficients. It credits Frauenfelder--Pajitnov [Frauenfelder-Pajitnov] with this property for rationally inessential manifolds, described there as those whose rational fundamental class maps to zero under the classifying map $M\to B\pi_1(M)$, using $S^1$-equivariant homology and localization. The orientation or coefficient conventions needed for that fundamental-class description are not specified.

The contributor reports in [BDHO-cotangent] a proof for every $M$ that is not a $K(\pi,1)$, but only for a restricted class of hypersurfaces that the supplied record does not define. The stated method is symplectic homology with differential graded local coefficients. The source expresses the hope that the complementary methods of [AFO17], [Frauenfelder-Pajitnov], and [BDHO-cotangent] can be combined to establish the full conjecture.

The source further reports that, in all cases then known to it where contractible almost existence holds for all closed hypersurfaces, it follows from finiteness of the $\pi_1$-sensitive Hofer--Zehnder capacity of a disc cotangent bundle $D^*Q$. It suggests the auxiliary conjecture that this capacity is finite for every manifold $Q$ that is not a $K(\pi,1)$. This auxiliary suggestion is not a fully specified additional statement here: its manifold hypotheses, metric defining $D^*Q$, and capacity conventions are not supplied.

Finally, the source motivates the non-$K(\pi,1)$ assumption by closed manifolds admitting metrics of nonpositive sectional curvature. It states that these are $K(\pi,1)$ manifolds with no nonconstant contractible closed geodesics, and hence that their positive-radius cotangent sphere bundles have no contractible closed characteristics. This motivation retains the contractibility requirement in the conjecture.

Short English statement

For a closed manifold that is not a $K(\pi,1)$, the conjecture asks whether every closed connected regular energy hypersurface in its cotangent bundle has an interval of nearby energies on almost every one of which there is a nonconstant periodic Hamiltonian orbit contractible in the ambient cotangent bundle. The connected interpretation of $M$ and standard smooth conventions are made explicit above. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status or the reported partial results.
