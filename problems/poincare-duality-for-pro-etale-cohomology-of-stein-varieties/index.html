Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ denote the prime implicit in the coefficients ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$, and let $X$ be a smooth, geometrically irreducible Stein variety of dimension $d$ over the base field $K$ appearing in the source. The supplied record does not specify the hypotheses on $K$, its relation to $p$, or the precise analytic category meant by "variety." These are unresolved hypotheses, and the statement must not be read as quantifying over arbitrary fields $K$.

For a Tate twist ${\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)$, write \[ H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) =H^i\left(R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j))\right). \] The source specifies compact supports using an exhaustive affinoid covering $X=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}U_n$ with $U_n\Subset U_{n+1}$, where $\Subset$ denotes relative compactness. It sets \[ \begin{aligned} R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(\partial X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) &:=\operatorname*{colim}_{n}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X\setminus U_n,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)),\\ R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) &:=\operatorname{fib}\!\left( R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(\partial X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \right),\\ H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) &:=H^i\left(R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j))\right). \end{aligned} \] Here the fiber is a derived fiber. The notation $\partial X$ is specified through the displayed colimit; the excerpt does not identify it with a separately constructed boundary space or specify the category in which the colimit is taken.

Conjectural topological assertion. For such $X$, the source asks whether \[ H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \quad\text{is nuclear Fr\'echet},\qquad H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \quad\text{is of compact type}. \] These are properties of topological ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$-vector spaces, not merely of their underlying vector spaces. A Fréchet space is a complete metrizable locally convex space. In the usual nonarchimedean conventions, nuclear Fréchet spaces admit countable projective presentations by Banach spaces with compact transition maps, and spaces of compact type admit countable locally convex inductive presentations by Banach spaces with injective compact transition maps. The excerpt does not specify the cohomology topologies or its precise functional-analytic conventions. The assertion is intended uniformly in cohomological degree and Tate twist, but explicit ranges for $i$ and $j$ are not supplied.

Conjectural duality assertion and unresolved dimension dependence. The clean statement asks whether cup product gives perfect duality between ordinary and compactly supported pro-étale cohomology. The extracted original text, despite introducing arbitrary dimension $d$, displays the numerical formulas \[ \begin{aligned} \gamma_{X,i}:H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) &\xrightarrow{\sim} \left(H^{4-i}_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(2-j))\right)^*,\\ \gamma^c_{X,i}:H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) &\xrightarrow{\sim} \left(H^{4-i}_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(2-j))\right)^*, \end{aligned} \qquad (-)^*:=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}(-,{\mathbb{Q}}_p). \] The extraction is visibly incomplete and interleaves these formulas with a higher-dimensional conjecture. Consequently, it does not establish that the constants $4$ and $2$ are the intended complementary degree and twist in arbitrary dimension. No replacement involving $d$ is inferred here. The displayed constants would require a cup-product pairing followed by a trace of the form \[ H^i_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \times H^{4-i}_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(2-j)) \longrightarrow H^4_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(2)) \longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}_p, \] but the trace and its hypotheses are absent from the supplied extract. The notation $\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}$ also leaves unresolved whether continuous maps are intended, which topology is placed on the dual, and whether the displayed isomorphisms are topological. These omissions prevent a fully specified higher-dimensional perfect-duality statement.

Qualifications retained from the surrounding source. The excerpt states that in dimension $1$, its compact-support complex with coefficients ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$ agrees with Huber's \’etale cohomology with compact support, and expresses the belief that this agreement holds in every dimension; the latter is not presented as established. For smooth dagger varieties over $K$ of dimension $1$, it describes proper cohomology groups as "finite," asserts the nuclear Fréchet/compact-type distinction above for Stein varieties, and says that the roles are reversed for dagger affinoids. The meaning of "finite" is not clarified in the extract.

The surrounding discussion also reports a derived duality for Stein $X$, written \[ R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(j)) \xrightarrow{\sim} D\!\left(R\Gamma_{\mathrm{pro\acute et},c}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_p(2-j))[4]\right), \qquad D(-):=R\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}(-,{\mathbb{Q}}_p). \] Its ambient derived-category notation is truncated, and the scope of this assertion relative to the surrounding curve discussion is unresolved. The preceding argument invokes vanishing of positive $\operatorname{Ext}$ groups for compactly supported cohomology of compact type, describes such spaces as colimits of Smith spaces that are projective solid objects, and invokes classical reflexivity to obtain the reverse duality. These are retained as contextual source assertions with incomplete categorical hypotheses, not as a proof of the higher-dimensional conjecture.

Short English statement

For a smooth geometrically irreducible Stein variety, the problem asks whether ordinary pro-étale cohomology is nuclear Fréchet, compactly supported cohomology is of compact type, and cup product gives perfect duality. The supplied extract leaves the base-field hypotheses, topologies, and higher-dimensional complementary degrees and twists unresolved. Its displayed $4-i$ and $2-j$ formulas are preserved without extrapolation. This is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
