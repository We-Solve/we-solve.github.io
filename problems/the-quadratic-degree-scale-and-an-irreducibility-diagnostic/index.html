Math-1286

The quadratic degree scale and an irreducibility diagnostic

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a degree-$d$ plane curve $C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ define \[H([a:b:c])=\max\{|a|,|b|,|c|\}\quad\text{for primitive integer coordinates},\qquad N(C;B)=\#\{P\in C({\mathbb{Q}}):H(P)\le B\}.\] The repeated $d$ in $N(C;d)$ is intentional: the height cutoff equals the degree. The original does not explicitly impose irreducibility or geometric integrality.

Original question (Heath-Brown). Improve the uniform bound $N(C;d)\ll d^3$, given examples with $N(C;d)\gg d^2$. A dated remark by Roger Heath-Brown, September 25, 2004, says that $d^{5/2}$ is easy and that the challenge is to improve that exponent. Uniformity in the curve is essential; a bound with an arbitrary constant depending on $C$ would not express this degree-varying question.

Attached unreviewed answer in the intended integral-curve reading. The aid attributes to Binyamini, Cluckers and Kato (2025) a uniform estimate \[N(C;d)\le c\,d^2d^{2/d}(\log d)^{12}=O\left(d^2(\log d)^{12}\right)=o(d^{5/2}),\qquad d\ge2,\] for the intended integral class of curves, with an absolute constant $c$. Here "integral" concerns irreducibility/reducedness, not a count only of affine integer points. The exact theorem's irreducible versus geometrically integral hypotheses and logarithm conventions are not supplied; the case $d=1$ cannot literally use the displayed $\log d$ expression and must be treated separately.

The aid additionally claims that for every integer $d\ge1$ one can choose an integer $\lambda_d$ with $1\le\lambda_d\le(d-1)^2+1$ such that \[\prod_{i=1}^d(X-iZ)+\lambda_d\prod_{j=1}^d(Y-jZ)=0\] is smooth and geometrically integral over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. It contains every grid point $[i:j:1]$ with $1\le i,j\le d$, all of height at most $d$, and hence has at least $d^2$ counted points. Thus the quadratic scale is reported already in a smooth geometrically integral family.

In contrast, the aid claims that for every sufficiently large $d$ there is a reduced reducible degree-$d$ curve with $N(C;d)=\Omega(d^{8/3})$, using a coefficient-height and line-lattice construction not supplied in the summary. This shows why the omitted irreducibility convention matters: that reducible growth exceeds even the dated $d^{5/2}$ bound. The theorem attribution and both construction claims require independent review.

Short English statement

The degree and height bound are both $d$. Heath-Brown's dated challenge is to beat a uniform $d^{5/2}$ bound. The aid reports a 2025 quadratic bound up to logarithms for integral curves, a smooth $d^2$ grid construction, and faster reducible examples showing that the omitted irreducibility assumption is essential.
