Generic Singularities of Mean Curvature Flow

OpenAI's claim

Uniqueness of surface tangent flow: At the first singular time of a smooth closed embedded surface flow in $\mathbb R^3$, the paper claims a unique multiplicity-one self-shrinking tangent flow at each fixed singular point. Its position and axes are unique in the original coordinates.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let a smooth compact connected embedded boundaryless surface $M_0\subset\mathbb R^3$ evolve by its maximal smooth mean-curvature flow to first singular time $T$. At every singular $(x_0,T)$, the claim provides one smooth properly embedded self-shrinker $S$ such that \[\lambda(M_{T+s/\lambda^2}-x_0)\longrightarrow\sqrt{-s}\,S\quad(\lambda\to\infty,\ s<0).\] Convergence holds for the full dilation family, locally smoothly in space with multiplicity one and uniformly on compact negative-time intervals, in the original ambient coordinates. The backward Brakke tangents quantified here are weakly measurable Radon families $(\mu_s)_{s<0}$ obtained as spacetime weight limits of dilation subsequences, with integral two-varifold slices for almost every $s$, generalized mean curvature $\mathbf H_s$, and $\delta V_s=-\mathbf H_s\mu_s$. For each compact spatial $K$ and compact time interval $J\subset(-\infty,0)$ they obey $\int_J\int_K(1+|\mathbf H_s|^2)\,d\mu_s\,ds<\infty$. Every choice of $a<b<0$ and nonnegative $\varphi\in C_c^1(\mathbb R^3\times[a,b])$ obeys \[\mu_b(\varphi_b)-\mu_a(\varphi_a)\le\int_a^b\int(\partial_s\varphi+\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf H_s-\varphi|\mathbf H_s|^2)\,d\mu_s\,ds.\] Tests may be nonzero at $a,b$, and these endpoints need not be typical slice times. All such tangents satisfy $\mu_s(A)=\mathcal H^2(A\cap\sqrt{-s}\,S)$ for every Borel $A$ and every $s<0$. Later singularities, higher dimensions and arbitrary weak initial flows are not included.

Outline of the argument

Uniqueness of surface tangent flow: The stationary analysis handles conical and cylindrical shrinker ends together, including quadratic opening modes for which ordinary analytic graph arguments do not suffice. Finite Taylor jets and exponentially small end terms produce a localized gradient inequality. Increasing graph radii are chosen from preceding energy drops so that cutoff errors are summable; finite rescaled length gives convergence with its ambient position and axes fixed.
