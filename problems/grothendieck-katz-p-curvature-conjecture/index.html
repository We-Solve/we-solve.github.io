Grothendieck–Katz $p$-Curvature Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field and $(F,\partial_F)$ the one-variable differential field over $K$ in the source. In the usual arithmetic-function-field setup, $F/K$ is finitely generated of transcendence degree one and $\partial_F$ is a nonzero derivation killing $K$; the extracted statement does not explicitly give these model hypotheses. A differential module $(M,\partial_M)$ has finite rank $r$ over $F$ and an additive operator satisfying \[ \partial_M(fm)=\partial_F(f)m+f\partial_M(m). \] A full system of algebraic solutions means that there exists a finite algebraic extension $L/F$, with its extended characteristic-zero derivation, such that $L\otimes_F M$ has an $L$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ with $\partial(e_i)=0$. It does not mean just one nonzero algebraic horizontal vector.

To define reduction, one must spread the field/curve and connection data over an arithmetic model and exclude its finitely many bad primes. For a good nonarchimedean prime $v$ of $K$, let $p_v$ be its residue characteristic and $\nabla_v$ the reduced connection. Its $p_v$-curvature at a derivation $\delta$ of the reduced function field is \[ \psi_v(\delta)=(\nabla_{v,\delta})^{p_v}-\nabla_{v,\delta^{[p_v]}}, \] where $\delta^{[p_v]}=\delta^{p_v}$ is again a derivation. Trivial $p_v$-curvature means $\psi_v(\delta)=0$ for every such $\delta$. This is vanishing, not merely nilpotence. In a one-derivation presentation the second term must not be dropped unless the reduced base derivation has zero $p_v$-th iterate; no special coordinate is assumed.

The Grothendieck--Katz $p$-curvature conjecture in the supplied formulation asks for the equivalence \[ M\text{ has a full system of algebraic solutions} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \psi_v=0\text{ for all but finitely many good primes }v. \] Both directions are retained. Under the customary arithmetic convention, "almost all" means outside a finite set of nonarchimedean places after choosing a good model, not just infinitely many primes or a density-one subsequence. The source writes rational primes $p$ without specifying the places above them; this convention needs confirmation in its precise setup. The same missing model data determine the admissible reductions, rather than permitting a fresh choice at each prime to force vanishing.

The literature triage checked 2026-08-21 identifies the question as the general Grothendieck--Katz conjecture, reports important proved cases, and leaves the general local--global assertion open. The exact special-case hypotheses are absent. The open label and verification note preserving conditions while normalizing delimiters remain supplied metadata, not independently verified current conclusions.

Short English statement

Does a differential module become trivial with a full horizontal basis after a finite algebraic extension exactly when its reductions have zero $p$-curvature at all but finitely many primes? The complete-basis condition, both directions, true vanishing and finite-exception quantifier are preserved. The arithmetic model needed for reduction is not supplied.
