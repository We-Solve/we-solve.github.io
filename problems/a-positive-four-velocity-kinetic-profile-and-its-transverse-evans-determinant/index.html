A positive four-velocity kinetic profile and its transverse Evans determinant

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The extraction begins with three questions: under what conditions do profiles for "this equation" exist, are they stable to multidimensional perturbations, and how should their Evans function be constructed? It suggests discrete-velocity models as a first step. The preceding equation is absent. The aid identifies this as P. Szmolyan's kinetic/Boltzmann question, rather than the subsequent Y. Li water-wave contribution. Its reconstructed target asks for collision, end-state, and speed conditions giving a positive heteroclinic profile, transverse stability criteria, and an Evans construction. The exact kinetic equation, referred to as (1.3) in the clean statement, is not supplied in this record.

For a discrete-velocity notation, write \[ \partial_t f+A_x\partial_x f+A_y\partial_y f=Q(f),\qquad D\bar f'=Q(\bar f),\quad D=A_x-cI,\quad z=x-ct, \] where $A_x,A_y$ are diagonal velocity matrices and $Q$ is a specified collision law. This notation does not fill in the missing source collision data. A collision invariant $\ell$ satisfies $\ell\cdot Q(f)=0$ and imposes the Rankine--Hugoniot condition $\ell\cdot D(f_+-f_-)=0$.

The aid's concrete four-velocity Broadwell claim uses $c=\tfrac12$ and the strictly positive profile \[ \bar f(z)=f_-+\frac{(-2,\,2/3,\,-2,\,-2)}{16(1+e^{-z/3})},\qquad f_-=(\tfrac12,2,1,1),\quad f_+=(\tfrac38,\tfrac{49}{24},\tfrac78,\tfrac78). \] It reports all collision-invariant constraints and a one-dimensional unstable-to-stable connection on an invariant leaf. The actual four velocities and collision normalization are not displayed in the supplied summary, so those data must be recovered before verifying this as a profile for an identified model.

For perturbations $e^{\lambda t+i\xi y}v(z)$ the corresponding spectral equation, when $D$ is invertible, is \[ D v'=\left(DQ(\bar f(z))-\lambda I-i\xi A_y\right)v. \] The aid reports a detailed-balance symmetrizer giving three solutions decaying at $z=-\infty$ and one decaying at $z=+\infty$ for every real $\xi$ and $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0$. With compatible bases and a common finite $z_0$, this gives an Evans determinant \[ E(\lambda,\xi)=\det\left(v^-_1(z_0),v^-_2(z_0),v^-_3(z_0),v^+_1(z_0)\right). \] Its nonvanishing is a separate stability question, not a consequence of the splitting alone. The reported rank-one far-field collision derivative $DQ(f_\pm)=a_\pm b_\pm^T$ leads, in this notation, to the dispersion determinant \[ \det M-b_\pm^T\operatorname{adj}(M)a_\pm=0, \qquad M=\mu D+\lambda I+i\xi A_y. \] The missing model data prevent specialization of these expressions here. Three collision invariants place the origin in essential spectrum at zero transverse frequency, while $\bar f'$ is a localized translation mode there. Thus the aid explicitly rejects treating the origin as a naive isolated simple Evans zero without a weighted, integrated, or renormalized construction.

The adjacent source material is attributed to Y. Li, "Stability of travelling waves of the full water wave problem near the critical case." It describes travelling waves $(\eta_c,\Phi_c)=(\eta(x-ct),\Phi_c(x-ct))$ for a variety of speeds and records the multiplier $\widehat G(k)=k\tanh k$. The nonlinear displayed equations are visibly merged: the kinematic relation $\eta_t=G\Phi$ runs into the dynamic equation, whose fractions and coefficients are not reliably recoverable. This water-wave setup is preserved as adjacent context, not substituted for the missing kinetic equation or silently repaired into a full water-wave problem.

Short English statement

The record splices a kinetic profile/stability question with a later water-wave presentation. The aid gives a positive four-component shock and a transverse Evans framework, but the actual collision law and velocities are missing here. Essential spectrum at the translation threshold prevents a simple-zero shortcut, and no full multidimensional stability conclusion follows.
