Symmetry-Preserving Evaluation of Orbitwise Generating Functions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For integer points $a$ in a polyhedron, a generating function sums monomials $z^a$. Orbitwise organization groups terms according to the symmetry action. Rational expressions may have apparent poles at $z=(1,\ldots,1)$ even when a bounded-set count has a finite value.

A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed affine half-spaces; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Its faces are its intersections with supporting hyperplanes.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Given an orbitwise rational generating function for the integer points of a symmetric polyhedron, how can its value at the singular point $z=(1,\ldots,1)$ be evaluated in an orbitwise way while preserving the symmetry? [S2]
Scope qualification.
Boundedness or a regularization convention is needed when the actual lattice-point set is infinite; the supplied question does not specify this.

Literature and context check

The primary report, printed pages 2267--2268, describes short rational generating functions and explicitly identifies generic evaluation directions as a symmetry difficulty. Its counting application is for a polytope. The attempt below assumes a bounded lattice-point set and an explicitly supplied finite lattice-automorphism group. It preserves orbit blocks by finite averaging, but obtains no savings that avoid expansion over the group.

Research attempt: Orbit-block constant terms and the invariant-direction obstruction

An exact finite evaluation route
Suppose a Laurent polynomial $F(z)=\sum_{a\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}z^a$ for bounded $P$ is given as a finite sum of terms $\varepsilon z^a/\prod_{j=1}^r(1-z^{b_j})$, with nonzero integer $b_j$. Choose an integer vector $v$ with $v\cdot b_j\ne0$ for every denominator vector of every term used. Such a vector can be found by enumerating integer vectors: finitely many proper rational hyperplanes cannot cover all of $\mathbb Z^n$. Explicitly $v=(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1})$ works for some integer $t$, because each nonzero $b$ yields a nonzero polynomial in $t$ with finitely many roots.

Substitute $z_i=e^{v_i\tau}$. Each rational term becomes a Laurent series with pole order at most $r$. Its constant term can be computed with finitely many rational operations: factor $1-e^{c\tau}=-c\tau(1+c\tau/2+\cdots)$, invert the analytic factor to the required finite degree by recursive coefficient comparison, and multiply with the Taylor series of $e^{(v\cdot a)\tau}$. Terms beyond degree $r$ cannot affect the constant term after the pole of order at most $r$ is extracted. By linearity of coefficient extraction, \[ |P\cap\mathbb Z^n|=F(1)=\sum_i\operatorname{CT}_{\tau=0}F_i(e^{v\tau}). \tag{1} \] The equality holds because the full Laurent polynomial is analytic at $\tau=0$ with value equal to its number of monomials counted with multiplicity one. Individual terms may have poles; their constant terms alone need not be counts.

Keeping a group orbit together
Let a finite $G\leq\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ act on exponents. Suppose an orbit block consists of the distinct transforms of a rational term $R$, whose stabilizer as a rational function is $H\leq G$. Choose $v$ generic for all transformed denominators. Its contribution to $1$ can be evaluated as \[ \frac1{|H|}\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{CT}_{\tau=0}R(e^{(g^Tv)\tau}). \tag{2} \] Indeed a transformed exponent $ga$ satisfies $v\cdot ga=(g^Tv)\cdot a$, and each distinct transform is counted exactly $|H|$ times. The entire block value $2$ is unchanged if $v$ is replaced by $h^Tv$ for $h\in G$, since this merely permutes the group sum. Thus this finite algorithm retains group-invariant orbit blocks. It can require $|G|$ evaluations per representative and does not solve the requested efficient orbitwise-evaluation challenge. If a block still has an uncanceled pole, its extracted constant term can depend on the generic direction beyond its finite group orbit; only the total in $1$ is intrinsically the count.
Why an invariant direction may not exist
For the segment joining $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, the swap group interchanges its two integer points. A rational decomposition is \[ F(z_1,z_2)=\frac{z_1}{1-z_2/z_1}+\frac{z_2}{1-z_1/z_2}=z_1+z_2. \] Every swap-invariant direction has $v_1=v_2$ and is orthogonal to the denominator exponent $(1,-1)$, so it makes both rational summands undefined identically along the proposed path. A generic $v_1\ne v_2$ works. Since $1/(1-e^{c\tau})=-1/(c\tau)+1/2+O(\tau)$, the two constant terms are \[ \tfrac12-\frac{v_1}{v_2-v_1},\qquad \tfrac12+\frac{v_2}{v_2-v_1}, \] whose sum is two. This exhibits both the failure of an invariant generic direction and cancellation across an orbit.

Boundedness is essential to interpreting the output as a count. For the nonnegative integer ray, $F(z)=1/(1-z)$ has infinitely many lattice points, whereas this constant-term rule gives $1/2$ along $z=e^\tau$. That number is a regularization, not the cardinality. No regularization convention for general unbounded polyhedra is supplied by the original question.

Verification and adversarial audit

Genericity is checked for all transformed denominators, not only representatives. Stabilizer division counts distinct rational transforms exactly. The two-point example verifies the Laurent signs and shows that demanding a group-fixed direction can destroy transversality. The unbounded ray separates an algebraic constant term from a finite counting interpretation.

Scope and remaining gaps

A finite exact orbit-block procedure is proved for bounded input, but it expands over the group and provides no orbit-compression efficiency theorem. Avoiding this expansion while preserving the useful symmetry, and selecting any intended unbounded regularization, remain unresolved. The source's generic-direction method is context rather than claimed novelty.
