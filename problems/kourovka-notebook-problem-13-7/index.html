Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.7

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite. It is simple if $G\ne\{1\}$ and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $G$. For a subgroup $H\le G$, its centralizer is \[ C_G(H)=\{g\in G: gh=hg\text{ for every }h\in H\}. \] A group $G$ is linear if there exist a field $K$, an integer $n\ge1$, and an injective group homomorphism $G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(K)$.

Is it true that, for every simple locally finite group $G$, \[ \left(\exists H\le G:\ |H|<\infty\ \text{and}\ |C_G(H)|<\infty\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\exists\text{ a field }K\ \exists n\ge1:\ G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(K)\right)? \] Here the centralizer is taken in the whole group $G$. The source specifies no field or characteristic, so linearity is interpreted as existence of a faithful representation of finite dimension over some field. No infinitude or nonabelian hypothesis is stated in the supplied question; neither is imposed here.

Short English statement

Must every simple locally finite group that contains a finite subgroup with finite centralizer admit a faithful representation of finite dimension over some field? The supplied registry labels this question open; that status has not been independently verified.
