Holomorphic Symplectic Geometry — Question 7

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question does not specify its conventions for hyperkähler manifolds, deformations, or Lagrangian fibrations. The following is a provisional formulation under the compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic convention; these conventions require confirmation against the source.

A hyperkähler manifold in this convention is a compact, simply connected Kähler manifold $X$ of complex dimension $2n$, where $n\geq 1$, such that \[ H^0(X,\Omega_X^2)={\mathbb{C}}\sigma_X \] for an everywhere nondegenerate holomorphic two-form $\sigma_X$. Two such manifolds are deformation equivalent if they can be connected by a finite chain of smooth proper holomorphic families over connected complex bases, with successive manifolds occurring as fibers of a common family.

For this formulation, a holomorphic Lagrangian fibration on such a manifold $Y$ is a surjective holomorphic map $f:Y\to B$ with connected fibers, where $B$ is a normal compact complex space of dimension $n$, for which there is a dense open subset $B^\circ\subseteq B$ over which $f$ is smooth and every fiber $F$ satisfies \[ \dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}F=n,\qquad \sigma_Y|_F=0. \]

Question (Beauville, 2010, Question 7). Is it true that for every hyperkähler manifold $X$, there exists a deformation-equivalent hyperkähler manifold $Y$ admitting a holomorphic Lagrangian fibration?

The compact irreducible interpretation, the deformation-equivalence convention, and the stated conventions concerning the base and fibers are not explicit in the supplied record. Thus this precise formulation remains conditional on those interpretations. The record supplies no projectivity hypothesis on $X$ or $Y$, and no prescribed isomorphism type for $B$.

Short English statement

Beauville asks whether every hyperkähler manifold can be deformed to one admitting a Lagrangian fibration. Under the provisional compact irreducible interpretation, the general fibers have half the complex dimension of the manifold and the holomorphic symplectic form restricts to zero on them. The source record leaves several conventions unspecified.
