Open Problems on Central Simple Algebras — Problem 19

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a field and let $A$ be a finite-dimensional central simple $F$-algebra, meaning a nonzero associative unital $F$-algebra with no nontrivial two-sided ideals and with center $F$. An involution of the first kind on $A$ is an $F$-linear map $\sigma:A\to A$ satisfying \[ \sigma(ab)=\sigma(b)\sigma(a),\qquad \sigma^2=\operatorname{id}_A. \] After extending scalars to an algebraic closure of $F$, such an involution is adjoint to a nonsingular bilinear form: the adjoint relation is $b(Tu,v)=b(u,\sigma(T)v)$. The involution is called symplectic when this form is alternating, and orthogonal when it is symmetric and nonalternating. Here alternating means $b(v,v)=0$ for every vector $v$.

The involution $\sigma$ is anisotropic if \[ \forall x\in A,\qquad \sigma(x)x=0\ \Longrightarrow\ x=0. \] For a field extension $K/F$, set $A_K=A\otimes_F K$ and $\sigma_K=\sigma\otimes\operatorname{id}_K$.

Question (supplied Problem 19). For every field $F$, every central simple $F$-algebra $A$, every anisotropic orthogonal or symplectic involution $\sigma$ on $A$, and every finite field extension $K/F$ of odd degree, must $\sigma_K$ be anisotropic? Equivalently, does \[ \left(\forall x\in A,\ \sigma(x)x=0\Longrightarrow x=0\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\forall y\in A_K,\ \sigma_K(y)y=0\Longrightarrow y=0\right) \] hold whenever $[K:F]$ is odd?

Scope qualification. The supplied statement gives no characteristic restriction and no separability hypothesis on $K/F$; none is added here. Thus this rendering includes characteristic $2$ and finite inseparable extensions of odd degree where applicable. Whether the source list imposes additional ambient conventions is unresolved from the supplied record.

Short English statement

Does every anisotropic orthogonal or symplectic involution on a central simple algebra remain anisotropic over every finite field extension of odd degree? The supplied statement imposes no characteristic or separability restriction; any additional conventions in the source list require review.
