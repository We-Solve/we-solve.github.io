Wall Crossing Between Adjacent Tropical Toric Degenerations

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T$ denote the tropicalization intended in the source, equipped with a polyhedral fan structure. Let $C_+$ and $C_-$ be distinct maximal-dimensional prime cones of $T$ sharing a facet $F$. Thus, writing $d=\dim C_+=\dim C_-$, the adjacency hypothesis is \[ C_+\cap C_-=F,\qquad \dim F=d-1. \] The ambient variety, ground field, embedding, and fan structure are not specified in the supplied record.

In an initial-ideal formulation, after choosing a field $k$, an ambient polynomial or Laurent polynomial ring, a defining ideal $I$, and a convention for initial forms, a cone is called prime when its associated initial ideal $\operatorname{in}_w(I)$, constant for $w$ in the relative interior of the cone, is prime. The supplied record does not specify these choices or the precise convention for a prime cone. A toric degeneration is a flat degeneration of the original variety with a toric special fiber; the source does not specify whether toric varieties must be normal or whether the construction is affine or projective.

An affine semigroup $S$ is a finitely generated additive submonoid of a lattice, containing zero. Its semigroup algebra over $k$ is \[ k[S]=\bigoplus_{s\in S}k\chi^s, \qquad \chi^s\chi^t=\chi^{s+t}. \] When an affine toric special fiber is presented by such an algebra, its semigroup can be described through $S$. The supplied record does not identify the relevant semigroups, their lattices, or whether valuation semigroups or graded semigroups for projective constructions are intended.

Problem as supplied. Given the adjacent maximal-dimensional prime cones $C_+$ and $C_-$ and their associated toric degenerations, determine how the two degenerations are related across $F$, including their wall-crossing maps and semigroups.

Unresolved qualification. This is a request to describe a relationship, rather than a specified conjectural identity or existence theorem. Short English statement

The problem asks how the toric degenerations associated with two adjacent maximal-dimensional prime cones in a tropicalization are related across their common facet, particularly through wall-crossing maps and semigroups. The ambient setting and the maps and semigroups to be compared require specification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
