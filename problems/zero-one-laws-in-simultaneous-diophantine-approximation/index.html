Zero–One Laws in Simultaneous Diophantine Approximation

For $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$, set \[ \alpha(x)=(x_1,\ldots,x_d,f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x))\in{\mathbb{R}}^n. \] For $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$, define its distance to the nearest integer by \[ \|t\|_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|t-a|. \] The source does not specify the admissible dimension ranges, the regularity or nondegeneracy of $f$, or the hypotheses on the approximation function $\psi$.

To express the proposed statement, provisionally interpret denominators as positive integers and take $\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\to[0,\infty)$. These conventions require confirmation against the source; they are not complete hypotheses for the claimed law. For each integer $h\geq1$, define \[ A(h)=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\ \exists(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\in\{1,\ldots,h\}^n\ \forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad \|q_j\alpha_j(x)\|_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq\psi(h)\right\}. \] Thus each coordinate has an independently chosen denominator; the denominators need not be distinct. Define \[ A^*=\limsup_{h\to\infty}A(h) =\bigcap_{H=1}^{\infty}\ \bigcup_{h\geq H}A(h). \] The recorded question asks whether, under suitable nondegeneracy assumptions on $f$ and any necessary additional hypotheses, these sets are Lebesgue measurable and satisfy \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{n-1}\psi(h)<\infty &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_d(A^*)=0,\\ \sum_{h=1}^{\infty}h^{n-1}\psi(h)=\infty &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda_d({\mathbb{R}}^d\setminus A^*)=0, \end{aligned} \] where $\lambda_d$ denotes $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Full measure here means that the complement in the parameter space ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ has measure zero. The series is reproduced exactly as supplied. Short English statement

The problem asks for a measure-zero or full-measure criterion for points on the graph of $f$ whose coordinates admit simultaneous approximation with separately chosen bounded denominators. The proposed criterion is convergence or divergence of $\sum_{h\geq1}h^{n-1}\psi(h)$. Essential assumptions on $f$ and $\psi$ are missing. A rational-height formulation is also supplied as a possible equivalent version, with unresolved representation and norm conventions.
