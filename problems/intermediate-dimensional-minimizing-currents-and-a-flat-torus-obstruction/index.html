Math-1638

Intermediate-dimensional minimizing currents and a flat-torus obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M^n,g)$ be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. A real $q$-current is a continuous linear functional on smooth $q$-forms. It is closed if $T(d\eta)=0$ for every smooth $(q-1)$-form $\eta$; closed currents, rather than arbitrary currents, determine homology classes. Use pointwise comass \[ \|\omega_x\|_{\mathrm{comass}}= \sup\{|\omega_x(v_1,\ldots,v_q)|:v_1,\ldots,v_q\text{ orthonormal}\}, \] and mass $\mathbf M(T)=\sup\{T(\omega):\sup_x\|\omega_x\|_{\mathrm{comass}}\le1\}$. A closed finite-mass current is normal. Minimizing mass among real normal representatives of $h\in H_q(M;{\mathbb{R}})$ defines its stable norm. Minimizing among integral rectifiable currents is a different admissible class and requires integral homology data.

Victor Bangert's question asks for the structure of homologically mass-minimizing currents in intermediate degrees $1<q<n-1$. The source contrasts geodesic descriptions in degree one and measured minimizing hypersurface laminations in codimension one, possibly with singularities. Its intermediate-dimensional question is open ended, with no one proposed regularity theorem or explicit coefficient category. The comass convention and real-versus-integral distinction are essential.

The aid's flat-torus result works on the unit-volume $T^n={\mathbb{R}}^n/{\mathbb{Z}}^n$. For a constant $q$-vector $v$, define \[ T_v(\omega)=\int_{T^n}\omega_x(v)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] It is a closed real normal current. If $|v|_{\mathrm{mass}}$ denotes the algebraic mass norm dual to comass, the aid asserts \[ \mathbf M(T_v)=|v|_{\mathrm{mass}}=\|[T_v]\|_{\mathrm{stable}}. \] A constant closed calibrating form of comass one attaining the dual norm proves minimality; the norm is not silently replaced by the Euclidean norm on all $q$-vectors.

For every $n\ge4$ and $2\le q\le n-2$, let \[ v=e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{q-2}\wedge (e_{q-1}\wedge e_q+e_{q+1}\wedge e_{q+2}), \] where the initial wedge is one when $q=2$. The constant form \[ \theta=dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q-2}\wedge (dx_{q-1}\wedge dx_q+dx_{q+1}\wedge dx_{q+2}) \] has comass one and $\theta(v)=2$. Thus $T_v$ has mass two and minimizes its integral homology class viewed over ${\mathbb{R}}$. Its mass distribution is diffuse with respect to $n$-dimensional volume, so it is not a rectifiable $q$-current. More strongly, its nondecomposable polar $q$-vector prevents it from being the current of one oriented measured $q$-lamination with one tangent $q$-plane almost everywhere. This does not forbid a sum of currents from different laminations.

The same integral class has an integral calibrated minimizer: the sum of the coordinate $q$-tori in directions $(e_1,\ldots,e_q)$ and $(e_1,\ldots,e_{q-2},e_{q+1},e_{q+2})$, each with positive orientation. Their intersection has lower dimension, both are calibrated by $\theta$, and the sum has mass two. Thus a class admitting an integral minimizer can simultaneously admit a diffuse nonlaminar real minimizer. The obstruction is to extending the endpoint lamination picture to all real minimizers, not a general impossibility of rectifiable or integral minimizing representatives.

Short English statement

The intermediate-dimensional structure depends on the current category and the comass mass norm. The aid gives, in every intermediate degree, a flat-torus integral class with both a mass-two integral minimizer and a mass-two diffuse real minimizer with nondecomposable tangent polar, obstructing a universal lamination description.
