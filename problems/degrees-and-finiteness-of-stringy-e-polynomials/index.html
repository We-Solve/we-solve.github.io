Math-3454

Degrees and Finiteness of Stringy $E$-Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a lattice of rank $d$, and let $P\subset M\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}$ be a full-dimensional lattice polytope, meaning the convex hull of finitely many lattice points with $\dim P=d$. Lower-dimensional polytopes are understood in their affine lattice. Write $M^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})$. A lattice polytope $Q$ containing the origin in its interior is reflexive if its polar \[ Q^{\vee}=\{y\in M^{\vee}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}:\langle y,x\rangle\geq -1\text{ for every }x\in Q\} \] is a lattice polytope. A Gorenstein polytope of index $r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$ is a lattice polytope $P$ such that $rP$ has a unique interior lattice point $m$ and $rP-m$ is reflexive. Set \[ n(P)=\dim(P)+1-2r. \] The supplied record denotes the stringy $E$-function of $P$ by $E_{\mathrm{st}}(P,t)$, but supplies neither its defining formula nor the convention relating this single-variable notation to any multivariable invariant. We retain this notation without selecting a specialization or normalization. For a nonzero polynomial $f(t)=\sum_i a_i t^i$, its ordinary degree is $\deg_t f=\max\{i:a_i\neq0\}$.

The source asks the following two questions for arbitrary Gorenstein polytopes:

  1. For every Gorenstein polytope $P$ of index $r$, does its stringy $E$-function have the expected degree \[ \deg_t E_{\mathrm{st}}(P,t)\stackrel{?}{=}\dim(P)+1-2r? \]
  2. For each fixed integer $n$, are there only finitely many stringy $E$-polynomials in the family \[ \left\{E_{\mathrm{st}}(P,t): P\text{ is a Gorenstein polytope of index }r,\ \dim(P)+1-2r=n\right\} \] up to multiples?
Here dimension and index may vary while $n$ remains fixed. The phrase "up to multiples" is retained from the source: the permitted multipliers and the resulting equivalence relation are unspecified. The record also gives no restriction to nonnegative $n$, no treatment of an identically zero $E$-polynomial, and no degree convention for that case. Consequently, the displayed questions preserve the source formulation but require these qualifications to be resolved before becoming fully specified mathematical assertions.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether every Gorenstein polytope has a stringy $E$-polynomial of degree $\dim(P)+1-2r$, and whether fixing this value leaves only finitely many such polynomials up to multiples. The invariant's variable convention, the meaning of multiples, and the treatment of zero polynomials and negative expected degrees remain unspecified. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that label.
