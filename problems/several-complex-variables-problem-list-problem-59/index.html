Several Complex Variables Problem List — Problem 59

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a Stein manifold of complex dimension $n$, and let $T^{1,0}M$ denote its holomorphic tangent bundle. Say that $M$ is holomorphically parallelizable if \[ T^{1,0}M\cong M\times{\mathbb{C}}^n \] as holomorphic vector bundles. A holomorphic map $F:M\to{\mathbb{C}}^n$ is an immersion if its differential \[ {\,\mathrm{d}} F_p:T^{1,0}_pM\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^n \] is injective for every $p\in M$. Since both spaces have dimension $n$, this means that ${\,\mathrm{d}} F_p$ is an isomorphism at every point; global injectivity of $F$ is not required.

Under the holomorphic interpretation of parallelizability, the question is: does every holomorphically parallelizable Stein manifold $M$ of complex dimension $n$ admit a holomorphic immersion into ${\mathbb{C}}^n$? Equivalently, must there exist holomorphic functions $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ on $M$ such that \[ {\,\mathrm{d}} f_1\wedge\cdots\wedge{\,\mathrm{d}} f_n\ne0 \quad\text{at every point of }M? \]

Unresolved qualification. The source says only "parallelizable" and does not specify the category. If smooth real parallelizability is intended, the hypothesis instead means that the underlying real tangent bundle satisfies $T_{{\mathbb{R}}}M\cong M\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}$ as smooth real vector bundles, with the same requested holomorphic immersion. These hypotheses must not be silently identified. Dimension is interpreted here as complex dimension, as suggested by the Stein setting and target ${\mathbb{C}}^n$.

The supplied source states that the answer is affirmative for $n=1$, attributing this to Gunning and Narasimhan, and that such an $M$ admits an immersion into ${\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}$, attributing this to Eliashberg--Gromov. These are retained as contextual claims of the source, with the same unresolved meaning of "parallelizable"; they have not been independently verified here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every parallelizable Stein manifold of complex dimension $n$ admits a holomorphic map into ${\mathbb{C}}^n$ whose differential is invertible everywhere. The source does not specify whether parallelizability is holomorphic or smooth real. Its positive statements for dimension one and for target ${\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}$ are supplied background claims.
