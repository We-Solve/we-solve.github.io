Kourovka Notebook Problem 12.64

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For integers $k\geq 2$ and $n\geq 1$, define the following assertion $P(k,n)$. For a finite group $G$, let $1_G$ denote its identity and let \[ \exp(G)=\operatorname{lcm}\{\operatorname{ord}(g):g\in G\}. \] Then $P(k,n)$ is the statement \[ \begin{gathered} \exists N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\ \forall\text{ finite groups }G\ \forall(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in G^k,\\ \left[\ G=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_k\rangle\ \land\ \left(\forall(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in (\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}\cup\{1_G\})^N,\ (x_1\cdots x_N)^n=1_G\right)\ \right] \Longrightarrow \exp(G)\leq n. \end{gathered} \] Here $N=N(k,n)$ must be independent of the finite group and its generating family. The factors in the tested products are generators or the identity; inverses are not separately included. Identity factors allow every product of at most $N$ generators to be tested. No minimality requirement on the generating family is stated.

The source asks whether $P(k,n)$ holds for every $k\geq 2$ and every "(prime) number" $n$. This wording leaves unresolved whether the intended range is all positive integers $n$, only primes, or both versions of the question. The displayed assertion defines the question for each positive integer $n$ without choosing between those ranges. The conclusion preserves the numerical inequality $\exp(G)\leq n$ exactly as supplied. It has not been replaced by $\exp(G)\mid n$ or by $g^n=1_G$ for every $g\in G$, which are generally different conditions.

For the supplied contextual remarks, let $F_k$ be the free group on $k$ generators and define the free Burnside group \[ B(k,n)=F_k/\langle\!\langle w^n:w\in F_k\rangle\!\rangle, \] where the denominator is the normal subgroup generated by all $n$-th powers. The source states that, for fixed $k,n$, a negative answer implies, for example, that $B(k,n)$ is infinite. It also states that a positive answer, for sufficiently large $n$, gives an opportunity to find a hyperbolic group $H$ that is not residually finite and that has a hyperbolic subgroup $K$ of finite index with no proper subgroup of finite index. The latter subgroup condition means \[ [H:K]<\infty,\qquad \forall L\leq K,\quad [K:L]<\infty\Longrightarrow L=K. \] A group is residually finite if every nonidentity element has nonidentity image under some homomorphism to a finite group. A hyperbolic group means a finitely generated group with a Cayley graph whose geodesic triangles are uniformly thin. These contextual implications are reported as supplied source claims, not established here; the source gives neither a threshold for "sufficiently large" nor precise hypotheses for the stated construction opportunity.

Short English statement

For each fixed number $k\geq 2$ of generators and each allowed $n$, must there be a uniform length $N(k,n)$ such that testing $n$-th powers of all products of at most $N(k,n)$ generators forces every finite group under consideration to have exponent at most $n$? The intended restriction of $n$ to primes and the meaning of the exponent bound require review. The supplied record labels the problem open; that label is not independently verified.
