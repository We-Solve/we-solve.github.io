Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.66

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A variety of groups is a class of groups defined by identities in the language of groups. For a variety $\Theta$ and a positive integer $r$, let $F_\Theta(r)$ denote the relatively free group in $\Theta$ on $r$ generators. Groups $G$ and $H$ are elementarily equivalent, written $G\equiv H$, if they satisfy exactly the same first-order sentences in the language of groups.

The variety $\Theta$ is of Tarski type if \[ \forall r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\qquad \left(F_\Theta(r)\text{ and }F_\Theta(s)\text{ are non-abelian}\right) \Longrightarrow F_\Theta(r)\equiv F_\Theta(s). \] The non-abelian restriction is part of the definition. The source does not require that any such non-abelian relatively free groups exist; whether examples with this additional non-vacuity condition are intended is unresolved.

For a positive integer $n$, define the Burnside variety \[ B_n=\{G: \forall x\in G,\ x^n=1\}. \] Here the identity requires every element order to divide $n$; it does not require the least exponent of every group to equal $n$. Using the convention $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$, put \[ e_1(x,y)=[x,y],\qquad e_{k+1}(x,y)=[e_k(x,y),y]\quad(k\geq 1). \] The $n$-Engel variety is \[ E_n=\{G: \forall x,y\in G,\ e_n(x,y)=1\}. \] Thus the Engel word is left-normed, with $n$ occurrences of $y$. This makes explicit the conventional reading of the source's compressed displayed identity.

  1. (a) Find examples of varieties $\Theta$ of Tarski type distinct from the variety of all groups.
  2. (b) Is $B_n$ of Tarski type for sufficiently large $n$? Under the usual eventual interpretation, this asks whether \[ \exists N_B\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\qquad n\geq N_B\Longrightarrow B_n\text{ is of Tarski type}. \]
  3. (c) Is $E_n$ of Tarski type for sufficiently large $n$? Under the same interpretation, this asks whether \[ \exists N_E\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\qquad n\geq N_E\Longrightarrow E_n\text{ is of Tarski type}. \]
In (b) and (c), the source says only that $n$ is "big enough." The displayed eventual quantifiers state a conventional interpretation, not a verified clarification from the proposers; no restriction to a special class of integers is supplied.

Short English statement

A variety is of Tarski type when all its non-abelian relatively free groups of finite rank have the same first-order theory. The problem asks for examples other than the variety of all groups, and asks whether the Burnside and Engel varieties have this property for sufficiently large parameters. The intended non-vacuity requirement and the precise scope of "sufficiently large" remain unclarified.
