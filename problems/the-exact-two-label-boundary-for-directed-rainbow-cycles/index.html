Math-254

The exact two-label boundary for directed rainbow cycles

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Together with the preceding local record, this fragment states Conjecture 6.8 under the hypotheses of Seymour--Sullivan Conjecture 6.1; the printed initial "Conjecture 6.1" is the continuation of that sentence.

Let $V$ be finite with $n\geq1$ vertices, let $k\geq1$, and let $E_1,\ldots,E_k\subseteq\Omega=\{(u,v)\in V^2:u\ne v\}$. Layer overlap is allowed: an arc has label set $S_{uv}=\{i:(u,v)\in E_i\}$, and $G_i=(V,E_i)$. A rainbow directed cycle admits one distinct chosen label on each of its arcs; no numerical ordering of those labels is imposed. Opposite arcs are allowed in the host, so a rainbow digon is a valid cycle. The conjecture asks that for all nonnegative integers $r_i$, \[ \left[\delta^+(G_i)\geq r_i\text{ for all }i,\quad \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^k r_i\geq n\right] \ \Longrightarrow\ \text{there is a rainbow directed cycle}. \] Equivalently the threshold is $\sum_i\delta^+(G_i)\geq n$. If loops are permitted in another convention, any labeled loop is already a rainbow cycle; the nontrivial case is the loopless one above.

The aid proves the following pairwise special case: for distinct labels $i,j$, if $\delta_i+\delta_j\geq n$, where $\delta_i=\delta^+(G_i)$, then a rainbow digon exists in the loopless case. It reports an exact boundary classification for two layers without a rainbow digon and with $\delta_1+\delta_2=n-1$. Writing $F^R=\{(v,u):(u,v)\in F\}$, those pairs are precisely \[ E_1=F,\qquad E_2=\Omega\setminus F^R, \] where $F$ is a diregular digraph, meaning every vertex has the same indegree and outdegree $r$. The second layer is then diregular of degree $n-1-r$. Conversely every such $F$ yields a two-label obstruction at total $n-1$. This establishes the two-label threshold; the one-label loopless threshold is impossible since outdegree is at most $n-1$.

For $k\geq2$, the aid's additional incidence bound is \[ \sum_{i=1}^k|E_i|\leq k\binom n2 \] when there is no labeled loop or rainbow digon. The restriction $k\geq2$ matters in this overlapping directed model: with only one label, both directions of every pair may occur without a rainbow digon. Thus the summary's unqualified general-incidence wording is not extended to that exceptional palette. Pairwise obstructions do not prove the conjecture for arbitrary numbers of labels.

Short English statement

The recovered conjecture forces a rainbow directed cycle when minimum layer outdegrees sum to the vertex count. The supplied aid settles the two-label threshold and classifies its sharp boundary, while larger palettes remain outside that special case.
