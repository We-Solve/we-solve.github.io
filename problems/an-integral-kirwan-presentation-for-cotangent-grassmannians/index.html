Math-1481

An integral Kirwan presentation for cotangent Grassmannians

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Hausel's Conjecture 6.1 asks for surjectivity of the hyperkähler Kirwan map \[ \kappa:H_G^*(T^*A)\cong H^*(BG)\longrightarrow H^*(T^*A\mathbin{///}_{\xi}G). \] In a smooth finite-dimensional version, $G$ is compact, $T^*A$ has a tri-Hamiltonian action with moment map $\mu$, $\xi$ is an invariant regular value, and $G$ acts freely on $\mu^{-1}(\xi)$; the quotient is $\mu^{-1}(\xi)/G$. Restriction followed by the free-action identification defines $\kappa$. The displayed $H^*(BG)$ identification requires contractibility of $T^*A$, as in a linear or affine model. The extract omits the definition of $A$, coefficient ring, and precise regularity, isotropy and possibly gauge-theoretic hypotheses, so this version is explicit rather than silently universal.

The supplied aid treats an integral special case. For integers $1\leq k\leq n$, take $A=\operatorname{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^k,{\mathbb{C}}^n)$, pairs $(I,J)$ in $T^*A$, and the action $(I,J)\mapsto(Ig^{-1},gJ)$ of $U(k)$. Choose moment-map sign and scale so the positive real level is \[ I^*I-JJ^*=\zeta\operatorname{Id}_k,\qquad JI=0,\qquad\zeta>0. \] The aid identifies its quotient with $T^*\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$. Since this retracts onto the Grassmannian, the integral Kirwan map is, with the tautological-subbundle convention, \[ {\mathbb{Z}}[c_1,\ldots,c_k]\longrightarrow H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n);{\mathbb{Z}}), \qquad c_i\longmapsto c_i(S),\quad |c_i|=2i. \] Define $b_r$ by \[ \sum_{r\geq0}b_rt^r=(1+c_1t+\cdots+c_kt^k)^{-1}. \] The reported kernel is $(b_{n-k+1},\ldots,b_n)$. Schubert Giambelli polynomials give an additive section: for a partition $\lambda$ inside the $k\times(n-k)$ rectangle, lift its Schubert class to $\det(b_{\lambda_i-i+j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}$, with $b_0=1$ and $b_r=0$ for $r<0$, using the corresponding Schubert convention. This is an additive, not a ring, splitting. The first nonzero kernel occurs in cohomological degree $2(n-k+1)$, where it is the rank-one group generated by $b_{n-k+1}$. This special-case presentation does not prove the full conjecture.

The historical Comment 6.2 reports results for $M^1_{\mathrm{Dol}}(\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))$ and $M^d_{\mathrm{Dol}}(\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))$, a quiver-variety conjecture, and proved hyperpolygon and hypertoric cases, with the references below. Their stability, degree and smoothness hypotheses are not present in the extract, so the historical list is not a blanket theorem for arbitrary parameters.

The appended integration section calls an oriented circle manifold circle-compact when $M^{U(1)}$ is compact, and defines \[ \widehat H_{U(1)}^*(M)=H_{U(1)}^*(M;{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}[u]}{\mathbb{Q}}(u),\qquad |u|=2, \] \[ \int_M\alpha:=\sum_{F\subset M^{U(1)}}\int_F \frac{i_F^*\alpha}{e_{U(1)}(N_F)}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(u). \] The sum is over fixed components, with compatible orientations; nonzero normal circle weights make the Euler classes invertible after localization. This is a localized definition, not ordinary integration of arbitrary forms over noncompact $M$. The source says fiber rotation induces circle-compact actions on its examples; descent at a general moment-map value needs checking, since fiber rotation scales the complex moment map. Wu mentions Prato--Wu's tempered-distribution interpretation, and Hausel asks for a formulation using equivariant differential forms. That additional question is not answered by the Grassmannian calculation.

Short English statement

The source conjectures hyperkähler Kirwan surjectivity. The supplied aid gives an integral cotangent-Grassmannian presentation, its exact inverse-Chern kernel and an additive Schubert splitting. The appended localized integration definition and differential-form question are separate and retain their coefficient and compact-fixed-set qualifications.
