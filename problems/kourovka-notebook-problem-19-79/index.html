Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.79

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be an abstract group. For each integer $n\geq 1$, a complex linear representation of dimension $n$ is a group homomorphism $\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$. It is irreducible if the only $\rho(G)$-invariant complex linear subspaces of ${\mathbb{C}}^n$ are $\{0\}$ and ${\mathbb{C}}^n$. Two such representations $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are isomorphic if there exists $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$ such that $\sigma(g)=A\rho(g)A^{-1}$ for every $g\in G$.

Suppose that, for every integer $n\geq 1$, there exists exactly one isomorphism class of irreducible complex linear representations of $G$ of dimension $n$. Must there exist a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ such that \[ G/N\cong\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}}), \qquad \operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})=\{M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times2}({\mathbb{Q}}):\det M=1\}? \] The supplied source states that $G=\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})$ satisfies the representation hypothesis, citing a theorem of Borel--Tits. This example and its cited justification are retained as supplied source claims and are not independently verified here. The record names J.-P. Serre as proposer and prefixes the statement with "(T. Springer)"; the relationship between these attributions is not explained.

Short English statement

If an abstract group has exactly one irreducible complex representation, up to isomorphism, in every positive finite dimension, must it have a quotient isomorphic to $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})$? The supplied registry labels this problem open; that label has not been independently verified.
