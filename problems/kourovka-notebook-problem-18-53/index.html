Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is locally finite if every finitely generated subgroup of $G$ is finite. It is simple if $G\neq\{1\}$ and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $G$. We use the standard convention that $G$ is linear if there exist a field $K$, an integer $n\geq 1$, and an injective homomorphism $G\to\operatorname{GL}_n(K)$; non-linear means that no such choices exist. The supplied record does not explicitly define linearity, so this convention requires review against the original source.

For $g\in G$, define its centralizer by \[ C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}. \] For a group $H$, put $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(j+1)}=[H^{(j)},H^{(j)}]$, where $[A,A]$ is the subgroup generated by commutators of elements of $A$. A group $H$ is soluble if $H^{(d)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $d\geq 0$. A group is almost soluble if it has a soluble subgroup of finite index.

Problem (catalogue entry). Does there exist a non-linear simple locally finite group $G$ such that \[ \forall g\in G\setminus\{1\}\quad \exists H_g\leq C_G(g)\quad \exists d_g\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}\quad [C_G(g):H_g]<\infty \quad\text{and}\quad H_g^{(d_g)}=\{1\}? \] Neither the finite index nor the derived length is required to have a bound independent of $g$. The supplied registry labels this problem open; that label is supplied source metadata, not an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

Does a non-linear simple locally finite group exist in which the centralizer of every nonidentity element has a soluble subgroup of finite index? Both that index and the subgroup's derived length may depend on the element.
