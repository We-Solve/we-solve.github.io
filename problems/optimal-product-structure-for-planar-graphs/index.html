Math-7497

Optimal Product Structure for Planar Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The following is a precise formulation under the convention that all graphs are finite, simple and undirected. This convention is not explicit in the supplied record, so its agreement with the intended source domain requires review.

A graph is planar if it admits a drawing in the plane in which edges intersect only at common endpoints. A path is a graph with vertices $p_1,\ldots,p_m$, where $m\geq 1$, and edges exactly $p_ip_{i+1}$ for $1\leq i<m$.

For graphs $H$ and $P$, their strong product $H\boxtimes P$ has vertex set $V(H)\times V(P)$. Distinct vertices $(h,p)$ and $(h',p')$ are adjacent precisely when \[ \left(h=h'\text{ or }hh'\in E(H)\right) \quad\text{and}\quad \left(p=p'\text{ or }pp'\in E(P)\right). \] Saying that $G$ is a subgraph of $H\boxtimes P$ means that there is an injective map $\varphi:V(G)\to V(H)\times V(P)$ such that every edge of $G$ maps to an edge of the product. Nonedges need not be preserved.

A tree decomposition of a nonempty graph $H$ consists of a finite nonempty tree $T$ and bags $B_t\subseteq V(H)$, indexed by $t\in V(T)$, such that the bags cover $V(H)$, the endpoints of every edge of $H$ lie together in some bag, and for each vertex $v\in V(H)$, the set $\{t:v\in B_t\}$ induces a connected subtree of $T$. Its width is $\max_{t\in V(T)}|B_t|-1$, and $\operatorname{tw}(H)$ is the minimum width of a tree decomposition of $H$.

For each integer $k\geq 0$, let $\mathcal A(k)$ be the assertion \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every finite simple undirected planar graph }G,\\ \text{there exist a finite nonempty simple undirected graph }H \text{ and a finite nonempty path }P\\ \text{such that }\operatorname{tw}(H)\leq k \text{ and }G\text{ is isomorphic to a subgraph of }H\boxtimes P. \end{gathered} \] Both $H$ and $P$ may depend on $G$; the integer $k$ must be uniform over all such $G$.

The problem is to determine \[ k_{\mathrm{planar}}=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:k\geq 0\text{ and }\mathcal A(k)\}. \] The supplied source describes the bounds $3\leq k_{\mathrm{planar}}\leq 6$ and asks to close this gap. Thus, under the stated graph convention, the target is to determine which of $3,4,5,6$ is the optimum. These bounds and the claim that the gap remains open are supplied source claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft. The original excerpt refers to an upper bound in its Theorem 3, whose statement is not included in the record.

Short English statement

Determine the least uniform treewidth bound $k$ such that every planar graph embeds as a subgraph of the strong product of a graph of treewidth at most $k$ and one path. The factors may depend on the planar graph. The supplied record reports a gap between $3$ and $6$. The finite simple undirected interpretation used here requires source confirmation.
