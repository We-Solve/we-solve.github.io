Statement

Fix integers $n\ge 1$ and $k\ge 2$.

A $k$-sunflower (also called a $\Delta$-system) is a collection of $k$ sets $S_1,\dots,S_k$ such that there exists a set $C$ (the core) with \[ S_i\cap S_j = C\quad\text{for all }i\ne j. \] Equivalently, the sets $P_i:=S_i\setminus C$ are pairwise disjoint.

Let $f(n,k)$ be the smallest integer with the following property:

Every family $\mathcal F$ of sets, each of size exactly $n$ (i.e., $n$-uniform), with \[ |\mathcal F|\ge f(n,k) \] contains a $k$-sunflower.

Question: For each fixed $k$, does there exist a constant $c_k>0$ such that for all $n$, \[ f(n,k) < c_k^n\ ? \]

Problem 20 (Sunflowers): Round 3 --- extremal structure closure for the $n=2$ slice

1) ROUND-3 OBJECTIVE

Path (A) inside the nontrivial special slice already opened in Round 2.

Round 2 determined the exact value of $f(2,k)$ for every $k\ge 2$, but it stopped at the level of the numerical extremal value. The remaining structural gap in that slice is to determine all extremal $2$-uniform families of size $f(2,k)-1$.

In this round I close that gap completely: I prove a full extremal-structure theorem for the entire $n=2$ slice. In particular:

  • for odd $k$, the extremal family is unique up to relabeling;
  • for even $k>2$, I characterize all extremal families by their degree sequence on the support, and I exhibit two explicit nonisomorphic extremal constructions.
This is the most promising continuation because it strictly strengthens Round 2's exact formula into a complete classification theorem for the same infinite family of cases.

2) Round-2 FOUNDATION USED

I use only the following Round 2 results.
  • Round 2 Lemma 20.4: for $2$-sets, a $k$-sunflower is equivalent to either a matching of size $k$ or a star $K_{1,k}$.
  • Round 2 Theorem 20.5: for every $k\ge 2$, \[ f(2,k)= \begin{cases} k(k-1)+1, & k\ \text{odd},\\[3pt] \left\lfloor \dfrac{(k-1)(2k-1)}{2}\right\rfloor+1, & k\ \text{even}. \end{cases} \] Equivalently, if \[ M(k):=\max\{|E(G)|:\Delta(G)\le k-1,\ \nu(G)\le k-1\}, \] then $f(2,k)=M(k)+1$.
  • Round 2 explicit constructions: the odd case $K_k\cup K_k$, and the even-case circulant construction on $2k-1$ vertices.
I do not reprove these facts.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-3)

New tool: the equality characterization in the Abbott--Hanson--Sauer theorem.

Round 2 used only the numerical upper bound from Abbott--Hanson--Sauer. The new ingredient here is the extremal-case classification (as stated in Theorem 3.2 of [KupavskiiNoskov2025]): once equality holds, the extremal graph is either

  • exactly $K_k\cup K_k$ when $k$ is odd, or
  • when $k$ is even and $k>2$, a graph on $2k-1$ vertices with one vertex of degree $k-2$ and all other vertices of degree $k-1$.
A second new point is the support reduction: for a $2$-uniform family $\mathcal F$, viewing its support graph on $\bigcup\mathcal F$ automatically removes isolated vertices, which is exactly the hypothesis needed to apply the equality case.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-3)

Gap left by Round 2. Round 2 computed the exact number $f(2,k)$, but it did not determine whether the extremal family is unique, nor did it characterize all equality cases.

Claims proved in this round.

  1. Every extremal $k$-sunflower-free family of $2$-sets is completely classified.
  2. For odd $k$, the extremal family is unique up to isomorphism.
  3. For even $k>2$, extremal families are not unique; indeed there are at least two explicit nonisomorphic extremal families for every even $k\ge 4$.

Why this overcomes the previous obstacle. The only missing ingredient after Round 2 was structural, not numerical. Equality in Theorem 3.2 of [KupavskiiNoskov2025] supplies exactly the missing structure.

5) WORK (ROUND-3)

Setup.
Let $\mathcal F$ be a family of $2$-element sets, and let $G$ be its support graph: \[ V(G)=\bigcup\mathcal F, \qquad E(G)=\mathcal F. \] Then $G$ has no isolated vertices by definition.

By Round 2 Lemma 20.4, $\mathcal F$ is $k$-sunflower-free if and only if \[ \Delta(G)\le k-1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \nu(G)\le k-1. \] If moreover $|\mathcal F|=f(2,k)-1$, then Round 2 Theorem 20.5 gives \[ |E(G)|=M(k). \] So extremal $2$-uniform sunflower-free families are exactly the edge sets of graphs achieving equality in the graph extremal problem defining $M(k)$.

Theorem 20.7 (Abbott--Hanson--Sauer with equality structure).
Let $s\ge 2$. Let $G$ be a graph with no isolated vertices, no matching of size $s$, and no vertex of degree $s$. Then \[ |E(G)|\le \begin{cases} s(s-1), & s\ \text{odd},\\[3pt] (s-1)^2+\dfrac{s-2}{2}, & s\ \text{even}. \end{cases} \] Moreover, if equality holds, then:
  1. if $s$ is odd, $G\cong K_s\cup K_s$;
  2. if $s$ is even and $s>2$, then $|V(G)|=2s-1$ and the degree multiset is \[ \{s-2,(s-1)^{(2s-2)}\}; \]
  3. if $s=2$, then $G\cong K_2$.
This is exactly Theorem 3.2 of [KupavskiiNoskov2025], where the equality characterization is stated under the standing assumption that $G$ has no isolated vertices.
Theorem 20.8 (Full extremal classification for $f(2,k)$).
Let $k\ge 2$. Let $\mathcal F$ be a family of $2$-sets with no $k$-sunflower and with \[ |\mathcal F|=f(2,k)-1. \] Let $G$ be its support graph. Then exactly one of the following holds.
  1. If $k=2$, then $G\cong K_2$.
  2. If $k$ is odd, then \[ G\cong K_k\cup K_k. \] In particular $|V(G)|=2k$, and the extremal family is unique up to relabeling.
  3. If $k$ is even and $k>2$, then \[ |V(G)|=2k-1, \qquad \deg_G(v)=k-1\ \text{for }2k-2\text{ vertices}, \qquad \deg_G(u)=k-2\ \text{for one vertex }u. \] Conversely, every graph with this degree multiset is $k$-sunflower-free and has exactly $f(2,k)-1$ edges.

Proof. Because $\mathcal F$ is $k$-sunflower-free, Round 2 Lemma 20.4 gives \[ \Delta(G)\le k-1, \qquad \nu(G)\le k-1. \] Because $|\mathcal F|=f(2,k)-1=M(k)$, the graph $G$ attains equality in the extremal problem for $M(k)$. Since $G$ has no isolated vertices (it is the support graph), Theorem 20.7 applies with $s=k$.

If $k=2$, Theorem 20.7 gives $G\cong K_2$.

If $k$ is odd, Theorem 20.7 gives $G\cong K_k\cup K_k$.

If $k$ is even and $k>2$, Theorem 20.7 gives that $G$ has $2k-1$ vertices and degree multiset \[ \{k-2,(k-1)^{(2k-2)}\}. \] This proves the forward implication.

For the converse in the even case, let $G$ be any graph on $2k-1$ vertices with one vertex of degree $k-2$ and all other vertices of degree $k-1$. Then $\Delta(G)=k-1$, and also $\nu(G)\le k-1$ because $|V(G)|=2k-1<2k$. Therefore, by Round 2 Lemma 20.4, $E(G)$ is $k$-sunflower-free.

Finally, by the handshake lemma, \[ |E(G)|=\frac{(k-2)+(2k-2)(k-1)}{2} =(k-1)^2+\frac{k-2}{2} =\left\lfloor \frac{(k-1)(2k-1)}{2}\right\rfloor =f(2,k)-1, \] where the last equality is Round 2 Theorem 20.5. Hence every such graph is extremal. $\square$

Corollary 20.9 (Parity dichotomy on uniqueness).
For the $n=2$ slice, the extremal family of size $f(2,k)-1$ is unique up to isomorphism when $k$ is odd, and nonunique when $k$ is even and greater than $2$.

Proof. The odd case is immediate from Theorem 20.8. To show nonuniqueness for even $k>2$, it suffices to give two nonisomorphic extremal constructions. This is done in Proposition 20.10 below. $\square$

Proposition 20.10 (Two explicit nonisomorphic extremal families for each even $k\ge 4$).
Fix an even integer $k\ge 4$. Then there exist at least two nonisomorphic extremal $k$-sunflower-free families of $2$-sets.

Proof. We describe two support graphs.

Construction A (circulant type). Take the even-case graph from Round 2: on $\mathbb Z/(2k-1)\mathbb Z$, let $H$ be the circulant graph with step set \[ \{\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,\pm (k/2-1)\}, \] and let $C$ be the cycle consisting of the step-$k/2$ edges. Add to $H$ a maximum matching $M$ of $C$ leaving one vertex, say $0$, uncovered. Call the resulting graph $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_k$.

Round 2 already proved that $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_k$ is extremal.

Construction B (bipartite type). Let $A=\{a_1,\dots,a_{k-1}\}$ and $B=\{b_1,\dots,b_{k-1}\}$, and start with the complete bipartite graph $K_{k-1,k-1}$ on $A\sqcup B$. Let \[ M_0=\{a_i b_i:1\le i\le (k-2)/2\} \] be a matching of size $(k-2)/2$. Delete the edges of $M_0$. Then add one new vertex $u$ and join $u$ to all $2\cdot (k-2)/2=k-2$ endpoints of the deleted matching. Call the resulting graph $G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k$.

We now verify that $G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k$ is extremal. The new vertex $u$ has degree $k-2$. Every endpoint of a deleted matching edge loses one edge in $K_{k-1,k-1}$ and gains the edge to $u$, so its degree is again $k-1$. Every other old vertex still has degree $k-1$. Thus $G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k$ has degree multiset \[ \{k-2,(k-1)^{(2k-2)}\}. \] By Theorem 20.8, it is extremal.

It remains to prove that the two extremal graphs are not isomorphic. In each graph there is a unique vertex of degree $k-2$; call it $u_{\mathrm{circ}}$ in $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_k$ and $u_{\mathrm{bip}}$ in $G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k$.

Now, \[ G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k-u_{\mathrm{bip}}=K_{k-1,k-1}-M_0, \] which is bipartite.

On the other hand, $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_k-u_{\mathrm{circ}}$ is not bipartite. Indeed:

  • if $k\ge 6$, then the vertices $1,2,3$ form a triangle, because the differences $1$ and $2$ belong to the step set of $H$;
  • if $k=4$, then $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_4-u_{\mathrm{circ}}$ contains the triangle $\{2,3,4\}$: the edges $23$ and $34$ lie on the base $7$-cycle, while $24$ is one of the added matching edges.
So one deletion graph is bipartite and the other is not; hence $G^{\mathrm{circ}}_k\not\cong G^{\mathrm{bip}}_k$.

Therefore there are at least two nonisomorphic extremal families for every even $k\ge 4$. $\square$

Consequence for the global problem.
Round 2 already ruled out any counterexample strategy living purely in $2$-uniform families. Round 3 strengthens this: the full $2$-uniform extremal picture is now completely understood, including the parity-dependent uniqueness/nonuniqueness dichotomy. In particular, any future attack on the full sunflower conjecture cannot hope to extract a single parity-blind "canonical" extremal $2$-uniform core.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

(i) Isolated-vertex hypothesis in Theorem 20.7. The equality characterization quoted from [KupavskiiNoskov2025] assumes no isolated vertices. This is harmless here because I work with the support graph $V(G)=\bigcup\mathcal F$, which has no isolated vertices by definition.

(ii) Converse in the even case. I checked that no hidden matching argument is needed: any graph on $2k-1$ vertices is automatically free of a matching of size $k$. Thus degree sequence alone really suffices.

(iii) Boundary cases. For $k=2$, Theorem 20.8 returns the unique one-edge family. For $k=3$, it recovers the Round 1/2 extremal graph $K_3\cup K_3$. For $k=4$, Proposition 20.10 gives two concrete $10$-edge extremal graphs on $7$ vertices.

(iv) Nonisomorphism certificate in Proposition 20.10. The certificate uses the unique degree-$(k-2)$ vertex in each graph, so it is isomorphism-invariant. Deleting that vertex produces a bipartite graph in Construction B and a nonbipartite graph in Construction A.

(v) Reference correction. Round 2 cited arXiv:2511.17142 as a single-author paper "Erdős's forbidden sunflower problem." The checked source is the two-author paper Exact results and the structure of extremal families for the Duke--Erdős forbidden sunflower problem by Kupavskii and Noskov; this is the source used here for Theorem 3.2.

For fixed $k\ge3$, the sunflower conjecture asks for $f(n,k)\le C_k^n$, where $f(n,k)$ is the least forcing size for a $k$-sunflower among distinct $n$-sets. Here I investigate how much that fixed-rank information propagates when the rank grows.

WORK: EXACT PRODUCT OBSTRUCTION

Let $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ be uniform $k$-sunflower-free families on disjoint ground sets. Their block product \[ \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B=\{A\cup B:A\in\mathcal A,\ B\in\mathcal B\} \] is $k$-sunflower-free. Indeed, suppose $A_i\cup B_i$, $1\le i\le k$, have all pairwise intersections equal. Projecting onto the first block gives equal pairwise intersections of the $A_i$. If two $A_i$ coincide, their intersection equals this entire set; every other $A_j$ contains it and, by uniformity, equals it. Otherwise all $A_i$ are distinct and form a forbidden sunflower. Thus every $A_i$ is equal. The identical argument makes every $B_i$ equal, contradicting distinctness of the original $k$ product sets.

This proves, writing $M(n,k)=f(n,k)-1$, \[ M(n+m,k)\ge M(n,k)M(m,k). \] Combining the supplied two-element extremizers with $k-1$ distinct singletons yields \[ M(n,k)\ge M(2,k)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(k-1)^{n\bmod2}, \] where $M(2,k)=k(k-1)$ for odd $k$ and $(k-1)^2+(k-2)/2$ for even $k$. In particular any proposed exponential upper-bound constant must satisfy $C_k\ge\sqrt{M(2,k)}$. This block-product observation is standard and is not claimed as a new lower bound.

ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

The projection argument must allow repeated projected sets; merely saying that a sunflower projects to a sunflower would be incorrect. Uniformity closes precisely that loophole. Disjoint blocks make the product map injective. The argument provides a lower bound, whereas the conjecture needs an upper bound. The complete $n=2$ classification does not imply that high-rank extremizers split into blocks. Establishing such a decomposition, or a substitute controlling families with strongly coupled coordinates, remains wholly open in this attempt.

SOURCES CHECKED

Complete local source: gpt_pro_5.4/20.tex. Primary source checked: Kupavskii--Noskov, Theorem 3.2, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17142v1. The checked theorem is used only for the already known value of $M(2,k)$; the product lemma is proved above.

FINAL

UNRESOLVED. The fixed-rank results give a necessary exponential base, not the conjectured upper bound. No new general upper bound was obtained.

Problem 20 (Sunflowers): fixed-window entropy ledger framework

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT

For integers $n\ge 1$ and $k\ge 2$, a $k$-sunflower is a collection of distinct sets $S_1,\ldots,S_k$ for which there is a common kernel $K$ such that \[ S_i\cap S_j=K\qquad (i\ne j). \] Let $f(n,k)$ be the least integer such that every $n$-uniform family $\mathcal F$ with $|\mathcal F|\ge f(n,k)$ contains a $k$-sunflower. Problem 20 asks whether, for every fixed $k$, there is a constant $C_k$ such that \[ f(n,k)\le C_k^n\qquad\text{for all }n. \]

This attempt focuses on the three-petal case after the standard reduction to balanced two-collision codes. It does not claim a complete proof of the sunflower conjecture.

2) QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

The classical Erdos--Rado sunflower lemma gives a superexponential-in-$n$ bound for fixed $k$. The modern line of work of Alweiss--Lovett--Wu--Zhang, Rao, Tao, and Bell--Chueluecha--Warnke gives the current logarithmic-base improvements through spread or robust-sunflower methods. Entropy arguments are therefore already standard in this problem. Collision estimates are also standard local tools, and potential/amortized accounting is a standard proof technique.

The proposed novelty of this attempt is architectural rather than a new standalone inequality. It asks whether the reusable spread invariant in ALWZ/Rao/Tao-type arguments can be replaced by a coordinate-level collision/accounting invariant.

3) ATTACK PLAN

Reduction target. Reduce the three-petal sunflower problem to bounding balanced two-collision codes: \[ \mathcal C\subseteq \prod_{i=1}^n \Omega_i, \] where every three distinct codewords have a coordinate in which exactly two of the three symbols agree. A bound $|\mathcal C|\le D^n$ with $D$ independent of the alphabets would imply a constant-base sunflower bound after the standard random-coloring reduction.

Proposed replacement for spread refinement. Instead of maintaining a spread subfamily, maintain:

  • per-coordinate collision information,
  • a fixed labelled hash window for low-collision coordinates,
  • terminal cylinder shadows for high-mass values,
  • fresh/old/scale potentials that pay for repeated use.

Audit target. The attempt succeeds only if every nonterminal transition can be assigned to a finite list of local account inequalities, with constants independent of $n$ and independent of the alphabet sizes.

4) WORK

4.1. Standard reduction to balanced two-collision codes

Pad a rank-$n$ sunflower-free family to an $n$-uniform family by adding private dummy elements. Randomly color the ground set by colors $1,\ldots,n$ and keep the colorful sets. Some coloring keeps at least $(n!/n^n)|\mathcal F|$ sets. Encoding a colorful set by its unique element of each color gives a code \[ \mathcal C\subseteq \prod_i\Omega_i . \] If three codewords have no coordinate with exactly two equal symbols, then in each color class the corresponding set-elements are either all equal or all distinct, so the three pairwise intersections are equal and the original three sets form a sunflower. Thus the resulting code is balanced two-collision.

Therefore an alphabet-free estimate $|\mathcal C|\le D^n$ implies $|\mathcal F|\le (eD)^n$.

4.2. Terminal entropy descent

Let $\mu$ be the uniform law on a balanced two-collision code $\mathcal C$. The proof tree samples one or more independent codewords from $\mu$, but the terminal object is only a projected cylinder: \[ \theta=((J_s,a_s))_{s\in S}, \] meaning $X^s_{J_s}=a_s$ for each sample label $s$. Write \[ T(\theta)=\sum_s |J_s|,\qquad R(\theta)=|S|. \] If a terminal cylinder has mass \[ \Pr[\theta]\ge \exp(-\lambda T(\theta)-cR(\theta)), \] then the usual induction on rank gives $|\mathcal C|\le D^n$ whenever $\log D\ge \lambda+c$. Indeed, each cylinder $\{x:x_{J_s}=a_s\}$ is again a balanced two-collision code after deleting the fixed coordinates.

Thus the goal is to produce a random terminal projected cylinder $\Theta$ with \[ \mathbf H(\Theta) \le \mathbb E[\lambda T(\Theta)+cR(\Theta)] \] for absolute constants $\lambda,c$.

4.3. Fixed labelled hash window

For a one-coordinate law $p$, write \[ \operatorname{Coll}(p)=\sum_a p(a)^2. \] If $X,Y,Z$ are independent samples and coordinate $i$ has collision at most $\beta$, then \[ \Pr[i\in \operatorname{Split}(X,Y,Z)]\le 3\beta. \] Hence a block $H$ of $h$ low-collision coordinates satisfies \[ \Pr\left[|\operatorname{Split}_H(X,Y,Z)|\le 6\beta h\right]\ge 1/2. \]

The proposed mechanism collects low-collision coordinates in a fixed labelled hash window. The label is one of the active sample labels. The window has a fixed transcript budget $B_{\rm win}$ and stops before exceeding that budget. High-mass values are not exposed recursively: if a coordinate develops an atom of mass at least $\beta$, the branch terminalizes by pinning that value as a projected shadow.

If a nonterminal labelled window obtains $h$ surviving coordinates, three conditioned copies under the same public transcript produce a near-sunflower block. The transcript key has Renyi-3 copy cost \[ 2\mathbf H_3(K)+O(1)\le 2\mathbf H(K)+O(1)\le O(B_{\rm win}). \] The sparse split exposure costs $O(h)$. Thus the local hash-return cost is \[ C_{\rm hash}=O(B_{\rm win}+h), \] with $B_{\rm win}$ and $h$ fixed constants.

4.4. Product-KL routing

In a close-pair node, one has a block $J$ on which two sampled codewords agree and the third differs: \[ X_J=Y_J=A_J,\qquad Z_J=B_J\ne A_J . \] The intended product-KL branch theorem scans the actual tuple-coordinate list \[ (A_{j_1},B_{j_1},A_{j_2},B_{j_2},\ldots). \] Every branch must route to exactly one of:

  1. an amortized terminal prefix;
  2. a one-coordinate heavy value pin;
  3. a charged pruning event not inherited by descendants;
  4. a genuine low-collision law of an actual sample coordinate $X_j$, $Y_j$, or $Z_j$, sent to the labelled hash window.
The critical requirement is that the low-collision law is the actual conditional sample-coordinate law, not a law on the pair alphabet $(A_j,B_j)$ and not the auxiliary reference product law.

4.5. Global account inequality

The fixed hash window gives only a one-time near-sunflower block. It does not produce a reusable spread subfamily. The proposed replacement invariant is a state with potential \[ \Phi = B_f |[n]\setminus U| +(B_o|U|-N_{\rm old}) +B_s\sum_{\text{active }J}|J|. \] Here $U$ is the old support, $N_{\rm old}$ is a weighted count of registered old incidences, and the last term pays active scale/rank bookkeeping.

The desired branch inequality is \[ \operatorname{spent}(\pi) + C_{\rm hash}q(\pi) + \Phi(S_\pi) \le \Phi(S_0)+\operatorname{terminal\ shadow\ charge}(\pi), \] where $q(\pi)$ is the number of completed hash windows on the branch.

At the one-step level, the required inequalities have the form \[ (B_f-B_o)|F| \ge \text{fresh selector cost}+\text{child deposit}+\text{possible hash token}, \] \[ \omega_{\rm old}m \ge \text{old transcript cost}+\text{child deposit}+\text{possible hash token}, \] and \[ \text{rank/scale drop} \ge \text{new active-block deposit}+\text{selector cost}. \] If all transition modes are exhaustive and these inequalities cover every selector, the entropy criterion above would close with constants independent of the alphabet.

5) VERIFICATION / ATTEMPTED BREAKS

Known verification obligations. The following are the main places where the attempt can fail:

  1. The fixed-window hash return must really have per-window cost $O(B_{\rm win}+h)$, not a hidden per-branch or alphabet-dependent cost.
  2. The product-KL branch theorem must output actual sample-coordinate laws.
  3. Completed hash-window tokens must be matched injectively to hard exits; tokens cannot pile up along a nested chain.
  4. The residual repair procedure must immediately route every returned near-sunflower block to fresh, old, scale, or terminal progress.
  5. The transition system must be exhaustive; no branch may continue without a terminal shadow or a potential drop.
