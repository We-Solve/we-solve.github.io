Math-268

Property (T), Property (tau), and quantitative finite-quotient transfer for Out(F_n)

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F_n$ be free of rank $n$ and $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)=\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)/\operatorname{Inn}(F_n)$. Property $(T)$ means that every unitary representation with almost-invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector. For a fixed finite symmetric generating multiset $S$, Property $(\tau)$ relative to a family $\mathcal F$ of finite-index subgroups means a uniform positive spectral gap on $\ell^2(H\backslash G)\ominus{\mathbb{C}}\mathbf1$, $H\in\mathcal F$, for the normalized Schreier Laplacian. The source asks whether $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ has $(T)$ or $(\tau)$ for $n\ge4$. A family must be specified for $(\tau)$; "all finite-index subgroups" is the strong interpretation used in the supplied aid.

The historical motivation states that $\operatorname{Out}(F_3)$ has a finite-index subgroup with infinite abelianization and hence lacks $(T)$. The aid reports an affirmative answer for all $n\ge4$: results for $\operatorname{Aut}(F_4)$, $\operatorname{Aut}(F_5)$, and $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$, $n\ge6$, pass to the outer quotient, and $(T)$ implies $(\tau)$ for every finite-index family. It expressly distinguishes a computer-assisted rank-four arXiv preprint, whose attached Sage verifier was not rerun, from peer-reviewed rank-five and higher-rank inputs. No identifiers or complete references for these inputs are supplied, so this is a qualified literature report, not an independently verified certificate.

The aid proves a quantitative quotient transfer. For a finitely generated group $G$, put \[ \Delta_S=|S|\,1-\sum_{s\in S}s\in{\mathbb{R}}[G], \] and use the involution $g^*=g^{-1}$. If an exact identity \[ \Delta_S^2-\epsilon\Delta_S=\sum_j\xi_j^*\xi_j,\quad \epsilon>0, \] holds, then a quotient $q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q$ gives the same identity with parameter $\epsilon$ for the projected multiset $q(S)$ and $q(\xi_j)$. Multiplicities and identity images are retained. Thus the unnormalized gap parameter is unchanged; deleting repetitions or changing Laplacian normalization is a separate operation.

Conversely, suppose a finite-index subgroup $H\le G$ has an epimorphism $\chi:H\to{\mathbb{Z}}$. For integers $q\ge2$, put $H_q=\chi^{-1}(q{\mathbb{Z}})$. Choose representatives $r$ for $H\backslash G$, write $rs=c(r,s)r'$ with $c(r,s)\in H$, and put $M=\max_{r,s}|\chi(c(r,s))|$. The reported normalized Schreier-gap bound is \[ \lambda_1(H_q\backslash G)\le\frac{2\pi^2M^2}{q^2}. \] The constant is independent of $q$. Pullback to the normal cores $C_q=\bigcap_{g\in G}gH_qg^{-1}$ gives the same failure of uniform gap for an all-normal finite-index family; choosing divisibility-nested $q$'s makes a tower. No trivial-intersection assertion is needed. In particular virtual infinite abelianization obstructs $(\tau)$ for the all-finite-index family as well as $(T)$, but says nothing by itself about an arbitrarily restricted family.

Short English statement

The supplied literature report answers the historical rank-at-least-four question affirmatively, while flagging the unrerun computer-assisted rank-four preprint. Its quantitative lemmas preserve exact sum-of-squares gaps through quotients and turn virtual infinite abelianization into vanishing finite-quotient Schreier gaps.
