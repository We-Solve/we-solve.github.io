Failure Rates in Sequential Aperiodic Tiling

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal T$ be a finite tile set intended to tile the Euclidean plane ${\mathbb{R}}^2$. Definitions of admissibility are relative to its tile shapes, permitted placements and matching rules; these data are not specified in the supplied record. A global admissible tiling covers ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ by permitted tiles with pairwise disjoint interiors and satisfies the matching rules. Write $X_{\mathcal T}$ for the set of such tilings. The source calls $\mathcal T$ aperiodic. Under the usual tileable interpretation, this requires $X_{\mathcal T}\ne\varnothing$ and excludes periodic global tilings; the record does not specify the precise convention for periodicity.

A finite locally admissible patch $P$ is a finite collection of permitted placed tiles with pairwise disjoint interiors satisfying the applicable local matching rules. No connectedness or boundary-shape restriction is imposed here, since the source supplies none; whether such restrictions are intended remains unresolved. The patch is globally extendible if there exists $x\in X_{\mathcal T}$ containing every placed tile of $P$. Thus the globally nonextendible patches form \[ \mathcal B_{\mathcal T}=\{P: P\text{ is a finite locally admissible patch and }\nexists x\in X_{\mathcal T}\text{ with }P\subseteq x\}. \] Here containment means containment as a collection of placed tiles.

A sequential assembly history consists of locally admissible patches $P_0,P_1,\ldots$, for as long as the assembly continues, with \[ P_{n+1}=P_n\cup\{t_{n+1}\}, \] where $t_{n+1}$ is one new permitted placed tile. The seed, permitted attachment locations, selection rule and stopping rule are unspecified. For a history that has been specified, define its first occurrence of a global obstruction by \[ \tau=\inf\{n:P_n\in\mathcal B_{\mathcal T}\},\qquad \inf\varnothing=\infty. \] Once a patch is globally nonextendible, every subsequent patch containing it is globally nonextendible. This obstruction need not coincide with an immediate inability to attach another tile. Also, an infinite sequence of additions need not cover the whole plane without additional growth requirements.

Source problem, with unresolved qualifications. For a finite aperiodic tile set, determine how often globally nonextendible finite patches cause failure during assembly one tile at a time. A precise quantitative version requires a specified assembly model and a definition of failure. If a probability law on assembly histories is supplied, possible quantities include $\Pr(\tau\le n)$ and $\Pr(\tau<\infty)$; if a counting or frequency interpretation is intended, its sampling class and normalization must instead be supplied. These are conditional formulations, not choices made by the source. The supplied record does not determine a unique failure rate, a probability space, or whether the intended result concerns every finite aperiodic tile set or a particular family.

Short English statement

The problem asks how often assembling an aperiodic plane tiling one tile at a time produces a finite patch that cannot belong to any complete tiling. The assembly rules, meaning of failure, and probability or frequency measure are unspecified, so a numerical or asymptotic target cannot yet be stated uniquely. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
