Euler integrands, Schouten symmetric functions, and total Q-curvature

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 8 asks to express the Gauss--Bonnet integrand as a Schouten symmetric-function term plus explicit Weyl corrections, and then explain the relation between Q-curvature and topology. Fix $n=2m\geq4$, the Laplacian $\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla$, and \[ J=\frac{R}{2(n-1)},\qquad P=\frac{\operatorname{Ric}-Jg}{n-2},\qquad \operatorname{Rm}=W+P\mathbin{\star} g. \] Use the Kulkarni--Nomizu convention \[ (P\mathbin{\star} g)_{ij}{}^{kl} =P_i{}^k\delta_j^l+P_j{}^l\delta_i^k -P_i{}^l\delta_j^k-P_j{}^k\delta_i^l. \] Let the generalized delta be the determinant of Kronecker deltas and define the standard Euler scalar \[ E_{2m}=2^{-m}\delta^{i_1\cdots i_{2m}}_{j_1\cdots j_{2m}} \prod_{a=1}^m R_{i_{2a-1}i_{2a}}{}^{j_{2a-1}j_{2a}}. \] With these conventions $E_4=|\operatorname{Rm}|^2-4|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+R^2$, and on a closed manifold $\int E_{2m}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}=(4\pi)^m m!\chi(M)$.

The aid's all-even-dimensional finite contraction formula is \[ E_{2m}=2^m(m!)^2\sigma_m(P)+\sum_{r=1}^m C_{m,r}(W,P), \] where an explicit definition of each correction is \[ C_{m,r}=2^{-m}\binom mr \delta^{i_1\cdots i_{2m}}_{j_1\cdots j_{2m}} \left(\prod_{a=1}^r W_{i_{2a-1}i_{2a}}{}^{j_{2a-1}j_{2a}}\right) \left(\prod_{a=r+1}^m (P\mathbin{\star} g)_{i_{2a-1}i_{2a}}{}^{j_{2a-1}j_{2a}}\right). \] Empty products are one. Every correction contains a Weyl factor, and the displayed coefficient of the pure Schouten term fixes its normalization. This is a complete contraction expression, not a claim that every correction has been simplified into a minimal list of independent invariants. For dimension two, where the usual Schouten formula is undefined, the Euler scalar is directly $E_2=R=2K$.

In dimension four use the full tensor norm $|W|^2=W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}$, rather than a curvature-operator norm differing by a fixed factor. The aid gives \[ E_4=|W|^2+16\sigma_2(P),\qquad Q_4=4\sigma_2(P)-\Delta J, \quad J=R/6. \] Equivalently $Q_4=-\frac16\Delta R-\frac12|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+\frac16R^2$. Thus every closed Riemannian four-manifold satisfies \[ \int_MQ_4\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol} =8\pi^2\chi(M)-\frac14\int_M|W|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol}. \] In particular total Q is bounded above by $8\pi^2\chi$, with equality exactly when $W$ vanishes identically. The closedness assumption removes the Laplacian integral and the Gauss--Bonnet boundary correction.

For the worked family, let $S^2(a)$ and $S^2(b)$ denote round spheres of positive Gaussian curvatures $a,b$, not radii. On their product put $r=a/b$. Then \[ \int Q_4\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol} =\frac{16\pi^2}{3}(4-r-r^{-1}),\qquad \int|W|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{vol} =\frac{64\pi^2}{3}(r+2+r^{-1}). \] The Euler characteristic is four. Within this product family total Q is at most $32\pi^2/3$, with equality precisely at $a=b$, and tends to $-\infty$ as $r\to0$ or $r\to\infty$. This family maximum is not the unrestricted topological upper bound. The unit round $S^4$ independently has $E_4=24$, $Q_4=6$, $W=0$, and $\int Q_4=16\pi^2$, checking the normalization. The aid does not claim a complete all-even-dimensional Q-topology formula or a fully simplified basis for all Weyl corrections.

Short English statement

Expanding the Euler scalar gives an explicit Schouten term and finitely many contractions containing Weyl curvature. In dimension four the resulting integral Q identity separates topology from the Weyl norm. The sphere-product family checks the constants and shows total Q can decrease without bound even at fixed topology.
