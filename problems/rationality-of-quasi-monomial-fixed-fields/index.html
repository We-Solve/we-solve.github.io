Math-1136

Rationality of Quasi-Monomial Fixed Fields

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a field, let $K/k$ be a finite field extension, and let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be algebraically independent over $K$. Write $L=K(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. Let a group $G$ act on $L$ by $k$-automorphisms, meaning that every group element fixes $k$ pointwise.

The monomial-form interpretation of the supplied term quasi-monomial is that every $\sigma\in G$ preserves $K$ and satisfies \[ \sigma(x_j)=c_j(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_i^{a_{ij}(\sigma)}\qquad(1\leq j\leq n), \] where $c_j(\sigma)\in K^{\times}$ and $A(\sigma)=(a_{ij}(\sigma))\in\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{Z}})$. The maps are required to constitute a group action. The source does not supply a definition of quasi-monomial action; in particular, whether its intended convention additionally requires $K^G=k$ remains unresolved. Finiteness of $G$, faithfulness of its action, and separability or normality of $K/k$ are not explicitly stipulated and are not imposed here.

Define the fixed field by \[ L^G=\{f\in L:\sigma(f)=f\text{ for every }\sigma\in G\}. \] A field extension $F/k$ is $k$-rational if there are an integer $m\geq0$ and elements $t_1,\ldots,t_m\in F$, algebraically independent over $k$, such that $F=k(t_1,\ldots,t_m)$; for $m=0$, this means $F=k$.

Problem. Determine necessary and sufficient conditions on the extension $K/k$ and the specified quasi-monomial action of $G$ for \[ K(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^G \] to be $k$-rational. This is a request for a characterization, not an assertion of rationality for every such action. Its intended scope requires clarification of the quasi-monomial convention and the group hypotheses identified above.

Short English statement

Characterize when the field of rational functions fixed by a quasi-monomial group action is a purely transcendental extension of $k$. The source leaves some action hypotheses implicit, particularly finiteness of $G$ and whether $K^G=k$ is required. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
