Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $P=(P_1,P_2):{\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^2$, where $P_1,P_2\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]$. Its real Jacobian determinant is \[ J_P(x,y)=\det\begin{pmatrix} \partial P_1/\partial x & \partial P_1/\partial y\\ \partial P_2/\partial x & \partial P_2/\partial y \end{pmatrix}. \] Under the usual total-degree convention, set \[ \deg P=\max\{\deg P_1,\deg P_2\}. \] If "invertible" means invertible as a map from ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ onto ${\mathbb{R}}^2$, define \[ \mathcal D=\left\{\deg P:\begin{array}{l} P\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]^2,\\ J_P(a)\ne0\text{ for every }a\in{\mathbb{R}}^2,\\ P:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^2\text{ is not bijective} \end{array}\right\}. \] The question, under these conventions, is to determine $\min\mathcal D$, provided $\mathcal D\ne\varnothing$: what is the lowest degree of a real polynomial map of the plane whose Jacobian determinant never vanishes but which fails to be globally invertible?

The supplied statement does not explicitly define the degree convention or whether invertibility requires a polynomial inverse. Thus the displayed formulation is conditional on total degree and global invertibility as a function; requiring a polynomial inverse would give a different formulation. The hypothesis is nowhere vanishing of the real Jacobian determinant, not a requirement that it be constant. The source labels this a new problem in its 1995 list; the supplied registry labels it open, without supplying an independently verified current status.

Short English statement

Determine the smallest degree of a polynomial map ${\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^2$ with a nowhere-zero real Jacobian determinant that is not globally invertible. The displayed formulation uses total degree and interprets invertibility as bijectivity; the source does not explicitly settle these conventions.
