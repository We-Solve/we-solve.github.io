Math-5473

Generic specialization and wall cancellation for Witt–Toledo classes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 4.5 of Marc Burger starts from a symplectic vector space $V$ over a field $k$ and the stated central extension \[ 1\longrightarrow W(k)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(V) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(V)\longrightarrow1. \] In characteristic different from two, $W(k)$ is the Witt group of nonsingular symmetric forms modulo hyperbolic forms. For a closed oriented surface $S$ and a representation $\rho:\pi_1S\to\operatorname{Sp}(V)$, pull back the extension class and evaluate it on $[S]$ to obtain $\tau(\rho)\in W(k)$. The source asks to study this class for the tautological representation associated with an algebraic parameter family. Burger's Comment 4.6 says the point-parameter case is the classical Toledo invariant, with its numerical normalization understood.

The supplied partial result makes the family precise over the reals. Let $A$ be an integral real parameter variety, let $u:A\to\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1S,\operatorname{Sp}(2n,{\mathbb{R}}))$ be regular, and set $K={\mathbb{R}}(A)$. Evaluation at the generic point gives $\rho_K:\pi_1S\to\operatorname{Sp}(2n,K)$. Integrality and regularity matter: an arbitrary set map, a reducible algebraic set without a chosen component, or merely a map to a character quotient does not supply this tautological representation as stated.

Fix a finite bar cycle $z=\sum_j e_j[\gamma_j\mid\delta_j]$ representing the surface fundamental class, with $e_j\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, and a fixed Kashiwara/Maslov cocycle convention for the extension. The aid asserts that on a dense principal open $U\subset A$ one can simultaneously split the radicals of the finitely many cocycle forms with constant rank and obtain a model \[ \widetilde\tau_U\in W(\mathcal O(U)) \quad\text{mapping to }\tau(\rho_K)\in W(K). \] For every $a\in U({\mathbb{R}})$ its evaluation is $\tau(\rho_a)\in W({\mathbb{R}})$. Its signature is constant on connected components of $U({\mathbb{R}})$; the corresponding normalized classical Toledo number is also constant along continuous real families, including across walls where a chosen cocycle presentation degenerates. This does not define unrestricted specialization of every class in $W(K)$ at every point of $A$.

In rank one, write $c(g)=g_{21}$ for $g\in SL_2({\mathbb{R}})$. The supplied cocycle convention represents a summand by the rank-one form with coefficient \[ F(g,h)=-c(g)c(gh)c(h), \] when it is nonzero, and by zero when degenerate. For a $C^1$ path $\rho_t$ put $F_j(t)=F(\rho_t(\gamma_j),\rho_t(\delta_j))$. Suppose every $F_j$ vanishing at $t_0$ has a simple zero there. Local constancy of the total signature then gives the simultaneous-wall identity \[ \sum_{j:F_j(t_0)=0}e_j\,\operatorname{sgn}(F'_j(t_0))=0. \] The jump of each such sign is twice its derivative sign, whereas the nonzero terms keep their signs. The formula requires the whole bar-cycle sum and does not assert cancellation term by term. Extension-class signs and factors must be fixed before identifying its signature with a particular classical Toledo normalization.

These specialize and constrain the invariant in the stated real integral-family setting; they do not classify it for all fields, singular parameter spaces or arbitrary families. In particular characteristic two and relative/punctured-surface versions require additional definitions.

Short English statement

Study the Witt-valued Toledo invariant of an algebraic family of symplectic surface-group representations. The supplied aid constructs a model on a dense open set for regular real integral families and matches its real fibers. In rank one, local constancy forces an explicit cancellation among all simple discriminant walls in a fixed surface-cycle representative.
