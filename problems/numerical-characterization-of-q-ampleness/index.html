Math-4789

Numerical Characterization of $q$-Ampleness

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For clarity, the following is the conventional cohomological definition; the supplied record does not specify the intended geometric hypotheses. Let $k$ be a field, let $X$ be a projective $k$-scheme, let $L$ be a line bundle on $X$, and let $q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$. The line bundle $L$ is $q$-ample if, for every coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$, there exists an integer $m_0=m_0(\mathcal F,L,q)\geq 0$ such that \[ H^i\!\left(X,\mathcal F\otimes L^{\otimes m}\right)=0 \qquad\text{for every integer }m\geq m_0\text{ and every integer }i>q. \] For $q=0$, this is the usual cohomological characterization of ampleness. If $q\geq\dim X$, the condition is automatic in this framework.

Numerical equivalence of line bundles means equality of their degrees on every integral curve in $X$. This explains one conventional meaning of numerical data; the supplied record does not specify which intersection numbers, cycle cones, or other numerical data a characterization may use.

Problem. Give a necessary-and-sufficient numerical characterization of $q$-ampleness that specializes, when $q=0$, to Kleiman's ampleness criterion in its applicable geometric setting.

Unresolved scope. The projective framework above supplies a definition, not an attribution of missing hypotheses to the source. The record does not specify the base field or its characteristic, whether $X$ must be a variety, smooth, or irreducible, or the intended range of $q$. It also gives no proposed numerical criterion. These choices require source clarification before a fully scoped problem statement can be asserted.

Short English statement

Find numerical conditions that are both necessary and sufficient for a line bundle to be $q$-ample, recovering Kleiman's criterion for ordinary ampleness at $q=0$. The supplied record leaves the geometric hypotheses and permitted numerical data unspecified; the displayed definition uses a conventional projective framework.
