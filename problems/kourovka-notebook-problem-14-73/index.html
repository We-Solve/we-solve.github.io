Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.73

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite graph, write $V(\Gamma)$ for its vertex set, and put $G=\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$. For $\alpha\in V(\Gamma)$, let \[ \Gamma(\alpha)=\{\beta\in V(\Gamma):\beta\text{ is adjacent to }\alpha\},\qquad G_\alpha=\{g\in G:g(\alpha)=\alpha\}. \] The graph is vertex-transitive if $G$ acts transitively on $V(\Gamma)$; its valency is then the common neighbourhood size $v=|\Gamma(\alpha)|$. The supplied record does not specify graph conventions or whether connectedness is implicit. No connectedness hypothesis has been added here; this omission requires clarification before the formulation can be considered definitive.

For a permutation group $L\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ on a nonempty set $\Omega$, call $L$ quasiprimitive if it is transitive and every nonidentity normal subgroup of $L$ is transitive on $\Omega$. Define the induced local permutation group by \[ L_\alpha=\{g|_{\Gamma(\alpha)}:g\in G_\alpha\} \leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Gamma(\alpha)). \] Using the standard induced-action interpretation of the source terminology, $\Gamma$ is locally quasiprimitive if $L_\alpha$ is quasiprimitive for every vertex $\alpha$. Vertex transitivity makes it sufficient to check one vertex. This condition concerns the induced permutation group, whereas the conjectured bound concerns the entire stabilizer $G_\alpha$, including the kernel of its action on $\Gamma(\alpha)$.

Conjecture (C. E. Praeger, 1999; source formulation). There exists a function $f:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}$ such that, for every finite vertex-transitive, locally quasiprimitive graph $\Gamma$ of valency $v$, and every $\alpha\in V(\Gamma)$, \[ |\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)_\alpha|\leq f(v). \] Here ${\mathbb{N}}$ retains the source's unspecified convention. The source does not explain whether valency zero is included or how quasiprimitivity on an empty neighbourhood should be interpreted. These boundary conventions, together with the missing connectedness convention, remain unresolved.

Supplied reductions and qualifications. The record states that proving the conjecture can be reduced to graphs whose full automorphism group satisfies \[ \text{every }1\ne N\trianglelefteq G\text{ has at most two orbits on }V(\Gamma). \] It attributes this reduction to C. E. Praeger, Ars Combin. 19 A (1985), 149--163.

The source also describes the analogous conjecture for locally primitive graphs, attributed to R. Weiss in 1978, as still open. Here a transitive permutation group is primitive if it preserves no partition other than the singleton partition and the one-part partition. For non-bipartite graphs, the source reports a "reduction" of Weiss' conjecture to the case of an almost simple full automorphism group, citing M. Conder, C. H. Li and C. E. Praeger (2000). A finite group $H$ is almost simple if $T\leq H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(T)$ for some nonabelian finite simple group $T$, identifying $T$ with its inner automorphism group. The source refers to Problem 14.46 for definitions, but that record is not supplied.

The supplied Comment of 2013 reports that these conjectures have been proved when there is an upper bound on the degree of any alternating group occurring as a quotient of a subgroup, citing Praeger, Pyber, Spiga and Szabó (2012). An alternating group of degree $m$ is $A_m$; occurrence as a quotient of a subgroup means a section $K/J\cong A_m$, with $J\trianglelefteq K$. The supplied wording does not explicitly identify the ambient group containing $K$, the uniform scope of the degree bound, or the permitted dependence of the resulting stabilizer bound. Accordingly, this comment is retained as a qualified source report rather than expanded into a precise additional theorem.

The supplied Comment of 2021 reports that the conjecture has been proved when $L_\alpha$ has an abelian regular normal subgroup, citing P. Spiga (2016). This means that there is an abelian subgroup $A\trianglelefteq L_\alpha$ whose action on $\Gamma(\alpha)$ is transitive and whose point stabilizers are trivial. All reductions, special-case results and open-status descriptions above are supplied source assessments, not independently verified literature conclusions.

Short English statement

Praeger's conjecture asks whether the size of a vertex stabilizer in the full automorphism group of a finite vertex-transitive, locally quasiprimitive graph is bounded solely by the valency. The supplied record labels the problem open and reports reductions and special cases. Connectedness, graph conventions and the valency-zero boundary require clarification; the reported literature assessments have not been independently verified.
