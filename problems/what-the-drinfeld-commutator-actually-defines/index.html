What the Drinfeld commutator actually defines

Definitions and mathematical statement

This record is explanatory context at the end of Pheidas's Question 5, after Fact 6 and before Question 7, according to the supplied source-verification history. It is not an independent question. It proposes replacing the displayed Frobenius commutation condition by commutation with $\rho_T$ for a Drinfeld ${\mathbb{F}}_q[T]$-module $\rho$ over $L$. The excerpt does not define its target $A_L$, state Fact 6, or give the ambient language in which definability is intended.

The aid makes the algebraic content precise as follows. Let $q=p^e$, let $L$ be a field of characteristic $p$ containing ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, and let $L\{\tau\}$ be the ring of $q$-additive polynomials, with multiplication given by composition and relation \[ \tau a=a^q\tau\qquad(a\in L). \] Here $\tau$ acts as $Z\mapsto Z^q$. A Drinfeld ${\mathbb{F}}_q[T]$-module is an ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-algebra homomorphism $\rho:{\mathbb{F}}_q[T]\to L\{\tau\}$ with $\rho_T$ of positive $\tau$-degree, and with constant coefficient given by the structural characteristic map. Its $L$-endomorphism ring is \[ \operatorname{End}_L(\rho) =\{u\in L\{\tau\}:u\rho_a=\rho_a u\text{ for every }a\in{\mathbb{F}}_q[T]\} =\{u\in L\{\tau\}:u\rho_T=\rho_Tu\}. \] The equality uses the fact that $q$-additive polynomials commute with the scalar ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-action. This is not in general the smaller distinguished image $\rho({\mathbb{F}}_q[T])$.

The aid's obstruction family is: for each integer $m>1$, take $L={\mathbb{F}}_{q^m}$ and the rank-$m$, special-characteristic module \[ \rho_T=\tau^m,\qquad \gamma(T)=0. \] Since $a^{q^m}=a$ for every $a\in L$, the element $\tau^m$ is central in $L\{\tau\}$, so \[ \operatorname{End}_L(\rho)=L\{\tau\} \supsetneq\rho({\mathbb{F}}_q[T])={\mathbb{F}}_q[\tau^m]. \] For example $\tau$ lies in the centralizer and not in that image. Thus a commutator condition alone cannot be identified with the distinguished image without further endomorphism hypotheses.

If the ambient ring instead consists of all $p$-additive polynomials, put $L\{\sigma\}$ with $\sigma a=a^p\sigma$ and $\tau=\sigma^e$. Choose $\omega\in{\mathbb{F}}_q$ with ${\mathbb{F}}_p(\omega)={\mathbb{F}}_q$. For $u=\sum_i a_i\sigma^i$, \[ u\omega=\omega u \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_i(\omega^{p^i}-\omega)=0\text{ for every }i \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_i=0\text{ whenever }e\nmid i. \] Consequently, in this larger ambient ring, \[ \{u:u\rho_T=\rho_Tu,\ u\omega=\omega u\} =\operatorname{End}_L(\rho)\subseteq L\{\tau\}. \] For $e=1$ the scalar restriction is automatic. The added scalar equation repairs the $p$-additive versus $q$-additive ambiguity; it does not remove extra endomorphisms or force $u\in\rho({\mathbb{F}}_q[T])$. These equations are conditions in the additive-polynomial composition ring. Turning them into a definition in an unspecified field or arithmetic language is a further issue not addressed by the supplied excerpt. The OCR notation "$Zp$" is interpreted as a Frobenius polynomial only conditionally; the missing original notation and definition of $A_L$ remain unresolved.

Short English statement

The Drinfeld commutator defines the endomorphism ring in the $q$-additive polynomial ring, which may be larger than the module's distinguished polynomial image. In a $p$-additive ambient ring, a scalar commutator enforces $q$-additivity but does not eliminate extra endomorphisms. The record is context, not a separate open problem.
