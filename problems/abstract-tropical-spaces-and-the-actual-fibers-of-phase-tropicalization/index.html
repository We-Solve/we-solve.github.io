Math-1508

Abstract tropical spaces and the actual fibers of phase tropicalization

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Section C.2, workshop proposals moderated and contributed by Margaret Symington. The source asks for intrinsic tropical varieties and abstract idempotent varieties: appropriate local models, charts and gluing, possibly in ringed-space or non-Archimedean analytic language. It further asks when a complex tropical limit is homeomorphic or homotopy equivalent to a nearby algebraic variety; for a Hodge theory compatible with classical limiting Hodge theory; for a refinement/patchworking theorem giving information about a single classical hypersurface; and for maps, line bundles and vector bundles. These are several foundational questions, not a single settled definition or conjecture.

A convention-explicit interpretation of the proposed coordinates. Take the formal Puiseux field $K=\bigcup_{r\ge1}{\mathbb{C}}((t^{1/r}))$ and an ideal $I\subset K[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_n^{\pm1}]$, with $X=V(I)\subset(K^*)^n$. For $z=c t^\lambda+\text{higher terms}$, $c\ne0$, define $\operatorname{val}(z)=\lambda\in{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\operatorname{ph}(z)=c/|c|\in S^1$. The raw tropical image is $\operatorname{val}(X)\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^n$; the usual closed tropical support is its closure in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. A phase image lies in ${\mathbb{Q}}^n\times(S^1)^n$, or after closure in ${\mathbb{R}}^n\times(S^1)^n$. A chosen encoding $(w,\theta)\mapsto(e^{-w_j+i\theta_j})_j$ identifies the latter ambient space with $({\mathbb{C}}^*)^n$; this encoding is a convention, not the raw valuation map itself. Real phases are the signs $\{\pm1\}^n$, equivalently $\theta_j\in\{0,\pi\}$, rather than the vacuous statement that numerical arguments are real.

The extraction's $K={\mathbb{C}}(t)$ called Puiseux series, ideals "in $K^*$", and valuation targets ${\mathbb{R}}^*$ or ${\mathbb{Q}}^*$ are not adopted literally: an ideal belongs to a ring, a Puiseux series field differs from the rational-function field, and valuation zero must be allowed. Raw rational-valued images and their closures must also be distinguished.

Supplied correction of the torus-fiber assertion. The workshop says that each complex tropical projection fiber is a torus. The aid instead identifies the fiber over $w$, in the appropriate phase/initial-degeneration model, with the coamoeba or closed coamoeba of $\operatorname{in}_wX$. Here a coamoeba is the argument image in $(S^1)^n$. Closure and lifting conventions must be fixed; general coamoebas need not be tori.

For a hypersurface initial form $c_a z^a+c_b z^b$, put $u=a-b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n\setminus\{0\}$ and $g=\gcd(|u_1|,\ldots,|u_n|)$. Its exact phase fiber is \[ \{\theta\in({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^n: e^{i\langle u,\theta\rangle} =\operatorname{ph}(-c_b/c_a)\}. \] It has exactly $g$ connected components, each an $(n-1)$-torus, and is a single torus precisely when $u$ is primitive. For real coefficients, writing $\operatorname{sgn}(-c_b/c_a)=(-1)^\tau$, its real phase subset is \[ \{s\in{\mathbb{F}}_2^n:(u\bmod2)\cdot s=\tau\}. \] It is empty if that affine equation is inconsistent; otherwise it has $2^{n-1}$ points when $u\bmod2\ne0$, and $2^n$ points when $u\bmod2=0$ and $\tau=0$. The aid gives $1+x+y$ at the tropical-line vertex as a non-torus coamoeba fiber, so even the binomial union-of-tori description is not universal.

Remaining source qualifications. The $p$-adic discussion proposes valuation plus residue/phase data, informally written ${\mathbb{Q}}\times\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_p^*$, but does not define its angular component, topology or the stated "closure of multiplicative generators". No canonical phase choice is inferred. Viewing an ideal over formal Puiseux series as a family $I_t$ over complex numbers additionally requires convergence or algebraic family data. A Hausdorff-limit assertion needs rescaled logarithmic coordinates and, for noncompact spaces, a local Hausdorff or compactification convention. The source's claim that a smooth complex tropical curve is homeomorphic to the small nonzero fibers is retained as a claim needing a precise smoothness/degeneration hypothesis, not an unrestricted theorem. The aid mentions a qualified modern status synthesis for intrinsic spaces, analytification/skeleta, Hodge theory, topology, patchworking and bundles, but supplies no specific references or complete resolutions for those questions here.

Short English statement

The workshop asks for intrinsic tropical foundations, topology and Hodge theory of limits, patchworking, maps and bundles. The supplied aid corrects one concrete assertion: phase fibers are initial-degeneration coamoebas, not always tori. A binomial fiber has as many torus components as the gcd of its exponent difference, while a trinomial already gives a non-torus example.
