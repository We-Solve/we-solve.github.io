Math-4494

Freely Complemented and Maximal Amenable MASAs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d$ be a finite positive integer and let $\mathbb F_d$ denote the free group on $d$ generators. Write $L(\mathbb F_d)$ for its group von Neumann algebra, with its canonical trace. The supplied record does not specify the intended range of $d$.

For a conventional interpretation of the polynomial question, let $(M,\tau)$ be a tracial von Neumann algebra generated by freely independent self-adjoint semicircular elements $S=(S_1,\ldots,S_d)$, so that $M=W^*(S_1,\ldots,S_d)\cong L(\mathbb F_d)$. Here a centered semicircular element of variance $\sigma^2>0$ has spectral distribution \[ \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2}\sqrt{4\sigma^2-t^2}\,\mathbf 1_{[-2\sigma,2\sigma]}(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] Freeness means that alternating products of centered elements from distinct members of the family of generated unital algebras have trace zero. The source says only "semicircular $d$-tuple"; free independence, generation of the ambient algebra, and normalization are not explicitly stated. The identification above therefore describes the usual free semicircular interpretation, rather than an additional verified source hypothesis.

Under the usual interpretation of polynomial evaluation, take \[ p=(p_1,\ldots,p_d),\qquad p_j\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle, \qquad x=p_1(S),\qquad A=W^*(x). \] Here $W^*(x)$ is the unital von Neumann algebra generated by $x$ and $x^*$. The source does not specify the polynomial class, require the polynomials to be self-adjoint, or impose conditions on the other coordinates of $p$. If $A$ is abelian, then $x$ must in particular be normal.

A unital von Neumann subalgebra $A\subseteq M$ is a maximal abelian self-adjoint subalgebra, abbreviated MASA, if it is abelian and \[ A'\cap M=A. \] Use the following tracial free-product formulation of a freely complemented MASA: there exists a unital von Neumann subalgebra $B\subseteq M$ such that \[ W^*(A\cup B)=M \quad\text{and}\quad (M,\tau)\cong(A,\tau|_A)*(B,\tau|_B), \] where the isomorphism respects the given inclusions. Equivalently, $A$ and $B$ generate $M$ and are free with respect to $\tau$: whenever $n\geq1$, each $a_k$ belongs to $A$ or $B$, consecutive factors belong to different algebras, and $\tau(a_k)=0$, one has $\tau(a_1\cdots a_n)=0$. The supplied record does not define "freely complemented" or specify whether the complement must additionally be nontrivial or diffuse; those conventions require confirmation.

An amenable von Neumann subalgebra $A\subseteq M$ is maximal amenable in $M$ if every amenable von Neumann algebra $N$ satisfying $A\subseteq N\subseteq M$ equals $A$. Amenability is understood in its usual von Neumann algebra sense.

The supplied problem asks for conditions on $p$ under which $W^*(p_1(S))$ is a freely complemented MASA in the intended ambient algebra, and asks whether this property is generic. It also asks whether most MASAs in $L(\mathbb F_d)$ are maximal amenable and whether every maximal amenable MASA in $L(\mathbb F_d)$ is freely complemented. For a fixed intended $d$, the last question has the quantified form \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every MASA }A\subseteq L(\mathbb F_d),\\ A\text{ maximal amenable in }L(\mathbb F_d) \ \Longrightarrow\ A\text{ freely complemented in }L(\mathbb F_d). \end{gathered} \] The terms "generic" and "most" cannot be given precise quantifiers from the supplied record: no parameter space, topology, measure, or equivalence convention is specified. Thus these portions remain formulation questions, and even the displayed implication depends on resolving the intended range of $d$ and complement convention.

Short English statement

The problem asks when a polynomial in a semicircular family generates a freely complemented MASA, whether this happens generically, whether most MASAs in $L(\mathbb F_d)$ are maximal amenable, and whether every maximal amenable MASA is freely complemented. The source leaves the polynomial class, several ambient conventions, and the meanings of "generic" and "most" unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
