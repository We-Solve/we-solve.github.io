Tomographic Reconstruction near Discontinuities

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a research question about reconstructing functions with discontinuities from discrete tomographic measurements. For schematic notation only, write \[ \widehat f=\mathcal R\left(\mathcal D(\mathcal T f)\right), \] where $f$ is the unknown function, $\mathcal T$ denotes a tomographic measurement operator, $\mathcal D$ denotes discrete sampling of its data, and $\mathcal R$ denotes a reconstruction procedure. These symbols are placeholders: the source record does not specify their domains, formulas, admissibility conditions, or sampling parameters.
  1. Describe the behavior of $\widehat f$ near singularities of $f$.
  2. Determine how singularities of $f$ remote from a reconstruction location affect the reconstruction at that location.
  3. Explain under what conditions, and in what mathematical sense, functions with discontinuities can be reconstructed accurately from discrete tomographic data.

The supplied formulation does not specify the spatial dimension or domain, the class of functions or singularities, the measurement geometry, the reconstruction algorithm, the treatment of noise, or boundary conditions. It also does not define proximity, remoteness, accuracy, an error norm, or a limiting sampling regime. Consequently, these questions cannot be sharpened into a uniquely determined quantified theorem or conjecture from the supplied evidence. In particular, the question about accurate reconstruction does not assert accurate reconstruction for every discontinuous function or every discrete acquisition scheme.

Short English statement

The problem asks how discrete tomographic reconstructions behave near singularities, how distant singularities affect them, and what explains accurate reconstruction of discontinuous functions. The supplied record leaves the mathematical model and the meaning of accuracy unspecified, so a precise theorem requires additional hypotheses.
