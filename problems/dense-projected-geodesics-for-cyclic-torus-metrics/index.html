Math-1462

Dense projected geodesics for cyclic torus metrics

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a smooth closed connected orientable surface of genus at least one, with a smooth Riemannian metric $g$. Question 8.5.2 asks whether there always exists a complete unit-speed geodesic $\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}\to M$ such that \[ \overline{\gamma({\mathbb{R}})}=M. \] Density here concerns the projected image in the surface, not the lifted orbit $(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))$ in $SM$. A forward-dense ray, $\overline{\gamma([0,\infty))}=M$, gives a stronger affirmative example.

The supplied research aid reports forward-dense geodesics for every cyclic torus metric \[ M={\mathbb{R}}^2/(a{\mathbb{Z}}\times b{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad g={\,\mathrm{d}} x^2+f(x)^2{\,\mathrm{d}} y^2,\qquad a,b>0, \] where $f$ is smooth, positive and $a$-periodic. The conserved Clairaut momentum $J=f(x)^2\dot y$ is a nonconstant continuous function on $SM$, so every orbit lies in one of its proper level sets and no orbit is dense in all of $SM$. This obstruction is consistent with a geodesic dense in the base torus.

More generally, the aid reports the sufficient measure-theoretic criterion: if $\nu$ is an ergodic geodesic-flow-invariant probability measure on $SM$ and its base marginal $\pi_*\nu$, for $\pi:SM\to M$, has full support, then $\nu$-almost every orbit projects forward densely in $M$. Full support of $\pi_*\nu$ means positive measure for every nonempty open subset of $M$; it does not require full support of $\nu$ on $SM$. The aid says that, combined with published results, this answers the question for all positive-genus surface metrics without conjugate points. Those published results are not identified, and the unrestricted question is not claimed solved.

The extract also begins section 8.6 with a separate theorem attributed to Gaidukov. For every Riemannian metric on an oriented closed surface of genus at least one, every nontrivial free homotopy class $\Gamma$, and every $p\in M$, it states the existence of a periodic geodesic $\eta:{\mathbb{R}}\to M$ in $\Gamma$ and a geodesic half-line $\beta:[0,\infty)\to M$, $\beta(0)=p$, with synchronized parametrizations satisfying \[ d_g(\beta(t),\eta(t))\longrightarrow0\qquad(t\to\infty). \] Here "ray" is not taken to mean globally distance-minimizing in the compact base surface. The source attributes derivation from Morse and Hedlund to [Bia-Pol1986]. This asymptotic-to-a-periodic-geodesic statement is adjacent background; it does not imply a dense geodesic in $M$.

Short English statement

Does every positive-genus surface metric have a geodesic dense in the surface? The supplied aid gives cyclic torus examples and an invariant-measure criterion, while distinguishing base density from a dense orbit in the full unit tangent bundle. The appended Gaidukov theorem is separate background.
