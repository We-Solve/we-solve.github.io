FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #140; partial reconstruction, 2026-09-23. Let $r_3(N)$ be the largest cardinality of a subset of $[N]$ without a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Prove that for every fixed $C>0$, $r_3(N)\ll_C N/(\log N)^C$.

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

Kelley--Meka proved a stronger stretched-exponential upper bound; Bloom--Sisask give a primary mathematical exposition. We verify its implication for the question, prove a Fourier obstruction, and reconstruct the contrasting Behrend lower construction. The central Kelley--Meka density-increment argument is not reproduced, so this attempt remains partial. No new result is claimed.

WORK

What the known theorem gives

The published bound is \[ r_3(N)\le K N\exp(-c(\log N)^{1/12}) \tag{1} \] for absolute positive $c,K$. For any $C>0$, the function $t^C\exp(-ct^{1/12})$ is bounded on $[1,\infty)$: its logarithm is $C\log t-ct^{1/12}$ and tends to $-\infty$. Indeed its only possible interior maximum has $t^{1/12}=12C/c$. Thus $1$ implies $r_3(N)\ll_C N/(\log N)^C$ after enlarging the constant for small $N$. This deduction is complete, but $1$ is a cited theorem, not a theorem proved by the following partial work.

A proved Fourier obstruction

Let $p>2N$ be an odd prime, embed a progression-free $A\subseteq[N]$ in $\mathbb F_p$, and put $\alpha=|A|/p$. No extra cyclic three-term progressions arise: the integer $x+z-2y$ has absolute value less than $2N<p$. Define normalized Fourier coefficients \[ \widehat{1_A}(r)=p^{-1}\sum_x1_A(x)e^{-2\pi irx/p}. \] Fourier orthogonality and Parseval give \[ \frac1{p^2}\sum_{x,d}1_A(x)1_A(x+d)1_A(x+2d) =\sum_r\widehat{1_A}(r)^2\widehat{1_A}(-2r),\qquad \sum_r|\widehat{1_A}(r)|^2=\alpha. \] Only $d=0$ contributes on the left, so it equals $\alpha/p$. Writing $M=\max_{r\ne0}|\widehat{1_A}(r)|$, and noting that $r\mapsto-2r$ permutes the nonzero residues, we obtain \[ \alpha^3-\alpha/p\le M\alpha. \] If $\alpha^2\ge2/p$, this forces \[ M\ge\alpha^2/2. \tag{2} \] Thus a progression-free set of non-negligible density has a nonzero Fourier bias. Turning that bias into sufficiently efficient density increments over many scales is a separate task: $2$ alone does not prove the logarithmic bounds asked for here.

Why polynomial-saving guesses would be false

Choose integers $d\ge3,m\ge2$. Partition the vectors $x\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^d$ by $\|x\|_2^2$. There are at most $d(m-1)^2+1\le dm^2$ possible values, so one sphere contains at least $m^{d-2}/d$ such vectors. Map them into integers by \[ \phi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}x_j(2m)^j. \] These values lie in $[0,(2m)^d)$. If $\phi(x)+\phi(z)=2\phi(y)$, neither side has carries in base $2m$, so $x+z=2y$ coordinatewise. For points on a common sphere, \[ \|x\|^2+\|z\|^2-2\|(x+z)/2\|^2=\tfrac12\|x-z\|^2 \] then forces $x=z=y$. Thus the image, and its translate by $1$, are progression-free. For large $N$, take $d=\lfloor\sqrt{\log N}\rfloor$ and $m=\lfloor N^{1/d}/2\rfloor$. Then $(2m)^d\le N$ and $m\ge N^{1/d}/4$. Consequently \[ \log r_3(N)\ge(d-2)\log m-\log d \ge\log N-2\log N/d-d\log4-\log d, \] which proves \[ r_3(N)\ge N\exp(-C_0\sqrt{\log N}) \tag{3} \] for some absolute $C_0$. This is the classical sphere construction of Behrend, reconstructed here. In particular $r_3(N)$ cannot be $O(N^{1-\epsilon})$ for any fixed $\epsilon>0$. The lower bound $3$ is compatible with the requested upper bound; confusing these very different scales would give a false obstruction.

VERIFICATION

The no-carry base is $2m$, not $m$. All three vectors in the progression test lie on the same sphere. The Fourier coefficients are normalized by $p$, which is essential for the diagonal count $\alpha/p$. The batch checks exhaust the small sphere examples and compare the Fourier identity with direct progression counts.

FINAL STATUS

UNRESOLVED within this partial reconstruction; the requested bound is proved in the literature. The Fourier obstruction, Behrend construction, and deduction from $1$ are proved. The substantial missing step is a proof of $1$, or another density-increment estimate strong enough to beat every fixed logarithmic power. The existing theorem is explicitly attributed rather than relabelled as a new solution.
