Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.77

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is periodic if every element of $G$ has finite order. It is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite. For a subset $S\subseteq G$, define \[ C_G(S)=\{g\in G:gs=sg\text{ for every }s\in S\}, \qquad C_G(r)=C_G(\{r\}). \] An involution is an element of order $2$. An elementary abelian subgroup of order $4$ is a subgroup $R\cong C_2\times C_2$, where $C_2$ is the cyclic group of order $2$; its three nonidentity elements are involutions.

For a prime $p$, the quasicyclic group is \[ C_{p^\infty}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^\times:z^{p^n}=1\text{ for some integer }n\geq 0\}. \] A group $H$ is a Chernikov group if it has a normal subgroup of finite index isomorphic to \[ \prod_{i=1}^{k}C_{p_i^\infty}, \] where $k\geq 0$ is finite and each $p_i$ is prime, with repetitions permitted. The case $k=0$ includes finite groups.

Let $G$ be any periodic group containing an elementary abelian subgroup $R$ of order $4$. The following are two separate questions.

(a) Does \[ |C_G(R)|<\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ is locally finite} \] hold for every such pair $(G,R)$?

(b) Does \[ \left(\forall r\in R\setminus\{1\},\ C_G(r)\text{ is a Chernikov group}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ is locally finite} \] hold for every such pair $(G,R)$? No residual finiteness assumption is imposed in either general question.

Supplied historical qualification (remark of 1999). The source reports that P. V. Shumyatsky gave a positive answer to part (a) when $G$ is residually finite, citing the 1998 publication listed in Sources. Here residually finite means that for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$, there exist a finite group $F$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ with $\varphi(g)\neq 1$. This special-case result is recorded as supplied source information and has not been independently verified.

Short English statement

V. P. Shunkov asks whether a periodic group containing a subgroup $R\cong C_2\times C_2$ must be locally finite under either of two separate hypotheses: the common centralizer of $R$ is finite, or the centralizer of each of its three involutions is Chernikov. A supplied remark of 1999 reports a positive answer to part (a) for residually finite groups. The registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied curation, not an independently verified assessment.
