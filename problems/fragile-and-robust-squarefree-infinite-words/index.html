Math-7357

Fragile and Robust Squarefree Infinite Words

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A=\{0,1,2\}$. The source does not specify whether infinite words are one-sided or two-sided. Accordingly, let $I$ denote either $\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ or ${\mathbb{Z}}$, with the intended choice unresolved. A word $w=(w_i)_{i\in I}\in A^I$ is squarefree if it contains no contiguous factor $uu$, where $u$ is a nonempty finite word. Equivalently, there are no $i\in I$ and integer $m\geq1$, with $i,i+1,\ldots,i+2m-1\in I$, such that \[ w_{i+j}=w_{i+m+j}\qquad(0\leq j<m). \] For a nonempty finite set $F\subset I$ and a replacement assignment $a\in\prod_{i\in F}(A\setminus\{w_i\})$, define the modified word \[ w^{F,a}_i=\begin{cases}a_i,&i\in F,\\w_i,&i\notin F.\end{cases} \] This interprets a symbol flip as replacing a letter by either of the other two letters; the source does not specify a ternary flip operation.

Under this interpretation, a squarefree word $w$ is fragile if \[ \forall\,\varnothing\ne F\subset I\text{ finite},\quad \forall\,a\in\prod_{i\in F}(A\setminus\{w_i\}),\quad w^{F,a}\text{ is not squarefree}. \] A candidate precise interpretation of robust is that there exist nonempty finite sets $F_1,F_2,\ldots\subset I$ and assignments $a^{(k)}\in\prod_{i\in F_k}(A\setminus\{w_i\})$ such that \[ F_k\cap F_\ell=\varnothing\quad(k\ne\ell),\qquad w^{F_k,a^{(k)}}\text{ is squarefree for every }k\geq1. \] Here each modification is applied separately to the original word. Whether every replacement assignment must work, or whether combinations of these modifications must also preserve squarefreeness, is not specified.

Provisional existence questions. For the intended index set and flip convention, does there exist an infinite ternary squarefree fragile word? Separately, does there exist an infinite ternary squarefree robust word? These are provisional separate questions: the source's phrase "such words that are robust" could instead require the same word to be both fragile and robust, which is incompatible with the displayed definitions. The intended relationship between the two questions requires clarification.

Short English statement

The record asks about infinite squarefree words on three letters whose squarefreeness is destroyed by every finite nontrivial change, and about words admitting infinitely many changes on disjoint finite sets of positions that preserve squarefreeness. The indexing, meaning of a ternary flip, replacement quantifiers, and relationship between the two existence questions remain unresolved.
