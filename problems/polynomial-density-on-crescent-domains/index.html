Polynomial Density on Crescent Domains

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}$ be a domain described in the supplied source as a "crescent domain" or "crescent-type domain," with bounded complementary component $G$ and unbounded complementary component $\Omega_\infty$. The source record does not define the crescent condition or specify the boundary regularity it entails; that geometric qualification must therefore be retained pending clarification. Set \[ \delta(z)=\operatorname{dist}(z,\Omega_\infty) =\inf_{w\in\Omega_\infty}|z-w|. \] Let $\omega$ be the source's harmonic measure on $\partial G$. Its defining domain, side of the boundary, and pole are not specified in the supplied record.

Write $dA$ for planar area measure and, for $1\leq p<\infty$, define \[ L_a^p(\Omega,dA)=\left\{f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}:\ f\text{ is holomorphic and }\int_\Omega |f(z)|^p\,dA(z)<\infty\right\}. \] The source asks whether \[ \int_{\partial G}\log\delta(z)\,d\omega(z)=-\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H^p(\Omega,dA)=L_a^p(\Omega,dA) \quad\text{for every }1\leq p<\infty. \] Here $\log 0=-\infty$, and the integral condition is understood as an extended Lebesgue integral equal to $-\infty$, rather than an undefined difference of infinite quantities.

The supplied record does not define $H^p(\Omega,dA)$. Its title and literature assessment indicate that polynomial density is intended. To express that interpretation explicitly, put \[ \mathcal P_p(\Omega)=\{P|_\Omega:P\in{\mathbb{C}}[z],\ P|_\Omega\in L^p(\Omega,dA)\}. \] If the intended definition is \[ H^p(\Omega,dA)=\overline{\mathcal P_p(\Omega)}^{\,L^p(\Omega,dA)}, \] then the proposed conclusion says that, for every real $p$ with $1\leq p<\infty$, every $f\in L_a^p(\Omega,dA)$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a polynomial $P$ whose restriction belongs to $L^p(\Omega,dA)$ such that \[ \left(\int_\Omega |f(z)-P(z)|^p\,dA(z)\right)^{1/p}<\varepsilon. \] This interpretation of $H^p$ is conditional and requires confirmation from the source, together with the precise crescent-domain and harmonic-measure conventions.

Short English statement

The question asks whether divergence to $-\infty$ of the harmonic-measure integral of the logarithmic distance to the outer complementary component forces polynomial density in every finite-exponent Bergman space. The crescent geometry, harmonic measure, and notation $H^p$ require clarification before this interpretation is fully specified.
