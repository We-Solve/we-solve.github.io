Polyhedrality of Movable Cycle Cones on Toric Varieties

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a smooth toric variety, and write $n=\dim X$. The source does not specify the ground field or assume that $X$ is complete or projective. These omissions require clarification before fixing the numerical cycle space and the meaning of movability.

In the usual proper, smooth setting over a fixed field, let $N_d(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}$ denote the real vector space of algebraic $d$-cycles modulo numerical equivalence: a cycle is numerically trivial if its intersection with every cycle of complementary dimension has degree zero. Its rational structure is given by classes of cycles with rational coefficients. A customary interpretation of a movable integral $d$-dimensional subvariety is one occurring in an algebraic family of subvarieties whose union is Zariski dense in $X$. The source does not specify the precise family convention; this interpretation requires confirmation.

With the intended numerical equivalence and movability conventions fixed, define \[ \operatorname{Mov}_d(X) = \overline{\operatorname{cone}\left\{[V]: V\subseteq X\text{ is a movable integral subvariety of dimension }d\right\}} \subseteq N_d(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}, \] where $\operatorname{cone}$ consists of finite linear combinations with nonnegative real coefficients, and the closure is taken in the usual topology of the numerical cycle space. A cone is rational polyhedral if it is generated by finitely many rational numerical classes; equivalently, it has the form \[ \sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\alpha_i, \qquad \alpha_i\in N_d(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}. \]

Question as supplied. For every smooth toric variety $X$, is $\operatorname{Mov}_d(X)$ rational polyhedral for every relevant dimension $d$? The natural dimension range is $0\leq d\leq n$, including its endpoints, but the source states no explicit range or exclusions. No completeness, projectivity, or ground-field hypothesis has been added to the question. The definitions above describe a customary proper-setting interpretation, rather than resolving these missing hypotheses.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the closed cone of numerical classes of movable $d$-dimensional subvarieties of a smooth toric variety always has finitely many rational generators. The ground field, completeness or projectivity assumptions, dimension range, and precise movability convention need clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
