Certified Probability Landscapes for Stochastic Reaction Networks

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A probability distribution on a measurable state space $(S,\Sigma)$ is a countably additive measure $\mu:\Sigma\to[0,1]$ with $\mu(S)=1$. For an event $A\in\Sigma$, its probability is $\mu(A)$. These definitions provide notation only: the source does not specify the state space, reaction network, stochastic dynamics, initial conditions, or target distribution. In particular, it does not say whether the intended probability landscape is a transient distribution, a stationary distribution, a density, or a transformation of a distribution.

The supplied research problem asks, for stochastic chemical reaction networks, how to certify the accuracy of a computed probability landscape, determine the best attainable accuracy for laptop-scale and large-scale computation, and control errors including missed probability peaks and errors in rare-event probabilities.

A precise mathematical assertion cannot be extracted without further specifications. The source gives no class of admissible networks, computational approximation, error functional, tolerance, confidence requirement, or resource budget. It also supplies no definition of a probability peak, criterion for detecting all relevant peaks, or threshold defining a rare event. Absolute and relative errors in event probabilities are distinct possible targets, and the source does not select either. Consequently, the requested certification, optimal accuracy, and error control remain research objectives rather than a fully quantified theorem or conjecture. No additional hypotheses or quantifiers are imposed here.

Short English statement

The problem asks how to certify numerical probability landscapes for stochastic chemical reaction networks, compare attainable accuracy at laptop and larger computational scales, and control missed peaks and rare-event errors. The target landscape, error criterion, network class, and computational budgets are unspecified, so the supplied record does not determine a unique precise mathematical claim.
