Boundaries of Groups and Kleinian Groups — Problem 79

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A finitely generated Coxeter group has a presentation \[ G=\langle S:(st)^{m_{st}}=1\text{ whenever }m_{st}<\infty\rangle, \] where $S$ is finite, $m_{ss}=1$, and for $s\ne t$ the symmetric entries $m_{st}$ lie in $\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$. Assume $G$ is Gromov hyperbolic, meaning its Cayley graph has uniformly thin geodesic triangles.

Let $\mathbb H^n$ denote real hyperbolic $n$-space and $\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H^n)$ its full isometry group with its usual topology. A discrete embedding is an injective homomorphism \[ \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H^n) \] whose image is a discrete subgroup. The source asks whether every Gromov-hyperbolic Coxeter group admits such an embedding for sufficiently large finite $n$; in particular, is \[ \forall G\text{ as above}\quad\exists n<\infty\quad \exists\rho\text{ a discrete embedding} \] true? The dimension may depend on $G$; no uniform dimension bound is stated. No torsion-free, orientation-preserving, cocompact, or convex-cocompact condition is added.

The source does not explicitly identify whether $\mathbb H^n$ means real hyperbolic space or another hyperbolic model, although real hyperbolic space is the usual reading of this notation. The research summary's parenthetical description of a discrete embedding as having a "bounded orbit" is not adopted: boundedness of an orbit is not the definition of discreteness and is a different dynamical property.

Short English statement

Can every hyperbolic Coxeter group act faithfully as a discrete group of isometries of some finite-dimensional real hyperbolic space? A linear representation alone does not supply the rank-one isometry target and discreteness requested here.

The supplied summary reports linear reflection representations and mentions Šapirovskii and Monod--Cornulier, but gives no exact theorem matching the target. It labels the question partial progress/open. The source's historical problem status and registry assessment are retained with current status explicitly needing review.
