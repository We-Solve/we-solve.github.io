A two-gate effective reduction and a Diophantine torus model

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

G. Margulis's Question 16 asks for an effective version of \[ \frac1{\operatorname{Vol}(A_n)}\int_{A_n}f(vx)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} v\longrightarrow\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu, \qquad f\in C_c(G/\Gamma), \] where $A_n\subset V$, ${\,\mathrm{d}} v$ is Haar measure and $\mu$ probability Haar measure. The family is called "good" without a definition in the extraction. The intended group, orbit and nonescape hypotheses and a regularity norm controlling an error are also missing. Positive finite averaging volume is required; volume growth or a Følner boundary condition alone is not a claimed sufficient quantitative hypothesis.

The aid's precise smoothing reduction is conditional. On a $D$-dimensional smooth probability space, put $g_T=A_Tf-\mu(f)$. Suppose \[ \|g_T\|_2\leq E_TS(f),\qquad |g_T(y)-g_T(x)|\leq K_TS(f)\,d(x,y) \] near $x$, and there are normalized nonnegative local smoothing densities supported at scale $\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_x$ with $L^2$ norm at most $B_x\varepsilon^{-D/2}$. Then \[ |A_Tf(x)-\mu(f)|\leq S(f)\left(K_T\varepsilon+B_xE_T\varepsilon^{-D/2}\right). \] For $K_T>0$, if $\varepsilon=(B_xE_T/K_T)^{2/(D+2)}$ is locally admissible, this gives \[ |A_Tf(x)-\mu(f)|\leq 2S(f)(B_xE_T)^{2/(D+2)}K_T^{D/(D+2)}. \] The $L^2$ rate and pointwise regularity/local-geometry conditions are separate inputs; no rate follows from $E_T$ alone without controlling the other factors.

The aid's exact model is continuous translation on ${\mathbb{R}}^d/{\mathbb{Z}}^d$: \[ A_Tf(x)=T^{-1}\int_0^T f(x+t\omega)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] Assume an absolute linear-form bound $|k\cdot\omega|\geq c\|k\|^{-\tau}$ for every nonzero $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^d$. For integer $s>d+\tau$, the reported smooth-observable conclusion is a uniform-in-$x$ estimate \[ |A_Tf(x)-\textstyle\int f|\leq C T^{-1}\|f\|_{C^s}. \] Constants depend on the stated Diophantine and regularity data. This continuous-time hypothesis is not distance of $k\cdot\omega$ to the integers. Conversely the aid constructs a fixed dense Liouville direction for which no polynomial $C^s$ operator-norm rate holds: for each fixed finite $s$, positive exponent $\eta$ and proposed constant, suitably chosen smooth observables and unbounded times violate a bound by $T^{-\eta}\|f\|_{C^s}$. This does not deny qualitative unique ergodicity or say every fixed observable has slow convergence. All these flows average over the same intervals, so averaging volume and boundary alone cannot replace Diophantine information.

The extraction continues into the separate Section 4.3. It introduces $L$, a closed subgroup $G$, lattice $\Lambda$, semisimple $a\in G$ and $U=\{u\in G:a^{-n}ua^n\to e\}$. It reports Haar equidistribution of $a^n$-translates of an absolutely-continuous-$U$ orbit probability when $Gx_0$ is dense, under the surrounding unstated homogeneous-dynamics hypotheses, citing [177]. It then proposes an analytic-curve refinement with $\gamma:[0,1]\to U$ and pushed-forward Lebesgue measure. These are next-subsection data, not hypotheses completing Question 16; no unqualified equidistribution theorem for every analytic curve is asserted. The page marker and integer-part footnote are extraction spill.

Short English statement

The effective equidistribution question needs quantitative orbit and test-function hypotheses. The supplied aid separates smoothing and mean-ergodic inputs and uses torus flows to show why averaging-set geometry alone cannot give uniform polynomial rates.
