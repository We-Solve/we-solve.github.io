Conjecture 7.5 — For non-vanishing $\tau_r^G(M)$, the absolute value $|\tau_r^G(M)|$ depends only on the fundamental group $\pi_1(M)$.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\tau_r^G(M)\in{\mathbb{C}}$ be the source's quantum 3-manifold invariant for fixed admissible group and level data $(G,r)$, with its specified normalization and complex root conventions. Conjecture 7.5 asserts that on the nonzero locus its complex absolute value depends only on the abstract fundamental group.

Precisely, the natural pairwise formulation is: for manifolds $M,N$ in the intended domain, \[ \pi_1(M)\cong\pi_1(N),\qquad \tau_r^G(M)\ne0,\quad\tau_r^G(N)\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\tau_r^G(M)|=|\tau_r^G(N)|. \] The same $G,r$, normalization and embedding into ${\mathbb{C}}$ are used on both sides. Equivalently, the nonzero moduli factor through the group isomorphism class. The assertion does not claim that the complex phase is determined, that all levels or all groups give the same value, or that vanishing itself is a fundamental-group invariant.

The excerpt does not define $\tau_r^G$, the allowed $G,r$, or the manifold and auxiliary-structure category. The conventional closed-oriented 3-manifold quantum-invariant setting is suggested by the source context but is not fully stated here. These definitions and normalizations must be recovered before a concrete comparison can be made.

Short English statement

For fixed quantum-invariant parameters, do two manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups have equal absolute invariant values whenever both values are nonzero? The nonvanishing restriction does not itself compare zero with nonzero values.
