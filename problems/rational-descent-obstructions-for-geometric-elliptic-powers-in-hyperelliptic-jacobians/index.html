Math-1281

Rational descent obstructions for geometric elliptic powers in hyperelliptic Jacobians

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a separable polynomial $g(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t]$ of degree at least three, let $C$ be the smooth projective hyperelliptic curve associated to $s^2=g(t)$, and let $\operatorname{Jac}(C)$ be its Jacobian. An isogeny over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ is a surjective homomorphism of abelian varieties over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with finite kernel. Silverberg's question asks for such a $g$, an elliptic curve $E/{\mathbb{Q}}$, an abelian variety $B/{\mathbb{Q}}$, and an isogeny \[ \operatorname{Jac}(C)\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}E^r\times B, \qquad r\ge4\quad\text{or}\quad(r=3\text{ and }\dim B\le1). \] The trivial abelian variety is allowed when $\dim B=0$. There is no dimension bound on $B$ in the $r\ge4$ alternative.

Ellenberg's related question asks for a map ${\mathbb{P}}^1\to E^4/\{\pm1\}$, with simultaneous inversion on all four factors, whose image is contained in no $H/\{\pm1\}$ for a proper subgroup $H\subsetneq E^4$. For precision a geometric version should specify a nonconstant morphism, algebraic subgroups, and the ground field/field of definition of $H$. Those conventions are not explicit in the source; the surrounding rational construction suggests ${\mathbb{Q}}$, while a separate remark explicitly asks about ${\mathbb{C}}$. The later report claims a Jacobian--Kummer equivalence between a surjection $\operatorname{Jac}(C)\to E^r$ and a nondegenerate rational curve on $E^r/\{\pm1\}$, under nonconstancy and field conventions not supplied here. No unconditional equivalence with all descent and basepoint issues suppressed is asserted in this draft.

The historical remarks report that $(r,\dim B)=(2,0)$ and $(3,2)$ are possible; that such constructions would improve lower bounds for the density of quadratic twists of $E$ of rank at least $r$; and that over ${\mathbb{C}}$, $(3,0)$ is possible, while large $r$ might be impossible. They suggest composite-exponent curves $s^2=t^k+1$, including the map toward $s^2=t^3+1$ when $3\mid k$, and insist that hyperellipticity is needed for the applications. A random-matrix heuristic is said to suggest a positive-power density bound, without a specified exponent. Another proposed route is to start with pairwise ${\mathbb{Q}}$-isogenous elliptic curves $E_1,E_2,E_3$, seek a nonsplit principal polarization on $A=E_1\times E_2\times E_3$, and then seek a genus-three hyperelliptic Jacobian realization. This is a proposed route, not a verified descent or hyperellipticity theorem.

The supplied partial-result report analyzes \[ C_0:y^2=x^8+14x^4+1,\quad E:Y^2=X^3-5616X+120960,\quad E^{(-1)}:Y^2=X^3-5616X-120960. \] It claims the exact arithmetic decomposition \[ \operatorname{Jac}(C_0)\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}E^2\times E^{(-1)}, \] from three ${\mathbb{Q}}$-defined involution quotients. At the common good prime eleven it gives Frobenius traces $a_{11}(E)=4$, $a_{11}(E^{(-1)})=-4$, where $a_p(E)=p+1-\#E({\mathbb{F}}_p)$, certifying that these factors are not ${\mathbb{Q}}$-isogenous. Thus this geometric elliptic-cube example is not a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-elliptic cube. A separate prime-three trace obstruction is reported for a standard genus-five geometric fifth-power example and a displayed $E_5^5$, but neither that curve nor $E_5$ appears in this record. These descent obstructions do not supply the requested ${\mathbb{Q}}$-example or rule out all others.

Short English statement

Find a hyperelliptic Jacobian over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with a large repeated elliptic factor, or a related nondegenerate rational curve on a Kummer quotient. The supplied report shows that a familiar geometric elliptic cube decomposes into nonisogenous quadratic-twist factors over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, illustrating a descent obstruction rather than solving the construction problem.
