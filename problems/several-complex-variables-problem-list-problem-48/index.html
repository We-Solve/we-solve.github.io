Several Complex Variables Problem List — Problem 48

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard interpretation of a complete hyperbolic manifold as a connected complex manifold whose Kobayashi distance is a genuine complete metric. The source does not explicitly define this terminology or prescribe a complex dimension.

Let $\Delta=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$, equipped with the Poincaré distance \[ \rho_\Delta(a,b)=\frac12\log\frac{1+\left|\frac{a-b}{1-\overline b a}\right|}{1-\left|\frac{a-b}{1-\overline b a}\right|}. \] For complex manifolds $Y,Z$, write $\operatorname{Hol}(Y,Z)$ for the set of holomorphic maps from $Y$ to $Z$. For a connected complex manifold $X$, its Carathéodory pseudodistance is \[ c_X(p,q)=\sup_{f\in\operatorname{Hol}(X,\Delta)}\rho_\Delta(f(p),f(q)). \] Its Kobayashi pseudodistance is \[ k_X(p,q)=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\rho_\Delta(a_j,b_j): \begin{array}{l} m\geq1,\quad a_j,b_j\in\Delta,\quad f_j\in\operatorname{Hol}(\Delta,X),\\ f_1(a_1)=p,\quad f_m(b_m)=q,\\ f_j(b_j)=f_{j+1}(a_{j+1})\quad(1\leq j<m) \end{array}\right\}. \] Completeness means that every $k_X$-Cauchy sequence converges in the metric $k_X$. A convex domain in ${\mathbb{C}}^n$ means a nonempty open convex subset of ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, with convexity taken in the underlying real vector space.

Problem (M. Abate; Problem 48 in the supplied 1995 list). Determine whether there exist an integer $n\geq1$ and a connected complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n$ such that

  1. $k_X$ is a genuine complete metric;
  2. $k_X(p,q)=c_X(p,q)$ for every $p,q\in X$;
  3. there is no convex domain $D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ biholomorphic to $X$.
Construct such a manifold or prove that none exists. The conventions above make explicit the standard reading of the source; the source itself supplies no further hypotheses.

Short English statement

Does there exist a complete Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold on which the Kobayashi and Carathéodory distances agree for every pair of points, but which is not biholomorphic to any convex domain? The dimension is unrestricted. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
