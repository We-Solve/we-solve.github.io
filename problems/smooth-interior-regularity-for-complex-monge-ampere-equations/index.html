Smooth Interior Regularity for Complex Monge–Ampère Equations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A domain is a connected open set. A bounded domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, with $n\geq 1$, is hyperconvex if it admits a continuous negative plurisubharmonic exhaustion $\rho\colon\Omega\to(-\infty,0)$: for every $c<0$, the set $\{z\in\Omega:\rho(z)<c\}$ is relatively compact in $\Omega$. A function is plurisubharmonic if it is upper semicontinuous and its restriction to each complex line is subharmonic wherever defined. Let $d\lambda$ denote Lebesgue measure on ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, and let $dd^c$ denote the complex differential operator used to form the complex Monge--Ampère operator; its normalization is not specified in the supplied record.

The supplied question asks whether the following implication holds for every bounded hyperconvex domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, every $f\in C^\infty(\Omega)$ with $f(z)>0$ for every $z\in\Omega$, and every solution $u$ of the indicated Dirichlet problem: \[ \left. \begin{aligned} (dd^c u)^n&=f\,d\lambda &&\text{in }\Omega,\\ u&=0 &&\text{on }\partial\Omega \end{aligned} \right\} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\in C^\infty(\Omega). \] Only interior smoothness is requested. The record imposes no smoothness of $\partial\Omega$, no extension of $f$ to the boundary, and no stated global integrability or boundedness condition on $f$.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The record does not specify the admissible class of solutions, explicitly require $u$ to be plurisubharmonic, define the weak meaning of $(dd^c u)^n$, or explain how the zero boundary condition is attained. For example, a locally bounded plurisubharmonic solution with its Monge--Ampère measure interpreted in the Bedford--Taylor sense is a possible interpretation, but those hypotheses are not supplied and are not adopted here. Consequently, the displayed implication preserves the source question but requires clarification of its solution and boundary notions before it is a fully specified conjecture. The background's reference to arbitrary bounded domains is broader than the explicit hyperconvex-domain hypothesis retained above.

Short English statement

Does a solution of the complex Monge--Ampère equation on a bounded hyperconvex domain, with zero boundary values and a strictly positive smooth interior density, necessarily become smooth throughout the interior? The intended weak solution class and meaning of the boundary values remain unspecified.
