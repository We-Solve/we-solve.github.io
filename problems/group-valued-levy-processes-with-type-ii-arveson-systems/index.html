Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A group-valued Lévy process starts at the identity, has stationary independent increments interpreted through group multiplication, and is stochastically continuous in the chosen group topology. Increment probability spaces give a continuous product of Hilbert spaces. A unit is a multiplicative measurable section; the associated Arveson system is type I when generated by units and type II when it has units whose generated subsystem is proper. A homeomorphism group uses its specified topology, so the group and topology are part of the model.
Source scope and qualifications.
Freshly consulted primary publication evidence gives a positive answer to the broad group-valued existence question: Floricel--Wadel construct type-II_infinity systems from infinite-dimensional Banach-space-valued pure-jump Lévy processes, and an additive Banach space is a topological group. This does not answer the narrower suggested homeomorphism-group example. The saved partially_solved status is retained as provenance rather than changed.

A finite-rate Poisson candidate gives type I.
Let $N_t$ be a rate-$\lambda>0$ Poisson process valued in the additive group $\mathbb Z$, and take the Hilbert space of its increment sigma-field on $(0,t)$. For every real $\theta$, the section $u_t^\theta=e^{i\theta N_t}$ has norm one and multiplies under independent increments. Products of these units on a partition yield all functions $\exp(i\sum_j\theta_j\Delta_jN)$ of the interval counts.
Totality of the generated vectors.
If $F$ is orthogonal to every such function for a fixed partition, then its conditional expectation given the count vector has zero discrete Fourier transform. The coefficients are absolutely summable by Cauchy--Schwarz against the count probabilities. Multiplying by the opposite character and integrating each angle over $[0,2\pi]$ recovers each coefficient, so the conditional expectation is zero. Rational-time partitions generate the Poisson path sigma-field because the paths are right-continuous step functions. Approximation by functions measurable on successively finer finite partitions then implies $F=0$. Hence products of units are total and the associated system is type I.
