Math-5098

An explicit cubic point and its descent certificate

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $g(t)=3(t^4-54t^2-117t-243)$, and let $S$ be the intended smooth projective model over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ of the affine surface \[y^2=g(t)(x^2+1),\qquad z^2=g(t)(x^2+2).\] The source describes this as a one-parameter genus-one family and attributes to Skorobogatov $S({\mathbb{Q}})=\varnothing$ but $S({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})^{\operatorname{Br}}\ne\varnothing$. The Brauer--Manin set consists of adelic points orthogonal to the Brauer group under the sum of local invariants.

Original question (Colliot-Thélène). Is $S(k)\ne\varnothing$ for some number field $k/{\mathbb{Q}}$ of odd degree? The original remarks motivate this by existence of a zero-cycle of degree one and assert the converse implication for this example. A zero-cycle is an integral formal sum of closed points, with degree the corresponding sum of residue degrees; it need not be effective and does not itself give a rational point. The implication from an odd-degree point to degree-one index uses additional coprime-degree cycle information for this particular surface, not a general principle for all varieties.

The remarks say random searching even in a fixed cubic field is unlikely to find a point; propose a quotient of $S\times S\times S$ with an unspecified "action by three" (Ellenberg); suggest fixing $x$ to obtain $y^2=g(t)(x^2+1)$ and then searching for $z$ (Voloch); and observe that $(y/z)^2=(x^2+1)/(x^2+2)$ wherever defined (Poonen). McCallum asks whether degree three should suffice, and Colliot-Thélène says not necessarily. Poonen proposes instead finding a cubic point on $w^2=(x^2+1)(x^2+2)$, putting it in Weierstrass form and searching for $t$.

Attached unreviewed solution attribution. The aid credits Brendan Creutz with an affirmative solution in 2017: the surface has a point over $L={\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)$, where $\theta^3+\theta+1=0$, so degree three does suffice. It says it translates and exactly verifies Creutz's published point in the AIM convention, but the full $(x,t,y,z)$ coordinates are not in this supplied summary. The original negative expectations are historical, not a denial of this later claimed result.

The aid supplies a descent bridge from \[D_0:\quad w^2=(x^2+1)(x^2+2)\] to $E:v^2=u^3-6u^2+u$ by \[u=2w+2x^2+3,\qquad v=2xu.\] On the associated untwisted cover $Y^2=x^2+1$, $Z^2=x^2+2$, $w=YZ$, the square root of $u$ is $Y+Z$. The aid also lists a low-height twist certificate \[a=9-4\theta+6\theta^2,\quad U_0=2133-1224\theta+1494\theta^2,\quad Y=\theta^2,\quad Z=1-\theta^2,\quad N_{L/{\mathbb{Q}}}(a)=1,\] and says the Weierstrass coordinate is $u=a$. The twist model, role of $U_0$ and map to the AIM coordinates are absent. These displayed $Y,Z$ have sum one, so they cannot be substituted into the untwisted formula $u=(Y+Z)^2$ to obtain $a$ without the missing twist normalization. The two coordinate conventions are therefore kept separate. The full solution and cubic point remain credited to Creutz; no independent verification or new solution is claimed.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether a specified Brauer--Manin counterexample surface acquires a point over an odd-degree field. The aid credits Creutz's 2017 cubic-field solution and gives a descent bridge and twist data. The full point and twist normalization are missing, so the listed coordinates are not silently combined into an unsupported explicit point.
