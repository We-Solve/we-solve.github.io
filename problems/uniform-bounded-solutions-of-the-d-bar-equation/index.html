Math-15803

Uniform Bounded Solutions of the d-Bar Equation

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer and let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ be a domain, meaning a nonempty connected open set. For a precise weak formulation, write a bounded $(0,1)$-form as \[ f=\sum_{j=1}^n f_j\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\bar z_j,\qquad f_j\in L^\infty(\Omega), \] and set \[ \|f\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{z\in\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^n|f_j(z)|^2\right)^{1/2}. \] Interpret $\bar\partial f=0$ distributionally, that is, \[ \frac{\partial f_j}{\partial\bar z_k}=\frac{\partial f_k}{\partial\bar z_j}\quad\text{in distributions on }\Omega\quad(1\leq j,k\leq n). \] For a fixed domain $\Omega$, the uniform bounded solvability property asked about is \[ \begin{gathered} \exists\,C_\Omega\in(0,\infty)\ \ \forall\,f\in L^\infty_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\text{ with }\bar\partial f=0,\\ \exists\,u\in L^\infty(\Omega):\qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial\bar z_j}=f_j\ \text{in distributions }(1\leq j\leq n),\qquad \|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\leq C_\Omega\|f\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}. \end{gathered} \] Thus the constant is independent of $f$; neither uniqueness nor a linear choice of solution is requested. No boundary values are prescribed.

Unresolved scope. The supplied statement asks whether this property holds for a domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, without imposing boundedness of $\Omega$, pseudoconvexity, boundary regularity, finite type, or a restriction on $n$. Here "bounded" explicitly modifies the form $f$. However, the supplied background describes the intended general problem as concerning arbitrary pseudoconvex finite-type domains in ${\mathbb{C}}^n$ for $n\geq3$. These formulations have different hypotheses. The record does not specify the intended boundary regularity or meaning of finite type, or whether the domains in that narrower formulation must be bounded. Consequently, the display specifies the analytic property precisely, but the class of domains over which it is being asked remains unresolved. The measurable-coefficient and distributional formulation above is an explicit convention; the source does not specify the regularity of $f$ or the sense of either equation.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every bounded, $\bar\partial$-closed $(0,1)$-form on the intended domain has a bounded solution of $\bar\partial u=f$, with one estimate constant depending only on the domain. The statement leaves the domain unrestricted, while the background points to pseudoconvex finite-type domains in dimension at least three. The intended hypotheses and solution regularity require review.
