Universal-Cover Isomorphism Algorithms for Cube Complexes

Universal-Cover Isomorphism Algorithms for Cube Complexes

Claim type: **Partial result**.

Exact scope: For supplied Cartesian products of finite connected simple regular graphs, cubical universal-cover isomorphism is decided by equality of the multisets of positive factor valences, in polynomial time in the factor encoding.

Product covers, including higher dimensions

Let X=G₁×⋯×G_r be the standard cubical Cartesian product, where each supplied factor is a finite connected simple d_i-regular graph. A one-vertex factor has d_i=0 and is omitted. The remaining d_i are positive; r can grow and need not be one. Each graph's universal cover is the d_i-regular tree T_{d_i}: for d_i=1 this is K₂, for d_i=2 the doubly infinite path, and for d_i≥3 the infinite regular tree. One can construct it as the tree of reduced walks from a chosen graph vertex. Each walk has d_i available neighboring walks after cancellation, and path lifting proves the local covering property. Trees are simply connected, so this is the universal cover.

The product map ∏T_{d_i}→∏G_i is a cubical covering: the local incident directions and their commuting cubes are exactly the products of those in the factors. The product of trees is contractible, by contracting each factor along its unique paths; in particular it is simply connected. Thus it is the cubical universal cover of X. The complex is nonpositively curved in the cubical sense: each vertex link is the join of discrete direction sets, hence flag. This uses the actual cubes of the product, not just the one-skeleton.

A complete invariant in this supplied product class

At any vertex of the universal cover, its link's one-skeleton is a complete multipartite graph with part sizes d₁,…,d_r: directions in different factors span a square, and distinct directions in the same tree do not. Its complement is therefore a disjoint union of complete graphs of those sizes, including isolated vertices for d_i=1. A cubical isomorphism preserves this link and hence its complement's connected-component sizes. Equality of the valence multisets is necessary.

It is sufficient as well. Match equal-valence factors, choose arbitrary roots, and recursively biject their unused neighbors. Unique tree paths make these bijections consistent, yielding an isomorphism T_d≅T_d for each matched pair. Their product, followed by the matching permutation of factors, is a cubical isomorphism. If all factors were points, the cover is a point and is treated separately. This also explains why finite d=1 factors cannot be silently replaced by infinite lines.

To decide, verify each supplied graph is connected and regular by a traversal and degree count, discard point factors, sort their positive valences and compare. This is polynomial in the encoded factor-list size, including vertex/edge labels. It does not require expanding a potentially exponentially large product or writing an infinite cover isomorphism; the output is the decision plus its finite invariant. If the entire product is instead explicitly supplied without a factorization, this report does not claim the same bound for discovering factors. Arbitrary finite NPC cube complexes remain outside the subclass.

Earlier contributions and limits

The earlier attempt gives directed-edge color refinement for one-dimensional complexes and a caution involving a filled square. The supplied higher-dimensional regular-product class and its link-complement classification add genuine cubical information; the result is not a restatement of the one-dimensional test.

Research attempt: A finite graph-cover algorithm and the missing cube data

First let $G,H$ be finite connected simple graphs. The universal cover of a graph, rooted over a vertex $r$, is the tree of finite nonbacktracking walks starting at $r$: an edge either appends one step without immediately reversing the last step or removes the last step. Each vertex other than the root has one predecessor, so this graph is connected and has no cycles; projection to the walk endpoint gives a local bijection of incident edges, hence a covering tree.

Construct a finite state system from all directed edges $(u,v)$ of $G$ and $H$, together with a root state for one fixed vertex $r$ of $G$ and root states for every vertex of $H$. A directed-edge state $(u,v)$ has successors $(v,w)$ for all neighbors $w\ne u$. A root state at $v$ has successors $(v,w)$ for every neighbor. These successor trees are exactly the forward branches of the rooted covering trees. Let $D$ be the total number of states; $D\geq1$ includes isolated one-vertex graphs.

Initially give all states the same color. Repeatedly assign a common canonical new color to the pair consisting of the old color and the multiset of successor colors. Two states have equal colors after $t$ rounds if and only if their rooted successor trees truncated at depth $t$ are isomorphic. The proof is induction: at the next depth, rooted trees are isomorphic precisely when their child subtrees can be paired by equal isomorphism types. Retaining the old color is redundant for this characterization but makes refinement explicit.

Every strict refinement increases the number of color classes, at most $D$, so stability occurs after at most $D$ rounds. At stability, equally colored states have equal successor-color multisets. Choose a bijection pairing their successors by color, then do the same recursively at each paired descendant. These choices give compatible isomorphisms at every depth, whose union is an isomorphism of the infinite rooted trees. Conversely a rooted tree isomorphism preserves every finite-depth color. Thus stable equality is equivalent to rooted universal-cover isomorphism.

Finally compare the fixed root state of $G$ with every root state of $H$. An unrooted cover isomorphism sends the chosen lift of $r$ to a lift of some vertex $v$ of $H$, producing one of these rooted comparisons. The covering tree rooted at any lift of $v$ has the same nonbacktracking-walk description, by unique lifting of paths. Conversely a successful rooted comparison is already an unrooted isomorphism. This proves a finite decision algorithm for this graph subclass. With at most $D$ successors per state, sorting and comparing signatures each round gives a coarse bound $O(D^3\log(D+1))$ elementary comparisons.

Why the one-skeleton algorithm cannot solve the cube problem.
Take the boundary of a square as a one-dimensional cube complex, and the filled square as a two-dimensional cube complex. Their one-skeleta are identical four-cycles. Both are nonpositively curved in the link sense: at a boundary vertex of the one-dimensional complex the link is two isolated vertices, a flag complex; at a vertex of the filled square the link is a single edge, also flag. The boundary has universal cover an infinite path, as is seen by unwinding successive edges. The filled square is contractible by linear contraction to its center, hence is its own universal cover. These covers are not isomorphic, even in dimension. Any algorithm discarding the square cell sees the same input in both cases and cannot distinguish them. A general procedure must incorporate cell incidence and its consequences for lifted paths, beyond this finite directed-edge refinement.
