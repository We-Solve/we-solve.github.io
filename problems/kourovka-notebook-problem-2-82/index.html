Kourovka Notebook Problem 2.82

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, use the commutator convention $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ and define left-normed iterated commutators by \[ [x,{}_{1}y]=[x,y],\qquad [x,{}_{k+1}y]=[[x,{}_{k}y],y]\quad(k\geq 1). \] For an integer $n\geq 1$, let $\mathcal E_n$ be the class of groups satisfying the $n$th Engel identity: \[ G\in\mathcal E_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\forall x,y\in G)\;[x,{}_{n}y]=1. \] A positive word is a word formed by multiplication of variables without inverse letters. A positive identity is an equation $u=v$ between such words, required to hold under every assignment of its variables to elements of the group. One may require the words to be nonempty: if an empty word occurs, appending a fresh variable to both sides gives an equivalent identity in groups.

The problem asks whether, for every integer $n\geq 1$, there exists a family $\Sigma_n$ of positive identities such that, for every group $G$, \[ G\in\mathcal E_n \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\models\Sigma_n. \] Here $G\models\Sigma_n$ means that every identity in $\Sigma_n$ holds for every assignment of its variables in $G$. The family may depend on $n$, but must work uniformly for all groups. The source does not specify whether a single identity or a finite family is required; the displayed formulation permits an arbitrary family and leaves any stronger finiteness requirement unresolved.

The supplied statement attributes affirmative results for $n=1,2,3$ to A. I. Shirshov. The supplied editors' comment attributes an affirmative result for $n=4$ to G. Traustason. These are source-reported results, not independently verified here.

The editors' comment also records O. Macedońska's observation concerning locally graded $n$-Engel groups. A group is locally graded if every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup has a nontrivial finite quotient. A group is locally nilpotent if every finitely generated subgroup is nilpotent. According to the supplied comment, a locally graded $n$-Engel group is locally nilpotent by a result of Y. Kim and A. Rhemtulla, and then, by a result of R. G. Burns and Yu. Medvedev, has a normal nilpotent subgroup whose nilpotency class is bounded in terms of $n$ alone, with quotient of exponent bounded in terms of $n$ alone. The comment invokes a classical result of Mal'cev to conclude that such groups satisfy a positive law.

The precise scope of this last assertion is unresolved in the supplied record: satisfying a positive law does not by itself state that positive identities characterize the Engel condition. The comment does not explicitly supply an equivalence relative to locally graded groups, a common defining family, or the intended uniformity of the positive law. Its assertion that the result is also true for the locally graded class is therefore retained as a source qualification, without treating it as a precise additional equational characterization.

Short English statement

For each positive integer $n$, ask whether the groups satisfying $[x,{}_{n}y]=1$ can be characterized, among all groups, by identities involving only products of variables and no inverse letters. The supplied source reports affirmative answers for $n\leq 4$. Its additional comment about locally graded groups requires clarification about characterization and uniformity. The registry labels the problem open; this label and the reported results have not been independently verified.
