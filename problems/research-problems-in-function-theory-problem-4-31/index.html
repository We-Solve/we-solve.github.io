Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Consider $f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^na_kz^k$ with $|a_k|=1$. The problem asks about uniform flatness on the unit circle, including whether the upper bound $\sqrt n+O(n^{3/10+\varepsilon})$ can be sharpened to $\sqrt n+O(n^\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. It also asks whether the minimum modulus can exceed $(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt n$ for sufficiently large $n$, whether both minimum and maximum can lie between $(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt n$ and $(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt n$, and whether an $O(1)$ error can replace those relative errors. Further versions ask for a uniform gap above $\sqrt n$ when the coefficients are signs, either at consecutive exponents or at arbitrary distinct positive integer exponents.

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

OpenAI claim summary for Math-2282

The Littlewood maximum results rule out a fixed positive gap above the square-root scale for consecutive real-sign polynomials. Those polynomials also have distinct exponents, so they refute the universal-gap version allowing arbitrary distinct positive exponents after multiplication by $z$. The ultraflat theorem additionally supplies the requested relative two-sided modulus bounds. The quantitative $O(n^{\varepsilon})$ and $O(1)$ error improvements are not supplied by these asymptotic results, so the full bundle retains a partial claim.

Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.31

OpenAI's claim

Nearly optimal Littlewood maxima: The optimal supremum norm of a real sign polynomial of length $N$ is claimed to approach $\sqrt N$ relatively, with $N$ running through every integer; the resulting binary sequences have merit factors tending to infinity.

Littlewood polynomials with a lower margin: The paper claims sign polynomials at every sufficiently large length with maximum at most $(1+\eta)\sqrt N$ and minimum at least $\sqrt N/16$, for any fixed positive $\eta$; both inequalities concern the same polynomial.

Uniformly flat real sign polynomials: OpenAI claims that real sign polynomials can have normalized modulus uniformly as close to one as desired once the integer length is large enough; choices at different lengths are independent of one another.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For each $\eta>0$, all sufficiently large integers $N$ admit signs $\epsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}$ such that the real Littlewood polynomial $P_N(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\epsilon_kz^k$ obeys \[\max_{|z|=1}|P_N(z)|\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N.\] Thus the minimum possible normalized maximum $m_N=\min_{\epsilon_k=\pm1}\max_{|z|=1}|P_N(z)|/\sqrt N$ tends to $1$. This result gives an upper maximum, without a pointwise lower bound.
Mathematical claim 2.
Given $\eta>0$, there is $N_0(\eta)$ such that for every integer $N\ge N_0(\eta)$ one can choose real signs $\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{N-1}$ with \[\frac{\sqrt N}{16}\le\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\epsilon_kz^k\right|\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N\quad(|z|=1).\] The same polynomial has both bounds uniformly on the circle; the lower constant is $1/16$.
Mathematical claim 3.
For every $0<\epsilon<1$, all sufficiently large integers $N$ admit signs $a_k\in\{-1,1\}$ such that \[(1-\epsilon)\sqrt N\le\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}a_kz^k\right|\le(1+\epsilon)\sqrt N\quad\text{for all }|z|=1.\] The quantifier covers every sufficiently large length, and both inequalities refer to one polynomial.

Outline of the argument

Nearly optimal Littlewood maxima: The construction starts with bounded real coefficients obtained by sampling quadratic-phase waves on an auxiliary torus. Interval packing separates the relevant frequency ranges and keeps almost all of the mean-square mass. A discrepancy rounding step changes the coefficients to signs while keeping the circle supremum error small compared with $\sqrt N$; Parseval’s identity then supplies the lower comparison.

Littlewood polynomials with a lower margin: The relaxed-coefficient construction for small maxima is augmented on arcs where its leading amplitude is too small. Carefully matched quadratic phases create a positive lower margin without introducing excessive Fourier coefficients outside the permitted degree range. A rounding estimate expressed in terms of the remaining coefficient defect preserves the margin while turning the coefficients into signs.

Uniformly flat real sign polynomials: A continuous circle function is built with nearly constant modulus, real coefficients of nearly sign size in the retained frequency range, and a small Fourier tail. Auxiliary-torus waves and phase corrections coordinate the upper and lower modulus bounds. Parseval controls the aggregate coefficient defect, allowing discrepancy rounding to preserve uniform flatness.
