Math-237

Bounded-degree decisions and local-density support for rational curves on quartic K3 surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ be a smooth quartic K3 surface and $H$ a height for its hyperplane polarization. A geometrically rational curve means a geometrically integral curve whose normalization over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ has genus zero. Being defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, having normalization ${\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$, and having a rational branch over a prescribed $x\in X({\mathbb{Q}})$ are different requirements. The source's phrase "rational curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$" does not resolve them.

Original Problem 10 and its attributed questions. Peyre takes $U$ to be the complement of all rational curves over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, assumes $U({\mathbb{Q}})\ne\varnothing$, and reports numerical evidence for \[\#\{u\in U({\mathbb{Q}}):H(u)\le B\}\sim c(\log B)^{\rho(X)},\] where $\rho(X)$ is the rank of the Picard group of $X$ and $c$ a product of local densities. Can such a scalar formula hold without weak approximation? This $U$ is a set-theoretic complement, not necessarily a Zariski open. The height, Picard-field convention and local-density normalization are not specified.

Tschinkel asks for a procedure deciding whether a given rational point of a quartic K3 lies on a rational curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. Skorobogatov asks about also removing elliptic curves. Browning asks for numerical evidence, referring to van Luijk, and asks for the Peyre freedom of small-degree rational curves on K3 surfaces. Colliot-Thélène asks whether a K3 surface over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ with a rational point has any rational curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and whether one can find such a curve containing the prescribed point. Tschinkel asks the analogous existence and prescribed-point questions for $X/{\mathbb{F}}_q$, with curves defined over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$. The source reports that Bogomolov--Tschinkel obtain rational curves for Kummer surfaces over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ for "most" points, without defining that qualification. Skorobogatov and Testa ask whether a K3 surface over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ can have infinitely many rational curves over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ while $\operatorname{Pic}(X_{{\mathbb{C}}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}$.

Attached unreviewed bounded-degree claim. For an explicit smooth quartic $X/{\mathbb{Q}}$, a point $x\in X({\mathbb{Q}})$ and integer $D\ge1$, the aid claims a terminating Hilbert-scheme procedure listing the ${\mathbb{Q}}$-defined geometrically integral geometrically rational curves through $x$ of projective degree at most $D$. It separately decides descent of the Hilbert point, geometric genus zero, whether the genus-zero normalization is ${\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$, and whether that normalization has a rational point above $x$. It says a finite-field version detects Frobenius descent. A terminating answer for each fixed $D$ does not by itself decide the original unbounded-degree problem or termination of the search for a negative answer.

The aid further reports that a localized counting law with full support forces weak approximation. Precisely, the intended support condition requires positive leading constants for every nonempty product of open neighborhoods at a finite set of places; then such neighborhoods must contain rational points. A scalar total-count law alone gives no such distribution assertion. The precise measures and asymptotic uniformity are not supplied.

It also reports exact curve-degree and singularity constraints in the rank-one case $\operatorname{Pic}(X_{{\mathbb{C}}})={\mathbb{Z}} H$, and a smooth quartic over ${\mathbb{F}}_5$ with no ${\mathbb{F}}_5$-defined geometrically rational curve. Neither the full constraints nor equations or verification for the finite-field example are supplied. All these results are descriptions of the machine-generated aid and require independent review.

Short English statement

The source combines quartic K3 counting, weak approximation, rational-curve decision and existence questions over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ and finite fields. The aid offers a bounded-degree procedure, carefully separating descent, geometric rationality, split normalization and a rational branch. Its localized full-support counting implication is stronger than the scalar asymptotic in the original question.
