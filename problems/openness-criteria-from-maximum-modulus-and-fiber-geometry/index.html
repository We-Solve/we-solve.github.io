Math-1691

Openness criteria from maximum modulus and fiber geometry

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

S. Berhanu's source question takes a smooth real hypersurface $M^{2n-1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ with a strong maximum principle for continuous CR functions and asks whether local continuous CR functions are open. The intended maximum principle is for modulus: on a connected local domain, a continuous CR function with a weak local maximum of its absolute value must be constant. Complex values themselves have no order. The conclusion must exclude constant functions, which are never open as maps to ${\mathbb{C}}$; this exclusion is implicit in the natural interpretation. CR equations for continuous functions are understood distributionally in tangential $(0,1)$ directions. The original remark says the hypothesis cannot hold in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$.

The aid's extension-free criterion fixes a nonconstant continuous CR function $f$ on a connected neighborhood $U\subset M$. Let $p\in D\Subset U$ with $D$ connected open and \[ f(p)\notin f(\partial D),\qquad \delta=\min_{x\in\partial D}|f(x)-f(p)|>0. \] The reciprocal maximum-principle argument gives the explicit sufficient conclusion \[ \{a\in{\mathbb{C}}:|a-f(p)|<\delta/2\}\subset f(D). \] Indeed a missing value in that disk would make $1/(f-a)$ have an interior maximum larger than all boundary values, contrary to the hypothesis and nonconstancy. Existence of such trapping domains inside every prescribed neighborhood gives openness at $p$. If openness fails in some neighborhood, every such relatively compact connected $D$ within it must instead have $f(p)\in f(\partial D)$. One trapped domain alone is not silently substituted for an arbitrarily local openness statement.

The aid also rules out several local image barriers for a nonconstant function: a complementary exterior circular disk tangent at the image value, a supporting-line half-plane barrier, or containment in a locally regular real-analytic arc that can be holomorphically straightened. Reciprocal or holomorphic-composition maximum arguments exclude these cases. Singular or irregular one-dimensional image sets are not automatically covered. If $f$ is $C^1$, a non-open point must satisfy $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{R}} df_p<2$, since rank two gives a local submersion.

The supplied literature assessment attributes to Berhanu (2020) affirmative cases for functions extending holomorphically across the hypersurface, ambient-holomorphic restrictions, a dense open subset of each strong-maximum-principle hypersurface, and real-analytic tube structures. Their exact hypotheses and dense-open quantifiers are absent from the record. The general remaining continuous case is not claimed solved. Any proposed counterexample must respect the same-value boundary-fiber and image-barrier restrictions, and the literal constant-function and modulus ambiguities must first be removed.

Short English statement

Under the intended nonconstant maximum-modulus interpretation, trapped same-value fibers give a local image disk by a reciprocal argument. The aid also records known special cases and barriers that a counterexample must avoid, but does not prove the unrestricted continuous open-mapping statement.
