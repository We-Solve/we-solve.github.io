Math-14372

Universal Almost-Sure Prediction for Stationary Gaussian Processes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal G_0$ denote the class of laws $P$ of real-valued processes $(Y_k)_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}$ that are stationary, ergodic, mean-zero, and Gaussian. Here Gaussian means that every finite vector of coordinates has a multivariate Gaussian distribution, allowing degeneracy; stationarity means invariance of the law under the coordinate shift; and ergodicity means that every shift-invariant event has probability zero or one. Mean-zero means ${\mathbb{E}}_P[Y_k]=0$ for every $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$. The real-valued interpretation is conventional but is not explicitly specified in the supplied record.

For $n\geq2$, a predictor is a Borel measurable function $g_n:{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}$, using only the observed finite past $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1})$. Write \[ m_{n,P}={\mathbb{E}}_P\!\left[Y_n\mid\sigma(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1})\right]. \] Gaussian coordinates are integrable, so this conditional expectation exists. Its choice of version does not affect the almost-sure convergence question.

The title and supplied literature assessment suggest the following universal prediction question: does there exist a single sequence $(g_n)_{n\geq2}$, independent of $P$, such that \[ \forall P\in\mathcal G_0,\qquad P\!\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[m_{n,P}-g_n(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1})\right]=0\right)=1? \] The probability-one event may depend on $P$. The target is convergence of the prediction error relative to the conditional mean at each successive time, without time averaging, and the conditioning is on the finite observed past.

Unresolved quantifier qualification. The original wording instead places the process before the existence of a prediction rule. Taken literally, it asks \[ \forall P\in\mathcal G_0\ \exists(g_{n,P})_{n\geq2},\qquad P\!\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[m_{n,P}-g_{n,P}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1})\right]=0\right)=1, \] which permits dependence on the process law. The displayed source statement also does not explicitly require law independence. These quantifier orders must not be identified; the universal formulation above is an intended-target interpretation supported by the supplied title and assessment, pending source review. The original wording also omits the mean-zero hypothesis present in the clean and displayed statements; the formulations above retain that hypothesis.

Short English statement

The apparent intended question asks whether one sequence of measurable predictors, using only the observations so far, can approximate the next conditional mean almost surely for every stationary ergodic mean-zero Gaussian process. The source leaves unresolved whether the same predictors must work for all process laws; its literal wording permits a separate rule for each law.
