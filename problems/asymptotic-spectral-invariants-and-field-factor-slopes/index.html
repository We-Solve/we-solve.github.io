Math-1539

Asymptotic spectral invariants and field-factor slopes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,\omega)$ be a closed symplectic manifold in a setting where Hamiltonian Floer theory, its filtered inclusion maps, the PSS identification and quantum product are defined over a chosen Novikov/coefficient field. For a nonzero quantum class $\alpha$ and a Hamiltonian path/lift $\widetilde f\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Ham}}(M)$, define \[ c(\widetilde f,\alpha)=\inf\{a:\operatorname{PSS}(\alpha) \in\operatorname{im}(HF^{\le a}(\widetilde f)\to HF(\widetilde f))\}. \] The source says "smallest"; using an infimum avoids adding an unstated attainment/spectrality hypothesis. A normalization of the Hamiltonian and a fixed capped-loop/action convention are part of the data. For instance with $\mathcal A_H([x,u])=\int_0^1H_t(x(t))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t-\int_u\omega$, adding a time-dependent constant $k(t)$ shifts $c$ by $\int_0^1k(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t$. One may impose zero spatial mean at every time and retain the universal-cover path class; descent to the time-one map requires its own theorem.

Question 1.22, raised by Polterovich. Study the asymptotic behavior of $c(\widetilde f^{,n},\alpha)$ as $n\to\infty$, including existence and dependence of the slope $\lim_n c(\widetilde f^{,n},\alpha)/n$. The source writes $f\in\operatorname{Ham}(M)$ without these path/normalization qualifications and does not specify a particular universal asymptotic conjecture.

Supplied axiomatic partial result. Write $Q$ for the quantum algebra. Assume the triangle inequality \[ c(\widetilde f\widetilde h,\alpha*\beta) \le c(\widetilde f,\alpha)+c(\widetilde h,\beta), \] the non-Archimedean sum inequality $c(\widetilde f,\alpha+\beta)\le\max(c(\widetilde f,\alpha),c(\widetilde f,\beta))$ for nonzero sums, and Hofer/Lipschitz control giving finite linear bounds along powers for each nonzero fixed label. The aid asserts that every nonzero idempotent $e*e=e$ has a finite slope \[ \mu_e(\widetilde f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c(\widetilde f^{,n},e)}n. \] Every nonzero element of a field factor $eQ$ has that same slope. More generally, if \[ Q=K_1\oplus\cdots\oplus K_s \] is a finite direct sum of fields with orthogonal units $e_i$, then every $\alpha\ne0$, with $\alpha_i=e_i*\alpha$, satisfies \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c(\widetilde f^{,n},\alpha)}n =\max_{i:\alpha_i\ne0}\mu_{e_i}(\widetilde f). \] Thus in this semisimple setting the slope depends on field-factor support. The maximum uses the stated action/filtration inequalities; an opposite spectral convention would need translation. The claim does not classify arbitrary nonsemisimple labels, give a bounded remainder in general, or imply a quasimorphism without further assumptions.

Normalization obstruction from the aid. An unnormalized constant Hamiltonian $H\equiv a$ has the identity time-one map, while its $n$-fold path iteration shifts spectral values by $na$. Hence the corresponding unnormalized slopes can be arbitrary even for the identity map. This does not contradict a normalized path-class invariant; it shows why the source notation cannot discard normalization or assume time-one descent.

Short English statement

Polterovich asks about spectral invariants under iteration. The supplied aid proves slopes for nonzero idempotents and, in a finite direct sum of quantum field factors, identifies every nonzero label's slope with the largest active factor slope. A normalized Hamiltonian path/lift is essential; the unnormalized time-one map alone does not determine a slope.
