Third Homology and Pre-Bloch Groups of $S$-Arithmetic $\operatorname{SL}_2$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a global field, meaning a finite extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ or of ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$ for a prime $p$. Let $V_F$ be its set of places and $S_\infty$ its set of archimedean places; thus $S_\infty=\varnothing$ in the function-field case. For a finite set $S\subseteq V_F$, put \[ \mathcal O_S=\{a\in F: v(a)\geq 0\text{ for every nonarchimedean place }v\notin S\}, \] where each nonarchimedean valuation is normalized to have value group ${\mathbb{Z}}$. For such a place $v$, let $k(v)$ be the residue field of its valuation ring. Write $R={\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]$, and let $H_3(G,R)$ denote group homology of the discrete group $G$ with trivial coefficients in $R$. The inclusion $\mathcal O_S\hookrightarrow F$ induces a homomorphism \[ \iota_{S,*}:H_3(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathcal O_S),R)\longrightarrow H_3(\operatorname{SL}_2(F),R). \] Set $K_S=\ker(\iota_{S,*})$.

The source uses $\mathcal P(k)$ for a pre-Bloch group. A usual presentation is the abelian group generated by symbols $[x]$, for $x\in k\setminus\{0,1\}$, subject to \[ [x]-[y]+[y/x] -\left[\frac{1-x^{-1}}{1-y^{-1}}\right] +\left[\frac{1-x}{1-y}\right]=0 \qquad(x,y\in k\setminus\{0,1\},\ x\ne y). \] The supplied record does not specify whether this precise presentation, including its conventions for small residue fields, is intended. It also names a natural map $\pi_S$ without giving its construction or a characterization determining it.

With those unresolved source qualifications, the supplied question is whether, for every global field $F$, the map \[ \pi_S:K_S\longrightarrow \bigoplus_{v\in S\setminus S_\infty} \left(\mathcal P(k(v))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}R\right) \] is an isomorphism for every sufficiently large finite set $S$ of places. Interpreting "sufficiently large" as eventuality under inclusion, the intended quantifiers would be \[ \forall F\ \exists S_0\subseteq V_F\text{ finite}\ \forall S\subseteq V_F\text{ finite},\qquad S_0\subseteq S\ \Longrightarrow\ \pi_S\text{ is an isomorphism}. \] One may include $S_\infty$ in $S_0$ under this interpretation. The record does not explicitly specify this meaning of "sufficiently large," any further restrictions on $F$ or $S$, or the definition of $\pi_S$. Consequently, the displayed assertion remains a source question whose complete formulation requires clarification of these points.

Short English statement

For a global field $F$, consider the third homology classes of $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathcal O_S)$, with coefficients in ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]$, that become zero over $F$. The question asks whether, once the finite set of places $S$ is sufficiently large, the source's natural map identifies this kernel with the direct sum of residue-field pre-Bloch groups at the nonarchimedean places in $S$, after tensoring with ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]$. The map, precise pre-Bloch conventions, and meaning of sufficiently large require source clarification.
