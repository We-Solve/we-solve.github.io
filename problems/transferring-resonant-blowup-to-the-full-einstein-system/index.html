A resonant system retains the secular mode interactions of a weakly nonlinear evolution on a long, amplitude-dependent timescale. A precise failure of pre-blowup approximation.
The scalar effective equation $\dot y=y^2$, $y(0)=1$, has $y(t)=(1-t)^{-1}$ and blows up at $t=1$. For $\epsilon>0$ consider the regularized equation \[ \dot y_\epsilon=\frac{y_\epsilon^2}{1+\epsilon y_\epsilon^2},\qquad y_\epsilon(0)=1. \] Its derivative is positive and at most $1/\epsilon$, so its solution is global. Separation gives the exact relation \[ t=1-y_\epsilon(t)^{-1}+\epsilon(y_\epsilon(t)-1). \] On every $[0,T]$ with $T<1$, comparison of the differential equations gives $1\leq y_\epsilon(t)\leq(1-t)^{-1}$, and the implicit relation then shows uniform convergence to $y(t)$ as $\epsilon\to0$.
The singular window.
At the effective blowup time, the same identity gives $\epsilon y_\epsilon(1)(y_\epsilon(1)-1)=1$, so $y_\epsilon(1)$ is of order $\epsilon^{-1/2}$ but finite. The regularizing term is negligible at every fixed bounded amplitude and decisive precisely in the diverging regime. Thus arbitrarily accurate approximation on every compact pre-blowup interval does not transfer blowup.
Full-system gap.
This is not an Einstein regularization and does not refute the source mechanism. It pinpoints the first required estimate: the resonant/full-system comparison must remain valid in an amplitude-dependent neighborhood of the singular time, or a robust geometric criterion must trigger before approximation breaks. The actual resonant equations, gauge and blowup norm need to be fixed from the source before such an argument. No transfer to the full Einstein evolution is established here.
