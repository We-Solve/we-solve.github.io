Math-1871

Decomposing rooted (k,l)-connected graphs into rooted k-connected parts

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an undirected graph with root $r\in V$. For a graph $H$ and integer $q>0$, $q$-edge-connectivity means \[ |\delta_H(A)|\geq q \quad\text{for every nonempty proper }A\subset V(H), \] where $\delta_H(A)$ is the edge cut. Retaining the source's definition, $G$ is rooted $(k,l)$-connected if, for every $X\subseteq V\setminus\{r\}$, \[ G-X\text{ is }(k-|X|)l\text{-edge-connected}. \] In the intended positive-integer parameter range, nonpositive thresholds are naturally vacuous; equivalently only $|X|<k$ imposes a positive cut requirement. That convention is not stated in the source and must be checked.

The question is whether every such $G$ contains subgraphs \[ G_i=(V,E_i)\quad(1\leq i\leq l),\qquad E_i\subseteq E,\quad E_i\cap E_j=\varnothing\ (i\ne j), \] with each $G_i$ spanning and rooted $k$-connected at the same root $r$. "Contains" requests disjoint edge sets, without explicitly requiring their union to exhaust $E$.

The source does not define rooted $k$-connected. A usual root-vertex-connectivity reading is that for each $v\ne r$ there are $k$ paths from $r$ to $v$ whose internal vertices are pairwise disjoint. This is distinct from $k$ edge-disjoint paths and must be verified as the intended target. The source also leaves finite versus infinite graphs, parallel-edge conventions, and small-order/parameter boundary cases unstated. No such choice is silently used to turn the conjecture into a known edge-connectivity decomposition theorem. The supplied summary places the problem in root-connectivity/branching decomposition theory and reports no verified resolution.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether the stated edge-connectivity conditions after deleting nonroot vertices force $l$ disjoint spanning subgraphs, each $k$-connected from the root in the intended sense. The deletion condition is explicit, but the target root-connectivity and graph conventions require source review.
