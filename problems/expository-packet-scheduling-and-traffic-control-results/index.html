Expository Packet-Scheduling and Traffic-Control Results

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied local registry record for permanent UnsolvedMath ID $30002805$ is marked statement_status: unrecoverable. Its verification note says that the extraction combined two illustrative results into a purported open problem. Consequently, the following are incomplete motivating tasks, not a recovered open conjecture or an asserted theorem.
  1. Let $N$ be the number of nodes, indexed by $i=1,\ldots,N$. Packets are sent at discrete times over an unreliable communication medium. For node $i$, let $p_i\in[0,1]$ denote its stated success probability and let $q_i\geq0$ denote its required throughput per unit time. The source describes successes as independent, but does not specify the full collection of events to which independence applies. Let $T$ denote the deadline period; its units and admissible values are not specified.

    The motivating task is to characterize exactly the feasible parameter collections \[ \left((p_i,q_i)_{i=1}^{N},T\right). \] Here feasibility requires a specified packet-arrival model, transmission constraints, scheduling information, deadline convention, and mathematical meaning of throughput. These are absent from the supplied statement. In particular, no choice between expected throughput, almost-sure long-run throughput, or another delivery criterion is justified. Thus the supplied data do not define a unique feasibility predicate.

  2. Consider the associated autonomous-traffic system described as communicating unicycle cars on multi-lane roads, including intersections. The motivating task is to design controls guaranteeing no collisions, no road departures, and safe traversal of intersections.

    For a fully specified traffic model $M$, let $S_M$ denote its set of configurations satisfying its collision and road-containment requirements. The safety component would require a control law under which every trajectory allowed by $M$, from every initial condition admitted by its assumptions, satisfies \[ x(t)\in S_M\qquad\text{for every }t\geq0. \] This is only a schematic expression of the requested safety guarantee. The record does not specify the unicycle equations, control bounds, vehicle geometry, road and intersection geometry, admissible initial conditions, disturbances, communication assumptions, or boundary-contact conventions. It also does not determine whether safe intersection traversal includes an eventual-passage requirement. These omissions prevent a complete control specification or a precise coupling to the packet problem.

The supplied verification note reports that the official report already provides a sharp packet-throughput feasibility characterization and develops provably safe traffic-control laws. It identifies no separate clean open statement. This source qualification must accompany the motivating tasks above.

Short English statement

The record asks which packet-throughput requirements can be met before deadlines, and how communicating autonomous cars can avoid collisions, remain on the road, and traverse intersections safely. Essential model assumptions are missing. Moreover, the supplied verification note identifies the report's material as illustrative results that were incorrectly combined into an open problem, so no precise unresolved conjecture can be recovered here.
