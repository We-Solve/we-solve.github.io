Math-1578

Bounded square-class width and sign-symmetric factorization in C(Z)

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $K={\mathbb{C}}(Z)$, and let $v_0,v_\infty$ denote the orders of a nonzero rational function at $Z=0$ and $Z=\infty$, with zeros positive and poles negative. For $x\in K$ set \[ h(x)=\frac{1+Zx^2}{1-Zx^2}. \] Both numerator and denominator are nonzero, and the source records $v_0(h(x))=v_\infty(h(x))=0$. Question 24 (Pheidas), with the corrupted product layout restored as reported in the supplied verification, asks whether every eligible $f\in K^\times$ has witnesses $u\in K^\times$, $x_1,\ldots,x_{1000}\in K$ such that \[ f=u^2\prod_{i=1}^{1000}h(x_i). \] The extracted eligibility phrase is "$v_0(f)=v_\infty(f)$ even." Read literally this requires equal even integers. The aid instead discusses the potentially larger set with each valuation even, without equality. This difference is preserved explicitly; neither is silently substituted for the other. The weaker source question restricts $f$ to ${\mathbb{Q}}(Z)^\times$ while still allowing all witnesses in ${\mathbb{C}}(Z)$. The source says that $1000$ may be replaced by another integer; the exact fixed bound and the broader question of existence of some uniform finite bound should be distinguished. Padding a representation with $x_i=0$ changes no factor.

For the square-class reduction let \[ H=\{[f]\in K^\times/K^{\times2}:v_0(f),v_\infty(f)\in2{\mathbb{Z}}\},\qquad \mathcal S=\{[h(x)]:x\in K\}. \] Parity of valuations is well defined on square classes, and \[ [h(x)]=[1-Z^2x^4]. \] The aid reformulates the requested uniform factorization as width at most $1000$: every element of $H$ is a product of at most $1000$ elements of $\mathcal S$. Even under the literal equal-valuation reading, the universal assertion has the same square-class formulation: a square multiple can independently adjust the two even orders to zero. For example $Z^a(Z-1)^{-a-b}$ has orders $a,b$ at zero and infinity, so it supplies the needed order adjustment after squaring. This does not erase the difference between the two original eligibility wordings.

The aid proves the requested factorization for a restricted family. If the odd divisor support of $f$ is contained in $m\leq1000$ sign-pairs $\{a_j,-a_j\}$ with $a_j\in{\mathbb{C}}^\times$, and is exactly a union of those pairs, choose constants $x_j$ with $x_j^4=a_j^{-2}$. Then $[1-Z^2x_j^4]=[(Z-a_j)(Z+a_j)]$, giving the desired square class and hence the factorization, padded if necessary. The odd divisor support means the points of ${\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{C}})$ where the order is odd; eligibility already excludes zero and infinity from it.

Every generator is a norm from ${\mathbb{C}}(s)$ with $s^2=Z$, but the aid notes that this norm map is surjective and hence yields no obstruction. Requiring witnesses in ${\mathbb{Q}}(Z)$ would be a different, stronger question: the leading residue at zero would then have to be a rational square up to sign, excluding $f=2$. This is not a counterexample to the source question, since its witnesses may be complex. Finally, the source asserts that a positive answer would imply undecidability of the existential theory of ${\mathbb{C}}(Z)$; the intended coefficient language and named parameters for that consequence are not specified.

Short English statement

Pheidas asks for a uniformly bounded product of special rational functions, up to a square, and also asks the rational-input version with complex witnesses. The aid gives a square-class width reformulation and a sign-symmetric special case. The equality-versus-parity wording, exact bound, and language of the claimed undecidability consequence remain qualifications.
