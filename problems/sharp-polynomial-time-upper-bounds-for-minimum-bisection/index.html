Math-15198

Sharp Polynomial-Time Upper Bounds for Minimum Bisection

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source formulation. Let $\mathcal G$ denote the graph class intended by the source, and let $\operatorname{OPT}(G)$ denote its minimum-bisection objective. The supplied record does not specify the graph class or fully define this objective.

Under the conventional interpretation for a finite, undirected, unweighted graph $G=(V,E)$, define \[ \delta_G(S)=\{e\in E: e\text{ has exactly one endpoint in }S\}. \] If bisections are permitted to have part sizes differing by at most one, their minimum cut size is \[ \operatorname{OPT}(G)=\min_{\substack{S\subseteq V\\ |S|=\lfloor |V|/2\rfloor}}|\delta_G(S)|. \] For even $|V|$, this requires two parts of equal size. This definition is a conditional interpretation: the record does not specify whether odd-order graphs are included, whether graphs are simple or weighted, or whether another normalization of the objective is intended.

Requested construction. Construct a function $\operatorname{UB}$, computable in time polynomial in the input encoding length, such that \[ \forall G\in\mathcal G,\qquad \operatorname{OPT}(G)\leq \operatorname{UB}(G), \] and such that $\operatorname{UB}(G)$ is asymptotically sharp on random graphs. The direction of the inequality is retained exactly as supplied: it requests an upper bound on a minimization objective.

The random-graph distribution, its parameter regime, and the meaning of asymptotic sharpness are unspecified. In particular, the record does not determine whether sharpness means a vanishing relative error, an additive error on a specified scale, or agreement of a centered leading-order term; nor does it specify a probabilistic mode of convergence. Consequently the random-graph requirement cannot be given a unique quantified formulation from this record. The background mentions sparse random graphs, but supplies no distribution or sparsity regime and does not establish that this is the entire intended target class.

Short English statement

Find a polynomial-time computable upper bound for the minimum-bisection value that is valid for every graph in the intended class and asymptotically matches that value on the intended random graphs. The graph conventions, random model, and required accuracy remain unspecified, so this is a conditional statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
