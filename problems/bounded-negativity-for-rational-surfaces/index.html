Bounded Negativity for Rational Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a field. A smooth projective surface $X$ over $k$ is $k$-rational if it is birational over $k$ to ${\mathbb{P}}^2_k$; it is geometrically rational if $X_{\overline{k}}$ is birational over $\overline{k}$ to ${\mathbb{P}}^2_{\overline{k}}$. The supplied record says only "rational" and does not specify which interpretation is intended over a nonalgebraically closed field.

For a curve $C\subset X$, write $C^2=C\cdot C$ for its self-intersection number in the intersection theory of the smooth projective surface $X$. A reduced irreducible curve means an integral closed subscheme of dimension one over $k$; geometric irreducibility is not imposed by the supplied wording.

Supplied question, with the meaning of rationality unresolved. For every field $k$ and every smooth projective rational surface $X$ over $k$, does there exist a real constant $B_X$, depending on $X$ but independent of $C$, such that \[ C^2\geq B_X \qquad\text{for every reduced irreducible curve }C\subset X? \] This is the literal unrestricted-field formulation in the clean statement. No characteristic assumption or algebraic-closedness hypothesis is supplied; such a hypothesis must not be inserted without source clarification. The rationality ambiguity above must also be resolved before this becomes a single precise conjecture.

Additional formulation in the original statement. The original statement instead asks for a constant $B_X^{\mathrm{red}}$ such that \[ C^2\geq B_X^{\mathrm{red}} \qquad\text{for every reduced curve }C\subset X, \] and asserts that it suffices to consider reduced irreducible curves. Here a reduced curve may have finitely many distinct irreducible components, so that $C=C_1+\cdots+C_r$ and \[ C^2=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_i^2+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}C_i\cdot C_j. \] The claimed equivalence is retained as a source assertion requiring review: the displayed identity alone does not convert an irreducible-curve lower bound into a uniform lower bound independent of the number of components. The intended target, including any hypotheses needed for that equivalence, remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether each smooth projective rational surface has a uniform lower bound for the self-intersections of its reduced irreducible curves. The field assumptions and meaning of rationality need clarification. The original version also includes reducible reduced curves and claims an equivalence that requires review. The supplied open label and literature assessment are dated curation, not independently verified conclusions.
