Statement

Complete assembly of repetitive genomes

Domain. Genome assembly theory   Retained status/source context. Open identifiable-class assembly problem; unrestricted unique recovery across unbridged repeats is impossible.

Definitions and assumptions.
A genome $G$ is a finite string or collection of chromosome strings over $\{A,C,G,T\}$, considered up to allowed orientation and circular rotation. A sequencing experiment supplies noisy substrings with known read-length distribution and optional long-range links. Define $G\sim_R G'$ when the complete measurement model assigns the same distribution of reads and links to both genomes. Restrict to a declared class $\mathcal G(L,r,p)$ with bounded genome length $L$, repeat multiplicity $r$ and ploidy $p$. Fix an identifiable feature vector $\psi(G)$, for example normalized word counts and independently bridged sequence-adjacency features determined by the specified read law; compare features using $\ell^1$ distance.
Problem statement.
Characterize recoverable classes and construct genome confidence sets with an explicit, weaker summary-width target: \[\mathbb{P}_G\{G\in\mathcal C_\alpha(R)\}\ge1-\alpha,\qquad \sup_{G,G'\in\mathcal C_\alpha(R)}\|\psi(G)-\psi(G')\|_1\le\varepsilon.\] Here $\mathcal C_\alpha(R)\subset\mathcal G$. Determine which additional molecule lengths or linkage measurements make $\psi$ injective on the compatible genome class and collapse alternatives to one chromosome arrangement. In plain language, find the information needed to finish difficult genomes and identify gaps that no assembler can resolve from the current experiment.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
Fix a nontrivial biologically relevant feature map $\psi$ independently of the data; choosing a constant summary cannot count as completing a genome. Treat confidence-set coverage and small diameter as separate stochastic guarantees. If read-equivalent genomes have different requested arrangements, complete reconstruction is impossible under that experiment; the residual target is the extra information or restricted genome class that removes the ambiguity.

Alternative formulations.
(i) In an error-free fixed-length read model, derive combinatorial reconstruction thresholds based on repeated substrings and their bridging. (ii) For heterozygous noisy genomes, combine high-accuracy and ultra-long reads and quantify sequence accuracy, phase and completeness separately. A metagenomic mixture adds unknown copy identity and is the distinct setting of Problem 67.
Obstructions and partial progress.
T2T-CHM13 and diploid Verkko assemblies resolve important historical targets. The original assertion of unique recovery when repeats exceed every informative linkage length is false: exchanging indistinguishable repeat-adjacent segments can leave all observations unchanged. Practical remaining problems concern difficult genome classes, cost, error and adequate linkage. Complete-looking assemblies must not be equated with proofs of uniqueness, and a confident reconstruction requires independent evidence for repeat copy number and arrangement.

Attempted answer

Proposition: Circular strings AACAGAT and AACATAG have identical multisets of circular two-letter reads, but different arrangements. Independent uniform reads of length at most two therefore cannot distinguish them, regardless of coverage.
Proof: Both have exactly the pairs AA,AC,CA,AG,GA,AT,TA. Their single-letter counts also agree. Their unique adjacent AA fixes possible rotation alignment, after which the strings differ; reverse-complement orientation also differs in base counts. ∎

What remains unresolved

Longer or linked reads can separate the examples. A narrow identifiable-feature confidence set does not alone certify unique chromosome arrangement.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
