Math-15026

Definability of Evaluation in Rings of Entire Functions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns rings denoted by \[ H=H_{t_1,t_2}({\mathbb{C}}^2),\qquad M=M_{t_1,t_2}({\mathbb{C}}^2), \] described as rings of entire functions on ${\mathbb{C}}^2$ and their meromorphic fraction fields. Here an entire function is a holomorphic function on all of ${\mathbb{C}}^2$. For an integral domain $H$ of such functions, its fraction field is \[ \operatorname{Frac}(H)=\{f/g:f,g\in H,\ g\ne0\}, \] with fractions identified by equality after cross multiplication. The record describes $M$ as this fraction field. It does not define the subscripts $t_1,t_2$, specify which entire functions belong to $H$, or explain whether the subscripts also indicate distinguished language symbols.

A relation on a structure is positive-existentially definable if it is defined by a first-order formula built from atomic formulas using finite conjunctions, finite disjunctions, and existential quantification. Such a formula uses neither negation nor universal quantification. Definability depends on the chosen language and the permitted parameters.

Evaluation-definability questions. For each of $A=H$ and $A=M$, is the evaluation predicate $\operatorname{Eval}_A$ positive-existentially definable in the corresponding ring language? The original statement specifies that the meromorphic language includes the ordinary valuation predicate. Schematically, if the intended evaluation relation has arity $r$, the question asks whether there is a positive-existential formula $\varphi_A(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ such that \[ \forall(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in A^r,\qquad \operatorname{Eval}_A(a_1,\ldots,a_r) \Longleftrightarrow A\models\varphi_A(a_1,\ldots,a_r). \] This is a schema, not a complete specification: the record supplies neither the arity nor the interpretation of $\operatorname{Eval}_A$. It also leaves unspecified the valuation, its predicate, the full language signatures, and whether parameters are allowed. Consequently, no particular evaluation point or specialization map is asserted here.

Additional decision questions from the original statement. Is there an algorithm deciding whether an algebraic equation with coefficients in ${\mathbb{Z}}[z]$ has a solution analytic at $0$, on a neighbourhood of the unit disc, or on all of ${\mathbb{C}}$? These are three separate analytic existence conditions.

For clarity, if an input equation is intended to be \[ P(z,X_1,\ldots,X_n)=0, \qquad P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z,X_1,\ldots,X_n], \] then the usual holomorphic-function interpretation would ask for holomorphic functions $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ on a suitable open set $U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}$ satisfying \[ P\left(z,f_1(z),\ldots,f_n(z)\right)=0 \qquad\text{for every }z\in U. \] The three domain conditions would respectively be: some open neighbourhood $U$ of $0$; some open neighbourhood $U$ of the intended unit disc; and $U={\mathbb{C}}$. This explains a possible formal template without fixing the missing input specification. The record does not say whether there is one unknown function or several, whether their number is fixed or part of the input, or whether systems of equations are included. It also does not specify whether the unit disc is open or closed. These choices must be resolved before a precise decision problem can be asserted.

The supplied curation labels the evaluation questions open and states that a negative result in the report concerns a different differential-equation variant. That variant is not specified here, and its negative result is not asserted for the questions above.

Short English statement

The main questions ask whether evaluation can be expressed using positive-existential formulas in the stated entire-function ring and meromorphic fraction field, with a valuation predicate in the meromorphic language. The original record also asks about deciding the existence of analytic solutions of polynomial equations in three domains. Essential definitions and input conventions are missing, so these remain explicitly qualified statement schemas requiring mathematical review.
