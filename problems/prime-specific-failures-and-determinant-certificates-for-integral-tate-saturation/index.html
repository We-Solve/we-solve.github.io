Math-1267

Prime-specific failures and determinant certificates for integral Tate saturation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

J. Ellenberg's AIM question asks: when rational Tate classes are known to be algebraic, what is the integral cycle-class image, and for which primes $\ell$ can surjectivity fail? In codimension notation the comparison is between \[ \operatorname{CH}^i(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\longrightarrow H^{2i}(X_{\overline k},{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(i)) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{CH}^i(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\longrightarrow H^{2i}(X_{\overline k},{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell(i)), \] with the intended Galois-invariant Tate targets and field of definition specified. The original loses superscripts/subscripts in its $C_i$ notation. Its hypersurface example concerns one-dimensional cycles mapping to $H^4$ on a threefold, hence codimension two, not divisors. Integral cohomology with torsion and its quotient modulo torsion are also distinct targets.

The original comments attribute to Kollár the statement that for a very general hypersurface $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^4$ with $d^3\mid\deg X$, the one-cycle image lies in $dH^4(X,{\mathbb{Z}})$, and say it was not known whether this occurs over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. The hypotheses on $d$ and the precise theorem are not supplied; the unknown-over-$\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ statement is historical source context. C. Schoen's comment says the rational Tate conjecture implies the integral version for one-cycles over finite fields at $\ell\ne p$; T. Geisser's says a rational-to-integral implication holds for Weil-étale cohomology, relating the question to comparison with étale cohomology. Their exact scopes and global versus fixed-variety hypotheses are not included and must not be strengthened silently.

The attached report claims that when the finite-field variety is allowed to vary, failure occurs at every prime $\ell$: projective approximations in Kameko's codimension-two examples have rational degree-four cohomology spanned by algebraic Chern classes but integral cycle-class failure even modulo torsion. No explicit varieties, fields, or full references are supplied.

Its characteristic-zero candidate applies, after a finite field extension, to a very general principally polarized abelian $g$-fold over a finitely generated field. With polarization class $\Theta$, the intermediate minimal-class lines are \[ {\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\,\frac{[\Theta]^c}{c!},\qquad 2\leq c\leq g-1 \] in the usual nondivisor, nontop interpretation. Combining the March 2026 Engel--de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder results with Milne's absolute-Hodge descent, it asserts a $2$-adic integral Tate failure on every such line for $g\geq4$, exact line index $2$ for $g=4,5$, and a $3$-adic failure on the minimal curve line $c=g-1$ for $g\geq7$. The exact intermediate range and line-index normalization must be checked against the cited theorem. A very general finitely generated-field assertion is not an assertion about every abelian variety or automatically about one defined over a number field or $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

The report also claims a two-sided determinant bound on integral defects and a conditional finite exceptional-prime set from uniform perfect-pairing data. No determinant formula or uniformity hypotheses are printed. Thus these are additional conditional audit claims, not a theorem that one finite prime set works for all varieties. Rational algebraicity does not itself establish integral saturation.

Short English statement

Investigate which primes obstruct integral saturation of a rationally algebraic Tate lattice. The report cites varying finite-field failures and proposes prime-specific minimal-class failures on very general abelian varieties, while retaining torsion and field-of-definition distinctions.
