Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

Edge intensification refers to enhanced convection near the edge of a warm region rather than its warmest interior. Convergence lines collect horizontal flow feeding rising air. Their number and spacing are observables of the convection pattern; rotation introduces Coriolis effects. Sea-surface-temperature gradients are possible forcing rather than a stipulated necessary cause.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For edge-intensified tropical convection, determine (1) what sets the number and separation distance of convergence lines, including without a sea-surface-temperature gradient; (2) why convection does not occur over the highest sea-surface temperature and whether a predictive model of edge intensification can be derived; and (3) how rotation changes these effects.

Short English statement

Explain convergence-line spacing, edge-focused convection and the influence of rotation, including settings without a sea-surface-temperature gradient.

A wavelength-selection diagnostic.
On a periodic one-dimensional domain, consider the explicitly auxiliary linear amplitude equation \[ \eta_t=a\eta-b\eta_{xx}-c\eta_{xxxx},\qquad b,c>0. \] A Fourier mode $e^{ikx}$ grows at rate $\sigma(k)=a+bk^2-ck^4$. In a continuous wavenumber approximation the maximum occurs at $k_*^2=b/(2c)$, with rate $a+b^2/(4c)$ and wavelength $2\pi\sqrt{2c/b}$. On a finite periodic domain only its discrete wavenumbers are available. Hence the number of preferred lines depends on domain size as well as coefficients. A positive maximum can occur with spatially uniform coefficients and no imposed sea-surface-temperature gradient.
Testing a superficial rotation correction.
If one merely couples two amplitudes by a skew matrix, $\dot z=(\sigma(k)I+fJ)z$, where $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, the exact solution is $z(t)=e^{\sigma(k)t}e^{fJt}z(0)$. Since the second factor is a rotation, growth rates and $k_*$ do not change. Thus appending an isolated Coriolis-like rotation term to a scalar instability is insufficient to predict a changed convergence-line spacing.
