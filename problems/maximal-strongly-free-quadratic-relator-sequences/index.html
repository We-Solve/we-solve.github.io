Maximal Strongly Free Quadratic Relator Sequences

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $d$, consider the source's strongly free sequences of relators on $d$ generators, with every relator of degree two. The ambient algebraic or pro-$p$ presentation category, the grading, the coefficient field, and the definition of strongly free are not supplied. Length means the number of relators in the sequence.

The conjectured largest possible length is exactly \[ t(d)= \begin{cases} d^2/4,&d\text{ even},\\[2pt] (d-1)^2/4,&d\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \] Once the intended category is fixed, the maximality assertion has two parts: there exists an admissible strongly free quadratic-relator sequence of length $t(d)$, and every such sequence has length at most $t(d)$. The supplied assessment attributes a construction and the displayed quadratic-degree bound to the report, but says maximality is unproved.

The odd case is reproduced as $(d-1)^2/4$, not changed to $(d^2-1)/4$ or $\lfloor d^2/4\rfloor$. The report must confirm the category and hypotheses supporting this particular parity-dependent expression. In particular, notions of strongly free sequences for associative algebras, Lie or restricted Lie algebras, and pro-$p$ presentations cannot be silently identified. The meaning of quadratic relators also depends on which grading or filtration is used.

The assessment additionally mentions unresolved nil and self-similar algebra questions in remaining characteristics, but gives no characteristic range or precise connection to this maximal-length problem. Those statements are recorded as context rather than used to supply missing hypotheses or formulate another problem.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the displayed parity-dependent value is the exact maximum number of strongly free quadratic relators on $d$ generators. The odd formula is preserved exactly; the ambient category, strong-freeness definition, and characteristic assumptions are missing.
