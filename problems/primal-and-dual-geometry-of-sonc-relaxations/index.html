Verbatim supplied catalogue question

The actual source relaxation

Dressler's report, printed pages 694--696, first uses exponential sums on real space and equivalently polynomials on the positive orthant. It describes fixing coefficient variables in a lifted dual-cone formulation, leaving an LP in auxiliary variables. It does not print the full LP or the resulting primal inequalities. Thus an LP fiber cannot be identified here with the entire coefficient cone. We compute the primal cone, dual cone, and moment issue completely for support $A=\{0,1,2\}$ on $t>0$, then test two proposed generalizations. This model does not substitute a reconstructed LP for the missing one.

The full three-coefficient calculation

Write $f(t)=a+bt+ct^2$. Nonnegativity for all $t>0$ is equivalent to \[ C=\{(a,b,c):a,c\ge0,\ b\ge-2\sqrt{ac}\}. \] Necessity of $a,c\ge0$ follows by taking $t\downarrow0$ and $t\to\infty$. If $b\ge0$ these conditions suffice. If $b<0$ and $a=0$ or $c=0$, the polynomial is negative for sufficiently small or large positive $t$, respectively. For $a,c>0$, its minimum on the positive axis is at $t=-b/(2c)$, with value $a-b^2/(4c)$, by completing the square. This proves the stated condition in every case. When $b<0$, the polynomial is itself the one-dimensional nonnegative circuit with endpoints 0 and 2 and interior exponent 1. When $b\ge0$ it is a sum of nonnegative monomials. Conversely every such circuit or monomial is nonnegative. Thus on this support the SONC cone, with the positive-orthant convention, equals $C$.

Use the ordinary coefficient pairing $\langle(a,b,c),(u,v,w)\rangle=au+bv+cw$. The dual is \[ C^*=\{(u,v,w):u,v,w\ge0,\ v^2\le uw\}. \] Testing the three nonnegative coordinate rays shows $u,v,w\ge0$. For fixed $a,c\ge0$ the smallest pairing over allowed $b$ is $au+cw-2v\sqrt{ac}$. If $v^2\le uw$, this is nonnegative because $au+cw\ge2\sqrt{acuw}\ge2v\sqrt{ac}$, where the first inequality is $(\sqrt{au}-\sqrt{cw})^2\ge0$. Conversely, if $u,w>0$, choose $a=w,c=u$ to get $2uw-2v\sqrt{uw}\ge0$, forcing $v\le\sqrt{uw}$. If $u=0$ and $v>0$, choose $c=1$ and let $a$ grow; the expression $w-2v\sqrt a$ becomes negative. The case $w=0$ is symmetric. This proves the formula including all zero boundaries.

Dual membership need not be an actual positive-axis moment

A finite positive measure $\mu$ on $(0,\infty)$ with finite second moment has vector $(\int1\,d\mu,\int t\,d\mu,\int t^2\,d\mu)$ in $C^*$: the middle coordinate is nonnegative, and Cauchy--Schwarz follows by integrating $(t-s)^2\ge0$ against the product measure, giving $2uw-2v^2\ge0$. But $(1,0,1)\in C^*$ is not such a vector. If $\int t\,d\mu=0$, then each set $\{t\ge1/r\}$ has measure zero, since its contribution to the integral is at least its measure divided by $r$. Their union is $(0,\infty)$, so $\mu=0$, contradicting mass one.

It is a limit of genuine two-atom moment vectors. For $0<e<1$ take \[ \mu_e=(1-e^2)\delta_e+e^2\delta_{1/e}. \] Its moments are $(1,2e-e^3,1+e^2-e^4)$ and converge to $(1,0,1)$. This shows that even in a case where SONC equals all nonnegative polynomials on the support, the dual need not equal the unclosed cone of actual finite moments. The boundary of the domain and escape of small mass to infinity both matter.

Why a closure is the natural general relation.
For any finite real function family with evaluation vectors $v(t)$, let $M$ be their finite nonnegative conic combinations and let $P$ be the coefficient cone of functions nonnegative everywhere on the domain. Then $P=M^*$ directly from the definition of the pairing, and $P^*=\overline M$. Here is a finite-dimensional proof of the second assertion. Inclusion $\overline M\subseteq P^*$ follows from continuity. If $z\notin\overline M$, choose a closest point $y$ in the closed cone $\overline M$; existence follows by restricting to a sufficiently large closed ball and compactness. Minimizing squared distance along the segment from $y$ to any $v\in\overline M$ gives $(z-y)\cdot(v-y)\le0$. Taking $v=0$ and $v=2y$ shows $(z-y)\cdot y=0$. Thus $h=y-z$ satisfies $h\cdot v\ge0$ for all $v\in\overline M$, while $h\cdot z=-\|y-z\|^2<0$. Hence $h\in P$ and $z\notin P^*$, as required. For a general SONC subcone $S\subseteq P$, this gives only $\overline M=P^*\subseteq S^*$; equality needs a further argument.

Why polyhedral fibers do not make the cone polyhedral

For fixed $a,c\ge0$, the allowed $b$ form the half-line $b\ge-2\sqrt{ac}$, a polyhedron in one dimension. Nevertheless $C$ is not polyhedral. Intersect it with $a+c=1$ and $b\le0$, and use coordinates $x=2a-1$, $y=b$. The section is exactly the lower half disk $x^2+y^2\le1$, $y\le0$. Its curved boundary contains infinitely many points of the open lower semicircle. If this set were given by finitely many linear inequalities, every boundary point would make at least one nontrivial inequality tight: otherwise finitely many strict margins would give a small open ball inside the set. Each corresponding line meets the circle in at most two points, by substituting its linear equation into the quadratic circle equation. Finitely many lines cannot cover the semicircle, a contradiction.

Nor can $C$ be the exact linear projection of a finite-dimensional polyhedron. Eliminating one variable from finitely many linear inequalities preserves polyhedrality: write all nonzero-coefficient inequalities as lower or upper bounds on that variable, keep the zero-coefficient ones, and require each lower bound to be at most each upper bound. These conditions are sufficient by choosing a value between the largest lower and smallest upper bounds, with one-sided cases unrestricted on the other side. Repetition eliminates every auxiliary variable. An exact projected LP description would therefore make $C$ polyhedral, contrary to the preceding section. Fixing coefficients before taking an LP dual is compatible with this fact; it is not an exact global linear extended formulation of $C$.
