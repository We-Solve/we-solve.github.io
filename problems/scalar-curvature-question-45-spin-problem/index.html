Scalar Curvature Question [?45]: Spin Problem

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For oriented smooth manifolds $M,N$, a map $f:M\to N$ is called a spin map, in the usual relative sense, when the stable oriented bundle $TM\oplus f^*TN$ admits a spin structure. Its obstruction is \[ w_2(TM)=f^*w_2(TN)\quad\text{in }H^2(M;{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}). \] This does not require $M$ and $N$ separately to be spin. For closed connected oriented manifolds of the same dimension, $\deg f$ is defined by $f_*[M]=(\deg f)[N]$.

Gromov's Spin Problem asks whether the spin-map hypothesis is necessary in the preceding scalar curvature extremality and rigidity statements, especially for equidimensional maps with $\deg f\ne0$. The preceding statements are not included in this item, so their curvature assumptions and conclusions cannot be reconstructed as a single quantified theorem. The source explains that the hypothesis allows the required twisted Dirac operator to be defined; it does not assert that all nonzero-degree maps must be spin.

The appended passage makes a separate approximation assertion about "Extremality via Lipschitz Maps": if the indicated nonzero-degree, length decreasing, strictly scalar-curvature decreasing maps do not exist in class $C^2$, then they do not exist in the Lipschitz class either. To distinguish the two metrics lost in extraction, write $f:(M,g)\to(N,h)$. The usual nonexpanding interpretation is \[ d_h(f(x),f(y))\leq d_g(x,y),\qquad \operatorname{Scal}_h(f(x))<\operatorname{Scal}_g(x). \] For smooth maps the first condition is equivalent to $f^*h\leq g$. These conventions, the source/target distinction, compactness assumptions, and whether "length decreasing" is strict require checking. The excerpt supplies no approximation details guaranteeing that its exact inequalities survive smoothing. The appended assertion is retained as a source report, not established here.

Short English statement

Can scalar curvature extremality and rigidity results for nonzero-degree maps dispense with their relative spin condition? The excerpt also reports a passage from smooth to Lipschitz maps by approximation, but omits the surrounding hypotheses.
