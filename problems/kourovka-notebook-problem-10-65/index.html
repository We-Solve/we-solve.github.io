Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.65

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be an infinite set and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ act $2$-transitively: for any $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\Omega$ with $\alpha\ne\beta$ and $\gamma\ne\delta$, there exists $g\in G$ such that $g(\alpha)=\gamma$ and $g(\beta)=\delta$. For distinct $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$, define the pointwise stabilizers \[ G_\alpha=\{g\in G:g(\alpha)=\alpha\},\qquad G_{\alpha\beta}=G_\alpha\cap G_\beta. \] Suppose there exists a subgroup $A\leq G_\alpha$ such that \[ G_\alpha=A\cdot G_{\alpha\beta} \quad\text{and}\quad \exists t\in G_{\alpha\beta}\ \forall a\in A,\quad tat^{-1}=a^{-1}. \] Here the displayed product is read as the set product $\{ah:a\in A,\ h\in G_{\alpha\beta}\}$. The source does not explicitly state whether the notation additionally requires $A$ to be normal in $G_\alpha$, or the product to be a semidirect product; this qualification remains unresolved. No order condition on $t$ is explicitly supplied.

Classification problem. Determine the structure of all infinite $2$-transitive permutation groups satisfying the preceding hypotheses, subject to clarification of the product notation.

Additional question. The source further supposes that $A\setminus\{1\}$ "contains at most two conjugacy classes of $G_\alpha$." One precise possible interpretation is that the elements of $A\setminus\{1\}$ represent at most two $G_\alpha$-conjugacy classes. Writing \[ a\sim b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists h\in G_\alpha,\quad hah^{-1}=b \qquad(a,b\in A\setminus\{1\}), \] this interpretation gives the condition \[ \left|(A\setminus\{1\})/{\sim}\right|\leq 2. \] Under the intended conjugacy-class condition, must there exist a field $K$ and a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ such that \[ N\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(K),\qquad \operatorname{PSL}_2(K)=\operatorname{SL}_2(K)/Z(\operatorname{SL}_2(K))? \] Here $\operatorname{SL}_2(K)$ is the group of determinant-one $2\times2$ matrices over $K$, and $Z(H)$ denotes the center of a group $H$. The source imposes no explicit characteristic restriction on $K$.

The conjugacy-class wording needs review: it may instead require $A\setminus\{1\}$ to be a union of at most two entire $G_\alpha$-conjugacy classes. This is stronger than the displayed interpretation unless the requisite conjugation invariance of $A$ is established or assumed. A literal bound only on classes wholly contained in $A\setminus\{1\}$ would be another, weaker reading. The source does not resolve these readings explicitly.

Short English statement

Classify the infinite $2$-transitive permutation groups whose point stabilizer is a product of a subgroup $A$ and a two-point stabilizer containing an element that inverts every element of $A$. Under the additional condition involving at most two point-stabilizer conjugacy classes in $A\setminus\{1\}$, must the group contain a normal subgroup isomorphic to $\operatorname{PSL}_2(K)$ for some field $K$? The meanings of the product notation and the conjugacy-class condition require clarification.
