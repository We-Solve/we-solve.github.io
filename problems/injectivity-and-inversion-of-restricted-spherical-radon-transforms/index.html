Math-3695

Injectivity and Inversion of Restricted Spherical Radon Transforms

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be the intended hypersurface of centers, with $n\geq2$. In the unnormalized spherical-integral convention, define \[ (R_Sf)(x,r)=\int_{|y-x|=r}f(y)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(y), \qquad x\in S,\quad r>0. \] Here $\sigma$ is Euclidean surface measure. The spherical-mean convention differs by the known factor $|S^{n-1}|r^{n-1}$; a convention must be fixed for inversion and range formulas. Smooth compactly supported functions $f\in C_c^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^n)$ provide a concrete test class, while the source's full compact-support class is not specified.

For the intended hypersurfaces and function spaces, determine:

  1. injectivity, in particular whether $R_Sf=0$ implies $f=0$ for every compactly supported admissible $f$;
  2. inversion formulas recovering $f$ from $R_Sf$ when recovery is possible, and stability in specified input and data topologies;
  3. how these properties change when only data on a proper subset $D\subset S\times(0,\infty)$ are available;
  4. the range, namely necessary and sufficient conditions on data $g(x,r)$ for the existence of an admissible $f$ with $g=R_Sf$.
The source asks to determine these properties, not to assume injectivity for every hypersurface. Geometry and support restrictions matter: if $S$ is a hyperplane, a compactly supported function odd under reflection across $S$ has zero spherical integrals centered on $S$. Thus a universal affirmative injectivity statement with arbitrary $S$ and unrestricted compact support would need qualification.

The extract supplies no dimension, regularity or global geometry of $S$, support region relative to $S$, precise function class, incomplete-data set, or stability norms. These missing choices prevent a unique inversion, stability or range assertion; the smooth test class above does not replace the source's intended generality.

Short English statement

Determine when spherical integrals with centers restricted to a hypersurface determine a compactly supported function, how to reconstruct it stably, what incomplete measurements lose, and which data occur. The geometry of the center surface and the admissible support and function spaces must be specified.
