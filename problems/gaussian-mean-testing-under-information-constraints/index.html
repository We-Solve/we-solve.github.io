Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Gaussian mean testing observes samples from a multivariate normal distribution $N(\mu,\Sigma)$ and distinguishes two specified sets of means. For example, a zero-versus-separated-mean test compares $\mu=0$ with $\lVert\mu\rVert_2\geq\varepsilon$ under a specified covariance; this example explains the terminology without fixing the entire source family. Information constraints restrict which observations or messages reach the decision procedure. Optimality compares achievable sample, communication and error tradeoffs with lower bounds applying to every permitted test.

Literature and context check

The primary report, p. 703, presents Gaussian mean testing within broad channel-family information constraints. Canonne--Nimitt's May 2026 primary abstract was also consulted; it describes communication models with varying sample counts, bit budgets, and shared randomness. Only that abstract is used for this context, not an unverified transfer of its full results. The calculation below is an elementary scalar, one-sample-per-user case and does not claim a solution of the high-dimensional allocation problem.

Research attempt: A one-bit scalar test and a matching scalar information lower bound

Fix $0<\varepsilon\leq1$. Each of $N$ users sees one independent $X_i\sim N(\mu,1)$ and transmits the bit $Z_i=\mathbf1\{X_i\geq0\}$. We test $\mu=0$ against $|\mu|\geq\varepsilon$. Let $\phi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}$ and $c=\phi(1)>0$. The bit mean is $p_\mu=\Phi(\mu)$. By symmetry, monotonicity, and integration over $[0,\varepsilon]$, \[ |p_\mu-1/2|\geq\int_0^\varepsilon\phi(t)\,dt\geq c\varepsilon \quad\text{whenever }|\mu|\geq\varepsilon. \] Reject the null when $|\overline Z-1/2|>c\varepsilon/2$. Under the null, an error requires $|\overline Z-p_0|>c\varepsilon/2$. Under an alternative, acceptance requires $|\overline Z-p_\mu|\geq c\varepsilon/2$ by the triangle inequality. Since $\operatorname{Var}(\overline Z)=p_\mu(1-p_\mu)/N\leq1/(4N)$, each error probability is at most \[ {1\over Nc^2\varepsilon^2}. \] This follows directly by taking expectations in $\mathbf1\{|V-\mathbb EV|\geq t\}\leq(V-\mathbb EV)^2/t^2$. Thus $N\geq4/(c^2\varepsilon^2)$ suffices for both errors at most $1/4$, with one bit per user and no shared randomness.

For a converse, even allow the referee the full samples and restrict the alternative to $\mu=\varepsilon$. Let $P=N(0,1)^{\otimes N}$ and $Q=N(\varepsilon,1)^{\otimes N}$ have densities $p,q$. Completing the square in one coordinate gives \[ \int\sqrt{p q}=\exp(-N\varepsilon^2/8)=:A. \] More explicitly, the one-coordinate integrand is $e^{-\varepsilon^2/8}$ times the density of $N(\varepsilon/2,1)$; multiply over independent coordinates. Cauchy--Schwarz now gives \[ \operatorname{TV}(P,Q)={1\over2}\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q) \leq\tfrac12\sqrt{(2-2A)(2+2A)}=\sqrt{1-A^2}. \] For every randomized test with rejection probability $f(x)\in[0,1]$, the sum of its errors is $1-\int f(q-p)\geq1-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)$: the positive part of $q-p$ integrates to total variation and bounds the integral against any $0\leq f\leq1$. If $N\varepsilon^2\leq1$, then $A^2\geq e^{-1/4}>3/4$ (use $e^{-t}>1-t$ for $t>0$), so total variation is strictly less than $1/2$. The two errors therefore cannot both be at most $1/4$.

Any communication protocol, including one with randomization and interaction, merely induces such a test function of the complete sample vector after averaging its internal coins. Hence the same lower bound holds under communication restrictions. Together these arguments establish order $\varepsilon^{-2}$ sample complexity in this specified scalar model. They also identify why this route does not detect a scalar communication penalty: thresholding preserves a mean shift of constant order $\varepsilon$ in a single Bernoulli parameter.

Verification and adversarial audit

The upper bound treats both signs of the mean and all $|\mu|\geq\varepsilon$, not only the boundary alternative. The range $\varepsilon\leq1$ is essential to the uniform constant $\phi(1)$. The converse uses an allowed simple alternative and therefore applies to the composite testing problem. Constants and the strict inequality excluding two errors of $1/4$ at $N\varepsilon^2\leq1$ are checked explicitly.
Audit corrections.

Scope and remaining gaps

The result is a known elementary scalar order bound, not a sharp constant and not the original general information-constraint tradeoff. Dimension dependence, covariance families, privacy channels, heterogeneous budgets, and the source's full channel constraints remain untreated.
