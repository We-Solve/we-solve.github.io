Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

Angular synchronization estimates a vector $z\in\mathbb C^n$ with $|z_i|=1$ from noisy relative-phase measurements. In the standard source model $C=zz^*+\sigma W$, $W$ is Hermitian Gaussian noise. The optimization maximizes $x^*Cx$ subject to $|x_i|=1$, with a common global phase unidentifiable. A projected or generalized power iteration applies the source linear update and normalizes each nonzero coordinate to unit modulus; initialization, shifts and zero-coordinate conventions are part of the algorithm. High probability refers to the source noise law as $n$ grows.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Does the projected power method converge (with high probability) to the optimal solution of the angular synchronization problem with (small enough) gaussian noise?

Attempt and results

The source asks for high-probability convergence under a Gaussian noise model. Initialization, shifts, and handling zero coordinates affect the algorithm. We analyze an explicitly stated unshifted update and then a shifted ascent argument. Neither is substituted for an unspecified universal algorithm. The optimal noisy estimator is not in general the latent vector.
Noiseless calculation and an initialization obstruction.
Let $C=zz^*$ with $|z_i|=1$. Define $\operatorname{phase}(w)_i=w_i/|w_i|$ when $w_i\ne0$, retaining the previous iterate's coordinate when $w_i=0$. For an iterate $x$ with unit coordinates, put $c=z^*x$. If $c\ne0$, then $Cx=zc$ and the next iterate is $z(c/|c|)$. Its objective is $n^2$, which is maximal since $|z^*x|\le\sum_i|\overline z_ix_i|=n$. Thus one iteration suffices from every nonorthogonal initialization. But if $c=0$, the update is zero everywhere and the stated convention retains $x$. For $n=2$, $z=(1,1)$ and $x=(1,-1)$, this is a stationary iterate of objective zero although the maximum is four. So no proof can assert convergence from every starting point, even at zero noise, for this update convention. This does not refute the source's probabilistic question with a suitable specified initialization.
Why noisy optimum is not exactly the latent vector.
Take $z=(1,1)$ and \[ C=\begin{pmatrix}1&1+i\epsilon\\1-i\epsilon&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\epsilon>0. \] This is an arbitrarily small Hermitian perturbation of $zz^*$. Write $c=1+i\epsilon$ and choose $x=(1,\overline c/|c|)$. Direct substitution gives $x^*Cx=2+2|c|>4=z^*Cz$. Thus a claim of exact recovery of $z$ cannot be inferred from convergence to the global maximizer of the noisy problem. The displayed perturbation is deterministic and is not asserted to have positive probability as one exact Gaussian realization; its role is to falsify that deterministic identification of the two objectives.
A monotonicity proof and its missing global step.
Choose a real shift $\alpha$ so $B=C+\alpha I\succeq0$. The shift changes the objective only by the constant $\alpha n$ on unit-modulus vectors, but it changes the iteration. Set $y=\operatorname{phase}(Bx)$ with any unit-modulus choices at zero coordinates. Coordinatewise maximization gives $\operatorname{Re}(y^*Bx)\ge\operatorname{Re}(x^*Bx)=x^*Bx$. Writing $d=y-x$, \[ y^*By-x^*Bx=2\operatorname{Re}(d^*Bx)+d^*Bd\ge0. \] This proves objective ascent. It does not prove global convergence: for the noiseless example above, take any $\alpha>0$. The bad $x$ obeys $Bx=\alpha x$, so it is a fixed point of the shifted update too. Therefore the exact attempted implication "positive shift plus monotone objective implies convergence to a global optimum" is false. Its counterexample retains a strict objective gap. Controlling a specified initialization and its entire noisy trajectory is an additional task.
