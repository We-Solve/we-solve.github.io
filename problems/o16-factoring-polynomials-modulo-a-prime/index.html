Math-8895

O16 — Factoring polynomials modulo a prime

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The input is a prime $p$ and a nonzero polynomial $f\in\mathbb F_p[X]$, given by its dense coefficient list. A complete irreducible factorization means an equality \[ f=c\prod_{i=1}^t f_i^{e_i}, \] where $c\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, the $f_i$ are pairwise distinct monic irreducible polynomials of positive degree, and the $e_i$ are positive integers. Irreducible means that $f_i$ has no factorization into two positive-degree polynomials over $\mathbb F_p$. Constants have an empty factor list.

Is there a deterministic classical algorithm computing such a factorization for every input in time polynomial in $d=\deg f$ and $\log p$? All field elements are represented by their standard binary residues and factors are output densely. Thus the cost counts bit operations rather than treating arbitrarily large field operations as free.

The task includes multiplicities, including inseparable behavior when the characteristic divides relevant exponents. The order in which factors are listed is immaterial. The prime is allowed to vary, and the coefficients need not come from reductions of one previously fixed integer polynomial.

The sought guarantee is unconditional and worst-case. Algorithms that are randomized, work only for fixed characteristic, depend on an unproved hypothesis, or achieve a polynomial cost averaged across many primes do not establish this uniform deterministic bound for each individual pair $(p,f)$.

Short English statement

Can every polynomial over every prime field be factored completely by an unconditional deterministic polynomial-time algorithm?
