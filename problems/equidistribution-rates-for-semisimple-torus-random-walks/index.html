Math-14540

Equidistribution Rates for Semisimple Torus Random Walks

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $d$, write $\mathbb T^d={\mathbb{R}}^d/{\mathbb{Z}}^d$, and let $m_d$ denote its normalized Haar probability measure. The supplied excerpt asks whether a sequence denoted by $(\nu_n)$ converges to $m_d$ in the weak-$*$ topology and how fast or slowly this convergence occurs.

If the intended $\nu_n$ are Borel probability measures on $\mathbb T^d$, the convergence question has the precise meaning \[ \nu_n\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{weak}\text{-}*}m_d, \qquad\text{that is,}\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb T^d}f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\nu_n =\int_{\mathbb T^d}f\,{\,\mathrm{d}} m_d \quad\text{for every }f\in C(\mathbb T^d;{\mathbb{R}}). \] This describes the convergence predicate only; it does not assert convergence for every sequence of measures. The excerpt does not define $\nu_n$, the random walk, its acting group, its step distribution, its initial point or distribution, or the hypotheses under which convergence is sought. It also does not specify a distance between measures or a class and norm of test functions for a quantitative rate. Consequently, the intended quantified random-walk statement and its rate question cannot be recovered precisely from the supplied record.

The registry marks the statement as unrecoverable. Its supplied verification note says that the motivating question is answered immediately in the contribution by two stated semisimple random-walk theorems, and that no separate clean statement is promoted. Those theorem statements are not supplied here. The broader question concerning nondense orbits and general acting groups appears only in the supplied literature assessment and lacks sufficient hypotheses to serve as a separate mathematical statement.

Short English statement

The excerpt asks whether the measures of a torus random walk approach normalized Haar measure and at what rate. Weak-$*$ convergence can be defined, but the measures and random-walk hypotheses are missing. The supplied record therefore does not determine a precise open problem; its partially solved label and accompanying assessment require human review.
