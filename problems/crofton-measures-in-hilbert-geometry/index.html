Crofton Measures in Hilbert Geometry

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be a convex body, understood in the usual sense of a compact convex set with nonempty interior, and put $\Omega=\operatorname{int}C$. The supplied record does not specify the range of dimensions $n$ or impose boundary regularity. For distinct $x,y\in\Omega$, let $a,b\in\partial C$ be the endpoints of the chord through them, ordered $a,x,y,b$. With the conventional normalization, the Hilbert distance is \[ d_C(x,y)=\frac12\log\frac{\lVert y-a\rVert\,\lVert x-b\rVert}{\lVert x-a\rVert\,\lVert y-b\rVert}. \] For $x\in\Omega$ and $v\ne0$, let $t_+,t_->0$ satisfy $x+t_+v,x-t_-v\in\partial C$. The corresponding Hilbert norm is \[ F_C(x,v)=\frac12\left(t_+^{-1}+t_-^{-1}\right),\qquad F_C(x,0)=0. \] Under the usual induced one-dimensional Holmes--Thompson convention, the length of a piecewise $C^1$ curve $\gamma:[\alpha,\beta]\to\Omega$ is \[ \operatorname{vol}_1(\gamma)=\int_\alpha^\beta F_C\left(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t)\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] The source does not define the carrier or admissibility conditions of its Crofton measure. A conventional affine-hyperplane interpretation can be made explicit as follows. Let $\mathcal H(\Omega)$ be the space of unoriented affine hyperplanes meeting $\Omega$, equipped with its usual Borel structure. For a curve $\gamma$, put \[ N_\gamma(H)=\#\{t\in(\alpha,\beta):\gamma(t)\in H\}, \] counting parameter occurrences and assigning $+\infty$ when the set is infinite. On this interpretation, a positive Crofton representation means a nonnegative Borel measure $\mu$ such that \[ \operatorname{vol}_1(\gamma)=\int_{\mathcal H(\Omega)}N_\gamma(H)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(H) \] for every piecewise $C^1$ curve in $\Omega$, with any fixed positive normalization constant absorbed into $\mu$.

Source assertion to prove or disprove. In every intended dimension, the existence of a positive Crofton measure for Holmes--Thompson curve length in the Hilbert geometry of $C$ implies that $C$ is an ellipsoid. Here an ellipsoid means a set \[ C=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:(x-c)^{\mathsf T}A(x-c)\leq1\}, \] where $c\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$ and $A$ is a symmetric positive-definite real matrix. The source describes this conclusion equivalently as the Hilbert geometry being hyperbolic.

Unresolved formulation qualification. The hyperplane carrier, curve class, intersection convention, and measure conditions above specify a conventional candidate formulation; they are not supplied definitions from the record. Their agreement with the intended conjecture requires checking the original report. The asserted direction is necessity only. Short English statement

The proposed conjecture says that a positive Crofton formula for Holmes--Thompson curve length in a Hilbert geometry forces the defining convex body to be an ellipsoid. The supplied record leaves the precise Crofton framework and dimension range unspecified, so the explicit hyperplane formulation above remains provisional.
