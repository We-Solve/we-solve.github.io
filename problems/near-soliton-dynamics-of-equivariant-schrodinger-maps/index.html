Near-Soliton Dynamics of Equivariant Schrödinger Maps

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns equivariant Schrödinger maps on the spatial domain ${\mathbb{R}}^2$. It does not specify the target manifold, the precise evolution equation, the equivariance action, or the admissible values of the equivariance index $m$. These are unresolved parts of the formulation; in particular, no particular target or range of $m$ is assumed here.

Conditionally on fixing those data, let $\mathcal Q_m$ denote the harmonic-map soliton family called $Q_m$ in the record. For a specified target metric, a harmonic map is a map $Q$ whose tension field vanishes, $\tau(Q)=0$. The record describes $\mathcal Q_m$ as noncompact but does not specify its parameters or the topology in which noncompactness is intended.

If the maps are realized in a fixed common Euclidean ambient space, define the homogeneous Sobolev distance to this family by \[ d_m(v):=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q_m}\|v-Q\|_{\dot H^1({\mathbb{R}}^2)}, \qquad \|v-Q\|_{\dot H^1({\mathbb{R}}^2)}^2 :=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^2}|\nabla(v-Q)(x)|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] This definition is conditional on an ambient realization and its norm; neither is supplied. If another definition of the map-valued $\dot H^1$ distance is intended, it must be specified instead.

Problem. For equivariant initial data $u_0$ close to $\mathcal Q_m$ in $\dot H^1$, characterize the global dynamics of the corresponding Schrödinger map evolution. In particular, determine whether finite-time blow-up can occur while the solution remains close to $\mathcal Q_m$.

More explicitly, after fixing a solution class and a positive closeness tolerance $\varepsilon$, the latter question asks whether there can exist a solution $u$ on its maximal forward interval $[0,T_+)$ such that \[ 0<T_+<\infty, \qquad d_m(u(0))<\varepsilon, \qquad d_m(u(t))<\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }0\leq t<T_+, \] with no continuation through $T_+$ in that solution class. The approximating member of $\mathcal Q_m$ may depend on $t$; closeness to the family does not require closeness to one fixed soliton. The record does not specify the permitted tolerances, whether such behavior is sought for arbitrarily small tolerances, or whether initial and subsequent closeness should use different thresholds. Thus the displayed conditions explain the finite-time scenario without imposing an unsupported smallness quantifier. The solution class, blow-up criterion, spatial boundary conditions, and precise alternatives required for a characterization of global dynamics also remain unspecified.

Short English statement

Characterize the evolution of equivariant Schrödinger maps on ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ starting near a noncompact harmonic-map soliton family. In particular, decide whether a solution can develop a finite-time singularity while staying near that family throughout its lifespan. The target, equivariance class, closeness thresholds, and meaning of blow-up must be specified before this becomes a fully determined mathematical problem.
