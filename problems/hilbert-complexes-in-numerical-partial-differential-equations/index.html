Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks an expository and historical question, rather than specifying a conjecture with a definite mathematical conclusion. The following standard definitions provide context; their conventions are not additional hypotheses supplied by the source.

Let $\mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}$. A chain complex consists of $\mathbb K$-vector spaces $V_k$ and linear maps $\partial_k:V_k\to V_{k-1}$ satisfying \[ \partial_{k-1}\circ\partial_k=0. \] Its cycles and boundaries are respectively \[ Z_k=\ker\partial_k,\qquad B_k=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}. \] Thus $B_k\subseteq Z_k$, and its homology is $H_k=Z_k/B_k$. The supplied excerpt instead says "boundaries modulo cycles." That wording reverses the standard quotient and does not in general define a quotient space. The definition here explicitly uses cycles modulo boundaries.

A cochain complex has maps $d^k:V^k\to V^{k+1}$ with $d^{k+1}d^k=0$. In a standard analytic convention, a Hilbert complex consists of Hilbert spaces $W^k$ over $\mathbb K$ and densely defined closed linear operators \[ d^k:D(d^k)\subseteq W^k\longrightarrow W^{k+1} \] satisfying \[ d^k(D(d^k))\subseteq D(d^{k+1}),\qquad d^{k+1}d^k u=0\quad\text{for every }u\in D(d^k). \] These conditions alone do not impose closed ranges. The excerpt does not specify an analytic convention for Hilbert complexes or additional operator hypotheses. It mentions simplicial chain complexes associated with triangulations by simplices of a domain in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ as an illustrative example, without specifying a particular domain or triangulation.

The source question is: How did Hilbert complexes come to play a major role in numerical partial differential equations? In the supplied workshop context, this requests a conceptual and historical account of their relationship with numerical PDE.
