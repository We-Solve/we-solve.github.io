Uniform-horizon convergence from the Nash system

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb T^d=\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$ have normalized Lebesgue measure, and let $\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)$ denote its Borel probability measures. Fix couplings $F,G:\mathbb T^d\times\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)\to\mathbb R$ that are smooth and Lasry--Lions monotone: \[ \int_{\mathbb T^d}[K(x,m)-K(x,m')](m-m')(dx)\geq0, \qquad K\in\{F,G\},\quad m,m'\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d). \] For a concrete smooth class, require continuous flat functional derivatives through order four, jointly $C^6$ with bounded spatial derivatives in all their spatial arguments. A first flat derivative $\delta K/\delta m$ means \[ \left.\frac d{d\theta}K(x,(1-\theta)m+\theta m')\right|_{\theta=0^+} =\int\frac{\delta K}{\delta m}(x,m,y)(m'-m)(dy); \] higher derivatives are defined recursively. Assume the classical solutions specified below exist for each horizon $T>0$.

For $N\geq2$, $\boldsymbol x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ and $m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{x_j}$, let $v^{N,T,i}$ solve the no-common-noise Nash system \[ \begin{split} -\partial_t v^{N,T,i}-\sum_{j=1}^N\Delta_{x_j}v^{N,T,i} +\tfrac12|D_{x_i}v^{N,T,i}|^2 +\sum_{j\ne i}D_{x_j}v^{N,T,i}\cdot D_{x_j}v^{N,T,j} &=F(x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}),\\ v^{N,T,i}(T,\boldsymbol x)&=G(x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}). \end{split} \] Define $U^T(t,x,m)$ as the representative player's value in the unique monotone MFG equilibrium on $[t,T]$ with population law $m$ at time $t$. Its optimal-control dynamics are $dX_s=\alpha_s ds+\sqrt2\,dB_s$, and its cost is \[ \mathbb E\!\left[\int_t^T\left(\tfrac12|\alpha_s|^2+F(X_s,m_s)\right)ds +G(X_T,m_T)\right],\qquad X_t=x. \] Here $B$ is a standard $d$-dimensional Brownian motion, and the state dynamics are interpreted modulo $\mathbb Z^d$. The value is the infimum of this cost over bounded $\mathbb R^d$-valued controls progressively measurable with respect to the completed natural filtration of $(B_s-B_t)_{s\in[t,T]}$; no common bound is imposed across admissible controls or horizons. Here $(m_s)$ is the equilibrium population flow starting from $m$, kept fixed in the representative player's optimization. This characteristic definition specifies the master-equation solution and its terminal condition $U^T(T,x,m)=G(x,m)$.

For a function $f(\boldsymbol x)$ set \[ \mathcal C_i f(\boldsymbol x)=f(\boldsymbol x)-\int_{\mathbb T^d}f(x_1,\ldots,z,\ldots,x_N)dz, \quad \mathcal C U^T(t,x,m)=U^T(t,x,m)-\int U^T(t,z,m)dz. \] The following norm choice is editorial. Determine whether \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{T>0}\max_{1\leq i\leq N} \sup_{(t,\boldsymbol x)\in[0,T]\times(\mathbb T^d)^N} \begin{aligned}[t] \big(&|\mathcal C_i v^{N,T,i}(t,\boldsymbol x) -\mathcal C U^T(t,x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x})|\\[-2pt] &+|D_{x_i}v^{N,T,i}(t,\boldsymbol x) -D_xU^T(t,x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x})|\big)=0. \end{aligned} \] If these assumptions do not suffice, identify the additional quantitative stability hypothesis needed for this estimate.

Short English statement

Can the finite-player feedbacks and centered values approximate the MFG values with an error independent of the time horizon?

Variants and scope boundaries

An uncentered value estimate uniform over all horizons is a stronger question: a small error in an ergodic cost may accumulate linearly in $T$. Convergence of empirical trajectories, estimates only away from terminal time, and a common-noise version have different quantifiers and norms.

Source relationship and status

The source asks for convergence uniform in time; the centered norm and smooth class here are editorial specifications. Its source-date open status is not an exhaustive 2026 literature certificate.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.
