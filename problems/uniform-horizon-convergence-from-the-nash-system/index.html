Uniform-horizon convergence from the Nash system

LLM status Open unverified

C73 — Stochastic and Dynamic Games - Evolutionary Games - Repeated Games Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra ultra 20% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

GT-078 | Mean-field games | R: precise instantiation of a published question; review required

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb T^d=\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$ have normalized Lebesgue measure, and let $\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)$ denote its Borel probability measures. Fix couplings $F,G:\mathbb T^d\times\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d)\to\mathbb R$ that are smooth and Lasry--Lions monotone: \[ \int_{\mathbb T^d}[K(x,m)-K(x,m')](m-m')(dx)\geq0, \qquad K\in\{F,G\},\quad m,m'\in\mathcal P(\mathbb T^d). \] For a concrete smooth class, require continuous flat functional derivatives through order four, jointly $C^6$ with bounded spatial derivatives in all their spatial arguments. A first flat derivative $\delta K/\delta m$ means \[ \left.\frac d{d\theta}K(x,(1-\theta)m+\theta m')\right|_{\theta=0^+} =\int\frac{\delta K}{\delta m}(x,m,y)(m'-m)(dy); \] higher derivatives are defined recursively. Assume the classical solutions specified below exist for each horizon $T>0$.

For $N\geq2$, $\boldsymbol x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ and $m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{x_j}$, let $v^{N,T,i}$ solve the no-common-noise Nash system \[ \begin{split} -\partial_t v^{N,T,i}-\sum_{j=1}^N\Delta_{x_j}v^{N,T,i} +\tfrac12|D_{x_i}v^{N,T,i}|^2 +\sum_{j\ne i}D_{x_j}v^{N,T,i}\cdot D_{x_j}v^{N,T,j} &=F(x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}),\\ v^{N,T,i}(T,\boldsymbol x)&=G(x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x}). \end{split} \] Define $U^T(t,x,m)$ as the representative player's value in the unique monotone MFG equilibrium on $[t,T]$ with population law $m$ at time $t$. Its optimal-control dynamics are $dX_s=\alpha_s ds+\sqrt2\,dB_s$, and its cost is \[ \mathbb E\!\left[\int_t^T\left(\tfrac12|\alpha_s|^2+F(X_s,m_s)\right)ds +G(X_T,m_T)\right],\qquad X_t=x. \] Here $(m_s)$ is the equilibrium population flow starting from $m$, kept fixed in the representative player's optimization. This characteristic definition specifies the master-equation solution and its terminal condition $U^T(T,x,m)=G(x,m)$.

For a function $f(\boldsymbol x)$ set \[ \mathcal C_i f(\boldsymbol x)=f(\boldsymbol x)-\int_{\mathbb T^d}f(x_1,\ldots,z,\ldots,x_N)dz, \quad \mathcal C U^T(t,x,m)=U^T(t,x,m)-\int U^T(t,z,m)dz. \] The following norm choice is editorial. Determine whether \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{T>0}\max_{1\leq i\leq N} \sup_{(t,\boldsymbol x)\in[0,T]\times(\mathbb T^d)^N} \begin{aligned}[t] \big(&|\mathcal C_i v^{N,T,i}(t,\boldsymbol x) -\mathcal C U^T(t,x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x})|\\[-2pt] &+|D_{x_i}v^{N,T,i}(t,\boldsymbol x) -D_xU^T(t,x_i,m^{N,i}_{\boldsymbol x})|\big)=0. \end{aligned} \] If these assumptions do not suffice, identify the additional quantitative stability hypothesis needed for this estimate.

Short English statement

Can the finite-player feedbacks and centered values approximate the MFG values with an error independent of the time horizon?

Variants and scope boundaries

An uncentered value estimate uniform over all horizons is a stronger question: a small error in an ergodic cost may accumulate linearly in $T$. Convergence of empirical trajectories, estimates only away from terminal time, and a common-noise version have different quantifiers and norms.

Source relationship and status

The source asks for convergence uniform in time; the centered norm and smooth class here are editorial specifications. Its source-date open status is not an exhaustive 2026 literature certificate.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.

Centered Nash errors: an exact subcase and a uniform stability reduction

Problem C73-41. Status: unresolved. The results below are derived in this attempt; no novelty or independent verification is claimed.

Target and notation

The question is whether the centered values and own-state gradients of the Nash system approach the master values uniformly over every horizon $T$. Finite-horizon convergence is treated in [CDLL]; the uniform-time question is stated in [CD, Section 3.2]. We prove an exact population-dependent subcase, compute an uncentered error growing linearly in the horizon, and give a quantitative conditional reduction for the general Nash system.

Let $n=N-1$, $m^{-i}=n^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{x_j}$, and let $v_i$ solve \[ -\partial_tv_i-\sum_j\Delta_jv_i+\tfrac12|D_iv_i|^2 +\sum_{j\ne i}D_jv_i\cdot D_jv_j=F(x_i,m^{-i}),\qquad v_i(T)=G(x_i,m^{-i}). \] All torus measures are normalized. The centering operator is $\mathcal C_i h=h-\int_{\mathbb{T}} h(x_1,\ldots,z,\ldots,x_N){\mathrm d} z$; $\mathcal C U$ has the analogous meaning with the population held fixed.

An exact coupled-cost subcase

Theorem: Suppose on ${\mathbb{T}}^d$ \[ F(x,m)=f(x)+h(m),\qquad G(x,m)=g(x)+k(m), \] where $f,g$ are smooth and $h,k$ have bounded continuous flat functional derivatives through order four, jointly $C^6$ with bounded spatial derivatives in all their spatial arguments. Then there is a classical symmetric Markov-perfect equilibrium whose values satisfy, for every $N,T,t,\boldsymbol x$, \[ \mathcal C_i v_i(t,\boldsymbol x)=\mathcal C U^T(t,x_i,m^{-i}),\qquad D_iv_i(t,\boldsymbol x)=D_xU^T(t,x_i,m^{-i}). \] If the classical Nash solution is unique in the stipulated class, these equalities hold for that solution. The couplings above satisfy Lasry--Lions monotonicity, regardless of $h,k$.
Proof: Let $w$ be the smooth scalar optimal-control value solving $-w_t-\Delta w+|Dw|^2/2=f$, $w(T)=g$. Let $Y$ denote independent diffusions with drift $-Dw(s,Y_s)$ and diffusion coefficient $\sqrt2$. Define \[ q_i(t,\boldsymbol x_{-i})=E\left[\int_t^T h\left(n^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{Y_s^j}\right){\mathrm d} s +k\left(n^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{Y_T^j}\right)\right] \] with $Y_t^j=x_j$. Smooth coefficients and data give a classical solution of its linear backward equation. Set $v_i=w(t,x_i)+q_i(t,\boldsymbol x_{-i})$. Then $D_iv_i=Dw(t,x_i)$. Substitution in the Nash system leaves exactly the scalar equation for $w$ and the backward equation for $q_i$, proving it is a Nash solution. Alternatively, against the other players' independent drifts, the $h,k$ costs are unaffected by player $i$'s control. The standard square-completion verification for $w$ therefore proves optimality after every time and state vector, so these feedbacks are Markov-perfect.

In the mean field game, every representative player's optimizing drift is likewise $-Dw$, since $h(m_s),k(m_T)$ are scalars independent of that player's state and control. The flow $m_s$ is the linear Fokker--Planck flow generated by this drift, and $U^T(t,x,m)=w(t,x)+\int_t^Th(m_s){\mathrm d} s+k(m_T)$. Centering or differentiating in the own state removes the respective scalar terms from both formulas. Finally, for any $m,m'$, the monotonicity integral of $F(x,m)-F(x,m')=h(m)-h(m')$ against $m-m'$ is zero, since the latter has total mass zero. The same calculation applies to $G$. ∎

Proposition (The uncentered error really can accumulate): On ${\mathbb{T}}$, take $f=g=k=0$ and $h(m)=(\int\cos(2\pi y)m({\mathrm d} y))^2$. For the solutions in Theorem Theorem, let $\tau=T-t$ and $\lambda=(2\pi)^2$. Then \begin{align*} v_i(t,\boldsymbol x)-U^T(t,x_i,m^{-i}) =\frac1{n^2}\sum_{j\ne i}\int_0^\tau \left[\frac12+\frac12e^{-4\lambda s}\cos(4\pi x_j) -e^{-2\lambda s}\cos^2(2\pi x_j)\right]{\mathrm d} s. \end{align*} Its difference from $\tau/(2n)$ has absolute value at most $5/(8\lambda n)$. Hence no estimate of the uncentered error can be uniform in $T$ for this smooth monotone example, while the target centered error is exactly zero.
Proof: Here all optimal drifts vanish. Brownian motion with generator $\Delta$ satisfies ${\mathbb{E}}\cos(2\pi Y_s)=e^{-\lambda s}\cos(2\pi Y_0)$ and ${\mathbb{E}}\cos^2(2\pi Y_s)=1/2+(1/2)e^{-4\lambda s}\cos(4\pi Y_0)$. The empirical average of the cosines has squared expectation equal to the square of its mean plus $n^{-2}$ times the sum of these variances, by independence. The master value uses just the squared mean. Integrating their difference gives the formula. The integral of the absolute remainder for each summand is at most $1/(8\lambda)+1/(2\lambda)=5/(8\lambda)$, proving the bound. ∎

The precise lift residual

Let $U=U^T$ be a classical master solution. Write $D_mU(t,x,m,y)=D_y(\delta U/\delta m)(t,x,m,y)$ and $D^2_{mm}U$ for its second intrinsic measure derivative. The no-common-noise master equation in these conventions is \begin{align}\label{wsref-0-6d-61-73-74-65-72} -U_t-\Delta_xU+\tfrac12|D_xU|^2 &-\int\operatorname{div}_yD_mU(t,x,m,y)m({\mathrm d} y)\nonumber\\ &+\int D_mU(t,x,m,y)\cdot D_xU(t,y,m)m({\mathrm d} y)=F(x,m). \end{align} Suppose the derivatives below exist also at empirical measures. Set $w_i(t,\boldsymbol x)=U(t,x_i,m^{-i})$.
Lemma: Let $R_i$ be the left side of the Nash PDE evaluated at $w$, minus $F(x_i,m^{-i})$. Then \begin{align}\label{wsref-0-52} R_i={}&-\frac1{n^2}\sum_{j\ne i}\operatorname{Tr}D^2_{mm}U(t,x_i,m^{-i},x_j,x_j)\\ &+\frac1n\sum_{j\ne i}D_mU(t,x_i,m^{-i},x_j)\cdot [D_xU(t,x_j,m^{-j})-D_xU(t,x_j,m^{-i})].\nonumber \end{align} If, uniformly in $T,t,x,m,y$, the first factor is bounded by $A$, the absolute trace of the second measure derivative by $B$, and the flat derivative $\delta(D_xU)/\delta m$ by $L$, then $\max_i\|R_i\|_\infty\le(B+2AL)/n$.
Proof: For $j\ne i$, the empirical-measure chain rule gives $D_jw_i=n^{-1}D_mU(t,x_i,m^{-i},x_j)$ and \[ \Delta_jw_i=n^{-1}\operatorname{div}_yD_mU(t,x_i,m^{-i},x_j) +n^{-2}\operatorname{Tr}D^2_{mm}U(t,x_i,m^{-i},x_j,x_j). \] Also $D_iw_i=D_xU(t,x_i,m^{-i})$. Subtract (master), replacing its integrals by empirical sums; this yields (R). Since $m^{-j}-m^{-i}=n^{-1}(\delta_{x_i}-\delta_{x_j})$, the flat-derivative formula along the segment between these two measures gives $|D_xU(t,x_j,m^{-j})-D_xU(t,x_j,m^{-i})|\le2L/n$. There are $n$ summands, giving the bound. ∎

A sufficient uniform stability hypothesis

For a vector of functions $z=(z_i)_{i\le N}$ define \[ \|z\|_X=\max_i(\|\mathcal C_i z_i\|_\infty+\|D_i z_i\|_\infty), \qquad \|z\|_\infty=\max_i\|z_i\|_\infty. \] This is a seminorm: the kernel includes functions independent of their own coordinate. We do not presume a quotient equation closes on this kernel.
Theorem (Conditional horizon-independent estimate): Assume the uniform bounds $A,B,L$ of Lemma Lemma. Let $e_i=v_i-w_i$ and define the backward linear operator \begin{align*} (\mathcal L z)_i={}&-\partial_tz_i-\sum_j\Delta_jz_i +\tfrac12(D_iv_i+D_iw_i)\cdot D_iz_i +\sum_{j\ne i}D_jv_j\cdot D_jz_i +\sum_{j\ne i}D_jw_i\cdot D_jz_j. \end{align*} Assume its homogeneous backward solution operators $S_{t,s}$ exist, give the variation-of-constants formula for continuous forcing, and satisfy \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-73-74-61-62-69-6c-69-74-79} \|S_{t,s}z\|_X\le C\left(1+(s-t)^{-1/2}\right)e^{-\gamma(s-t)}\|z\|_\infty, \qquad t<s, \end{equation} with $C,\gamma>0$ independent of $N,T$. Then \[ \sup_{T>0}\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|v(t)-w(t)\|_X \le\frac{C(B+2AL)}{N-1}\left(\gamma^{-1}+\sqrt\pi\,\gamma^{-1/2}\right). \] This implies both terms of the convergence assertion in the question.
Proof: Subtract the equations for $v$ and $w$. The own-gradient quadratic difference is $\tfrac12(D_iv_i+D_iw_i)\cdot D_ie_i$. For $j\ne i$, the cross term difference is exactly \[ D_jv_i\cdot D_jv_j-D_jw_i\cdot D_jw_j =D_je_i\cdot D_jv_j+D_jw_i\cdot D_je_j. \] Thus $\mathcal Le=-R$, with zero terminal value because $w_i(T)=G(x_i,m^{-i})$. Variation of constants, (stability), and Lemma Lemma yield \[ \|e(t)\|_X\le\frac{C(B+2AL)}n\int_0^{T-t}(1+s^{-1/2})e^{-\gamma s}{\mathrm d} s. \] The integral over $(0,\infty)$ equals $\gamma^{-1}+\sqrt\pi\gamma^{-1/2}$. Finally, because $m^{-i}$ is independent of $x_i$, $\mathcal C_iw_i=\mathcal CU(t,x_i,m^{-i})$ and $D_iw_i=D_xU(t,x_i,m^{-i})$. This identifies the seminorm with the exact target error. ∎

The estimate (stability) is a substantive additional assumption about a coupled operator in dimension $Nd$. A spectral gap for a single representative diffusion does not prove it: the last sum couples gradients of different error components, and centering does not automatically commute with the operator. We have not established (stability), or the needed horizon-independent master derivative bounds, from the catalogue's qualitative monotonicity and smoothness assumptions. These are the first remaining estimates in this route. The theorem states a sufficient quantitative hypothesis, not an equivalence or a claim of necessity. The exact subcase and the accumulating scalar error above are unconditional within their stated model; the general target remains unresolved.
