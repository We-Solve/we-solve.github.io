One-sample matroid prophet selection

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every finite known matroid with mutually independent nonnegative weights and one independent sample from each unknown marginal law, can a distribution-free online selector achieve one fixed positive fraction of expected offline optimum, even when the measurable arrival order depends on both samples, all realized weights and the selector’s random seed? The expected optimum is assumed finite and no running-time restriction is imposed.

One-sample matroid prophet selection

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 111; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that one sample per element gives a constant-factor matroid prophet rule even when the ordering adversary sees all values and the entire random seed, with guarantee $2^{-310}$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For any known finite labeled matroid $M=(E,\mathcal I)$, let $\operatorname{OPT}_M(v)=\max_{I\in\mathcal I}\sum_{e\in I}v_e$. Take mutually independent nonnegative $S_e,V_e$, each pair identically distributed with arbitrary law $D_e$, and assume $\mathbb E\operatorname{OPT}_M(V)<\infty$. An online rule sees exactly the sample vector $S$ before arrivals and an independent seed $R$. For every measurable arrival permutation allowed to depend on $M,(D_e),S,V,R$, it claims irrevocable independent selection $A$ with \[ \mathbb E\sum_{e\in A}V_e\ge2^{-310}\mathbb E\operatorname{OPT}_M(V). \] The rule does not know the value laws or future order.

The secretary consequence fixes nonnegative weights $w$ before randomness. A rule draws an independent full seed and random prefix length, observes and rejects that prefix of a uniform permutation; then an adversary knowing the full weights, prefix and complete seed may order the suffix. The accepted independent set still satisfies \[ \mathbb E\sum_{e\in A}w_e\ge2^{-293}\operatorname{OPT}_M(w). \] The adversary may not change the prefix distribution or select the weights after seeing it.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 111; 2026-09-23), reverse exposure turns samples into safe hidden-vector evidence. Geometric weight layers and nested flats guide greedy feasible selections, while rank expansion and nominal-path guards pay for all later choices uniformly over arrival orders.
