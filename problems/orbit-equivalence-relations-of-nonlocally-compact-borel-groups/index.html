Math-3409

Orbit Equivalence Relations of Nonlocally Compact Borel Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $H$ be a Borel group in the sense intended by the report, and write $\tau_0$ for its "original topology." The extraction does not specify whether $H$ is a Borel subgroup of an ambient Polish group with its subspace topology, a standard Borel group with additional topological data, or another convention. This distinction must be resolved before the topological condition is definite.

Let $\mathcal T(H,\tau_0)$ be the class of Hausdorff locally compact second-countable group topologies $\tau$ on the same underlying group such that $\tau_0\subseteq\tau$. The inclusion expresses the source's word "extending" as refinement. Whether the Borel structure must also remain unchanged is not stated and is not silently imposed.

Define $E_0$ on $2^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ by eventual equality. On $(2^{{\mathbb{N}}})^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ define its countable product by \[ x\ E_0^{{\mathbb{N}}}\ y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \exists N_j\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \forall n\ge N_j: x(j)(n)=y(j)(n). \] Each coordinate may have its own eventual-equality threshold; no common threshold across all coordinates is required.

For a Polish $H$-space $X$ with its intended action, the orbit relation is \[ u\ E_H^X\ v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists h\in H:\ h\cdot u=v. \] A Borel reduction of $E_0^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ to $E_H^X$ is a Borel map $f:(2^{{\mathbb{N}}})^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to X$ satisfying \[ x\ E_0^{{\mathbb{N}}}\ y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)\ E_H^X\ f(y) \qquad\text{for all }x,y. \] The question asks whether, for every $H$ in the intended Borel-group class, \[ \mathcal T(H,\tau_0)=\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\text{ Polish }H\text{-space }X: E_0^{{\mathbb{N}}}\le_B E_H^X. \] The usual term Polish $H$-space means a continuous action on a separable completely metrizable space, but the topology or Borel-action convention for $H$ is not specified here. No requirement of freeness, transitivity, or a particular action is added. The question concerns the absence of an extending locally compact topology, not merely the failure of $\tau_0$ itself to be locally compact.

Short English statement

The proposed equivalence links the impossibility of refining the original group topology to a locally compact second-countable one with the existence of an action whose orbit relation contains coordinatewise eventual equality in Borel complexity. The meaning of Borel group and the action topology need clarification.
