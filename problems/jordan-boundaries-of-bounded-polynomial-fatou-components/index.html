Math-1088

Jordan Boundaries of Bounded Polynomial Fatou Components

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $P\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]$ be a polynomial, in the usual nonlinear polynomial-dynamics setting of degree at least two. Its Fatou set $F(P)$ is the open set on which the family of iterates $\{P^{\circ n}:n\geq0\}$ is normal in the spherical metric. A Fatou component is a connected component $U$ of $F(P)$; it is bounded if it is bounded as a subset of ${\mathbb{C}}$.

A Jordan curve is the image of a continuous injective map $S^1\to{\mathbb{C}}$. A bounded Jordan domain is the bounded complementary component of such a curve. The source conjecture asks whether, for every polynomial and every bounded Fatou component $U$, \[ U\text{ is a Jordan domain},\qquad\text{equivalently }\partial U\text{ is a Jordan curve}. \] The degree-at-least-two setting is conventional and not explicitly stated by the record. No connectedness assumption on the Julia set, periodicity assumption on $U$, boundary smoothness, or quasicircle conclusion is imposed. The assertion concerns every bounded component, not just immediate attracting basins.

For terminology, a periodic Siegel disk is a Fatou component on which an appropriate return iterate is holomorphically conjugate to an irrational rotation of a disk. The supplied assessment checked 2026-08-21 labels the full question partially solved: it says the Jordan-domain result is proved for every bounded polynomial Fatou component except possibly Siegel disks, and that the general Siegel-disk case remains unresolved. These are dated attributed claims; the precise hypotheses and treatment of components eventually mapping to Siegel disks are not reproduced as a theorem in the record. The corrected-verified note says the original conjecture was converted into a question without changing its content.

Short English statement

Ask whether the boundary of every bounded polynomial Fatou component is a Jordan curve. Preserve the full component-wise quantifier and the source's reported non-Siegel progress without strengthening the boundary conclusion to smoothness or a quasicircle.
