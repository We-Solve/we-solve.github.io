Representation-Theoretic Meaning of Steinberg Quotient Characters

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record calls $t(\lambda)$ the Steinberg quotient, indexed by dominant weights $\lambda$, but supplies neither its defining formula nor the ambient representation-theoretic setting. In particular, it does not specify the group $G$, the base field or its characteristic, the weight lattice, the choice determining dominance, or any restrictions on these data. Consequently, a fully specified mathematical statement cannot be recovered from this record alone.

Write $X^+$ provisionally for the unspecified set of dominant weights intended in the source. The stated task is: for every $\lambda\in X^+$, identify a natural representation-theoretic or geometric object whose character, in an appropriate specified sense, equals $t(\lambda)$. In particular, determine whether \[ \forall\lambda\in X^+\;\exists\text{ a finite-dimensional }G\text{-module }V_\lambda \quad\text{such that}\quad \operatorname{ch}(V_\lambda)=t(\lambda). \] Here $G$, its module category, and the character map remain to be specified from the original source; the display records the intended quantifiers and does not supply the missing hypotheses.

For clarity, if the intended meaning is the ordinary formal character of a representation over a field $k$, computed with respect to a torus $T\subseteq G$, its usual expression is \[ \operatorname{ch}_T(V)=\sum_{\mu\in X^*(T)}\dim_k(V_\mu)e^\mu, \] where $X^*(T)$ is the character lattice, $V_\mu$ is the weight space of weight $\mu$, and $e^\mu$ is the corresponding basis element of ${\mathbb{Z}}[X^*(T)]$. This explains a possible character convention conditionally; the supplied record does not establish that this is its precise setting or specify how $t(\lambda)$ is placed in this ring.

The supplied dated assessment reports a geometric and categorical realization through Smith--Treumann localization and parity-sheaf stalk data, while stating that identification with the ordinary character of a finite-dimensional $G$-module is not established. No precise realization theorem or hypotheses are supplied. This qualification is retained as supplied curation, not asserted here as a verified mathematical result.

Short English statement

For every dominant weight $\lambda$, seek a natural object with character equal to the Steinberg quotient $t(\lambda)$, and determine whether such a character comes from a finite-dimensional $G$-module. The record omits the definition of $t(\lambda)$ and the ambient setting. Its supplied dated assessment reports a geometric realization but leaves the ordinary module-character interpretation unresolved; that assessment requires independent verification.
