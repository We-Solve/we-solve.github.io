The GRH curve theorem and a Hecke-correlated CM conductor ray

Definitions and mathematical statement

The exact source item is a historical continuation of Conjecture 51: it says that the one-dimensional-Zariski-closure case was proved assuming "generalized Riemannian hypothesis", citing [203]. The supplied aid identifies that phrase as a typo for the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) and identifies the subject as André--Oort. The original conjecture and its ambient Shimura variety are not present in this fragment.

For context, the André--Oort assertion is that in a Shimura variety the irreducible components of the Zariski closure of a set of special points are special subvarieties, with "special" interpreted in its Shimura-theoretic sense. Thus a one-dimensional closure can be reducible: its curve components are treated by an irreducible-curve theorem, while any zero-dimensional components are special points from the original set. The aid reports [203] as Yafaev's conditional irreducible-curve theorem and separately reports the full general theorem in Pila--Shankar--Tsimerman. These historical and later claims are distinct from the supplied attempt's own contribution and require source verification.

The aid's explicit-family statement concerns only $Y(1)^n$, with the modular $j$-invariant $j:\mathbb H\to{\mathbb{C}}$, where $\mathbb H=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}:\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\}$. Fix \[ n\geq2,\qquad N_1=1,\qquad N_2,\ldots,N_n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq2}. \] Let $\Phi_N(X,Y)$ be the classical modular polynomial for cyclic $N$-isogenies and define \[ V=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in{\mathbb{C}}^n: \Phi_{N_r}(x_1,x_r)=0\ (2\leq r\leq n)\}, \] \[ C=\overline{\{(j(\tau),j(N_2\tau),\ldots,j(N_n\tau)): \tau\in\mathbb H\}}^{\mathrm{Zar}}. \] The claimed developed result is that $C$ is an irreducible special curve and an irreducible component of $V$, and that \[ \{(j(f\mathrm i),j(N_2f\mathrm i),\ldots,j(N_nf\mathrm i)): f\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\} \] consists of pairwise distinct special points Zariski dense in $C$. Here $\mathrm i^2=-1$ is fixed, so these are CM points from the fixed imaginary quadratic field ${\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm i)$ with increasing conductors; $f$ is an integer parameter, not an index on $j$. The common parameter $\tau$ selects a particular component of the simultaneous modular-polynomial intersection. The statement does not identify all of $V$ as a single irreducible curve or claim independent coordinates.

The registry labels the record solved. The supplied aid describes the full André--Oort problem as solved in the cited later literature, while classifying its own explicit-family work as a partial result. Neither label is a claim of independent mathematical review here.

Short English statement

The fragment records a historical GRH-dependent curve case of André--Oort; a supplied later reference is reported to prove the general theorem. Its separate explicit example is a fixed-field CM conductor ray, Zariski dense in the special curve selected by a common modular parameter, rather than in every component of the modular-polynomial intersection.
