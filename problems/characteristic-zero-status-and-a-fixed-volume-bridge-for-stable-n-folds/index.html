Math-89

Characteristic-zero status and a fixed-volume bridge for stable n-folds

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The historical AIM problem asks for a construction of moduli of stable $n$-folds avoiding the minimal model program (MMP), and lists three tasks, including varieties not assumed smoothable. In the stable-variety interpretation, an $n$-fold $X$ is projective, demi-normal (in particular $S_2$ with ordinary double crossings in codimension one), has $K_X$ ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier and ample, and is semi-log-canonical (slc): its normalization with the conductor boundary is log canonical. The ampleness/stability qualification is essential; boundedness of arbitrary slc varieties from a fixed number $K_X^n$ alone is not silently asserted.
  1. Prove boundedness of stable slc $n$-folds with fixed canonical volume $K_X^n=v$. Bounded means containment of all such objects in fibers of finitely many finite-type families.
  2. Prove preservation of slc singularities under sufficiently small ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Gorenstein deformations, with the appropriate flatness and canonical-sheaf base-change conditions. The source states the corresponding semi-log-terminal assertion as known.
  3. Verify the valuative properness condition for stable $n$-folds without smoothability: stable generic fibers should acquire stable extensions after the permitted finite base change, with the requisite uniqueness/separatedness for the moduli interpretation.
The source attributes construction in the smoothable case to Karu's use of semistable MMP in dimension $n+1$ and Abramovich--Karu weak semistable reduction. The previous-problem and later-Alexeev references are not included in this record.

The supplied report says the three mathematical tasks are solved in characteristic zero without smoothability: fixed-volume boundedness by Hacon--McKernan--Xu, one-parameter relative-${\mathbb{Q}}$-Gorenstein propagation of slc by Kollár, and stable extension after finite base change by Hacon--Xu. It explicitly says these solutions use MMP methods and therefore do not achieve the original MMP-avoidance aim. Complete references and theorem hypotheses are absent.

Its synthesis considers a characteristic-zero one-parameter Kollár family $f:\mathcal X\to T$, interpreted as a flat projective family with pure $n$-dimensional fibers and the required $S_2$ and codimension-one $G_1$ family conditions. Assume a single reflexive canonical power \[ \mathcal L=\omega_{\mathcal X/T}^{[a]},\qquad a>0, \] is invertible, relatively ample and compatible with base change, so its restriction is the corresponding canonical power on each fiber. If one fiber is slc, the report claims that after shrinking around it all fibers are stable and have the same canonical volume \[ K_{\mathcal X_t}^n=a^{-n}\,c_1(\mathcal L_t)^n=v. \] They therefore belong to a single fixed-volume Hacon--McKernan--Xu bounded class; a stable extension preserves the same volume. Flatness and a common ample canonical power, not slc alone, supply the stated volume comparison. This remains a conditional synthesis of supplied literature claims, with no assertion beyond characteristic zero.

Short English statement

Construct stable-variety moduli in higher dimension, including nonsmoothable objects, by boundedness, deformation stability and stable extension. The report says these tasks have characteristic-zero solutions using MMP methods, so the historical request to avoid MMP remains distinct. Its family lemma keeps nearby stable fibers and stable limits in one fixed-volume bounded class under explicit canonical-power assumptions.
