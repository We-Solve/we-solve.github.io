Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.99

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group and let $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $k>1$. For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$, let $G_i\leq G$ be a subgroup of finite index $n_i=[G:G_i]$, where the index is the number of left cosets of $G_i$ in $G$, and let $a_i\in G$. Define $a_iG_i=\{a_ig:g\in G_i\}$. Suppose these left cosets are pairwise disjoint: \[ (a_iG_i)\cap(a_jG_j)=\varnothing\qquad(1\leq i<j\leq k). \] The conjecture of Z.-W. Sun asserts that, for every such choice, there exist indices $1\leq i<j\leq k$ such that \[ \gcd(n_i,n_j)\geq k, \] where $\gcd$ denotes the greatest common divisor. No finiteness assumption on $G$, normality assumption on the subgroups, or requirement that the cosets cover $G$ is imposed. The supplied source states that the conjecture holds for $k=2,3,4$; this reported qualification is not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Z.-W. Sun conjectures that among any $k>1$ pairwise disjoint left cosets of finite-index subgroups of a group, two subgroup indices have greatest common divisor at least $k$. The supplied record labels the general conjecture open and reports that it holds for $k=2,3,4$.
