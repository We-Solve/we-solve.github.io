The following contextual descriptions are retained as reports of workshop talks, not as formulations of the problem. Tim Holzschuh is described as using the étale homotopy type, including for simply connected and possibly singular varieties over ${\mathbb{R}}$, to prove an analogue of the real section conjecture. Mair discussed condensed shapes; Lüdtke connected the section conjecture with the Chabauty--Kim approach to rational points; Kwon discussed the number theoretic Shafarevich conjecture; and Gropper discussed mapping class group techniques for outer automorphism groups of pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields. These references do not identify which, if any, conjecture is the intended target.

Further contextual topics include Kerz's inductive approach to Deligne's monodromy--weight conjecture based on an arithmetic Kashiwara conjecture; Saito's discussion of extending singular supports to mixed characteristic; Brunebarbe's discussion of fundamental groups and the Shafarevich conjecture in complex geometry; and Krämer's use of Tannakian methods, sheaves on complex abelian varieties, convolution, and big monodromy for arithmetic finiteness results related to Shafarevich's finiteness conjectures for varieties with good reduction outside a fixed set of places of a number field. Olsson discussed categorical reconstruction of point objects in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on torsors under abelian varieties. Litt discussed a criterion for a formal solution of an algebraic differential equation to be algebraic, related to the $p$-curvature conjecture. None of these descriptions supplies the hypotheses and conclusion of a standalone problem.

The summary also attributes to Shimizu a pointwise criterion for a $\mathbb Z_p$-local system to be semistable and purity for prismatic $F$-crystals; to Petrov, characteristic classes for $p$-adic étale local systems; to Niziol, Poincaré duality in proétale cohomology of partially proper rigid analytic varieties; and to Reinecke, relative mod-$n$ Poincaré duality for a smooth, proper morphism of locally noetherian analytic adic spaces. Achinger discussed tame fundamental groups of rigid analytic spaces and connections between finiteness properties and those of fundamental groups of complex analytic spaces, through reductions involving logarithmic fundamental groups in positive characteristic.
