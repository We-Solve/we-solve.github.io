Algebraic Complexity of Borel Constraint Problems

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A finite relational structure $D$ consists of a finite set and specified relations. A homomorphism preserves every relation; the product $D^4$ has coordinatewise relations. A Borel CSP instance has a standard Borel space of variables and Borel relations of the prescribed signature; a solution is a Borel homomorphism into $D$. The notation $\operatorname{CSP}_B(D)$ denotes the source decision problem for coded instances. A $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^1_2$ set is a projection of a coanalytic set; completeness uses the coding and reductions of the cited descriptive-set-theoretic formulation. The displayed identity is required for every $a,r,e\in D$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For a finite relational structure $D$, absence of a homomorphism $f:D^4\to D$ satisfying $f(a,r,e,a)=f(r,a,r,e)$ makes $\operatorname{CSP}_B(D)$ $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^1_2$-complete. Is the converse true? Can Borel complexity, efficient local solvability for directed graphs, and extension to all locally checkable labelings be characterized algebraically? [S2]

Short English statement

Does the absence of the stated four-variable algebraic operation exactly characterize the hardest Borel constraint problems, and can the same algebra explain local solvability?
