Alternativity of Parameters for $A_2$-Graded Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a possibly nonassociative ring $R$, write $(x,y,z)=(xy)z-x(yz)$ for its associator. The ring is alternative if it satisfies both alternative identities \[ (xx)y=x(xy),\qquad y(xx)=(yx)x \qquad\text{for every }x,y\in R. \] Equivalently, $(x,x,y)=(y,x,x)=0$ for all $x,y\in R$. No restriction on characteristic is needed to state these identities.

Question as supplied. Is every ring $R$ that parametrizes an $A_2$-graded group alternative? Formally, let $\operatorname{Par}_{A_2}(R,G)$ denote the source's intended assertion that $R$ parametrizes the $A_2$-graded group $G$. The requested assertion is \[ \forall R\;\left[ \left(\exists G\;\operatorname{Par}_{A_2}(R,G)\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\forall x,y\in R:\ (xx)y=x(xy)\ \text{and}\ y(xx)=(yx)x\right) \right]. \] Here $R$ ranges over the rings permitted by the intended parametrization framework. The notation $\operatorname{Par}_{A_2}$ is only a placeholder: the supplied record gives neither the axioms of an $A_2$-graded group nor the definition of parametrization. It also does not specify whether the rings must have an identity or satisfy additional hypotheses. Consequently, the displayed assertion records the intended quantifiers and conclusion but is not a fully specified standalone formulation. Associativity must not be silently imposed, since associative rings already satisfy the displayed identities.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every ring used to parametrize an $A_2$-graded group satisfies the left and right alternative identities. The source's grading and parametrization conventions are missing, so the precise class of rings covered by the question requires human review.
