Fixed-Delta Convolution-Body Extremizers

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed ambient dimension $n$, a convex body in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ means a compact convex set with nonempty interior, and $|\cdot|$ denotes $n$-dimensional Lebesgue volume. Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and a prescribed volume $V>0$, and set \[ \mathcal K_{n,V}=\{K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n:K\text{ is a convex body and }|K|=V\}. \] The supplied corrected statement asks for a characterization of \[ \mathcal M_{\delta,n,V} = \left\{K\in\mathcal K_{n,V}: |C_\delta K|\geq |C_\delta L| \text{ for every }L\in\mathcal K_{n,V} \right\}. \] Here $C_\delta K$ denotes the associated convolution body named in the source record. Its construction, normalization, and boundary convention are not supplied, so the displayed formulation is conditional on specifying that operator. The intended ambient dimension is also unspecified; the display does not assert that the question was posed in every dimension. No existence of a maximizer is assumed.

It mentions a symmetric planar radial set with radial function $\rho(v)=\cos^2(2mv)$, $v\in[0,2\pi]$, and an assertion involving every $\delta\in(0,1)$, some $m\in{\mathbb{N}}$, and a positive second derivative at $t=0$. The differentiated expression and the family depending on $t$ cannot be recovered reliably from the supplied text. This fragment supplies neither a usable definition of $C_\delta$ nor a reliable dimension restriction for the corrected extremizer question.

Short English statement

For fixed $\delta\in(0,1)$ and prescribed positive volume, characterize the convex bodies whose associated convolution bodies have maximal volume. The supplied record does not define the convolution-body operator or specify the intended ambient dimension, so a fully determined mathematical statement requires review of those points.
