Volumes of Line Bundles as Periods

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ denote the intended ground field, which the supplied statement does not specify. For a smooth projective variety $X$ over $k$, of dimension $n$, and a line bundle $L$ on $X$, define \[ \operatorname{vol}_X(L)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{n!}{m^n}\dim_k H^0\!\left(X,L^{\otimes m}\right), \] where $m$ ranges over positive integers. No assumption that $L$ is ample, effective, or big is included in the source question.

Under the usual real-period convention, a real number $p$ is a period if there exist a positive integer $r$, a semialgebraic set $D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^r$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and a rational function $f\in{\mathbb{Q}}(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ such that \[ p=\int_D f(t_1,\ldots,t_r)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t_1\cdots{\,\mathrm{d}} t_r, \qquad \int_D |f(t_1,\ldots,t_r)|\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t_1\cdots{\,\mathrm{d}} t_r<\infty. \] Here semialgebraic means describable by a finite Boolean combination of polynomial equalities and inequalities; being defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ means these polynomials have rational coefficients. The integral is taken where $f$ is defined, with any undefined subset of $D$ required to have measure zero. Write $\mathcal P_{{\mathbb{R}}}$ for this set of real periods.

With this convention, the supplied question is whether \[ \operatorname{vol}_X(L)\in\mathcal P_{{\mathbb{R}}} \] for every smooth projective variety $X$ over the intended ground field and every line bundle $L$ on $X$. The source does not specify the ground field, its characteristic, or a definition of period. Thus this is an explicit conditional formulation under the real-period convention above; the intended ground-field scope and period convention require confirmation from the source.

Short English statement

Is the asymptotic growth coefficient of the spaces of global sections of every line bundle on every smooth projective variety a period? The source supplies neither a ground field nor a definition of period; the formulation above uses the usual real-period convention and leaves the ground-field scope unresolved.
