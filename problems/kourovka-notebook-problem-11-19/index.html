Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.19

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be an absolutely free group, meaning a free group in the variety of all groups, and let $X$ be a fixed free generating set. Use the commutator convention $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$. Define the lower central series by \[ \gamma_1(F)=F,\qquad \gamma_{k+1}(F)=[\gamma_k(F),F]\quad(k\geq 1), \] where $[H,K]$ denotes the subgroup generated by all $[h,k]$ with $h\in H$ and $k\in K$.

Interpret basic commutators using a fixed Hall ordering: the elements of $X$ are basic commutators of weight $1$; higher weights follow lower weights in the ordering; and $[u,v]$ is a basic commutator of weight $\operatorname{wt}(u)+\operatorname{wt}(v)$ when $u,v$ are basic commutators, $u>v$, and, if $u=[u_1,u_2]$, then $u_2\leq v$. Fix an order among the basic commutators of each weight. Write $\mathcal B_n(X)$ for the resulting set of basic commutators of weight exactly $n$, evaluated in $F$. For $S\subseteq F$, its normal closure in $F$ is \[ \langle\!\langle S\rangle\!\rangle_F =\langle g^{-1}sg : g\in F,\ s\in S\rangle. \]

Question (attributed in the supplied statement to C. Sims). For every positive integer $n$, is \[ \gamma_n(F)=\langle\!\langle\mathcal B_n(X)\rangle\!\rangle_F? \] The question is posed for a free group with a fixed free basis. The supplied source imposes no explicit restriction on the rank of $F$, and does not specify the Hall ordering; the definitions above make the standard Hall interpretation explicit. The precise intended scope over ranks and choices of basic commutators requires source review.

The supplied record reports that D. Jackson announced a positive answer for $n\leq 5$, here understood as $1\leq n\leq 5$. This is retained as a reported announcement, without independent verification or an assertion that a proof was published.

Short English statement

For a free group with fixed free generators, does the normal closure of the basic commutators of weight exactly $n$ equal the $n$th term of its lower central series, for every positive integer $n$? The source reports a positive answer announced by D. Jackson for $1\leq n\leq 5$; the intended rank scope and commutator conventions need review.
