Math-4904

Sharp Width Bounds for Positive-Scalar-Curvature Bands

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer, and let $Y$ be a closed smooth manifold of dimension $n-1$, with $n-1\neq 4$. Here closed means compact and without boundary. Assume that $Y$ admits no smooth Riemannian metric $h$ with strictly positive scalar curvature everywhere. Set \[ M=Y\times[-1,1],\qquad \partial_-M=Y\times\{-1\},\qquad \partial_+M=Y\times\{1\}. \] For a smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $M$, smooth up to its boundary, define its band width by \[ \operatorname{width}(M,g)=\operatorname{dist}_g(\partial_-M,\partial_+M) =\inf_{\gamma} L_g(\gamma), \] where the infimum is over piecewise smooth curves $\gamma:[0,1]\to M$ with $\gamma(0)\in\partial_-M$ and $\gamma(1)\in\partial_+M$, and $L_g$ denotes Riemannian length.

The supplied conjecture asserts that, for every such $Y$, every real number $\sigma>0$, and every such metric $g$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{Scal}_g(p)\geq\sigma\qquad\text{for all }p\in M, \] one has \[ \operatorname{width}(M,g)\leq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{\sigma n}}. \] The source states no connectedness, orientability, spin, or boundary mean-curvature hypothesis; none is added here. It gives no further lower dimension bound beyond that implicit in $\dim Y=n-1$. Its supplied literature assessment concerns partial results and does not establish this full conjecture. In particular, the phrase "orientable dimensions through seven" in that assessment does not specify whether it refers to $\dim Y$ or $\dim M$, nor does it give the precise additional hypotheses of those results.

Short English statement

The conjecture bounds the distance between the two boundary components of $Y\times[-1,1]$ whenever its scalar curvature is at least $\sigma>0$ and $Y$, of dimension other than four, admits no positive-scalar-curvature metric. The proposed bound is $2\pi\sqrt{(n-1)/(\sigma n)}$. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved; this draft requires human mathematical review.
