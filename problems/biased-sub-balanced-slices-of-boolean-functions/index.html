Math-14253

Biased Sub-Balanced Slices of Boolean Functions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An indicator function on a finite set $\Omega$ is a function $f:\Omega\to\{0,1\}$. Under uniform averaging, for every nonempty subset $T\subseteq\Omega$, define \[ \mathbb E_T f=\frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{x\in T}f(x). \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify the averaging measure. Under the customary Boolean-coordinate interpretation, the balanced slice would be \[ \Omega_n=\left\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:\sum_{i=1}^n x_i=n/2\right\},\qquad n\in2{\mathbb{N}}. \] This ambient-domain convention requires confirmation; it is not defined in the supplied text.

Let $\mathcal S_n$ denote the intended family of nonempty sub-balanced slices of the ambient balanced slice, and let $d(T)$ denote their intended dimension. These are placeholders: the supplied record does not define either the permitted sub-balanced slices or their dimension.

Question, conditional on resolving these definitions. For every indicator function $f$ on the balanced slice satisfying $\mathbb E_{\Omega_n}f=1/2$, does there exist $T\in\mathcal S_n$ such that \[ d(T)=\log^*n \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbb E_Tf>0.51? \] Here $\log^*n$ is retained from the source. The usual base-two convention is the number of successive base-two logarithms needed to obtain a value at most $1$; the record does not specify a convention, rounding adjustment, or admissible dimension restriction.

The source also asks for the weaker conclusion that the dimension tends to infinity. A precise possible asymptotic reading of that alternative is: there exist an integer-valued function $g(n)\to\infty$ and a threshold $N$, both independent of $f$, such that for every admissible $n\ge N$ and every such $f$, there exists $T\in\mathcal S_n$ with \[ d(T)\ge g(n),\qquad \mathbb E_Tf>0.51. \] This quantifier interpretation, the admissible values of $n$, and the threshold convention require confirmation. The record does not determine a fully specified mathematical statement without these qualifications.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether every Boolean function of mean $1/2$ on a balanced slice has a sub-balanced slice on which its mean exceeds $0.51$, with dimension $\log^*n$, or at least with dimension growing without bound. The permitted sub-slices, dimension convention, averaging measure, and precise asymptotic quantifiers need clarification.
