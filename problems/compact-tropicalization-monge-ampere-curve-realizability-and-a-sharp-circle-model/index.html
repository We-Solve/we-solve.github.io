Compact tropicalization, Monge–Ampère, curve realizability, and a sharp circle model

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Zharkov's A.23 contains three related research questions, using Gross's tropical Calabi--Yau setup: a compact base $B$ with an integral affine structure on its nonsingular part $B_0$, and local potentials differing by affine functions. First, construct natural compact tropicalizations, modifying the valuation map by removing "pseudo-pods" while retaining as much affine structure as possible. A polarization may enter the construction, but the desired underlying affine structure should be algebraic and independent of that choice. Second, define a tropical Monge--Ampère operator in a polarization class in $H^1(B,\operatorname{Aff}(B,{\mathbb{R}}))$, where the sheaf represents the relevant local affine functions, and determine appropriate existence and uniqueness statements for its measures/potentials. The source proposes piecewise-linear differential graphs in place of smooth ${\,\mathrm{d}} K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n\times({\mathbb{R}}^n)^*$, interpreting the primal/dual volume condition distributionally. It warns that lattice-regulated bendings may destroy uniqueness, citing different Voronoi decompositions on higher-dimensional tori.

Third, define tropical Gromov--Witten calculations on Calabi--Yau spaces and determine which tropical curves lift to holomorphic curves. The source describes interval area by pairing its primitive tangent with dual displacement and an eigen-direction restriction for ends at singularities. The singularity model and precise correspondence hypotheses are not specified. Its reference to Mikhalkin's surface correspondence is not an unrestricted theorem for every tropical Calabi--Yau surface; the source itself warns that higher-dimensional tropical curves need not be realizable.

The supplied aid reports only a precise one-dimensional model. Let $B={\mathbb{R}}/L{\mathbb{Z}}$, $L>0$, and $a>0$. Consider convex functions $K:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying \[ K(x+L)-K(x)=ax+b \] for some $b\in{\mathbb{R}}$, modulo addition of arbitrary affine functions. Distributional differentiation gives a periodic positive Radon measure $\mu=K''$, descending to $B$, with exact normalization $\mu(B)=a$. The aid reports a bijection between these affine-equivalence classes and positive Radon measures of mass $a$. Thus a prescribed measure determines the potential modulo affine functions; the polarization degree $a$ alone does not determine a unique measure.

Finite piecewise-linear potentials modulo translation periods correspond exactly to measures with finitely many atoms. To state the integral-slope qualification with $b$ fixed, choose a cut with no atom at zero and write \[ \mu=\sum_i m_i\delta_{t_i},\quad0<t_i<L,\quad m_i>0,\quad\sum_i m_i=a. \] A potential with the specified $b$ has integer slopes exactly when \[ m_i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\forall i,\qquad \frac{b-\sum_i m_i(L-t_i)}L\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] The fraction is its initial slope. If arbitrary real affine functions are quotiented out, $b$ changes by arbitrary multiples $cL$; the fixed-$b$ congruence must therefore not be imposed as an intrinsic condition on the measure alone. This distinguishes real potentials, integral representatives and a fixed polarization normalization.

The aid mentions Berkovich analytification and extended tropicalizations as modern compact replacements, hypothesis-dependent tropical/nonarchimedean Monge--Ampère results, and continuing global obstructions for superabundant curve realizability. It supplies no full references and does not claim a general solution of the three-part program. The appended Chapters B and C begin new material and are not part of A.23.

Short English statement

The source asks about compact tropicalization, tropical Monge--Ampère theory and realizability of tropical curves. The supplied aid gives a circle model where prescribed measures determine convex potentials modulo affine functions. It does not give uniqueness of a measure from a polarization alone or solve the general curve-counting problem.
