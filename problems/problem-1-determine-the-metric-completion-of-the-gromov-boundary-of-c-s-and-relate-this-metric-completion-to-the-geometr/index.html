Problem 1: — Determine the metric completion of the Gromov boundary of C(S) and relate this metric completion to the geometry of C…

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal C(S)$ be the curve complex/graph of the source's finite-type surface $S$, with essential-curve vertices and its graph metric $d$. The surface complexity and low-complexity edge conventions are not supplied. Its Gromov boundary is denoted $\partial\mathcal C(S)$. Problem 1 asks to determine the metric completion of that boundary and relate it to the geometry of $\mathcal C(S)$.

A metric completion requires the metric on the boundary, not only its topology. For example a visual metric involves a basepoint, a visual parameter and a comparison with the boundary Gromov product, derived from \[ (x\mid y)_o=\tfrac12\left(d(o,x)+d(o,y)-d(x,y)\right). \] The extract does not specify its boundary metric or the sense of the desired geometric relationship. The completion would add equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences for that chosen metric; no particular space of added points is proposed here. The summary's ending-lamination identification of the boundary is topological context, not by itself a computation of this metric completion.

The following context constructs a coarse curve choice from Teichmüller space $\mathcal T_{g,m}$, the marked complete finite-volume hyperbolic metrics on $S$. It identifies that space with a domain in ${\mathbb{C}}^{3g-3+m}$. It cites a Bers constant $\chi>0$ giving an $h$-geodesic pants decomposition with every curve of length at most $\chi$. It further reports a bound $D>0$ on the curve-graph diameter of all essential curves of $h$-length at most $\chi$, independent of $h$. Thus choosing one such curve defines \[ \Psi:\mathcal T_{g,m}\longrightarrow\mathcal C(S),\qquad \sup_h d(\Psi(h),\Psi'(h))\le D \] for any two choices. Dependence of the called-universal constants on surface type must be checked.

The source describes the Teichmüller metric as complete Finsler and equal to the Kobayashi metric of that complex domain, with a unique geodesic through any two distinct points. It says each geodesic line is determined by its endpoints in the Thurston boundary $\mathrm{PML}(S)$. Their measured-lamination supports together fill $S$: every relevant essential simple closed curve meets at least one transversely. It connects these laminations to a holomorphic quadratic differential, citing [Ke], Section 4. The passage introduces a result on unparametrized uniform quasi-geodesics, implicit in Masur--Minsky [MM1] and proved using Rafi [R] in [H4], Section 4, but its actual statement is absent. No missing quasi-geodesic constants, theorem, or implication for completion is invented.
