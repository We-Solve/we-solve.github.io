Math-4406

Separation Between Higher Graph Equivalence Levels

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not define its graph category or the term $q$-equivalent. The following formulation uses the usual edge-Ramsey interpretation, provisionally taking all graphs to be finite, simple, and undirected, with copies understood as subgraphs rather than induced subgraphs. These conventions require confirmation against the source; no connectedness or absence of isolated vertices is assumed unless stated explicitly.

For an integer $q\geq 2$, write $[q]=\{1,\ldots,q\}$. For graphs $G$ and $H$, define $G\longrightarrow_q H$ to mean that every edge-coloring $c:E(G)\to[q]$ admits an injective map $f:V(H)\to V(G)$ and a color $i\in[q]$ such that \[ \{f(u),f(v)\}\in E(G) \quad\text{and}\quad c(\{f(u),f(v)\})=i \qquad\text{for every }\{u,v\}\in E(H). \] The coloring need not use every color. Define $q$-Ramsey equivalence by \[ H\sim_q H' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall G\;\left(G\longrightarrow_q H\ \Longleftrightarrow\ G\longrightarrow_q H'\right), \] where $G$ ranges over the provisional graph category above.

Under these conventions, the source asks:

  1. Does \[ \forall H,H'\;\left(H\sim_2 H'\ \Longrightarrow\ H\sim_3 H'\right) \] hold?
  2. Do there exist graphs $H,H'$ such that \[ H\sim_{100}H' \qquad\text{and}\qquad H\not\sim_{101}H'? \] Here $H\not\sim_{101}H'$ means that some graph $G$ satisfies exactly one of $G\longrightarrow_{101}H$ and $G\longrightarrow_{101}H'$.
  3. The source reports known pairs satisfying $H\sim_3 H'$ and $H\not\sim_2 H'$ in which at least one graph is disconnected, and asks whether this is coincidental. A precise possible interpretation is whether there exist connected graphs $H,H'$ satisfying \[ H\sim_3 H'\qquad\text{and}\qquad H\not\sim_2 H'. \] The source's explanatory question does not explicitly specify this existential target, so this interpretation remains provisional.
The supplied record labels the problem open and reports the existence of the mentioned known pairs; these are supplied source claims, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

Under the provisional edge-Ramsey interpretation, two graphs are $q$-equivalent when exactly the same host graphs force a monochromatic copy of either graph in every $q$-coloring of their edges. The questions concern whether equivalence for two colors implies equivalence for three, whether equivalence for $100$ colors can fail for $101$, and whether connected graphs can exhibit equivalence for three colors but failure for two. The graph conventions and the precise intended meaning of the final question require source confirmation.
