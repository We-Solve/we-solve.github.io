Finite-Field Detection of Area-Preserving Dynamics

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether reduction of an area-preserving map over finite fields can detect dynamical signatures of order, chaos, or time-reversal symmetry in the original system. An arbitrary real smooth area-preserving map has no canonical finite-field reduction: an arithmetic model must first be specified.

For example, in a rational algebraic setting one may have a birational map $F$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, preserving a two-form $\omega$ by $F^*\omega=\omega$ on its regular domain. At a prime of good reduction its formulas induce a map $\overline F_p$ on the appropriate finite-field domain. Denominators, indeterminacy points, and their iterates must be handled; it is not automatic that every point of ${\mathbb{F}}_p^2$ belongs to a permutation system. If an invariant finite set $S_p$ is chosen on which $\overline F_p$ is bijective, one may study its cycle lengths, orbit statistics, and reversing symmetries as $p$ varies. This is an illustrative arithmetic framework, not a source-specified restriction to ${\mathbb{Q}}$ or to a particular surface.

An algebraic first integral is a nonconstant rational function $I$ satisfying $I\circ F=I$; where reduction is defined and remains nonconstant, this gives the necessary reduced identity $\overline I_p\circ\overline F_p=\overline I_p$. Time-reversal symmetry can be expressed by an involution $R$ with \[ R^2=\operatorname{id},\qquad RFR=F^{-1}. \] At compatible good reductions these identities descend. A reduced identity or suggestive orbit distribution alone is not stated to imply the corresponding characteristic-zero property.

The research question is to determine which such finite-field data, over what family of fields, with what hypotheses and error guarantees, identify meaningful features of the original dynamics. "Order" and "chaos" have no single supplied definition: algebraic integrability, entropy and Lyapunov behavior are distinct possibilities, while every permutation of a finite set has periodic orbits. Consequently finite-field cycles must be interpreted as signatures, not literally as a definition of real chaotic behavior.

The supplied literature assessment checked 21 August 2026 reports rigorous necessary tests for integrability and reversibility, and probabilistic signatures in studied classes, but no general if-and-only-if identification theorem. That dated assessment is preserved without claiming an independent negative theorem or a current literature survey.

Short English statement

Can orbit data and symmetry identities after finite-field reduction reveal integrability, chaos or time reversal in an area-preserving map? A precise detection theorem needs an arithmetic model, good-reduction rules and a definition of the original dynamical property. The supplied assessment reports necessary tests and class-specific statistical signatures, not a general equivalence.
