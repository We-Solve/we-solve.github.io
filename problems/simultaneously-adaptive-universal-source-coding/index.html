Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A coding probability $Q_n$ assigns probabilities to length-$n$ source blocks. Redundancy is the excess expected codelength relative to the source entropy; minimax redundancy minimizes the worst such excess over a class.

A code $C\subseteq\Sigma^N$ is a set of permitted length-$N$ words. Its Hamming minimum distance is the minimum number of unequal coordinates of two distinct words. For a linear $[N,K,D]_q$ code, rate is $K/N$ and relative distance is $D/N$; decoding recovers a codeword from corrupted observations.

For a discrete random variable $X$, entropy is $H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)$, and mutual information is $I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)$. Coding rates, capacities, and exponents depend on the stated channel or source model and error criterion.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let $(C(\alpha))_{\alpha\in A}$ be source subclasses with nontrivial minimax redundancy rates. Does there exist a single sequence of coding probabilities $Q_n$, independent of $\alpha$, whose redundancy is asymptotically optimal, or optimal within a fixed factor, simultaneously for every subclass in the collection?

The source convention matters

Boucheron's report, printed pages 1921--1924, concerns stationary memoryless sources on a countable alphabet, and uses block redundancy $D(P^n\Vert Q_n)$, not channel-code error or Hamming distance. It allows a constant approximation factor depending on the subclass index. Its original theorem gives a $\log\log n$ factor for regularly varying envelope classes; it does not state full constant-factor adaptivity over every such envelope. We use natural logarithms below, so changing the log base scales every redundancy by the same constant. The mixture route and bounded-redundancy example were suggested by the coordinating Codex GPT-6 model and independently checked within this model-assisted attempt. They are standard known mechanisms, not claimed novel source-coding theorems.

A full countable-mixture sufficiency theorem

Let $C_j$, $j\ge1$, be a countable collection of source classes and let $Q_{j,n}$ be coding probabilities for each block length. Choose fixed $w_j>0$ with $\sum_jw_j=1$ and put \[ Q_n(x)=\sum_{j\ge1}w_jQ_{j,n}(x). \] This is a probability distribution: sums over the countable block alphabet can be interchanged for nonnegative terms, giving total mass $\sum_jw_j=1$. For every $j$, $Q_n\ge w_jQ_{j,n}$ pointwise. Thus for any source $P$, \[ D(P^n\Vert Q_n)\le D(P^n\Vert Q_{j,n})+\log(1/w_j). \] To justify the inequality even on an infinite alphabet, when the right-hand divergence is infinite it is trivial; otherwise integrate the pointwise logarithmic ratio inequality. Negative parts are integrable: on entries with $P^n(x)<Q_n(x)$, writing $z=P^n(x)/Q_n(x)\in[0,1]$ gives $-P^n(x)\log z=Q_n(x)(-z\log z)\le Q_n(x)/e$, and summing bounds their total by $1/e$. The same observation applies to $Q_{j,n}$, so extended expectations are well defined and monotone integration is legitimate. A zero coding mass at a positive source mass gives the usual infinite divergence.

Define \[ R_j^*(n)=\inf_Q\sup_{P\in C_j}D(P^n\Vert Q), \qquad R(Q,C_j)=\sup_{P\in C_j}D(P^n\Vert Q). \] Taking suprema gives $R(Q_n,C_j)\le R(Q_{j,n},C_j)+\log(1/w_j)$. Suppose $0<R_j^*(n)<\infty$ eventually, $R_j^*(n)\to\infty$, and $R(Q_{j,n},C_j)\le(1+o_j(1))R_j^*(n)$ for each fixed $j$. Divide the mixture inequality by $R_j^*(n)$; the fixed overhead $\log(1/w_j)$ vanishes relatively. Since every coding probability has worst-case redundancy at least the infimum $R_j^*(n)$, we conclude \[ \frac{R(Q_n,C_j)}{R_j^*(n)}\longrightarrow1 \quad\text{for every fixed }j. \] The single sequence $Q_n$ is independent of $j$. The convergence need not be uniform in $j$, and evaluating an infinite mixture need not be computationally effective. The statement establishes existence under its hypotheses, not an efficient online code for the entire source family.

Bounded redundancy changes the conclusion

On alphabet $\mathbb N$, let $C_j$ consist of the two iid point-mass sources concentrated on symbols $2j$ and $2j+1$. Their length-$n$ outputs are the two distinct constant words $(2j)^n,(2j+1)^n$. For every $n$ the minimax redundancy is exactly $\log2$. Indeed assigning each word mass $1/2$ achieves that risk; conversely their two masses sum to at most one, so one is at most $1/2$ and the corresponding point-mass divergence is at least $\log2$.

There is no asymptotically optimal coding sequence for even two distinct classes in this family. If there were, for each of the four constant words it would have $-\log Q_n(x)\le(1+o(1))\log2$, hence $Q_n(x)\ge\tfrac12(1-o(1))$. Their sum would tend to at least two, contradicting total mass one. There are only two classes in this argument, so their separate asymptotic thresholds can be maximized; no unjustified uniform limit is used.

More generally, a common factor $C<\infty$ for $J$ disjoint classes would force each of their $2J$ words to have mass at least $2^{-C}$, so $1\ge2J\,2^{-C}$ and $C\ge\log_2(2J)$. For infinitely many classes, no one finite common factor can hold eventually for every class. Given a proposed $C$, choose a finite $J>2^{C-1}$ and then a block length beyond the largest of those $J$ individual eventual thresholds to get the same contradiction. If the bound is stated with an additional vanishing error, use $C+1$ first and choose $J>2^C$.

The source, however, permits class-dependent factors. They do exist here: use the mixture of the two-word uniform distributions with weights $w_j=2^{-j}$, $j\ge1$. Each word in class $j$ then has mass $2^{-j-1}$, giving risk $(j+1)\log2$, exactly a factor $j+1$ of its minimax risk. Thus the example does not refute the source's class-dependent constant-factor possibility. Nor does its bounded redundancy refute the divergent-rate sufficiency theorem above.

Where the mixture attempt stops for the envelope problem

The source's family of envelope functions is not presented as a countable list of classes. A probability distribution has positive atoms at only countably many indices: for every integer $r\ge1$ there are at most $r$ atoms of mass at least $1/r$, and every positive atom lies in one of these finite sets. Consequently assigning a fixed positive individual weight to every member of an uncountable family is impossible. A continuous mixture or countable approximation could still work, but the pointwise inequality $Q_n\ge w_jQ_{j,n}$ with fixed $w_j>0$ would have to be replaced by a quantified approximation argument. In particular one would need nearby dominating envelopes and control of their redundancy loss and description overhead. Such an argument is not supplied here.
