Math-5380

Branch-aware well balancing and a resonance obstruction for oscillatory forcing

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Debora Amadori asks about numerical approximation of the scalar balance law \[ \partial_tu^\varepsilon+\partial_x f(u^\varepsilon) =\varepsilon^{-1}h(x/\varepsilon),\qquad u^\varepsilon(0,x)=u_0(x),\quad u_0(x+1)=u_0(x),\quad\varepsilon>0. \] The supplied assumptions are $h\in C({\mathbb{R}})$, $h(y+1)=h(y)$, $\int_0^1h(y)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y=0$, $f\in C^1({\mathbb{R}})$, $f(u)\to+\infty$ as $|u|\to\infty$, and the printed condition $uf'(u)>0$. The latter can only hold for $u\ne0$; with that interpretation $f$ decreases on $( -\infty,0)$, increases on $(0,\infty)$, and has minimum $f(0)$. No regularity or boundedness class for $u_0$, solution concept, convergence norm, time interval, or desired error bound is specified. The research request is to construct and analyze numerical approximations capturing the oscillations as $\varepsilon\to0$, and more broadly to study resonance phenomena; it is not a stated convergence theorem.

For a periodic primitive $H'=h$, fix its additive constant, for example by $\int_0^1H=0$. The exact conservation form is \[ \partial_tu^\varepsilon+\partial_x\left(f(u^\varepsilon)-H(x/\varepsilon)\right)=0. \] On the unit torus ${\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$, the forcing must itself be 1-periodic. For nonconstant $h$ of minimal period 1 this requires $\varepsilon^{-1}=N\in{\mathbb{N}}$; the original initial-data condition alone does not impose this compatibility on arbitrary $\varepsilon$. Consider this compatible sequence in the following auxiliary statements.

The supplied research aid describes separated stationary entropy states. Let $\phi_+$ and $\phi_-$ be the inverse branches of $f$ on $[0,\infty)$ and $( -\infty,0]$, respectively. If \[ C+\min H>f(0),\qquad U^\pm_{C,N}(x)=\phi_\pm(C+H(Nx)), \] then $f(U^\pm_{C,N})-H(Nx)=C$. The aid reports that every periodic BV stationary entropy solution staying away from branch contact belongs to one of these two pure branches; the precise separation and entropy hypotheses require review. Their means are \[ m_\pm(C)=\int_0^1\phi_\pm(C+H(y))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y, \] and their oscillation amplitudes do not depend on $N$. For nonconstant $H$ these oscillatory profiles converge weakly to their means and do not converge strongly in $L^p({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})$, $1\le p<\infty$. These statements concern fixed separated equilibria, not arbitrary time-dependent solutions.

The aid proposes reconstructing equilibria from exact cell averages on any mesh $I_j$: \[ \overline U_j=|I_j|^{-1}\int_{I_j}\phi_\pm(C_j+H(Nx))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] For a fixed branch this relation determines the admissible $C_j$ uniquely. When all $C_j=C$, reconstruction gives the same invariant flux $f(U)-H=C$ at every interface, permitting a consistent finite-volume flux to preserve that equilibrium even on a mesh that does not resolve $N^{-1}$. This is an equilibrium-preservation target; no general stability or convergence rate is supplied.

At branch contact the aid gives a separate example, using a different convenient additive normalization: \[ f(u)=u^2/2,\quad H(y)=1-\cos(2\pi y),\quad h(y)=2\pi\sin(2\pi y). \] For $N\in{\mathbb{N}}$, independently choose $\sigma_j\in\{-1,1\}$, $0\le j<N$, and put \[ U_\sigma(x)=\sigma_j\sqrt{2H(Nx)} \quad (j/N\le x\le(j+1)/N). \] The aid reports $2^N$ distinct periodic Lipschitz stationary entropy states with invariant flux zero. Their means are $4(\pi N)^{-1}\sum_j\sigma_j$. For even $N$, exactly $\binom{N}{N/2}$ of these states have mean zero. Thus mass and invariant flux alone do not determine the microscopic branch pattern. This obstruction does not establish a full numerical theory of resonance.

Short English statement

Amadori seeks numerical approximations for a scalar conservation law with rapidly oscillating forcing and mentions pointwise oscillation results. The supplied aid treats separated stationary branches and exact equilibrium cell averages, then exhibits many branch-contact equilibria with the same mass and flux. These partial results leave the general numerical approximation and resonance questions open in the supplied formulation.
