Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.39

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a free group of finite rank at least $2$. An element $w\in F$ is a proper power if $w=u^m$ for some $u\in F$ and integer $m\ge 2$. For $w\in F\setminus\{1\}$ and an integer $n\ge 1$, define \[ G(F,w,n)=\left(F*\langle t\rangle\right)\big/\left\langle\!\left\langle t^n w^{-1}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle, \] where $\langle t\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}$, the symbol $*$ denotes the free product, and the denominator is the normal closure of $t^n w^{-1}$ in that free product. A group is linear of degree $2$ over a field $K$ if it admits an injective homomorphism into $\operatorname{GL}_2(K)$, the group of invertible $2\times2$ matrices over $K$.

Question (A. Jaikin-Zapirain, Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.39). For every such $F$, every nonidentity element $w\in F$ that is not a proper power, and every integer $n\ge 1$, do there exist a field $K$ of characteristic $0$ and an injective homomorphism \[ \rho:G(F,w,n)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(K)? \] The field $K$ may depend on $F,w,n$; no single common field is requested.

Supplied motivation. The source relates this question to Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.39(b), which asks whether the free ${\mathbb{Q}}$-group $F^{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ is linear over a field. Here a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-group is understood in the standard uniquely divisible sense: for every element $g$ and every integer $m\ge1$, there is exactly one element $h$ with $h^m=g$. The free ${\mathbb{Q}}$-group on a free basis of $F$ is the free object on that basis among uniquely divisible groups. The motivational question asks for a faithful representation into $\operatorname{GL}_d(L)$ for some field $L$ and some positive integer $d$; the supplied wording imposes neither degree $2$ nor a characteristic condition on this separate question.

Short English statement

For every nonabelian finitely generated free group $F$, nontrivial element $w$ that is not a proper power, and integer $n\ge1$, does adjoining an element $t$ with $t^n=w$ produce a group admitting a faithful two-dimensional representation over some field of characteristic $0$? The supplied source attributes the question to A. Jaikin-Zapirain and motivates it by the linearity question for the free ${\mathbb{Q}}$-group $F^{{\mathbb{Q}}}$.
