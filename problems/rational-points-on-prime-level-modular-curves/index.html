Rational Points on Prime-Level Modular Curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p>37$ be a prime. The supplied record does not define its modular-curve notation. Under the usual conventions, $X_0(p)$ is the smooth projective modular curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ whose noncuspidal geometric points classify pairs $(E,C)$, where $E$ is an elliptic curve and $C\subset E[p]$ is a cyclic subgroup of order $p$. The Atkin--Lehner involution is \[ w_p(E,C)=(E/C,E[p]/C), \] and $X_0^+(p)=X_0(p)/\langle w_p\rangle$.

For the other two curves, let \[ C_{\mathrm{sp}}=\{\operatorname{diag}(a,b):a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times\} \subseteq\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p). \] Choose an ${\mathbb{F}}_p$-basis of ${\mathbb{F}}_{p^2}$, and let $C_{\mathrm{ns}}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p)$ be the image of ${\mathbb{F}}_{p^2}^{\times}$ acting on ${\mathbb{F}}_{p^2}$ by multiplication. For $\star\in\{\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{ns}\}$, put \[ N_\star=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p):gC_\star g^{-1}=C_\star\}. \] Under the standard normalizer convention, $X_\star^+(p)$ is the smooth projective modular curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ associated with $N_\star$. Its noncuspidal geometric points classify elliptic curves with a basis of their $p$-torsion modulo the action of $N_\star$, up to isomorphism. Changing the chosen basis used to define $C_{\mathrm{ns}}$ gives an isomorphic modular curve.

For any of these curves $X$, write $\operatorname{Cusps}(X)$ for its geometric cusps. A noncuspidal geometric point is a CM point if it has a modular representative with underlying elliptic curve $E/\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{End}_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}(E)\supsetneq{\mathbb{Z}}. \] On $X_0^+(p)$, this means the image of a CM point of $X_0(p)$; the condition is independent of the representative because the two elliptic curves are isogenous. Denote the resulting set by $\operatorname{CM}(X)$. Rationality here means rationality of the point on the coarse modular curve; it does not require a chosen elliptic-curve representative or level structure to be defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$.

With these explicitly stated conventions, the supplied question is whether \[ \forall p\text{ prime},\quad p>37,\quad \forall X\in\{X_0^+(p),X_{\mathrm{sp}}^+(p),X_{\mathrm{ns}}^+(p)\},\qquad X({\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\operatorname{Cusps}(X)\cup\operatorname{CM}(X). \] Thus every rational noncuspidal point is required to be a CM point. This is a proposed assertion, not an established conclusion here.

There is an unresolved qualification: the supplied literature assessment calls the first case the Borel case, whereas the usual Borel modular curve is $X_0(p)$, and the displayed source statement uses its Atkin--Lehner quotient $X_0^+(p)$. These are different rational-point questions. The draft retains the supplied superscript $+$; the intended first curve and the applicability of the supplied literature assessment require source review.

Short English statement

For every prime $p>37$, the supplied question asks whether every rational point on each of the three displayed modular curves is either a cusp or a CM point. The notation $X_0^+(p)$ is retained, but its compatibility with the source's description of a Borel case remains unresolved. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation, not a conclusion independently established here.
