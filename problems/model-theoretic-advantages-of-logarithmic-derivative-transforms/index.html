Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the usual interpretation, a real entire function is a holomorphic function $F\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$ satisfying $F({\mathbb{R}})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$. Its associated expansion of the real field is \[ \mathcal R_F=({\mathbb{R}};0,1,+,\cdot,<,F|_{{\mathbb{R}}}). \] The supplied record does not specify a narrower class of functions or additional hypotheses on $F$.

If $F\not\equiv0$, put \[ U_F=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:F(z)\ne0\},\qquad G\colon U_F\to{\mathbb{C}},\qquad G(z)=\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}\left(z\frac{F'(z)}{F(z)}\right). \] Here primes denote complex differentiation. This formula defines $G$ on $U_F$; its real restriction is initially defined on $U_F\cap{\mathbb{R}}$. Source question. For an expansion of the real field by a real entire function $F$, what technical, mathematical, and model-theoretic advantages arise from studying \[ G(z)=\left(z\frac{F'(z)}{F(z)}\right)' \] instead of $F$ itself?

Short English statement

The question asks why the transform $G=(zF'/F)'$ is useful when studying a real field expanded by a real entire function $F$.
