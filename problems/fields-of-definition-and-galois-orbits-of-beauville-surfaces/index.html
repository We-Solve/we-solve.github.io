Fields of Definition and Galois Orbits of Beauville Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a Beauville surface $S$, determine a field of definition of $S$ and the $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})$-orbit of its moduli point $[S]$.

The source gives no presentation of $S$. In the usual terminology a Beauville surface is a rigid surface isogenous to a product of curves of genus at least two; in an unmixed presentation one writes $S=(C_1\times C_2)/G$ with a free diagonal finite-group action and the relevant rigidity data. The extraction does not specify an unmixed or mixed case, curves, group, or ramification data, so the unmixed presentation is only explanatory and is not imposed as a source hypothesis.

Fixing the algebraic-closure and complex-field identifications needed to interpret the question, a field of definition $K\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ is one for which there exists a surface $S_K$ with \[ S_K\times_K\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\cong S. \] If the given surface is initially viewed over ${\mathbb{C}}$, the right-hand side is interpreted after the chosen embedding into ${\mathbb{C}}$ and requires the algebraic model intended by the source. The question asks for a field of definition, not explicitly a smallest such field or a descent theorem to the field of moduli.

For $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})$, let $S^\sigma$ be the conjugate surface. The orbit to determine is \[ \{[S^\sigma]:\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\}. \] This concerns isomorphism classes of surfaces as moduli points, unless markings or covering data are expressly included. The stabilizer is $\{\sigma:S^\sigma\cong S\}$; its fixed field describes the field-of-moduli notion, which is not automatically identified here with a field of definition.

A concrete answer requires the particular Beauville surface or a precisely specified input family and the intended moduli equivalence. The dated assessment says the definition of $S$ is omitted. Naming the general surface class does not recover those missing instance data, and no surface, group, field, or Galois orbit is invented.

Short English statement

The question asks for an algebraic field model and all Galois conjugate moduli classes of a Beauville surface. The specific surface is missing, and the field of moduli must be kept distinct from an actual field of definition.
