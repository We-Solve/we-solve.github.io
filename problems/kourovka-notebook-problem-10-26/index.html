Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.26

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a free group with identity $1_F$. For $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ and $g\in F$, write $g^\varphi=\varphi(g)$. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ denote the distinct unknowns occurring in the equation, and let $i_1,\ldots,i_n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$; indices may repeat, so different automorphisms may be applied to the same unknown.

Part (b): requested decision problem. Given a word expression $w$ in $n$ argument slots and automorphisms $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)$, does there exist an algorithm that always halts and decides whether \[ \exists(g_1,\ldots,g_m)\in F^m\qquad w\left(\varphi_1(g_{i_1}),\ldots,\varphi_n(g_{i_n})\right)=1_F? \] Here the automorphisms are prescribed data, and the elements $g_1,\ldots,g_m$ are the unknowns. For a coefficient-free group word, $w$ is a finite word in formal symbols $Y_1^{\pm1},\ldots,Y_n^{\pm1}$, evaluated by substitution. The supplied record does not specify whether this coefficient-free convention is intended or whether coefficients from $F$ are permitted. It also does not specify the rank of $F$, whether $F$ is fixed or varies with the input, or effective representations of the group and automorphisms. Thus the displayed solvability predicate is explicit, but the precise scope of the algorithmic question remains qualified by these unresolved input conventions.

Archived part (a). Given $a,b\in F$ and $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)$, does there exist an algorithm deciding whether \[ \exists x\in F\qquad a\varphi(x)=xb? \] The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer to part (a) and cites the references listed below. The original text suggests that this question may be useful for solving the problem of equivalence of two knots; this is preserved as source motivation, without asserting a reduction or application. The supplied registry labels the overall problem partially solved, with part (a) archived and part (b) retained in the main section. These are supplied source classifications, not an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

Part (b) asks for an algorithm deciding whether a word equation has a solution in a free group when each occurrence of an unknown may be transformed by a prescribed automorphism. Repeated indices refer to the same unknown. The intended free-group rank, input representations, uniformity, and allowance of coefficients need clarification. Part (a), concerning $a\varphi(x)=xb$, is reported solved in the supplied archive.
