Geometry of Continued Fractions — Combinatorial structure of sails

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the source's dimension parameter $n\geq3$, the literal task is: prove the existence of a cone realizing a single non-periodic combinatorial structure of a sail.

In a standard contextual model, a pointed cone $C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ with vertex $0$ gives a lattice convex hull \[ K_C=\operatorname{conv}\left((C\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^n)\setminus\{0\}\right), \] and the boundary faces facing the origin form its sail $\Sigma(C)$. A combinatorial structure would specify the incidence relations of the vertices, edges and higher faces, and realization would require a combinatorial isomorphism from the specified complex to $\Sigma(C)$. The exact source conventions for cones, lattice points and sail boundaries are not supplied, so this is context rather than a recovered definition.

No particular non-periodic complex is supplied, and "single" does not specify whether the task is to choose one example with a fully described structure or realize one already prescribed elsewhere in the source. The notion of periodicity, including the acting group and what part of the combinatorial or lattice structure it preserves, is also undefined. Thus the exact existential/constructive assertion cannot be determined from the isolated statement.

The supplied assessment instead discusses realizing arbitrary non-periodic combinatorial structures. A universal claim that every admissible non-periodic complex is realizable is stronger than constructing one realization, and is not silently substituted for the literal source. A precise target requires recovering the class of admissible complexes, the periodicity definition, the dimension convention, and which quantifiers are intended.

Short English statement

Construct a cone with the non-periodic sail structure sought by the source, in dimensions $n\geq3$. The original wording asks for a single structure; the later summary broadens this to arbitrary structures without supplying the missing formulation. The summary is explicitly moderate-confidence OPEN-TRIAGE, reports no verified resolution in its search, and mentions related periodic/algebraic realizability work.
