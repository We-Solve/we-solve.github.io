Math-1011

Uniform Rank Bounds for Elliptic Function Fields

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $T$ be transcendental over $F$, and let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $F(T)$. Write \[ r_F(E)=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\left(E(F(T))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\right) \] for its rank, allowing this definition even when finite generation has not been established. In the characteristic-zero part of the question, the source restricts to "nonconstant" curves. A customary intended meaning is nonisotriviality, expressed by $j(E)\notin F$; merely not having a model over $F$ is a different condition, so the intended meaning must be checked.

The wording "as $E$ and/or $F$ vary" leaves two questions to distinguish. For a fixed characteristic-zero field $F$, is there an integer $B_F\geq0$ such that \[ \forall E/F(T)\text{ in the intended nonconstant class},\qquad r_F(E)\leq B_F? \] Is there moreover one integer $B\geq0$, independent of both $F$ and $E$, such that \[ \forall F\ (\operatorname{char}F=0)\quad \forall E/F(T)\text{ in that class},\qquad r_F(E)\leq B? \] The source singles out fields $F\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}$, including the case $F={\mathbb{C}}$; this is a subclass of characteristic-zero fields, not a claim that every such field embeds in ${\mathbb{C}}$. It also asks, more broadly, what can be said about ranks over $F(T)$ for general fields $F$, without giving a definite bound or specifying the intended scope in positive characteristic.

The raw statement calls $E(F(T))$ finitely generated, whereas the cleaned statement omits that description. Finite generation and the treatment of constant or isotrivial curves and their constant-field contribution must be reviewed under the precise hypotheses; they are not imposed here without qualification.

Short English statement

For nonconstant elliptic curves over $F(T)$ in characteristic zero, are the ranks uniformly bounded for fixed $F$, or even by one bound as $F$ also varies? The source also asks about general constant fields, without a precise universal claim.
