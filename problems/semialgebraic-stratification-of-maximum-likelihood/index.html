Semialgebraic stratification of maximum likelihood

A semialgebraic set is described by finitely many polynomial equalities and inequalities combined by Boolean operations. Many finite-dimensional statistical parameter spaces are semialgebraic. Maximum-likelihood estimation optimizes a likelihood over that space, and stratification divides it into geometrically regular pieces on which rank or boundary behavior can be studied.

Research attempt: A semialgebraic likelihood graph still needs boundary and fiber analysis

Consider Bernoulli data with $n_1$ successes and $n_0$ failures, nonnegative integers with $N=n_0+n_1>0$. On the closed interval $0\le p\le1$ the likelihood is $p^{n_1}(1-p)^{n_0}$, interpreting a zero exponent as one. If both counts are positive, its log derivative is $(n_1-Np)/(p(1-p))$, changing sign exactly at $p=n_1/N$. If one count is zero, the likelihood is monotone and its unique maximizer is the corresponding endpoint. Thus the closed-model MLE graph is the semialgebraic relation $Np=n_1$, $0\le p\le1$ with the count constraints. For the open interval, either zero-count case instead has no maximizer: its supremum is approached only at an excluded endpoint. An algebraic score equation alone does not record that distinction.

Now parameterize the same closed family by $p=\theta^2$, $-1\le\theta\le1$. For $0<n_1<N$ its MLE set is the two points $\theta=\pm\sqrt{n_1/N}$. They yield one fitted distribution. For $n_1=0$ the set is $\{0\}$, and for $n_0=0$ it is $\{-1,1\}$. This proves an exact parameter-fiber stratification in a small model, including endpoint cases. It shows why a count of parameter maximizers cannot by itself count distinct statistical explanations.

As a further audit, take $n_0=n_1=1$. The polynomial likelihood is $\theta^2(1-\theta^2)$, with derivative $2\theta(1-2\theta^2)$. Its stationary point zero is a strict local minimum, whereas $\pm1/\sqrt2$ are the maxima. Listing all roots of the polynomial score therefore does not solve the likelihood optimization even in degree four. The second derivative at zero is two, verifying the rejected candidate.

The concrete route works here because monotonicity between finitely many critical points is explicit. No general efficient stratification algorithm or complexity guarantee follows for arbitrary semialgebraic statistical models.
