Math-4973

Absence of Absolutely Periodic Euclidean Billiard Orbits

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source conjecture. Euclidean billiards have no absolutely periodic orbits.

The supplied record does not define "absolutely periodic" or specify the dimension, boundary regularity, convexity, or admissible class of billiard tables. Consequently, a fully specified universal conjecture cannot be recovered without further source clarification. The following gives an explicit conditional interpretation consistent with the record's distinction between finite-order degeneracies and infinite-order examples.

In a Euclidean billiard table $Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$, a trajectory travels along straight segments and reflects at a regular boundary collision according to \[ v_{\mathrm{out}}=v_{\mathrm{in}}-2\langle v_{\mathrm{in}},n(x)\rangle n(x), \] where $n(x)$ is a unit normal at the collision point $x$. Let $T$ denote the collision map on collision states consisting of a boundary point and an outgoing unit velocity. Its iterates are considered only where the relevant collisions and return map are defined. For the interpretation below, restrict to non-tangent collisions at smooth boundary points for which the required iterate is $C^\infty$ on a neighborhood of the state.

A periodic state satisfies $T^m(p)=p$ for some integer $m\geq1$; here $m$ need not be the least period. If "absolutely periodic" means that an iterate agrees with the identity to infinite order at a periodic state, its precise local condition is as follows. In local coordinates $\varphi$ near $p$, put $z_0=\varphi(p)$ and \[ F(z)=\varphi\left(T^m(\varphi^{-1}(z))\right)-z. \] The condition is \[ D^\alpha F(z_0)=0 \qquad\text{for every multi-index }\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_0^r, \] where $r$ is the dimension of the collision phase space and ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. This includes the periodicity condition when $|\alpha|=0$.

Under this interpretation, the conjecture asserts that for every table in the intended admissible class, every integer $m\geq1$, and every regular periodic state $p$ with a smooth local return map, there exists a multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\geq1$ such that \[ D^\alpha F(z_0)\ne0. \] The intended admissible class and the identification of absolute periodicity with this infinite-order condition require confirmation. Vanishing through any prescribed finite order does not by itself establish vanishing at every order.

Short English statement

The source conjectures that Euclidean billiards have no absolutely periodic trajectories. Interpreting this as infinite-order agreement of a local return map with the identity gives the conditional statement above. The intended billiard class and terminology remain unspecified; the supplied partial-resolution assessment is dated curation requiring independent review.
