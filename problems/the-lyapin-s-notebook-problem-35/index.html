The Lyapin's Notebook — Problem 35

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A semigroup $S$ is regular if \[ \forall s\in S\ \exists t\in S:\quad sts=s. \] A group $G$ is periodic if every element has finite order: \[ \forall g\in G\ \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}:\quad g^n=1_G. \] Under the interpretation of the source's phrase "power one" as the power semigroup of nonempty subsets, put \[ \mathcal P_+(G)=\{A\subseteq G:A\ne\varnothing\}, \qquad AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}. \] An embedding $\phi:S\hookrightarrow\mathcal P_+(G)$ is an injective map satisfying $\phi(st)=\phi(s)\phi(t)$ for all $s,t\in S$.

The requested characterization, conditional on this interpretation, is: find necessary and sufficient conditions on a regular semigroup $S$ for \[ \exists\text{ a periodic group }G\ \exists\text{ an injective semigroup homomorphism } \phi:S\longrightarrow\mathcal P_+(G). \] The supplied excerpt does not define "power one" or specify whether the empty subset is admitted. If the full power set is intended, replace $\mathcal P_+(G)$ by $\mathcal P(G)$, with the same setwise multiplication. These conventions require source confirmation; no equivalence of the resulting embedding conditions is asserted here.

A semigroup is completely regular if every element belongs to a subgroup of the semigroup. The source reports that, for completely regular semigroups, the necessary and sufficient conditions are "inversion and periodicity," citing $[\mathrm{Bersh+Kubl}]$. The excerpt does not define "inversion." One possible interpretation is the inverse-semigroup property: for every $s\in S$, there exists a unique $t\in S$ such that $sts=s$ and $tst=t$. This interpretation remains unconfirmed. In the usual semigroup terminology, periodicity means that $\{s^n:n\geq1\}$ is finite for every $s\in S$. The reported completely regular case is retained as supplied background, with its terminology and reference unresolved.

Short English statement

Characterize the regular semigroups that embed into a power semigroup of a periodic group. The subset convention and the reported completely regular case need clarification, especially the meaning of "inversion."
