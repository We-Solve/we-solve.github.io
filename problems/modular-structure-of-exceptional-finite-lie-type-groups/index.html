Modular Structure of Exceptional Finite Lie-Type Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record presents three related classification problems, rather than a single conjectural identity. Their precise ranges are not fully specified.

Write $r$ for the characteristic of the defining finite field and $p$ for the modular prime; the record does not specify their relationship. A standard ambient description of a finite group of Lie type is $G=\mathbf G^F$, where $\mathbf G$ is a connected reductive algebraic group over $\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_r$ and $F$ is a Frobenius or Steinberg endomorphism with finite fixed-point group. Central quotients, derived subgroups, and simple versions also occur under the name "finite groups of Lie type"; the intended versions are not specified here. A prime is bad for a root system if it divides a coefficient of the highest root of an irreducible component, expressed in simple roots. This root-system definition does not resolve which prime the record means by "bad characteristic."

  1. Radical subgroups. For a finite group $H$, let $O_p(H)$ denote its largest normal $p$-subgroup. A subgroup $R\leq G$ is $p$-radical if it is a $p$-subgroup and \[ R=O_p\left(N_G(R)\right), \qquad N_G(R)=\{g\in G:gRg^{-1}=R\}. \] Determine the radical subgroups of exceptional finite groups of Lie type in the bad-characteristic cases intended by the source. For each specified pair $(G,p)$, a classification up to $G$-conjugacy would determine \[ \{R\leq G:R\text{ is a }p\text{-subgroup and }R=O_p(N_G(R))\}/G. \] The record does not specify the radical prime, whether "bad characteristic" refers to $r$ or $p$, or whether conjugacy-class representatives are the intended form of the answer. Thus it does not determine the pairs $(G,p)$ over which this request is quantified.

  2. Modular decomposition matrices. For a finite group $G$ and prime $p$, let $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ denote its ordinary irreducible characters and $\operatorname{IBr}_p(G)$ its irreducible Brauer characters over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$. Let $G_{p\text{-reg}}$ be the elements whose orders are prime to $p$. The decomposition numbers are defined by \[ \chi\big|_{G_{p\text{-reg}}} =\sum_{\varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}_p(G)}d_{\chi\varphi}\varphi, \qquad d_{\chi\varphi}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}, \] and the $p$-modular decomposition matrix is \[ D_p(G)=(d_{\chi\varphi})_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G),\, \varphi\in\operatorname{IBr}_p(G)}. \] An ordinary basic set means a subset $B\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ whose restrictions to $G_{p\text{-reg}}$ form a ${\mathbb{Z}}$-basis of the lattice generated by $\operatorname{IBr}_p(G)$. Unitriangularity for such a set means that, after appropriate orderings, the square submatrix indexed by $B$ and $\operatorname{IBr}_p(G)$ is triangular with every diagonal entry equal to $1$. These definitions also have blockwise versions.

    Determine the $p$-modular decomposition matrices for finite groups of Lie type, including the basic-set and unitriangularity questions intended by the source, and obtain complete determinations for the exceptional families. The record does not specify restrictions on $p$, whether the matrices are whole-group or blockwise targets, whether selected character families are intended, or which basic sets and orderings are prescribed. It does not assert a precise universal unitriangularity theorem.

  3. Modular Harish--Chandra series. In the usual cross-characteristic setting $p\ne r$, let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$. For a rational parabolic subgroup $P=LU$ of $G$, with Levi subgroup $L$ and unipotent radical $U$, Harish--Chandra induction is \[ R_L^G(X)=\operatorname{Ind}_P^G \left(\operatorname{Inf}_L^P X\right). \] A simple $kL$-module $X$ is cuspidal if its Harish--Chandra restriction to every proper rational Levi subgroup of $L$ vanishes. The Harish--Chandra series associated with a cuspidal pair $(L,X)$ consists of the isomorphism classes of simple $kG$-modules that occur as quotients of $R_L^G(X)$.

    Completely determine the modular Harish--Chandra series for the groups of Lie type intended by the source: identify the relevant cuspidal pairs, up to the appropriate $G$-conjugacy, and identify the simple modules in each series. The supplied record does not state the cross-characteristic hypothesis, the range of primes, or the precise group families. The preceding standard definition therefore explains one usual setting without resolving the intended scope, particularly any defining-characteristic interpretation.

All three requests retain the source's demand for determination, including complete exceptional-family decomposition results and complete modular Harish--Chandra series. The missing scope restrictions must be resolved from the underlying source before this can serve as a fully quantified mathematical problem.

Short English statement

The record asks for three classifications: radical subgroups of exceptional finite groups of Lie type in bad characteristic, modular decomposition matrices with basic-set and unitriangularity information, and complete modular Harish--Chandra series. The relevant primes, precise group versions, and several representation-theoretic scope restrictions remain unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
