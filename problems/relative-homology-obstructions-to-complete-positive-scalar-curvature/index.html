Relative Homology Obstructions to Complete Positive Scalar Curvature

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $Y$ be an $n$-dimensional manifold with boundary $\partial Y$, and write $M=\operatorname{int}(Y)=Y\setminus\partial Y$. The supplied statement uses groups $\Gamma'$ and $\Gamma$, a homomorphism $\iota\colon\Gamma'\to\Gamma$, and classifying data denoted by $\nu$. For a discrete group $G$, $BG$ denotes a classifying space for $G$. The intended classifying data must induce a homomorphism on relative homology carrying a rational relative fundamental class to \[ \alpha=\nu_*[Y,\partial Y]\in H_n(B\Gamma,B\Gamma';{\mathbb{Q}}). \] Here $H_n$ denotes relative singular homology with rational coefficients. The hypotheses ensuring the existence of $[Y,\partial Y]$, the precise classifying data, and the interpretation of the target pair are not specified in the supplied record. Thus the following questions are conditional on these data being well defined; they do not silently supply compactness, orientability, spin, or connectivity assumptions.

A positive-scalar-curvature metric on $M$ means a smooth Riemannian metric $g$ satisfying $\operatorname{Scal}_g(x)>0$ for every $x\in M$. Completeness means geodesic completeness. Finite volume means $\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)<\infty$. No uniform positive lower bound for scalar curvature is stated.

Intended obstruction questions. Assuming $\alpha\ne0$, does each of the following assertions hold separately?

  1. If $\iota\colon\Gamma'\to\Gamma$ is injective, then $M$ admits no complete positive-scalar-curvature metric: \[ \alpha\ne0\ \text{and}\ \iota\ \text{injective} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nexists\,g\text{ on }M\text{ with }g\text{ complete and }\operatorname{Scal}_g>0. \]
  2. Without additionally requiring injectivity, $M$ admits no complete positive-scalar-curvature metric of finite volume: \[ \alpha\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nexists\,g\text{ on }M\text{ with }g\text{ complete},\ \operatorname{Scal}_g>0,\ \operatorname{Vol}_g(M)<\infty. \]
The finite-volume condition concerns the same metric whose completeness and scalar curvature are in question.

Supplied special-case qualification. The report excerpt identifies the case in which both $Y$ and $\partial Y$ are aspherical and asserts that $\alpha$ is then automatically nonzero. For a connected manifold, aspherical means that its universal cover is contractible; a disconnected boundary would require a componentwise interpretation. The automatic nonvanishing is retained as a supplied source assertion, conditional on the intended fundamental-class and classifying-map setup. The excerpt does not provide enough surrounding hypotheses to state it here as an independently justified general fact.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a nonzero rational relative fundamental class, after pushforward to the indicated classifying spaces, rules out complete positive scalar curvature when the boundary-group homomorphism is injective, and separately rules out complete positive scalar curvature with finite volume. The source also highlights an aspherical special case. Missing hypotheses concerning the fundamental class and classifying data require review. The supplied status is partially solved, not an independently verified conclusion.
