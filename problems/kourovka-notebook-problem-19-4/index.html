Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group is finitely generated free if it has a finite free basis. A finitely generated free abelian group is a group isomorphic to ${\mathbb{Z}}^n$ for some integer $n\geq 0$. A cyclic group is a group generated by one element.

The supplied record asks the following two questions.

  1. (a) Let $I$ be an infinite index set and let $(F_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of finitely generated free groups. If $G$ is their free product with cyclic amalgamation, must \[ \psi(G)=1 \] hold?
  2. (b) Let $I$ be an infinite index set and let $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of finitely generated free abelian groups. If $G$ is their free product with cyclic amalgamation, must \[ \psi(G)=1 \] hold?

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The supplied record does not define $\psi(G)$, so the displayed conclusions retain an undefined source symbol. In particular, interpreting $1$ as a trivial subgroup would require knowing that $\psi(G)$ denotes a subgroup. The record also does not specify the amalgamation construction for infinitely many factors: it does not identify a common cyclic subgroup and its embeddings, or describe an iterated amalgamation or an underlying graph of groups. It gives no explicit restrictions on the orders of the cyclic amalgamating groups, their possible triviality, the ranks of the factors, or the cardinality of the infinite family. Thus these questions preserve the supplied wording but do not determine fully specified mathematical assertions without additional source context.

Short English statement

The source asks whether $\psi(G)=1$ for a free product of infinitely many finitely generated free groups with cyclic amalgamation, and asks the same question for finitely generated free abelian factors. The meaning of $\psi$ and the intended infinite amalgamation construction are missing from the supplied record.
