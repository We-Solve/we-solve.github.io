Definitions and conventions.
Buoyancy reversal is the production of negatively buoyant mixtures during mixing of cloudy and clear air, for example through evaporation near a cloud-top inversion. Buoyancy drives vertical acceleration; radiative heating or cooling supplies an external thermal forcing. Nonlinear coupling means that the combined evolution cannot be accounted for by adding two independent linear responses.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source concerns cloud-top entrainment and specifically lists additional unresolved roles of shear, evaporation rates, droplet settling, and clustering. The extracted coupling question has no single specified PDE or coupling coefficient, so none is invented.

An explicit mixed-response test.
Let $x$ represent a small mixing/evaporation anomaly and consider the imposed scalar relaxation law $\dot x=-ax+F+R+cx^2$, with $a>0$, forcing amplitudes $F,R$, and $c\ne0$. The equilibrium tending to zero with $s=F+R$ is \[ x_*(s)=\frac{a-\sqrt{a^2-4cs}}{2c} =\frac{s}{a}+\frac{cs^2}{a^3}+O(s^3). \] It exists for sufficiently small real $s$, and its linearized derivative $-a+2cx_*=-\sqrt{a^2-4cs}<0$, so it is locally stable. The combined response is not additive: \[ x_*(F+R)-x_*(F)-x_*(R)=\frac{2c}{a^3}FR+O((|F|+|R|)^3). \] Thus even separately additive forcing terms can interact through nonlinear state response.
A measurable diagnostic, not a constitutive derivation.
The mixed derivative $\partial_F\partial_Rx_*(0,0)=2c/a^3$ is a precise quantity distinguishing nonlinear coupling from the sum of isolated responses. If $c=0$, it vanishes and superposition is exact; near the discriminant boundary the stable relaxation rate weakens, so a small-forcing expansion is no longer uniform.
