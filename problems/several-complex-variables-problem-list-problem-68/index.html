Several Complex Variables Problem List — Problem 68

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $k\geq 1$, write ${\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})$ for complex projective $k$-space. For a holomorphic self-map $F:{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})$, interpret its degree as its algebraic degree: the common degree $d$ of homogeneous coordinate polynomials representing $F$, with no common zero in ${\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}\setminus\{0\}$. A point $p$ is periodic of least period $n\geq 1$ if $F^n(p)=p$ and $F^j(p)\neq p$ for $1\leq j<n$. It is repelling if every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of the complex-linear derivative \[ D(F^n)_p:T_p{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow T_p{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}}) \] satisfies $|\lambda|>1$.

Under these conventional interpretations, the first question is whether \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every integer }k\geq 1\text{ and every holomorphic }F:{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\text{ with }\deg F\geq 2,\\ \text{there exist }n\geq 1\text{ and }p\in{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\text{ of least period }n\text{ such that} \quad \operatorname{Spec}\left(D(F^n)_p\right)\subseteq\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\lambda|>1\}. \end{gathered} \] The source does not explicitly specify the range of $k$, the base field, or the convention for degree; the formulation above makes the usual complex-projective interpretation explicit.

The source separately asks whether meromorphic maps have periodic orbits. If this means meromorphic self-maps $G:{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})$, an ordinary periodic orbit would consist of distinct points $p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1}$, for some $n\geq 1$, satisfying \[ p_j\in{\mathbb{P}}^k({\mathbb{C}})\setminus I(G),\qquad G(p_j)=p_{(j+1)\bmod n}\quad(0\leq j<n), \] where $I(G)$ is the indeterminacy locus. This supplies a precise meaning of an orbit avoiding indeterminacy, but the intended class of meromorphic maps remains unspecified: the source does not state explicitly whether the same domain, degree restriction, or a dominance hypothesis is intended, nor whether another convention involving indeterminacy is allowed. Consequently, the second question cannot be given an unqualified universal formulation from the supplied record.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether every holomorphic self-map of complex projective space of algebraic degree at least two has a periodic point at which all eigenvalues of the return-map derivative have modulus greater than one. The source also asks whether meromorphic maps have periodic orbits, but leaves their admissible class and the treatment of indeterminacy unspecified.
