Kourovka Notebook Problem 12.55

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a prime number $p$ and an integer $n\geq 5$, let \[ f(n,p)=\#\{[G]_{\cong}:G\text{ is a finite group and }|G|=p^n\}, \] where $[G]_{\cong}$ denotes the isomorphism class of $G$. Counting groups up to isomorphism is the conventional interpretation of the supplied wording; the source record does not explicitly specify this convention.

The question attributed to A. Mann asks whether, for every fixed integer $n\geq 5$, the function $p\mapsto f(n,p)$, with domain the prime numbers, is increasing. The supplied record does not specify whether increasing means strictly increasing or nondecreasing. Thus the two possible readings are \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ n\geq 5,\quad \forall p,q\text{ prime},\quad p<q\ \Longrightarrow\ f(n,p)<f(n,q), \] or, under the nondecreasing interpretation, \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ n\geq 5,\quad \forall p,q\text{ prime},\quad p<q\ \Longrightarrow\ f(n,p)\leq f(n,q). \] The intended inequality requires clarification from the source.

Short English statement

For each fixed integer $n\geq 5$, does the number of isomorphism classes of groups of order $p^n$ increase as the prime $p$ increases? The supplied wording leaves strict versus weak increase unspecified.
