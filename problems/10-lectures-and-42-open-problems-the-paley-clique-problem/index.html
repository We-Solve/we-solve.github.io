Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the Paley graph $G_p$ has vertex set $\mathbb{F}_p$, with distinct $u,v$ adjacent exactly when $u-v$ is a nonzero square in $\mathbb{F}_p$. Since $-1$ is a square, adjacency is symmetric. A clique is a vertex subset whose distinct pairs are all adjacent; $\omega(G_p)$ is the largest possible cardinality of a clique.

The Boolean clique formulation maximizes $\sum_v x_v$ subject to $x_v^2=x_v$ and $x_ux_v=0$ for nonedges $uv$. Its degree-four sum-of-squares upper bound replaces evaluation at a Boolean vector by a linear functional $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}$ on real polynomials of degree at most four, with \[ \widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[1]=1,\qquad \widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[f^2]\ge0\ (\deg f\le2), \] and $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[(x_v^2-x_v)q]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[x_ux_vq]=0$ for every polynomial $q$ of degree at most two and every relevant vertex or nonedge. Maximizing $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[\sum_vx_v]$ is a semidefinite relaxation: every genuine clique supplies a feasible functional, so its value upper-bounds $\omega(G_p)$. The question concerns the growth of these quantities as $p$ tends to infinity through the stated primes.

Scope qualification.
The prime-order convention is the one used in the author's lecture context; it should not be silently extended to square prime powers, which have different clique behavior. Kunisky and Yu's 2023 paper supplies a lower bound on the relaxation's value and numerical evidence, not a determination of the true clique number. The 2026 Randomstrasse101 problem collection still poses polylogarithmic clique growth and polynomial improvement by degree-four sum of squares as conjectures. Verbatim supplied catalogue question

What is the clique number of the Paley graph? Can the the SOS degree 4 analogue of the theta number help upper bound it?

Attempt and results

The target concerns primes tending to infinity and both the actual clique number and the degree-four SOS relaxation. We work with precisely the pseudoexpectation constraints in the preserved definition. A feasible pseudoexpectation is positive on squares of degree at most two; it is not assumed to be a probability distribution on actual cliques.
A degree-four certificate for every triangle-free graph.
Let $T=\sum_i x_i$, and let $m_j=\widetilde{\mathbb E}[T^j]$. Boolean reduction makes every repeated variable idempotent. In a triangle-free graph every set of at least three distinct vertices contains a nonedge, so every monomial on such a set of total degree at most four has zero pseudoexpectation: factor the nonedge and use its constraint with the remaining polynomial of degree at most two. Consequently, by expanding powers and grouping one- and two-vertex supports, \[ m_3=3m_2-2m_1,\qquad m_4=7m_2-6m_1. \] For completeness, a fixed unordered pair appears twice in $T^2$, six times in $T^3$, and fourteen times in $T^4$; a single vertex appears once in each. These coefficients give the two identities. Positivity of the square of the degree-two polynomial $T(2-T)$ implies \[ 0\le\widetilde{\mathbb E}[T^2(2-T)^2] =4m_2-4m_3+m_4=2m_1-m_2. \] Positivity of $(T-m_1)^2$ gives $m_2\ge m_1^2$. Combining them yields $m_1^2\le2m_1$, hence $m_1\le2$. If the graph contains an edge, evaluation at its two-vertex clique attains two, so the relaxation value is exactly two. All constraint multipliers used have allowed degree; no unproved pointwise-to-pseudoexpectation inference is needed.
Application and failed asymptotic extension.
For $p=5$ the nonzero squares are $1,4$, so $G_5$ is the cycle with steps $\pm1$. The neighbors of any vertex differ by two, a nonsquare; thus it is triangle-free. It has an edge, hence both clique number and SOS value are two. For $p=13$, squares are $1,3,4,9,10,12$, and $\{0,1,4\}$ is a triangle: its pairwise differences are $\pm1,\pm3,\pm4$. Thus the crucial vanishing of three-vertex moments cannot be used for all Paley primes. Indeed actual evaluation at that triangle gives $m_3-3m_2+2m_1=27-27+6=6\ne0$. This explicitly falsifies the proposed unchanged extension of the degree-four certificate. It does not imply that another degree-four certificate is weak.
What remains after symmetry.
Automorphisms $x\mapsto ax+b$ with nonzero square $a$ preserve adjacency. Averaging a feasible functional over this finite group preserves every constraint, because each transformed square is still a square of the same degree, and preserves the objective. Vertex transitivity therefore permits equal first moments. But it does not force clique-supported third and fourth moments to vanish; the concrete $p=13$ evaluation already shows why that extra assumption would be false. A proof controlling these surviving moments as $p$ grows is missing.

Context and adversarial audit

Kunisky and Yu's primary CCC 2023 paper was inspected at its abstract on 11 October 2026. It reports a lower bound of order $p^{1/3}$ for the SOS relaxation value. That is not a lower bound on the actual clique number. The present certificate is an elementary special-case calculation, not a new asymptotic Paley result.
