Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the Paley graph $G_p$ has vertex set $\mathbb{F}_p$, with distinct $u,v$ adjacent exactly when $u-v$ is a nonzero square in $\mathbb{F}_p$. Since $-1$ is a square, adjacency is symmetric. A clique is a vertex subset whose distinct pairs are all adjacent; $\omega(G_p)$ is the largest possible cardinality of a clique.

The Boolean clique formulation maximizes $\sum_v x_v$ subject to $x_v^2=x_v$ and $x_ux_v=0$ for nonedges $uv$. Its degree-four sum-of-squares upper bound replaces evaluation at a Boolean vector by a linear functional $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}$ on real polynomials of degree at most four, with \[ \widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[1]=1,\qquad \widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[f^2]\ge0\ (\deg f\le2), \] and $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[(x_v^2-x_v)q]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[x_ux_vq]=0$ for every polynomial $q$ of degree at most two and every relevant vertex or nonedge. Maximizing $\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[\sum_vx_v]$ is a semidefinite relaxation: every genuine clique supplies a feasible functional, so its value upper-bounds $\omega(G_p)$. The question concerns the growth of these quantities as $p$ tends to infinity through the stated primes.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
What is the clique number of the Paley graph? Can the the SOS degree 4 analogue of the theta number help upper bound it? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The prime-order convention is the one used in the author's lecture context; it should not be silently extended to square prime powers, which have different clique behavior. Kunisky and Yu's 2023 paper supplies a lower bound on the relaxation's value and numerical evidence, not a determination of the true clique number. The 2026 Randomstrasse101 problem collection still poses polylogarithmic clique growth and polynomial improvement by degree-four sum of squares as conjectures. These primary sources were inspected on 2026-10-11.
