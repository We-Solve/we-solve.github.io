Bounded Negativity and Infinite Families of Negative Curves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface over ${\mathbb{C}}$, and let $\mathcal C(X)$ denote the set of reduced irreducible curves on $X$. Curves are counted as distinct closed subvarieties, rather than as numerical or linear equivalence classes. For $C\in\mathcal C(X)$, write $C^2=C\cdot C\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ for its self-intersection in the intersection pairing on $X$. Its arithmetic genus is \[ p_a(C)=1-\chi(C,\mathcal O_C),\qquad \chi(C,\mathcal O_C)=\sum_{i=0}^{1}(-1)^i\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}H^i(C,\mathcal O_C). \] No smoothness assumption on $C$ is imposed.

The bounded-negativity conjecture in the supplied record asks whether \[ \forall X\text{ smooth projective over }{\mathbb{C}}\text{ of dimension }2,\quad \exists b_X\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\quad \forall C\in\mathcal C(X),\qquad C^2\geq-b_X. \] The bound may depend on $X$.

The record also asks whether, for every integer $g\geq2$, there exists a smooth projective surface $X_g$ over ${\mathbb{C}}$ such that \[ \left\{C\in\mathcal C(X_g): C^2=-1\text{ and }p_a(C)=g\right\} \quad\text{is infinite}. \] Here the integer domain for $g$ expresses that $g$ is an arithmetic genus. This formulation of the additional question reads its word "curves" as referring to the reduced irreducible curves specified in the preceding conjecture; the supplied wording does not explicitly repeat those restrictions, so this interpretation requires review. The surface may depend on $g$, while infinitely many distinct curves must lie on that one surface for each fixed $g$. The condition $C^2=-1$ alone does not impose smoothness or rationality.

Both questions are presented as questions, with the supplied registry labeling the record open. That label and its supplied verification metadata are not independent mathematical verification.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether each smooth complex projective surface has its own lower bound for the self-intersection of all reduced irreducible curves. The additional question asks whether every integer arithmetic genus $g\geq2$ occurs for infinitely many distinct curves of self-intersection $-1$ on some such surface, possibly depending on $g$. The reduced irreducible interpretation of the additional question remains a source qualification requiring review.
