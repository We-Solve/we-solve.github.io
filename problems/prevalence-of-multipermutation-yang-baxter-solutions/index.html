Prevalence of Multipermutation Yang–Baxter Solutions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite nonempty set $X$, write $X^2=X\times X$. A map $r:X^2\to X^2$ satisfies the set-theoretic Yang--Baxter equation if \[ (r\times\mathrm{id}_X)(\mathrm{id}_X\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id}_X) = (\mathrm{id}_X\times r)(r\times\mathrm{id}_X)(\mathrm{id}_X\times r) \quad\text{on }X^3. \] Its size is $|X|$. Write $r(x,y)=(\sigma_x(y),\tau_y(x))$. Nondegeneracy means that every $\sigma_x:X\to X$ and every $\tau_y:X\to X$ is bijective; involutivity means $r^2=\mathrm{id}_{X^2}$.

For nondegenerate involutive set-theoretic solutions, the usual retraction identifies $x,y\in X$ when $\sigma_x=\sigma_y$; the quotient carries the map induced by $r$. With $\operatorname{Ret}^0(X,r)=(X,r)$ and subsequent retractions defined iteratively, such a solution is called a multipermutation solution if $\operatorname{Ret}^m(X,r)$ has one element for some integer $m\geq 0$. This definition specifies the usual nondegenerate involutive convention only; the supplied record does not establish that this is the intended class.

To express the counting target, fix a class $\mathcal C$ of finite solutions, a definition of multipermutation within that class, and a counting convention. For labelled counting, let $\mathcal C_n$ consist of the eligible maps on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For counting up to isomorphism, let $\mathcal C_n$ instead consist of their isomorphism classes, where $(X,r)$ and $(Y,s)$ are isomorphic if a bijection $f:X\to Y$ satisfies \[ (f\times f)\circ r=s\circ(f\times f). \] Let $\mathcal M_n\subseteq\mathcal C_n$ be the multipermutation solutions under the same convention, and put $a_n=|\mathcal C_n|$, $b_n=|\mathcal M_n|$.

Question, conditional on these conventions. Provided $a_n>0$ for all sufficiently large positive integers $n$, is \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{a_n}=1? \] Equivalently, does every $\varepsilon>0$ admit an integer $N\geq1$ such that, for every integer $n\geq N$, $a_n>0$ and $b_n\geq(1-\varepsilon)a_n$?

The source does not specify the eligible class, including whether bijectivity, nondegeneracy, involutivity, square-freeness, or other restrictions are required, nor whether counting is labelled or up to isomorphism. Consequently this is a conditional formulation of the supplied asymptotic question, pending clarification of those choices.

Short English statement

The question asks whether multipermutation solutions constitute an asymptotic proportion of $1$ among Yang--Baxter solutions of size $n$. The eligible solutions and counting convention are unspecified, so the precise counting problem requires clarification. The supplied record labels it open; that status is not independently verified here.
