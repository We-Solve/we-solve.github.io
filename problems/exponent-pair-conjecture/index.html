Exponent pair conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The exponent pair conjecture asks whether \[ \forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad (\varepsilon,1/2+\varepsilon) \text{ is an exponent pair}. \] An exponent pair encodes a uniform bound for one-dimensional oscillatory sums. For clarity, one classical phase convention uses $e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$ and asserts a bound of the form \[ \left|\sum_{a<m\leq b}e(f(m))\right| \leq C\left((ya^{-s-1})^k a^l+(ya^{-s-1})^{-1}\right). \] Here $s>0$, $a\geq1$, $a\leq b\leq2a$, $y>0$, and $f$ belongs to a prescribed smooth class close in sufficiently many derivatives to $x\mapsto yx^{-s}$. For example, a derivative convention requires \[ \left|f^{(j)}(x)-y\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}^j}{{\,\mathrm{d}} x^j}x^{-s}\right| \leq\delta yx^{-s-j}\qquad(1\leq j\leq J,\ a\leq x\leq b), \] with $J,\delta,C$ depending on fixed phase and exponent parameters but not on $a,b,y,f$. The exact phase class and admissible-pair convention are not supplied in the excerpt, so this display gives conventional context rather than certifying the source's definition.

The proposed pairs approach $(0,1/2)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; no uniform constant in $\varepsilon$ and no endpoint bound are asserted. Definitions restricting exponent pairs to $0\leq k\leq1/2$, $1/2\leq l\leq1$ require reading the source's all-positive-$\varepsilon$ wording through arbitrarily small positive $\varepsilon$ or an extended convention; this needs confirmation.

The summary calls $(0,1/2)$ trivial while saying the conjectured near-endpoint pairs are unknown. Those claims are incompatible in the usual exponent-pair framework and the trivial-endpoint claim is not adopted. Its best-known and Lindelöf-equivalence claims also require precise hypotheses and independent checking.

Short English statement

Can exponential-sum exponent pairs approach $(0,1/2)$ in the form $(\varepsilon,1/2+\varepsilon)$ for every arbitrarily small positive $\varepsilon$? The source's exponent-pair convention and the summary's inconsistent endpoint claim need review.
