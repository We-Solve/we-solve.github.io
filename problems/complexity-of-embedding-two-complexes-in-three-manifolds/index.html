Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Determine the decidability and computational complexity of deciding whether a given 2-complex embeds in a 3-manifold, including the case of non-orientable surfaces of even Euler genus and the related thickenability problem.

The original distinctions

De Mesmay's report, printed pages 2666--2668, separates embeddings into a specified 3-manifold, embeddings into $\mathbb R^3$, existence of some 3-manifold containing the complex (thickenability), and non-orientable surface subcases. The odd-genus result in the report does not cover even genus. We keep the piecewise-linear convention in the preserved definition and test an attempted route based on contractibility, local links, and finite simplicial embedding certificates. No missing ambient-manifold quantifier is filled in by silently choosing the easiest of these problems.

A contractible complex that fails every ambient choice

Let $C$ be the simplicial cone over the graph $K_5$, with apex $v$. It is contractible: in cone coordinates $(x,t)$, where all $(x,0)$ are the apex, the homotopy $(x,t)\mapsto(x,(1-s)t)$ contracts it as $s$ goes from zero to one. This is well defined at the collapsed apex and continuous on each cone simplex, hence on the finite complex. Nevertheless $C$ has no PL embedding into any 3-manifold.

We justify the local obstruction. In a PL coordinate neighborhood of the image of $v$, subdivide so the embedding is linear on the finitely many incident simplices. A sufficiently small sphere centered at that image meets each incident edge in a point and each incident triangular sector in an arc. The arcs are disjoint except at their prescribed endpoints because the map is injective. Equivalently, radial intersection embeds a subdivision of the link of $v$ in the surrounding two-sphere. Shrinking the sphere keeps all nonincident simplices away: there are finitely many compact nonincident pieces disjoint from the apex. If the image lies on a manifold boundary, the local sphere is a hemisphere, which still embeds in a sphere. Thus the vertex link must be a planar graph. At the cone apex it is exactly $K_5$; subdivision cannot cure a nonplanar graph, since suppressing a degree-two vertex simply concatenates its two embedded arcs.

For completeness $K_5$ is nonplanar by the elementary planar edge bound. A connected simple planar graph with at least three vertices and no bridges has Euler relation $v-e+f=2$ and every face boundary has length at least three. Counting edge sides gives $3f\le2e$, hence $e\le3v-6$. Euler's relation follows by deleting cycle edges one at a time: deleting a nonbridge merges its two incident faces, decreasing both $e$ and $f$ by one; what remains is a spanning tree, with $e=v-1$ and one face. For $K_5$, $v=5,e=10>9$. This proves the contradiction. The cone example is explicitly mentioned by the primary report; the proof here rederives it rather than claiming a new obstruction.

This also defeats the proposed inference that contraction to a point should permit an embedding into a 3-ball. A contraction is a continuous family of maps; its intermediate maps need not be injective. It provides no control of the nonplanar local link. Checking global homotopy triviality therefore cannot replace local embedding constraints.

What an explicit simplicial certificate does certify

For fixed finite complexes $K'$ and $M'$, a map on their vertices that is injective and sends each simplex of $K'$ to a simplex of $M'$ gives an embedding of their realizations. To prove this, the affine map is injective on each simplex because its image vertices form an affinely independent simplex. Images of two source simplices intersect exactly in the face on their common image vertices, by the defining intersection property of an ambient simplicial complex. Vertex injectivity makes these precisely the images of the common source vertices. Thus the map is globally injective. It is continuous, and its inverse onto the image is continuous because a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is closed: a closed subset is compact and its image is compact, hence closed. This proves a topological, and indeed simplicial, embedding.

Such a certificate is checkable in polynomial time in the explicit sizes of $K',M'$: compare vertex images for injectivity and check each simplex image against the target simplex list. But the original inputs are $K,M$, not arbitrary already-supplied refinements. An attempted exhaustive search would need certificates that $K',M'$ are the appropriate subdivisions and would need a bound on their sizes to prove NP membership or a running-time classification. Finite enumerations through a bounded set of subdivisions can prove yes on a found certificate and can fail to find any certificate without proving no. Our argument supplies no uniform bound on the needed subdivisions, no terminating negative-case test, and no resolution of the even-genus surface case. In particular the vertex-map certificate alone does not establish the complexity claimed in the broad catalogue question.
