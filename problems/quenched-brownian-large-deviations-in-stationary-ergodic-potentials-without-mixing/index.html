Math-1643

Quenched Brownian large deviations in stationary ergodic potentials without mixing

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ carry a measurable measure-preserving action $(\tau_x)_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n}$ ergodic under the full translation group. The source considers \[ u_t+H(D_xu,\tau_x\omega)=0, \] with $H$ convex in momentum and suitable unspecified regularity, for example $H(p,\omega)=\tfrac12|p|^2-V(\omega)$. It asks for a large-deviation result in a stationary ergodic environment without independence or mixing assumptions. Ergodicity is not a substitute for all integrability, coercivity, spectral or nondegeneracy assumptions. The source does not specify the diffusion, killing convention, normalization, topology or rate function. Its claimed Brownian/homogenization connection also requires an explicit scaling convention: a Feynman--Kac logarithmic transform naturally gives a viscous Hamilton--Jacobi equation, so equivalence with the first-order display is not asserted without those missing details.

One precise quenched endpoint template fixes an environment $\omega$ and asks for speed-$t$ large-deviation bounds for \[ \mu_t^\omega(A)=\mathbb E_0\left[ e^{-\int_0^tV(\tau_{B_s}\omega)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s} \mathbf1_{\{B_t/t\in A\}}\right]. \] For nonnegative killing this is a subprobability measure; a result would specify a deterministic rate $I$ and almost surely give the usual lower bound $-\inf_G I$ for open $G$ and upper bound $-\inf_F I$ for closed $F$. Normalizing by survival changes the rate by the corresponding normalization exponent. This is an explicit target convention, not a recovered unique formulation of the source request.

The supplied assessment reports broad no-mixing results of Armstrong--Tran (2014) for diffusions with regular absorbing random potentials, and Boivin--Le (2020) removing spatial regularity under explicit moment, spectral and low-dimensional nondegeneracy hypotheses. Their complete assumptions are not supplied; no theorem for arbitrary stationary ergodic potentials is inferred.

The aid's separate special case is one-dimensional standard Brownian motion, generator $\tfrac12\partial_x^2$, and a measurable potential $0\le V\le M<\infty$ stationary under an ergodic real-translation action. For $\lambda>0$ let $H_y$ be the first hitting time of $y$ and define \[ a_\lambda^\omega(x,y)=-\log\mathbb E_x\left[ e^{-\int_0^{H_y}(\lambda+V(\tau_{B_s}\omega))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s};\ H_y<\infty\right]. \] For ordered triples these costs are exactly additive. The reported deterministic directional limits and cell formulas are \[ \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{a_\lambda^\omega(0,\pm R)}R =\alpha_\pm(\lambda),\qquad \alpha_\pm(\lambda)=\mathbb E[a_\lambda^\omega(0,\pm1)]. \] Birkhoff's theorem for the unit shift initially yields a conditional expectation; bounded endpoint-shift comparisons make the limit invariant under every real translation, and full-action ergodicity makes it deterministic. Ergodicity of $\tau_1$ itself is not assumed. The cost bounds are \[ \sqrt{2\lambda}\,|x-y|\le a_\lambda(x,y) \le\sqrt{2(\lambda+M)}\,|x-y|. \] If $G_\lambda^\omega$ is the density of the killed resolvent, its hitting representation is \[ G_\lambda(x,y)=e^{-a_\lambda(x,y)}G_\lambda(y,y), \qquad\frac1{\sqrt{2(\lambda+M)}}\le G_\lambda(y,y) \le\frac1{\sqrt{2\lambda}}, \] so its directional logarithmic decay rates are the same $\alpha_\pm(\lambda)$. Finally, for $v\ne0$ the ballistic hitting upper bound is \[ \limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log \mathbb E_0\left[e^{-\int_0^{H_{vt}}V(\tau_{B_s}\omega)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s};\ H_{vt}\le t\right] \le\lambda-|v|\alpha_{\operatorname{sgn}v}(\lambda). \] These hitting/resolvent statements are a special-case diagnostic, not by themselves the full endpoint large-deviation lower and upper bounds requested in general.

Short English statement

Seek quenched large deviations in stationary ergodic random environments without mixing. The supplied assessment reports results under additional hypotheses and gives one-dimensional bounded-potential hitting and resolvent exponents; these do not alone constitute a full endpoint large-deviation theorem.
