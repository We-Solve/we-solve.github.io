Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks about holomorphic self-maps of the complex projective plane and leaves the degree range, the meaning of "attractive basins," and the measure convention unspecified. The following is a precise formulation under explicit standard conventions; these conventions require review against the intended source meaning.

For a fixed positive integer $d$, interpret degree as algebraic degree and put \[ H_d=\left\{[F_0:F_1:F_2]: F_j\in{\mathbb{C}}[z_0,z_1,z_2]\text{ are homogeneous of degree }d,\quad \bigcap_{j=0}^2\{F_j=0\}\cap{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})=\varnothing\right\}. \] Here triples are identified under multiplication by a common nonzero scalar, and the corresponding map is \[ [z_0:z_1:z_2]\longmapsto[F_0(z):F_1(z):F_2(z)]. \] Thus $H_d$ is an open subset of the projective space of coefficient triples. Interpret measure zero using the smooth measure class on this parameter space: a subset is null if its intersection with every projective coefficient chart has Lebesgue measure zero in the underlying real coordinates.

A periodic orbit $\mathcal O=\{p,f(p),\ldots,f^{m-1}(p)\}$, where $p$ has least period $m\geq1$, is attracting if every eigenvalue of \[ D(f^m)_p:T_p{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow T_p{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}}) \] has modulus strictly less than $1$. Fix any smooth Riemannian distance on ${\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})$ and define its full attracting basin by \[ B_f(\mathcal O)=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}}):\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{dist}(f^n(z),\mathcal O)=0\right\}. \] Under the convention that basins are counted by their distinct attracting periodic orbits, set \[ E_d=\{f\in H_d: f\text{ has infinitely many distinct attracting periodic orbits}\}. \] The question is whether $E_d$ has measure zero in $H_d$, for each fixed degree $d$ in the intended range.

The source does not specify whether the intended range is $d\geq1$, $d\geq2$, or another range. It also does not specify whether "basins" means the full sets $B_f(\mathcal O)$, their connected components, immediate basins, or a broader notion of attraction. Consequently, the displayed formulation is conditional on the stated conventions and does not resolve these source ambiguities.

Short English statement

For each fixed intended degree, is the set of holomorphic self-maps of ${\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})$ with infinitely many attracting basins null in coefficient parameter space? The formulation above counts basins by distinct attracting periodic orbits and uses the smooth measure class; the source does not explicitly fix these conventions or the degree range.
