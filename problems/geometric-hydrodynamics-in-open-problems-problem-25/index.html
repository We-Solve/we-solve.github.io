Geometric Hydrodynamics in Open Problems — Problem 25

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks for wandering solutions of the three-dimensional Euler equation, but does not specify the fluid domain, boundary conditions, solution class, or topology in which wandering is measured. The following gives a conditional precise interpretation of the requested assertion; these missing choices remain part of the formulation requiring review.

Fix the intended three-dimensional fluid domain $M$, its metric, and its boundary conditions. Under the incompressible interpretation of Eulerian hydrodynamics, the velocity $u$ and pressure $p$ satisfy \[ \partial_t u+\nabla_u u=-\operatorname{grad}p, \qquad \operatorname{div}u=0, \] where $\nabla$ is the covariant derivative for the chosen metric. In a Euclidean domain, $\nabla_u u=(u\cdot\nabla)u$. The incompressible interpretation is conventional here, but is not explicitly stated in the supplied excerpt.

Let $X$ be the intended topological phase space of admissible velocity fields. Suppose that a specified solution class provides a unique global forward evolution $S_t:X\to X$, $t\geq0$, with $S_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $S_{s+t}=S_s\circ S_t$. Call $u_0\in X$ a forward wandering point if there are an open neighborhood $U\subseteq X$ of $u_0$ and a time $T>0$ such that \[ S_t(U)\cap U=\varnothing \qquad\text{for every }t\geq T. \] Thus every solution starting in $U$ avoids $U$ at all sufficiently late times.

Requested assertion, subject to the unresolved choices above. Prove that the intended three-dimensional Euler evolution has a forward wandering point: \[ \exists u_0\in X\;\exists U\subseteq X\text{ open}\;\exists T>0: \quad u_0\in U\quad\text{and}\quad \forall t\geq T,\ S_t(U)\cap U=\varnothing. \] The assumed global evolution is a prerequisite for this formulation, not an assertion of global regularity for arbitrary three-dimensional Euler data. The source does not determine whether this neighborhood-return definition, or a weaker condition concerning only the trajectory of $u_0$, is intended.

As motivation, the supplied source attributes to Nadirashvili a two-dimensional annulus example with a neighborhood that does not return sufficiently close after a certain time. It describes a three-dimensional analogue as unknown. These are supplied source claims, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

Show that a three-dimensional Euler flow has an initial velocity with a neighborhood that never returns to itself after some time. A precise statement still requires the intended domain, boundary conditions, solution class, topology, and meaning of neighborhood return. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
