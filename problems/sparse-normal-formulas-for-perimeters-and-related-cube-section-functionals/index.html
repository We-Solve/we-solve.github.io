Math-1389

Sparse-normal formulas for perimeters and related cube-section functionals

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

The principal source question, Max/min Perimeter of central cross-sections (Hermann König), concerns $Q_n=[-1,1]^n$, $n\ge2$, its central sections $Q_n\cap u^\perp$, $u\in S^{n-1}$, and their intrinsic perimeter \[ P_d(K)=\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial K),\qquad d=n-1, \] where boundary and Hausdorff measure are taken inside the section space. It asks for maximum and minimum perimeter, then analogous extrema for mean width and intrinsic volumes, other codimensions, other measures such as Gaussian measure, and perimeters of projections. Those extensions do not specify a unique measure, normalization or codimension; each must be specified before it becomes one extremal problem.

The aid reports the known global maximum \[ \max_{u\in S^{n-1}}P_{n-1}(Q_n\cap u^\perp)=2^{n-1}\left(1+(n-2)\sqrt2\right), \] attained at $u=(1,1,0,\ldots,0)/\sqrt2$, attributed to König--Koldobsky. The coordinate-section global minimum is described as an open natural conjecture in the literature checked by the aid; it is not established by the following sparse-normal calculation.

For $u=(a,b,0,\ldots,0)$, $a\ge b\ge0$, $a^2+b^2=1$, the section is isometric to a box with side lengths $2/a,2,\ldots,2$, and its orthogonal projection onto $u^\perp$ is isometric to a box with side lengths $2(a+b),2,\ldots,2$. Normalize intrinsic volumes by \[ \operatorname{vol}_d(K+tB_2^d)=\sum_{j=0}^d\kappa_{d-j}V_j(K)t^{d-j},\qquad \kappa_i=\operatorname{vol}_i(B_2^i),\quad\kappa_0=1. \] The aid's complete two-coordinate ledger is \[ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}V_j(Q_n\cap u^\perp)t^j=(1+2t/a)(1+2t)^{n-2},\qquad \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}V_j(Q_n|u^\perp)t^j=(1+2(a+b)t)(1+2t)^{n-2}. \] Thus $P_d=2V_{d-1}$ gives section perimeter $2^{n-1}(1+(n-2)/a)$ and projection perimeter $2^{n-1}(1+(n-2)(a+b))$. Mean width in the convention of the spherical average of directional widths is $2\kappa_{d-1}V_1/(d\kappa_d)$. These formulas determine the corresponding monotonicity within this family, not all global extrema. For induced standard Gaussian measure on the section space, one particular interpretation of the Gaussian extension is \[ \gamma_d(Q_n\cap u^\perp)=\Pr\{|Z|\le1/a\}\Pr\{|Z|\le1\}^{n-2},\quad Z\sim N(0,1). \] Ambient $n$-dimensional Gaussian measure of a hyperplane section would instead be zero. For the equal three-coordinate normal and $n\ge3$, the aid gives the benchmark \[ P_{n-1}\left(Q_n\cap(1,1,1,0,\ldots,0)^\perp\right)=2^{n-3}\left(6\sqrt2+3\sqrt3(n-3)\right). \] Combining the Vaaler section-volume bound $\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_n\cap u^\perp)\ge2^d$ with the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality gives the supplied nonsharp universal lower bound $P_d\ge d\kappa_d^{1/d}2^{d-1}$.

Problem 2, Mathieu Meyer: for origin-symmetric convex bodies $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, find the asymptotic order in $n$ of the supremum over $K$ of \[ \inf_{u,v\in S^{n-1},\,u\cdot v\ne0}\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(P_{v,u^\perp}K)}{\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K\cap u^\perp)},\qquad P_{v,u^\perp}x=x-\frac{x\cdot u}{v\cdot u}v. \] This repairs the extracted numerator, which displays a set rather than its volume, and excludes undefined parallel projection directions. The source writes maximum/minimum; attainment is not established here. It notes affine invariance of the inner value, value 1 for ellipsoids, hence lower bound 1, and a rough bound $2^n$. Its projection-norm argument, interpreted in the norm with unit ball $K$, gives $P_HK\subseteq2(K\cap H)$ and the sharper stated volume factor $2^{n-1}$.

Problem 3, Maria Alfonseca: for an origin-symmetric star body $K$, define its radial function $\rho_K(u)=\max\{a\ge0:au\in K\}$ and intersection body by $\rho_{IK}(u)=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K\cap u^\perp)$. Ask what conditions on the "convex kernel" imply convexity of $IK$. The supplied definition is the intersection of all convex bodies contained in $K$, with an additional case mismatch $M/m$. Taken literally, intersections of arbitrary contained convex bodies may be empty; an intended alternative kernel definition is not supplied.

Problem 4, Carsten Schütt: determine cotype and volume ratio of $\ell_p^n\otimes_\pi\ell_q^n\otimes_\pi\ell_r^n$. The projective norm of a tensor $z$ is the infimum of $\sum_i\|x_i\|_p\|y_i\|_q\|z_i\|_r$ over decompositions $z=\sum_i x_i\otimes y_i\otimes z_i$. Standard Banach-space notation suggests $1\le p,q,r\le\infty$, but ranges are not printed. A cotype-$s$ inequality compares $(\sum_i\|w_i\|^s)^{1/s}$ to $({\mathbb{E}}\|\sum_i\epsilon_iw_i\|^2)^{1/2}$ up to a constant, with independent random signs; the question does not specify whether optimal exponents or dimension-dependent constants are sought. Volume ratio ordinarily means $(\operatorname{vol}(B_X)/\max_{E\subseteq B_X}\operatorname{vol}(E))^{1/\dim X}$, with the maximum over ellipsoids, and requires a normalization convention.

Problem 5, Mark Rudelson: for $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and symmetric convex bodies $K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, suppose all common sections of dimension $\alpha n$ have Banach--Mazur distance at most $b>0$. Is $d_{\rm BM}(K,L)\le f(b,\alpha)$ for some dimension-independent function? Here the usual distance is the infimum of $\lambda\ge1$ such that $K\subseteq TL\subseteq\lambda K$ for an invertible linear map $T$. The extraction quantifies $E$ over a class of bodies rather than subspaces; the section-space interpretation and rounding when $\alpha n$ is not integral are unresolved. The source reports that Rudelson showed the analogous assertion for geometric distance $d_g$ during a problem session, but does not define $d_g$.

Short English statement

The main question asks for extremal perimeters and related functionals of cube sections and projections. The aid gives the reported global perimeter maximum, explicit two-coordinate formulas, a three-coordinate benchmark and a nonsharp lower bound; it does not settle all minima or extensions. Several later convex-geometry problems accidentally merged into this entry are retained with their missing definitions and damaged quantifiers identified.
