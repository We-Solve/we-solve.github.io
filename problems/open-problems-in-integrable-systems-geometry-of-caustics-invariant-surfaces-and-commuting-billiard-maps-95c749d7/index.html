Open Problems in Integrable Systems — Geometry of caustics, invariant surfaces, and commuting billiard maps

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K_1\subset\operatorname{int}K_2\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$, $d\ge2$, be convex bodies with boundaries $\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2$. To give the usual inner-billiard meaning, suppose these boundaries have enough smoothness and strict convexity to define the reflection maps away from tangencies. An oriented line has a unit direction $v$. At its forward intersection $x$ with $\Sigma_i$, specular reflection replaces $v$ by \[ v'=v-2\langle v,\nu_i(x)\rangle\nu_i(x), \] where $\nu_i(x)$ is the unit normal, producing the outgoing oriented line $B_i(\ell)$. Let $\mathcal L$ denote the intended set of oriented lines meeting both hypersurfaces, with tangencies treated according to a specified convention.

The supplied commuting-inner-billiards problem asks whether \[ B_1B_2=B_2B_1\text{ on the intended common domain} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Sigma_1,\Sigma_2\text{ are confocal ellipsoids}. \] The conclusion means that, after one Euclidean change of coordinates, there are real parameters $a_1,\ldots,a_d,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$, with $a_j-\lambda_i>0$, such that \[ \Sigma_i=\left\{x:\sum_{j=1}^d\frac{x_j^2}{a_j-\lambda_i}=1\right\},\qquad i=1,2. \] The premise must specify whether $B_i$ preserve $\mathcal L$ and commute as self-maps there, or whether equality is required only where both compositions are defined. The excerpt does not state this domain convention, differentiability assumptions, strict convexity, or treatment of tangencies. These are necessary qualifications of the reflection formulation, not silently added source hypotheses.

The supplied research summary reports the inner problem solved in the plane and higher dimensions with confocal-ellipsoid conclusion. It separately reports a planar outer-billiard conclusion of concentric homothetic ellipses and says the higher-dimensional outer/dual version appears open. Those outer statements are contextual claims, not replacements for the source's inner-billiard target. No supporting result citations are supplied.

Short English statement

Must two nested convex hypersurfaces with commuting inner-billiard reflections be confocal ellipsoids? The supplied summary reports yes, but the map domains, boundary regularity, and exact supporting theorem need review.
