Problem 5 — (Fuchsian groups).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A Fuchsian group is a discrete subgroup of $\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$. In the usual flat-surface setting, a nonzero quadratic differential $q$ on a Riemann surface determines a Teichmüller disc $D_q\subset\operatorname{Teich}(S)$. Its setwise stabilizer is \[ \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{Mod}(S)}(D_q) =\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Mod}(S):\varphi(D_q)=D_q\}. \] The induced action on the disc gives a projective derivative group in $\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$, customarily called the Veech group. In a translation-surface convention the derivative group is initially in $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$. The disc stabilizer can have a finite kernel in its action on the disc, so it need not be abstractly identical to the projectivized derivative group.

Problem 5 asks two realization questions: which Fuchsian groups occur as Veech groups, and which subgroups of the mapping class group occur as Veech groups, equivalently as stabilizers of Teichmüller discs in the source convention? The intended object is the full Veech group or full stabilizer, not merely a subgroup contained in one.

The excerpt does not specify genus, surface type, differential stratum, translation versus half-translation surfaces, or compactness/finite-area conventions. It also does not say whether realization is up to abstract isomorphism or conjugacy in $\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$, and analogously in the mapping class group. No finite-covolume or lattice restriction is stated. The source's equivalence between the two questions must be understood with its derivative/projectivization and finite-kernel conventions fixed.

Short English statement

Classify the Fuchsian groups arising as Veech groups and the mapping-class subgroups that are full Teichmüller-disc stabilizers, keeping track of derivative and finite-kernel conventions.
