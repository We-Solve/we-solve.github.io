Counting Palindromic and Digit-Reversible Squares

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The numeral base and conventions for reversing terminal zeros are not specified in the supplied record. The following is a conditional decimal formulation, using ordinary positional digit reversal and discarding any leading zeros in the reversed string. These conventions require confirmation against the source.

For a positive integer $n$, write its canonical decimal expansion as \[ n=\sum_{j=0}^{k}d_j10^j,\qquad d_j\in\{0,1,\ldots,9\},\quad d_k\ne0, \] and define \[ \operatorname{rev}_{10}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}d_j10^{k-j}. \] Call $n$ palindromic if $d_j=d_{k-j}$ for every $0\le j\le k$. Thus palindromicity is equivalent to $\operatorname{rev}_{10}(n)=n$. A positive square means an integer $m^2$ with $m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}$.

For real $X\ge1$, define \[ P(X)=\#\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}:n\le X,\ n=m^2\text{ for some }m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1},\ \operatorname{rev}_{10}(n)=n\} \] and \[ \begin{split} R(X)=\#\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}:{}&n\le X,\ n=m^2\text{ for some }m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1},\\ &\operatorname{rev}_{10}(n)=\ell^2\text{ for some }\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1},\ \operatorname{rev}_{10}(n)\ne n\}. \end{split} \] These functions count square values, not ordered pairs of square roots. The bound $X$ applies only to $n$; no separate bound on its reversal is imposed. Squares ending in zero are included under the stated conditional convention.

The supplied cleaned statement asks for an asymptotic formula for $P(X)$ as $X\to\infty$, and asks whether infinitely many palindromic squares lie outside the four infinite families attributed to Keith in the editorial note. If $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_4$ denote those particular families of square values, the latter question is whether \[ \#\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}:n\text{ is a palindromic square},\ n\notin\textstyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal F_i\right\}=\infty. \] The record supplies no definitions of $\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_4$, so this exclusion condition cannot yet be made explicit.

The record also asks to determine the number of nonpalindromic squares whose reversal is a square, and whether there are infinitely many. With the bounded counting interpretation above, these tasks concern $R(X)$ and whether \[ \lim_{X\to\infty}R(X)=\infty. \] The source does not specify the desired precision for counting these nonpalindromic squares. Its supplied dated literature assessment labels the overall problem partially solved and reports that infinitude in this second question is already known through an explicit family. That family is not supplied here, and this reported assessment is not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

Short English statement

Conditionally using decimal digits and ordinary reversal, count positive palindromic square values up to $X$ and seek their asymptotic number. Decide whether infinitely many lie outside Keith's four cited families, whose formulas are missing from the record. Also count nonpalindromic square values whose reversals are squares. The supplied dated assessment reports that infinitely many of the latter are known; it leaves the broader palindromic counting and classification questions open. The digit conventions, missing families, and reported status require review.
