Math-1207

The one-point count for a plane cubic and the degree-nine log-BPS answer

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied verification says the preceding workshop item defines $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2$ as an elliptic curve, hence a smooth plane cubic. For an integer $d\geq1$, the original asks for the number of degree-$d$ rational plane curves meeting $X$ at exactly one point. For an irreducible curve $C\ne X$, the literal condition is $\operatorname{Supp}(C\cap X)=\{p\}$. This does not by itself require a single branch over $p$ on the normalization. A one-place maximal-contact condition instead requires the normalization map $\nu:{\mathbb{P}}^1\to C$ to satisfy $\nu^*X=3d\,q$ for one point $q$, with $\nu(q)=p$. The report warns that these counting conventions, logarithmic stable-map lengths, and counts of distinct reduced image curves must be separated.

In the characteristic-zero elliptic-curve interpretation, choose a flex origin $o\in X$, so $\mathcal O_X(1)\cong\mathcal O_X(3o)$. A maximal contact point must satisfy $3d(p-o)=0$. Define its minimal index to be the smallest $k\geq1$ with $3k(p-o)=0$. For such points in degree $d$, $k\mid d$. The report's candidate torsion audit gives \[ A_k=9J_2(k),\qquad J_2(k)=k^2\prod_{\ell\mid k,\ \ell\ \mathrm{prime}}(1-\ell^{-2}). \] These count available contact points of each minimal index, not automatically distinct curves.

The report attributes to Bousseau's all-degree theorem the fixed primitive degree-nine log-BPS invariant, equivalently the length of the finite primitive log-map moduli scheme, \[ \Omega_9=1{,}027{,}737. \] Primitive here means minimal contact index $9$. This value does not by itself count distinct reduced image curves, since scheme length need not equal the number of reduced points. Using the report's normalization for the fixed-primitive invariants $\Omega_e$ and total unspecified-contact relative invariant $N_d$, its aggregate audit identity is \[ (-1)^{d-1}N_d=\frac9{d^2}\sum_{e\mid d}e^4\Omega_e. \] The exact virtual-count and sign normalizations defining these symbols are not otherwise supplied.

In degree nine the possible minimal indices and their contact-point counts are \[ \begin{array}{c|ccc} k&1&3&9\\\hline A_k&9&72&648 \end{array} \] and the asserted all-primitive aggregate length and full unspecified-contact relative invariant are respectively \[ 648\cdot1{,}027{,}737=665{,}973{,}576,\qquad N_9=749{,}220{,}300+\frac19. \] The fractional total illustrates why it cannot simply be the literal number of distinct image curves. The report's three-stratum distinction does not remove the original branch, reducedness, and counting ambiguities.

The workshop text says the answer was known for $d\leq8$ "by Takashi," that $d=9$ would be new and interesting, and that the motivation is local mirror symmetry; it suggests degenerating $X$ to three lines and asks about a tropical approach. These are retained as historical workshop remarks, not a current literature assessment.

Short English statement

Count rational plane curves meeting a smooth cubic at one point, while distinguishing literal image curves from one-place logarithmic counts. The report gives a degree-nine primitive invariant and contact-stratum audit, but does not identify these lengths with counts of distinct reduced curves.
