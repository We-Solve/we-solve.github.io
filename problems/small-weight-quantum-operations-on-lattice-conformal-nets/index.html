Small-Weight Quantum Operations on Lattice Conformal Nets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For the source vacuum state $\omega$, a quantum operation is a compatible family $\phi_I$ of extreme normal unital completely positive, $\omega$-preserving maps on $\mathcal A(I)$, preserving local conformal symmetry and admitting a Markov adjoint in the same class. Composition is map composition. A nontrivial two-element hypergroup has an element $\phi\ne\operatorname{id}$ satisfying the displayed quadratic law, and $w$ is its hypergroup weight, not an arbitrary scalar attached to the identity map.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For a lattice conformal net or a strongly local vertex-operator-algebra net $\mathcal A$, find $\phi\in\operatorname{QuOp}(\mathcal A)$ satisfying $$\phi\circ\phi=w^{-1}\operatorname{id}+(1-w^{-1})\phi$$ for arbitrarily small real $w>1$.
Source scope and qualification.
Primary Problem 9 (PDF page 30, printed page 3082) asks for small positive real weights $w>1$. The catalogue adds arbitrarily small, which can be read as weights approaching $1$; that stronger quantifier was not verified in the primary source. The original small-weight construction question is preserved as source context, and the inherited stronger wording is explicitly not endorsed as a recovered theorem target. The nontriviality and actual weight convention exclude the vacuous identity operation.

Short English statement

Find conformal-net quantum operations with the stated quadratic composition law and weights arbitrarily close to one.

An apparent small-weight solution that fails extremality.
On the commutative two-point algebra let $X$ interchange the two points and put $\phi=aI+(1-a)X$, where \[ a=\frac{1-w^{-1}}2,\qquad w>1. \] This is a unital positive (hence here completely positive) map preserving the uniform state, and is self-adjoint for that state's inner product. Its eigenvalues on constants and mean-zero functions are $1$ and $2a-1=-1/w$. Both roots satisfy $t^2=w^{-1}+(1-w^{-1})t$, so \[ \phi^2=w^{-1}I+(1-w^{-1})\phi. \] There is no difficulty making $w\downarrow1$ in this family.
The decisive audit.
For every $w>1$, one has $0<a<1/2$. Thus $\phi$ is a nontrivial convex combination of the distinct state-preserving maps $I$ and $X$, and is not extreme. At the endpoint $w=1$ it becomes the involution $X$, but that endpoint is excluded. It therefore does not belong to the source class of extreme conformal quantum operations, and a conformal-net realization was never supplied.
