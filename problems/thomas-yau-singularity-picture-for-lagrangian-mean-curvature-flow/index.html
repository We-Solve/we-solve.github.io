Thomas Yau Singularity Picture for Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flow

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ denote the ambient manifold intended by the source, and let $\omega$ and $g$ denote its symplectic form and Riemannian metric. Since the evolving Lagrangians are surfaces, $X$ has real dimension four. The ambient geometry and the hypotheses ensuring preservation of the Lagrangian condition are not specified in the supplied excerpt. A smooth Lagrangian mean curvature flow is a family of immersions $F_t\colon L\to X$ satisfying \[ F_t^*\omega=0,\qquad (\partial_tF_t)^\perp=H_t, \] where $H_t$ is the mean curvature vector and $\perp$ denotes normal projection with respect to $g$.

At a singular space-time point $(x,T)$, a tangent flow means a local weak limit of parabolic rescalings about $(x,T)$. In local coordinates centered at $x$, these rescalings have the form \[ L_s^{(j)}=\lambda_j\left(L_{T+\lambda_j^{-2}s}-x\right),\qquad \lambda_j\longrightarrow\infty,\quad s<0, \] where $L_t=F_t(L)$. This expression is interpreted locally in the tangent space $T_xX$; subtraction denotes the chosen local coordinates. The weak convergence framework and any continuation through earlier singularities remain unspecified by the record.

The supplied optimistic expectation is the following schematic assertion: for generic admissible initial Lagrangian surfaces, every tangent flow at every singularity is a static union of two distinct multiplicity-one Lagrangian planes. More explicitly, if $(\mu_s)_{s<0}$ is any such tangent flow, there should exist distinct two-dimensional Lagrangian linear subspaces $P_1,P_2\subset T_xX$ such that \[ \mu_s=\mathcal H^2\!\llcorner P_1+\mathcal H^2\!\llcorner P_2 \quad\text{for every }s<0, \qquad \dim(P_1\cap P_2)\in\{0,1\}. \] Here $\mathcal H^2\!\llcorner P_i$ is two-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to $P_i$, so each plane occurs with multiplicity one. The case $\dim(P_1\cap P_2)=0$ means that the planes meet only at the rescaling origin, and they are transverse in the four-dimensional tangent space. The case $\dim(P_1\cap P_2)=1$ means that they meet along a line. This is a formulation of the proposed classification, not an assertion that it holds. In particular, the admissible initial-data class, its topology, and the meaning of "generic" must be supplied before this becomes a precise conjecture.

The surrounding excerpt gives additional, incomplete context. It attributes to Thomas--Yau's Conjecture 7.3, cited there as [14], a prediction that a finite-time singularity permits a decomposition $L=M_1\#M_2$ as a graded connected sum satisfying conditions labeled (1), which are absent from the record. It reports a result supporting one such scenario when the tangent flow at the first singular time consists of two transverse planes, and says that the graded connected-sum decomposition is guaranteed when $L$ is a sphere in that setting. The full hypotheses of that result are not supplied. Joyce's conjectural picture, cited as [3], allows other singularities, notably tangent flows consisting of two static planes meeting along a line; the excerpt attributes some progress on these singularities to the authors' previous work [5].

The excerpt also mentions a holomorphic volume form $\Omega$ on $X$, a Lagrangian angle $\theta$, and "cohomological" Lagrangian angles $\phi(M_i)$. Under an additional almost-calibrated hypothesis it displays, in incomplete surrounding text, \[ \phi(M_1),\ \phi(M_2)\subset\left(\inf_L\theta,\sup_L\theta\right). \] This is retained as a transcription of the supplied qualification, not as a fully specified mathematical assertion: the definitions, grading choices, full hypotheses, and whether the quantities $\phi(M_i)$ are numbers or sets are missing. These omissions prevent recovery of a precise connected-sum or angle theorem from this record.

Short English statement

The proposed generic-singularity picture predicts that every tangent flow of a Lagrangian surface consists of two static planes, each with multiplicity one, meeting either at one point or along a line. The record leaves the ambient hypotheses and the meaning of "generic" unspecified, so this remains a schematic conjecture requiring mathematical review. Its connected-sum and angle statements are incomplete contextual qualifications. The supplied status is "partially solved"; this label and the accompanying literature assessment are dated curation, not independently verified conclusions.
