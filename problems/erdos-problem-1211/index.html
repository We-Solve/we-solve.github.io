Erdős Problem 1211: the extremal logarithmic block construction

Statement and scope

For a partition $\mathbb N=A\sqcup B$, let $S(A)$ and $S(B)$ be the sets of nonempty sums of distinct elements of the corresponding part. How small can \[ \max\left(\overline\delta_{\log}(S(A)), \overline\delta_{\log}(S(B))\right) \] be? Conlon--Fox--Pham proved that the optimum is $(2+\sqrt3)/4$. We reconstruct the sharp upper-bound construction, including its logarithmic-density calculation, and prove the elementary universal lower bound $1/2$. The stronger universal lower bound matching the construction remains unresolved in this attempt.

A universal elementary lower bound

Singletons are allowed as sums, so $A\subseteq S(A)$ and $B\subseteq S(B)$. Since the two parts partition the positive integers, \[ \sum_{\substack{n\in A\\n\le x}}\frac1n+ \sum_{\substack{n\in B\\n\le x}}\frac1n =\log x+O(1). \] Taking upper limits proves that at least one of $S(A),S(B)$ has upper logarithmic density at least $1/2$. This gives no justification for the much larger sharp constant.

Alternating blocks on the logarithm-of-logarithm scale

Fix $b>2$. Assign the integers in \[ [e^{b^{2j}},e^{b^{2j+1}})\quad(j=0,1,\ldots) \] to $A$, and those in the intervening blocks to $B$. Assign the finitely many initial integers arbitrarily. Let $R_j=e^{b^{2j+1}}$ be the upper endpoint of the $j$th $A$-block and $L_{j+1}=e^{b^{2j+2}}$ the lower endpoint of the next one.

Any element of $S(A)$ below $L_{j+1}$ uses only summands below $R_j$. Its value is at most the sum of all positive integers below $R_j$, which is less than $R_j^2$ for large $j$. Hence $S(A)$ avoids the entire integer interval \[ (R_j^2,L_{j+1}) =\left(e^{2b^{2j+1}},e^{b^{2j+2}}\right). \tag{1} \] The same argument gives the shifted family of gaps for $S(B)$. All earlier blocks are automatically accounted for by bounding their total by the sum of all integers below $R_j$.

Computing an upper density uniformly over the cutoff

For a set of integer gaps $(e^u,e^v)$, the harmonic weight removed is $v-u+O(e^{-u})$. The errors over the gap family $1$ have bounded sum. Thus, on the variable $t=\log x$, it suffices to examine the length proportion removed by intervals \[ (u_j,v_j)=(2b^{2j+1},b^{2j+2}). \] Their lengths are $(b-2)b^{2j+1}$, and $u_{j+1}/u_j=b^2$.

The removed-length proportion decreases between gaps and increases inside a gap: during a gap its numerator is $t$ minus a fixed nonnegative covered length. Its asymptotic minimum therefore occurs immediately before a gap, at $t=u_j$. At that point the earlier removed length is \[ \sum_{h<j}(v_h-u_h) =(b-2)b\,\frac{b^{2j}-1}{b^2-1}, \] up to a fixed initial adjustment. Division by $u_j=2b^{2j+1}$ gives the limit \[ \frac{b-2}{2(b^2-1)}. \] The uniform-over-cutoffs conclusion is \[ \overline\delta_{\log}(S(A)),\ \overline\delta_{\log}(S(B)) \le D(b):=1-\frac{b-2}{2(b^2-1)}. \tag{2} \] For $B$, multiplying all gap endpoints by $b$ leaves this limiting proportion unchanged. Integer rounding and finite initial choices contribute only $O(1)/\log x$.

Optimizing the parameter

The derivative of $(b-2)/(b^2-1)$ has numerator $-b^2+4b-1$. On $b>2$ it changes from positive to negative at $b=2+\sqrt3$. Thus $2$ is minimized there, and direct substitution gives \[ D(2+\sqrt3)=\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4}. \] The classical choice $b=4$ gives $D(4)=14/15$. This proves the sharp construction-side bound with no assumption that subset sums fill every interval outside the exhibited gaps. Only the proved exclusions are needed.

What is missing

The construction establishes an upper bound on the optimal guarantee. To identify that guarantee exactly, one must show that every partition has a colour whose subset sums have upper logarithmic density at least $(2+\sqrt3)/4$. The primary paper proves this using a substantial interval-of-subset-sums lemma and a further continuous optimization argument. Neither is replaced here by the elementary $1/2$ argument. Sources: https://www.erdosproblems.com/1211; D. Conlon, J. Fox and H. T. Pham, The upper logarithmic density of monochromatic subset sums, Mathematika 68 $2022$, 1292--1301, https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15195v3, especially Section 1 for the construction and Sections 2--3 for the missing lower bound. No novelty is claimed.
