Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.55

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$, $B$, and $F_{24}'$ denote the Monster, Baby Monster, and sporadic simple group denoted by $F_{24}'$ in the supplied record, respectively.

For a finite group $G$, write $o(g)$ for the order of $g\in G$. A conjugacy class $T$ of involutions makes $G$ a $6$-transposition group if \[ G=\langle T\rangle,\qquad o(st)\leq 6\quad\text{for every }s,t\in T. \] For involutions $s,t$, the subgroup $\langle s,t\rangle$ is dihedral of order $2o(st)$, allowing the degenerate case $s=t$, when this subgroup has order $2$. Define \[ \mathcal D(G,T)=\{\langle s,t\rangle:s,t\in T\}/G, \] where the quotient denotes conjugacy of subgroups under $G$. For $[D]\in\mathcal D(G,T)$, put $n([D])=|D|/2$; this equals $o(st)$ for any generating pair of involutions defining $D$.

To specify a possible meaning of the root coefficients, let $\Phi$ be a finite irreducible root system of type $E_r$, choose simple roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$, and write its highest root as \[ \theta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i\alpha_i. \] The marks of the associated untwisted affine diagram are $a_0=1,a_1,\ldots,a_r$. Equivalently, with affine simple root $\alpha_0=\delta-\theta$, they satisfy \[ \delta=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_i\alpha_i. \] This gives a conventional interpretation of the source's phrase "coefficient of the highest root of affine type"; an affine root system does not itself have a highest root in the finite-root-system sense.

(a) Explanatory problem. The source asserts that $M$ is a $6$-transposition group and that pairs of its "Fischer transpositions" generate nine conjugacy classes of dihedral subgroups. Let $T_M$ provisionally denote the intended class of involutions. Under the affine-mark interpretation above, the proposed numerical correspondence is \[ |\mathcal D(M,T_M)|=9, \qquad \left\{\!\left\{n([D]):[D]\in\mathcal D(M,T_M)\right\}\!\right\} = \left\{\!\left\{a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_8\right\}\!\right\} \quad(\Phi=E_8), \] where double braces denote a multiset. Explain the relationship between these group-theoretic data and the affine $E_8$ diagram.

The source additionally asks for analogous explanations relating $B$ to $E_7$ and $F_{24}'$ to $E_6$, using the central covers denoted by $\widetilde B=2.B$ and $\widetilde F=3.F_{24}'$, with products read modulo their centres. Explicitly, for a specified pair $x,y\in\widetilde G$, the relevant product order under this instruction is \[ o\left((xZ(\widetilde G))(yZ(\widetilde G))\right) =o\left(xyZ(\widetilde G)\right) \quad\text{in }\widetilde G/Z(\widetilde G). \] The source does not specify the corresponding element classes, admissible pairs, or exact correspondence with the $E_7$ and $E_6$ diagrams. Consequently these analogies remain explanatory targets requiring clarification, rather than fully quantified assertions supplied by the record. The class $T_M$, the treatment of equal pairs, and the multiset interpretation above also require confirmation against the original source.

(b) Explanatory problem. For a finite group $G$, let $\operatorname{Mult}(G)=H_2(G,{\mathbb{Z}})$ be its Schur multiplier. For a finite root system $\Phi$, let $Q(\Phi)$ be the lattice generated by its roots and \[ P(\Phi)=\{\lambda\in\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\Phi): \langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ for every }\alpha\in\Phi\} \] its weight lattice, where $\alpha^\vee=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$. A conventional meaning of the fundamental group of a root system is $P(\Phi)/Q(\Phi)$. Under this interpretation, the observation to be explained is the sequence of abstract group isomorphisms \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Mult}(M)&\cong P(E_8)/Q(E_8)\cong\{0\},\\ \operatorname{Mult}(B)&\cong P(E_7)/Q(E_7)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}},\\ \operatorname{Mult}(F_{24}')&\cong P(E_6)/Q(E_6)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}. \end{aligned} \] Explain why these correspondences occur. The source does not identify a particular Lie group or specify the meaning of "fundamental group"; the lattice interpretation is therefore explicit and provisional. Neither part specifies a formal criterion for what would constitute an explanation. These are source-reported observations and explanatory questions, not mathematical conclusions established by this draft.

Short English statement

Explain the reported relationships between the Monster, Baby Monster, and $F_{24}'$ and the root systems $E_8,E_7,E_6$: first through orders of products of distinguished elements and affine root coefficients, and second through Schur multipliers and root-system fundamental groups. The precise element classes, the two smaller-group analogies, and the intended meaning of an explanation require clarification. The registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
