Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Does the Benedetti–Risler finiteness conjecture hold for every axiomatic complexity measure on semialgebraic sets: for each fixed value of such a complexity measure, are there only finitely many homeomorphism types?

Source scope and attempted route

The report states that axiomatic polynomial complexity includes a Bezout-like bound on isolated nondegenerate solutions, but does not list all the axioms. It also reports established special cases for fewnomial and additive complexity; we do not reproduce their proofs or treat the universal axiomatic conjecture as resolved by those reports. We test the route from bounded roots to finitely many types in one variable, then examine a proposed extension using only regular-point counts.
A complete elementary bounded-degree case on the line.
Fix nonnegative integers $s,D$. Let a set $X\subseteq\mathbb R$ be described by any Boolean combination of signs of at most $s$ real polynomials of degree at most $D$. Discard zero polynomials from the root list; their sign conditions are constant truth values. A nonzero degree-$d$ polynomial has at most $d$ real roots: division by $(x-a)$ at a root reduces the degree by one, inductively. Thus the combined root list has $h\le sD$ distinct points $r_1<\cdots<r_h$. Every polynomial has a constant sign on each complementary open interval. Indeed a sign change between two points of an interval would give a root by the intermediate value theorem. Every Boolean formula in those signs is therefore constant there. Hence $X$ is a union of some of the $2h+1$ cells consisting of the roots and intervening open intervals, including two rays.

For a fixed $h$, two choices of root locations with the same selected-cell pattern give homeomorphic sets: take the increasing piecewise linear map of $\mathbb R$ sending the first root list to the second, linear on bounded intervening intervals and with slope one on the outside rays. It is continuous, strictly increasing, onto, and has a continuous piecewise linear inverse. Restricting it gives the required homeomorphism. For $h=0$, the only sets are empty and the entire line. Thus the number of homeomorphism types for this family is at most \[ \sum_{h=0}^{sD}2^{2h+1}, \] a deliberately nonsharp finite bound valid for arbitrary Boolean formulas. This fixes the number of polynomials, their degrees, and the one-dimensional ambient space; it does not instantiate every axiomatic complexity measure.

Why counting only regular points of the defining equation fails.
For $m\ge1$, define \[ Q_m(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^m\left((x-3j)^2+y^2-1\right),\qquad P_m=Q_m^2. \] The zero set is the union of $m$ unit circles with centers $(3j,0)$. Distinct circles are disjoint because their center distance is at least three, greater than the sum of radii two. Each circle is connected, as follows from its continuous parametrization by an interval of angles, and is separated from the other finitely many circles by a positive distance. Thus these are exactly $m$ connected components, so differing $m$ give nonhomeomorphic sets: a homeomorphism and its inverse preserve connected components. Yet $\nabla P_m=2Q_m\nabla Q_m$ vanishes at every zero of $P_m$. A method examining only nonsingular zeros of this displayed equation would see none for any $m$, while the number of components grows arbitrarily. This example does not refute the Benedetti--Risler axioms: the degrees and likely other allowed complexities grow, and the full axioms may control perturbations or auxiliary systems. It pinpoints the failure of replacing those axioms by a regular-point count for just one defining equation.
