Definitions and conventions.
A bivariational approximation varies a ket $|\psi\rangle$ and an independently parametrized dual bra $\langle\widetilde\psi|$. A typical action has integrand $\langle\widetilde\psi|(i\partial_t-H)|\psi\rangle$; stationarity on the trial manifolds yields coupled evolution equations. The initial-value problem asks for a trajectory through prescribed initial bra and ket data. The trial manifolds, nondegeneracy of their induced pairing and the operator domains control whether this system defines an ordinary differential equation.

A finite-dimensional local existence criterion.
Let local real coordinates $z\in\mathbb R^m$ parametrize a trial bra/ket manifold, and suppose variation of the action gives $B(z)\dot z=f(z)$, with $B$ and $f$ continuously differentiable near initial data $z_0$ and $\det B(z_0)\ne0$. The inverse matrix formula makes $V(z)=B(z)^{-1}f(z)$ continuously differentiable on a small closed ball, hence bounded by $M$ and Lipschitz with constant $L$ there. On continuous paths staying in the ball, the map $\mathcal Tz(t)=z_0+\int_0^tV(z(s))ds$ preserves the ball for $TM$ below its radius and is a contraction when $TL<1$. Iteration is Cauchy in the supremum norm and its limit is a differentiable solution; the same estimate proves uniqueness locally.
An explicit degeneracy failure.
The scalar variational equation $z\dot z=1$ has no differentiable trajectory through $z(0)=0$, because substitution at zero would give $0=1$. Integrating away from zero yields $z(t)^2=2t+C$, whose zero-initial branches have unbounded derivative at the endpoint. Smooth coefficients alone thus do not ensure a classical trajectory if the induced pairing degenerates.
Scope.
The source does not assume a finite-dimensional nondegenerate trial manifold. For an actual bivariational ansatz one must verify this matrix comes from its independent bra/ket pairing, fix gauge redundancies and keep the Hamiltonian action in its operator domain. Global existence additionally requires avoiding pairing degeneracy and escape from coordinate charts. Establishing those properties for the intended trial spaces is the first gap; the local ODE criterion is an elementary sufficient case, not a general bivariational well-posedness theorem.
