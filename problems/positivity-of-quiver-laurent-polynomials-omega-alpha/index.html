Positivity of Quiver Laurent Polynomials $\Omega_\alpha$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A quiver is a directed graph $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,h)$, where $Q_0$ is its vertex set, $Q_1$ is its arrow set, and $s,h:Q_1\to Q_0$ give the source and target of each arrow. An element $\alpha\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}$ assigns a natural number to each vertex. The supplied record does not specify finiteness restrictions on $Q$, restrictions on loops or multiple arrows, or whether $\mathbb N$ includes zero; these conventions remain unresolved. No exclusion of the zero dimension vector is stated.

Let $t=q^{1/2}$ be a formal indeterminate. A Laurent polynomial in $t$ with nonnegative integer coefficients is a finite sum \[ \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}c_k t^k,\qquad c_k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}, \] where only finitely many coefficients are nonzero.

The conjectural target reported in the source record is \[ \text{for every quiver }Q\text{ and every }\alpha\in\mathbb N^{Q_0},\qquad \Omega_\alpha(-t)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}[t,t^{-1}]. \] Here $\Omega_\alpha$ is the source's notation, whose dependence on $Q$ is implicit. The supplied record gives no definition or normalization of $\Omega_\alpha$, no ambient algebraic object in which it is initially defined, and no construction explaining the substitution $-t$. Consequently this display preserves the reported conjecture but is not a self-contained mathematical statement. A precise formulation requires the source definition of $\Omega_\alpha$ and its applicable quiver and dimension-vector conventions; none is inferred here.

Short English statement

The reported conjecture asks whether, for every quiver and every permitted dimension vector, substituting $-q^{1/2}$ into the source invariant $\Omega_\alpha$ produces a Laurent polynomial in $q^{1/2}$ with nonnegative integer coefficients. The invariant and its domain conventions are missing, so the formulation remains incomplete and requires human mathematical review.
