CS-91 · Math-7753

Existence of a p-Optimal Propositional Proof System

Status OpenLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems openaigpt 6 astra ultra Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathrm{TAUT}$ be the set of encoded propositional tautologies. A Cook--Reckhow proof system is a polynomial-time computable surjection $P:\{0,1\}^*\to\mathrm{TAUT}$; a string $\pi$ proves the formula $P(\pi)$. A system $P$ polynomially simulates $Q$ if there is a polynomial-time function $t_Q$ such that \[ P(t_Q(\pi))=Q(\pi)\qquad\text{for all proof strings }\pi. \] A $p$-optimal system is one that polynomially simulates every Cook--Reckhow system. Determine whether such a $P$ exists. The translator and its polynomial bound may depend on $Q$, but $P$ must be fixed. Merely having polynomially short translations without an efficient procedure is the weaker notion of optimality and is not this target.

Formulation and concept references: [S1].

Short English statement

Is there one propositional proof system that can efficiently translate and simulate proofs from every other propositional proof system?

Sources
