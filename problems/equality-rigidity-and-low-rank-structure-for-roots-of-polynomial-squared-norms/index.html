Math-1696

Equality rigidity and low-rank structure for roots of polynomial squared norms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

John D'Angelo and Dror Varolin ask for the power radical of polynomial squared norms. Let $\mathcal P$ be Hermitian polynomial kernels $R(z,\bar w)=\overline{R(w,\bar z)}$, and let $\mathcal S$ consist of kernels $\sum_jp_j(z)\overline{p_j(w)}$ with holomorphic polynomials $p_j$. The target is to characterize \[ \{R\in\mathcal P:\exists N\ge1,\ R^N\in\mathcal S\}. \] Equality on the diagonal polarizes to kernel equality. Positive exponents are intended; the zero kernel is a trivial case. The original OCR loses several conjugation bars and superscripts.

The source records two exact criteria only for $N=1$. First, $R\in\mathcal S$ precisely when every finite evaluation matrix $(R(z_i,\bar z_j))_{i,j=1}^k$ is positive semidefinite, for all $k$ and all point choices. Second, writing $R=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}z^\alpha\bar w^\beta$, it gives positivity for every finite-size commuting tuple $A=(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$: \[ R[A,A^*]=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}(A^*)^\beta A^\alpha\ge0. \] Adjoints are placed first; the $A_i$ commute with one another but need not be normal or commute with adjoints. Neither $N=1$ test by itself classifies roots of squared norms.

The aid reports that a nonzero $R$ admitting a power has constant weak diagonal sign off its complex-algebraic zero set. Choose $Q=R$ or $-R$ with nonnegative diagonal. It satisfies the polarized two-point inequality \[ |Q(z,\bar w)|^2\le Q(z,\bar z)Q(w,\bar w). \] The source already notes that this condition can hold without being a squared norm. The aid adds equality rigidity: if $Q(a,\bar a),Q(b,\bar b)>0$ and equality holds for $(a,b)$, then \[ Q(u,\bar b)=\frac{Q(a,\bar b)}{Q(a,\bar a)}Q(u,\bar a) \quad\text{as a polynomial identity in }u. \] Thus a positive-power representation forces proportional full polarized rows at every such equality pair, not merely the two-point numerical equality.

Combining this with the cited low-rank theory, the aid reports a complete special case: if the Hermitian coefficient matrix has rank at most two, some positive power belongs to $\mathcal S$ exactly when $R\in\mathcal S$ or $-R\in\mathcal S$. The negative case is permitted by even powers.

It also treats, in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$, the family \[ r_{k,t}(z,\bar w)=(z_1\bar w_1+z_2\bar w_2)^{2k} -t(z_1z_2\bar w_1\bar w_2)^k. \] For every integer $k\ge2$, the aid states that $t=4^k/2$ is the sharp Cauchy--Schwarz boundary member, satisfies that inequality, and has no squared-norm power. The equality pair $a=(1,1)$, $b=(1,-1)$ has positive diagonals and equal absolute cross value, but its two polarized rows are not proportional, supplying the stated obstruction. The restriction $k\ge2$ is essential. These results do not give the general necessary-and-sufficient characterization, which the aid describes as open in its checked literature.

Short English statement

The source's first-power tests are kept distinct from its power-radical question. The aid supplies an additional equality-row obstruction, a rank-at-most-two classification, and Cauchy–Schwarz boundary examples with no squared-norm power. It leaves the general characterization unresolved.
