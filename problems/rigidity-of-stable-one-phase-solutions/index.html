Rigidity of Stable One-Phase Solutions

OpenAI's claim

Critical Bernoulli dimension: The claimed first nonflat minimizing Bernoulli cone occurs in dimension seven; all homogeneous global solutions that minimize in lower dimensions are flat. Dimensions at most six have smooth local free boundaries, with the announced sharp singular-set bounds in higher dimensions.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For nonnegative $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^d)$, call it a global one-phase minimizer if on every ball it minimizes \[J(u;B)=\int_B(|\nabla u|^2+\mathbf1_{\{u>0\}})\,dx\] against all nonnegative Sobolev competitors with the same boundary trace. The claim states that every nonzero one-homogeneous global minimizer, $u(rx)=ru(x)$, in $1\le d\le6$ is $(x\cdot e)_+$ with $|e|=1$, and some nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer exists in $\mathbb R^7$. Thus the least nonflat minimizing-cone dimension is $d_*=7$. This concerns global minimizers rather than all stable critical points.

Outline of the argument

Critical Bernoulli dimension: A first hypothetical nonflat minimizer below dimension seven is reduced to a spherical component in dimension six. Tensor identities and stability produce a flux comparison whose algebraic estimate is verified with exact coefficient data. Removing cutoffs contradicts the spherical configuration; the known nonflat seven-dimensional construction gives the other side and the sharpness consequences.
