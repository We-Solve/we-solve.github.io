Testing coordinate truncation against both energy restrictions

Use the report's energy definitions: controllability energy is the least $\frac12\int_{-\infty}^0\|u(t)\|^2dt$ needed to reach a given state from zero in the remote past; observability energy is $\frac12\int_0^\infty\|y(t)\|^2dt$ under zero input. For the explicit stable full system \[ \dot x=-x+u,\qquad y=x,\qquad x,u,y\in\mathbb R^2, \] make the invertible nonlinear change $z_1=x_1$, $z_2=x_2-x_1^2$. Drop $z_2$ and evaluate the first transformed equation and output on $z_2=0$. This produces $\dot z=-z+u_1$ and $y=(z,z^2)$, with embedding $\iota(z)=(z,z^2)$.
Proposition: The full energies are $E_c(x)=\|x\|^2$ and $E_o(x)=\|x\|^2/4$. The truncated model has $\widehat E_c(z)=z^2$ and $\widehat E_o(z)=z^2/4+z^4/8$. Neither is the corresponding full energy restricted along $\iota$, except at $z=0$.
Proof: For each coordinate, reaching $x(0)=x_0$ requires $x_0=\int_{-\infty}^0 e^t u(t)dt$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives $x_0^2\le(\int e^{2t}dt)(\int u^2dt)=\frac12\int u^2dt$. Equality is achieved by $u(t)=2x_0e^t$, whose corresponding state tends to zero as $t\to-\infty$. Summing coordinates proves $E_c(x)=\|x\|^2$. Under zero input the state is $e^{-t}x$, and integration yields $E_o(x)=\frac12\|x\|^2\int_0^\infty e^{-2t}dt=\|x\|^2/4$. In the reduced scalar state equation the same one-coordinate calculation gives $\widehat E_c(z)=z^2$; an unused second input, if retained in the interface, is optimally zero. Under zero input its output is $(ze^{-t},z^2e^{-2t})$, so integrating half its squared norm gives $z^2/4+z^4/8$. The restricted full energies are $z^2+z^4$ and $(z^2+z^4)/4$, respectively. Their differences from the reduced energies are $z^4$ and $z^4/8$, positive for every nonzero $z$. ∎
Thus coordinate deletion, even after a globally invertible smooth change of variables, does not automatically preserve either energy as a restriction. In the original coordinates retaining the first axis instead yields a different embedding and matching energies for that simpler restriction. The failure is specifically of this naive truncation route; it is not an impossibility proof for an intrinsically chosen nonlinear reduced manifold.

Verification and remaining gaps

The quadratic and quartic integrals were evaluated, and at $z=1$ the controllability values are 1 versus 2 and observability values $3/8$ versus $1/2$. This example has a linear full model in nonlinear coordinates, sufficient to test coordinate dependence but not a proposed general nonlinear reduction.
