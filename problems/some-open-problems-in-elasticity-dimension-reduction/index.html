Some Open Problems in Elasticity — Dimension reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $h>0$ denote thickness and let $\Omega_h\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be a family of thin reference bodies. Rods shrink in two transverse directions, plates shrink in one direction about a planar mid-surface, and shells shrink about a curved mid-surface. For example, a plate family can have the form $\omega\times(-h/2,h/2)$ for a planar region $\omega$; this is illustrative, not a restriction supplied in the source.

A representative three-dimensional elastic energy is \[ E_h(y)=\int_{\Omega_h}W(x,\nabla y(x))\,dx-\mathcal L_h(y), \] where $y:\Omega_h\to{\mathbb{R}}^3$ is an admissible deformation, $W$ is stored energy, and $\mathcal L_h$ represents loads. After a specified rescaling to a fixed reference domain, one seeks an effective one-dimensional rod functional or two-dimensional plate or shell functional as $h\downarrow0$. The energy normalization, loads, material assumptions, and limiting variables depend on the regime and are not specified in the record.

Problem. Give rigorous derivations of models of rods, plates, and shells from three-dimensional elasticity as thickness tends to zero. A variational version, as discussed in the supplied progress summary, is to identify effective functionals $E_0$ and normalizations $c_h$ for which rescaled $c_hE_h$ $\Gamma$-converge in a specified topology: every convergent sequence obeys the lower bound \[ E_0(u)\leq\liminf_{h\downarrow0}c_hE_h(y_h), \] and every admissible limit $u$ has a recovery sequence with \[ \limsup_{h\downarrow0}c_hE_h(y_h)\leq E_0(u). \] Here convergence must be defined after identification of the varying domains and deformations with the chosen limiting variables. Compactness/coercivity assumptions are also needed to obtain conclusions about minimizing sequences. This is a description of a rigorous derivation framework, not a claim that the source fixes one scaling, topology, or particular model.

The supplied research summary calls plate and rod derivations solved in the literature, citing Friesecke--James--Müller (2002) and follow-ups, and says many shell regimes have been derived rigorously. It leaves open aspects of a unified full nonlinear hierarchy for arbitrary shell geometry. Its broad solved labels do not specify unrestricted material, loading, or boundary hypotheses and are not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Derive lower-dimensional rod, plate, and shell theories as limits of three-dimensional elasticity when thickness vanishes. The summary reports established variational derivations for rods and plates and many shell regimes, while leaving a fully unified nonlinear shell theory incomplete. The exact scalings and hypotheses are absent.
