Math-2019

Constant mean curvature in homogeneous $3$-manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A Riemannian 3-manifold $X$ is homogeneous if its isometry group acts transitively. A constant mean curvature sphere is an immersion $F:S^2\to X$ for which the mean curvature is constant, with the customary choice of local or global normal convention. Such an immersion is not assumed embedded.

For a point $x\in X$, the source asks whether the family of CMC spheres "about $x$" in "such a space" forms a foliation of $X\setminus\{x\}$. In a foliation formulation, their images must partition $X\setminus\{x\}$ into disjoint embedded 2-dimensional leaves, locally given by the slices in smooth product charts. Thus the request includes disjointness, full coverage of the punctured space, and local regularity; a collection of separate CMC immersions does not suffice.

The second question asks whether establishing such a foliation could prove embeddedness of CMC spheres in general. That proposed strategy also requires knowing which CMC spheres belong to the centered families; the excerpt supplies no such classification. It does not identify a center convention, the parameter or mean curvature range of the family, or the precise class of homogeneous spaces denoted by "such a space". Geodesic distance spheres and rotational symmetry must not be substituted for the unspecified CMC family. Completeness, simple connectedness, and a topology such as $X\cong{\mathbb{R}}^3$ are not explicitly stated and are not imposed.

Short English statement

Do the CMC spheres centered at a point in the intended homogeneous 3-manifold foliate its punctured complement? Could proving this yield embeddedness of CMC spheres? The ambient class and the meaning of centered need the missing surrounding context.
