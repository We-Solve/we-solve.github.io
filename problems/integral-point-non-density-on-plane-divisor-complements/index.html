Math-993

Integral-Point Non-Density on Plane-Divisor Complements

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ be a normal-crossing divisor of degree at least four, let $X={\mathbb{P}}^2_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\setminus D$, fix an affine embedding $\iota:X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^n_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$, and let $S$ be a finite set of rational primes. Put \[ {\mathbb{Z}}[S^{-1}]=\{a/b\in{\mathbb{Q}}:\text{every prime dividing }b\text{ lies in }S\} \] and define the integral points with respect to this fixed embedding by \[ X({\mathbb{Z}}[S^{-1}];\iota) =\{x\in X({\mathbb{Q}}):\iota(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[S^{-1}]^n\}. \] The conjecture is that, for every allowed $D$, embedding, and finite $S$, \[ \overline{X({\mathbb{Z}}[S^{-1}];\iota)}^{\mathrm{Zar}}\ne X. \] Equivalently the integral points lie in a proper Zariski-closed subset, allowed to depend on $D$, $\iota$, and $S$. The conclusion is non-density, not finiteness of the point set or a uniform exceptional subset for all $S$ and embeddings.

The usual reduced normal-crossings convention means that, after passage to an algebraic closure, the divisor is locally a smooth branch or two smooth transverse branches in surface coordinates. The source does not explicitly state reducedness, multiplicity conventions, or simple versus ordinary normal crossings. This matters: degree of a divisor with multiplicities and degree of its support need not agree, while the complement depends only on the support. The intended reduced/support-degree hypothesis must be checked rather than silently counted with arbitrary multiplicities.

No splitting of the components over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, line-arrangement assumption, or restriction on $|S|$ is imposed. The supplied assessment places the question in higher-dimensional nonabelian-Chabauty motivation but calls it a finiteness question; that wording is not substituted for the explicit weaker non-Zariski-density target.

Short English statement

For a normal-crossing plane-divisor complement of degree at least four, must the rational points integral outside a fixed finite set of primes in a chosen affine embedding lie in a proper algebraic subset? The claim is non-density, with reducedness and divisor-degree conventions needing confirmation.
