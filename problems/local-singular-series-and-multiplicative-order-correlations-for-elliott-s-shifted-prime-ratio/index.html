Local singular series and multiplicative-order correlations for Elliott's shifted-prime ratio

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed integer $a>1$, the damaged source equation is reconstructed as \[ a^k=\frac{p+1}{q+1},\qquad p,q\text{ prime},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ k\ge2. \] The historical text attributes to P. D. T. A. Elliott (2003) infinitely many such solutions with $k\le\log q$. K. B. Ford suggests adapting the GPY method to reduce this range. J. Pintz's suggested target is reconstructed from corrupted characters as $k\ll (\log q)^{1/2+\epsilon}$, with $\epsilon>0$; its exact quantifiers and implied-constant dependence are not recoverable here. Participants identify the need for an analogue of Gallagher's average singular-series result, and A. Granville notes independent interest in the singular series itself.

For $A=a^k$, set $q=n$ and $p=An+A-1$. Let $\nu_\ell$ count the residues $n\pmod\ell$ for which $n(An+A-1)=0$. The aid states \[ \nu_\ell=\begin{cases}1,&\ell\mid A(A-1),\\2,&\ell\nmid A(A-1),\end{cases} \] so the pair is admissible. Its singular series is \[ \mathfrak S_a(k)=\prod_\ell\frac{1-\nu_\ell/\ell}{(1-1/\ell)^2} =2C_2\prod_{\substack{\ell>2\\\ell\mid a(a^k-1)}}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2},\qquad C_2=\prod_{\ell>2}\left(1-\frac1{(\ell-1)^2}\right). \] Geometric tuples of forms $a^{k_j}(n+1)-1$ are likewise admissible, since $n=-1$ avoids every zero modulo every prime.

For a fixed prime cutoff $z\ge2$, write $\mathfrak S_{a,z}(k)$ for the truncated product and put \[ B_a(z)=2\prod_{\substack{2<\ell\le z\\\ell\mid a}}\frac\ell{\ell-1} \prod_{\substack{2<\ell\le z\\\ell\nmid a}}\frac{\ell(\ell-2)}{(\ell-1)^2},\qquad \mathcal P_z=\{\ell:2<\ell\le z,\ \ell\nmid a\}. \] For $\ell\in\mathcal P_z$ let $r_\ell=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(a)$. For $D\subseteq\mathcal P_z$ let $c_D=\prod_{\ell\in D}(\ell-2)^{-1}$ and $r_D=\operatorname{lcm}_{\ell\in D}r_\ell$, with $c_\varnothing=r_\varnothing=1$. The aid's exact finite-cutoff identity is \[ \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\mathfrak S_{a,z}(k)}{B_a(z)} =\sum_{D\subseteq\mathcal P_z}c_D\frac{\lfloor K/r_D\rfloor}{K}. \] For fixed $z$, its limit is $\sum_Dc_D/r_D$, which is at least the independent-event benchmark $\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P_z}(1+1/((\ell-2)r_\ell))$. In the limiting uniform exponent average the divisibility indicators have covariance \[ \frac1{\operatorname{lcm}(r_\ell,r_m)}-\frac1{r_\ell r_m} =\frac{\gcd(r_\ell,r_m)-1}{r_\ell r_m}\ge0. \] These statements average over exponents starting at $1$ for convenience, whereas the prime-ratio problem requires $k\ge2$. They do not give uniform control as $z\to\infty$, a full Gallagher analogue, or the requested improvement of Elliott's exponent range.

Short English statement

Improve the exponent range in Elliott's multiplicative shifted-prime problem. The supplied aid computes its local singular series and exact finite-cutoff exponent averages, showing correlations between multiplicative orders while leaving the required prime-producing argument open.
