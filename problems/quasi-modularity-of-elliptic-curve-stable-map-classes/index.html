Math-3778

Quasi-Modularity of Elliptic-Curve Stable-Map Classes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}$ denote the moduli stack of stable one-pointed curves of arithmetic genus one, and let $\pi:\mathcal E\to\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}$ be its universal curve. The compactified base includes nodal curves, so this family is not everywhere a family of smooth elliptic curves. For nonnegative integers $g,n,d$, the supplied statement uses $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(\pi,d)$ for a moduli space of genus-$g$, $n$-pointed stable maps of degree $d$ into fibres of $\pi$, with virtual fundamental class $[\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(\pi,d)]^{\mathrm{vir}}$. The precise compactification, degree convention on singular fibres, and virtual-class construction are not specified in the supplied record.

The requested moving-target assertion is that \[ \sum_{d\geq 0}q^d[\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(\pi,d)]^{\mathrm{vir}} \] is quasi-modular as a cohomology-valued generating series. As written, its coefficients belong to different moduli spaces. A common coefficient space and a map taking each virtual class into that space are therefore necessary to formulate the assertion precisely.

The original discussion refers to classes on the moduli space of domain curves. A possible precise formulation, conditional on the missing choices, is the following. Suppose $2g-2+n>0$, use rational cohomology, and suppose the chosen stable-map compactification carries the required virtual class and a proper morphism \[ \operatorname{st}_d:\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(\pi,d)\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n} \] that forgets the map and stabilizes the marked domain. Here $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ denotes the moduli stack of stable $n$-pointed curves of arithmetic genus $g$. Define \[ A_{g,n,d}=\operatorname{cl}\!\left((\operatorname{st}_d)_*[\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(\pi,d)]^{\mathrm{vir}}\right) \in H^*(\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n};{\mathbb{Q}}), \] where $\operatorname{cl}$ is the cycle-class map. If quasi-modularity means holomorphic quasi-modularity for $\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})$, the proposed assertion becomes \[ \sum_{d\geq0}A_{g,n,d}q^d \in H^*(\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{Q}}[E_2,E_4,E_6], \] interpreted by Fourier expansion at $q=0$, where $E_2,E_4,E_6$ are the normalized Eisenstein series of weights $2,4,6$. Equivalently, the series is a finite polynomial in these Eisenstein series with cohomology-class coefficients. This is a conditional interpretation, not a uniquely determined reconstruction: the source record does not specify the coefficient field, modular group, allowed poles, weight or depth conditions, admissible $(g,n)$, or the degree-zero convention.

The original discussion distinguishes eight variants according to fixed or moving elliptic target, stable maps or admissible covers, and cohomology or Chow classes. The supplied displayed statement selects moving targets, stable maps, and cohomology; it does not assert all eight variants. The supplied open label and verification label are registry assessments, not mathematical conclusions established by this draft.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether classes obtained from stable maps to a varying elliptic curve form a quasi-modular generating series when summed by degree. The intended classes apparently lie on the moduli space of domain curves. A precise statement still requires the pushforward construction, the treatment of nodal target fibres and degree zero, the range of genera and markings, and the meaning of quasi-modularity. The displayed polynomial formulation is conditional on explicit conventional choices.
