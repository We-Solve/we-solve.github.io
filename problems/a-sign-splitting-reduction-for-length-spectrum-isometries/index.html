Math-1730

A sign-splitting reduction for length-spectrum isometries

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix a finite-type hyperbolic surface $S$, with its Teichmüller space and the theorem range specified. Use the unscaled length-spectrum metric \[ d_{\rm ls}(X,Y)=\max\{d_{\rm Th}(X,Y),d_{\rm Th}(Y,X)\} =\sup_\gamma\left|\log\frac{\ell_\gamma(Y)}{\ell_\gamma(X)}\right|. \] A convention multiplying this by $1/2$ gives the same isometry group. The question attributed to A. Papadopoulos is whether every bijective length-spectrum isometry comes from a mapping class. The intended candidate must be the effective extended group \[ \operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S)/\ker\left(\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S)\curvearrowright\mathcal T(S)\right), \] including orientation reversals and dividing out ineffective low-complexity kernels. Where orientation-reversing actions are effective, restricting to orientation-preserving mapping classes makes the proposed equality false. Degenerate and exceptional surface types require their own conventions; the source gives no surface range.

The supplied aid reports that every effective extended mapping class is a length-spectrum isometry, and that the equality with this corrected candidate remains open in the literature it checked. It records the following separation. The subgroup of maps preserving the directed distance $d_{\rm Th}$ globally is the effective extended mapping class group, in the applicable Thurston-rigidity range. Maps reversing that distance globally satisfy \[ d_{\rm Th}(FX,FY)=d_{\rm Th}(Y,X) \] and form at most one coset of the forward-preserving subgroup, if any exist. A remaining length-spectrum isometry would be genuinely mixed: neither globally forward-preserving nor globally reversing. The existence or exclusion of that reversing coset is a separate issue.

The aid gives a conditional infinitesimal reduction for $C^1$ isometries. Let \[ p_X(v)=\sup_\gamma\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\ell_\gamma|_X(v)}{\ell_\gamma(X)},\qquad N_X(v)=\max\{p_X(v),p_X(-v)\}. \] Suppose the unordered pair of opposite gauges $\{p_X,p_X\circ(-I)\}$ can be recovered from the symmetric norm $N_X$ so that every derivative isometry of $N$ either preserves or exchanges the two gauges, with a locally unique sign choice. Then for a $C^1$ length-spectrum isometry $F$, at every $X$ either \[ p_{FX}(D_XF(v))=p_X(v)\quad\forall v, \] or the same equality holds with $p_X(-v)$ on the right. Under the stated local uniqueness/continuity hypothesis, connectedness of Teichmüller space forces one global sign. Integrating the gauge along paths then makes $F$ either a forward Thurston isometry, hence a mapping-class action in the reported rigidity range, or a global forward-to-reverse anti-isometry. This does not prove gauge recoverability, $C^1$ regularity of every metric isometry, or nonexistence of reversing maps. None may be omitted when trying to deduce the original full isometry-group equality.

Short English statement

Determine whether length-spectrum isometries are exactly effective extended mapping classes. The supplied reduction separates directed-distance-preserving, reversing and mixed maps; eliminating mixed maps requires a conditional recovery of the two asymmetric tangent gauges, and does not itself eliminate global reversals.
