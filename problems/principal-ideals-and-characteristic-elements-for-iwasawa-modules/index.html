Math-3626

Principal Ideals and Characteristic Elements for Iwasawa Modules

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $G$ be a compact $p$-valued group with no elements of order $p$, in the sense intended by the report. The original extraction places the question in the noncommutative case. Write \[ \Lambda_G={\mathbb{Z}}_p[[G]] =\varprojlim_{U}{\mathbb{Z}}_p[G/U], \] where $U$ ranges over open normal subgroups; this is the usual integral Iwasawa-algebra convention. Let $M$ be a finitely generated torsion $\Lambda_G$-module, as specified in the restored statement.

The questions are:

  1. Can the reflexive right ideals $L_i$ in the report's structure theorem be replaced, possibly after changing the choices allowed by that theorem, by principal right ideals? A right ideal is principal if $L=a\Lambda_G$ for some $a\in\Lambda_G$. Reflexivity means that the canonical evaluation map to the module's double dual is an isomorphism, with the appropriate right/left module duals over $\Lambda_G$. The requested replacement must preserve the structure-theorem description of $M$; it is not the assertion that every reflexive right ideal in the ring is principal.
  2. How should a characteristic element be defined in general for the intended torsion modules over this noncommutative Iwasawa algebra?

The structure theorem itself is not supplied: the role of the ideals, the side of the module $M$, and the permitted equivalence or modification of representatives are missing. The original wording incorrectly or differently refers to a theorem "for pseudonull modules," whereas the supplied verification expressly restores a finitely generated torsion-module hypothesis. This discrepancy is retained rather than treating torsion and pseudonull as synonymous. Torsion requires the report's rank or localization convention; no unprovided Ore-localization hypotheses are invented.

For the characteristic-element question the target group, existence properties, ambiguity up to units or another equivalence, and functorial or exact-sequence requirements are not specified. The dated assessment reports localized $K_1$ constructions for broad classes, but does not state a localization or boundary map covering every module here. Those constructions do not by themselves answer the stronger principal-ideal question or define an ordinary ring element in complete generality.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether the ideals appearing in a torsion-module structure theorem can be chosen principal while preserving that description. The second asks for a general noncommutative characteristic element. The exact theorem, allowed replacements, and characteristic-element target are missing.
