Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Problem 14. Use the transformations from probabilities to moments to cumulants to de-scribe independence models/ hierarchical models in terms of polynomial functions in the cumulants.

Attempt with an explicit finite convention

The original task does not fix a hierarchy or a number of variables. Here we work with $n$ binary variables $X_i\in\{0,1\}$ and normalized probabilities, and use only square-free cumulants. This gives an exact transport procedure for a specified finite model, followed by a test of whether a simpler vanishing rule can replace that procedure. It is not a classification of unspecified hierarchical models.
Polynomial coordinate change, with proof.
For $A\subseteq[n]$ put $m_A=\mathbb E\prod_{i\in A}X_i$, with $m_\varnothing=1$. Work in the finite ring $\mathbb Q[t_1,\ldots,t_n]/(t_1^2,\ldots,t_n^2)$ and write $M=\sum_A m_A t_A$, where $t_A=\prod_{i\in A}t_i$. The sum $M-1$ is nilpotent, so $K=\log M$ is a finite polynomial. Define $\kappa_A$ to be its coefficient of $t_A$ for $A\ne\varnothing$. The logarithm expansion gives \[ \kappa_A=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(A)}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)! \prod_{B\in\pi}m_B, \qquad m_A=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(A)}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_B. \] Here $\Pi(A)$ denotes set partitions. Indeed, a nonzero contribution to $(M-1)^k$ consists of $k$ disjoint nonempty blocks covering $A$; each unordered partition occurs $k!$ times. Multiplication by $(-1)^{k-1}/k$ gives the first coefficient. Applying the finite identity $\exp(\log M)=M$, the factor $1/k!$ in the exponential cancels the same $k!$ orderings and proves the inverse formula. If $p_S$ is the probability that exactly the coordinates in $S$ are one, then $m_A=\sum_{S\supseteq A}p_S$, and inclusion--exclusion gives \[ p_S=\sum_{B\supseteq S}(-1)^{|B\setminus S|}m_B. \] To check this inversion, the coefficient of $p_T$ in the right side is $\sum_{S\subseteq B\subseteq T}(-1)^{|B\setminus S|}$, equal to one for $T=S$ and zero otherwise. Thus normalized probabilities, nonempty moments and nonempty square-free cumulants are related by invertible polynomial transformations. Substituting these formulas in every specified probability equation transports that equation exactly; cell nonnegativity must still be imposed after reconstruction.
Where vanishing is sufficient.
For a partition of the variables into blocks, independence of these blocks implies $M$ is the product of their moment polynomials. Hence $K$ is the sum of their logarithms and every cumulant involving two blocks vanishes. Conversely, these vanishings split $K$ into polynomials on the blocks. Exponentiation splits $M$ into a product, so all moments factor. The inversion just proved then factors the joint cell probabilities, which proves block independence. These finite formal identities require neither convergence of an analytic series nor an assumption of positive cells.
The attempted hierarchical shortcut fails.
A tempting extension is to set a cumulant to zero whenever the corresponding log-linear interaction is absent. Consider positive binary $X,Y,Z$ with $\Pr(X=1)=1/3$, with $Y,Z$ conditionally independent given $X$, and with \[ \Pr(Y=1\mid X)=\Pr(Z=1\mid X)=1/4+X/2. \] All eight cells are positive. The logarithm of the joint probability is the sum of a function of $X$, a function of $(X,Y)$ and a function of $(X,Z)$, so there is no three-factor log-linear interaction. On the other hand, conditional independence and the displayed means give \[ \kappa_{XYZ}=\mathbb E[(X-p)(Y-\mathbb EY)(Z-\mathbb EZ)] =\tfrac14\mathbb E(X-p)^3 =\tfrac14p(1-p)(1-2p)=1/54, \quad p=1/3. \] The first equality follows by expanding the product and using the three-variable cumulant formula above. For the second, condition on $X$ and multiply the two conditional centered means $(X-p)/2$. Finally a Bernoulli variable has central third moment $p(1-p)^3-(1-p)p^3=p(1-p)(1-2p)$. Thus the shortcut gives a false constraint even for this elementary hierarchy. Exact substitution works, but a useful hierarchy-wide simplification remains unproved.
