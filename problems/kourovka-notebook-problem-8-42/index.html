Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.42

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. A subgroup $H\leq G$ is soluble if its derived series terminates at the trivial group. Its index is $[G:H]=|G|/|H|$. For a finite soluble group $H$, define its $2$-length $\ell_2(H)$ to be the minimum number of nontrivial $2$-group factors in a normal series \[ 1=H_0\leq H_1\leq\cdots\leq H_r=H, \] where every $H_i$ is normal in $H$, and every factor $H_i/H_{i-1}$ is either a $2$-group or a group of odd order. Here a $2$-group is a finite group whose order is a power of $2$.

The problem is to classify, up to isomorphism, all finite groups $G$ such that \[ \forall H\leq G,\qquad \left(H\text{ is soluble and }[G:H]\text{ is odd}\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ \ell_2(H)=1. \] The quantifier includes $H=G$ whenever $G$ is soluble. No existence hypothesis for soluble subgroups of odd index is stated; if there are none, the displayed condition holds vacuously.

The equality $\ell_2(H)=1$ follows the literal source wording. Under the definition above, odd-order groups have $2$-length $0$, so replacing this equality by $\ell_2(H)\leq1$ would change the problem. The supplied record does not clarify whether such a weaker interpretation was intended.

The source reports that the groups denoted by $L_n(2^m)$ are known to satisfy the condition. In standard notation these are projective special linear groups $\operatorname{PSL}_n(\mathbb F_{2^m})$; the record does not explicitly specify parameter ranges or exceptional cases. This example is retained as a source assertion, without independent verification.

Short English statement

Classify the finite groups for which every soluble subgroup of odd index has $2$-length exactly $1$, using the literal wording of the source. The source lists $L_n(2^m)$ as examples. The exact-versus-at-most interpretation and the example parameters require review.
