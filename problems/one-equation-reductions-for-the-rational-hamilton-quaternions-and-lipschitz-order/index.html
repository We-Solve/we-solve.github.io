One-equation reductions for the rational Hamilton quaternions and Lipschitz order

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $\mathbb H_{\mathbb Q}$ be the Hamilton quaternion algebra over $\mathbb Q$, and let $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z+j\mathbb Z+k\mathbb Z$ be its Lipschitz order. Decide the solvability of arbitrary noncommutative polynomial equations with either rational or quaternion coefficients, and with unknowns constrained either to $\mathbb H_{\mathbb Q}$ or to $\mathcal O$. Also ask for existential definitions of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathcal O$ and of $\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb H_{\mathbb Q}$, and for the analogous decision questions in $M_n(\mathbb Z)$ and $M_n(\mathbb Q)$.

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

OpenAI claim summary for Math-2303

Rational undecidability can be interpreted in central quaternion or matrix variables. The bundle also includes integral-order equations, and those are not resolved merely by the rational-polynomial reduction.

One-equation reductions for the rational Hamilton quaternions and Lipschitz order

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 004; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that rational solvability of integer-coefficient polynomials with an unrestricted number of variables is undecidable.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
With $n$ variable and integral polynomial coefficients explicitly encoded, the rational Diophantine decision claim is \[ \{f\in\mathbb Z[X_1,\ldots,X_n]:\exists x\in\mathbb Q^n,\ f(x)=0\} \quad\text{is undecidable}. \] There is no algorithm giving the correct yes/no answer for every such input; the coefficient ring is $\mathbb Z$, but sought solutions lie in $\mathbb Q$.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 004; 2026-09-24), integer solvability is represented by an effective sequence of finite rational tests. Elliptic indices, odd valuation contacts, and a height argument force an ordinary integer witness when every test succeeds; a hypothetical rational decision procedure would then decide the integer problem by two concurrent searches.
