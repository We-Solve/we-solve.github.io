Statement

Predicting trophic cascades

Domain. Community ecology; trophic perturbations.   Retained status/source context. Open nonlinear intervention forecasting problem. Linear press responses are established; full species removal can cross bifurcations and change interactions.

Definitions and assumptions.
Let community state $N$ have stable equilibrium $N^*(u)$ in dynamics $\dot N=f_\theta(N)+u b$, where vector $b$ specifies a controlled pressure on focal species $k$ and $u$ its intensity. Parameters include measured functional responses and uncertainty from pairwise assays. The Jacobian $J(u)$ is invertible along an initial branch. A removal intervention instead enforces $N_k=0$ and changes the survivor system explicitly; its eventual state is denoted $N^{(-k)}$. The removal outcome is evaluated over a declared horizon when no unique final equilibrium exists. In that case define $\Delta_i$ using a time-averaged or distributional abundance contrast. Such a response must not be assigned the sign of a nonexistent equilibrium difference.
Problem statement.
Forecast the signs and sizes of survivor responses, distinguishing small and large perturbations: \[ \frac{\,\mathrm{d} N^*}{\,\mathrm{d} u}=-J(u)^{-1}b, \qquad \Delta_i=N_i^{(-k)}-N_i^*(0),\quad i\ne k. \] Construct a parameter uncertainty set on which each predicted sign is robust, or report an unresolved sign if both positive and negative responses remain compatible with observations.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
The inverse-Jacobian derivative is the established implicit-function formula on a smooth invertible equilibrium branch. It does not extend through a bifurcation or approximate removal without a finite-perturbation error bound. Specify feasible pressure directions preserving nonnegative populations, the post-removal initial survivor state and the endpoint convention. Sign robustness requires a nonzero margin throughout the data-compatible parameter set.

Alternative formulations.
(i) For small sustained pressures validate the inverse-Jacobian response with linear-error bounds. (ii) For complete removal track nonlinear continuation, alternate attractors and changed diet choice; compare with short pulse disturbances whose responses may return to baseline.
Mathematical-status qualification.
The restricted calculation, identity, obstruction or construction identified in this entry is not itself a new open conjecture. The residual target is the explicitly delimited extension, identification, implementation or independent biological validation; an already known special case does not satisfy that residual target.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Recent energetic rules improve prediction of trophic interaction strengths, and established linear theory supplies a local benchmark. Sign patterns alone do not fix an inverse matrix when multiple pathways compete. Removal can release resources, alter behaviour or eliminate an interaction that existed only at positive abundance. The unresolved task is reliable whole-community intervention prediction from sparse ecological measurements, with explicit limits on extrapolating local press responses to large trophic cascades.

Attempted answer

Proposition: For finitely many calibrated linear restoration scenarios, stack required improvements as $Mu\geq b$ with $u\geq0$ and cost $c^Tu$. Every $y\geq0$ satisfying $M^Ty\leq c$ gives a certified necessary budget $b^Ty$ for any feasible action vector.
Proof: Multiply the restoration inequalities by $y$ to obtain $b^Ty\leq y^TMu=(M^Ty)^Tu\leq c^Tu$. Maximizing this lower bound is the linear-program dual when the finite optimization is well posed. ∎

What remains unresolved

The certificate does not identify real intervention response matrices or guarantee nonlinear basin recovery. Model uncertainty outside the scenarios, extinction, transient damage and feasible ecological actions remain unresolved.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed.
