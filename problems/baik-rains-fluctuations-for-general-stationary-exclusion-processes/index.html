Baik–Rains Fluctuations for General Stationary Exclusion Processes

Definitions and mathematical statement

An exclusion process is a particle process whose configurations assign an occupation variable $\eta_t(x)\in\{0,1\}$ to each site $x$; particle jumps are permitted only to vacant destination sites. Stationarity means that the initial configuration has an invariant probability law, so that the configuration law is unchanged by time evolution. The supplied record does not specify the site space, jump mechanism, stationary law, or the precise class of permitted non-nearest-neighbor jumps.

Let $\chi_{\mathrm{BR}}$ denote a random variable with the Baik--Rains distribution. Once a model, an observation path, and a current convention have been specified, let $J_T$ denote its signed integrated particle current up to time $T$. The proposed fluctuation limit has the schematic form \[ \frac{J_T-b_T}{cT^{1/3}}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\chi_{\mathrm{BR}}, \] where $b_T$ is the appropriate deterministic centering, $c>0$ is a model-dependent normalization, and convergence is in distribution. Source question. Does the stationary current fluctuation limit with $T^{1/3}$ scaling and Baik--Rains limiting distribution, attributed in the supplied record to Aggarwal for ASEP, extend to general stationary exclusion processes with non-nearest-neighbor jumps?

Short English statement

The question asks whether the stationary Baik--Rains current fluctuations attributed to Aggarwal for ASEP persist when particles may make non-nearest-neighbor exclusion jumps.
