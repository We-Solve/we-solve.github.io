Math-3929

Rank-Metric Stability and Finite-Group Exponent Problems

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

  1. For a field $K$ and a positive integer $d$, let $M_d(K)$ denote the $d\times d$ matrices over $K$, and let $\mathrm{GL}_d(K)$ denote the invertible matrices. Define the normalized rank distance by \[ d_{\mathrm{rk}}(S,T)=\frac{\operatorname{rank}_K(S-T)}{d}. \] The source asks whether almost commuting invertible matrices admit nearby commuting invertible matrices in this metric. The scalar field, the uniformity across fields, and the permitted dimensions of the approximating matrices are not specified. With a fixed field $K$ and approximants of the same dimension, the usual uniform stability formulation would ask whether \[ \begin{gathered} \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall d\geq1\ \forall A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_d(K),\\ d_{\mathrm{rk}}(AB,BA)<\delta \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists C,D\in\mathrm{GL}_d(K):\\ CD=DC,\qquad d_{\mathrm{rk}}(A,C)<\varepsilon,\qquad d_{\mathrm{rk}}(B,D)<\varepsilon. \end{gathered} \] Here $\delta$ is independent of $d,A,B$; its possible dependence on $K$ remains unresolved. This display is an explicitly conditional interpretation of the source question, requiring confirmation of the field and approximation conventions.

  2. For a finite group $G$, define its exponent by \[ \exp(G)=\min\{e\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:g^e=1_G\text{ for every }g\in G\}. \] Is the following assertion true? For every integer $k\geq2$ and every prime integer $n$, there exists an integer $N=N(k,n)\geq1$ such that, for every finite group $G$ and every generating set $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}$ of $G$, \[ \left[ \forall(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in(A\cup\{1_G\})^N, \quad (x_1\cdots x_N)^n=1_G \right] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exp(G)\leq n? \] The integer $N$ must be independent of $G$ and its chosen generators. Since identity elements may be inserted, the hypothesis also covers all positive words of length at most $N$ in the specified generators; inverses are not included unless they already belong to $A\cup\{1_G\}$. The inequality on the exponent is retained as supplied. The original extract says "any (prime) number $n$", whereas the cleaned registry statement explicitly restricts $n$ to primes; the intended scope beyond primes remains unclear.
The supplied dated curation reports partial results for unitary or self-adjoint matrices and for finite solvable groups, and describes the arbitrary-invertible-matrix and general finite-group assertions as unresolved. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified mathematical conclusions here.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether two invertible matrices whose products almost agree in normalized rank distance can be approximated by commuting invertible matrices, uniformly in dimension. Its field and approximation conventions need clarification. The second asks whether, for fixed generator count $k$ and prime $n$, a bounded test on positive words forces every finite group passing the test to have exponent at most $n$.
