Two-Step Path Density in Tripartite Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record leaves the parameter domains, graph conventions, and universal quantifier in the definition of $\phi$ implicit. The following is a conditional formalization using finite simple undirected graphs, real density parameters $x,y\in(0,1]$, positive integers $k$, and two-edge paths whose middle vertex lies in $B$. These conventions require confirmation against the cited source.

For such $x,y$, let $\mathcal G(x,y)$ consist of all tuples $(G,A,B,C)$, where $G$ is a finite simple undirected graph and $A,B,C\subseteq V(G)$ are pairwise disjoint nonempty sets satisfying \[ |N_G(a)\cap B|\ge x|B|\quad\text{for every }a\in A, \qquad |N_G(b)\cap C|\ge y|C|\quad\text{for every }b\in B. \] Here $N_G(v)$ denotes the set of neighbors of $v$. For $c\in C$, define \[ R_{A,B}(c)=\{a\in A:\text{ there exists }b\in B\text{ with }ab,bc\in E(G)\}. \] Thus $R_{A,B}(c)$ counts distinct vertices of $A$, rather than paths. Interpret the universal guaranteed proportion as \[ \phi(x,y)=\inf_{(G,A,B,C)\in\mathcal G(x,y)} \max_{c\in C}\frac{|R_{A,B}(c)|}{|A|}. \] Equivalently, $\phi(x,y)$ is the largest $z\in[0,1]$ such that every tuple in $\mathcal G(x,y)$ has some $c\in C$ with $|R_{A,B}(c)|\ge z|A|$. The record does not explicitly state this quantification over all admissible graphs; without it, the largest proportion would depend on the particular graph.

Under these conventions, the questions are whether \[ \forall x\in(1/3,1],\qquad \phi(x,x)\ge\frac12, \] and, more generally, whether \[ \forall k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\ \forall x,y\in(0,1],\qquad \left(x+ky>1\ \text{and}\ kx+y\ge1\right) \Longrightarrow \phi(x,y)\ge\frac1k. \] The strict inequality in $x+ky>1$ and the weak inequality in $kx+y\ge1$ are retained exactly. The supplied record does not settle whether zero density parameters are intended to be included, or specify the intended range of $k$.

Short English statement

Assume every vertex of $A$ has neighbors in at least an $x$-fraction of $B$, and every vertex of $B$ has neighbors in at least a $y$-fraction of $C$. Must some vertex of $C$ reach at least half of $A$ through $B$ when $x=y>1/3$? More generally, do $x+ky>1$ and $kx+y\ge1$ guarantee reachability of at least a $1/k$-fraction? The displayed formalization uses explicitly provisional domain and path conventions.
