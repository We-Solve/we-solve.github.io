Math-5416

Canonical supercharacter products: an exact divisibility criterion

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be finite and let $(\mathcal X,\mathcal K)$ be a supercharacter theory: $\mathcal X$ partitions the complex irreducible characters, $\mathcal K$ partitions $G$ into superclasses, the numbers of blocks agree, and the canonical characters \[ \sigma_A=\sum_{\chi\in A}\chi(1)\chi\qquad(A\in\mathcal X) \] are constant on superclasses and form their class-function basis. The identity singleton and trivial-character conditions use the usual convention. The question asks when all pointwise products have nonnegative integer coefficients in this canonical basis; zero coefficients are allowed by "positive integer combination."

Write $d_\chi=\chi(1)$ and let $m_{\chi,\psi}^{\theta}=\langle\chi\psi,\theta\rangle_G$ be the tensor-product multiplicity. Define \[ D_C=\sum_{\theta\in C}d_\theta^2,\qquad N_{AB}^{C}=\sum_{\substack{\chi\in A,\ \psi\in B\\\theta\in C}} d_\chi d_\psi d_\theta\,m_{\chi,\psi}^{\theta}. \] The aid's exact formula is \[ \sigma_A\sigma_B=\sum_{C\in\mathcal X}a_{AB}^{C}\sigma_C, \qquad a_{AB}^{C}=N_{AB}^{C}/D_C. \] Superclass-function closure ensures this expansion exists, and character orthogonality gives the coefficient. Hence all coefficients are automatically nonnegative rational, and the requested property is exactly \[ D_C\mid N_{AB}^{C}\qquad(A,B,C\in\mathcal X). \]

The aid reports integrality for every abelian supercharacter theory and every maximal theory. In the abelian case each degree is one, so the common coefficient on a character block is an integer tensor-product count. For the maximal theory of a nontrivial group, with $\sigma=\rho_{\mathrm{reg}}-1$, \[ \sigma^2=(|G|-1)1+(|G|-2)\sigma. \] For the minimal theory, however, the canonical singleton is $d_\chi\chi$, not the unscaled irreducible character. In $A_5$, a degree-three irreducible satisfies $\chi_3^2=1+\chi_3+\chi_5$, giving \[ \sigma_3^2=9\sigma_1+3\sigma_3+\tfrac95\sigma_5. \] Thus minimal-theory integrality is not automatic. The original remark asserts it for minimal theories of $S_n$, but that assertion conflicts with this canonical normalization: the standard degree-three character of $S_4$ has square $1+\chi_2+\chi_3+\chi'_3$, producing coefficient $9/2$ on $\sigma_2$. With unscaled irreducibles ordinary tensor multiplicities are integral; these normalizations must not be interchanged.

For another supercharacter normalization $\tau_A=c_A\sigma_A$, $c_A\ne0$, if $\tau_A\tau_B=\sum_C n_{AB}^{C}\tau_C$, then \[ a_{AB}^{C}=n_{AB}^{C}\frac{c_C}{c_Ac_B}. \] Consequently known integral products for orbit-normalized algebra-group supercharacters do not alone answer the canonical-$\sigma$ question; the scaling factors must pass the divisibility test. The original remark also hopes for an assertion about four supercharacter theories of $S_n$ and asks about natural algebra-group characters. It does not specify which theories or the relevant $n$ range, and the aid does not classify those remaining canonical cases.

Short English statement

Canonical supercharacter products have nonnegative rational coefficients, integral exactly under an explicit degree-weighted divisibility condition. Abelian and maximal theories pass, while minimal examples fail. Rescaling natural algebra-group characters can change integrality, so normalization is essential.
