Relaxation and Microstructure Evolution in Finite Plasticity

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source requests two related tasks for rate-independent finite plasticity: determine the relaxation of the first incremental variational problem, including the quasiconvex envelope of its effective energy density; and formulate an appropriate time-continuous evolution of microstructures and pattern formation.

Let $\mathcal Z$ denote the missing state space, including deformation and any internal variables, and let $I_1$ denote the first incremental functional with its initial state, load, constraints and boundary data. For a specified sequential convergence $\mathcal T$, its sequential relaxation would be \[ \overline I_1(z)=\inf\{\liminf_{j\to\infty}I_1(z_j):z_j\to z\text{ in }\mathcal T\}. \] The question asks to identify the corresponding relaxed functional for the intended model. The record gives neither $I_1$ nor $\mathcal T$, so the display defines the task's general meaning rather than a computed answer. A typical incremental form involves stored energy at the new load plus dissipation from the previous state, but its coefficients, constraints and elimination of internal variables cannot be invented here.

If $W_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the intended density on deformation gradients, its quasiconvex envelope $QW_{\mathrm{eff}}$ means the largest quasiconvex function below it, in the specified class. In the usual finite-valued setting, a function $V$ on matrices is quasiconvex if, for each constant matrix $F$, bounded test domain $U$ and $\psi\in W^{1,\infty}_0(U;{\mathbb{R}}^d)$ of the matching dimension, \[ V(F)\leq\frac1{|U|}\int_U V(F+\nabla\psi(x))\,dx. \] The density, dimensions, growth and determinant constraints, and any extended-value convention are missing. Equality of the relaxation with an integral of $QW_{\mathrm{eff}}$ requires hypotheses; no cell formula, rank-one-convex substitute or laminate formula is asserted to give the full relaxation automatically.

The time-continuous request requires a state description capable of representing microstructure, a loading history, dissipation, a solution concept and convergence of any discrete-time approximations. Rate independence ordinarily means invariance under increasing time reparametrization, with time-dependent loads reparametrized as well; it does not by itself select an energetic, differential or other evolution law. No unique law or convergence theorem can be specified without these model choices.

The literature triage checked 2026-08-21 reports advances in incremental relaxation, laminate evolution and continuous-time variational models, while retaining physical softening, general relaxation and experimentally validated evolution as unresolved parts of the broad agenda. These qualifications are preserved as dated assessment, not added as proved mathematical conclusions. The partially-solved label and reported source normalization are not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Determine the relaxed first-step problem and its effective quasiconvex density, then formulate a continuous evolution that represents plastic microstructures and pattern formation. The energy, dissipation, admissible states and convergence topology are missing. Laminate constructions or a generic evolution framework are not assumed to solve the full model.
