Math-1245

Singularities, Fano type, and anticanonical height zeta functions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Cheltsov's AIM question asks for the appropriate class of varieties on which to study height zeta functions, including smooth Fano varieties, and asks whether klt varieties or varieties of Fano type should be allowed. For a number field $k$, an open subset $U\subset V$ and a chosen adelic height $H_L$, the associated series is \[ Z_{U,H_L}(s)=\sum_{x\in U(k)}H_L(x)^{-s} \] where it converges. The height, deleted exceptional/accumulating sets and desired analytic properties must be specified; the original item does not do so.

The source's printed definition says "$(V,\Delta)$ is ample", which the supplied verification confirms is in the original. This is not a defined positivity property of a pair. The standard proposed repair is: $V$ is of Fano type if some effective ${\mathbb{Q}}$-boundary $\Delta$ makes $(V,\Delta)$ klt and $-(K_V+\Delta)$ ample. Klt means positive log discrepancies, equivalently discrepancies greater than $-1$ on a resolution in the intended characteristic-zero setting. This repair is explicit, not a certified recovery of the author's wording. The source suggests quasi-smooth weighted hypersurfaces of degree less than the sum of weights as examples; ambient and well-formedness hypotheses are not supplied.

The report separates local discrepancy-measure integrability, a birational Fujita invariant, and global height behavior. It claims klt is the exact local integrability threshold in the relevant resolved discrepancy-measure setting. For a smooth projective model $X$ and a big nef divisor $L$, set \[ a(X,L)=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:K_X+tL\text{ is pseudoeffective}\}. \] For a canonical ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Fano $Y$, it claims $a(X,f^*(-K_Y))=1$ on a resolution $f:X\to Y$. The precise local measure conventions and full theorem hypotheses are absent.

Its global example blows up $r\geq4$ distinct rational collinear points of ${\mathbb{P}}^2$ to obtain $X_r$. The strict transform $C$ of the line has $C^2=1-r$ and anticanonical degree $3-r<0$. The report claims $X_r$ is smooth and of Fano type but that its all-points anticanonical height zeta series diverges for every real $s>0$. It further contracts $C$ to a klt noncanonical ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Fano surface $f:X_r\to Y_r$ and claims \[ a(X_r,f^*(-K_{Y_r}))=\frac{3(r-1)}{2r}>1. \] Thus Fano type alone does not guarantee a viable raw all-points anticanonical series, and merely klt need not give the canonical-model invariant $1$.

The comparison across $f$ is only an identification of divisor classes on the common open set with transported metrics there. The report expressly does not claim a bounded comparison of arbitrary global metrics near the deleted curve. These are obstructions and conditional criteria, not a complete answer specifying all admissible zeta-function theories.

Short English statement

Choose geometric and metric hypotheses for height zeta functions, distinguishing local singularity integrability from global height growth. The report gives smooth Fano-type surfaces whose raw anticanonical series diverges and klt contractions with a resolution Fujita invariant greater than one. It also flags the original malformed Fano-type definition and limits its metric comparison to a common open set.
