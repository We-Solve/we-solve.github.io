Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let $CP_n=\{BB^T:B\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{n\times k}\text{ for some }k\}$ be the cone of completely positive matrices. Is membership in $CP_n$ in NP? How can the cp-rank $\operatorname{cp}(A)=\min\{k:A=BB^T,\ B\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{n\times k}\}$ of an arbitrary $A\in CP_n$ be determined?

The attempted certificate argument

Dur's report, printed pages 2721--2723, asks both about NP membership and cp-rank, and describes a factorization heuristic whose success is not universal. For a standard Turing-model membership question we take the input symmetric matrix $A$ to have rational entries in binary. Arbitrary unencoded real inputs do not define that NP question. The proposed shortcut is to bound factor width and then declare the factor a polynomial certificate. The first part can be proved; the second needs additional information about the entries.
A complete polynomial-width proof.
Let $D=n(n+1)/2$, the dimension of the space of real symmetric matrices. If $A$ is completely positive, start with a finite sum $A=\sum_{j=1}^k b_jb_j^T$, $b_j\ge0$, discarding zero terms. If $k>D$, the symmetric rank-one matrices are linearly dependent: $\sum_j c_jb_jb_j^T=0$ for some nonzero real vector $c$. The coefficients have both signs: taking traces gives $\sum_jc_j\|b_j\|^2=0$, which cannot hold with nonzero coefficients of just one sign. Choose $t=\min_{c_j>0}1/c_j$. Then \[ A=\sum_j(1-tc_j)b_jb_j^T, \] all new coefficients are nonnegative and at least one vanishes. Absorb each positive coefficient into its vector by its nonnegative square root and repeat. Each iteration decreases the number of terms, proving $\operatorname{cp}(A)\le D$. The zero matrix has an empty decomposition (or zero columns if required by a width convention).

This proof gives an existential polynomial number of real parameters, not polynomially many bits. Even $A=(2)$ has cp-rank one with its unique nonnegative one-column factor $(\sqrt2)$, which is not rational: if $\sqrt2=a/b$ in lowest terms then $a^2=2b^2$ forces both $a$ and $b$ even. This example does not disprove NP membership, because $A=(2)$ also has the rational two-column factor $(1,1)$ and a short algebraic description of the one-column factor. It merely rejects the inference that a minimum-width factor can always be listed as rational numbers without further argument. No general polynomial bound on a suitable exact certificate's bit length or algebraic representation is proved here.

The exact real-feasibility reduction and its limit.
For each integer $k\le D$, the system \[ b_{ir}\ge0,\qquad\sum_{r=1}^k b_{ir}b_{jr}=A_{ij} \quad(1\le i\le j\le n) \] is feasible over the reals if and only if $\operatorname{cp}(A)\le k$; zero-padding proves the forward comparison of widths, and its equations are exactly the required factorization. Thus an exact existential-real feasibility subroutine would determine cp-rank by testing finitely many widths. This is a reduction to another decision task, not a polynomial-time algorithm or a supplied proof of that subroutine's complexity.

A family where ordinary rank badly underestimates cp-rank

Let $G$ be a finite simple triangle-free graph with $m$ edges and no isolated vertices. Set \[ A_G=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T. \] Its diagonal is the degree sequence, and its off-diagonal positive entries are exactly the graph edges, all equal to one. The displayed nonnegative factor proves $\operatorname{cp}(A_G)\le m$. In any nonnegative factorization, each column's positive support is a clique: if two support entries were positive at a nonedge $i,j$, their positive product would contradict $A_{ij}=0$, since all other products are nonnegative. Triangle-freeness therefore restricts a column to at most two positive entries. It can account for at most one positive off-diagonal graph edge. Every edge must be accounted for by some column, so at least $m$ columns are necessary. Consequently $\operatorname{cp}(A_G)=m$.

For $G=K_{a,b}$ with $a,b\ge2$, this is $ab$. The ordinary rank is $a+b-1$. Indeed, if $B$ is the displayed edge-column matrix, $B^Tx=0$ says $x_i+x_j=0$ for every cross-part pair. These equations force one constant on the first part and its negative on the second, giving a one-dimensional kernel. Also $\ker(BB^T)=\ker(B^T)$ because $x^TBB^Tx=\|B^Tx\|^2$. Hence the rank assertion follows. Balanced parts thus give quadratic cp-rank but only linear ordinary rank. An attempt to find a nonnegative factor merely by rotating an ordinary rank-width factor must fail on this family: rotation preserves its width, which is too small. Additional zero columns are necessary before such a rotation can even be possible.

Audit and remaining scope

The width reduction, support lower bound and bipartite family are elementary known mechanisms with complete proofs, not new general cp-rank results. The audit distinguished real width from bit length, and irrationality of a minimal factor from absence of every short certificate. It also checked zero entries, nonnegative cancellation, and the exact obstruction to a fixed-width orthogonal-rotation heuristic. The source's NP question and arbitrary-matrix factorization complexity are still unanswered in this attempt. No numerical or formal proof checking was performed.
