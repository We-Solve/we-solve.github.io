Math-311

Effective fragments of the maximal abelian and solvable extensions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$. Let ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be the union of all finite abelian Galois extensions of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ in $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$, and let ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{solv}}$ be the union of all finite solvable Galois extensions. Put \[ {\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{ab}}={\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}\cap\overline{{\mathbb{Z}}},\qquad {\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{solv}}={\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{solv}}\cap\overline{{\mathbb{Z}}}, \] where $\overline{{\mathbb{Z}}}$ is the ring of algebraic integers in $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$. Interpreting the unqualified source word "decidability" in the pure unital ring language $\{0,1,+,-,\cdot\}$, Koenigsmann's question asks, for each of these four structures $R$, whether there is an algorithm deciding \[ \operatorname{Th}(R)=\{\varphi:R\models\varphi\} \] for every ring-language sentence $\varphi$. The corresponding existential question restricts inputs to sentences $\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_N\,\psi$, where $\psi$ is quantifier free. The original short question does not distinguish full from existential theories; the supplied aid discusses both and reports all eight unrestricted decision questions as remaining open.

The supplied partial result concerns integer-coefficient polynomial systems, allowing inequations, whose geometric solution set is effectively certified to be finite. For example, take \[ X=\{z\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{,N}:f_1(z)=\cdots=f_r(z)=0, \ g_1(z)\cdots g_s(z)\ne0\}, \] with a certified zero-dimensional equation ideal; the empty set is allowed. List the finitely many Galois orbits of points of $X$. For a representative $z$, set $K_z={\mathbb{Q}}(z_1,\ldots,z_N)$, let $\widetilde K_z$ be its normal closure, and set $G_z=\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde K_z/{\mathbb{Q}})$. The criterion for an $R$-valued solution is that some representative satisfy \[ \begin{array}{c|c} R&\text{criterion}\\ \hline {\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}&G_z\text{ abelian}\\ {\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{solv}}&G_z\text{ solvable}\\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{ab}}&G_z\text{ abelian and every }z_i\text{ integral}\\ {\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{solv}}&G_z\text{ solvable and every }z_i\text{ integral}. \end{array} \] The aid asserts that exact algorithms for these finite algebraic computations give a uniform decision procedure. It further includes the complete one-quantified-variable existential fragment, with arbitrary quantifier-free Boolean combinations and inequations. In a disjunctive normal form, a nonzero equation leaves only finitely many algebraic candidates. If a disjunct has no nonzero equation, its identically zero equations and identically zero inequations are treated separately; finitely many nonzero univariate inequations exclude only finitely many points and can be simultaneously satisfied by an integer. This qualification is needed for the zero-polynomial and infinite-solution cases.

A separate reported structural distinction is \[ \forall n\ge1\ \forall a\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{solv}}\ \exists b\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{solv}}\ (b^n=a), \qquad \forall n\ge1\ \forall a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{solv}}\ \exists b\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{solv}}\ (b^n=a). \] Here $n$ indexes ordinary first-order sentences, rather than being a quantified exponent in the ring language. By contrast, neither ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ nor ${\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ contains a root of $X^3-2$. Thus the single ring sentence $\exists y\,(y^3=1+1)$, and also $\forall x\,\exists y\,(y^3=x)$, separates the solvable structure from the corresponding abelian structure. These finite-fragment and closure assertions do not decide unrestricted existential or full theories.

Short English statement

Determine decidability of the maximal abelian and solvable extensions of ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and of their rings of integers. The supplied aid reports decision procedures for finite algebraic solution systems and all one-variable existential sentences, together with a cube-root distinction, while retaining the unrestricted questions as open.
