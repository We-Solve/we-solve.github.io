Math-4271

Mode Counts of Gaussian Mixtures

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k,d\geq 1$ be integers. A standard nonsingular $k$-component Gaussian mixture on ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ has density \[ f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_i\frac{\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12(x-\mu_i)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_i^{-1}(x-\mu_i)\right)}{(2\pi)^{d/2}(\det\Sigma_i)^{1/2}}, \qquad w_i>0,\quad \sum_{i=1}^{k}w_i=1, \] where $\mu_i\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$ and each $\Sigma_i$ is a real symmetric positive definite $d\times d$ matrix. Write $\mathcal G_{k,d}$ for this class. These are explicit working conventions: the supplied record does not specify covariance nonsingularity, whether zero weights or repeated components are permitted, or whether $k$ denotes a displayed representation or the smallest possible number of components.

Under the strict-local-maximum convention, define \[ \operatorname{Modes}_{\mathrm{str}}(f)=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\ \exists r>0\ \forall y\in{\mathbb{R}}^d,\quad 0<\lVert y-x\rVert<r\ \Longrightarrow\ f(y)<f(x)\right\}, \qquad N_{\mathrm{str}}(f)=\#\operatorname{Modes}_{\mathrm{str}}(f). \] An infinite count is recorded as $+\infty$. The source does not specify whether modes must be strict, isolated, or nondegenerate, or whether all local maxima are counted. Consequently, the following formulation is conditional on the strict-local-maximum convention and the nonsingular mixture class above.

  1. Determine the possible mode counts and their sharp upper bound: \[ \mathcal A_{k,d}=\{N_{\mathrm{str}}(f):f\in\mathcal G_{k,d}\}, \qquad M_{k,d}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal G_{k,d}}N_{\mathrm{str}}(f). \] Determine whether the supremum is finite and attained. The source also asks for the "distribution" of the number of modes. If this means a probability distribution, a probability law on mixture parameters must first be supplied; none is specified. The set $\mathcal A_{k,d}$ describes attainable counts, not a probability distribution.
  2. For the componentwise isotropic interpretation, let \[ \mathcal G^{\mathrm{iso}}_{k,d}=\{f\in\mathcal G_{k,d}:\Sigma_i=\sigma_i^2I_d,\ \sigma_i>0\text{ for every }i\}. \] Is it true that \[ \forall d\geq1\ \exists C_d\in(0,\infty)\ \forall k\geq1\ \forall f\in\mathcal G^{\mathrm{iso}}_{k,d},\qquad N_{\mathrm{str}}(f)\leq C_d k? \] Here $C_d$ must be independent of the weights, means, variances, and $k$. The source does not say whether isotropy instead requires a common variance $\sigma_1=\cdots=\sigma_k$; that narrower version must be distinguished from the displayed version.
  3. Give explicit, verifiable sufficient conditions on the mixture parameters ensuring $N_{\mathrm{str}}(f)\leq k$. The source specifies neither a required form of these conditions nor a requirement that they be necessary.
  4. Formulate a Gaussian analogue of "strong modes." The supplied record gives no definition of strong modes or reference identifying the intended antecedent notion, so this part remains a request for a definition and cannot yet be stated as a unique mathematical assertion.

The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved. Its dated curation reports advances in mode-count bounds and constructions, says the full distribution and sharp maximum remain open, and reports that a binomial maximum conjecture is false already for three bivariate heteroscedastic components. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified facts in this draft. The record supplies no formula or precise hypotheses for that binomial conjecture.

Short English statement

For a mixture of $k$ Gaussian distributions in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, determine the possible numbers of modes and the sharp maximum. In fixed dimension, ask whether isotropic mixtures have at most $C_d k$ modes, find checkable conditions guaranteeing at most $k$ modes, and define an appropriate Gaussian notion of a strong mode. The meaning of mode, the isotropic covariance convention, and any probability law underlying "distribution" require clarification. The supplied partially solved status is dated curation requiring independent review.
