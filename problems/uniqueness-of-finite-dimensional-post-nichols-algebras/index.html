Math-904

Uniqueness of Finite-Dimensional Post-Nichols Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work over the field ${\mathbb{C}}$. Let $G$ be a group, with no finiteness assumption in the supplied statement. A left-left Yetter--Drinfeld module $V\in{}_{{\mathbb{C}} G}^{{\mathbb{C}} G}\mathcal{YD}$ is a $G$-graded complex vector space $V=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_g$ with a left $G$-action satisfying \[ h\cdot V_g\subseteq V_{hgh^{-1}}\qquad(g,h\in G). \] Its braiding is $c(v\otimes w)=g\cdot w\otimes v$ for $v\in V_g$. The Nichols algebra $\mathcal B(V)$ is the quotient of the braided tensor Hopf algebra $T(V)$, whose elements of $V$ are primitive, by the largest homogeneous braided Hopf ideal contained in $\bigoplus_{n\ge2}V^{\otimes n}$.

For this draft, a post-Nichols algebra of $V$ means a connected nonnegatively graded braided Hopf algebra \[ R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}R_n\quad\text{in }{}_{{\mathbb{C}} G}^{{\mathbb{C}} G}\mathcal{YD},\qquad R_0={\mathbb{C}} 1, \] with a specified identification $R_1\cong V$ and with \[ P(R):=\{x\in R:\Delta(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\}=R_1. \] Under this convention, the braided Hopf subalgebra generated by $R_1$ is the canonical copy of $\mathcal B(V)$. The supplied excerpt does not explicitly define post-Nichols algebra, so this convention requires comparison with the full report.

Question. For every group $G$ and every $V\in{}_{{\mathbb{C}} G}^{{\mathbb{C}} G}\mathcal{YD}$ such that $\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\mathcal B(V)<\infty$, is every finite-dimensional post-Nichols algebra $R$ of $V$ generated as an algebra by $R_1$? Equivalently, must the canonical inclusion \[ \mathcal B(V)\hookrightarrow R \] be an isomorphism of graded braided Hopf algebras respecting the specified degree-one identification?

Scope qualification. The supplied mathematical statement allows arbitrary groups, whereas the supplied literature assessment discusses arbitrary finite groups and the surrounding excerpt concerns finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras. This draft retains the stated arbitrary-group quantifier; it does not silently restrict $G$ to be finite.

Source context. The excerpt places this question, described there as generation in degree one, among subproblems of a conjecture about finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras. For such an algebra $H$, with group of group-like elements $G(H)$ and infinitesimal braiding $V$, that surrounding conjecture asserts \[ H\simeq\left(\mathcal B(V)\#{\mathbb{C}} G(H)\right)_{\sigma}, \] where $\#$ denotes bosonization and $\sigma$ is an invertible Hopf $2$-cocycle on the bosonization with values in ${\mathbb{C}}$. This is supplied conjectural context, not an additional assertion or a replacement for the question above.

Short English statement

Over ${\mathbb{C}}$, suppose the Nichols algebra of a Yetter--Drinfeld module $V$ over a group $G$ is finite-dimensional. Must every finite-dimensional post-Nichols algebra with degree-one part $V$ equal its Nichols subalgebra, and hence be generated in degree one? The supplied statement allows arbitrary groups; the finite-group emphasis of the literature assessment and the precise post-Nichols convention require review.
