Spectral Geometry and Cyclic Homology of $SU_q(2)$

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For $0<q<1$, $\mathcal A(SU_q(2))$ denotes the polynomial algebra of the compact quantum-group deformation of $SU(2)$. A spectral triple $(\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D)$ represents $\mathcal A$ on a Hilbert space with a selfadjoint operator $D$, compact resolvent, and bounded commutators $[D,a]$ on the chosen algebra. The source also uses a real structure $J$ and an equivariant quantum-group action. Its ideal $K_q$ is a compact-operator ideal generated by operators whose spectra decay exponentially; the commutant and first-order axioms hold only modulo this ideal. Hochschild cycles express an orientation axiom, while cyclic cocycles encode index pairings. The metric associated to $D$ is read from the commutator seminorm $\|[D,a]\|$.
Source scope and qualifications.
Keep all bundled questions: Paschke duality and K_q; orientation; a canonical local-index cocycle; differential calculus and Dirac-operator freedom; equivariant cyclic theory. The source also asks to understand a suitable smooth algebra and calculate its cyclic homology. These are distinct requirements, not one interchangeable index computation.
Additional source context.
The report first asks for a satisfactory $C^\infty$ completion of the polynomial algebra and its cyclic homology. In the local-index question the desired geometric three-cocycle is distinguished from the already computed one-cocycle image.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the spectral geometry of $SU_q(2)$, what are the geometric roles of Paschke duality and the ideal $K_q$? Can the Hochschild-cycle axiom be satisfied or suitably modified? Is there a canonical local-index cocycle from the spectral data? What differential calculus and metric arise, and what freedom in the Dirac operator changes that metric? What is the correct equivariant cyclic-homology framework, and how can it be computed?
