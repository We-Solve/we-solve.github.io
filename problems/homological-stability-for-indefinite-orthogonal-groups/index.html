Homological Stability for Indefinite Orthogonal Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns a field $k$ described as a "maximal ordered field." The record does not define maximality or specify the extensions relative to which it is taken; this hypothesis is therefore retained as an unresolved source qualification.

Under the conventional signature interpretation of the orthogonal-group notation, define, for nonnegative integers $n,i$, \[ q_{n,i}(x,y)=\sum_{a=1}^{n}x_a^2-\sum_{b=1}^{i}y_b^2, \qquad (x,y)\in k^n\oplus k^i, \] \[ O_{n,i}(k)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n+i}(k):q_{n,i}(gv)=q_{n,i}(v)\text{ for every }v\in k^{n+i}\}, \qquad O_m(k)=O_{m,0}(k). \] The negative definite form $-\sum y_b^2$ has the same isometry group as $\sum y_b^2$, so block diagonal matrices give an inclusion \[ \iota_{n,i}:O_n(k)\times O_i(k)\hookrightarrow O_{n,i}(k), \qquad (A,B)\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(A,B). \] Here $H_d(G;{\mathbb{Z}})$ denotes homology of the abstract group $G$, regarded as discrete, with trivial integral coefficients. If zero-dimensional forms are included, their orthogonal groups are trivial.

Question, conditional on these conventions. For every field $k$ satisfying the intended maximal-ordered-field hypothesis and every fixed pair of nonnegative integers $d,i$, does there exist a nonnegative integer $N=N(k,d,i)$ such that, for every integer $n\geq N$, the map \[ (\iota_{n,i})_*: H_d\left(O_n(k)\times O_i(k);{\mathbb{Z}}\right) \longrightarrow H_d\left(O_{n,i}(k);{\mathbb{Z}}\right) \] is an isomorphism?

The form conventions and nonnegative indexing above make explicit a conventional reading, but are not defined in the supplied source record. The wording "sufficiently large relative to $d$ and $i$" may additionally require a bound $N(d,i)$ uniform over the permitted fields $k$; the record does not resolve this quantifier distinction. No restriction on $i$ is supplied. These qualifications require source clarification before the displayed formulation can be treated as definitive.

Short English statement

For a field satisfying the source's undefined "maximal ordered" hypothesis, ask whether the block diagonal inclusion of two definite orthogonal groups into the corresponding indefinite orthogonal group induces an isomorphism on integral discrete group homology once $n$ is sufficiently large for fixed $d$ and $i$. The meaning of maximality, the precise form and index conventions, and uniformity in the field remain unresolved.
