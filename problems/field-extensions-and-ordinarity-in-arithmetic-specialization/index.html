Math-14770

Field Extensions and Ordinarity in Arithmetic Specialization

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k'/k$ denote the finite field extension appearing in the result attributed to Bright and Newton in the supplied record; thus $[k':k]<\infty$. The record describes this result as an arithmetic specialization result establishing the existence of certain primes after a finite extension under a good ordinary reduction hypothesis. It does not state the theorem, specify the fields and geometric objects involved, define the relevant primes, or identify the precise ordinarity condition.

The two source questions are:

  1. Under what circumstances is passage from $k$ to $k'$ necessary in the cited result? In particular, under what circumstances does its conclusion hold over $k$ itself, so that one may take $k'=k$?
  2. Can the ordinarity hypothesis in that result be removed while retaining its remaining hypotheses and conclusion?
The second question concerns ordinarity; the supplied record does not ask to remove good reduction altogether. Neither question can be made into a self-contained quantified statement from the supplied excerpt: the theorem's hypotheses, conclusion, domains, and quantifiers are missing. In particular, the meaning of necessity of the extension depends on the unspecified conclusion and its quantifiers. These are source-relative questions requiring recovery of the cited theorem before mathematical review.

Short English statement

The problem asks when the finite extension in a cited Bright--Newton result can be avoided, and whether its ordinarity assumption can be dropped. The supplied record omits the theorem needed to specify either question precisely. Its label partially solved is a supplied curation assessment, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.
