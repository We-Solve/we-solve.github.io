Local-Cohomology Obstructions to Regular Maps and Projections

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks a research question rather than stating a conjecture with specified hypotheses. Its question is: can local cohomology obstruct the existence of injective or $k$-regular maps of complex varieties, or of low-dimensional projections defined by separating invariants? The following definitions explain possible formulations; the source does not determine a unique quantified mathematical statement.

For complex algebraic varieties $X,Y$, a map $f:X\to Y$ is injective on complex points if \[ \forall x,x'\in X({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad f(x)=f(x')\Longrightarrow x=x'. \] This condition alone does not assert that $f$ is a closed immersion. The record does not specify whether all maps under consideration must be algebraic morphisms, holomorphic maps, or continuous maps, nor whether injectivity is intended only on complex points or in a stronger scheme-theoretic sense.

One convention for $k$-regularity, for integers $k\geq 1$ and $N\geq 0$, requires a map $f:X({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^N$ to satisfy \[ \forall x_1,\ldots,x_k\in X({\mathbb{C}})\text{ pairwise distinct},\qquad \operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}f(x_1)&\cdots&f(x_k)\end{pmatrix}=k. \] A different convention requires the images of every $k$ distinct points to be affinely independent, equivalently \[ \operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\ f(x_1)&\cdots&f(x_k)\end{pmatrix}=k. \] The supplied record does not choose between these conventions or specify the target spaces.

To describe separating invariants conditionally, let a group $G$ act algebraically on an affine complex variety $V$, and write $A={\mathbb{C}}[V]$ and \[ A^G=\{h\in A: h(g\cdot v)=h(v)\text{ for all }g\in G,\ v\in V({\mathbb{C}})\}. \] A finite family $f_1,\ldots,f_m\in A^G$ is separating if, for all $v,w\in V({\mathbb{C}})$, \[ \left(\exists h\in A^G:\ h(v)\ne h(w)\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:\ f_j(v)\ne f_j(w)\right). \] It defines the invariant map $F:V\to{\mathbb{A}}^m_{{\mathbb{C}}}$, $F(v)=(f_1(v),\ldots,f_m(v))$. Separation concerns pairs distinguishable by invariant functions; it need not mean injectivity on $V({\mathbb{C}})$. Calling such a map a projection requires additional ambient data absent from the record.

For a Noetherian commutative ring $B$, an ideal $I\subseteq B$, and a $B$-module $M$, define \[ \Gamma_I(M)=\{u\in M: I^n u=0\text{ for some }n\geq1\}, \qquad H_I^i(M)=R^i\Gamma_I(M)\quad(i\geq0). \] These are local cohomology modules. An obstruction to a specified class of maps would require a proved necessary local-cohomology condition for existence, so that failure of that condition implies nonexistence. Here the ring, support ideal, module, cohomological degrees, and necessary condition are all unspecified.

Accordingly, the statement retained is the source's question about obtaining such obstructions. No particular nonvanishing criterion, numerical lower bound, universally quantified nonexistence assertion, or equivalence is supplied. The class of varieties, map category, invariant-theoretic setting, and meaning of low dimension require clarification before a precise conjecture can be extracted.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether local cohomology can obstruct injective maps, $k$-regular maps, or maps defined by separating invariants of complex varieties. The supplied record leaves the hypotheses, the convention for $k$-regularity, the relevant local cohomology, and the dimension bounds unspecified. This is a statement-only draft of that research question requiring human mathematical review.
