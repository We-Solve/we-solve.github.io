Math-14141

Topology of Strict-to-General Contactomorphism Inclusions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A contact form on a smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $2n+1$ is a smooth one-form $\alpha$ satisfying $\alpha\wedge({\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha)^n\neq 0$ everywhere. Write $\xi=\ker\alpha$ for its contact distribution. Define the strict contactomorphism group and the contactomorphism group by \[ \operatorname{Cont}(M,\alpha)=\{f\in\operatorname{Diff}(M):f^*\alpha=\alpha\}, \qquad \operatorname{Cont}(M,\xi)=\{f\in\operatorname{Diff}(M):f_*\xi=\xi\}. \] Once topologies on these groups have been specified, the subscript $0$ denotes the connected component of the identity in the respective group. Since preserving $\alpha$ implies preserving $\xi$, the inclusion induces \[ j:\operatorname{Cont}_0(M,\alpha)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Cont}_0(M,\xi),\qquad j(f)=f. \] Source question. For a contact manifold equipped with a contact form $\alpha$, determine the topology of this natural inclusion $j$.

Unresolved qualifications. This is an open-ended request to describe an inclusion, rather than a specified proposition about a particular invariant. The supplied statement does not specify the topology on the transformation groups; a customary interpretation uses a smooth $C^\infty$ topology, but its precise meaning must be fixed, particularly for noncompact $M$. It also does not specify dimension restrictions, compactness, boundary conditions, support conditions, or whether $M$ is assumed connected. The notation $\alpha$ requires a globally specified contact form. The definition of $\operatorname{Cont}(M,\xi)$ above allows all distribution-preserving diffeomorphisms; the source does not explicitly state a coorientation convention. Finally, although the supplied literature assessment discusses general regular contact manifolds, the displayed question does not impose regularity. The intended scope and the precise topological information requested therefore require review.

Short English statement

The question asks how the identity component of the group preserving a contact form sits inside the identity component of the group preserving its contact distribution. The group topology, manifold hypotheses, and intended topological invariants are not specified. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved, describing progress for the standard three-sphere and an unresolved general regular case; these are supplied assessments requiring independent review.
