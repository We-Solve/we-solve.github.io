Math-1066

Torsion-Unit Conjugacy and Local Endomorphism Orders

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite group $H$, let $\varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}} H\to{\mathbb{Z}}$ be augmentation, $\varepsilon(\sum_h a_hh)=\sum_h a_h$, and put \[ V({\mathbb{Z}} H)=\{u\in({\mathbb{Z}} H)^\times:\varepsilon(u)=1\}. \] A torsion unit is a unit $u$ such that $u^m=1$ for some integer $m\geq1$.

The first question has the quantifiers \[ \forall H\text{ finite}\quad\exists G\text{ finite with }H\leq G\quad \forall u\in V({\mathbb{Z}} H)\text{ torsion}\quad \exists x\in({\mathbb{Q}} G)^\times\ \exists h\in H:\ x^{-1}ux=h. \] Here ${\mathbb{Z}} H\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}} G$ is induced by the chosen inclusion. A single overgroup $G$ must work for every torsion unit of ${\mathbb{Z}} H$. The source then asks the stronger rational-conjugacy question: can $x$ always be chosen in $({\mathbb{Q}} H)^\times$? Such a choice would not depend on enlarging the ambient group algebra. Containment is written $H\leq G$ without imposing an unmentioned requirement that it be proper.

The second collection of questions concerns a finite group $G$, a prime $p$, a simple ${\mathbb{Q}} G$-module $X$, and a ${\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}G$-lattice $L\subset X$, where \[ {\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}=\{a/b\in{\mathbb{Q}}:p\nmid b\}. \] Under the usual full-lattice convention, $L$ is finitely generated free over ${\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}$, is $G$-stable, and satisfies ${\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}L=X$. Determine the structure of \[ E=\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}G}(L) =\{a\in D:aL\subseteq L\},\qquad D=\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{Q}} G}(X), \] an order over ${\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}$ in the rational division algebra $D$. The problem further requests the local-order building blocks arising from central primitive idempotents of ${\mathbb{Q}} G$, and a characterization of which such local orders can occur.

Because $X$ is simple, exactly one central primitive idempotent of ${\mathbb{Q}} G$ acts as the identity on $X$, and the others act as zero. Thus the phrase about building blocks is not by itself a nontrivial product decomposition of this particular $E$; the intended order construction, decomposition and equivalence notion are not specified. Likewise "local order" may refer to localization at $p$ rather than asserting that $E$ is a local ring. These meanings must be clarified for a complete classification statement.

The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the problem partially solved. It reports a positive answer for the prime-order restriction of the overgroup question and many rational-conjugacy cases, while calling the unrestricted finite-overgroup and endomorphism-ring questions unresolved. These are dated attributed claims, not conclusions proved here.

Short English statement

Ask whether one finite overgroup suffices to conjugate every normalized torsion unit of ${\mathbb{Z}} H$ to an element of $H$, and whether conjugation inside ${\mathbb{Q}} H$ suffices. Separately, determine the endomorphism orders of localized group lattices and classify their proposed local building blocks.
