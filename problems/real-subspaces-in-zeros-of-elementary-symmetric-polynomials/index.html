Math-6750

Real Subspaces in Zeros of Elementary Symmetric Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $m$ and an integer $r$ with $1\le r\le m$, define the elementary symmetric polynomial by \[ e_r(y_1,\ldots,y_m)=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_r\le m}y_{i_1}\cdots y_{i_r}, \qquad (y_1,\ldots,y_m)\in{\mathbb{R}}^m, \] and let \[ Z_{m,r}=e_r^{-1}(0)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^m:e_r(y)=0\}. \] A real vector subspace here means a linear subspace of ${\mathbb{R}}^m$, with dimension over ${\mathbb{R}}$.

The supplied conjecture, expressed in the conventional positive-degree range, asks whether for every positive integer $m$ and every even integer $r$ satisfying $2\le r\le m$, \[ \nexists\,L\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^m\quad\text{such that }L\text{ is a real linear subspace},\quad \dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}L=r,\quad L\subseteq Z_{m,r}. \] Equivalently, every real linear subspace $L\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^m$ of dimension $r$ must contain a vector $y$ with $e_r(y)\ne0$. The extracted conjecture does not explicitly state the ranges of $m$ and $r$; the displayed ranges make explicit the conventional nontrivial interpretation and require confirmation against the cited source.

The report attributes the conjecture to the author of its reference [5]. It also reports a proved special case, labeled Theorem 7: for $m=2k+1$, with $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$, the set $Z_{m,m-1}$ contains no real vector subspace of dimension $m-1$. The report says that its proof of Theorem 4 includes this special case, which reference [5] had described as particularly difficult. These are supplied source claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft. The source does not specify the convention for ${\mathbb{N}}$.

The preceding source passage separately says that extending its Theorem 6(b) to $m=2k+1>3$ is technically more complicated and had not been proved. The statement of that theorem is not supplied, so its precise relationship to the conjecture cannot be reconstructed here.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether, for every even degree $2\le r\le m$, each $r$-dimensional real linear subspace of ${\mathbb{R}}^m$ contains a point where the elementary symmetric polynomial $e_r$ is nonzero. The report claims the special case $r=m-1$ for odd $m$. The general open status is supplied dated curation, and the intended parameter ranges and source claims require human review.
