Erdős Problem 79: infinitely many minimal Ramsey obstructions

1) Statement and the external input

A finite graph $F$ is Ramsey size-linear if $r(F,H)=O_F(e(H))$ for every nonempty graph $H$ with no isolated vertices. Are there infinitely many graphs which fail this property although every proper subgraph has it? The answer is yes. We reconstruct Wigderson's argument and prove the elementary forest and large-girth ingredients. Graphs are considered up to isomorphism throughout.

The external Ramsey lower bound used is the following standard consequence of the Lovász local lemma, stated in Wigderson's note: for a fixed graph $F$ with $v=v(F)\geq3$ and $e=e(F)$, \[ r(F,K_q)=\Omega_F\left((q/\log q)^{(e-1)/(v-2)}\right). \] Only graphs satisfying $e\geq2v-2$ are needed. For these, $(e-1)/(v-2)\geq2+1/(v-2)>2$, and hence $r(F,K_q)/e(K_q)\to\infty$. Such $F$ are not Ramsey size-linear. The cited lower bound, rather than its probabilistic proof, is the sole non-elementary dependency of this reconstruction.

2) Forests are Ramsey size-linear

Let $F$ be a forest on $s\geq2$ vertices, and let $H$ have $m\geq1$ edges and no isolated vertices. Then $v(H)\leq2m$. A graph of minimum degree at least $s-1$ contains $F$: embed each tree from a root, always extending a previously embedded parent to an unused neighbor. At each step at most $s-2$ neighbors are already used; new component roots can likewise be chosen among unused vertices.

Consider a red--blue coloring on $N=2(s-1)m$ vertices with no red $F$. Every nonempty induced red subgraph has a vertex of degree at most $s-2$, by the preceding embedding argument. Remove such vertices successively and color them in reverse order with $s-1$ colors. This gives a proper vertex coloring of the red graph. One color class has at least $N/(s-1)=2m$ vertices, all mutual edges blue. It contains a blue copy of $H$. Thus $r(F,H)\leq2(s-1)m$. A one-vertex forest is trivial, so every forest has the required property.

3) Graphs of arbitrary girth and average degree at least four

Fix an integer $g\geq3$. In the random graph on $n$ vertices with independent edge probability $p=n^{-1+1/(2g)}$, let $X$ be the number of edges and $Y$ the number of cycles of length less than $g$. Then \[ \mathbb EX=\binom n2p,\qquad \mathbb EY\leq\sum_{j=3}^{g-1}\frac{n^jp^j}{2j} =O_g(n^{(g-1)/(2g)}). \] For sufficiently large $n$, $\mathbb E(X-Y)>2n$. Some realization therefore has $X-Y>2n$. Delete one edge from each original cycle of length less than $g$. At most $Y$ edges are deleted, and deletion creates no cycles. The resulting graph has girth at least $g$ and more than $2n$ edges, hence average degree greater than four.

4) Producing infinitely many minimal obstructions

For each $g$, choose such a graph $G_g$. Since $e(G_g)>2v(G_g)\geq2v(G_g)-2$, the external lower bound shows it is not Ramsey size-linear. Among its finitely many subgraphs choose one, $F_g$, minimal under proper subgraph containment with that failure. Every proper subgraph of $F_g$ is size-linear by minimality. Since forests are size-linear, $F_g$ contains a cycle. Every cycle in $F_g$ has length at least $g$.

If there were only finitely many possible isomorphism types of these minimal obstructions, choose one cycle in each and let $L$ be the maximum of their lengths. Taking $g>L$ contradicts that $F_g$ must be one of those types. Thus there are infinitely many. This proves existence; it does not identify the minimal subgraph inside any particular large-girth example.

Outcome: SOLVED, Wigderson's known resolution reconstructed with its Ramsey lower bound explicit. No novel proof or local formal verification is claimed.
