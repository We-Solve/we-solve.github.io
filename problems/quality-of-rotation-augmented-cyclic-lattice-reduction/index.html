Math-13118

Quality of rotation-augmented cyclic-lattice reduction

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A rank-$d$ Euclidean lattice is a discrete subgroup $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ generated over ${\mathbb{Z}}$ by a basis. A cyclic lattice is preserved by the intended cyclic coordinate rotation; a standard choice is \[ R(v_0,\ldots,v_{d-1})=(v_{d-1},v_0,\ldots,v_{d-2}), \qquad R(L)=L. \] For block-structured lattices the relevant rotation may act simultaneously on several blocks, rather than rotate all coordinates as one block.

One familiar cyclic NTRU lattice model uses coefficient vectors in ${\mathbb{Z}}[x]/(x^m-1)$ and \[ L_{h,q}=\{(u,v)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^m\times{\mathbb{Z}}^m: v\equiv h u\pmod q\}, \] where multiplication is in that quotient ring; simultaneous cyclic rotation preserves the congruence. This is an illustrative model, not a claim that the source fixes this modulus, quotient polynomial, dimension, or NTRU convention.

The problem asks how effective rotation-augmented lattice reduction is on cyclic or NTRU lattices. The source does not define the algorithm: it may generate additional rotated lattice vectors, reduce with them, and select a new basis, but its update rules, reduction primitive, parameters, and termination condition are absent. Rotated vectors are valid lattice vectors only for the chosen invariant rotation; their inclusion alone does not specify a basis-reduction procedure.

A quantitative analysis needs a quality measure and input regime. Examples of possible measures are the shortest-vector approximation ratio $\|b_1\|/\lambda_1(L)$ or the Hermite ratio $\|b_1\|/\det(L)^{1/d}$, together with running time, but none is prescribed. The task remains to analyze the specified rotation augmentation once recovered, not to assert a universal approximation guarantee for all algorithms on every structured lattice.

Short English statement

Determine whether exploiting cyclic rotations improves lattice-reduction quality on cyclic and NTRU lattices, and by how much for the intended algorithm and input class. The rotation action, augmentation procedure, and quality criterion are missing.

The supplied summary mentions scattered NTRU/ideal-lattice and cyclic-SVP analyses but gives no exact paper or definitive resolution of this 2006 slide question. Its OPEN-TRIAGE label is retained with current primary-literature audit still required.
