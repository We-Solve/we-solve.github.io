Finite-Time Invariant Manifolds and Unsteady Transport

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a research programme rather than a fully specified conjecture. Its subject is finite-time invariant manifolds in aperiodic, unsteady flows and the transport associated with stable and unstable manifold segments.

For notation only, let $I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ denote a time interval and let $M$ denote an unspecified state space. Write $\Phi_{t,s}$ for evolution from time $s$ to time $t$, wherever that evolution is defined. The usual evolution identities are \[ \Phi_{s,s}=\operatorname{id},\qquad \Phi_{t,r}\circ\Phi_{r,s}=\Phi_{t,s}, \] wherever the compositions exist. A family of sets $W(t)\subseteq M$ is transported by this evolution if \[ \Phi_{t,s}\left(W(s)\right)=W(t) \] for the relevant times and domains. This identity supplies notation for material evolution; it does not by itself define finite-time stability, instability, or a transport barrier. Neither $M$, the admissible evolutions, nor the observation interval is specified by the supplied record.

Source-qualified research question. Develop a theory of finite-time invariant manifolds that captures the time dependence of aperiodic unsteady flows. For stable and unstable manifold segments, denoted provisionally by $W^s(t)$ and $W^u(t)$, quantify transport associated with their intersections or their absence, and relate these structures to practical diagnostics of flow barriers. In particular, address the source fragment's question: does the proposed description capture time variation, and how do intersections or their absence produce transport?

The superscripts $s$ and $u$ are labels for the structures requested in the record, not definitions of finite-time stability. The supplied fragment does not identify the proposed description. It also does not specify stability criteria, endpoint conditions, a transported quantity or measure, regions between which transport is measured, or a criterion for agreement with barrier diagnostics. Consequently, it does not determine a precise universally or existentially quantified theorem. These choices remain unresolved parts of the formulation; no particular choice is asserted here.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a finite-time theory that describes changing structures in aperiodic unsteady flows, quantifies transport associated with intersecting or nonintersecting stable and unstable manifold segments, and connects those structures to practical flow-barrier diagnostics. The supplied fragment leaves essential definitions and hypotheses unspecified, so this is a source-qualified research programme requiring mathematical review.
