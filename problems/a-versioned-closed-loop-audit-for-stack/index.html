Attempt: a delayed instructional-response loop

To test the inference that a stronger corrective response always reinforces a process, consider a declared linear controller for an error variable $e_t$. Immediate correction follows $e_{t+1}=(1-k)e_t$. A one-step delay instead follows $e_{t+1}=e_t-ke_{t-1}$. This is an illustrative control model, not an empirical law for learners or a description of STACK.
Proposition: For $k>0$, every immediate-correction trajectory tends to zero exactly when $k<2$. Every delayed-correction trajectory tends to zero exactly when $k<1$. Hence a correction gain can stabilize the immediate loop and destabilize the delayed loop.
Proof: The immediate solution is $e_t=(1-k)^te_0$, so convergence for every initial value is equivalent to $|1-k|<1$. For the delayed recurrence the characteristic polynomial is $r^2-r+k$. When $0<k<1/4$, its roots $(1\pm\sqrt{1-4k})/2$ are both strictly between zero and one, so every linear combination of their powers tends to zero. At $k=1/4$, the repeated root is $1/2$ and solutions are $(A+Bt)2^{-t}$, also converging to zero. For $k>1/4$, the conjugate roots have modulus $\sqrt{k}$ because their product is $k$. Thus all solutions converge when $k<1$, whereas at $k=1$ nonzero periodic solutions exist and for $k>1$ some real solutions have unbounded subsequences. To see the last assertion, write a nonzero solution as $k^{t/2}\cos(t\theta+\phi)$; the pair of consecutive cosine factors cannot both tend to zero when $\sin\theta\ne0$. This proves the stated all-trajectory criterion. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At $k=3/2$, the immediate multiplier is $-1/2$, but delayed roots have modulus $\sqrt{3/2}>1$. Iteration from $e_0=e_1=1$ checks the divergent oscillatory behavior over a finite horizon; the root argument supplies the unrestricted proof. The failed inference is retaining an immediate-response stability rule after adding a delay. Whether an educational deployment has any such state, gain or delay relation requires evidence. No instructional effect or optimal feedback gain is inferred from this controller example.
