Computability and Complexity Characterizations of Poisson Genericity

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not define Poisson genericity or specify the space, representation, or probability measure for its reals. Consequently, the following is a schematic formulation whose missing data require mathematical review.

Let $X$ denote the intended space of represented reals, and let $P\subseteq X$ denote the intended class of Poisson-generic reals. Here $P$ is a placeholder for the missing definition. Once an effective representation of $X$ is fixed, write $\operatorname{Comp}(X)$ for its computable elements. Once the probability measure and effective test conventions are fixed, write $\operatorname{MLR}(X)$ for the corresponding Martin--Löf random elements.

The source asks the following four questions:

  1. Does a computable Poisson-generic real exist; equivalently, is \[ P\cap\operatorname{Comp}(X)\ne\varnothing? \]
  2. Is every Martin--Löf random real Poisson generic; equivalently, does \[ \operatorname{MLR}(X)\subseteq P \] hold?
  3. Find a necessary and sufficient characterization of membership in $P$ in terms of Kolmogorov complexity. The source does not specify the complexity variant, the representation of finite data extracted from a real, or the permissible form of the characterization.
  4. Is $P$ $\Pi^0_3$-complete? The source does not specify whether this denotes an effective arithmetical classification or a Borel classification, nor the reductions under which completeness is intended.

The definition of Poisson genericity, its parameters and quantifiers, and the conventions above must be supplied before these questions constitute a fully specified mathematical statement. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved: it reports affirmative results for the first two questions, settlement of the completeness question without stating its answer, and an unresolved complexity-characterization question in the requested form. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether Poisson genericity admits a computable example, contains every Martin--Löf random real, has a Kolmogorov-complexity characterization, and defines a $\Pi^0_3$-complete class. The supplied record omits the definition and conventions needed for a precise formulation. Its dated curation reports partial resolution, with the requested complexity characterization still unresolved in this form.
