Eigenfunction Averages Along Simple Closed Geodesics

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be the compact hyperbolic surface in the supplied record. Write $\phi_n$ for its Laplace eigenfunctions and $\lambda_n$ for the corresponding eigenvalues. With the nonnegative sign convention for the Laplacian, the eigenvalue equation is $\Delta_S\phi_n=\lambda_n\phi_n$; the source does not explicitly specify this sign convention, an eigenfunction normalization, or boundary conditions if $S$ has boundary.

For a simple closed geodesic $C\subset S$, let $\ell(C)$ denote its hyperbolic length and let $\mathrm{d}s$ denote hyperbolic arclength. Put \[ \mathcal G_S(T)=\{C\subset S:C\text{ is a simple closed geodesic and }\ell(C)\leq T\},\qquad N_S(T)=\#\mathcal G_S(T). \] Here the set notation treats geodesics as geometric curves counted once; the supplied record does not explicitly state its counting convention. Whenever $0<N_S(T)<\infty$, define the signed average of geodesic periods by \[ A_n(T)=\frac{1}{N_S(T)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal G_S(T)}\int_C\phi_n\,\mathrm{d}s. \] The integrals are not divided by $\ell(C)$, and no absolute values are placed inside the sum.

The supplied question asks whether there exist constants $\alpha,\beta>0$ such that \[ A_n(T)=O\!\left(\lambda_n^{-\alpha}T^{-\beta}\right). \] For positive eigenvalues and positive length cutoffs with nonzero denominator, an estimate of this form would mean \[ \left|\sum_{C\in\mathcal G_S(T)}\int_C\phi_n\,\mathrm{d}s\right| \leq K\,N_S(T)\lambda_n^{-\alpha}T^{-\beta} \] with $K>0$ independent of the variables over which the estimate is asserted to be uniform. The source does not specify that uniformity, the joint asymptotic regime in $n$ and $T$, or any relation between these variables. It also does not specify whether the exponents and implicit constant may depend on $S$, the eigenbasis, or the individual eigenfunction. The expression with $\lambda_n^{-\alpha}$ requires a restriction to positive eigenvalues or a separately stated treatment of zero eigenvalues, which is absent. These qualifications remain unresolved; the displayed target is therefore a statement-only transcription requiring clarification, not a fully specified conjecture.

Short English statement

The supplied problem asks whether averaging the signed integrals of a Laplace eigenfunction over simple closed geodesics of length at most $T$ gives simultaneous power decay in its eigenvalue and in $T$. Eigenfunction normalization, the treatment of zero eigenvalues, surface boundary assumptions, and the intended uniformity and limiting regime require clarification.
