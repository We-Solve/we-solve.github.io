A logarithmic-volume identity and zero-angular-momentum obstruction

Shared notation.

Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For four spatial positions, tetrahedron volume is $V(t)=\tfrac16|\det(q_2-q_1,q_3-q_1,q_4-q_1)|$. Remaining bounded away from zero and infinity means that positive finite constants $v_-,v_+$ bound $V(t)$ throughout the stated time interval.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
What restrictions govern the volume of the tetrahedron in the four-body problem? Can an orbit exist with $0<v_-\le V(t)\le v_+<\infty$ forever?

An exact logarithmic-volume identity.
For a nondegenerate spatial four-body configuration set $X=(q_2-q_1,q_3-q_1,q_4-q_1)$, an invertible $3\times3$ matrix. On a collision-free interval with nonzero determinant, $V=|\det X|/6$. Differentiating the determinant gives \[\frac{d}{dt}\log V=\operatorname{tr}(X^{-1}\dot X),\qquad \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\log V=\operatorname{tr}(X^{-1}\ddot X)-\operatorname{tr}[(X^{-1}\dot X)^2]. \] The second trace need not be nonnegative: for a skew-symmetric matrix $A$, $\operatorname{tr}(A^2)=-\|A\|_F^2$. Thus a concavity argument based on dropping this term is invalid without further dynamical restrictions.
A rigid-tetrahedron obstruction.
A three-dimensional regular tetrahedron is a central configuration, but it cannot simply rotate rigidly in $\mathbb R^3$ as a relative equilibrium. More generally, if a full-span central configuration $a_i$ satisfies $\ddot q_i=-\lambda q_i$, $\lambda>0$, and $q_i=e^{t\Omega}a_i$ with constant skew $\Omega$, then spanning forces $\Omega^2=-\lambda I_3$. Every real three-dimensional skew matrix has a nonzero kernel, so this is impossible. A four-dimensional double rotation is not an admissible spatial tetrahedron example.
Gap.
The identity does not rule out nonrigid solutions with volume bounded above and below forever, since shape distortion and velocity terms can compensate. No zero-angular-momentum assumption is inserted into the source's unrestricted question. The first missing step is a global estimate connecting $X^{-1}\ddot X$ and its deformation rate strongly enough to force collapse/escape or construct a bounded positive-volume orbit. Both time directions and collision-free lifetime still need specification in such a theorem.
