Complex rank-one spherical integrals and a two-atom zero barrier

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 22, including its first sentence. Find a combinatorial interpretation of free cumulants through enumeration and operations on maps. The same question block then asks about rank-one spherical integrals. Let $E_n$ be self-adjoint $n\times n$ matrices with uniformly bounded spectra and empirical laws tending weakly to $\mu_E$. With unnormalized matrix trace and Haar probability measure on $\mathcal U(n)$, put \[ D_n=\operatorname{diag}(z,0,\ldots,0),\qquad I_n(z)=\int e^{n\operatorname{Tr}(UD_nU^*E_n)}\,\mathrm dm_n(U), \qquad f_n(z)=\frac{I_n'(z)}{nI_n(z)}. \] The logarithmic derivative is meromorphic and equals $\partial_z(n^{-1}\log I_n)$ on zero-free neighborhoods, without requiring a global logarithm. The source attributes convergence near zero to $R_{\mu_E}$ to Guionnet and Maïda. Here the $R$-transform is the germ $G_{\mu_E}^{-1}(z)-1/z$, using the Cauchy-transform inverse near infinity/zero. Determine the largest complex convergence domain, ask whether the whole upper half-plane works when $\mu_E$ is freely infinitely divisible, and ask for noncompact-support extensions. The source suggests normal-family methods. The trace normalization, convergence topology, zero avoidance and analytic branch must be fixed; weak empirical convergence alone does not formulate all these choices.

Reported balanced two-atom family. Restrict to even $n$, with half the eigenvalues equal to $a$, half to $b$, $a>b$. Set $c=(a+b)/2$, $d=(a-b)/2$, $\nu=(n-1)/2$. The aid reports \[ I_n(z)=e^{ncz}\Gamma(\nu+1) \left(\frac2{ndz}\right)^\nu I_\nu(ndz),\qquad f_n(z)=c+d\frac{I_{\nu+1}(ndz)}{I_\nu(ndz)}. \] Here $I_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function, and the normalized first expression is interpreted by its entire power series at zero; $f_n(0)=c$ is removable. If $j_{\nu,1}$ is the first positive zero of the ordinary Bessel function $J_\nu$, the nearest poles occur at \[ z=\pm\frac{\mathrm i j_{\nu,1}}{nd} \longrightarrow\pm\frac{\mathrm i}{a-b}. \] The limiting transform is \[ R(z)=c+\frac{\sqrt{1+4d^2z^2}-1}{2z}, \] with square root equal to one at zero and removable value there. The aid reports locally uniform holomorphic convergence on the natural cut plane \[ {\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{\mathrm i y:|y|\geq1/(a-b)\}, \] and exact maximal centered convergence-disk radius $1/(a-b)$. This is a sharp family-specific obstruction; no assertion that the two-atom law is freely infinitely divisible, or that it settles the infinitely-divisible upper-half-plane question, is made. Nor is a particular cut plane identified as the uniquely largest possible analytic-continuation domain.

Reported sufficient normal-family criterion. Write $I_n(z)=\mathbb E e^{nzY_n}$ for the rank-one spectral average, with $|Y_n|\leq K$ under the uniform spectral bound. A precise sufficient phase-noncancellation condition on a connected domain $D$ is: for each compact $C\subset D$, there is $\eta_C>0$, independent of $n$, such that \[ |I_n(z)|\geq\eta_C\mathbb E e^{n\operatorname{Re}(z)Y_n} \quad(z\in C). \] Then $I_n$ is zero free there and $|f_n(z)|\leq K/\eta_C$. If $D$ contains the established small-zero germ, local boundedness gives normal-family continuation to the corresponding analytic limit. This is a conditional test; no verification on a general domain or substitute tail condition for noncompact spectra is supplied. It does not address the combinatorial map question.

Appended separate section fragment. The extraction continues into section 0.9, "Focus Group on Invariant Subspaces (day 4)". For commuting $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ in a $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor, it proposes, with an explicit question mark, a joint Brown-measure identity involving \[ \log\Delta\left(1-\sum_i\alpha_iT_i\right) \quad\text{and}\quad \int\log\left(1-\sum_i\alpha_i\zeta_i\right)\,\mathrm d\mu(\zeta). \] Here $\Delta(A)=\exp\tau(\log|A|)$ is the Fuglede--Kadison determinant, possibly zero. The printed complex logarithm without absolute values is not directly a real logarithmic-potential identity; a candidate repair would use $\log|1-\sum_i\alpha_i\zeta_i|$, subject to integrability and extended-value conventions. The fragment supplies no complete existence/uniqueness hypotheses or requested theorem. Commutativity alone is not silently strengthened to joint normality, and this incomplete neighboring discussion is not treated as part of the spherical-integral result.

Short English statement

The source combines a cumulant/map question with complex spherical-integral questions and an appended Brown-measure fragment. The aid solves one balanced two-atom family, where Bessel poles approach exact $R$-transform branch points, and gives a conditional noncancellation test for normal-family continuation. The other questions remain separately qualified.
