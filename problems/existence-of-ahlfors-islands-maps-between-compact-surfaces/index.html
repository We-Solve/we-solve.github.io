Existence of Ahlfors Islands Maps Between Compact Surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ and $Y$ be compact Riemann surfaces, with $X$ hyperbolic, meaning that its universal covering surface is conformally equivalent to $\mathbb D=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$. Following the original statement, let $W\subseteq Y$ be a domain, that is, a nonempty connected open subset.

The supplied record does not define an Ahlfors islands map. Retain this source terminology by writing \[ \mathcal I(W,X)=\{f:W\to X : f\text{ is an Ahlfors islands map in the source's intended sense}\}. \] This notation does not supply the missing definition: the precise islands condition, its boundary quantifiers, and any exceptional or excluded maps must be checked in the cited source before this becomes a complete formal statement.

The problem is to characterize the triples $(Y,W,X)$ satisfying the preceding assumptions for which \[ \mathcal I(W,X)\ne\varnothing. \] The target-dependence question asks whether, for each fixed admissible pair $(Y,W)$, this existence property is independent of the compact hyperbolic target. Explicitly, determine whether \[ \mathcal I(W,X_1)\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal I(W,X_2)\ne\varnothing \] holds for every pair of compact hyperbolic Riemann surfaces $X_1,X_2$, and characterize the dependence if it does not.

Unresolved scope qualification. The record does not explicitly say whether $W$ must be proper. The notation above records the domain assumption without resolving whether $W=Y$ is admissible; that boundary case and the missing definition require source review.

Short English statement

Characterize when a domain in a compact Riemann surface admits an Ahlfors islands map to a compact hyperbolic Riemann surface, and determine whether existence changes with the target. The supplied record leaves the exact islands-map definition and the admissibility of the whole-surface domain unresolved.
