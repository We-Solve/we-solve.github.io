Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.81

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a topological group with identity $e$, and let $\mathcal N_G(e)$ denote its neighborhoods of $e$. For subsets $A,B\subseteq G$, put $AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}$. The group $G$ is called $F$-balanced if \[ \forall X\subseteq G\;\forall U\in\mathcal N_G(e)\;\exists V\in\mathcal N_G(e),\qquad VX\subseteq XU. \] Here $V$ may depend on both $X$ and $U$. A subset $W\subseteq G$ is invariant if $gWg^{-1}=W$ for every $g\in G$. The group $G$ is called balanced if its identity has a neighborhood basis consisting of invariant sets; equivalently, \[ \forall U\in\mathcal N_G(e)\;\exists W\in\mathcal N_G(e),\qquad W\subseteq U\quad\text{and}\quad \forall g\in G,\ gWg^{-1}=W. \] Question (I. V. Protasov, 1990; Kourovka Notebook 11.81). Is every $F$-balanced topological group balanced?

The supplied formulation does not specify a separation axiom for topological groups. Whether Hausdorffness is an implicit source convention remains unresolved.

Short English statement

If every subset $X$ can absorb sufficiently small left multiplication into right multiplication by any prescribed identity neighborhood, must the group have a basis of conjugation-invariant identity neighborhoods? The source does not explicitly specify whether topological groups must be Hausdorff.
