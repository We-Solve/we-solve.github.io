Irrationality of Seshadri Constants

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a standard single-point interpretation, fix an algebraically closed field $k$. Let $X$ be a smooth projective integral variety over $k$ with $\dim X\geq 1$, let $L$ be an ample line bundle on $X$, and let $x\in X(k)$. The Seshadri constant of $L$ at $x$ is \[ \varepsilon(L;x)=\inf_{\substack{C\subseteq X\text{ an integral curve}\\x\in C}}\frac{L\cdot C}{\operatorname{mult}_x(C)}, \] where $L\cdot C$ is the degree of the pullback of $L$ to the normalization of $C$, and $\operatorname{mult}_x(C)$ is the multiplicity of $C$ at $x$.

Under this interpretation, the question is whether there exist $X,L,x$ satisfying these hypotheses such that \[ \varepsilon(L;x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}. \] This is a conditional formulation: the supplied record does not specify the ground field, its characteristic, the dimension or admissible class of varieties, or whether single-point Seshadri constants are intended. The assumptions above describe a standard setting but are not explicitly supplied hypotheses. The record also does not distinguish single-point constants from multipoint or global variants, or specify the permitted positivity and coefficient class. Consequently, the precise intended scope cannot be uniquely recovered.

Short English statement

Can a Seshadri constant be irrational? In the standard single-point setting, this asks whether the infimum of the ratios of an ample line bundle's degree on a curve to that curve's multiplicity at a fixed point can be irrational. The source leaves the field, geometric setting, and variant of the constant unspecified.
