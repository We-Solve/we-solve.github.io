Fixed-Occurrence Permutation Pattern Asymptotics

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not identify the permutation pattern or specify the convention for an occurrence. Consequently, it does not determine a unique enumeration problem. The following definitions give a conditional interpretation using classical permutation patterns; this interpretation requires confirmation from the cited sources.

Let $k\geq 1$, and let $\tau\in S_k$ be a fixed permutation, where $S_k$ denotes the permutations of $\{1,\ldots,k\}$. Under the classical-pattern interpretation, an occurrence of $\tau$ in $\sigma\in S_n$ is a tuple $1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n$ such that \[ \sigma(i_a)<\sigma(i_b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau(a)<\tau(b) \qquad(1\leq a,b\leq k). \] Distinct index tuples are counted separately, including overlapping occurrences. For integers $n,r\geq0$, set \[ \psi_r(n)=\#\{\sigma\in S_n:\sigma\text{ has exactly }r\text{ occurrences of }\tau\}. \] Here $S_0$ contains the empty permutation. Interpreting the generating function as an ordinary generating function gives \[ \Psi_r(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}\psi_r(n)x^n. \] Both the classical-occurrence convention and the ordinary-generating-function convention are explicit provisional interpretations, not definitions recovered unambiguously from the supplied excerpt.

The recoverable asymptotic question is whether, for the intended fixed pattern and each fixed integer $r\geq0$, there exists a constant $C_r>0$, independent of $n$, such that \[ \psi_r(n)\sim C_r n^r\psi_0(n)\qquad(n\to\infty), \] meaning \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi_r(n)}{C_r n^r\psi_0(n)}=1. \] This ratio requires $\psi_0(n)>0$ for all sufficiently large $n$; the supplied record does not identify the intended pattern or give hypotheses ensuring this. No assertion for every permutation pattern, or uniformly for $r$ growing with $n$, can be extracted reliably from the record.

The source also conjectures an algebraic form for $\Psi_r(x)$. Its damaged display contains the expression \[ P_1(x)-P_2(x)\sqrt{1-4x}, \] with $P_1,P_2\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]$, and stipulates \[ \begin{aligned} \deg P_1=\deg P_2&=\frac{5r}{2} &&\text{if }r\text{ is even},\\ \deg P_1&=\frac{5r+1}{2},\qquad \deg P_2=\frac{5r-1}{2} &&\text{if }r\text{ is odd}. \end{aligned} \] The polynomials may depend on $r$ and the intended pattern. The prefactor or denominator involving $2$, $x$, and $2r+1$ is not preserved reliably enough to state the full equality. Thus the generating-function conjecture remains incompletely specified.

A further damaged display involves a polynomial $Q_{2r}(n)$ with integer coefficients and degree $2r$, factorials $(2n-r)!$, $(n+2r+1)!$, and $(n-r)!$, and asymptotic factors involving $C_r$, $4^n$, $n$, and $\pi$. Their arrangement cannot be recovered reliably from the supplied text; no exact coefficient formula or additional asymptotic formula is asserted here.

The excerpt further reports that the amplitudes appear computationally to grow exponentially, seemingly like $\lambda^r$ with $\lambda\approx2.67$, but says this increase cannot continue indefinitely because the histogram of $\psi_r(n)$ against $r$ is asymptotically normal and its heights must eventually decrease. These are supplied heuristic qualifications: no limiting regime, normalization, or precise normal-limit statement is given. The additional assertion that only the amplitude changes with $r$ is not reconciled in the record with the displayed factor $n^r$. These remarks do not provide a precise further conjecture without source clarification.

Short English statement

For an unidentified fixed permutation pattern, the record asks whether the number of permutations with exactly $r$ occurrences is asymptotic to $C_r n^r$ times the number avoiding the pattern, for fixed $r$ as $n\to\infty$. It also proposes an algebraic generating function. The intended pattern and occurrence convention are missing, and two displayed formulas are damaged, so the formulation above is conditional and requires mathematical review.
