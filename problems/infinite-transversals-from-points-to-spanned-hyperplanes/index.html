Math-3473

Infinite Transversals from Points to Spanned Hyperplanes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\geq1$ be an integer and let $P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ satisfy $\operatorname{aff}(P)={\mathbb{R}}^d$. No finiteness or countability assumption is imposed. A hyperplane spanned by $P$ means an affine hyperplane generated by $d$ affinely independent points of $P$; explicitly, put \[ H(P)=\{\operatorname{aff}(F):F\subseteq P,\ |F|=d,\ \dim\operatorname{aff}(F)=d-1\}. \] Hyperplanes are geometric sets, so different generating subsets defining the same hyperplane yield just one element of $H(P)$.

The conjecture asks whether \[ \forall d\geq1\ \forall P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\quad \operatorname{aff}(P)={\mathbb{R}}^d \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists f:P\hookrightarrow H(P)\quad\forall p\in P,\ p\in f(p). \] Thus different points must be assigned different incident spanned hyperplanes, with no requirement that every hyperplane be used. Equivalently, in the bipartite incidence graph with parts $P$ and $H(P)$, is there a matching saturating $P$? The hyperplanes are affine, not necessarily linear through the origin, and not all incident hyperplanes are allowed unless they are spanned by $P$.

The original short extraction omitted the full-affine-span hypothesis; the cleaned source-verified statement restores it, and the displayed conjecture retains it as substantive. The supplied literature triage checked 2026-08-21 treats the unrestricted infinite assertion as conjectural, noting that the cited finite Hall--matroid result does not prove it. Finite-case matching arguments are not silently applied to arbitrary infinite neighborhoods or uncountable point sets.

Short English statement

Can every affinely spanning point set in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, including arbitrary infinite sets, assign each point a different hyperplane spanned by the set and containing that point? This is an incidence matching that must cover all points.
