Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied statement of the Ehrenpreis Conjecture says that two closed Riemann surfaces have finite unbranched covers with homeomorphic total spaces arbitrarily close in the Teichmüller metric. The usual hyperbolic formulation requires both base genera to be at least $2$, a hypothesis missing from this excerpt.

With that qualification, let $X_1,X_2$ be closed Riemann surfaces of genera $g_1,g_2\geq2$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, the assertion is that there exist connected finite unbranched holomorphic covers $p_i:Y_i\to X_i$ and an orientation-preserving quasiconformal homeomorphism $h:Y_1\to Y_2$ such that \[ \tfrac12\log K(h)<\varepsilon, \] where $K(h)$ is maximal dilatation. Equivalently, after using an appropriate marking to identify their underlying surfaces, the total-space structures have Teichmüller distance less than $\varepsilon$. Covering degrees and common genus may depend on $\varepsilon$. If the degrees are $d_1,d_2$, homeomorphism requires $d_1(g_1-1)=d_2(g_2-1)$; equal covering degrees are not required. The covers need not be conformally identical or be one common holomorphic cover.

The literal unrestricted phrase "two closed Riemann surfaces" includes incompatible genus types, for example sphere and positive-genus surfaces, that cannot have homeomorphic finite unbranched covers. Thus the hyperbolic hypothesis must be checked rather than silently omitted. The registry marks the conjecture solved in the literature by Kahn--Markovic; this is supplied status information distinct from the older source's problem label.

Short English statement

For any two closed hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, finite unbranched covers can be made arbitrarily close after choosing a homeomorphism of their total spaces. The missing hyperbolic-genus hypothesis and supplied solution citation require review.
