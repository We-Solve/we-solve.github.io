Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks: What are caustics in the oscillatory-integral setting, and how can their geometry and order be determined? It describes caustics as envelopes of optical rays or as degeneracies of stationary points of a phase, associated with failure of uniform $L^\infty$ bounds. This is a research question whose mathematical setting is not specified sufficiently to give a unique quantified statement.

To explain the phase terminology only, let $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ and $\Theta\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^m$ be open sets, with $d,m\geq1$, and let $\phi\in C^2(X\times\Theta;{\mathbb{R}})$. These are auxiliary conventions, not hypotheses supplied for the intended problem. A stationary point in the integration variables is a pair $(x,\theta)$ satisfying \[ \nabla_\theta\phi(x,\theta)=0. \] It is degenerate when \[ \det D^2_{\theta\theta}\phi(x,\theta)=0. \] The corresponding set of parameter values is \[ \mathcal K_\phi=\left\{x\in X:\ \exists\theta\in\Theta,\ \nabla_\theta\phi(x,\theta)=0,\ \det D^2_{\theta\theta}\phi(x,\theta)=0\right\}. \] This set expresses a possible local phase description of a caustic. Identifying it with an envelope of optical rays requires additional geometric hypotheses and a specified ray family, neither of which is supplied.

Statement of the supplied research target. Determine the geometry and order of caustics in the intended oscillatory-integral or optical-ray setting, and characterize the associated failure of the intended uniform $L^\infty$ estimates. The source does not specify the family of integrals, phase class, amplitude, parameter regime, ray dynamics, domains, boundary conditions, or meaning of "order." It also does not identify which bound fails or what its uniformity means. Degeneracy alone does not imply failure of every uniform $L^\infty$ bound; the relevant normalization, amplitude, and limiting regime must be specified. Thus the displayed definitions clarify terminology but do not complete the missing hypotheses or establish a classification theorem.

Short English statement

The question asks for the geometry and order of caustics, described through optical-ray envelopes or degenerate stationary points of oscillatory phases.
