Math-1256

Elliptic surfaces, orthogonal monodromy, and BKLPR

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Jordan Ellenberg asks which algebro-geometric properties of elliptic surfaces explain the Poonen--Rains and BKLPR distribution models. In a function-field formulation, an elliptic surface over a curve over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$ has generic fiber an elliptic curve over that curve's function field. A proposed explanation should specify the family, height ordering, Selmer level, geometric realization, monodromy and limiting distribution. None of these choices or the precise model law is given in the original.

The attached unreviewed aid reports substantial function-field progress: finite étale Selmer spaces, a quadratic intersection form on primitive elliptic-surface cohomology, large orthogonal monodromy and Frobenius equidistribution produce random orthogonal fixed-space kernels. It attributes a stable identification with the finite-level BKLPR law to Feng--Landesman--Rains, in a limit taking large $q$ first and then height. The field/characteristic restrictions, family and exact two-stage limit are not reproduced; this does not assert an arbitrary joint limit or a number-field theorem.

Its separate linear-algebra bridge fixes an odd prime $\ell$ and a nondegenerate finite-dimensional quadratic space $(V,Q)$ over ${\mathbb{F}}_\ell$. For every $g\in O(V,Q)$, set \[ \Delta=\{(v,v):v\in V\},\qquad\Gamma_g=\{(v,gv):v\in V\}. \] The aid claims that these are maximal isotropic subspaces of $(V\oplus V,Q\oplus(-Q))$, that $\Delta\cap\Gamma_g\simeq\ker(g-1)$, and that \[ (-1)^{\dim\ker(g-1)}=(-1)^{\dim V}\det(g). \] The determinant is identified with its sign $\pm1$; no semisimplicity assumption on $g$ is imposed. If $V$ contains a hyperbolic plane, the aid additionally claims \[ \frac1{|O(V,Q)|}\sum_{g\in O(V,Q)}|\ker(g-1)|=\ell+1. \] A hyperbolic plane is a two-dimensional nondegenerate quadratic subspace with isotropic lines. Uniform sampling here is from the full orthogonal group, including both determinant components; applying the mean to an arithmetic subgroup or Frobenius coset needs a separate comparison. These finite-space statements illustrate an intersection-of-maximal-isotropics mechanism, but do not themselves prove the original geometric distribution assertion.

Short English statement

Explain random Selmer models through elliptic-surface geometry, cohomological quadratic forms, monodromy and Frobenius statistics. The aid supplies a finite-orthogonal-space bridge while retaining the specific function-field limit and group-sampling qualifications.
