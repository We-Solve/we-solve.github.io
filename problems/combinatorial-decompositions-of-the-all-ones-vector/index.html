Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can the all-ones vector be represented as a linear or integer combination of combinatorially interesting vectors, such as incidence vectors of tours?

Source scope retained

Sebo's report, printed pages 2912--2913, opens with the broad question retained in the catalogue and then considers several distinct families. It defines a tour by an edge-multiplicity vector in $\{0,1,2\}^E$ with even degree at each vertex and connected spanning support. The catalogue's incidence-vector terminology alone would obscure multiplicity two. We use this precise tour convention for the following component of the broad question, without replacing all its unspecified vector families by tours.

The report states that in a 3-edge-connected graph the all-ones vector lies in the convex hull of tours and asks whether that uniform bound can improve. Its spanning-tree and parity-correction argument invokes polyhedral results not reproved here. Instead we attempt to strengthen the coefficients from real to integer, and test exact uniform covers in the smallest complete example.

An integer-coefficient obstruction with a complete classification

Let $G$ be a finite loopless connected graph with at least one edge. If $\mathbf1_E=\sum_t a_t T_t$ is any finite integer combination of tours, with coefficients of either sign, then for each vertex $v$, \[ \deg_G(v)=\sum_{e\ni v}1=\sum_t a_t\sum_{e\ni v}(T_t)_e \] is even. Thus every vertex degree must be even. Conversely, if every vertex of connected $G$ has even degree, $\mathbf1_E$ itself is a tour under the source definition: its entries are one, every star has even sum, and its support is connected and spanning. Hence, for this specified tour family, \[ \mathbf1_E\text{ is an integer combination of tours} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\text{ has only even degrees}. \] Allowing negative integer coefficients does not remove the parity obstruction. This is an elementary classification within the tour convention, not a resolution of the source's broader unspecified combination problem.

For a convex combination of tours a different necessary condition holds. Every nonempty proper vertex cut is crossed an even number of times by a tour, since the sum of degrees on one side is twice its internal multiplicity plus its crossing multiplicity. Connected spanning support makes that even number positive, hence at least two. Therefore \[ \alpha\mathbf1_E\in\operatorname{conv}\{\text{tours}\} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha|\delta(S)|\ge2 \] for every such cut. In particular a bridge prevents $\mathbf1_E$ from being a convex combination. This is only a necessary cut test in general.

K4 separates convex and integer representations

In $K_4$ there are three undirected Hamilton cycles, obtained by omitting one of the three perfect matchings. Every edge lies in exactly two cycles: it belongs to one perfect matching and is omitted only for that matching. A Hamilton cycle is a tour, so their uniform average is $(2/3)\mathbf1_E$. Each vertex cut has three edges, and the preceding cut bound forces $\alpha\ge2/3$. Thus the smallest uniform scalar for $K_4$ is exactly $2/3$.

The vector $\mathbf1_E$ also has a direct convex representation avoiding any claim that upward monotonicity holds automatically. For each Hamilton cycle $C$, and each of the two edges $e$ in its omitted matching, form $T_{C,e}=\mathbf1_C+2\mathbf1_{\{e\}}$. This has entries in $\{0,1,2\}$, is connected and spanning because $C$ is, and has even degrees because a doubled edge changes both endpoint degrees by two. There are six such tours. Fix an edge $a$. It belongs to two Hamilton cycles, each giving two tours, so its ordinary-cycle contribution across the six is four. It is the doubled edge in exactly one of the two tours from the remaining cycle, contributing two more. Its average multiplicity is therefore $(4+2)/6=1$. The uniform average of the six tours is exactly $\mathbf1_E$.

Nevertheless no integer combination of tours equals $\mathbf1_E$ in $K_4$, since each vertex has degree three. Thus even on a 3-edge-connected graph, convex representability cannot be upgraded to integral coefficients by clearing denominators and then cancelling: clearing denominators changes the target to a multiple of $\mathbf1_E$. This is the exact failed inference in the attempted strengthening.
