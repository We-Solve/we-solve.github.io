Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Let $\mathcal P^d$ denote convex $d$-dimensional polytopes and $f(P)=(f_0(P),\ldots,f_{d-1}(P))$, where $f_j$ counts $j$-dimensional faces. Let $\sigma^d$ be a $d$-simplex and $a=f(\sigma^d)$. The cone with apex $a$ generated by face vectors is $a+\operatorname{cone}\{f(P)-a:P\in\mathcal P^d\}$. A hyperplane through $a$ supports this cone when the cone lies in one of its closed halfspaces; an interior ray is a ray from $a$ lying in its relative interior.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For fixed $d$, can membership in $f(\mathcal P^d)$ be decided in time polynomial in the binary encoding length of the input vector? Is it decidable whether a hyperplane through $f(\sigma^d)$ supports $\operatorname{Cone}_{f(\sigma^d)}(f(\mathcal P^d))$ or contains an interior ray? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The input consists of an integer vector or finitely encoded hyperplane coefficients. The supplied question does not select a computational model for arbitrary real hyperplanes; decidability requires an effective input representation.

Decide which integer vectors are polytope face counts and recognize supporting hyperplanes of the cone they generate.

A complete known boundary case in dimension three

The primary cone question explicitly fixes $d\ge4$. The following dimension-three calculation is a controlled attempt to identify which part of the argument would have to generalize; it is outside that hard range. It is classical low-dimensional geometry, not new completion of the source target. Write $(v,e,f)$ for numbers of vertices, edges and facets.
Proposition: An integer triple is the face vector of a convex three-polytope if and only if \[ v\ge4,\quad f\ge4,\quad e=v+f-2,\quad v\le2f-4,\quad f\le2v-4. \] These conditions can be checked in time polynomial in the binary input length.
Proof: The boundary graph gives Euler's relation $v-e+f=2$: project the boundary minus a facet to a plane, delete edges on cycles one at a time to merge two faces, and then delete pendant tree edges and their leaf vertices until one vertex and the outside face remain. Each operation preserves $v-e+f$, whose final value is two. Every vertex has at least three incident edges, so $2e\ge3v$; every facet has at least three edges and each edge has two incident facets, so $2e\ge3f$. Combining these with Euler gives the last two inequalities. A full-dimensional three-polytope requires at least four vertices and facets. For sufficiency let $A=2f-v-4$ and $B=2v-f-4$. They are nonnegative integers and $A-B=3(f-v)$, so their common residue modulo three is some $c\in\{0,1,2\}$. Put $a=(A-c)/3$ and $b=(B-c)/3$, which are nonnegative integers. Start with a pyramid over a $(3+c)$-gon; it has $v_0=f_0=4+c$, triangular side facets, and simple base vertices of degree three. Stacking a vertex just beyond a triangular facet increases $(v,e,f)$ by $(1,3,2)$ and produces triangular facets and a new simple vertex. Truncating a simple vertex by a sufficiently close plane increases the vector by $(2,3,1)$ and produces a triangular facet and three simple vertices. These operations preserve convexity: choose the stacking point close enough to the relative interior of the facet to remain beneath all other facet planes, or choose the cutting plane close enough to the vertex to meet only its three incident edges. Each operation leaves both resources needed for further operations. Perform $a$ stackings and $b$ truncations. The resulting counts are \[ v=4+c+a+2b,\qquad f=4+c+2a+b, \] which agree with the desired integers by substitution; Euler fixes $e$. Recognition uses a fixed number of integer comparisons and additions. This does not assert polynomial output time for an explicit polytope with a binary-encoded, potentially enormous number of vertices. ∎
The cone with apex $(4,6,4)$ is therefore generated by $r_s=(1,3,2)$ and $r_t=(2,3,1)$. Indeed every permitted vector difference satisfies Euler and the two inequalities, and is a nonnegative real combination of these rays; conversely each ray is realized by repeated corresponding operations. Given a hyperplane through the apex with nonzero rational normal $z$, set $\alpha=z\cdot r_s$ and $\beta=z\cdot r_t$. The cone lies in one closed half-space exactly when $\alpha\beta\ge0$. The hyperplane contains a relative-interior ray exactly when $\alpha\beta<0$ or $\alpha=\beta=0$, since such a ray uses both generators with strictly positive coefficients. When both vanish it contains the entire cone, so the two descriptions need not be mutually exclusive under the closed-half-space convention.

Verification and remaining gaps

The arithmetic construction was checked for all $4\le v,f\le100$ satisfying the inequalities, and rational normals in a finite grid were checked against the cone criterion. In dimension at least four there is no analogous complete two-inequality face-vector characterization supplied here; that is the first missing premise in attempting the same algorithm. The known three-dimensional case proves neither polynomial recognition nor cone decidability in the source's intended dimensions.
