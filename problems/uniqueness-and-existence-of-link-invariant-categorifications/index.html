Uniqueness and Existence of Link-Invariant Categorifications

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks two questions about categorifications of link invariants. It does not specify the admissible categorifications, their equivalences, or the conventions for the link invariants. Consequently, the following is a conditional formulation schema, rather than a fully specified conjecture.

Fix a class $\mathcal L$ of links modulo the intended link equivalence, a coefficient ring $R$, and a link invariant $I\colon\mathcal L\to R$. The orientation, framing, and normalization conventions are unresolved in the supplied record. Once a collection $\mathcal A$ of admissibility requirements and a decategorification procedure $\operatorname{Dec}$ have been specified, write \[ \mathfrak C_{\mathcal A}(I) =\{\mathcal H: \mathcal H\text{ satisfies }\mathcal A \text{ and }\operatorname{Dec}(\mathcal H)=I\}. \] Here $\mathcal H$ denotes an entire categorification theory for the chosen links, and the equality means equality of the resulting link invariants. Let $\simeq$ denote the intended equivalence of such theories, which also remains unspecified.

  1. For a given link invariant $I$, under what admissibility requirements and notion of equivalence, if any, does uniqueness hold in the sense that \[ \forall\mathcal H_1,\mathcal H_2\in\mathfrak C_{\mathcal A}(I), \qquad \mathcal H_1\simeq\mathcal H_2? \] This formula expresses uniqueness conditional on existence. The source does not say whether existence for the given invariant is assumed, nor whether the question concerns every link invariant or a particular intended family.
  2. Does the Kauffman polynomial admit a categorification? If $K$ denotes the Kauffman polynomial with the intended conventions, the corresponding existence question is \[ \mathfrak C_{\mathcal A}(K)\ne\varnothing. \] The polynomial variant, normalization, coefficient ring, grading conventions, decategorification procedure, and admissibility requirements needed to interpret this assertion are not specified.

The source wording that categorifications might "become unique" does not identify any additional hypotheses or operation under which uniqueness is intended. Neither the parameters above nor a relationship between the two questions can therefore be fixed from the supplied record alone. The supplied dated curation labels the problem open and distinguishes uniqueness under restricted tensor-product categorification axioms from the broader question; that label and assessment are not independently verified here.

Short English statement

The questions ask whether categorifications of a fixed link invariant are unique up to an appropriate equivalence, and whether the Kauffman polynomial has a categorification. A precise conjecture requires additional choices about admissible theories, equivalence, decategorification, and polynomial conventions. The supplied open-status assessment is dated curation, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.
