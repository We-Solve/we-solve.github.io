Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.112

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

All groups considered are finite; no solubility hypothesis is imposed. A formation is a nonempty class of finite groups closed under isomorphisms, homomorphic images, and finite subdirect products. A formation $\mathfrak F$ is hereditary if every subgroup of each member belongs to $\mathfrak F$. It is saturated if, for every finite group $X$, \[ X/\Phi(X)\in\mathfrak F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\in\mathfrak F, \] where $\Phi(X)$ is the intersection of the maximal subgroups of $X$, with $\Phi(1)=1$.

Write \[ \pi(X)=\{p:p\text{ is prime and }p\mid |X|\},\qquad \pi(\mathfrak F)=\bigcup_{X\in\mathfrak F}\pi(X). \] For $H\leq X$, its core in $X$ is $\operatorname{Core}_X(H)=\bigcap_{x\in X}x^{-1}Hx$. Under the conventional formation-theoretic interpretation used in this draft, $H$ is $\mathfrak F$-subnormal in $X$ if either $H=X$, or there is a chain \[ H=H_0<H_1<\cdots<H_n=X \] in which $H_{i-1}$ is maximal in $H_i$ and \[ H_i/\operatorname{Core}_{H_i}(H_{i-1})\in\mathfrak F \qquad(1\leq i\leq n). \] The supplied record does not define $\mathfrak F$-subnormality, so this convention requires confirmation against the cited source.

Let $\mathfrak F$ be a hereditary saturated formation. For $P\leq G$, let $N_G(P)=\{g\in G:g^{-1}Pg=P\}$, and let $\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$ denote the set of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. Define \[ \mathrm{w}^{*}\mathfrak F= \left\{G:\begin{array}{l} G\text{ is finite},\quad \pi(G)\subseteq\pi(\mathfrak F),\\ N_G(P)\text{ is }\mathfrak F\text{-subnormal in }G\\ \text{for every }p\in\pi(G)\text{ and every }P\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G) \end{array}\right\}. \] The source states that $\mathrm{w}^{*}\mathfrak F$ is a formation. The question is whether it must be saturated: for every hereditary saturated formation $\mathfrak F$ and every finite group $G$, must \[ G/\Phi(G)\in\mathrm{w}^{*}\mathfrak F \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\in\mathrm{w}^{*}\mathfrak F \] hold?

Short English statement

Given a hereditary saturated formation $\mathfrak F$, consider the finite groups whose prime divisors occur in $\mathfrak F$ and whose Sylow subgroup normalizers are $\mathfrak F$-subnormal. The source states that these groups form a formation and asks whether that formation is always saturated. The intended convention for $\mathfrak F$-subnormality requires source confirmation.
