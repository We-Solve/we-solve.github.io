Khintchine Theory for Symbolic Self-Similar Approximation

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$ be a finite nonempty family of contractions of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, with attractor $F$, so that \[ F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\phi_i(F). \] Here the usual interpretation of contraction is a map with Lipschitz constant strictly less than one, and the attractor is the nonempty compact invariant set. Put $\mathcal A=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ and $\mathcal A^+=\bigcup_{k\geq1}\mathcal A^k$. For a word $w=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$, write $|w|=k$ and \[ \phi_w=\phi_{s_1}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{s_k}. \] Fix $x\in F$. Interpret the approximating function as a radius function $\psi:\mathcal A^+\to[0,\infty)$; this codomain is a convention needed to make the ball radii explicit, since the source does not specify it. With the explicit convention that $B(z,r)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\|y-z\|<r\}$, define \[ \begin{aligned} W(x,\psi) &=\{y\in F:\|y-\phi_w(x)\|<\psi(w) \text{ for infinitely many distinct }w\in\mathcal A^+\}\\ &=F\cap\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k\geq m}\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal A^k}B(\phi_w(x),\psi(w)). \end{aligned} \] Words are counted as symbolic words, even if different words give the same map or approximation centre. The source does not specify open versus closed balls, positivity or decay of $\psi$, or any monotonicity condition; the conventions above require review.

Requested classification. For the given system and chosen $x\in F$, classify the functions $\psi$ for which $W(x,\psi)$ is large, and establish a Khintchine theory under the additional condition that $\dim_{\mathrm H}F$ equals the similarity dimension.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not define "large": it specifies neither a reference measure and a full-measure conclusion nor a Hausdorff-dimension or Hausdorff-measure criterion. It also gives no convergence/divergence criterion defining the requested Khintchine theory. Moreover, arbitrary contractions need not be similarities, so their similarity dimension is not defined by the supplied statement. If the intended maps are contracting similarities with ratios $r_i\in(0,1)$, the usual similarity dimension is the unique $s\geq0$ satisfying \[ \sum_{i=1}^{N}r_i^s=1, \] and the stated additional condition would be $\dim_{\mathrm H}F=s$. This is a conditional explanation of the terminology, not an additional hypothesis supplied by the record. No separation hypothesis, measure, restriction on $\psi$, or uniformity requirement in $x$ is specified. Consequently, the source determines the approximation set but does not determine a single precise largeness theorem to formulate.

Short English statement

Starting from a chosen point of a contraction attractor, apply every finite symbolic composition and place a ball of word-dependent radius around the resulting point. The problem asks which radius functions make infinitely many such approximations occur on a large subset of the attractor, especially when Hausdorff and similarity dimensions agree. The intended meaning of largeness, the measure and divergence criterion, and the similarity hypotheses remain unspecified.
