Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For every extreme point $x^*$ of the standard feedback-vertex-set relaxation, must some vertex $v$ satisfy $x^*(v)\geq\tfrac12$?

The source LP and the nontrivial-instance gap

The primary source uses the specific relaxation \[ \sum_{v\in S}(d_S(v)-1)x_v\ge|E(S)|-|S|+1 \quad\text{for }S\subseteq V\text{ with }E(S)\ne\varnothing, \qquad x_v\ge0, \] where $d_S(v)$ is degree in the induced subgraph. It is not merely the relaxation with one unit-cover constraint per cycle. As the preserved definition already warns, an unqualified assertion about every graph includes forests. We document that boundary issue but do not present it as resolving the intended iterative-rounding question on a nontrivial cyclic instance.
The literal forest boundary case.
If $G$ is a forest, every induced subgraph is a forest, so for nonempty $S$ one has $|E(S)|\le|S|-1$. This follows by summing the tree edge-count identity over its components; isolated vertices only reduce the number of edges. Therefore the right side of every displayed constraint is at most zero, and $x=0$ is feasible. It is extreme: if zero were a convex combination with coefficients strictly between zero and one of two nonnegative feasible vectors, every coordinate of both would have to be zero. It has no coordinate at least one half. Thus a literal universal statement needs a stopping or nontriviality condition. Removing forest instances is natural for the intended algorithm, but is an additional hypothesis, not a proved solution of the source's cyclic-instance goal.
A false counterexample obtained from the weaker cycle LP.
On $K_4$, the vector $x_v=1/3$ satisfies every cycle-cover constraint $\sum_{v\in C}x_v\ge1$, since every cycle has three or four vertices. Its four triangle constraints are tight. They uniquely determine the vector: subtracting any two equations shows that the omitted coordinates are equal, then each triangle sum gives $x_v=1/3$. Thus it is an extreme point of the cycle-cover LP. More explicitly, in any convex decomposition into feasible vectors, each tight triangle constraint must be tight at both endpoints, so uniqueness forces the endpoints to coincide.

It is not feasible for the source relaxation. With $S=V(K_4)$, each coefficient $d_S(v)-1$ equals two, giving left side $8/3$, while the right side is $6-4+1=3$. Therefore this standard cycle-LP fractional example cannot disprove the stated half-coordinate property of the stronger LP.

A symmetric feasible candidate is not extreme either.
For $K_n$, $n\ge3$, set $x_v=(n-1)/(2n)<1/2$. For a subset of size $s\ge2$, its density inequality is \[ (s-2)\sum_{v\in S}x_v\ge(s-1)(s-2)/2. \] At $s=2$ both sides are zero. At $s\ge3$, it holds because $(n-1)/(2n)\ge(s-1)/(2s)$ when $s\le n$; after multiplying by $2ns$, this is $s\le n$. The full-set inequality is tight, while proper subsets with $s\ge3$ are strict. All nonnegativity constraints are strict. Perturb one coordinate by $+\eta$ and another by $-\eta$. The full-set equality is preserved, and choosing $\eta>0$ smaller than the finitely many positive slacks divided by the corresponding nonzero coefficient changes, and smaller than the coordinate value, keeps both perturbations feasible. The $s=2$ constraints are identically zero and are unaffected. Thus the uniform vector is the midpoint of two distinct feasible vectors and is not extreme. Feasibility with every coordinate below one half does not itself contradict the source's extreme-point statement.

Audit and remaining question

The primary LP on printed page 2944 was inspected. The audit kept the strengthened density constraints, distinguished the existence of a large coordinate from full half-integrality, and checked the forest boundary separately.
