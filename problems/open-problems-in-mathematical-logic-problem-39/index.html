Open Problems in Mathematical Logic — Problem 39

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ be a permutation group. Its action is called oligomorphic if, for every integer $n\geq 1$, the coordinatewise action \[ g\cdot(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=(gx_1,\ldots,gx_n) \] has finitely many orbits on $X^n$. In the usual countable setting, $X$ is countably infinite and $G$ carries the topology of pointwise convergence, inherited from $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ with $X$ discrete. The supplied statement does not specify the underlying set, whether this topological setting is intended, or whether $G$ must be closed in $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$.

For an abstract group $G$, let $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{abs}}(G)$ be its group of abstract automorphisms and define \[ \operatorname{Inn}(G)=\{c_g:g\in G\},\qquad c_g(h)=ghg^{-1},\qquad \operatorname{Out}_{\mathrm{abs}}(G)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{abs}}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G). \] For a topological group $G$, another possible interpretation is \[ \operatorname{Out}_{\mathrm{top}}(G) =\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{top}}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G), \] where $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{top}}(G)$ consists of group automorphisms that are homeomorphisms. These interpretations are distinguished here because the source does not specify which is intended.

A topological group $H$ is profinite if it is isomorphic as a topological group to an inverse limit of finite discrete groups.

Question (supplied Problem 39). Is $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ profinite for every oligomorphic group $G$?

Unresolved qualifications. A fully specified proposition requires the intended class of oligomorphic groups, the meaning of automorphism in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, and the topology on that outer automorphism group. If a quotient topology is intended, the topology on the automorphism group must also be specified. If only an abstract outer automorphism group is intended, the question must instead clarify whether it asks for the existence of a profinite group topology. None of these choices is settled by the supplied record.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every oligomorphic group has a profinite outer automorphism group. Oligomorphic means that its permutation action has finitely many orbits on tuples of every finite positive length. The intended automorphisms, topology, and possible closedness assumption require clarification.
