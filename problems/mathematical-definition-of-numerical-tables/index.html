Mathematical Definition of Numerical Tables

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a historiographical question about the meaning of a numerical table, rather than a conjecture with a specified mathematical truth condition.

The proposed description is a list of equal-length lists of numbers expressing a correspondence between $n$ sets, where $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $n\geq 2$, together with a proposed mathematical dimension of $n-1$. If "correspondence" is interpreted as a relation between sets $A_1,\ldots,A_n$, it could be represented by \[ R\subseteq A_1\times\cdots\times A_n. \] This relational interpretation is only a possible clarification; the source does not specify whether the correspondence is a relation, a function, or another structure. It also does not identify the numerical domains, specify whether the lists must be finite, or explicitly identify their common length with $n$.

Question. How should a numerical table be defined? In particular, is the description as a list of equal-length lists of numbers expressing a correspondence between $n\geq 2$ sets an appropriate definition, and in what precise sense should such a table have mathematical dimension $n-1$?

The term "mathematical dimension" is not defined in the supplied record. Thus $n-1$ is part of the proposed description under discussion, not an established dimension formula. No uniquely determined formal proposition or criterion for an adequate definition is supplied.

Short English statement

The question asks for a suitable historical and mathematical definition of a numerical table. It specifically asks whether equal-length lists of numbers describing a correspondence between $n\geq 2$ sets should constitute such a table, with dimension $n-1$. The meanings of correspondence and dimension remain unspecified.
