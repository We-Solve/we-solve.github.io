Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let $f$ be a neutral social choice function for $n$ voters and $m$ alternatives, and let $M_i(f)$ be voter $i$'s manipulation power. For $m=3$, if $f$ is $\varepsilon$-far from dictatorship then $\sum_iM_i(f)\geq C\varepsilon^2$. Can the current $\Omega(1/n)$ lower bound on $\max_iM_i(f)$ be improved; can neutrality be replaced by $f$ being far from having range of size at most 2; and does an analogous theorem hold for $m>3$, perhaps with a bound polynomially weaker in $m$?

Primary-source normalization

In the original report $M_i(f)$ is the probability of a profitable randomly chosen new report, averaging over a uniform random profile and an independent uniform complete order for voter $i$. The supplied upstream original-statement paraphrase describes the probability that a profitable report exists. Keep these separate: denote the latter by $E_i(f)$ and let $m!$ be the number of possible orders. Conditioning on a profile, if there is a profitable report there are between one and $m!$ of them. Therefore \[ E_i(f)/m!\le M_i(f)\le E_i(f). \] This is an exact elementary comparison, but its loss is factorial in $m$. It cannot by itself extend a polynomial-in-$m$ existence-probability theorem to the report's random-misreport quantity. All three source questions remain in scope, rather than being replaced by one of these different normalizations.

Attempt at the individual-voter scale

We rederive an upper example for three alternatives. Let $f$ choose a plurality winner, breaking any tie according to voter 1's complete ranking. This deterministic tie rule is neutral: relabeling carries vote counts and the tie-breaking ranking together. It need not be anonymous. Let $C_a$ count votes having alternative $a$ first. If every pair of counts differs by more than two, the winner has a margin at least three and no single changed vote can change it, since a pairwise count difference changes by at most two. Thus, for every voter, both $M_i$ and $E_i$ are at most \[ \Pr[\exists a\ne b:|C_a-C_b|\le2]. \] Here is a complete $O(n^{-1/2})$ bound on this probability, with deliberately loose constants.

Fix a pair $a,b$. For each uniform independent ranking its top is $a,b$ or the third alternative with equal probability. Let $T$ be the number of tops in $\{a,b\}$; then $\mathbb ET=2n/3$ and $\operatorname{Var}T=2n/9$. Chebyshev's inequality, proved by $\mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^2\ge u^2\Pr(|T-\mathbb ET|\ge u)$, gives $\Pr(T<n/3)\le2/n$. Conditionally on $T=t$, the difference $C_a-C_b$ is a sum of $t$ independent fair signs. Its largest atom is at most $\sqrt{2/(t+1)}$. To verify this bound, put $b_j=\binom{2j}{j}/4^j$. The recursion $b_{j+1}/b_j=(2j+1)/(2j+2)$ and $(2j+1)^2(j+2)\le4(j+1)^3$ imply inductively $b_j\le1/\sqrt{j+1}$. The largest even-$t$ atom is $b_{t/2}$; for $t=2j+1$ the largest atom is $b_j(2j+1)/(2j+2)$. Both obey the asserted bound. There are at most five integer values between $-2$ and 2, so \[ \Pr(|C_a-C_b|\le2)\le 2/n+5\sqrt{6/n}. \] A union bound over the three unordered pairs gives \[ M_i(f)\le E_i(f)\le B_n:=6/n+15\sqrt{6/n}. \] Of course probabilities may also be bounded by one; the displayed estimate is useful asymptotically.

The example stays far from every dictatorship.
For a fixed voter $j$, define $g_j$ to be plurality among the other $n-1$ voters, with ties resolved using one fixed other voter's ranking. For $n\ge2$ this is independent of voter $j$'s ranking, hence $\Pr[g_j=\text{top}_j]=1/3$. If all pairwise counts among the other voters differ by more than two, adding voter $j$ cannot change the winner, regardless of the two tie rules. Therefore $\Pr[f\ne g_j]\le B_{n-1}$ and \[ \Pr[f\ne\text{top}_j]\ge 2/3-B_{n-1}. \] This holds for every $j$, so these neutral rules are, for example, $1/3$-far from every dictatorship for all sufficiently large $n$, while every individual's power is $O(n^{-1/2})$. This rederives the upper-scale obstruction described in the report. It neither improves the universal $\Omega(1/n)$ lower bound nor proves that either endpoint is sharp.

Attempt to weaken neutrality and extend the alternative count

For any neutral rule on $m$ alternatives under the uniform profile measure, the output is uniform: relabeling profiles preserves their probabilities and permutes any specified output to any other. For a fixed two-element set $A$, a rule with image in $A$ can agree with $f$ only where $f\in A$, a probability $2/m$. This is achievable by following $f$ there and choosing a fixed element of $A$ elsewhere. Thus the distance from the class of rules with range of size at most two is exactly $1-2/m$ when $m\ge2$. This proves a necessary quantitative consequence of neutrality, not its converse and not a replacement for its uses in a manipulation proof. The attempted extension by conditioning on an existing profitable report also stops at the factorial loss $m!$ above; a polynomial dependence on $m$ needs additional structure controlling which reports work.

Adversarial audit and remaining gaps

The audit checked the tie rule's neutrality, the fixed-voter independence in the distance estimate, the two-unit count-change bound, and the factorial normalization loss. No computation was needed for these proofs. The later Mossel--Racz primary abstract states a nonneutral quantitative theorem for profile manipulability and general numbers of alternatives. This is relevant historical context, not a full comparison proof for all three questions and this precise $M_i$. No assertion that all the 2008 questions remain presently open is made. The new universal individual lower bound, its sharp order, and a complete theorem with the requested weaker hypotheses and polynomial alternative dependence are not established here.
