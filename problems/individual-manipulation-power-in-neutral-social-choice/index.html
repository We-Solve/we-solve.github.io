Individual Manipulation Power in Neutral Social Choice

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Neutrality means that relabeling alternatives relabels the chosen outcome in the same way. A dictatorship always follows a designated voter's top choice. Distance from dictatorship is the probability of disagreement under the prescribed profile distribution; $M_i(f)$ measures profitable manipulation by voter $i$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $f$ be a neutral social choice function for $n$ voters and $m$ alternatives, and let $M_i(f)$ be voter $i$'s manipulation power. For $m=3$, if $f$ is $\varepsilon$-far from dictatorship then $\sum_iM_i(f)\geq C\varepsilon^2$. Can the current $\Omega(1/n)$ lower bound on $\max_iM_i(f)$ be improved; can neutrality be replaced by $f$ being far from having range of size at most 2; and does an analogous theorem hold for $m>3$, perhaps with a bound polynomially weaker in $m$? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The exact normalization of manipulation power and profile measure require the source. All three extension questions are retained.
