Degree-sensitive semi-algebraic Ramsey bounds

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's semi-algebraic Ramsey question, attributed to Bukh--Matoušek, asks whether an omitted preceding bound can be improved to a constant number of iterated logarithms. The extraction does not define $f$, $r_f$ or the antecedent "this." A precise natural interpretation fixes a semi-algebraic $k$-ary predicate $f$ on ordered distinct points of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, expressed by a Boolean combination of polynomial sign conditions, and asks for a homogeneous subset bound whose number of logarithms is independent of $k$ and $d$ under specified control of the predicate.

Let $R_f^{(k)}(s,n)$ be the least $N$ such that every ordered $N$-point set contains either $s$ points whose increasing $k$-tuples all satisfy $f$, or $n$ whose increasing $k$-tuples all fail $f$. Define $\operatorname{tw}_1(x)=x$ and $\operatorname{tw}_{h+1}(x)=2^{\operatorname{tw}_h(x)}$. This explicitly supplies conventions absent from the fragment.

The attached aid reports that an Adibelli--Tomon February 2026 preprint proves, for total polynomial degree bounded by an integer $D\ge1$, \[ R_f^{(k)}(n,n)\le\operatorname{tw}_{3D^3}(C n), \] with tower height independent of uniformity and dimension; other dependence of $C$ and the precise complexity hypotheses require the preprint statement. Inverting the tower gives at most $3D^3-1$ iterated logarithms for a guaranteed homogeneous size. This answers fixed-predicate or bounded-degree readings as reported, while the stronger dimension-only question uniform over degrees is expressly not settled.

For its refinement, divide variables into $k$ point-coordinate blocks and define the monomial block-support width $w$ as the largest number of distinct point blocks occurring in a monomial of any defining polynomial. The aid states that, for $1\le w\le k$, its quasi-algebraic theorem yields \[ R_f^{(k)}(n,n)\le\operatorname{tw}_{K_w}(C n),\qquad K_w=\frac{w(w^2+6w+1)}2, \] independent of the total powers within participating blocks. It calls the resulting relation $w$-dependent, but the exact quasi-algebraic definition is omitted. For $w=1$ it reports an injective coordinate lift and a stronger Jin--Tomon semi-linear one-exponential bound, without supplying that bound's complete parameter dependence. For $w=0$ all predicates are constant and every vertex set is homogeneous.

The original context attributes to Bukh--Matoušek a sharp two-logarithm result in dimension one. It attributes to Conlon--Fox--Pach--Sudakov--Suk the written bound $r_f(N)\ge c\log^{(k-2)}N$, where $\log^{(j)}$ means $j$ iterated logarithms, and sharpness in sufficiently high dimension ${\mathbb{R}}^{c(k)}$. Because the definition and normalization of $r_f$ are missing, this literal statement's possible power losses cannot be checked here. The original also asks for the unbalanced variant $R_f^{(k)}(s,n)$; the aid's displayed improvements concern diagonal thresholds only.

Short English statement

Clarify how many iterated logarithms are needed for semi-algebraic homogeneous sets. The supplied aid reports a preprint answer under bounded degree and a block-support refinement, while leaving the degree-uniform dimension-only question and unbalanced variant unresolved.
