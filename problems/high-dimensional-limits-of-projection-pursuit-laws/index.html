Math-3324

High-Dimensional Limits of Projection-Pursuit Laws

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an integer $m\geq 1$ and $\alpha\in(0,\infty)$. Let $(n_k,d_k)$ be positive integer pairs with $n_k,d_k\to\infty$, $d_k\geq m$, and $n_k/d_k\to\alpha$. For each $k$, let $X_{k,1},\ldots,X_{k,n_k}$ be independent random vectors with distribution $N(0,I_{d_k})$. No coupling of samples for different $k$ is specified in the supplied record.

An orthonormal frame is a matrix $W\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_k\times m}$ satisfying $W^{\mathsf T}W=I_m$. It represents an $m$-dimensional subspace together with an orthonormal coordinate system on that subspace. Its projected empirical law is \[ \widehat\mu_{k,W}=\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_k}\delta_{W^{\mathsf T}X_{k,i}}\in\mathcal P({\mathbb{R}}^m), \] where $\delta_y$ denotes unit point mass at $y$, and $\mathcal P({\mathbb{R}}^m)$ is the set of Borel probability measures on ${\mathbb{R}}^m$. The corresponding finite-sample collection, using all orthonormal frames, is \[ \mathcal C_k=\left\{\widehat\mu_{k,W}:W\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_k\times m},\ W^{\mathsf T}W=I_m\right\}. \] This coordinate representation must be distinguished from treating subspaces without a chosen basis, which would require specifying identification under orthogonal transformations of ${\mathbb{R}}^m$.

Requested characterization. Describe the set $\mathcal F_{m,\alpha}$ of limiting empirical projection laws in the regime above, allowing projection directions to range over the indicated subspaces. The supplied statement does not give a complete definition of membership in $\mathcal F_{m,\alpha}$.

For example, if weak convergence is intended, the underlying convergence of probability measures is defined by \[ \nu_k\Rightarrow\nu \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \int f\,\mathrm d\nu_k\longrightarrow\int f\,\mathrm d\nu \quad\text{for every bounded continuous }f:{\mathbb{R}}^m\to{\mathbb{R}}. \] An attainable-limit formulation would ask which $\mu$ can satisfy $\widehat\mu_{k,W_k}\Rightarrow\mu$ for a suitable sequence of frames $W_k$. However, the record does not specify whether those frames may depend measurably on the observed sample, whether $\mu$ must be deterministic, whether convergence is almost sure or in probability, whether subsequences are permitted, or whether the definition must hold for every sequence $(n_k,d_k)$ with the stated ratio. Nor does it specify whether a limit of the entire random collection $\mathcal C_k$ is intended instead. These choices remain unresolved; the displayed finite-sample collection and weak-convergence definition explain a possible formulation without assigning an unsupported definitive meaning to $\mathcal F_{m,\alpha}$.

Short English statement

Project independent standard Gaussian observations onto subspaces of a fixed dimension $m$, while the sample size and ambient dimension tend to infinity with ratio $\alpha\in(0,\infty)$. Characterize the probability laws attainable as limits of the projected empirical distributions. The supplied record leaves the convergence mode, admissible dependence of projections on the data, and precise meaning of the limiting set unresolved.
