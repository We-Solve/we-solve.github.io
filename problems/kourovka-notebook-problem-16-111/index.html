Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.111

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is periodic if every element of $G$ has finite order. It is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $G$. A $2$-subgroup is a subgroup all of whose elements have order a power of $2$; here a Sylow $2$-subgroup means a $2$-subgroup maximal under inclusion.

A field $P$ is locally finite if every subfield generated by finitely many elements is finite. Interpret the conventional notation $L_2(P)$ as \[ L_2(P)=\operatorname{PSL}_2(P) =\operatorname{SL}_2(P)/Z(\operatorname{SL}_2(P)), \] where $\operatorname{SL}_2(P)$ is the group of determinant-one $2\times2$ matrices over $P$, and $Z$ denotes the center.

Question (V. P. Shunkov, 2006; Kourovka Notebook 16.111). Let $G$ be an infinite simple periodic group possessing a dihedral Sylow $2$-subgroup. Must there exist a locally finite field $P$ of odd characteristic such that \[ G\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(P)? \] Any such $P$ must be infinite, since $G$ is infinite.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied record does not define "dihedral" in this infinite-group setting. It does not specify whether the Sylow subgroup must be a finite dihedral $2$-group or whether an infinite locally dihedral $2$-group is also intended, nor does it specify a minimum allowed finite order. The question above retains the source terminology; its precise range requires confirmation from the source's conventions.

Short English statement

Shunkov asks whether every infinite simple group in which every element has finite order, and which has a dihedral Sylow $2$-subgroup, is isomorphic to $\operatorname{PSL}_2(P)$ over a locally finite field of odd characteristic. The intended scope of "dihedral" remains to be clarified.
