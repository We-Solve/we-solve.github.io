Stationary and Optimal Linear Differential Behaviors

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A linear time-invariant differential behavior is a space of trajectories satisfying constant-coefficient linear differential equations. For a polynomial matrix $R$, $\ker R(d/dt)$ denotes trajectories annihilated by its associated differential operator. $\succeq0$ means positive semidefinite.

A state-space system evolves as $\dot x=f(x,u)$ with output $y=h(x,u)$. Model reduction seeks a lower-dimensional system preserving specified input-output properties. Observability distinguishes initial states from their output trajectories, while controllability concerns reachable states under allowed inputs.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Characterize the linear time-invariant differential behaviors $\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{L}^w$ for which there exists a symmetric two-variable polynomial matrix $\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{w\times w}[\zeta,\eta]$ satisfying $$\mathcal{B}=\ker\Phi\!\left(-\frac{d}{dt},\frac{d}{dt}\right).$$ These are the stationary behaviors. Characterize also the optimal ones, for which one can additionally require $\Phi(-i\omega,i\omega)\succeq0$ for every $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The trajectory regularity and interpretation of the two-variable operator are the report's conventions.

Short English statement

Characterize which linear differential behaviors arise as stationary ones and which satisfy the additional frequency-domain positivity condition.
