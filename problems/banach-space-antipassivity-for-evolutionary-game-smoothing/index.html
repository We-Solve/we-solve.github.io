Banach-space antipassivity for evolutionary-game smoothing

LLM status Open unverified

GT-024 | Evolutionary and population games

R | Functional-analytic extension agenda

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a compact metric strategy space. Write $\mathcal M(S)$ for the Banach space of finite signed regular Borel measures with total-variation norm, $\mathcal P(S)$ for its probability measures, and $C(S)$ for continuous real functions with supremum norm. The pairing is $\langle f,\eta\rangle=\int_S f\,d\eta$. A payoff map $F:\mathcal P(S)\to C(S)$ is monotone in the game's sign convention if \[ \langle F(\mu)-F(\nu),\mu-\nu\rangle\leq0 \qquad(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P(S)). \] Fix $\lambda>0$ and consider the smoothing dynamics \[ \dot\rho=u(\mu,\rho):=\lambda(F(\mu)-\rho). \] A non-strict $\delta$-antipassivity certificate consists of a nonnegative storage function $\Gamma:\mathcal P(S)\times C(S)\to\mathbb R$, extending to a Fréchet-differentiable function on an open neighborhood in $\mathcal M(S)\times C(S)$. Require $\Gamma$ to be continuous on $\mathcal P(S)\times C(S)$ for the product of the weak topology generated by $\eta\mapsto\int_S\varphi\,d\eta$, $\varphi\in C(S)$, and the supremum-norm topology on $C(S)$. Require also that $\Gamma(\mu,\rho)=0$ exactly when $u(\mu,\rho)=0$, and, along every strongly differentiable probability-valued input $\mu(t)$ and corresponding solution $\rho(t)$, \[ D_1\Gamma(\mu,\rho)[\dot\mu]+D_2\Gamma(\mu,\rho)[u(\mu,\rho)] \leq-\langle u(\mu,\rho),\dot\mu\rangle. \] The research target is to give verifiable Banach-space hypotheses on $F$ that guarantee such a certificate and extend the finite-dimensional invertibility-based criterion identified by [S1].

Short English statement

Which additional infinite-dimensional conditions make smoothed payoff dynamics antipassive?

Variants and scope boundaries

Monotonicity alone is not asserted to suffice. Derivatives require an extension because $\mathcal P(S)$ has no open interior in the ambient measure space. The source's continuity convention uses weak convergence for probability measures and norm convergence for payoff functions. A strict certificate includes a nonnegative dissipation term and additional zero-set requirements; it is stronger than the inequality displayed here.

Source relationship and status

[S1], Section 7, identifies the extension of the finite-dimensional condition as open. This entry makes the desired storage inequality explicit, but does not invent a universal invertibility notion or claim that every monotone smoothing has this property.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.

Banach-space antipassivity for evolutionary-game smoothing: research attempt

The requested certificate

Write $X=\mathcal M(S)$, $Y=C(S)$ and $\langle f,\eta\rangle=\int f\,d\eta$. The target is a nonnegative, Fréchet-differentiable storage $\Gamma$ for $u=\lambda(F(\mu)-\rho)$, continuous for weak convergence of probabilities and uniform convergence of payoffs, with zero set exactly $\rho=F(\mu)$. Along every strongly differentiable probability-valued input it must satisfy \[ D_1\Gamma[\dot\mu]+D_2\Gamma[u]\leq-\langle u,\dot\mu\rangle. \] The quantifier over every input velocity is unusually restrictive. The first calculation below exploits it before trying to import a Hilbert-space squared norm into a measure/function dual pair.

The inspected primary manuscript's Definitions 10--11 specify the storage topology and its final discussion asks for an infinite-dimensional invertibility extension. The following obstructions concern precisely the displayed storage inequality in the catalogue. They do not assert that all notions of dissipativity, or every stable smoothed game, require the same certificate.

A necessary exactness condition

Fix a finite set of atoms in $S$ and a probability $\mu$ strictly positive on those atoms. Any signed measure $\eta$ supported there with total mass zero is a two-sided tangent: $\mu+t\eta$ is feasible for sufficiently small positive and negative $t$. For each real $r$, one can choose a probability-valued smooth input with initial velocity $r\eta$. At a fixed initial payoff $\rho$, the inequality gives \[ r\left(D_1\Gamma(\mu,\rho)[\eta]+\langle u,\eta\rangle\right) +D_2\Gamma(\mu,\rho)[u]\leq0. \] Letting $r$ have either sign and arbitrarily large magnitude forces \[ D_1\Gamma(\mu,\rho)[\eta]=\lambda\langle\rho-F(\mu),\eta\rangle. \tag{1} \] Thus on every relative interior finite simplex the one-form $\eta\mapsto\langle F(\mu),\eta\rangle$ must be exact. If $F$ is continuously differentiable there, its tangent Jacobian must be symmetric: \[ \langle DF(\mu)[\eta],\zeta\rangle =\langle DF(\mu)[\zeta],\eta\rangle. \tag{2} \] Indeed (1) makes the restricted gradient of $\Gamma$ continuously differentiable whenever $F$ is; equality of its mixed derivatives proves (2). Merely assuming differentiability of $\Gamma$ does not evade this implication.
Proposition: Monotonicity alone, even strict linear monotonicity, does not imply the catalogue's certificate.
Proof: Take $S$ to have three points, identify measures with vectors, and set \[ K=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\\-1&1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad F(\mu)=(-I+K)\mu. \] For every difference $h$, $h^{\mathsf T}(F(\mu+h)-F(\mu))=-\|h\|^2$, so the required game monotonicity holds strictly. But for tangent vectors $\eta=(1,0,-1)$ and $\zeta=(0,1,-1)$, $\eta^{\mathsf T}K\zeta=-3$ while $\zeta^{\mathsf T}K\eta=3$. Equation (2) fails. Therefore no differentiable storage with the stated inequality exists, regardless of its zero-set or continuity properties. ∎

This gives an immediate diagnostic for a proposed Banach criterion: it must control the antisymmetric part on attainable tangents, not only the sign of $\langle DF\eta,\eta\rangle$. It also explains why blindly using a quadratic residual norm misses an input-velocity term.

An explicit certificate in the symmetric invertible case

In a finite strategy space let $A$ be symmetric positive definite and $F(\mu)=f-A\mu$. Put $r=\rho-F(\mu)$ and \[ \Gamma(\mu,\rho)=\frac{\lambda}{2}r^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}r. \] This is nonnegative, vanishes exactly at $r=0$, and extends smoothly off the simplex. Its derivative is \[ \dot\Gamma=\lambda r^{\mathsf T}\dot\mu -\lambda^2r^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}r \leq-\langle u,\dot\mu\rangle, \qquad u=-\lambda r. \] The equality uses symmetry and $\dot r=\dot\rho+A\dot\mu$; no assumption on the actual population adjustment rule is needed. All finite-dimensional weak and norm topologies here agree, so the continuity requirement is satisfied. This is a verification of the familiar quadratic construction under explicit hypotheses, not a new general theorem about infinite strategy spaces.

There is a formal dual-potential extension worth isolating. Suppose a differentiable convex functional $\Phi$ on an open measure domain and a differentiable dual functional $\Phi^*$ on $Y$ satisfy, throughout the domain under consideration, \[ \Phi(\mu)+\Phi^*(g)-\langle g,\mu\rangle\geq0, \] with equality exactly when $g=D\Phi(\mu)\in Y$. Suppose $D\Phi^*(g)\in X$ is an inverse of $D\Phi$ on all payoff arguments required, both derivatives have the indicated pairing interpretation, and the displayed gap is weak--uniform continuous on $\mathcal P(S)\times Y$. For $F=-D\Phi$, set \[ \Gamma=\lambda\{\Phi(\mu)+\Phi^*(-\rho)+\langle\rho,\mu\rangle\}. \] Writing $x_\rho=D\Phi^*(-\rho)$, differentiation gives (1) and \[ D_2\Gamma[u] =-\lambda^2\langle D\Phi(\mu)-D\Phi(x_\rho),\mu-x_\rho\rangle\leq0. \] Convexity proves the final inequality. These hypotheses are sufficient, but proving their availability for interesting maps between $\mathcal M(S)$ and $C(S)$ is precisely the difficult part. Defining the conjugate only over probabilities introduces boundary normal cones and can give extra zeros, so that shortcut does not establish the required zero set.

Why a continuous-kernel inverse is problematic

Consider $A\eta(s)=\int k(s,t)d\eta(t)$ with $k$ continuous on compact $S\times S$. Suppose $S$ contains distinct $s_n\to s_0$. Then $\eta_n=\delta_{s_n}-\delta_{s_0}$ has total variation norm two, whereas uniform continuity gives \[ \|A\eta_n\|_\infty =\sup_s|k(s,s_n)-k(s,s_0)|\longrightarrow0. \] Consequently $A$ has no bounded inverse on its range when that range carries the supremum norm and measures carry total variation. The finite-dimensional quadratic formula cannot simply be copied with $A^{-1}$ a bounded Banach operator. An injective positive kernel does not fix this mismatch; injectivity and a bounded inverse are distinct properties.

The same sequence is weakly convergent to zero, emphasizing the two separate requirements: weak continuity is natural for the storage on probability measures, but Fréchet derivatives are taken in the stronger ambient norm. Replacing either topology silently would change the target.

Completion Estimate

48%

This is a post hoc, heuristic estimate for the full catalogue target. The attempt proves necessary tangent exactness and explicit monotone and continuous-kernel inverse obstructions, and verifies finite symmetric and conditional dual-potential certificates. It does not establish an applicable broad Banach-space criterion for infinite strategy sets with the required differentiability, weak continuity, inverse structure, and exact storage zero set.

Status and source checked

The work proves a necessary tangent exactness condition, supplies a concrete monotone obstruction, verifies a symmetric invertible benchmark, and identifies a continuous-kernel inverse obstruction. It does not characterize the infinite-dimensional maps admitting a certificate, nor provide the sought broad Banach extension. The original problem remains unresolved.
