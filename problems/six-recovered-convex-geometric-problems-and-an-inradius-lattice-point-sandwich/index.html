Six recovered convex-geometric problems and an inradius lattice-point sandwich

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied verification identifies this as a fused extraction of official Problems 15--20, not one problem. The initial "5" has lost its leading digit. Let $B_\infty^n=[-1,1]^n$, let $B_2^n$ be the Euclidean unit ball, and let $S^{n-1}=\partial B_2^n$. Write $|\cdot|$ for the appropriate Euclidean volume.
  1. 15. Gaussian sections (A. Zvavitch). On an $(n-1)$-dimensional Euclidean subspace $H$, let $\gamma_{n-1}$ be standard Gaussian measure with density $(2\pi)^{-(n-1)/2}e^{-\|x\|_2^2/2}$ relative to its intrinsic volume. For $n\geq3$ and $r>0$, determine the maximizers \[ \theta(r)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\theta\in S^{n-1}} \gamma_{n-1}(rB_\infty^n\cap\theta^\perp). \] The source also asks whether its stated known upper bound at $r=1$ is sharp. That bound has the form \[ \gamma_{n-1}(B_\infty^n\cap\theta^\perp) \leq\gamma_{n-1}(a_nB_\infty^{n-1}), \] but the extracted scale "$\sqrt{\phantom n}$ nn $-1$" does not unambiguously recover $a_n$. A plausible reading is $a_n=\sqrt{n/(n-1)}$; it is recorded as tentative rather than certified from the truncated verification. The maximizing-section question is well defined independently of that damaged bound.
  2. 16. Minimum slabs of the cube. Under the explicit half-width convention, put \[ F_{n,t}(u)=|B_\infty^n\cap\{x:|\langle x,u\rangle|\leq t\}|, \quad u\in S^{n-1},\quad t\geq0, \] and $u_j=j^{-1/2}(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{j},0,\ldots,0)$. The source states that a coordinate direction $u_1$ minimizes for $t\leq2\sqrt2-2$, and conjectures thresholds $t_1,t_2$ for which minimizing directions are $u_2$ when $2\sqrt2-2<t<t_1$, $u_3$ when $t_1<t<t_2$, and $u_n$ when $t_2<t$. Its slab-width normalization, dimension range, dependence of the thresholds on $n$, tie cases, and treatment of widths for which the slab contains the whole cube are not specified. The displayed convention makes the question interpretable but requires source confirmation.
  3. 17. Covering the sphere. Given $n+k$ centers $x_i\in S^{n-1}$ with $k\leq n$, minimize the radius of equal-radius caps covering $S^{n-1}$, describe optimal configurations, and determine dependence on $n,k$. With angular radius this can be expressed as \[ r(n,k)=\inf_{x_1,\ldots,x_{n+k}\in S^{n-1}} \sup_{u\in S^{n-1}}\min_i\arccos\langle u,x_i\rangle. \] The source does not fix angular versus chordal radius or the lower bound on $k$; the commonly intended nontrivial range $1\leq k\leq n$ is not explicitly stated. Chordal radius is obtained from angular radius by $r\mapsto2\sin(r/2)$, so numerical radius formulas require the convention.
  4. 18. Mean width of an inscribed simplex. For $n+1$ points $x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}\in S^{n-1}$, maximize the mean width of their convex hull. With $h_K(u)=\sup_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle$, use \[ w(K)=\frac1{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}(h_K(u)+h_K(-u))\,d\sigma(u). \] Is the maximum attained by a regular inscribed simplex? The source does not assume affine independence of the initial points, so the optimization includes degenerate configurations.
  5. 19. Intersection with the negative. The source writes that a non-symmetric set $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ with centroid $0$ satisfies \[ |K\cap(-K)|\geq2^{-n}|K|, \] and asks whether a centered simplex is extremal and, if not, what is. For a measurable positive-volume set the centroid is $|K|^{-1}\int_Kx\,dx$ when defined. The word "set" supplies no compactness or convexity hypothesis; the quoted lower bound cannot be treated as an established statement for all such sets. In the intended convex-geometric setting, the target is to minimize $|K\cap(-K)|/|K|$ among suitably specified centered convex bodies and determine the extremizers. Adding that convexity assumption is an explicit interpretation needing source review, not a silent repair.
  6. 20. Lattice points and inradius. Find the smallest dimension-dependent Euclidean inradius threshold $R(n)$ such that every convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ containing some translate of $R(n)B_2^n$ has lattice-point count comparable to volume by a polynomial factor in $n$. More explicitly, seek a universal polynomial factor $p(n)\geq1$ and a sharp threshold or asymptotic scale for which \[ p(n)^{-1}|K|\leq\#(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^n)\leq p(n)|K|. \] The polynomial degree and whether "smallest" means exact or asymptotic are unspecified. The ball need not be centered at the origin or a lattice point.

The supplied machine aid reports the following partial theorem for Problem 20: if $K$ contains a Euclidean ball of radius $R>\sqrt n/2$, then \[ \left(1-\frac{\sqrt n}{2R}\right)^n|K| \leq\#(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^n) \leq\left(1+\frac{\sqrt n}{2R}\right)^n|K|. \] It reports that $R\geq n^{3/2}/\log(n+1)$ gives \[ \frac{|K|}{n+1}\leq\#(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^n)\leq\sqrt{n+1}\,|K|, \] and that no positive lower comparison is possible below $\sqrt n/2$, by a ball centered at a half-cell point and containing no lattice points. These bounds do not identify the optimal threshold in the intervening range. The aid additionally announces Gaussian small- and large-radius endpoint formulas, a restricted slab crossing, the exact $n+1$-center covering radius, the planar mean-width case, and a centered-simplex intersection formula. Their formulas and hypotheses are not supplied, so no stronger statements for Problems 15--19 are reconstructed.

Short English statement

This extraction combines six optimization questions. They concern Gaussian cube sections, minimal slabs, optimal spherical caps, the largest mean-width simplex, overlap with reflection, and lattice counts controlled by inradius. The supplied partial theorem gives explicit upper and lower lattice-count bounds; the other problems and their missing conventions remain separate.
