Unbounded Non-Grid Geproci Point Configurations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A conventional interpretation of the three-dimensional question can be formulated as follows. Fix an algebraically closed field $k$. This is an explicit provisional convention: the supplied record specifies neither the ground field nor its characteristic.

Let $Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}$ be a finite reduced set of points. For positive integers $a,b$, say that $Z$ has the $(a,b)$-geproci property if its projection from a general center $p\in\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}$ to a plane $H\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}$ is injective on $Z$ and its reduced image is a scheme-theoretic complete intersection \[ \pi_p(Z)=V_H(F_p,G_p),\qquad \deg F_p=a,\quad \deg G_p=b, \] where $F_p,G_p$ have no common curve component. Here "general" means that the condition holds for every center in some nonempty Zariski open subset of $\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}\setminus(H\cup Z)$. In particular, $|Z|=ab$. Say that $Z$ is geproci if it has this property for some $a,b$.

Under a positive-integer convention, an $(r,s)$-grid is a set \[ Z=\{L_i\cap M_j:1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq s\}, \] where the $L_i$ are pairwise skew lines, the $M_j$ are pairwise skew lines, every $L_i$ meets every $M_j$, and the $rs$ intersection points are distinct. Call $Z$ non-grid if it admits no such representation. The supplied record does not define grids or specify whether cases with $r=1$ or $s=1$, or geproci degrees equal to $1$, are admitted; this convention therefore requires confirmation.

With these provisional conventions, the first question is whether \[ \forall N\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \exists a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \exists Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}: \quad Z\text{ is finite and reduced},\quad |Z|=ab>N,\quad Z\text{ is non-grid and }(a,b)\text{-geproci}. \] The field $k$ is a parameter of this formulation; the source does not say whether existence is sought over one particular field or uniformly over a class of fields. The word "family" is interpreted here only as unbounded cardinality, without imposing an algebraic parameter space.

The second question asks for analogous examples in higher-dimensional projective spaces. The supplied record does not specify the projection target, the complete-intersection condition, a higher-dimensional definition of grid, or whether examples are required in some or every dimension greater than three. Consequently, no unique quantified higher-dimensional statement can be extracted.

The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and reports non-grid geproci examples, including infinite families and a $24$-point example, while describing the requested general series and higher-dimensional analogue as unresolved. These are supplied assessments, not independently verified conclusions; the relationship between the reported infinite families and the displayed unbounded-cardinality question is not explained.

Short English statement

The question asks whether non-grid finite point sets in projective three-space can have arbitrarily large cardinality while their general plane projections are complete intersections, and whether a comparable phenomenon occurs in higher dimensions. The ground field, degeneracy conventions, and higher-dimensional meaning require clarification. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation rather than an independently verified status.
