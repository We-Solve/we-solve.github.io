Additive Orthogonal Bases for Invariant Polynomials

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a field $k$, a polynomial $k$-algebra $A$, and a group $G$ acting on $A$ by $k$-algebra automorphisms, the invariant-polynomial ring is \[ A^G=\{f\in A: g\cdot f=f\text{ for every }g\in G\}. \] These data provide general notation only: the supplied record does not specify the field, polynomial algebra, class of groups, or permitted actions. A vector-space basis $\mathcal B$ of $A^G$ means a family such that every invariant polynomial has a unique expression as a finite $k$-linear combination of members of $\mathcal B$. The record does not specify whether the intended bases are taken in the whole ring, in individual homogeneous components, or in another specified subspace.

Research question, with unresolved qualifications. For invariant-polynomial rings under various group actions, characterize approximately orthogonal bases, and determine when such bases can simultaneously satisfy the additivity property referred to in the source, analogous to that of tensor cumulants.

Short English statement

The problem asks what approximately orthogonal bases of invariant polynomials look like for general group actions, and when they can also have an additivity property analogous to tensor cumulants. The supplied excerpt omits the pairing, approximation criterion, and referenced additivity identity, so a precise existence statement requires further source context.
