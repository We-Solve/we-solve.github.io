Problem 3.10 — Find a finitely presented subgroup H < Modg for which there are infinitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups…

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ be the mapping class group in the source's genus-$g$ surface convention. For $H\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$, let $\mathcal F(H)$ be its finite subgroups and identify $F,F'\in\mathcal F(H)$ when $F'=hFh^{-1}$ for some $h\in H$.

Problem 3.10 asks to find a finitely presented subgroup $H\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$ with \[ \#\left(\mathcal F(H)/H\text{-conjugacy}\right)=\infty. \] Finite presentation means a presentation with finitely many generators and relators. Conjugacy is taken inside $H$, not in the ambient mapping class group; the problem is not asking for infinitely many isomorphism types of finite subgroups. The excerpt leaves the genus range and whether one seeks an example in some genus or every specified genus unstated.

The source motivates this with Bridson's example [Br] in $\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{Z}})$. It suggests constructing an analogous subgroup in $\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})$ and pulling it back under the homological action $\pi:\operatorname{Mod}_g\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})$. Its phrase "injective on torsion" should be read with care: in the usual relevant range, torsion-freeness of the Torelli kernel implies injectivity on each finite subgroup, not necessarily injectivity on the entire set of torsion elements. The proposed strategy alone does not establish finite presentability of a pullback or the existence of suitable lifted finite subgroups.

The final extracted passage introduces a subsequent topic: the isomorphism problem for a collection $\mathcal S$ of finitely presented groups asks for an algorithm deciding, from two input presentations of members of $\mathcal S$, whether they define isomorphic groups. No additional isomorphism-problem demand is stated in this numbered item.

Short English statement

Find a finitely presented mapping-class subgroup with infinitely many conjugacy classes, within that subgroup, of finite subgroups. The suggested symplectic pullback strategy needs additional finiteness and lifting arguments.
