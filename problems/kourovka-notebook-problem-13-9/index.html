Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.9

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group. It is periodic if every element has finite order, and locally soluble if every finitely generated subgroup is soluble. A group is soluble if its derived series reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps. A group is nilpotent if it has a finite central series, and it is locally nilpotent if every finitely generated subgroup is nilpotent.

A group $K$ is Chernikov if it has a normal subgroup of finite index isomorphic to \[ \prod_{j=1}^{r} C_{p_j^\infty}, \] where $r\geq 0$ is finite, each $p_j$ is a prime, and $C_{p^\infty}$ is the quasicyclic $p$-group, isomorphic to the additive group ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/p]/{\mathbb{Z}}$. The empty product is trivial, so finite groups are included. For $H\leq G$, define \[ C_G(H)=\{g\in G:gh=hg\text{ for every }h\in H\}. \]

The question is whether, for every locally soluble periodic group $G$, the existence of a finite nilpotent subgroup $H\leq G$ such that $C_G(H)$ is Chernikov implies the existence of a finite normal series \[ \{1\}=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_n=G \] whose factors $G_{i+1}/G_i$ are locally nilpotent for every $0\leq i<n$.

Here the standard invariant-series convention is used: every $G_i$ is normal in $G$. The supplied excerpt does not explicitly distinguish this convention from requiring only $G_i\trianglelefteq G_{i+1}$; this terminology should be checked against the cited source. No uniform bound on $n$ is requested.

Short English statement

Must every locally soluble periodic group containing a finite nilpotent subgroup with Chernikov centralizer have a finite normal series with locally nilpotent factors? The normal-series convention requires source review. The registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
