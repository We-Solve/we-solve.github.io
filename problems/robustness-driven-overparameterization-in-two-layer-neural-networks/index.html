Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For scalar output, a two-layer network has the form $f(x)=b_0+\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\sigma(w_j^{\mathsf T}x+b_j)$ for a chosen activation $\sigma$. Interpolation of data $(x_i,y_i)$ means $f(x_i)=y_i$ for every $i$. On a specified domain $D$, with specified input and output norms, $\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in D,\,x\ne y}\|f(x)-f(y)\|/\|x-y\|$. A bound $\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le L$ controls output changes by $L$ times the input perturbation; any robustness conclusion must state these norms and the dataset assumptions.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Does interpolation by a two-layer neural network with few parameters necessarily force the represented function to have a large Lipschitz constant, thereby making overparameterization necessary for robustness?

Recovering the primary quantifiers

The report's short abstract points to Bubeck--Li--Nagaraj. Their primary paper, Conjecture 1, concerns random spherical or normalized Gaussian inputs, independent random sign labels, and the number $k$ of hidden neurons. It asks for a high-probability lower bound of order $\sqrt{n/k}$ on the sphere Lipschitz constant for interpolating networks with a Lipschitz activation. Thus neither arbitrary deterministic labels nor total parameter count can be substituted without justification. The original catalogue wording is preserved above, including its own warning about this scope. The July 2026 Shmalo primary abstract claims a result for continuous piecewise-linear activations with a logarithmic loss and arbitrary weights; that abstract was checked, but its proof was not audited here. It is not treated as a proof of the exact all-activation formulation. Our attempt studies whether interpolation geometry or a linear-algebra reduction can yield the desired width dependence.

The exact geometry-only obstruction

For distinct sample points $x_i$ and real labels $y_i$, put \[ L_0=\max_{i\ne j}\frac{|y_i-y_j|}{\|x_i-x_j\|} \] when there are at least two points, and set $L_0=0$ for one point. Every interpolant on a domain containing the samples has Lipschitz constant at least $L_0$, by its definition. This lower bound is best possible among unrestricted real-valued functions on the ambient Euclidean space: define \[ g(x)=\min_i\{y_i+L_0\|x-x_i\|\}. \] Each expression is $L_0$-Lipschitz by the triangle inequality. For any $x,z$, take an index minimizing at $z$ to get $g(x)\le g(z)+L_0\|x-z\|$; swap $x,z$ for the reverse inequality. At $x_j$ the $j$th expression equals $y_j$ and all others are at least $y_j$ by the definition of $L_0$. Thus $g(x_j)=y_j$ and its Lipschitz constant is exactly $L_0$ when $L_0>0$. In particular, pairwise distances at least one and sign labels imply $L_0\le2$, regardless of $n$. A lower bound growing with $n/k$ therefore needs the network architecture; sample separation alone cannot prove it. The construction supplies no bound on the number of hidden neurons representing $g$.

A full restricted lower bound with orthogonal data

Here is the linear-algebra route in a setting where it closes. Let $n\le d$, take $x_i=e_i$, and let $y_i\in\{-1,1\}$. Among all affine functions $f(x)=w\cdot x+b$ interpolating these data, the smallest Lipschitz constant on the unit sphere is \[ L_{\rm aff}=\left(n-\frac{S^2}{n}\right)^{1/2}, \qquad S=\sum_{i=1}^n y_i. \] Indeed, interpolation forces $w_i=y_i-b$ for $i\le n$. Coordinates beyond $n$ can only increase the norm, and \[ \sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-b)^2 =n-S^2/n+n(b-S/n)^2. \] Choose $b=S/n$ and all remaining coordinates zero to attain the minimum. The sphere Lipschitz constant of an affine function is $\|w\|$: Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound, and the antipodal points $w/\|w\|,-w/\|w\|$ give equality when $w\ne0$. The zero case is immediate, also covering $d=1$.

For independent uniform sign labels, \[ \Pr\{|S|>n/2\}\le2e^{-n/8}. \] For completeness, $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S}=(\cosh\lambda)^n$. The function $h(\lambda)=\log\cosh\lambda$ has $h(0)=h'(0)=0$ and $h''(\lambda)\le1$, so $h(\lambda)\le\lambda^2/2$. Markov's inequality gives $\Pr(S>n/2)\le\exp(-\lambda n/2+n\lambda^2/2)$; choose $\lambda=1/2$ and apply the same argument to $-S$. Hence, outside that exceptional event, $L_{\rm aff}\ge\sqrt{3n/4}$. This is a random-label theorem for a deterministic orthogonal design and affine functions, not the full generic-input, arbitrary-width assertion.

More generally, if $X$ has rows $x_i$ and an affine interpolant exists, then \[ \|w\|\ge\frac{\min_b\|y-b\boldsymbol{1}\|}{\|X\|_{\rm op}} \] provided $\|X\|_{\rm op}>0$. This follows from $Xw=y-b\boldsymbol{1}$ and the definition of the operator norm. For nonlinear two-layer networks the equation has no single vector $w$ representing the entire function, so this step does not extend by replacing $w$ with the collection of hidden weights.

Two failed shortcuts, checked explicitly

An arbitrary-data version cannot hold: the constant function one is represented by one ReLU neuron with zero input weight and bias one, fits any number of samples labelled one, and has Lipschitz constant zero. Even nonconstant low-Lipschitz affine labels admit two ReLU units, since $\max(t,0)-\max(-t,0)=t$. These examples do not contradict the primary random-label high-probability statement.

Nor does a lower bound on a weight-norm upper estimate imply a lower bound on the realized function. In one dimension and for any $M>0$, \[ M\max(x,0)-M\max(x,0)+\max(x,0)=\max(x,0). \] The usual sum-of-absolute-weight estimate is $2M+1$ while the actual global Lipschitz constant is one. Cancellation makes the reverse inference invalid. Eliminating this exact redundant pair does not prove that arbitrary networks possess a controlled minimal representation; that would be an additional theorem.

Audit and remaining gap

The adversarial audit separated neuron count from parameter count, sphere from whole-space Lipschitz constants, a random-label orthogonal design from random spherical inputs, and an upper norm estimate from the function's actual Lipschitz constant. The exact extension formula shows why the attempted geometry-only argument cannot supply the width factor. The affine argument cannot handle general hidden activations. No numerical experiments or theorem prover were used. These known elementary mechanisms are not credited as original-target progress, and no current-status claim stronger than the inspected primary abstracts is made.
