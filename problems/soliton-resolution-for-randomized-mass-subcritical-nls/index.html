Math-4415

Soliton Resolution for Randomized Mass-Subcritical NLS

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d$ denote the spatial dimension, implicitly a positive integer in the notation ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, and let \[ \frac{2}{d}<p<\frac{4}{d},\qquad \phi\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d). \] Here $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ denotes the space of square-integrable complex-valued functions, with norm $\|f\|_2=(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}|f(x)|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x)^{1/2}$, and $\Delta=\sum_{\ell=1}^d\partial_{x_\ell}^2$ is the spatial Laplacian. The source denotes a randomization of $\phi$ by $\phi^\omega$, but does not specify the randomization or its probability space.

The supplied question asks whether, for every such $p$ and $\phi$, for almost every $\omega$, the solution $u=u^\omega$ of \[ \begin{cases} i\partial_tu-\Delta u=|u|^pu, & x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d,\\ u(0,x)=\phi^\omega(x) \end{cases} \] admits solitons of the form \[ u_j(t,x)=e^{it a_j+i x\cdot k_j}\phi_j(x-c_jt) \] and a free term, written in the source as $e^{-it\Delta}\phi$, such that \[ \lim_{t\to+\infty} \left\|u(t,\cdot)-\sum_j u_j(t,\cdot)-e^{-it\Delta}\phi\right\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d)}=0. \] The operator $e^{-it\Delta}$ denotes free evolution for $i\partial_tv-\Delta v=0$.

Unresolved qualifications. The displayed assertion preserves the source notation, including its use of the same $\phi$ for the datum before randomization and for the free profile. It is unclear whether that equality is intended or whether a separately quantified asymptotic profile was intended. The probability law, solution concept, forward global existence assumptions, number of solitons, admissible parameter and profile classes, and any separation conditions are unspecified. If the sum is infinite, its convergence must also be specified. The source calls the $u_j$ solitons but gives no profile equation or parameter compatibility conditions defining that class. Consequently this is the supplied formulation with its gaps exposed, rather than a fully determined mathematical assertion.

Short English statement

For mass-subcritical NLS with $2/d<p<4/d$, the question asks whether a solution from randomized $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ data almost surely approaches a sum of traveling solitons and a free solution in $L^2$ as $t\to+\infty$. The randomization, solution and soliton classes, size of the sum, and identity of the free profile require clarification.
