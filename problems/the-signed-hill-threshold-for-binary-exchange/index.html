The signed Hill threshold for binary exchange

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a planar orbit write $C$ for scalar angular momentum; in spatial motion use its magnitude in $C^2E$. Let $L$ be the corresponding $C^2E$ value of the circular Euler collinear relative equilibrium for the stated masses. An exchange orbit connects one asymptotic binary-plus-escaping-body arrangement to another with a different binary. Hill stability here prohibits that exchange. A stable (respectively unstable) manifold consists of points tending to the invariant set in forward (respectively backward) time. A connecting chain must join the indicated invariant sets on compatible conserved-quantity levels.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For an exchange orbit in which $m_3$ escapes from the binary $\{m_1,m_2\}$ as $t\to-\infty$ and $m_2$ escapes from $\{m_1,m_3\}$ as $t\to+\infty$, how close to $1$ can $C^2E/L$ be, with $L=C^2E$ for circular Euler motion? The related question in the remarks asks whether a chain between the asymptotic binary invariant sets $P_{12},P_{13}$ can be constructed via the normally hyperbolic Euler invariant sphere $S^3_{\mathrm{Euler}}$, using suitable intersections of stable and unstable manifolds.

An energy-angular-momentum necessary inequality.
Fix center of mass zero and let $I=\sum m_i|q_i|^2$, $T=\tfrac12\sum m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $U>0$. Cauchy--Schwarz for angular momentum gives $C^2\le2IT$ in either the planar scalar or spatial-magnitude convention. Since $E=T-U$, \[E\ge C^2/(2I)-U. \] For a fixed shape with $I=r^2$ and $U=U_s/r$, $U_s>0$, the right side is $C^2/(2r^2)-U_s/r$. If $C\ne0$, completing the square in $1/r$ gives its minimum $-U_s^2/(2C^2)$, hence every configuration on that shape obeys \[C^2E\ge-U_s^2/2. \] Equality requires both the minimizing scale $r=C^2/U_s$ and purely rotational velocity saturating the angular-momentum inequality. A rotating central configuration is the relevant dynamical situation, but the inequality alone does not prove it is reached.
Sign and scaling checks.
Newtonian dilation $q(t)\mapsto a q(t/a^{3/2})$ sends $E\mapsto E/a$, $C\mapsto a^{1/2}C$, leaving $C^2E$ invariant. When the Euler reference $L<0$, dividing an inequality by $L$ reverses its direction. The case $C=0$ must be treated separately and cannot use the scale minimizer above.
Gap.
A necessary allowed-shape bound does not produce an exchange orbit or a chain through the Euler invariant sphere. The first missing step is proving the required stable/unstable intersections on matching conserved levels and following them without collision or escape into the wrong channel. No sharp approach to ratio one is established, and the sign-sensitive Hill-stability context is not promoted to a new sufficiency theorem.
