Fixation Times and Consensus in Majority Dynamics

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "For majority dynamics on a given graph, if fixation occurs, how quickly is the final configuration reached, and is unanimity reached or do both opinions survive?"

A precise framework for interpreting this question is as follows, conditional on specifying the missing model data. Let $G=(V,E)$ be the given graph, and let $\sigma_t\in\{0,1\}^{V}$ be its binary opinion configuration at time $t\in\mathcal T$, where $\mathcal T$ is the chosen discrete or continuous nonnegative time axis. The source does not specify the graph class, the initial configuration or its law, the update schedule, the treatment of ties, or the source of any randomness. These choices must be supplied before "majority dynamics" determines an evolution; in particular, no neighborhood-majority rule for infinite neighborhoods is specified.

Define vertexwise fixation by \[ \forall v\in V\;\exists t_v\in\mathcal T\;\exists a_v\in\{0,1\}\;\forall s\in\mathcal T,\quad s\ge t_v\Longrightarrow\sigma_s(v)=a_v. \] On this event, define the limiting configuration by $\sigma_\infty(v)=a_v$, and put \[ S=\{t\in\mathcal T:\ \sigma_s=\sigma_\infty\text{ for every }s\in\mathcal T\text{ with }s\ge t\}, \qquad T_{\mathrm{stab}}=\inf S, \] with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. A final configuration is reached and retained at a finite time precisely when $S\ne\varnothing$. In continuous time, the infimum need not itself belong to $S$ without additional assumptions. On an infinite graph, vertexwise fixation does not by its definition imply a common finite stabilization time.

For a vertexwise fixed trajectory, unanimity means \[ \exists a\in\{0,1\}\;\forall v\in V,\quad\sigma_\infty(v)=a, \] whereas coexistence in the limiting configuration means \[ \{v\in V:\sigma_\infty(v)=0\}\ne\varnothing \quad\text{and}\quad \{v\in V:\sigma_\infty(v)=1\}\ne\varnothing. \] These definitions make one possible meaning of "both opinions survive" explicit; the source does not specify whether survival instead requires infinitely many vertices, positive density, or persistence during the evolution.

Statement to be made precise. For majority dynamics on the given graph, conditional on fixation, determine the speed at which the final configuration is reached and characterize whether the eventual outcome is unanimity or survival of both opinions. Under the definitions above, this asks for quantitative information about $T_{\mathrm{stab}}$ and criteria for the two displayed limiting outcomes. The source does not determine whether the requested time information is a deterministic bound, an expectation, a tail estimate, an asymptotic order, or a collection of vertexwise stabilization times. Nor does it specify quantification over initial configurations or a probabilistic mode of assertion. Consequently, this is a conditional formulation of the research question, not a uniquely specified conjecture. The background also mentions median dynamics, but supplies no median-dynamics statement to formalize.

Short English statement

For a specified graph and majority-update model, ask how long stabilization takes when fixation occurs, and whether the limiting opinions are unanimous or coexist. The supplied record leaves the update rule, initial data, graph class, meaning of fixation and survival, and requested type of time estimate unresolved.
