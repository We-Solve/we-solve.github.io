Math-1563

One-sided lifting and polarized minimal counterexamples for Sullivan-3

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D$ be any nonempty finite simple directed graph without loops or digons, where a digon is a pair $u\to v$, $v\to u$. Write $d^+(v)$ and $d^-(v)$ for the outdegree and indegree of a vertex $v$. Let $N_2^+(v)$ be the set of vertices $w$ for which there is a directed path $v\to u\to w$, but no arc $v\to w$, with $w\ne v$. Equivalently, the shortest directed distance from $v$ to $w$ is exactly two. Several paths with endpoint $w$ contribute only one member to this set.

The question is whether there must exist a vertex $v$ satisfying \[ |N_2^+(v)|+d^+(v)\ge2\min\{d^-(v),d^+(v)\}. \] If $d^-(v)\ge d^+(v)$, this is the inequality $|N_2^+(v)|\ge d^+(v)$. If $d^-(v)\le d^+(v)$, it is instead $|N_2^+(v)|\ge2d^-(v)-d^+(v)$. The graph need only have one vertex satisfying the appropriate inequality for its own degrees. These two cases explain the role of the minimum without imposing a global relation between in- and outdegrees.

No assumptions on the underlying undirected graph, beyond simplicity, are made. A source or sink already satisfies the desired inequality. The statement is Conjecture 6.7 of the source, independently of its motivating implications from stronger rainbow or second-neighbourhood conjectures. Its target is this particular mixed-degree bound.

Short English statement

Is there always a vertex whose first two outgoing layers have size at least twice the smaller of its indegree and outdegree?

Seymour second neighborhoods: The paper claims that every finite nonempty oriented graph has a vertex whose second out-neighborhood is at least as large as its first. The second neighborhood excludes vertices already reachable in one step.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For a nonempty finite oriented graph $D$, with no loops or oppositely directed pair of edges, let $N_1^+(v)$ be the out-neighbors and $N_2^+(v)$ the vertices at directed distance exactly two. The claimed theorem gives \[\exists v\in V(D):\quad|N_1^+(v)|\le|N_2^+(v)|.\] For triangle-free $D$ a corollary yields a vertex with $d^+(v)\le|Z(v)|$ and $n-1\ge2d^+(v)+d^-(v)$, where $Z(v)$ consists of non-neighbors excluding $v$. Hence $\delta^+\ge n/3$ and $\delta^-\ge n/3$ force a directed triangle; a digraph regular in both directions with degree $r$ and girth four has $n\ge3r+1$. These consequences require the specified in-degree conditions and do not give the full out-degree-only Caccetta--Häggkvist assertion.

Outline of the argument

Seymour second neighborhoods: A smallest counterexample is pruned to yield strict neighborhood inequalities for all proper vertex sets. Replacing vertices by transitive tournaments strengthens these to a discrete concavity inequality. Extremal families of ordered pairs and an augmented bipartite matching are then controlled by two factorizations of one matrix, contradicting that inequality.
