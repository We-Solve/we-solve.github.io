Deformations of Canonical Projective Structures on Hyperbolic Three-Manifolds

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "Can the canonical projective structure on a hyperbolic three-manifold be nontrivially deformed?"

A hyperbolic three-manifold is a smooth three-dimensional manifold $M$ with a Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$. The record does not specify completeness, finite volume, compactness, orientability, or boundary conditions.

Under the conventional interpretation of "projective" as real projective, a projective structure on $M$ is a maximal atlas of charts into ${\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{P}^{3}$ whose transition maps are restrictions of elements of $\mathrm{PGL}(4,{\mathbb{R}})$. The Klein model identifies hyperbolic three-space with the interior of a projective ball. Its isometries act by projective transformations, so the hyperbolic charts induce a canonical real projective structure, denoted $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}$.

One precise interpretation of local nontrivial deformation uses marked projective structures: two structures on the same smooth manifold are equivalent if a diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity pulls one back to the other. With this convention, the deformation question for a specified $M$ asks whether there exist $\varepsilon>0$ and a smooth family $(\mathcal P_t)_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}$ of real projective structures on $M$ such that \[ \mathcal P_0=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}, \qquad \forall\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)\ \exists t\in(-\delta,\delta) \quad [\mathcal P_t]\ne[\mathcal P_{\mathrm{hyp}}]. \] Here brackets denote equivalence under the stated marking convention, and smoothness means smooth dependence of the projective charts on the parameter, locally in the manifold and parameter.

This is a conditional formulation, not a uniquely determined reading of the record. The source does not specify the projective category, the equivalence relation, the regularity of deformations, or whether actual deformations or only infinitesimal deformations are intended. It also does not say whether the question asks for the existence of some hyperbolic three-manifold with this property, asks about every manifold in a specified class, or seeks a criterion for a given manifold. Any required preservation of convexity, completeness, end geometry, cusp data, or boundary data remains unspecified.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the projective geometry induced by a hyperbolic metric on a three-manifold can vary nontrivially. A conventional real projective interpretation is given above, but the class of manifolds, the quantifier over them, and the permitted deformations require clarification.
