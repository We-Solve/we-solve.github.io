Scalar Curvature Question [?55]: 18

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source calls this Conjecture 18, the Interior Hemi-Spherical Area Inequality. Let $X$ be a compact Riemannian $n$-manifold with boundary and scalar curvature \[ \operatorname{Sc}_X\geq\operatorname{Sc}_{S^n}=n(n-1), \] where the comparison sphere is unit round. It asserts a compact-notation bound, printed as "co-s+ar"$(X^{-r})\leq2\pi$ for every $r\geq\pi/2$, and explains that no strictly area-decreasing proper map \[ f:X^{-r}\longrightarrow S^n_+,\qquad \deg f\ne0, \] exists when $r>\pi/2$. The exact invariant notation is not defined in the record; the value $2\pi$ is retained as printed, without guessing its normalization.

The source's $r$-interior refers to points at least $r$ from metric incompleteness: in an incomplete manifold a point belongs if its closed $r'$-balls are compact for every $r'<r$. For a compact manifold with boundary this must be interpreted through its interior, or via distance to the boundary, rather than compactness of balls in the already compact space including its boundary. The record does not specify whether $X^{-r}$ uses a strict or non-strict distance cutoff, an induced metric, or a smooth regularized domain.

For a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds of dimension at least two, a customary strictly area-decreasing condition is \[ \|\Lambda^2D_xf\|<1\qquad\text{at every }x, \] meaning strict contraction of every tangent two-dimensional area. A uniform bound by one fixed constant below one is stronger and is not stated. Proper means inverse images of compact sets are compact. Nonzero degree requires a specified orientation or coefficient convention and an appropriate proper-map or relative-boundary setup.

Whether $S^n_+$ denotes an open or closed hemisphere is missing and matters: compact-domain proper maps to a closed hemisphere need boundary/relative-degree conditions, while the open target uses a different proper-degree setting. The no-map formulation has the strict threshold $r>\pi/2$; its endpoint must not be inferred from the unexplained invariant bound at $r=\pi/2$. The source says the situation is unclear even for domains in closed spin manifolds, then ends at "For instance:" without the promised example.

Short English statement

At scalar curvature at least the unit sphere's, can a sufficiently deep interior map properly with nonzero degree to a hemisphere while strictly shrinking area? The source forbids this for $r>\pi/2$, with unresolved invariant, hemisphere and degree conventions.
