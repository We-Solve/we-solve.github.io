Isomorphism Data in Correspondence Two-Categories

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For context, let $A$ and $B$ be $C^*$-algebras. In a standard convention, an $A$-$B$ correspondence is a right Hilbert $B$-module $E$, together with a $*$-homomorphism $\varphi_E\colon A\to\mathcal L_B(E)$, where $\mathcal L_B(E)$ denotes the algebra of adjointable $B$-linear operators on $E$. Some conventions additionally require nondegeneracy, namely $\overline{\operatorname{span}(\varphi_E(A)E)}=E$; the supplied record does not specify this requirement. An isomorphism between two such correspondences $E$ and $F$ is a surjective $B$-linear map $U\colon E\to F$ satisfying \[ \langle U\xi,U\eta\rangle_B=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_B, \qquad U\varphi_E(a)\xi=\varphi_F(a)U\xi \quad(a\in A,\ \xi,\eta\in E). \] In the usual correspondence framework, objects are $C^*$-algebras, $1$-morphisms are correspondences, and composition uses the interior tensor product: an $A$-$B$ correspondence $E$ and a $B$-$C$ correspondence $F$ compose to $E\otimes_B F$. The supplied record does not specify the permitted $2$-morphisms. The ordinary tensor-product construction has associativity and unit identifications up to canonical isomorphism, so the term "$2$-category" may refer to a bicategory or a chosen strictification.

Source question. Why is it useful, in the correspondence $2$-category of $C^*$-algebras, to retain individual correspondences and isomorphisms between them instead of replacing correspondences by their isomorphism classes?

This is a conceptual request for an explanation. The supplied record gives no quantified conjecture, particular construction, universal property, or criterion of usefulness to establish. The definitions above provide a standard interpretive framework; they do not resolve the unspecified conventions or turn the question into a theorem.

Short English statement

The question asks why a correspondence $2$-category should retain isomorphic $C^*$-algebra correspondences as separate $1$-morphisms connected by isomorphisms. The intended benefit and precise categorical conventions are unspecified, so this remains a conceptual question requiring human review.
