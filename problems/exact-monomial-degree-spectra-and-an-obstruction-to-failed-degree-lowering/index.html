Math-1701

Exact monomial degree spectra and an obstruction to failed degree lowering

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Han Peters's source problem takes a rational proper map $R:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N$, $n\ge3$, of degree $d=d_0(R)$ and asks: is \[ d\le\frac{N-1}{n-1}? \] Does the same bound hold under the additional assumption $n\ge2d^2+2d$, and which maps attain equality? It records an affirmative monomial result for the latter question and, for $n=2$, the sharp monomial bound $d\le2N-3$. A further question asks, separately in the rational, polynomial, or monomial class, whether any degree $d>1$ occurring at fixed $(n,N)$ implies existence of a map in the same class of degree $d-1$. This asks for another map, not a deformation of the original map.

Use the usual rational degree $\max\{\deg p_i,\deg q\}$ after common factors are removed from $R=p/q$; the source does not otherwise define $d_0$. A monomial map has coordinates constant multiples of holomorphic monomials. The aid explicitly allows zero target coordinates, so the map need not be linearly full.

For $N\ge n$ its reported monomial degree spectrum is \[ \{d_0(f):f:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N\text{ proper monomial}\} =\begin{cases} \{1,\ldots,2N-3\},&n=2,\\ \{1,\ldots,\lfloor(N-1)/(n-1)\rfloor\},&n\ge3. \end{cases} \] The aid attributes the upper bounds to D'Angelo--Kos--Riehl and Lebl--Peters, and combines them with an explicit generalized Whitney construction and zero-coordinate padding to realize every integer in the intervals. The full construction and precise published bound hypotheses are not supplied here. Under that convention the degree-lowering question is affirmative for all proper monomial maps, at every occurring $d>1$.

Let $n\ge3$ and $D=\lfloor(N-1)/(n-1)\rfloor$. If a rational or polynomial map fails degree lowering, then \[ d\ge D+2. \] Indeed if $d-1\le D$, a monomial map supplies the requested lower-degree example in either broader class. This is a necessary obstruction to failure, not an existence theorem for counterexamples at higher degree. The interval theorem does not prove the full rational degree bound, settle its large-$n$ variant for all rational maps, or classify every equality map. Requiring linearly full targets would be a separate version not covered by the stated padding argument.

Short English statement

The aid reports exact monomial degree intervals when zero target coordinates are allowed, which gives monomial degree lowering. Any rational or polynomial failure must therefore occur at least two integers above the monomial endpoint. The general rational bounds and classification of equality maps remain separate questions.
