Structure and Asymptotics of Finite Group Characters

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record presents four broad research questions rather than a conjecture with specified hypotheses and quantifiers. Its title refers to finite group characters, but the quoted statement does not specify a class of groups, representations, or characters. The following definitions provide a conditional finite-group interpretation; they do not resolve the missing scope.

Let $G$ be a finite group. For a finite-dimensional complex representation $V$ of $G$, its character is the function \[ \chi_V\colon G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\qquad \chi_V(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid V). \] Write $\operatorname{Irr}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(G)$ for the set of irreducible complex characters of $G$. If $V$ and $W$ are complex representations, give $V\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}W$ the diagonal action $g(v\otimes w)=(gv)\otimes(gw)$. Its character is the pointwise product $\chi_V\chi_W$. Denote its irreducible multiplicities by the nonnegative integers $m_{\chi_V,\chi_W}^{\psi}$ in \[ \chi_V\chi_W=\sum_{\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(G)}m_{\chi_V,\chi_W}^{\psi}\,\psi. \] For a complex character $\chi$, define its vanishing set by \[ Z_G(\chi)=\{g\in G:\chi(g)=0\}. \] For a prime $p$, a field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and a finite-dimensional $kG$-module $M$, its representation dimension is $\dim_k M$. If "modular character" means a Brauer character $\varphi_M$ in an appropriate splitting-field setting, its domain consists of the $p$-regular elements, namely those whose orders are not divisible by $p$, and its degree is $\varphi_M(1)=\dim_k M$. The source does not specify this interpretation or these field hypotheses.

With these qualifications, the supplied research questions are:

  1. Describe the decomposition of the tensor product of a pair of complex characters, or equivalently determine its irreducible multiplicities in the intended setting.
  2. Understand the vanishing sets of complex characters.
  3. Determine the asymptotic behaviour of complex characters.
  4. Determine the dimensions associated with modular characters.
The source leaves unspecified whether the characters are irreducible, which groups or families are intended, what form of description is sought, which parameters tend to a limit, how character values are normalized, and which modular representations and characteristics are under consideration. Consequently, these questions cannot be converted from the supplied evidence into a single fully quantified mathematical assertion. In particular, no asymptotic limit, estimate, or modular dimension formula is asserted here.

Short English statement

The record asks how tensor products of complex characters decompose, where characters vanish, how characters behave asymptotically, and what dimensions occur for modular characters. It supplies a research programme whose group families, limiting regimes, and modular hypotheses remain unspecified. The finite-group definitions above are a conditional interpretation motivated by the supplied title. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
