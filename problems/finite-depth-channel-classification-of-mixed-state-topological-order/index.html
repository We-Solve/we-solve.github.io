Finite-Depth Channel Classification of Mixed-State Topological Order

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A quantum channel is a completely positive trace-preserving map on states. A finite-depth local channel is a composition of a bounded number of layers of bounded-range operations, uniformly in the system size. Mixed-state topological order concerns long-range structures not removable by the allowed operations. Anyons and their braiding provide potential obstructions.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Classify mixed-state topological orders under finite-depth quantum channels: given two states, determine whether a finite-depth channel maps one to the other, and characterize the obstructions supplied by anyons and nontrivial braiding.
Scope and source qualification.
The permitted ancillary systems and direction of channel conversion must be fixed. One-way conversion is not automatically an equivalence relation.

Short English statement

Decide which mixed topological states can be connected by finite-depth local channels and characterize their anyonic obstructions.

A depth-one irreversible conversion.
On each finite-dimensional site define the reset channel $\mathcal R(\rho)=|0\rangle\langle0|\operatorname{Tr}\rho$. Its Kraus operators $K_j=|0\rangle\langle j|$ satisfy $\sum_jK_j^*K_j=I$, proving complete positivity and trace preservation. Tensoring resets across sites maps every input state to the product vacuum in one local layer. Thus any supposed obstruction to arbitrary one-way conversion cannot prohibit conversion to that product state under this operation class.
The reverse direction has an exact locality obstruction.
Assume circuits have depth $D$, range at most $r$, disjoint gates within each layer and initially uncorrelated local ancillas. Backward causal neighborhoods of single-site observables at separation greater than $2Dr$ are disjoint. Pulling their product backwards through the local channels gives a product of operators on these disjoint input neighborhoods: no gate has support intersecting both cones. On a product input the expectation therefore factorizes. The output has zero connected correlation at such separations.
A mixed-state countercheck and source gap.
The classical mixed state $\rho_n=(|0\cdots0\rangle\langle0\cdots0|+|1\cdots1\rangle\langle1\cdots1|)/2$ has $\langle Z_i\rangle=0$, $\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle=1$ for distinct sites. It cannot be prepared exactly from product inputs by a uniformly bounded $D,r$ on growing lattices, though it resets to a product state in depth one. This proves directional asymmetry without asserting this state has anyonic topological order. The source task still requires a classification and braiding obstructions for the actual mixed topological states and allowed ancillas. Correlated shared ancillas or approximate preparation require separate estimates and are not covered here.
