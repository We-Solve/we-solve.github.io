The Hexagonal Conjecture for Spectral Minimal Partitions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^2=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^3:\lVert x\rVert=1\}$ carry its standard induced Riemannian metric, with area $4\pi$. For a nonempty open set $D\subseteq S^2$, define its first Dirichlet eigenvalue variationally by \[ \lambda_1(D)=\inf_{0\ne u\in C_c^\infty(D)}\frac{\int_D |\nabla_{S^2}u|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A}{\int_D |u|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A}. \] Here the gradient and area measure are those of the standard sphere; the variational formulation imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions without requiring a smooth boundary.

For each integer $k\ge1$, let $\mathfrak P_k(S^2)$ denote the intended class of admissible $k$-partitions. Writing a partition as $\mathcal D=(D_1,\ldots,D_k)$, with nonempty pairwise disjoint open cells, set \[ \mathcal L_k(S^2)=\inf_{\mathcal D\in\mathfrak P_k(S^2)}\max_{1\le i\le k}\lambda_1(D_i),\qquad \mathcal L_{k,1}(S^2)=\inf_{\mathcal D\in\mathfrak P_k(S^2)}\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_1(D_i). \] Unresolved admissibility qualification. The supplied record does not define the partition class, including its coverage condition, whether cells must be connected, or any boundary regularity or capacity conditions. The open-cell description above gives the setting for these formulas, but its agreement with the intended source convention requires review. The statement below is consequently conditional on identifying that convention; no equivalence between alternative partition classes is asserted.

Let $\operatorname{Hexa}_1\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be the interior of a regular Euclidean hexagon of area $1$, and let $\lambda_1(\operatorname{Hexa}_1)$ be its first Dirichlet eigenvalue for the Euclidean Laplacian, defined by the analogous Rayleigh quotient.

Hexagonal asymptotic conjecture, supplied corrected formulation. For the intended admissible partition classes, do both limits exist and satisfy \[ \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal L_k(S^2)}{k} =\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal L_{k,1}(S^2)}{k} =\frac{\lambda_1(\operatorname{Hexa}_1)}{4\pi}? \] The limits are taken over positive integers. Equivalently, multiplying each normalized energy by $\operatorname{Area}(S^2)=4\pi$ gives the proposed limit $\lambda_1(\operatorname{Hexa}_1)$.

Normalization qualification. The supplied original statement instead gives $\lambda_1(\operatorname{Hexa}_1)$ as the limit without the factor $1/(4\pi)$. The current statement explicitly adopts the corrected version supplied in the record. Its verification note attributes the discrepancy to an omitted area factor in the report's displayed conjecture and cites the preceding scale-invariant expression and a later general-domain formulation. That explanation is supplied source verification, not independent verification performed for this draft.

Short English statement

For partitions of the standard unit sphere into $k$ admissible cells, minimize either the largest first Dirichlet eigenvalue or their arithmetic mean. The supplied corrected conjecture says that each minimum, divided by $k$, tends to the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of a unit-area planar regular hexagon divided by $4\pi$. The precise admissible partition class remains unspecified, and the correction to the original normalization requires source review.
