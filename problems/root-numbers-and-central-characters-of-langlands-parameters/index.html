Root Numbers and Central Characters of Langlands Parameters

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a non-Archimedean local field and let $G$ be a reductive algebraic group over $k$; write $G(k)$ for its group of $k$-rational points. Let $\pi$ be an essentially discrete-series representation of $G(k)$, and let $\phi$ be its Langlands parameter, in the formal-degree setting intended by the source report.

The supplied record uses $\operatorname{Ad}_G$ for the adjoint representation occurring in that setting, $\omega$ for the orthogonal root-number invariant, $\phi_{\operatorname{prin}}$ for the principal parameter, $z_{\operatorname{Ad}_G}$ for the associated central involution, and $\chi_\phi$ for the central character attached to $\phi$. These descriptions retain the source notation; their precise constructions and conventions are not supplied.

Conjectural identity (reported as Conjecture 1.2). For every such $k$, $G$, and $\phi$, subject to the hypotheses and conventions of the report's preceding formal-degree setting, prove that \[ \frac{\omega\left(\operatorname{Ad}_G\circ\phi\right)} {\omega\left(\operatorname{Ad}_G\circ\phi_{\operatorname{prin}}\right)} =\chi_\phi\left(z_{\operatorname{Ad}_G}\right). \]

Unresolved qualifications. The original statement explicitly refers to a preceding formal-degree setting that is absent from the supplied record. In particular, the record does not specify the precise parameter category, the adjoint representation and its underlying space, the definition and normalization of $\omega$, the construction of the principal parameter, or the group containing $z_{\operatorname{Ad}_G}$ on which $\chi_\phi$ is evaluated. It also does not state whether additional restrictions on $k$, $G$, or $\pi$ are imposed there. Consequently, the display preserves the supplied conjecture but does not supply a complete standalone formulation without recovering those conventions and hypotheses.

Short English statement

The conjecture equates a ratio of orthogonal root numbers for a discrete-series Langlands parameter and the principal parameter with a central-character value at an associated central involution. The source's preceding formal-degree setting is needed to define these quantities precisely. The supplied partially solved label and literature assessment are dated curation, not independently verified conclusions about this identity.
