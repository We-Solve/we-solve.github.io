Math-12908

Almost spherical rank under non-collapsed convergence

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied source correction is $(M_i^n,g_i)$ and the time bound $\pi+1/i$, rather than the OCR fragments $M_{ni}$ and "$\pi+1>i$." Let these be complete connected boundaryless $n$-manifolds, in the intended setting, with \[ |\sec_{g_i}|\leq1, \] converging noncollapsed in Gromov--Hausdorff topology to $X$. Noncollapse is to preserve dimension $n$, for example through the appropriate uniform local volume lower bound. Assume every unit-speed geodesic from every starting point has a conjugate point to that starting point before $L_i=\pi+1/i$. The question is whether $X$ has a rigidity property, without specifying which classification or regularity conclusion is intended.

The aid gives unconditional comparison consequences. By Rauch comparison the first conjugate time satisfies \[ \pi\leq t_{\mathrm{conj}}(\gamma)<\pi+1/i. \] Minimizing geodesics cannot continue past that time, so completeness gives compactness and \[ \operatorname{diam}(M_i)<\pi+1/i,\qquad \operatorname{diam}(X)\leq\pi. \] Completeness and absence of boundary are needed for this global use of minimizing geodesics.

Its quantitative index-form statement uses any unit-speed geodesic segment of length $L>\pi$ with a conjugate point before $L$ and sectional curvature at most $1$. There is a normal field $V\in H_0^1([0,L];\gamma^\perp)$ normalized by $\int_0^L|V|^2dt=1$ with nonpositive index form. Set \[ E(V)=\int_0^L|D_tV|^2dt-\frac{\pi^2}{L^2},\qquad Q(V)=\int_0^L\left(|V|^2- \langle R(V,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma,V\rangle\right)dt. \] The first Dirichlet-eigenvalue inequality and curvature bound give $E(V),Q(V)\geq0$, and the aid's estimate is \[ E(V)+Q(V)\leq1-\frac{\pi^2}{L^2}. \] For $L=L_i$, this is $O(1/i)$. The field therefore has small first-mode error and small integrated curvature-one deficit; this is an averaged direction estimate, not a pointwise bound on every normal plane.

The stronger classification in the aid is conditional on convergence sufficient to pass Jacobi equations, for example $C^2$ convergence after diffeomorphic identifications to a smooth complete limit. Then every limiting geodesic has first conjugate time $\pi$, and equality in the curvature comparison supplies a parallel normal direction of curvature $1$, or positive spherical rank. The aid invokes Shankar--Spatzier--Wilking to identify the universal cover with a compact rank-one symmetric space in the corresponding normalization. The exact rank-rigidity hypotheses and reference require verification. Gromov--Hausdorff convergence with the stated curvature and noncollapse bounds is not silently replaced by this stronger convergence, so the original limit question is not resolved by that conditional step.

Short English statement

If every geodesic has a conjugate point before $\pi+1/i$ under $|\sec|\leq1$, what rigidity does a noncollapsed limit have? The supplied aid gives a diameter bound and an averaged almost-spherical Jacobi direction. A rank-one symmetric universal-cover conclusion is conditional on enough regularity to pass the Jacobi equations, not asserted from Gromov--Hausdorff convergence alone.
