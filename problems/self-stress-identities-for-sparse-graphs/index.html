Self-Stress Identities for Sparse Graphs

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the usual simple wheel graph $W_n$, with $n\geq 4$, the vertices consist of a cycle on $n-1$ vertices and one additional vertex joined to every cycle vertex. Thus $|V(W_n)|=n$ and $|E(W_n)|=2n-2$. This describes the standard simple-wheel convention; the supplied record does not explicitly specify its admissible values of $n$ or graph conventions for the proposed generalization.

A self-stress ordinarily refers to a geometric framework, not just an abstract graph. For example, for a framework $p:V(G)\to{\mathbb{R}}^d$, a real equilibrium self-stress is an assignment $\omega:E(G)\to{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying \[ \sum_{j:\,ij\in E(G)}\omega_{ij}(p_j-p_i)=0 \qquad\text{for every }i\in V(G). \] This is a conventional definition supplied to identify the missing geometric data. The record does not specify that this is the intended convention, the dimension $d$, the placement $p$, or its required properties. For scalar edge assignments $\omega=(\omega_{ij})$ and $f=(f_{ij})$ over a common field, write their edge pairing as \[ \langle\omega,f\rangle_{E(G)} :=\sum_{ij\in E(G)}\omega_{ij}f_{ij}, \] where each undirected edge is counted once. The record supplies no scalar field or definition of $f_{ij}$.

The supplied wheel assertion is that the intended self-stress and edge function satisfy \[ \langle\omega,f\rangle_{E(W_n)}=1. \] The requested problem is to determine which other graphs $G$ with \[ |V(G)|=n,\qquad |E(G)|=2n-2 \] admit the corresponding self-stress identity, and to identify a general phenomenon, possibly homological or cohomological, that explains it.
