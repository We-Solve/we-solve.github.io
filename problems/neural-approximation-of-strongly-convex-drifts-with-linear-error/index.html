Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

Under the Euclidean convention, a differentiable $V:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$ is $\mu$-strongly convex when $\mu>0$ and $V(y)\ge V(x)+\langle\nabla V(x),y-x\rangle+(\mu/2)\|y-x\|^2$ for all $x,y$. Its gradient is $L$-Lipschitz when $\|\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|$. The drift is $b(x)=-\nabla V(x)$; a neural approximation $N$ has pointwise error $\|N(x)-b(x)\|$. A feed-forward network composes affine maps with specified activation functions.

Record scope and qualifications.
The catalogue title is retained as metadata. Named certificates, contracts, bounds or frameworks in that title are not asserted as results of the original source extract. Source-record limitation: For an $L$-Lipschitz gradient, the zero approximation has the affine-linear error bound $\|\nabla V(x)\|\le\|\nabla V(0)\|+L\|x\|$. The constant network $N(x)=-\nabla V(0)$ even satisfies $\|N(x)+\nabla V(x)\|\le L\|x\|$. Thus merely requiring linear growth does not specify a nontrivial approximation target. The cleaned question omits the accuracy, norm, and coefficient constraints that would distinguish the intended result.

The error coefficient and activation matter

As the preserved definition notes, a constant network already has an error bounded by $L\|x\|$ for an $L$-Lipschitz gradient. The primary WIAS paper makes a stronger assumption in its Assumption1.2(i): for each requested $\varepsilon>0$, global error is at most $\varepsilon(1+\|x\|)$. Its local-approximation route with the extra condition $M<\sqrt2m$ is a distinct sampling theorem, not a claim that arbitrary potentials satisfy that stronger assumption. We test the stronger global statement for finite ReLU networks, an explicitly specified activation class. Define in one dimension \[ g(x)=2x+\frac{x}{8}\sin\!\left(\tfrac12\log(1+x^2)\right), \qquad V(x)=\int_0^xg(t)\,dt. \]
Proposition: The potential $V$ is $7/4$-strongly convex and has $9/4$-Lipschitz gradient. Nevertheless no finite feed-forward ReLU network $N:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfies \[ |N(x)+V'(x)|\le\varepsilon(1+|x|)\quad\hbox{for all }x\in\mathbb R \] when $0<\varepsilon<1/8$.
Proof: Put $\theta(x)=\tfrac12\log(1+x^2)$. Differentiation gives \[ g'(x)=2+\tfrac18\sin\theta(x)+\tfrac18\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\cos\theta(x), \] which lies in $[7/4,9/4]$. The mean value theorem gives the gradient Lipschitz bound. Integrating $V''\ge7/4$ twice along the segment from $x$ to $y$ yields $V(y)\ge V(x)+V'(x)(y-x)+(7/8)(y-x)^2$, including either ordering of $x,y$. Every finite one-input ReLU network is continuous piecewise affine with finitely many breakpoints. This follows by induction over its layers: finite affine combinations preserve a common finite interval partition, and on each interval ReLU of an affine function introduces at most one new breakpoint unless that function is identically zero. Hence on some positive tail $N(x)=ax+b$. For each large integer $j$, choose $x_j>0$ with $\theta(x_j)=\pi/2+2\pi j$ and $z_j>0$ with $\theta(z_j)=3\pi/2+2\pi j$; explicitly these are $\sqrt{e^{2\theta}-1}$ and tend to infinity. Then $g(x_j)/x_j=2+1/8$ and $g(z_j)/z_j=2-1/8$. Dividing the assumed error bound by the input and taking these two limits gives \[ |a+2+1/8|\le\varepsilon,\qquad |a+2-1/8|\le\varepsilon. \] The triangle inequality would imply $1/4\le2\varepsilon$, a contradiction. ∎
The constants even satisfy $(9/4)/(7/4)=9/7<\sqrt2$, emphasizing why the WIAS sampling theorem and the arbitrary-small-global-slope assumption must not be conflated. This is not a contradiction to its theorem. Other activations may have different tails; the argument does not cover them. The unrestricted wording "linear error growth" remains trivially satisfied with a sufficiently large coefficient, so this counterexample does not refute that raw statement.
