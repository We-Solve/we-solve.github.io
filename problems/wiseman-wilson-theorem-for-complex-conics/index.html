Wiseman–Wilson Theorem for Complex Conics

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $V={\mathbb{C}}[X_0,X_1,X_2]_2$ be the six-dimensional vector space of homogeneous quadratic forms. For $0\ne q\in V$, write \[ C_q=\{x\in{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}}):q(x)=0\}. \] Forms differing by a nonzero scalar represent the same projective conic. For a finite subset $S\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})$, define \[ W(S)=\{q\in V:q(s)=0\text{ for every }s\in S\}. \] Here vanishing is independent of the homogeneous coordinates chosen for each point. Say that five distinct points $S$ uniquely determine a conic if $\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}W(S)=1$. This uniqueness condition is stronger than merely lying on a conic.

Under the interpretation that conics include reducible and nonreduced quadratic curves, and that "determined" means uniquely determined among all projective conics, the supplied question is: \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every finite }P\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}})\text{ with }W(P)=\{0\},\\ \text{do there exist }S\subseteq P\text{ and }0\ne q\in V\text{ such that}\\ |S|=5,\qquad W(S)={\mathbb{C}} q,\qquad P\cap C_q=S\,? \end{gathered} \] The hypothesis says that no conic contains all of $P$; the conclusion asks for a uniquely determined conic containing exactly five points of $P$.

The supplied record does not explicitly specify whether reducible or nonreduced conics are admissible, or define "determined." Thus the displayed formulation is conditional on the stated conventions. If nonsingular conics are required, the conclusion must additionally require $C_q$ to be nonsingular; whether that restriction also changes the hypothesis requires clarification from the source. The supplied background also describes an incomplete higher-degree Sylvester--Gallai question, whereas the explicit statements concern degree two. No higher-degree assertion is reconstructed here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every finite set of points in the complex projective plane that is not contained in a conic has a conic uniquely determined by five of its points and containing no others. The precise draft uses all quadratic conics; admissibility of singular conics and the intended uniqueness convention require review. The supplied record labels the problem open, without independent verification here.
