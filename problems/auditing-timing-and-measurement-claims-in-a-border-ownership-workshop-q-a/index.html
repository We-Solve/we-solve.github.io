Auditing timing and measurement claims in a border-ownership workshop Q&A

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This source is a context-only Q&A after Rüdiger von der Heydt's talk, "Neural Mechanisms of Perceptual Inference," not an open mathematical problem. It reports that $50\%$ of recorded cells were in V1 and describes the selection as unbiased; response is total spikes in an $800$ ms window, and tested visual extent reached $8$ degrees. Receptive fields are mapped by moving an optimal bar until response ceases, with an explicit comment that other methods give different sizes. The speaker treats figure--ground and ownership as the same terminology, reports half-strength ownership signal at $70$ ms and IT silence up to $100$ ms, leaves an illusory-contour locality question unanswered, calls summation-field definitions controversial and summation insufficient as an explanation, and only suspects that some results persist in natural scenes.

The attached aid gives a conditional timing audit. Let $h_B$ be target half-rise time and $s_S$ the onset of a putative source signal, measured against a common baseline, and let $d_{\min}\ge0$ be a certified lower bound on every causal route lag. A sole-source account with no target response before source onset plus lag requires $h_B\ge s_S+d_{\min}$. If simultaneous uncertainty bounds are \[ h_B\le\widehat h_B+\epsilon_B,\qquad s_S\ge\widehat s_S-\epsilon_S, \] then that account is excluded only by the strict certificate \[ \widehat h_B+\epsilon_B<\widehat s_S-\epsilon_S+d_{\min}. \] Applied to the reported numbers, this requires $70\text{ ms}+\epsilon_B<100\text{ ms}-\epsilon_S+d_{\min}$ and a genuinely certified source-onset lower bound. Equality is insufficient. A source half-rise time is not its onset, and absence of above-threshold detected IT activity need not certify absence of a weaker causally effective source signal. The transcript does not supply these uncertainty or route-lag bounds, so its two numbers alone do not establish the certificate.

The aid also considers an inhomogeneous Poisson spike model on $[0,0.8\text{ s}]$. Its total count has law \[ N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}\left(\int_0^{0.8\mathrm{s}}\lambda(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t\right). \] Distinct nonnegative intensity profiles with the same integral therefore give exactly the same count distribution while differing in onset or transient-versus-sustained structure. The total count alone cannot identify those dynamics in this model.

Finally, a moved-bar response profile $R(r)$ measured with threshold $\vartheta$ gives an operational radius $r_\vartheta=\sup\{r:R(r)>\vartheta\}$. This quantity depends on threshold and stimulus protocol; increasing the threshold shrinks the detected region for a monotone response profile. The aid's three-part audit distinguishes a conditional causal timing exclusion, count-based nonidentifiability and threshold-dependent receptive-field measurement. It does not turn the transcript into a solved neural-inference conjecture.

Short English statement

This workshop discussion gives compressed timing and measurement claims. The supplied aid specifies when timing can exclude a sole source and shows why an $800$ ms count or a threshold-defined receptive field does not uniquely identify the underlying dynamics or spatial mechanism.
