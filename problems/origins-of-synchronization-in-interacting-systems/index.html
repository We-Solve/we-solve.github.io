Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns systems of interacting units, but specifies no mathematical class of systems. In particular, it gives no state space, index set of units, evolution law, interaction structure, parameters, initial conditions, or boundary conditions. The terms synchronization, rhythmic behavior, and origin are not mathematically defined in the record. No particular definition of these terms is imposed here.

Source question. What is the origin of synchronization and rhythmic behavior in systems of interacting units?

This is a research question rather than a quantified mathematical assertion. A precise formulation requires a specified class of interacting systems, definitions of synchronization and rhythmic behavior, and a target meaning of origin, such as a mechanism or a criterion for occurrence. The record does not determine which target is intended, or whether the question concerns every system in a class or particular examples. Consequently, no theorem, conjectured equivalence, or universal existence claim can be extracted without adding hypotheses.

The supplied background mentions pacemaker models and mean-field oscillator systems as explaining particular synchronization mechanisms. It does not restrict the question to either family. Its assertion that the general origin remains unresolved is a supplied dated literature assessment, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

Short English statement

The question asks what produces synchronization and rhythms among interacting units. The source supplies no model, definitions, or quantifiers, so a precise mathematical conjecture cannot yet be stated. The registry labels the problem open; that label and its literature assessment require independent review.
