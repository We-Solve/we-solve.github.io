Erdős Problem #340

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Unless a section says otherwise, ${\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is the ring of integers, ${\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}$ are the rational, real, and complex fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity, $f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$; $f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$; $f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds. A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence. The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than $n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.

A Sidon set has $a+b=c+d$ only when the multisets $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ agree. Equal summands are allowed, but interchanging their order is not a new representation. Unless a real or group domain is explicitly specified, the set here consists of integers. [S1] [S2]

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $A=\{1,2,4,8,13,21,31,45,66,81,97,\ldots\}$ be the greedy Sidon sequence: we begin with $1$ and iteratively include the next smallest integer that preserves the Sidon property (i.e. there are no non-trivial solutions to $a+b=c+d$). What is the order of growth of $A$? Is it true that $ \lvert A\cap \{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert \gg N^{1/2-\epsilon} $ for all $\epsilon>0$ and large $N$? [S1]

Short English statement

How rapidly does the greedy Sidon sequence grow, and can its counting function approach the square-root scale up to every positive power loss?
