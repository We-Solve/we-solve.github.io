Math-1755

The degree-four quaternionic Toledo invariant

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Oscar García-Prada's source question proposes a degree-four Toledo invariant for representations into a real group with quaternion-Kähler symmetric space. A precise representation invariant needs more than an arbitrary four-form. For the supplied interpretation, let $M$ be closed, oriented, and smooth of real dimension four, let $G$ have a contractible noncompact-type quaternion-Kähler symmetric space $X=G/K$, and choose a closed $G$-invariant four-form $\Omega$ with fixed normalization. Given $\rho:\pi_1(M)\to G$, choose a $\rho$-equivariant map $f:\widetilde M\to X$. Its pullback descends to $M$, and \[ \tau_\Omega(\rho)=\int_M f^*\Omega. \] Here $f$ is equivariant on the universal cover, not an ordinary map $M\to X$; contractibility gives the intended independence of the section choice. Compact-type targets or other surface/boundary conventions need a separate formulation.

Let $Q_\rho$ be the oriented rank-three real bundle of quaternionic structures obtained from the canonical $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-bundle over $X$ and the associated flat $X$-bundle/section. The aid's integral normalization is \[ \tau_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho)=\langle p_1(Q_\rho),[M]\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}, \] with the corresponding Chern--Weil normalization of $\Omega$. Its Dold--Whitney mod-four constraint is \[ p_1(Q_\rho)\bmod4=\mathcal P(w_2(Q_\rho))\in H^4(M;{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}), \] where $\mathcal P$ is the Pontryagin square. Evaluation gives the same congruence for the integral number. The canonical bundle choice and form scale must be fixed before using this identity as a numerical normalization.

For quaternionic hyperbolic targets, the aid invokes Pieters's norm theorem to give, in its metric normalization, \[ |\tau_{\mathrm{met}}(\rho)|\leq v_4\,\|M\|_1, \] where $\|M\|_1$ is simplicial volume and $v_4$ is the norm constant in that theorem. For a closed real-hyperbolic four-manifold, using compatible curvature and simplex-volume normalization, it reports \[ |\tau_{\mathrm{met}}(\rho)|\leq\operatorname{Vol}(M). \] The integral Pontryagin form and metric quaternionic form may differ by a scalar; no equality between $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{met}}$ is assumed without that conversion. The target metric, exact $v_4$ convention, and conversion factor are not supplied.

The explicit integral family uses $M=\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h$, $g,h\geq2$, with product orientation, and factor representations into $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ followed by the block inclusion \[ \mathrm{SU}(1,1)\times\mathrm{SU}(1,1)\longrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(2,2). \] Let $L_g,L_h$ be their negative-line bundles, pulled back to the product, with Chern numbers $d_g,d_h$ on the respective factors. The aid identifies $Q_\rho=\mathfrak{su}(W)$, $W=L_g\oplus L_h$, and uses \[ p_1(\mathfrak{su}(W))=c_1(W)^2-4c_2(W) \] to obtain \[ \tau_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho)=-2d_gd_h. \] For consistently oriented uniformizing factors it gives \[ \tau_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho)=-2(g-1)(h-1)=-\chi(M)/2, \] up to orientation conventions, and states compatibility with the Pontryagin-square congruence. This formula is in the integral characteristic-class normalization and is not automatically the metric-normalized value in the preceding inequality.

The original requests remain broader: Kähler surfaces are highlighted by García-Prada; Dieter Kotschick suggests aspherical $M$; Domingo Toledo suggests complex-hyperbolic surfaces, constant-curvature four-manifolds, and products of surfaces. The characteristic constraint, quaternionic-hyperbolic bound, and block-product calculation are supplied partial results, not a classification or a complete theory for all these cases.

Short English statement

The supplied refinement defines the invariant through equivariant pullback, identifies an integral version with a Pontryagin number, and records a mod-four constraint. A metric-normalized volume bound and an explicit product-of-surfaces formula are separate partial results whose normalization scales must be matched.
