Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.62

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group and let $p$ be a prime. A finite maximal subgroup of $G$ is a subgroup $M$ satisfying \[ |M|<\infty,\qquad M<G,\qquad \forall H\leq G\;\left(M\leq H\Longrightarrow H=M\text{ or }H=G\right). \] A $p$-group is a group whose elements all have finite order a power of $p$. A maximal $p$-subgroup of $G$ is a $p$-subgroup $P\leq G$ such that every $p$-subgroup $Q\leq G$ containing $P$ equals $P$. This differs from a maximal proper subgroup of $G$ which happens to be a $p$-group. A periodic group is a group in which every element has finite order.

The source asks whether a "(conjugacy, $p$-conjugacy) biprimitively finite" group must be finite if it has a finite maximal subgroup "($p$-subgroup)." For the finite maximal subgroup formulation, the requested implication has the form \[ \forall G\;\left[ \left(G\text{ has the intended biprimitive finiteness property}\right) \land \left(\exists M<G:\ |M|<\infty\text{ and }M\text{ is maximal in }G\right) \Longrightarrow |G|<\infty \right]. \] Here the phrase "intended biprimitive finiteness property" is a placeholder for the source's terminology, not a definition. The supplied record does not define biprimitive finiteness, conjugacy biprimitive finiteness, or $p$-conjugacy biprimitive finiteness. Consequently, this implication is a formulation schema requiring those definitions before it is a self-contained mathematical statement.

The parenthetical $p$-subgroup formulation also requires clarification: it may replace the subgroup hypothesis by the existence of a finite maximal $p$-subgroup, or require a finite maximal proper subgroup which is a $p$-group. The supplied record does not explicitly resolve this distinction or specify how its parenthetical property variants correspond to its subgroup variants. No assertion that every combination is intended is made here.

The source reports affirmative answers for conjugacy biprimitively finite $p$-groups and for $2$-conjugacy biprimitively finite groups, attributing these results to V. P. Shunkov. It also reports that the statement fails for arbitrary periodic groups and refers to Archive, Problem 3.9. These are preserved source qualifications; their precise hypotheses and correspondence with the variants above require checking the cited material.

Short English statement

Problem 6.62, proposed by V. P. Shunkov, asks whether the indicated forms of biprimitive finiteness force a group with a finite maximal subgroup, or the intended $p$-subgroup variant, to be finite. The record reports two affirmative special cases and failure for arbitrary periodic groups. Definitions of the specialized finiteness properties and the interpretation of the parentheses remain unresolved.
