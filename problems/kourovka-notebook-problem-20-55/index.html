Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.55

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $K$, define its derived series by $K^{(0)}=K$ and \[ K^{(i+1)}=[K^{(i)},K^{(i)}], \] where $[L,L]$ is the subgroup generated by all $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in L$. The group $K$ is soluble if $K^{(d)}=\{1\}$ for some nonnegative integer $d$; its derived length $\operatorname{dl}(K)$ is the least such $d$.

Interpret "character table" as the ordinary complex character table. Write $\operatorname{Irr}(K)$ for the set of irreducible complex characters of a finite group $K$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(K)$ for its set of conjugacy classes. For $C\in\operatorname{Cl}(K)$, let $\chi(C)$ denote the common value of $\chi$ on $C$. Two finite groups $G,H$ have identical character tables if there are bijections \[ \alpha:\operatorname{Irr}(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(H),\qquad \beta:\operatorname{Cl}(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(H) \] such that \[ \chi(C)=\alpha(\chi)\left(\beta(C)\right) \quad\text{for every }\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\text{ and }C\in\operatorname{Cl}(G). \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify the character field or the convention for table identity; the interpretation above is the ordinary complex convention, allowing row and column permutations.

Question (S. Mattarei). Do there exist finite soluble groups $G$ and $H$ with identical character tables and \[ \operatorname{dl}(G)=2,\qquad \operatorname{dl}(H)=4? \] Equivalently, the required derived-series conditions are $G^{(2)}=\{1\}$, $G^{(1)}\ne\{1\}$, $H^{(4)}=\{1\}$, and $H^{(3)}\ne\{1\}$.

The supplied background reports that, for every integer $n\ge2$, pairs of nonisomorphic finite soluble groups with identical character tables and derived lengths $n$ and $n+1$ were constructed by S. Mattarei in the cited 1995 paper. This reported construction concerns consecutive derived lengths and is distinct from the requested pair of lengths $2$ and $4$.

Short English statement

Do there exist two finite soluble groups with the same ordinary complex character table, up to permutations of rows and columns, whose derived lengths are respectively $2$ and $4$? The supplied background reports examples with consecutive lengths $n,n+1$ for every integer $n\ge2$.
