Math-824

Classifying Rings Up to Singularity Equivalence

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a commutative Noetherian ring $R$, let $\operatorname{mod}R$ denote the category of finitely generated $R$-modules, let $D^b(\operatorname{mod}R)$ be its bounded derived category, and let $\operatorname{Perf}(R)$ be its full subcategory of complexes quasi-isomorphic to bounded complexes of finitely generated projective modules. The singularity category is the Verdier quotient \[ D_{\mathrm{sg}}(R)=D^b(\operatorname{mod}R)/\operatorname{Perf}(R). \] Two rings are called singular equivalent when their singularity categories are equivalent as triangulated categories. The supplied excerpt does not specify whether equivalences must additionally be ${\mathbb{C}}$-linear or preserve an enhancement; those requirements remain unresolved.

Question 3 (supplied source). How fine is the invariant $R\mapsto D_{\mathrm{sg}}(R)$? Determine when two rings $R,S$ satisfy \[ D_{\mathrm{sg}}(R)\simeq D_{\mathrm{sg}}(S). \] The source's footnote restricts its displayed list of known examples to nonisomorphic commutative complete local Noetherian ${\mathbb{C}}$-algebras. This is therefore the principal setting indicated for the classification question, although the question itself says only "rings." In this setting, the target is to describe the equivalence relation on such rings induced by their singularity categories, including equivalences with $R\not\cong S$. Allowing noncommutative rings is discussed separately below; the excerpt does not formulate a precise classification target for that broader setting.

Source notation and examples. For a positive integer $n$, a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\epsilon_n\in{\mathbb{C}}$, and $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^m$, write \[ G_n(a_1,\ldots,a_m)=\left\langle\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon_n^{a_1},\ldots,\epsilon_n^{a_m})\right\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(m,{\mathbb{C}}). \] Its diagonal action on ${\mathbb{C}}[[z_1,\ldots,z_m]]$ gives the invariant ring ${\mathbb{C}}[[z_1,\ldots,z_m]]^{G_n(a_1,\ldots,a_m)}$. The source denotes this group by $\frac1n(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ and calls it a group of order $n$. For the stated unrestricted tuples, its order is actually $n/\gcd(n,a_1,\ldots,a_m)$; the order assertion needs the additional condition $\gcd(n,a_1,\ldots,a_m)=1$.

The source lists the following examples, as historical context rather than a classification:

  • (E0) $D_{\mathrm{sg}}(R)\simeq0\simeq D_{\mathrm{sg}}(S)$ for regular rings $R,S$, attributed to Auslander--Buchsbaum and Serre. The excerpt does not state the full ambient hypotheses for this contextual assertion.
  • (E1) The equivalence attributed to Knörrer (1987), reference [10], is \[ D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left(\frac{{\mathbb{C}}[[z_0,\ldots,z_d]]}{(f)}\right) \simeq D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left(\frac{{\mathbb{C}}[[z_0,\ldots,z_d,x_1,\ldots,x_{2m}]]}{(f+x_1^2+\cdots+x_{2m}^2)}\right), \qquad 0\ne f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[z_0,\ldots,z_d]]. \] The supplied excerpt does not explicitly give the ranges of $d,m$ or require $f$ to be a nonunit. These omissions are retained as qualifications rather than silently strengthened hypotheses.
  • (E2) The extracted display is read as \[ D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left({\mathbb{C}}[[y_1,y_2]]^{G_n(1,1)}\right) \simeq D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left(\frac{{\mathbb{C}}[z_1,\ldots,z_{n-1}]}{(z_1,\ldots,z_{n-1})^2}\right). \] It is attributed to Yang (2015), reference [12], Kawamata (2015), reference [9], and Kalck--Karmazyn (2017), reference [6]. The source says Yang's result is based on joint works [7,8]. A further footnote concerning a generalization is truncated. The range of $n$, the display's transcription, and the scope of that generalization require checking against the report.
  • (E3) The source attributes to Kalck (2021), reference [3], the equivalence \[ D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left({\mathbb{C}}[[x_1,x_2,x_3]]^{G_2(1,1,1)}\right) \simeq D_{\mathrm{sg}}\!\left({\mathbb{C}}[[y_1,y_2]]^{G_4(1,1)}\right). \] The source remarks that this equivalence, in contrast to (E1) and (E2), does not preserve the parity of Krull dimension.
The source's description of its list as complete is qualified by its footnote: it concerns singular equivalences between nonisomorphic commutative complete local Noetherian ${\mathbb{C}}$-algebras that the source authors could find in the literature. It also points to an older list in [11, Example 4.1]. Neither description supplies a classification theorem.

The source further discusses proofs of (E2) and (E3) using noncommutative resolutions and relative singularity categories, building on [7,8]. It alternatively describes maximal Cohen--Macaulay modules $M_R,M_S$ as unique up to taking syzygies and direct summands, with $M_R,M_S$ direct summands of $\Omega(\omega_R),\Omega(\omega_S)$, respectively, where $\omega_R,\omega_S$ are canonical modules and $\Omega$ denotes a syzygy. The exact scope and meaning of this uniqueness formulation are not specified sufficiently in the excerpt to make it a separate precise assertion.

If $S$ is allowed to be noncommutative, the source reports that for every ADE surface singularity $R$ except type $E_8$, its cited work [5] constructs an uncountable family of complete Noetherian ${\mathbb{C}}$-algebras $S_R$ singular equivalent to $R$, with pairwise nonisomorphic centers. The necessary conventions for singularity categories in this broader setting and the full reference [5] are not supplied.

The opening fragment about two-periodic stable ranges induced by matrix factorizations of $f$ refers to an absent condition (P2); it does not provide an additional well-specified hypothesis for Question 3. The registry labels the problem open and supplies a dated assessment that the examples and subsequent constructions leave the general commutative classification unresolved. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

Classify commutative complete local Noetherian ${\mathbb{C}}$-algebras according to equivalence of their singularity categories, which measure the part of the bounded derived category remaining after perfect complexes are removed. The source gives examples of nonisomorphic rings with equivalent singularity categories and discusses a broader noncommutative setting. Its exact equivalence conventions, some example hypotheses, and several bibliography details remain unresolved. The supplied open status requires independent review.
