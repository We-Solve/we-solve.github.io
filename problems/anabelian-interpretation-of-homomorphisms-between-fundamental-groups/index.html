Anabelian Interpretation of Homomorphisms between Fundamental Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p>0$ be a prime. For each $i\in\{1,2\}$, let $k_i$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and let \[ X_i^{\bullet}=(X_i,D_{X_i}) \] be a pointed stable curve of the same type $(g,n)$ over $k_i$. Here $X_i$ is the underlying semistable curve of genus $g$, and $D_{X_i}$ is its set of marked points, with $|D_{X_i}|=n$. In the usual stable-curve terminology, genus means arithmetic genus and marked points are distinct smooth points; stability requires every irreducible component of the normalization, of genus $h$ and with $r$ marked points or branches above nodes, to satisfy $2h-2+r>0$.

Write $\Pi_{X_i}^{\bullet}$ for the admissible fundamental group associated with $X_i^{\bullet}$ in the supplied source. The excerpt does not give the construction or conventions for this group. For topological groups $G,H$, the literal meaning of a continuous open homomorphism is a continuous group homomorphism $\varphi:G\to H$ that sends open subsets of $G$ to open subsets of $H$. On this literal reading, \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{op}}(G,H) =\{\varphi:G\to H:\varphi\text{ is a continuous open group homomorphism}\}. \] The source calls the relevant maps open continuous homomorphisms, but the supplied excerpt does not specify basepoint conventions or whether maps are identified up to inner automorphisms. Consequently this displayed definition explains the literal reading without resolving those source conventions.

Source question. Does there exist an anabelian interpretation of \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{op}} \left(\Pi_{X_1}^{\bullet},\Pi_{X_2}^{\bullet}\right)? \] The accompanying source discussion relates this question to deformation information of curves, moduli spaces of fundamental groups, and a homeomorphism conjecture. However, the supplied record does not define the desired geometric objects, equivalence relation, or correspondence constituting an anabelian interpretation. Thus the question remains a research target; the evidence does not determine a specific bijection, reconstruction theorem, or other precise conjectural assertion.

Short English statement

For two pointed stable curves of the same type over algebraically closed fields of a common positive characteristic, seek a geometric explanation of the open continuous homomorphisms between their admissible fundamental groups. The source connects this target with deformation information and moduli spaces, but leaves the precise correspondence and group conventions unspecified.
