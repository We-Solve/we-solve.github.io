Math-14964

Comparing Travelling-Salesman and Travelling-Polytope Metrics

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a graph, let $x,y\in V(X)$, and let $F\subseteq V(X)$ be finite. The supplied excerpt imposes no explicit connectedness, finiteness, or median-graph hypothesis on $X$.

The travelling-salesman quantity is \[ \operatorname{TS}(x,F,y) :=\min\{\ell(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a path from }x\text{ to }y,\ F\subseteq V(\gamma)\}. \] Here $\ell(\gamma)$ denotes path length. The source does not explicitly specify whether paths may repeat vertices, although this matters for a travelling-salesman problem, or how to treat an empty collection of admissible paths.

A polytope, in the terminology of the source, is the convex hull $\operatorname{conv}_X(A)$ of a finite set $A\subseteq V(X)$. This does not itself assert that its entire vertex set is finite. The excerpt does not define its convexity convention. One possible interpretation is graph geodesic convexity, under which a convex vertex set contains every shortest path between any two of its vertices; this interpretation requires confirmation.

A path of polytopes is a finite sequence \[ P_0=\{x\},\ P_1,\ldots,P_k=\{y\} \] such that for each consecutive pair, its two members can be designated $P,Q$ in an order for which \[ P=\operatorname{conv}_X\left(V(Q)\cup\{u\}\right) \qquad\text{for some }u\in\partial Q. \] The symbol $\partial Q$ is retained exactly from the source: its meaning is not supplied. In particular, an inner vertex boundary and an exterior vertex boundary would give materially different moves. Interpreting the length of this sequence as its number $k$ of transitions, the travelling-polytope quantity is \[ \operatorname{TP}(x,F,y) :=\min\left\{k:\begin{array}{l} P_0=\{x\},\ P_k=\{y\},\ (P_0,\ldots,P_k)\text{ is a path of polytopes},\\ F\subseteq\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}V(P_i) \end{array}\right\}. \] The transition-count interpretation of length and the convention when no such sequence exists require confirmation.

Problem 7 (Genevois), as supplied. Compare these two quantities. The source records the bound \[ \operatorname{TP}(x,F,y)\leq 2\operatorname{TS}(x,F,y). \] Seek sharp bounds in the reverse direction and determine the exponent introduced in the source: \[ \operatorname{TP}(X):= \sup\left\{\alpha\ \middle|\ \begin{array}{l} \exists C>0\ \forall x,y\in V(X)\ \ \forall F\subseteq V(X)\text{ finite},\\[2pt] \operatorname{TP}(x,F,y)+|F| \geq C\left(\operatorname{TS}(x,F,y)+|F|\right)^{\alpha} \end{array}\right\}. \] Thus $C$ is uniform over $x,y,F$, although it may depend on $X$ and $\alpha$. The excerpt does not explicitly specify the range of $\alpha$, conventions for zero bases or infinite distances, or the intended class of graphs for which this invariant is to be computed. In particular, $x=y$ and $F=\varnothing$ give a zero base under the usual length-zero convention. These issues, together with the undefined boundary operation, prevent an unqualified precise formulation of the invariant from the supplied excerpt alone.

Short English statement

For a graph, compare the shortest route visiting a prescribed finite set of vertices with the shortest sequence of convex polytopes whose union covers that set. The source attributes the problem to Genevois, states $\operatorname{TP}\leq2\operatorname{TS}$, and asks for reverse comparisons encoded by $\operatorname{TP}(X)$. The boundary operation, path conventions, and intended graph class need clarification. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
