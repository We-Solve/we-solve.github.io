Math-4549

S-Unit Differences of Singular Moduli

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}$ denote the field of algebraic numbers. A singular modulus is a value $j(\tau)$, where $j$ is the classical modular invariant and $\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}$ satisfies $\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0$ and $[{\mathbb{Q}}(\tau):{\mathbb{Q}}]=2$. Write $\mathcal J\subset\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ for the set of all singular moduli.

For a finite set $S$ of rational primes, call $u\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{\times}$ an $S$-unit if, in a number field $K$ containing $u$, one has \[ v_{\mathfrak p}(u)=0 \quad\text{for every nonzero prime ideal }\mathfrak p\subset\mathcal O_K \text{ lying above a rational prime outside }S. \] Here $v_{\mathfrak p}$ is the additive prime-ideal valuation. This condition is independent of the choice of $K$. Denote the set of these algebraic $S$-units by $U_S$.

Question (under the stated interpretation). For every finite set $S$ of rational primes, is the set \[ D_S=\{j_1-j_2:\ j_1,j_2\in\mathcal J,\ j_1\ne j_2,\ j_1-j_2\in U_S\} \] finite?

Both singular moduli vary. The requested finiteness concerns distinct difference values; the supplied wording does not explicitly ask for finiteness of the pairs producing them. Zero is excluded because an $S$-unit is nonzero. No bound independent of $S$ is asserted.

The original question says only a fixed set of primes. The finiteness of $S$ is taken from the supplied literature assessment, and the rational-prime and algebraic $S$-unit conventions above are explicit interpretations requiring source confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open. Its dated assessment states that finiteness is known when one singular modulus is fixed, whereas finiteness as both vary remains conjectural; these are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

For each fixed finite set $S$ of rational primes, ask whether only finitely many distinct nonzero differences of two singular moduli are algebraic $S$-units. Both moduli may vary. The finite-set hypothesis and the meaning of $S$-unit are made explicit from the supplied context and require human source review.
