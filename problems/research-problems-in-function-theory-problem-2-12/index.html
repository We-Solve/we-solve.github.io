Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.12

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n$ be a nonconstant entire function of finite order \[ \rho(f)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log T(r,f)}{\log r}<\infty, \qquad \Lambda_f=\{n\geq0:a_n\ne0\}. \] Here $T$ is the Nevanlinna characteristic. For $a\in{\mathbb{C}}$, its finite-value deficiency is \[ \delta(a,f)=1-\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{N(r,a;f)}{T(r,f)}, \] where $N(r,a;f)$ counts zeros of $f-a$ in the integrated Nevanlinna sense, with multiplicities.

The source calls $\Delta$ the maximal density of the nonzero coefficients, but gives no definition in the supplied excerpt. This is a density of $\Lambda_f$, not of the numerical coefficient values. A possible gap-theory convention is the upper Pólya density, expressed using short relative intervals by \[ \limsup_{\eta\uparrow1}\ \limsup_{R\to\infty} \frac{\#(\Lambda_f\cap(\eta R,R])}{(1-\eta)R}. \] This contextual formula must be checked against the source; ordinary upper asymptotic density or upper uniform density must not be silently substituted.

Question. With $\Delta$ interpreted as the intended maximal density, does \[ \rho(f)\Delta<\tfrac12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \delta(a,f)<1\quad\text{for every finite }a\in{\mathbb{C}} \] hold for every entire function in the stated class? The assertion excludes deficiency exactly one; it does not assert absence of every positive deficiency, and it excludes the value $\infty$ from its conclusion.

The supplied summary reports openness in the 2018 anniversary edition, with "no progress reported," and no post-2018 resolution located. It separately claims contributor verification of the $\Delta=0$ endpoint (attributed to Fuchs, 1963), the $\rho<1/2$, $\Delta=1$ case via the classical $\cos\pi\rho$ theorem, and a reduction from Kővari's conjectured minimum-modulus bound, Problem 2.12a. It also reports a quantitative estimate \[ \sum_a\delta(a,f)\leq C\lambda(f)\Delta, \qquad \lambda(f)=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log T(r,f)}{\log r}, \] attributed to Fuchs (1969) and Murai (1983), which it says yields the desired conclusion only when $\rho\Delta<1/C$ for a large absolute constant $C$.

Finally, the summary reports Mittag--Leffler extremal values $\delta=1-\sin(\pi\sigma)$ at $\rho\Delta=\sigma\in[1/2,1]$ and says all known deficiency-one examples have $\rho\Delta\geq1$. The displayed formula gives zero deficiency at $\sigma=1/2$; no strictly positive boundary deficiency is inferred from it. All these claims, including the contributor's purported verifications, remain unreviewed supplied evidence.

Short English statement

If an entire function has order $\rho$ and its nonzero Taylor coefficients have maximal density $\Delta$ with $\rho\Delta<1/2$, can a finite value have Nevanlinna deficiency exactly one? The density convention is missing, and the supplied partial results and claimed reductions require review.
