Definitions and mathematical statement

In the central simple algebra setting of the supplied source, interpret an $F$-division algebra as a finite-dimensional central division algebra $A$ over a field $F$. Write $n=\deg(A)$, so that $\dim_F A=n^2$. The supplied statement does not explicitly state centrality or finite dimensionality; this interpretation requires review.

A separable maximal subfield of $A$ is a subfield $K\subseteq A$ containing $F$ such that $K/F$ is separable and $[K:F]=n$. After choosing an $F$-embedding of $K$ into a separable closure of $F$, let $L_K/F$ be its Galois closure and put \[ G_K=\operatorname{Gal}(L_K/F). \] The extension $L_K$ splits $A$, meaning that \[ A\otimes_F L_K\cong M_n(L_K). \] Thus the groups under consideration are those arising from Galois closures of separable maximal subfields, rather than from arbitrary splitting extensions. Define their isomorphism classes by \[ \mathcal G(A)=\left\{[\operatorname{Gal}(L_K/F)]_{\cong}: K\subseteq A\text{ is a separable maximal subfield}\right\}. \] Each such group acts faithfully and transitively on the $n$ distinct $F$-embeddings of $K$ into $L_K$, and hence has a permutation representation as a subgroup of $S_n$.

Problem. Determine the smallest group or groups in $\mathcal G(A)$. The source does not specify whether "smallest" refers to group order, subgroup containment in a permutation representation, or another comparison, so this part does not yet specify a unique minimization problem. Its initial reference to a maximal subfield also does not explicitly impose separability; the definition above uses separable maximal subfields to give the Galois-closure construction its usual meaning.

In particular, writing $A_n$ for the alternating group on $n$ letters, determine whether the following assertion holds: \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every field }F,\text{ every integer }n\geq 1,\text{ and every finite-dimensional}\\ \text{central division }F\text{-algebra }A\text{ of degree }n,\text{ there exists a separable}\\ \text{maximal subfield }K\subseteq A\text{ such that } \operatorname{Gal}(L_K/F)\cong A_n. \end{gathered} \] No restriction on the characteristic of $F$ or lower bound on the degree is supplied. The displayed universal formulation retains that unrestricted wording; the intended range of degrees, including boundary cases, requires clarification. This is a statement-only draft, not an assertion that the proposed universal statement is true.

Short English statement

For a central division algebra, consider the Galois groups obtained by taking Galois closures of its separable maximal subfields. Determine which of these groups are smallest, with the meaning of "smallest" still unspecified. In particular, ask whether every algebra of degree $n$ admits such a subfield with Galois-closure group $A_n$. The intended degree range and implicit algebra hypotheses require review.
