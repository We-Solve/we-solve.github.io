Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $N\ge2$, let the unitary discrete Fourier matrix be $F_{rj}=N^{-1/2}\exp(2\pi i rj/N)$, with $0\le r,j<N$. Choose $R_1,\ldots,R_M$ independently and uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$, allowing repetitions, and put \[ A_{tj}=\sqrt{N/M}\,F_{R_tj} =M^{-1/2}\exp(2\pi i R_tj/N). \] The rescaling ensures $\mathbb E(A^*A)=I_N$ and gives every column Euclidean norm one.

For $1\le s\le N$, the event of interest is \[ \frac23\|x\|_2^2\le\|Ax\|_2^2\le\frac43\|x\|_2^2 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb C^N \text{ with }|\operatorname{supp}(x)|\le s. \] For $0<\eta<1/2$, determine the sharp order of the smallest sample size $M=M(N,s,\eta)$ for which this event has probability at least $1-\eta$. In particular, determine the necessary logarithmic factors in the high-probability regimes, for example $\eta=N^{-1}$.

The probability concerns the sampled rows, while the assertion within the event is uniform over every support and every coefficient vector. Recovery of one fixed sparse signal need not require the same number of measurements. Rows are sampled with replacement, exactly as in the primary formulation; sampling a subset without replacement is a related but different random model. Both lower and upper bounds are needed to identify the sample scale rather than merely provide a sufficient oversampling rule.

Short English statement

How many independently sampled Fourier rows are necessary and sufficient to preserve all sparse vectors with high probability?

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Consider a $A\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}$ obtained by sampling random rows of a Discrete Fourier Tranform. How large does $M$ need to be in order for, with high probability, $A$ to satisfy the $(s,\frac13)$ -RIP?

Attempt and results

Rows are independent samples with replacement and the matrix has exactly the normalization in the preserved definition. We retain uniformity over all complex sparse vectors and the failure parameter $0<\eta<1/2$.
A subgroup lower bound when $s\mid N$.
Assume $2\le s\le N$ and $s\mid N$. For $0\le a<s$, define a unit vector supported on $\{\ell N/s:0\le\ell<s\}$ by $x^{(a)}_{\ell N/s}=s^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi i a\ell/s}$. A geometric sum shows \[ \|Ax^{(a)}\|^2=\frac sM C_a, \qquad C_a=|\{t:R_t\equiv a\pmod s\}|. \] Thus RIP implies every residue class is sampled. Let $X$ count missing classes. Uniform independent sampling gives $\mathbb EX=s(1-1/s)^M=:u$ and \[ \mathbb EX^2=u+s(s-1)(1-2/s)^M\le u+u^2, \] because $1-2/s\le(1-1/s)^2$. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $X\boldsymbol1_{X>0}$ gives $\Pr(X>0)\ge u/(1+u)$. If RIP succeeds with probability at least $1-\eta$, necessarily \[ M\ge\frac{\log(s(1-\eta)/\eta)}{-\log(1-1/s)}. \] This is only a necessary coupon-collection condition; even all nonempty classes do not guarantee the required counts within $[2M/(3s),4M/(3s)]$. It yields order $s\log(s/\eta)$ for this divisor regime. It cannot be applied unchanged when $N$ is prime and $1<s<N$.
An elementary sufficient bound.
For distinct columns $i,j$, their inner product is the average of $M$ independent unit complex numbers of mean zero. A real zero-mean variable $Y\in[-1,1]$ satisfies $\mathbb E e^{\lambda Y}\le\cosh\lambda \le e^{\lambda^2/2}$: convexity bounds the exponential by its chord at $-1,1$, and comparison of power-series coefficients gives the second inequality. Independence and Markov's inequality imply that the mean of $M$ such variables has either real tail bounded by $e^{-Mt^2/2}$. If a complex average has magnitude at least $t$, one of its real or imaginary parts has absolute value at least $t/\sqrt2$. Hence \[ \Pr(|\langle a_i,a_j\rangle|\ge t)\le4e^{-Mt^2/4}. \] Taking a union bound over pairs, with $t=\sqrt{4\log(4N^2/\eta)/M}$, bounds the failure probability by $\eta$. Every column has norm one. For a support of size at most $s$, Cauchy--Schwarz as in the preceding argument gives a Gram-error norm at most $(s-1)t$. Thus for $s\ge2$ the sufficient condition \[ M\ge36(s-1)^2\log(4N^2/\eta) \] ensures $(s,1/3)$-RIP with probability at least $1-\eta$. For $s=1$ every $M\ge1$ works exactly. For all $s$, $M\ge s$ is also necessary: otherwise an $M$ by $s$ column submatrix has a nonzero kernel.

Context, audit, and remaining scope

Haviv and Regev's primary abstract, arXiv:1507.01768, was consulted on 11 October 2026 for its improved Fourier sampling result; we do not reprove or use its chaining argument. The pairwise upper bound above is deliberately weaker known material. The attempt to close the target by matching coupon collection with pairwise concentration leaves a factor comparable to $s$ and does not handle all cyclic-group arithmetic in the lower bound. The audit checked replacement sampling, Fourier normalization, divisibility, $s=1,N$, and the direction of the second-moment implication. No simulation or formal theorem proving was performed. No claim about sharp logarithmic factors follows from these two elementary bounds.
