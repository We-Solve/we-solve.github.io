Definitions and conventions.
For a lattice interaction of finite range, a Gibbs measure satisfies the Dobrushin--Lanford--Ruelle conditional-probability equations at inverse temperature $\beta\in(0,\infty)$. An extremal Gibbs measure is not a nontrivial convex mixture of other Gibbs measures for that same interaction. Periodicity means invariance under a finite-index subgroup of lattice translations, allowing a finite unit cell. Periodic is stronger than merely having a periodic-looking local correlation pattern.

A completely verified periodic subcase.
Let spins take values in a finite set $S$ and let the translation-invariant interaction consist only of an on-site energy $h:S\to\mathbb R$. At inverse temperature $0<\beta<\infty$, put $p(s)=e^{-\beta h(s)}/\sum_{r\in S}e^{-\beta h(r)}$. The DLR conditional law on any finite region $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^2$ is $\prod_{x\in\Lambda}p(\sigma_x)$, independent of the exterior. Integrating this identity shows every Gibbs measure has these same finite-dimensional marginals. Therefore the Gibbs measure is uniquely the product measure and is translation invariant, hence periodic and extremal.
What uniqueness would imply generally.
For any translation-invariant specification, a lattice translate of a Gibbs measure is again Gibbs: translating each finite-region conditional equation gives the same specification on the translated region. If the Gibbs measure is unique, this forces invariance under every translation. Without uniqueness, extremality alone does not imply that its translate equals itself; an affine automorphism can permute different extreme points.
Why the hard question survives.
These proofs cover the decoupled model and the abstract uniqueness regime. They do not control interacting coexistence phases, where the source asks whether the translation orbit of every pure phase is finite. Inhomogeneous fields could trivially produce nonperiodic product laws, but they are outside the intended source assumptions and are not counterexamples. The first gap is a two-dimensional finite-range argument restricting translation orbits of extremal phases without assuming uniqueness or enlarging the state-space class.
