Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 4.2

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^1=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}$ and let $\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(S^1)$ be the group of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the circle, with the compact-open topology. For an integer $k\geq1$, let \[ \mathcal C_k(S^1)=\{A\subset S^1:|A|=k\} \] be the space of unordered configurations of $k$ distinct points. The induced action of a subgroup $G$ is $g\cdot A=\{g(a):a\in A\}$. It is transitive if for every $A,B\in\mathcal C_k(S^1)$ there exists $g\in G$ such that $g(A)=B$.

Primary question. Does there exist a subgroup $G$ such that \[ G\subsetneq\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(S^1),\qquad G\text{ is closed},\qquad G\text{ acts transitively on }\mathcal C_4(S^1)? \] Further questions. For which positive integers $k\ne3$ does such a proper closed subgroup acting transitively on $\mathcal C_k(S^1)$ exist? What relation, if any, do these questions have to earthquakes? The source does not specify a proposition or a particular earthquake construction for this last request. Its unordered-tuples wording is read as configurations of distinct points; allowing repetitions would introduce different multiplicity strata.

The supplied research summary reports a negative answer for $k\geq4$ in the category of closed subgroups containing a nonconstant continuous path, attributed to Giblin--Markovic. It does not report a complete resolution without this additional hypothesis. The stated continuous-transitivity results and the claimed implications from ordinary unordered transitivity must not be identified without their additional hypotheses.

Short English statement

Ask whether a proper closed group of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms can move any four-point subset to any other, and ask the analogous questions for other $k\ne3$. The supplied progress addresses groups with a nonconstant continuous path and leaves the unrestricted four-point question unresolved.
