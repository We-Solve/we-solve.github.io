For every non-equilateral triangle T, show that it is possible to color the plane with three colors so that there is no

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T=\{t_1,t_2,t_3\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a nondegenerate triangle, meaning its vertices are noncollinear. It is non-equilateral if the three pairwise distances are not all equal. Graham's assertion is \[ \forall T\text{ non-equilateral}\quad \exists\chi_T:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to\{1,2,3\}\quad \forall\text{ Euclidean isometries }g:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^2, \] \[ \left|\{\chi_T(g(t_1)),\chi_T(g(t_2)),\chi_T(g(t_3))\}\right|\ge2. \] Thus no translated, rotated, or reflected congruent copy of that fixed triangle is monochromatic. Arbitrary rescalings are not congruent copies. The coloring may depend on $T$, and no measurability, continuity, periodicity, or explicit-construction condition is imposed. The source does not ask for one coloring avoiding all non-equilateral triangles simultaneously. Use of three colors means an available palette of three, without an additional surjectivity condition.

The source's word triangle is interpreted in its usual nondegenerate sense; a separate collinear-configuration version is not supplied. Equilateral triangles are excluded exactly as stated.

The registry status and research classification say open, while the supplied research-summary prose calls the problem solved or folklore. That prose provides no exact theorem or working coloring. Its use of "2-Ramsey" is inconsistent: a Ramsey property normally asserts that every coloring in a specified ambient setting contains the configuration, whereas the prose describes an avoiding coloring of some finite set. A coloring of one finite set avoiding a triangle does not by itself produce a coloring of the whole plane avoiding it. The suggested hexagonal/fundamental-rhombus construction is not specified. Consequently no solution or refutation is inferred from that summary, and the precise quantified assertion above is retained as the source request.

Short English statement

For each fixed non-equilateral triangle, find a three-coloring of the whole plane with no congruent monochromatic copy. The coloring may depend on the triangle and need not be measurable. The supplied prose claims a folklore solution but uses Ramsey terminology inconsistently and gives no verifying theorem or construction, so the contradictory status is flagged.
