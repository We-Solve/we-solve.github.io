Math-1206

Matroid classification and a sharp four-factor Cohen-Macaulay obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G/P$ be a flag variety, $W$ its Weyl group and $W_P$ the parabolic Weyl subgroup; write $X_s$ for the Schubert variety indexed by $s\in W/W_P$. For $S\subset W/W_P$, put $X_S=\bigcup_{s\in S}X_s$, with the reduced union structure. The source asks which $S$ deform to irreducibles, and whether Cohen--Macaulayness of $X_S$ suffices. An embedded interpretation asks for a flat family of closed subschemes of $G/P$, over an integral pointed curve, with special fiber $X_S$ and irreducible general fiber. The aid instead requires an integral general fiber; reducedness, geometric integrality and the base-field convention are unresolved distinctions in the source.

The damaged first test case is interpreted as $G/P\simeq({\mathbb{P}}^1)^n$, following the attached aid. Fix a base point in each factor, and for $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ let $X_I$ vary in the factors indexed by $I$ and equal the base point elsewhere. For a pure $r$-dimensional union, its nonredundant support $\mathcal B$ consists of $r$-element subsets.

The aid reports that such a union deforms to an integral variety exactly when $\mathcal B$ has a degree-one algebraic-matroid realization. One explicit meaning is an integral $r$-fold $Z\subset({\mathbb{P}}^1)^n$ for which projection to the factors in an $r$-subset $I$ is dominant of generic degree one precisely for $I\in\mathcal B$, and nondominant otherwise. Equivalently its coordinate functions have these bases and generate the function field already on each basis. The aid further reports, in characteristic zero, equivalence with $\mathcal B$ being the bases of a linearly representable matroid. The field and deformation hypotheses of this classification are not supplied in full.

Its separating example in four factors is \[ X_{\{1,2\}}\cup X_{\{2,3\}}\cup X_{\{3,4\}}. \] The aid reports this reduced pure surface union to be arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay, using a saturated height-two Cox ideal and a Hilbert--Burch resolution, but not deformable to an integral surface in the intended embedded sense. Its support fails matroid basis exchange: for bases $\{1,2\}$, $\{3,4\}$, removing $2$ from the first and adding either $3$ or $4$ produces neither a supported basis. It reports this counterexample as characteristic free and four as the least number of factors, since every pure union for $n\leq3$ is representable and deformable. The actual Cox ideal and resolution are absent.

The source's adjacent Remark 2.3 reports, in an unidentified speaker's first person, that a degeneration of Kazhdan--Lusztig varieties is a union of affine spaces and calls this an easier related result. It does not provide a theorem statement, hypotheses or attribution sufficient to use that remark as a classification result.

Short English statement

Classify reduced Schubert unions that occur as limits of irreducible varieties, and test whether Cohen--Macaulayness suffices. In the reconstructed product-of-projective-lines case, the attached aid reports a degree-one matroid criterion and a four-factor Cohen--Macaulay counterexample, while the original deformation category and field remain unspecified.
