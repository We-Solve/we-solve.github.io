Math-5252

What is deterministic in a quenched CLT?

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether normalizing constants and variance in a quenched central limit theorem are almost surely the same. It does not say whether "same" means across environments, between quenched and annealed laws, or between different centering conventions. The aid separates these questions in the following setting.

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P,\theta)$ be a probability-preserving ergodic environment, and let $Q_\omega$ be conditional laws of real variables $X_0,X_1,\ldots$. Put $S_n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}X_i$, $m_n(\omega)={\mathbb{E}}_\omega S_n$, and $v_n(\omega)=\operatorname{Var}_\omega(S_n)$. Suppose there are covariance diagonals $c_r\in L^1(\mathbb P)$, $r\geq0$, such that, on one common full-measure set, \[ \operatorname{Cov}_\omega(X_i,X_{i+r})=c_r(\theta^i\omega) \quad(i,r\geq0),\qquad \sum_{r\geq0}\int|c_r|\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathbb P<\infty. \] This is an explicit integrable absolute-summability interpretation of the aid's covariance-cocycle hypothesis. It reports \[ \frac{v_n(\omega)}n\longrightarrow\sigma_q^2 :=\int c_0\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathbb P+2\sum_{r\geq1}\int c_r\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathbb P \quad\text{for almost every }\omega. \] The Green--Kubo value is deterministic. If $\sigma_q^2>0$, the variance self-normalizer satisfies $\sqrt{v_n(\omega)}\sim\sigma_q\sqrt n$. A variance limit alone is not a quenched CLT.

Assume in addition the nondegenerate fiber-centered quenched CLT \[ \mathcal L_{Q_\omega}\!\left(\frac{S_n-m_n(\omega)}{\sqrt n}\right) \Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma_q^2)\quad\text{for almost every }\omega. \] For a deterministic real sequence $d_n$, the aid reports the exact centering criterion \[ \mathcal L_{Q_\omega}\!\left(\frac{S_n-d_n}{\sqrt n}\right) \Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma_q^2)\ \text{a.s.} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m_n(\omega)-d_n=o(\sqrt n)\ \text{a.s.} \] It is this pathwise error, not determinism of the fiber variance, that controls replacing the random fiber mean.

When $S_n$ has a finite second moment under the joint annealed law $\mathbb P({\,\mathrm{d}}\omega)Q_\omega$, the exact total-variance identity is \[ \operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{ann}}(S_n) =\int v_n(\omega)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathbb P(\omega) +\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P}(m_n). \] Under the displayed covariance summability, the first term divided by $n$ tends to $\sigma_q^2$. Equality of the raw annealed asymptotic variance with this value therefore requires $n^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P}(m_n)\to0$. Almost-sure smallness of a centering error and this second-moment condition are distinct; neither is silently inferred from the other.

The reported obstructions admit explicit Gaussian constructions. In an ergodic Bernoulli environment $\xi_i\in\{-1,1\}$, take conditionally independent $X_i=\xi_i(\omega)+Z_i$, where the $Z_i$ are independent standard Gaussians independent of the environment. Then $v_n=n$, the fiber-centered normalized sum is exactly standard Gaussian, but $m_n=\sum_{i<n}\xi_i$ is not $o(\sqrt n)$ almost surely. The deterministic center zero thus fails the stated criterion, while the raw annealed variance is $2n$. For a separate stationary nonergodic example, take an invariant environment parameter $V$ equal to one or four with positive probabilities and, conditionally, iid $X_i\sim N(0,V)$. The fiber means are zero but the quenched variance coefficient is the component-dependent $V$. These examples separate environment-independent scale, deterministic centering and agreement with raw annealed variance. No arbitrary-process universality statement or CLT beyond the stated additional hypothesis is asserted.

Short English statement

The aid distinguishes three issues: deterministic quenched variance under ergodic covariance assumptions, replacement of the random fiber mean by a deterministic center, and equality with raw annealed variance. They require different conditions and are separated by explicit Gaussian examples.
