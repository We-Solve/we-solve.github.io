Near-critical solitary gravity waves: recovered status and a two-term KP-II dispersion audit

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The recovered target is Y. Li's question about stability of finite-depth pure-gravity solitary water waves near their critical speed. The subsequent S. Benzoni-Gavage "Shocks with Capillarity" equations and spectral discussion belong to the next presenter, not to this water-wave problem. The preceding companion record contains the water-wave setup, although its displayed nonlinear equations are themselves extraction-damaged.

With depth normalized to one and gravitational acceleration $g>0$, let $(\eta_c,\Phi_c)$ denote a solitary traveling surface elevation and surface velocity potential, depending on $x-ct$ and approaching the rest state at infinity. The flat-surface Dirichlet-to-Neumann multiplier is $q\tanh q$, giving critical long-wave speed \[ c_* =\sqrt g. \] The question asks how to determine stability of these waves for $c$ near $c_*$. The supplied aid distinguishes longitudinal and transverse linear asymptotic and spectral stability from nonlinear stability of the full free-boundary system. It reports longitudinal linear results of Pego--Sun (2016) for small solitary waves and transverse linear results of Rousset--Sun (2025) for small line solitary waves, while leaving nonlinear full-system stability open. The relevant functional spaces, weights, perturbation classes and exact theorem hypotheses are not included in the record; no unrestricted linear or nonlinear theorem is asserted here.

The aid separately provides an exact flat-state dispersion benchmark, not a stability proof for the nonlinear wave. For longitudinal and transverse frequencies $k,\ell$, set \[ q=\sqrt{k^2+\ell^2},\qquad \omega(k,\ell)=\sqrt{gq\tanh q}. \] Use the right-going sector \[ k=\varepsilon K,\quad\ell=\varepsilon^2L,\quad c=\frac{\sqrt g}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}},\quad 0<\kappa\le K\le M,\quad |L|\le M. \] For fixed $\kappa,M$, as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the reported expansion is uniform on this sector: \[ \begin{split} \frac{\omega(k,\ell)-ck}{\sqrt g} ={}&\varepsilon^3 \left(\frac{L^2}{2K}-\frac K2-\frac{K^3}{6}\right)\\ &+\varepsilon^5 \left(-\frac{L^4}{8K^3}-\frac{KL^2}{4} +\frac{19K^5}{360}-\frac{3K}{8}\right) +O_{\kappa,M}(\varepsilon^7). \end{split} \] Its leading neutral curve is \[ L^2=K^2+\frac{K^4}{3}. \] On compact subsets of the stated sector separated from that curve, the moving-frame frequency has the sign of the leading displayed coefficient for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. This records the aid's KP-II sign check; it neither classifies the spectrum linearized about a solitary wave nor establishes nonlinear stability. The lower bound $K\ge\kappa>0$ is essential to this uniform expansion and is not silently extended to $K=0$.

Short English statement

Determine near-critical stability of finite-depth gravity solitary waves. The supplied assessment separates published small-wave linear results from the remaining nonlinear question and gives a uniform two-term KP-II dispersion check; the adjacent capillarity-shock problem is separate.
