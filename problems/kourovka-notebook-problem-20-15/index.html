Math-3139

Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.15

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal. For a set $X$, let $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ denote the group of all bijections from $X$ to itself, under composition. An embedding of groups means an injective group homomorphism. A group $G$ is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1_G\}$, there exist a finite group $F$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ such that $\varphi(g)\ne 1_F$.

Question (G. M. Bergman, Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.15). For every infinite cardinal $\kappa$ and every residually finite group $G$, does the existence of a set $X$ with $|X|=\kappa$ and an embedding \[ G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(X) \] imply that there exist an index set $I$ with $|I|=\kappa$, finite groups $F_i$ for all $i\in I$, and an embedding \[ G\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}F_i\,? \] Here the product is the unrestricted direct product, with coordinatewise multiplication. The factors need not be distinct, and trivial factors are permitted; thus indexing by exactly $\kappa$ is equivalent to allowing at most $\kappa$ factors.

Accompanying observations supplied by the source. The source states that the converse holds: every direct product of $\kappa$ finite groups is residually finite and embeds in the full permutation group of a set of cardinality $\kappa$. It also states that the requested implication holds if the number of finite factors is increased to $2^\kappa$, where $2^\kappa$ is the cardinality of the power set of a set of cardinality $\kappa$. The supplied explanation is that an embedding into $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ gives $|G|\leq 2^\kappa$, so residual finiteness supplies at most $2^\kappa$ homomorphisms to finite groups which together separate every nonidentity element from the identity. These observations are retained as source context, not as a resolution of the question with $\kappa$ factors.

Short English statement

Must every residually finite group admitting a faithful permutation action on a set of infinite cardinality $\kappa$ embed into a direct product of $\kappa$ finite groups? The supplied source states the converse and the version with $2^\kappa$ factors. The registry labels the question open; that status has not been independently verified.
