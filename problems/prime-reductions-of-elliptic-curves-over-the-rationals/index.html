Math-2045

Prime reductions of elliptic curves over the rationals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $E/{\mathbb{Q}}$ be an elliptic curve and interpret torsion free as $E({\mathbb{Q}})_{\mathrm{tors}}=\{O\}$. This does not mean $E({\mathbb{Q}})$ has rank zero. At a prime $p$ of good reduction, let $N_p=\#E({\mathbb{F}}_p)$ and define \[ \pi_E(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\text{ prime of good reduction and }N_p\text{ prime}\}. \] The literal source asks whether every such torsion-free $E$ satisfies $\pi_E(x)\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$.

The source summary relates this to Koblitz's conjecture and its refined local/entanglement obstructions. Trivial rational torsion alone does not state that all divisibility obstructions in the isogeny/Galois representation data have been excluded. The summary reports curves with no prime orders, but does not identify a curve or verify which of the source's torsion/admissibility hypotheses it meets. Therefore the universal literal assertion and the intended unobstructed version must be checked separately.

In a refined asymptotic formulation one expects, for suitable curves with positive conjectural constant, \[ \pi_E(x)\sim C_E\frac{x}{(\log x)^2},\qquad C_E>0. \] The record supplies no definition of $C_E$ or exact admissibility criterion. Positivity cannot simply be assumed from the word torsion-free, and the original request is infinitude, weaker than this asymptotic. Results averaged over curves and counts of almost-prime group orders do not establish prime-order infinitude for a specified fixed curve.

Short English statement

For a fixed rational elliptic curve without rational torsion, are there infinitely many good primes with prime group order? The refined conjecture needs a precise absence-of-obstructions condition, which the source does not provide.
