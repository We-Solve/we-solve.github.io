Combinatorial Invariance of Strong Lefschetz Properties

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks whether a particular Lefschetz property is determined by the combinatorics of an arrangement. It does not specify the base field, its characteristic, the ambient polynomial ring, or the admissible values of the exponent. The following is a conditional formulation using the usual maximal-rank interpretation of the Lefschetz terminology; these missing parameters require source verification.

Fix an ambient standard graded polynomial ring $R=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$, with $\deg x_i=1$. Here $k$ and $n$ are placeholders for the intended ambient data, not additional universally quantified parameters of a verified conjecture. For a homogeneous ideal $K\subseteq R$, write $A_K=R/K$ and let $(A_K)_d$ denote its degree-$d$ component. Under the usual interpretation, having the Strong Lefschetz Property at range $2$ in degree $b-2$ means that, for a general linear form $L\in R_1$, the multiplication map \[ \mu_{K,L}\colon (A_K)_{b-2}\longrightarrow (A_K)_b, \qquad \overline f\longmapsto\overline{L^2f}, \] has maximal rank, namely \[ \operatorname{rank}(\mu_{K,L}) =\min\{\dim_k(A_K)_{b-2},\dim_k(A_K)_b\}. \] Over an infinite field, the existence of such an $L$ is equivalent to this condition holding on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $R_1$. The record does not specify the field or its convention for the word "general." This displayed formulation directly applies when $b\geq2$; the intended lower bound on $b$ is not supplied.

For nonzero linear forms $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{2b+1}$, a standard interpretation of their arrangement is the central hyperplane arrangement \[ \mathcal A_{\ell}=\{\ker(\ell_i)\subseteq k^{n+1}:1\leq i\leq2b+1\}. \] Its intersection lattice consists of all intersections of subfamilies, including the ambient space as the intersection of the empty subfamily, ordered by reverse inclusion. Under the usual interpretation, two arrangements have the same combinatorics when their intersection lattices are isomorphic. The source record does not explicitly define this convention or identify the central or projective setting.

With these interpretations and the intended ambient parameters fixed, the question is whether, for every pair of lists of linear forms $(\ell_i)_{i=1}^{2b+1}$ and $(h_i)_{i=1}^{2b+1}$, the following implication holds. Set \[ I=(\ell_1^b,\ldots,\ell_{2b+1}^b), \qquad J=(h_1^b,\ldots,h_{2b+1}^b). \] Assume that both displayed generating lists are minimal, meaning that none of their generators belongs to the ideal generated by the others, and that $\mathcal A_{\ell}$ and $\mathcal A_h$ have isomorphic intersection lattices. Must maximal rank of $\mu_{I,L}$ for general $L$ imply maximal rank of $\mu_{J,M}$ for general $M\in R_1$? The general forms $L$ and $M$ need not be identical.

The supplied background concerns line arrangements, suggesting a projective-plane setting, but the statement itself does not specify it. No assertion for arbitrary dimensions or characteristics, and no additional Artinian hypothesis on the quotients, is supplied or established here.

Short English statement

If an ideal minimally generated by $2b+1$ powers of linear forms has the specified degree-wise Lefschetz property, must every such ideal with the same arrangement combinatorics have it too? The intended field, dimension, exponent range, and precise conventions need verification. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
