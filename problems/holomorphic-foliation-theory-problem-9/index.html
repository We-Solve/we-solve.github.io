Holomorphic Foliation Theory — Problem 9

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let \[ Z=a(z_1,z_2)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_1}+b(z_1,z_2)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_2},\qquad a,b\in\mathcal O({\mathbb{C}}^2), \] be a holomorphic vector field. Write $\operatorname{Sing}(Z)=\{a=b=0\}$ and $M={\mathbb{C}}^2\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(Z)$. For $Z\not\equiv0$, let $\mathcal L_Z$ denote the regular leaves: the maximal connected immersed Riemann surfaces in $M$ tangent to $Z$. Let $\mathbb D=\{\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\zeta|<1\}$. Interpret a leaf as being covered by ${\mathbb{C}}$, respectively $\mathbb D$, when its universal covering Riemann surface is biholomorphic to ${\mathbb{C}}$, respectively $\mathbb D$. Set \[ \mathcal P_Z=\{L\in\mathcal L_Z:\widetilde L\simeq{\mathbb{C}}\},\qquad \mathcal H_Z=\{L\in\mathcal L_Z:\widetilde L\simeq\mathbb D\}, \] and define their saturated unions \[ P_Z=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal P_Z}L,\qquad H_Z=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal H_Z}L. \] Here $\widetilde L$ denotes the universal cover. The term parabolic is interpreted below in this uniformization sense, namely $\widetilde L\simeq{\mathbb{C}}$; the supplied record does not explicitly define that term.

The source asks the following three questions.

  1. (i) Can a holomorphic vector field on ${\mathbb{C}}^2$ have both a positive-measure family of leaves covered by ${\mathbb{C}}$ and a positive-measure family of leaves covered by $\mathbb D$? The source does not specify the measure or the space on which it is defined. If positive measure refers to the saturated unions measured by ambient four-dimensional Lebesgue measure $\lambda_4$, the question becomes whether there exists $Z\not\equiv0$ for which both unions are measurable and \[ \lambda_4(P_Z)>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_4(H_Z)>0. \] This is a conditional rendering of the measure qualification, not a determination of the source's intended convention.
  2. (ii) If a non-pluripolar family of leaves is parabolic, must every leaf be parabolic? Under the interpretation that non-pluripolarity refers to the union of these leaves in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$, this asks whether, for every $Z\not\equiv0$, \[ P_Z\text{ is non-pluripolar in }{\mathbb{C}}^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal P_Z=\mathcal L_Z. \] A set is pluripolar if locally it is contained in the $-\infty$ locus of a plurisubharmonic function that is not identically $-\infty$ on the relevant connected neighborhood; non-pluripolar means not pluripolar. The source does not specify whether its qualification concerns saturated unions or intersections with local transversals, so that choice remains unresolved.
  3. (iii) Find conditions on $Z$, or on its induced foliation, sufficient to ensure \[ \forall L\in\mathcal L_Z,\qquad \widetilde L\simeq\mathbb D. \] No prescribed class of sufficient conditions is given.
The use of regular leaves and the restriction $Z\not\equiv0$ make explicit the usual one-dimensional foliation setting. The source does not state these conventions or explain how singular points or the identically zero vector field should be treated; those qualifications require review.

Short English statement

For a holomorphic vector field on ${\mathbb{C}}^2$, ask whether positive-measure families of plane-covered and disc-covered leaves can coexist, whether a non-pluripolar family of parabolic leaves forces every leaf to be parabolic, and which conditions force every leaf to be disc-covered. The measure, the meaning of a non-pluripolar family, and the treatment of singularities require clarification.
