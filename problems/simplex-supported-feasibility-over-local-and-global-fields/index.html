Simplex-supported feasibility over local and global fields

Question 17 (Rojas). For a fixed prime $p$, decide whether \[ \exists x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_p^n\quad f(x)=0, \] and determine whether there is an algorithm polynomial in the input size. Also determine whether feasibility $\exists x\in{\mathbb{Q}}^n\ f(x)=0$ is decidable. Dimension, sparse exponent data and coefficients form the input; a precise complexity statement must specify their encoding, ordinarily binary, rather than fixing the dimension silently. The source attributes to Poonen an encoding of $0/1$ knapsack and the answer "NO" to the polynomial-time question. Without a precise reduction and complexity hypothesis, this is recorded as a hardness assertion, not an unconditional proof that no polynomial algorithm exists. The source also states that the real version is in NP and probably in P, modulo technicalities; its computational model and qualifications are not supplied. For the rational question it highlights \[ ax^3+by^3=1, \] whose projective completion is a smooth genus-one curve when $ab\ne0$, and reports difficulty of deciding rational solubility. The original question concerns affine solutions; rational points at infinity are not silently substituted for them.

Reported local-field special case. Assume every $c_j\ne0$ and $p\nmid\Delta$. Let $v_p(p)=1$. For $w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n$, set \[ m_w=\min_j\{v_p(c_j)+a_j\cdot w\},\quad S_w=\{j:v_p(c_j)+a_j\cdot w=m_w\},\quad \operatorname{in}_w(f)(X)=\sum_{j\in S_w} \overline{p^{-v_p(c_j)}c_j}\,X^{a_j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_p[X_1,\ldots,X_n]. \] The research aid reports the exact criterion \[ f^{-1}(0)\cap({\mathbb{Q}}_p^*)^n\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n\ \exists u\in({\mathbb{F}}_p^*)^n\quad \operatorname{in}_w(f)(u)=0. \] It reports that every residue zero appearing here is nonsingular, by independence modulo $p$ of the augmented exponent columns, and lifts by Hensel's lemma, varying a coordinate with nonzero partial derivative. This criterion concerns torus roots only and does not itself supply a polynomial-time search over $w$, nor cover $p\mid\Delta$.

Reported unimodular special case. If every coefficient remains nonzero in an infinite field $K$ and $|\Delta|=1$, the exponent-difference map \[ (K^*)^n\longrightarrow(K^*)^n,\qquad x\longmapsto\left(x^{a_2-a_1},\ldots,x^{a_{n+1}-a_1}\right) \] is a torus automorphism. The aid reports a torus root of $f$ over every such $K$, in particular over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ and every ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$. It does not assert this over all finite fields.

Zero coordinates. The affine question is the disjunction over coordinate strata. For $Z\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, substitute $x_i=0$ for $i\in Z$, retaining only monomials with $a_{ij}=0$ for all $i\in Z$. The resulting polynomial must have a zero in $(K^*)^{\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus Z}$. An identically zero restriction satisfies that stratum, whereas a nonzero constant does not. This exact reduction includes the all-zero point and prevents a torus criterion from being mistaken for the original affine decision problem. Short English statement

Ask for local polynomial-time feasibility and global rational decidability for simplex-supported sparse polynomials. The aid supplies a torus criterion when the prime avoids the exponent determinant, a unimodular sufficient case, and a reduction of affine feasibility to coordinate strata. The stated hardness and real-field complexity claims need their missing reduction and computational conventions.
