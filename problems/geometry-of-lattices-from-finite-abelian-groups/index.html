Math-6770

Geometry of Lattices from Finite Abelian Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, written additively, and let $S=\{0=g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1}\}\subseteq G$ consist of $n\geq 1$ distinct elements. No assumption that $S$ generates $G$ is imposed. Give ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ its standard Euclidean inner product, and define \[ A_{n-1}=\left\{(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n:\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_j=0\right\},\qquad L_G(S)=\left\{x\in A_{n-1}:\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_jg_j=0\text{ in }G\right\}. \] Here integer multiples of elements of $G$ are interpreted using its additive group structure. Write $V=\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}L_G(S)$. The defining group homomorphism has finite image, so $L_G(S)$ has rank $n-1$ and $V=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\sum_jx_j=0\}$.

For $n\geq2$, define the minimum length, squared minimum, and set of minimal vectors by \[ \lambda_1(L)=\min_{0\ne v\in L}\|v\|_2,\qquad m(L)=\lambda_1(L)^2,\qquad \operatorname{Min}(L)=\{v\in L:\|v\|_2=\lambda_1(L)\}, \quad L=L_G(S). \] The count $|\operatorname{Min}(L)|$ counts opposite vectors separately. The lattice is well-rounded if $\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\operatorname{Min}(L)=V$. A basis of minimal vectors means a ${\mathbb{Z}}$-basis of $L$ whose members all belong to $\operatorname{Min}(L)$.

For a ${\mathbb{Z}}$-basis $b_1,\ldots,b_{n-1}$, define the Euclidean covolume and Gram determinant by \[ \operatorname{covol}_V(L)=\sqrt{\det\left(\langle b_i,b_j\rangle\right)_{i,j=1}^{n-1}},\qquad \operatorname{disc}(L)=\det\left(\langle b_i,b_j\rangle\right)_{i,j=1}^{n-1}. \] The source does not specify whether its term "determinant" denotes the covolume or its square; both quantities are displayed to retain this unresolved convention. Likewise, both minimum length and squared minimum are specified because the source does not fix its norm terminology.

The full Euclidean lattice automorphism group is \[ \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{Euc}}(L)=\{T\in O(V):T(L)=L\}, \] where $O(V)$ is the group of linear isometries of $V$. Its coordinate-permutation subgroup consists of restrictions to $V$ of permutations of the $n$ ambient coordinates that preserve $L$. The source's intended meaning of "automorphism group" is not explicitly defined; the full Euclidean group and the coordinate-permutation subgroup must therefore be distinguished.

Problem. For every finite abelian group $G$ and every subset $S$ as above, determine, in terms of $G$ and $S$, the minimum length or squared minimum of $L_G(S)$, its determinant under the intended convention, its number of minimal vectors, necessary and sufficient conditions for well-roundedness and for the existence of a basis of minimal vectors, and its automorphism group under the intended interpretation. This is a determination and classification problem, not an assertion that every such lattice is well-rounded or admits a basis of minimal vectors.

The permitted case $n=1$ gives $L_G(\{0\})=\{0\}$. There is then no nonzero vector over which to take a minimum. The source supplies no conventions for minimum-related questions or well-roundedness in rank zero; these boundary questions remain explicitly unresolved.

For $S=G$, write $L_G=L_G(G)$. The supplied dated assessment describes determinant, minimal-vector counts, well-roundedness, minimal bases, and a permutation part of the automorphism group for these full-group lattices as known. It describes results for general $L_G(S)$ as limited to special families and subgroup results. These are supplied literature qualifications, not independently verified mathematical conclusions, and they do not specify the individual theorems, exceptional cases, or families involved.

Short English statement

For each finite abelian group $G$ and subset $S$ containing zero, study the integer vectors whose coordinates sum to zero and whose corresponding group-valued sum vanishes. Determine the resulting lattice's shortest vectors, determinant, spanning and basis properties, and symmetries. The source labels the problem partially solved; determinant and automorphism conventions, rank-zero behavior, and the precise scope of the reported known results require review.
