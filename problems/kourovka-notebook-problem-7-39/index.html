Kourovka Notebook Problem 7.39

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $\sigma\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ satisfy $p\nmid\sigma$. Interpret the exponent $2/\sigma$ in the supplied presentation as division modulo $p$: choose $r\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $\sigma r\equiv2\pmod p$, and set \[ G_{p,\sigma}=\left\langle a,b\ \middle|\ a^p=(ab)^3=b^2=(a^\sigma b a^r b)^2=1\right\rangle. \] The relation $a^p=1$ makes this presentation independent of the representative chosen for $r$, and it also makes $\sigma$ depend only on its residue modulo $p$. This modular interpretation of $2/\sigma$ is needed to read the presentation as an ordinary group presentation; the source does not explicitly define that notation.

Write $\operatorname{PSL}_2(p)=\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p)/Z(\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p))$. The source asserts the existence of an epimorphism $\pi_{p,\sigma}:G_{p,\sigma}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{PSL}_2(p)$. For the epimorphism intended by the source, put \[ N_{p,\sigma}=\ker\pi_{p,\sigma},\qquad N_{p,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ab}}=N_{p,\sigma}/[N_{p,\sigma},N_{p,\sigma}], \] where $[N,N]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in N$. Thus the asserted sequence is \[ 1\longrightarrow N_{p,\sigma}\longrightarrow G_{p,\sigma} \xrightarrow{\pi_{p,\sigma}}\operatorname{PSL}_2(p)\longrightarrow1. \] The epimorphism is not specified in the supplied record, nor is independence of the resulting abelianization from its choice established there. The following questions therefore refer to the intended quotient map.

  1. (a) Is the set \[ \left\{p\text{ prime}:\ \text{for every }\sigma\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ with }p\nmid\sigma,\quad |N_{p,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ab}}|<\infty\right\} \] infinite?
  2. (b) Is there an arithmetic condition on $\sigma$ ensuring that $N_{p,\sigma}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is finite? The source does not specify the intended scope of such a condition, its permitted dependence on $p$, or what additional information it should provide beyond the sufficient example below.

As supplied background, the record states that for every prime $p>2$ there is an admissible $\sigma$ with $N_{p,\sigma}=1$, and gives $\sigma=4$ as an example. It also states that for some primes an admissible $\sigma$ gives infinite abelianization, citing $p=41$ with $\sigma^2\equiv2\pmod{41}$, whereas for some other primes the abelianization is finite for every admissible $\sigma$, citing $p=43$. The wording of the $p=41$ example does not explicitly distinguish existence of a suitable root from a claim about both roots. These background assertions are retained as supplied, without independent verification.

Short English statement

For Mennicke's presented groups and their intended quotients onto $\operatorname{PSL}_2(p)$, ask whether infinitely many primes give kernels with finite abelianization for every admissible parameter, and seek an arithmetic condition guaranteeing this finiteness. The modular exponent notation, choice of quotient map, and intended scope of the second question require clarification.
