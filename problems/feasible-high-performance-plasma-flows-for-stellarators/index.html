A geometry-preserving search neighborhood.
Start with the standard embedded torus \[ X(\theta,\varphi)=((R+r\cos\theta)\cos\varphi, (R+r\cos\theta)\sin\varphi,r\sin\theta),\qquad R>r>0. \] Its cylindrical radius is strictly positive, so $\varphi$ and then $\theta$ are recovered from the point modulo their natural periods; this proves injectivity on the parameter torus. For an ambient displacement $v:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$ with Lipschitz constant $L<1$, set $F(x)=x+v(x)$. Then \[ (1-L)|x-y|\leq|F(x)-F(y)|\leq(1+L)|x-y|. \] Thus $F\circ X$ has no self-intersections. If $v$ is continuously differentiable, $\|Dv\|<1$ also ensures that the differential of $F$ is invertible and preserves the surface immersion.
A meaningful but limited constraint.
This gives an explicit admissible geometric neighborhood and a lower bound on pairwise separation under deformation. It does not enforce prescribed curvature, coil spacing, manufacturability tolerances or magnetic topology. Merely requiring a local positive Jacobian would be weaker than the global separation bound and would not on its own rule out distant self-intersections.
