Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.8

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In a standard analytic convention, a quasiconformal map in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ is a Sobolev homeomorphism with bounded distortion. To make one common maximal-dilatation convention explicit, put \[ K_O(f)=\inf\{K\ge1:\|Df\|^3\le K|J_f|\text{ a.e.}\},\qquad K_I(f)=\inf\{K\ge1:|J_f|\le K\ell(Df)^3\text{ a.e.}\}, \] \[ K(f)=\max\{K_O(f),K_I(f)\}, \] where $\|Df\|$ and $\ell(Df)$ are the largest and smallest singular values and $J_f$ is the Jacobian. These inequalities avoid an undefined ratio at a zero derivative. The source does not specify whether maximal dilatation means this two-sided quantity, outer dilatation alone, or another convention; the distinction matters in dimension three.

On the global self-homeomorphism interpretation of "in $3$-space", Gehring asks whether \[ \forall K>1\ \forall f:{\mathbb{R}}^3\to{\mathbb{R}}^3\text{ quasiconformal with }K(f)\le K, \] \[ \exists g,h:{\mathbb{R}}^3\to{\mathbb{R}}^3\text{ quasiconformal},\qquad f=g\circ h,\qquad K(g)<K,\quad K(h)<K. \] The factors may depend on $f$ and $K$. No quantitative uniform improvement below $K$, formula for the factors, or prescribed allocation of distortion is requested. Requiring the strict improvement relative to the actual value $K(f)>1$ is another natural precise phrasing of the same intended factorization issue, but the source does not distinguish exact from upper-bound use of the label $K$.

The condition $K>1$ is necessary for this strict wording: every maximal dilatation is at least one, so the source's unqualified $K=1$ case cannot ask for both factors to have dilatation below one. This endpoint is flagged rather than silently included in an impossible assertion. The extract also leaves open whether the maps are global self-maps or homeomorphisms between arbitrary domains, what intermediate domain is allowed, and whether orientation preservation is required. For a domain version the appropriate formulation would be $h:\Omega\to U$, $g:U\to\Omega'$ with $f=g\circ h$, but neither the domain class nor $U$ is supplied.

The source says the corresponding planar result is true. Its supplied triage labels the three-dimensional question open as of the 2018 edition and reports no definitive later resolution. These are attributed source statements, not independently verified results.

Short English statement

Can every nonconformal quasiconformal map in three dimensions be factored into two maps each with strictly smaller maximal dilatation? The strict comparison requires $K>1$. The source leaves the global-versus-domain setting and dilatation convention unspecified and reports only that the planar analogue is true.
