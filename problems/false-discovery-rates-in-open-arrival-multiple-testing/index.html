Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a research question rather than a fully specified conjecture. The following notation makes its false-discovery-rate target explicit; the arrival model and admissible procedures remain unspecified.

For an arrival-indexed formulation, let $\mathcal P$ be a class of probability laws and let $H_i\subseteq\mathcal P$ denote the null hypothesis with arrival index $i$. For $P\in\mathcal P$, write $I_0(P)=\{i:P\in H_i\}$ for the indices of true null hypotheses. A $p$-value for $H_i$ is a measurable random variable $p_i\in[0,1]$ satisfying \[ P(p_i\le u)\le u\qquad(P\in H_i,\ u\in[0,1]). \] An $e$-value for $H_i$ is a nonnegative measurable random variable $e_i$ satisfying \[ \mathbb E_P[e_i]\le 1\qquad(P\in H_i). \] These are marginal validity definitions. They do not specify conditional validity relative to past observations, dependence between tests, or validity under optional sampling.

Let $\mathcal R_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}$ be the random set of rejected hypotheses after $t$ arrivals. Define \[ R_t=|\mathcal R_t|,\qquad V_t(P)=|\mathcal R_t\cap I_0(P)|, \qquad \operatorname{FDR}_P(t)=\mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{V_t(P)}{\max\{R_t,1\}}\right]. \] Thus the false discovery proportion is zero when there are no rejections. Indexing by arrivals here supplies notation only; it does not determine how evidence is observed, when decisions must be made, or whether past decisions can change.

The question in the supplied clean statement is: what false-discovery-rate guarantees can be obtained for multiple-testing procedures when hypotheses arrive over time, using $p$-values or $e$-values? For a chosen level $\alpha\in(0,1)$, one possible precise target would be to identify explicit assumptions and procedures for which \[ \operatorname{FDR}_P(t)\le\alpha \qquad\text{for every admissible }P\text{ and every deterministic arrival index }t. \] This displayed target is an illustrative specification of the requested guarantees, not a conjecture explicitly stated in the source. Short English statement

The question asks which false-discovery-rate guarantees are possible when hypotheses arrive over time, using either $p$-values or $e$-values. FDR is the expected fraction of rejected hypotheses that are true nulls, with fraction zero when there are no rejections. The supplied record leaves the arrival model, dependence assumptions, decision rules, and evaluation times unresolved; the original excerpt discusses a closed setting with a predetermined number $K$ of null hypotheses.
