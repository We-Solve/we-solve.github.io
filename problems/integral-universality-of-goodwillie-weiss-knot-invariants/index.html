Math-4868

Integral Universality of Goodwillie-Weiss Knot Invariants

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed knot model, let $\mathcal K$ be its set of isotopy classes of classical knots in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$, and let ${\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal K]$ be the free abelian group on this set. The source does not specify whether knots are long or closed, oriented or unoriented, or framed or unframed; these choices must be fixed consistently before specifying the embedding-calculus tower.

For $r\geq 1$, let $F_r\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal K]$ be generated by the alternating resolution sums \[ \sum_{\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}^{r}}(-1)^{\#\{i:\varepsilon_i=-\}}[K_{\varepsilon}], \] where a singular knot has exactly $r$ transverse double points and $K_{\varepsilon}$ denotes the knot obtained by resolving each double point with the indicated crossing sign. For an abelian group $A$, an invariant $v:\mathcal K\to A$ has finite type at most $m$ if its linear extension to ${\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal K]$ vanishes on $F_{m+1}$. Thus \[ U_m={\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal K]/F_{m+1},\qquad u_m(K)=[K]\bmod F_{m+1}, \] is a universal integral invariant of type at most $m$: every such $A$-valued invariant factors uniquely through a group homomorphism $U_m\to A$.

Let $\mathscr K$ denote the chosen space of knots, and write \[ \eta_n:\mathscr K\longrightarrow T_n\mathscr K \] for its map to the $n$th Goodwillie--Weiss approximation, once the knot model and tower convention are fixed. The supplied question asks whether, for each applicable positive integer $n$, this approximation gives a universal integral finite-type invariant of order at most $n-1$, without inverting any primes. In the universal abelian-group formulation above, this asks whether the invariant extracted from the stage has the universal property of $u_{n-1}$, with coefficients over ${\mathbb{Z}}$.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The source does not specify the invariant extracted from $T_n\mathscr K$, its target and algebraic structure, the precise meaning of universality, or the admissible low values of $n$. Consequently, the universal abelian-group formulation explains a standard meaning of integral universality, but its identification with the intended tower-stage assertion requires review. No identification of $\pi_0(T_n\mathscr K)$ with $U_{n-1}$ is asserted here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the $n$th Goodwillie--Weiss approximation captures universally all finite-type knot invariants of order at most $n-1$ over the integers, without inverting primes. A precise tower-stage statement still requires the knot model, indexing convention, target invariant, and meaning of universality to be specified.
