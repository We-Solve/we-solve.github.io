Heat Flows into Sum-of-Squares Cones

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt does not define $\operatorname{Pos}(n,m)$, $\operatorname{SOS}(n,m)$, or the ambient space on which the operators act. The following precise reconstruction is therefore conditional on a polynomial interpretation; these definitions require confirmation from the source.

For an integer $n\geq 1$ and an even integer $m\geq 0$, let \[ V_{n,m}={\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_{\leq m}, \qquad \operatorname{Pos}(n,m)=\{p\in V_{n,m}:p(x)\geq 0\text{ for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n\}, \] and let \[ \operatorname{SOS}(n,m)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}q_j^2:r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\ q_j\in{\mathbb{R}}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_{\leq m/2}\right\}, \] where the empty sum is zero. Here the degree bound is total degree. Define the Laplacian on this space by \[ \Delta_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i^2}. \] For a real linear endomorphism $A:V_{n,m}\to V_{n,m}$, define \[ e^{tA}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^kA^k}{k!}. \] In particular, $e^{t\Delta_n}$ is well defined on $V_{n,m}$, and its series terminates.

Under these conditional definitions, the two questions extracted from the original statement are as follows.

  1. Determine the integers $d\in{\mathbb{N}}$ for which \[ \exists\,\tau_{2,2d}\in[0,\infty)\quad e^{\tau_{2,2d}\Delta_2}\operatorname{Pos}(2,2d) \subseteq\operatorname{SOS}(2,2d). \] The same time $\tau_{2,2d}$ must work for every polynomial in the indicated cone. The displayed original question requires inclusion at this one time; it does not explicitly quantify over all later times.
  2. For each pair $(n,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times2{\mathbb{N}}$, ask whether there exist a real linear endomorphism $A:V_{n,m}\to V_{n,m}$ and a number $\tau_{n,m}(A)\in[0,\infty)$ such that \[ \forall\,t\in[\tau_{n,m}(A),\infty)\quad e^{tA}\operatorname{Pos}(n,m) \subseteq\operatorname{SOS}(n,m). \] Here $m$ denotes the degree parameter called $d$ in the original second question. Both $A$ and the threshold may depend on the pair $(n,m)$, but neither may depend on the input polynomial. The cleaned statement also asks which linear flows have this property, suggesting a characterization in addition to the displayed existence question.

Several qualifications remain unresolved. The cleaned statement calls the inputs positive binary forms, which ordinarily suggests homogeneous polynomials in two variables, whereas the reconstruction above uses polynomials of bounded degree and interprets positivity as nonnegativity on all of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. The ordinary Laplacian lowers homogeneous degree by two, so its exponential does not generally preserve the space of forms of a fixed degree. Thus the homogeneous formulation cannot simply be substituted into the displayed reconstruction without additional definitions. Strict positivity, any normalization, the precise admissible class of operators $A$, and the convention concerning zero in ${\mathbb{N}}$ are not specified in the supplied excerpt. No such additional hypotheses are asserted here.

Short English statement

Conditionally interpreting the cones as globally nonnegative polynomials and sums of polynomial squares of bounded degree, the first question asks which bivariate even degrees admit one heat-flow time that works for every input. The second asks whether each dimension and even degree admit a linear flow whose images lie in the sum-of-squares cone at every sufficiently large time. The source terminology concerning forms, positivity, and admissible operators needs clarification before this reconstruction can be accepted.
