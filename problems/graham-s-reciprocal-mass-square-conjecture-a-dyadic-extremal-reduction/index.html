Graham's reciprocal-mass square conjecture: a dyadic extremal reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 1.10 (R. Graham). Let $A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^2$ satisfy \[ \sum_{(x,y)\in A\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\frac1{x^2+y^2}=\infty. \] Must there be $x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $a\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}$ with $(x,y),(x+a,y),(x,y+a),(x+a,y+a)\in A$? More generally, for each fixed $k$, must $A$ contain $\{(x+ia,y+ja):0\leq i,j<k\}$ with common nonzero integer step? The source advertises a \$1000 conjecture. It does not exclude the origin or zero side length explicitly; the displayed exclusions avoid an undefined summand and a degenerate square. The intended squares are axis-aligned, with equal steps in both coordinates.

Reported dyadic reduction. Let \[ S_j=\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2:2^j\leq\|z\|_\infty<2^{j+1}\},\qquad \alpha_j=\frac{|A\cap S_j|}{|S_j|},\quad j\geq0. \] The aid proves that reciprocal mass diverges exactly when $\sum_j\alpha_j$ diverges, up to absolute comparison constants, since $|S_j|\asymp4^j$ and $x^2+y^2\asymp4^j$ there. Any square-free counterexample must have $\alpha_j\to0$ but $\sum_j\alpha_j=\infty$; the vanishing uses the finite positive-density square theorem, not the reciprocal comparison alone.

Define $r_\square(N)$ to be the largest cardinality of a subset of $[N]^2$ containing no nondegenerate axis-aligned square. The aid's sufficient condition for Graham's square conclusion is \[ \sum_{j\geq1}\frac{r_\square(2^j)}{4^j}<\infty. \] Bounding shell pieces by a translated box changes only an absolute dyadic index. In particular, a bound \[ r_\square(N)=O\!\left(\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^{1+\varepsilon}}\right) \qquad\text{for some fixed }\varepsilon>0 \] would imply the conjecture. This bound is a needed sufficient input, not proved in the report.

The aid reports an explicit zero-upper-density set with divergent reciprocal mass, demonstrating why a positive-density theorem alone does not settle the question. Its construction and upper-density convention are not printed in the summary; such an example is not asserted square-free. Natural centered-box density and upper Banach density must not be identified without checking that convention.

Reported down-set case. Under the positive-quadrant convention, a coordinatewise down-set $A\subset{\mathbb{N}}^2$ satisfies $(x,y)\in A\Rightarrow[1,x]\times[1,y]\subset A$. The aid proves the full all-$k$ grid conclusion for such sets with divergent reciprocal mass. Indeed, absence of $(k,k)$ confines the set to the two strips $x<k$ or $y<k$, whose reciprocal mass is finite; hence every $(k,k)$ is present and so is every unit-step $k\times k$ grid. The original summary does not specify its down-set domain, so this convention is explicit. The general square and grid conjectures remain open in the supplied assessment.

Short English statement

Graham asks whether divergent reciprocal radial mass forces an axis-aligned square, and more generally every fixed square grid. The aid translates divergence into nonsummable shell densities and gives a sufficient summable finite-extremal bound. It resolves a coordinatewise down-set case, while zero density alone does not provide a counterexample or a general proof.
