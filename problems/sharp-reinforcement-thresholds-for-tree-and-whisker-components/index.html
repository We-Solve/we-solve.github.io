Sharp Reinforcement Thresholds for Tree and Whisker Components

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns supercritical reinforcement, with parameter $\alpha>1$, on an underlying graph $G$, with vertex firing rates $p(v)$. It does not specify the reinforcement dynamics, admissible graphs, initial conditions, or construction of the surviving graph. Write $H_\alpha$ provisionally for that surviving graph, and $\mathcal C(H_\alpha)$ for its connected components. This notation does not supply the missing construction. A tree is a connected undirected graph containing no cycle. The source does not define a whisker; that term must retain its source-specific meaning pending verification.

The intended problem is to improve the supplied sufficient bounds $\alpha>2$ for the assertion \[ \text{every }C\in\mathcal C(H_\alpha)\text{ is a tree}, \] and $\alpha>25$, under the additional assumption $p(v)=1$ for every vertex $v$, for the assertion \[ \text{every }C\in\mathcal C(H_\alpha)\text{ is a whisker}. \] In particular, determine whether both assertions hold throughout $\alpha>4/3$, under their respective original hypotheses, and whether $4/3$ is the optimal threshold for each. The supplied excerpt states that replacing the bounds by $\alpha>\alpha_0$ with $\alpha_0<4/3$ does not give valid conclusions in general. It does not specify the counterexamples or the behavior at $\alpha=4/3$. The admissible graph and rate quantifiers, the probability qualifier for the conclusions, and the precise meaning of optimality require recovery from the complete original problem statements. Consequently, this is a conditional reconstruction of the target, not a complete specification of the stochastic model.

The supplied original-statement field contains the labels "Open Problem 1" and "Open Problem 2" without their statements, followed by the lower-bound observation and a separate discussion of the critical case $\alpha=1$. That discussion says that a function $L$, not defined in the record, is non-strictly concave and that equilibrium need not be unique. For an even cycle of length $2k$ with $p(v)\equiv1$, it gives equilibria \[ \{(a,b,a,b,\ldots,a,b):a+b=2\}, \] without specifying the admissible domain of $a,b$. It states that odd cycles have a unique equilibrium and that, as the even-cycle length increases, the law of $(a,b)$ converges to the Dirac measure at $(1,1)$; the equilibrium selection and probability law are not defined. Motivated by viewing ${\mathbb{Z}}$ as an infinite cycle, the excerpt also records a separate Conjecture 2: for $G={\mathbb{Z}}$ and $p(v)\equiv1$, almost surely $x_v(t)\to1$ as $t\to\infty$ for every $v$. The quantities $x_v(t)$ and their normalization are not defined here. These critical-case statements are supplied context and are not substituted for the supercritical threshold problem.

Short English statement

Determine whether the surviving components are trees for every $\alpha>4/3$, and whiskers for every $\alpha>4/3$ when all firing rates equal one, and whether these thresholds are optimal. The supplied record omits the stochastic model, graph hypotheses, probability qualifier, and definition of a whisker, so a fully quantified statement requires source review.
