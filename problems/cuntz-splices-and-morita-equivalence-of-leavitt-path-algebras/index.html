Cuntz Splices and Morita Equivalence of Leavitt Path Algebras

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question does not specify the coefficient field, the class of graphs, the determinant being considered, or the hypotheses for applying a Cuntz splice. Consequently, it does not determine a unique fully quantified mathematical statement. The following definitions describe a conditional finite-graph formulation; they do not assert that this is the intended scope.

Let $K$ be a field and let $E=(E^0,E^1,s,r)$ be a finite directed graph, with vertex set $E^0$, edge set $E^1$, and source and range maps $s,r:E^1\to E^0$. Its Leavitt path algebra $L_K(E)$ is the $K$-algebra generated by vertices $v$, edges $e$, and formal ghost edges $e^*$, subject to \[ vw=\delta_{v,w}v,\qquad s(e)e=e=er(e),\qquad r(e)e^*=e^*=e^*s(e),\qquad e^*f=\delta_{e,f}r(e), \] and \[ v=\sum_{\substack{e\in E^1\\s(e)=v}}ee^* \quad\text{whenever }0<|s^{-1}(v)|<\infty. \] In particular, the last relation is not imposed at sinks. Two such algebras are Morita equivalent if their categories of unital right modules are equivalent; denote this by $L_K(E)\sim_{\mathrm M}L_K(F)$.

For a finite graph, write \[ A_E(v,w)=|\{e\in E^1:s(e)=v,\ r(e)=w\}|, \qquad d(E)=\det(I-A_E)\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] This is one possible interpretation of the source's word "determinant." The record does not specify whether it means this integer, its sign, or another quantity.

Under the usual graph construction called a Cuntz splice at a vertex $v$, form $E_{v,-}$ by retaining $E$, adjoining two vertices $u_1,u_2$, and adjoining one edge in each of the following six positions: \[ v\longrightarrow u_1,\quad u_1\longrightarrow v,\quad u_1\longrightarrow u_1,\quad u_1\longrightarrow u_2,\quad u_2\longrightarrow u_1,\quad u_2\longrightarrow u_2. \] The source does not specify which vertices are admissible for its question, and the displayed construction alone does not supply those hypotheses.

The supplied problem asks whether the determinant is invariant under Morita equivalence of Leavitt path algebras and, in particular, whether a Cuntz splice preserves Morita equivalence. More explicitly, after fixing a coefficient field $K$, an intended graph class $\mathcal G$, a determinant quantity $D$, and admissible splice vertices $V_{\mathrm{adm}}(E)$, the two questions have the forms \[ \forall E,F\in\mathcal G,\qquad L_K(E)\sim_{\mathrm M}L_K(F) \ \Longrightarrow\ D(E)=D(F), \] and \[ \forall E\in\mathcal G\ \forall v\in V_{\mathrm{adm}}(E),\qquad L_K(E)\sim_{\mathrm M}L_K(E_{v,-}). \] These are question schemas, not assertions or an identification of the missing parameters. The source's phrase "in particular" is retained as source wording; no logical implication or equivalence between the two questions is asserted here. Additional source context is required to determine the intended determinant, graph and field hypotheses, admissible vertices, and relationship between the questions.

Short English statement

The supplied problem asks whether a determinant associated with a graph is preserved by Morita equivalence of its Leavitt path algebra, and whether performing a Cuntz splice preserves Morita equivalence. The determinant convention, coefficient field, graph hypotheses, and allowed splice vertices are unspecified. The displayed formulation is therefore conditional and requires human mathematical review. The supplied record labels the problem open; this status has not been independently verified.
