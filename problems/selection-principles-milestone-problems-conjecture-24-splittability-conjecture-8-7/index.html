Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record states the following conjecture, attributed to Tsaban (2003):
Neither of the classes $\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{T})$ and $\binom{\mathrm{T}}{\Gamma}$ is provably closed under taking finite products.
This is a metamathematical assertion. The supplied excerpt does not specify the underlying axiom system, the ambient class of spaces, or the admissibility conventions for covers. These omissions prevent an unconditional precise formulation. The following gives an explicit formulation template, with those parameters left unresolved.

For a topological space $X$, let $\mathcal C(X)$ denote the intended collection of admissible covers of $X$. Its restrictions, including openness, countability, and exclusion of a member equal to $X$, must be recovered from the source. For a cover $\mathcal U$ of $X$, set \[ \mathcal U(x)=\{U\in\mathcal U:x\in U\}\qquad(x\in X). \] Under the usual cover-theoretic interpretation of the symbols, a $\tau$-cover is a cover $\mathcal U$ such that every $\mathcal U(x)$ is infinite and, for all $x,y\in X$, \[ \mathcal U(x)\setminus\mathcal U(y)\text{ is finite} \quad\text{or}\quad \mathcal U(y)\setminus\mathcal U(x)\text{ is finite}. \] A $\gamma$-cover is an infinite cover $\mathcal U$ such that \[ \{U\in\mathcal U:x\notin U\}\text{ is finite for every }x\in X. \] Write $\mathrm T(X)$ and $\Gamma(X)$ for the admissible covers satisfying these respective conditions. Short English statement

Tsaban's supplied Conjecture 24 says that finite-product closure is not provable for either $\mathsf{Split}(\mathrm T,\mathrm T)$ or $\binom{\mathrm T}{\Gamma}$. The former concerns splitting every admissible $\tau$-cover into two $\tau$-covers; the latter concerns finding a $\gamma$-subcover in every admissible $\tau$-cover. The axiom system, ambient spaces, and cover conventions require source verification.
