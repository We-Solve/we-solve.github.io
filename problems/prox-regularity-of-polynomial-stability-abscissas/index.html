Research attempt: Closed quadratic stability and the limits of a root-sensitivity argument

An exact projection calculation
For $p(s)=s^2+as+b$ with real $a,b$, both roots have real part at most zero if and only if $a\geq0$ and $b\geq0$. Indeed, real roots with these sum and product conditions have sum $-a\leq0$ and product $b\geq0$, so they are both nonpositive (including a possible zero). Nonreal roots are a conjugate pair with common real part $-a/2$ and product equal to their squared modulus. These observations also prove necessity, considering the real and nonreal cases separately.

Consequently the closed stable coefficient set is exactly the quadrant $[0,\infty)^2$. The nearest stable polynomial in the stated Euclidean norm has coefficients \[ (a_*,b_*)=(\max(a,0),\max(b,0)). \] To prove uniqueness directly, the squared distance splits as $(u-a)^2+(v-b)^2$ with independent constraints $u,v\geq0$. Each term is uniquely minimized at the corresponding truncated coordinate: if its center is nonnegative it is the unconstrained minimum, and otherwise the square strictly increases on the nonnegative half-line. Thus this degree-two closed-real case has unique projection everywhere, in particular near $s^2$.

A failed attempt to infer a negative answer from root sensitivity
The root formula gives the exact abscissa \[ \alpha(a,b)=-a/2+\sqrt{\max(a^2/4-b,0)}. \] Along $p_\varepsilon(s)=s^2-\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon>0$, coefficient distance from $s^2$ is $\varepsilon$, whereas abscissa changes from zero to $\sqrt\varepsilon$. Hence the abscissa is not locally Lipschitz at the origin in this coefficient space. This does not refute uniqueness of nearest closed-stable projection: the preceding explicit calculation proves uniqueness for the very same family. Nor has any theorem equating lack of local Lipschitz continuity with failure of the requested prox-regularity been supplied. The singular root derivative therefore cannot by itself settle the report's question.
Why the stability convention matters
For the same real quadratics, strictly negative real parts are equivalent to $a>0,b>0$, by the same root argument with strict inequalities. This set is open. At $p=s^2$ the strictly stable sequence $s^2+\delta s+\delta$, $\delta>0$, approaches coefficient distance zero, but no strictly stable polynomial attains zero distance. More generally, from any strictly stable candidate for an exterior point one can move a sufficiently small positive distance toward that point and remain in the open quadrant, while strictly reducing the distance. Thus there is no nearest strictly stable polynomial for an exterior point. This is an ambiguity check, not a counterexample to an intended formulation with the closed stable set.
