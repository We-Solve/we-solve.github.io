Degree Automatability of Sum-of-Squares Proofs

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Degree-$d$ SOS search seeks a certificate using polynomial squares and constraint multipliers within the prescribed degree bound. Coefficient bounds concern exact encoding size, separately from the number of monomials or certificate degree.

Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.

A polynomial is a sum of squares (SOS) if it is $\sum_j q_j^2$ for polynomials $q_j$. Moment-SOS hierarchies impose positivity through increasingly large truncated moment matrices and corresponding degree-bounded SOS certificates.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Characterize meaningful conditions under which low-degree sum-of-squares proofs can be found in polynomial time with polynomially bounded coefficients. In particular, determine when the degree-$d$ sum-of-squares relaxation is algorithmically computable in time polynomial in the natural input size for fixed $d$. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The input coefficient model, precision, strict-feasibility promises, and allowed constraint multipliers are those of the source; fixed degree alone is not asserted to ensure efficient exact computation.

Literature and context check

The primary report, printed pp. 416--418, explicitly distinguishes SDP size from coefficient bit complexity, imposes an explicit Archimedean hypothesis in its property (P), and reports sufficient criteria for small approximate SOS proofs. Those reported results are not re-proved here. The following repair lemma tests what a numerical Gram-matrix route would additionally need.

Research attempt: Exact Gram repair and a bit-length obstruction

Let $z$ be the column of monomials of degree at most $d$ and let $A$ send a symmetric Gram matrix $G$ to the coefficient vector of $z^{\mathsf T}Gz$. Additional multiplier coefficients can be included as extra linear variables, but consider first this pure Gram case. Suppose $A$ is surjective onto a specified coefficient subspace containing the target $b$, and a linear right inverse $B$ on that subspace is known, with \[ AB=I,\qquad \|Bu\|_F\leq K\|u\|_2. \] Given a symmetric rational candidate $\widetilde G$ with $\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde G)\geq\gamma>0$, set $r=A\widetilde G-b$ and $G=\widetilde G-Br$. Then $AG=b$ exactly. For every unit vector $x$, \[ x^{\mathsf T}Gx\geq\gamma-|x^{\mathsf T}(Br)x| \geq\gamma-\|Br\|_F\geq\gamma-K\|r\|_2. \] Thus $K\|r\|_2\leq\gamma$ certifies a positive-semidefinite exact Gram matrix, and hence an SOS representation over the reals by diagonalizing $G$. If $A,B,\widetilde G,b$ are rational, the repaired Gram matrix is rational. This conclusion does not assert rational coefficients for a particular square-root decomposition.

The lemma isolates two absent guarantees: a positive Gram margin and a controlled right-inverse norm. For example, $x^2$ in basis $(1,x)$ has the unique Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, with zero margin; no strict-feasibility inference is available even though the SOS is trivial. The linear map $A_\tau(u)=\tau u$, $\tau>0$, has right inverse norm $1/\tau$; a coefficient residual of size $\tau$ may require a unit-size correction. Residual smallness alone is therefore insufficient.

Why matrix dimension does not bound output length.
A separate exact SDP example has real variables $x_0,\ldots,x_m$ and constraints \[ x_0\geq2,\qquad \begin{pmatrix}x_{i+1}&x_i\\x_i&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq0 \quad(0\leq i<m). \] The $2\times2$ condition is equivalent to $x_{i+1}\geq x_i^2$: complete the square in its quadratic form. Induction gives $x_m\geq2^{2^m}$. Equality at every step gives a feasible rational point. The input has $O(m)$ small-integer coefficients, but any explicitly written rational feasible $x_m=a/b$ with integers $b\geq1$ needs at least $2^m+1$ numerator bits since $a\geq2^{2^m}b$. Thus small SDP size alone does not imply a short exact rational output.

This SDP is not a counterexample to the source's explicitly bounded/Archimedean hypotheses; it demonstrates the invalid general inference that formulation size settles automatability. For the source route one must additionally derive quantitative margin, conditioning, or other certificate-structure bounds from those hypotheses. The repair lemma by itself supplies none.

Verification and adversarial audit

The repair bound uses the Frobenius norm to dominate the operator norm and treats the equality residual in the right-inverse domain. Zero-margin and ill-conditioned examples were checked directly. The SDP lower bound concerns ordinary explicit rational output encoding, not a compressed arithmetic circuit for huge numbers. The source's proof degree convention bounds squared terms and multiplier products, not just degrees of square roots.
