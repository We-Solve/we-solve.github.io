Foliations and Diffeomorphism Groups — Question 20

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is left-orderable if it admits a strict total order $<$ such that $g<h$ implies $ag<ah$ for all $a,g,h\in G$. A group is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are $\{1\}$ and $G$. It is finitely presented if it admits a presentation with finitely many generators and finitely many defining relations. It has type $F_\infty$ if it admits a classifying CW complex $K(G,1)$ whose $n$-skeleton is finite for every integer $n\geq 0$.

For an abstract group $G$, let $H_b^n(G;{\mathbb{R}})$ denote bounded group cohomology with trivial real coefficients: it is the cohomology of the subcomplex of bounded functions $G^n\to{\mathbb{R}}$ in the usual inhomogeneous group cochain complex. With this coefficient convention, $G$ is boundedly acyclic if \[ H_b^n(G;{\mathbb{R}})=0\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq 1. \] The supplied question does not explicitly specify the coefficients in the term boundedly acyclic; the convention above follows its preceding discussion of real bounded cohomology.

Existence questions. Interpreting the source's word orderable as left-orderable, as suggested by its preceding discussion, ask:

  1. Does there exist a finitely presented simple left-orderable group $G$ such that $H_b^n(G;{\mathbb{R}})=0$ for every integer $n\geq 1$?
  2. More strongly, does there exist a simple left-orderable group $G$ of type $F_\infty$ such that $H_b^n(G;{\mathbb{R}})=0$ for every integer $n\geq 1$?
Type $F_\infty$ implies finite presentability, so the second question strengthens the first. The source does not explicitly resolve whether orderable means left-orderable or bi-orderable. If it means bi-orderable, the order must additionally satisfy $g<h\Rightarrow ga<ha$ for all $a,g,h\in G$, and that gives a different formulation. The left-orderable interpretation above requires confirmation from the source context.

The supplied background attributes to Navas [navas2018group, Question 8] the related question whether there exists a finitely generated left-orderable group $G$ with no surjective homomorphism $G\to{\mathbb{Z}}$ and with $H_b^2(G;{\mathbb{R}})=0$. It reports a positive answer by the authors named there as Fournier-Facioa and Lodha [fournier2023second]. It further reports that the simple orderable groups of Matte Bon and Triestino are boundedly acyclic [campagnolo2023algebraic, Corollary 1.16], whereas the only then-known finitely presented simple orderable groups are not boundedly acyclic [hyde2023finitely, Corollary 1.7]. It also cites two constructions obtained after dropping orderability [skipper2019simple; belk2022twisted], without providing their detailed hypotheses in this record. These are supplied contextual reports, not independently verified literature assessments. In particular, the absence of a surjection onto ${\mathbb{Z}}$ and the degree-two vanishing condition belong to the related historical question; they do not replace the simplicity and all-positive-degree vanishing conditions of the present question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a nontrivial simple group can simultaneously be finitely presented, admit a left-invariant total order, and have vanishing bounded cohomology with trivial real coefficients in every positive degree. It also asks for the stronger finiteness property $F_\infty$. The meaning of orderable and the bounded-cohomology coefficient convention require source confirmation. The registry labels the problem open; that label and the historical claims are supplied information, not independently verified conclusions.
