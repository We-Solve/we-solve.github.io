Math-3337

Prescribed Q-Curvature in Conformal Classes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be the Riemannian manifold intended in the source and let $K:M\to{\mathbb{R}}$ be the prescribed curvature function. Under the usual smooth convention, its conformal class is \[ [g]=\{e^{2u}g:u\in C^\infty(M,{\mathbb{R}})\}. \] Writing $Q_{\widetilde g}$ for the source's chosen $Q$-curvature, the problem is to determine whether \[ \exists u\in C^\infty(M,{\mathbb{R}})\quad Q_{e^{2u}g}(x)=K(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in M. \] Equivalently, characterize membership of $K$ in the conformal curvature range \[ \mathcal Q([g])=\{Q_{e^{2u}g}:u\in C^\infty(M,{\mathbb{R}})\}. \] The equality is pointwise on the fixed manifold; it is not equality after an unspecified diffeomorphism, a constant-curvature problem only, or a request for uniqueness.

The dimension, definition and normalization of $Q$-curvature, compactness, boundary conditions, and regularity of $K$ are not specified in the record. Critical $Q$-curvature in even dimensions and lower-order or differently normalized versions require different operators and equations, so no particular Paneitz or higher-order conformal PDE is inserted silently. If a boundary is allowed, the admissible conformal factors and boundary data must also be fixed.

The supplied literature triage checked 2026-08-21 reports existence theorems under sign, operator, and geometric hypotheses, as well as explicit obstructions, but no general solution for arbitrary prescribed $K$ in a conformal class. Thus the source is a prescribed-curvature solvability and characterization question, not an assertion that every function is realizable on every manifold. Exact hypotheses for the cited partial results and obstructions are not reproduced.

Short English statement

Determine which functions occur as the $Q$-curvature of a metric conformal to the given metric. The dimension, curvature convention, and global or boundary hypotheses are needed to state a specific solvability theorem.
