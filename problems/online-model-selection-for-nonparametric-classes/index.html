Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

In an online decision problem, an algorithm selects an action at each of $T$ rounds and receives the feedback allowed by the source model. Regret compares its cumulative expected loss with the appropriate best comparator's cumulative loss. Model selection chooses adaptively among $M$ candidate function classes, seeking performance close to that of the best appropriate class without knowing its index. A nonparametric class is not restricted to a fixed-dimensional linear parameterization; its complexity still influences learning difficulty.

Testing logarithmic model overhead under bandit feedback

The source's logarithmic dependence on the number of linear models holds under a specified scenario and motivates a question about other model classes. We test an unrestricted extension when only the selected action's reward is observed. There are $M\ge2$ actions and $M$ singleton models. Model $j$ contains the deterministic reward function $r_j(a)=\mathbf1_{a=j}$. A learner told the correct model has zero regret by always selecting $j$; a model-selection learner knows this list but not $j$.
Proposition: For every possibly randomized learner and every integer $1\le T\le M$, there is a model $j$ for which its expected regret after $T$ rounds is at least \[ T-\frac{T(T+1)}{2M}. \]
Proof: First fix the learner's random seed and take the hidden index $J$ uniformly from $[M]$. Consider the sequence of its actions if all observed rewards were zero. By time $t$, this sequence has queried at most $t$ distinct indices. A first positive observation by that time occurs exactly when $J$ is among these queried indices: until that first observation, the actual history equals the zero history. Consequently its probability is at most $t/M$. A reward of one at round $t$ requires such a first hit by time $t$, so the expected total reward is at most $\sum_{t=1}^Tt/M=T(T+1)/(2M)$. The optimal action earns $T$. Averaging over the learner's seed preserves the bound; averaging over $J$ implies at least one fixed index has expected regret at least this average. ∎
For $M=2T$ the bound is $(3T-1)/4$. Thus an unrestricted model-selection overhead $C\sqrt{T\log M}$, with a universal constant $C$ and zero base-model regret here, is impossible for large $T$. Full-information exponential weighting cannot simply be transplanted: the learner does not observe the losses of the unqueried models. The exact missing step is an unbiased, sufficiently controlled comparison of those models without the necessary exploration cost.

Verification and remaining gaps

The uniform-hidden-index experiment was enumerated for small $M,T$ using the optimal search-until-hit strategy, which attains the reward bound by querying distinct actions and then exploiting. The proof applies to every strategy, not just this computation. This example changes neither the source's structured linear assumptions nor its theorem; it only rejects a universal extension to arbitrary listed reward models with no additional complexity or observability term. Conditions permitting logarithmic dependence for useful nonparametric classes remain to be established.
