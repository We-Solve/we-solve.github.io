Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For periodic motion in $\mathbb R^d$, time reparametrization by a rotation or reflection of the circle gives an $O(2)$ action; spatial orthogonal transformations give $O(d)$, and label permutations give $S_n$. The stabilizer consists of triples preserving the loop. With unequal masses, a label symmetry must respect the mass assignment to preserve the equations.

An effective-kernel constraint.
For a collision-free periodic solution, suppose a symmetry triple acts trivially in time and space and only permutes labels by $\sigma$. Invariance requires $q_{\sigma(i)}(t)=q_i(t)$ for every i,t. Collision freedom forces $\sigma(i)=i$ for all i. Thus the full stabilizer has trivial intersection with the pure-label subgroup. In particular an abstract proposed symmetry group with a nontrivial pure-label kernel cannot be realized by a collision-free labeled solution.
Continuous isotropy of a circle choreography.
For the equal-mass rotating regular n-gon, with $q_j(t)=R e^{i(\omega t+2\pi j/n)}$, every time shift $t\mapsto t+s$ can be canceled by spatial rotation $e^{-i\omega s}$. These triples form a continuous circle subgroup of the stabilizer with identity label permutation. The discrete time shift $P/n$ can also be matched with a cyclic label permutation. Hence specifying only a finite cyclic symmetry subgroup does not identify the full stabilizer; a realization may carry additional continuous symmetries.
