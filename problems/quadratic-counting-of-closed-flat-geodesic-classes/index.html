Math-5277

Quadratic counting of closed flat geodesic classes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Knieper's Question $6.5.1$ asks whether \[ \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\log N(T)}T=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists C>0\quad N(T)\leq CT^2\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }T. \] The source says the torus case is positive and suggests a Finsler analogue. The aid reconstructs $N(T)$ as a count of closed flat geodesic classes of length at most $T$: the PDF omits the length bound, and indispensable preceding sentences were assigned to another record. The exact equivalence relation on classes, metric hypotheses and prime-versus-iterate convention are not available here. Counting individual translated geodesics on a flat torus would not be a finite class count, so those choices cannot be suppressed.

The aid's conditional counting statements can be made independently precise. Suppose a collection of oriented length-realizing closed classes admits unique primitive roots, contains every positive iterate, has shortest positive length $\ell_0>0$, and iteration multiplies the counted length by its integer order. If $P(T)$ counts primitive classes and $A(T)$ counts all iterates, then \[ A(T)=\sum_{n\geq1}P(T/n). \] The sum is finite. Under these assumptions $P(T)=O(T^2)$ if and only if $A(T)=O(T^2)$, with the upper transfer involving $\sum n^{-2}=\zeta(2)$; their subexponential growth properties are also equivalent. This identity is not asserted without the unique-root and length-scaling conventions.

For the two-period criterion, let $\omega_1,\omega_2$ be closed one-forms with integral periods on a compact Finsler manifold, and let \[ B_j=\sup_{F(v)=1}|\omega_j(v)|<\infty. \] For a chosen collection $\mathcal C$ of oriented free-homotopy classes with length-realizing representatives, assume the map \[ [\gamma]\longmapsto\left(\int_\gamma\omega_1,\int_\gamma\omega_2\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2 \] has fibers of cardinality at most $q$. Then its length-bounded count satisfies \[ \#\{c\in\mathcal C:\ell(c)\leq T\} \leq q(2\lfloor B_1T\rfloor+1)(2\lfloor B_2T\rfloor+1). \] For every compact Finsler two-torus, two integral cohomology generators identify oriented free-homotopy classes with ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$, so $q=1$; the estimate applies in particular to any specified subcollection of flat classes. This is a statement about classes, not a bound on the number of all parametrized geodesics.

For a translation-invariant Finsler metric on ${\mathbb{R}}^2/\Lambda$, let $B_F=\{v:F(v)\leq1\}$, using the forward norm if it is asymmetric. Nonzero oriented classes correspond to $v\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$, with length $F(v)$. The aid gives the benchmarks \[ A(T)\sim\frac{\operatorname{Area}(B_F)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}T^2, \qquad P(T)\sim\frac{\operatorname{Area}(B_F)}{\zeta(2)\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}T^2. \] No unoriented or symmetry factor is inserted. These special and conditional results do not resolve Knieper's general implication.

The extraction then spills into Section $7$, concerning manifolds without conjugate points. Its separate background theorems are: a Riemannian torus without conjugate points is flat; and a complete Riemannian plane without conjugate points satisfies, for every $p$, \[ \liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Area}B(p,r)}{\pi r^2}\geq1, \qquad\text{with equality if and only if the metric is flat.} \] The text contrasts the universal-cover/unique-geodesic setting with the loss of Jacobi-length convexity, notes a false Lorentzian torus analogue, and ends before stating another conjecture motivated by the plane result. These are neighboring exposition, not extra hypotheses or a missing solution to the counting question.

Short English statement

The intended question asks whether subexponential length-bounded growth of closed flat-geodesic classes must be quadratic. The aid gives a bounded-period-fiber criterion, proves the torus class-count case, and supplies exact oriented flat-Finsler-torus asymptotics. Missing class conventions and the neighboring rigidity exposition are kept explicit.
