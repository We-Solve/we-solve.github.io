Rationally Connected Cycles on Hyperkähler Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a projective hyperkähler manifold with $\dim X=2n$. The usual setting is over ${\mathbb{C}}$. Under the irreducible holomorphic symplectic convention, this means that $X$ is a smooth connected simply connected complex projective variety and $H^0(X,\Omega_X^2)={\mathbb{C}}\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is everywhere nondegenerate. The supplied record does not explicitly specify the ground field or whether this particular convention for hyperkähler is intended; these are qualifications requiring review.

Write $CH_0(X)$ for the group of algebraic zero-cycles on $X$ modulo rational equivalence, with integral coefficients, and $[y]$ for the class of a closed point $y\in X$. A scheme has pure dimension $n$ if each of its irreducible components has dimension $n$. For an integral complex projective variety, rational connectedness means that two general points can be joined by the image of a morphism ${\mathbb{P}}^1\to Y$. The source uses rational connectedness for subschemes without specifying reducedness, irreducibility, or an extension of this definition to more general schemes. The questions below retain that unresolved terminology.

For every projective hyperkähler manifold $X$ of dimension $2n$, ask:

  1. Does there exist a nonempty closed subscheme $Y\subseteq X$ which is rationally connected and has pure dimension $n$?
  2. For every pair of nonempty rationally connected closed subschemes $Y_1,Y_2\subseteq X$ with $\dim Y_1=\dim Y_2=n$, and every choice of closed points $y_1\in Y_1$ and $y_2\in Y_2$, is \[ [y_1]=[y_2]\quad\text{in }CH_0(X)? \]
The second question includes $Y_1=Y_2$. If the first question has a positive answer and the second equality holds, denote the resulting common point class by $c_X\in CH_0(X)$; it is to be independent of both the subscheme and the point. Without existence of any eligible subscheme, the universal equality is vacuous and does not define $c_X$. The source explicitly requires pure dimension in the first question, but states only dimension $n$ in the second; no additional purity assumption has been inserted there.

Short English statement

Does every projective hyperkähler manifold of dimension $2n$ contain a rationally connected subscheme of pure dimension $n$, and do all points on all rationally connected $n$-dimensional subschemes represent one common class in $CH_0(X)$? The meaning of rational connectedness for the allowed subschemes and the precise hyperkähler convention require clarification.
