Strong Erdős–Hajnal Phenomena in Sparse H-Free Graphs

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not define $\varepsilon$-sparse or $H$-free, or explicitly specify the graph and disjointness conventions. Consequently, the following is a conditional formalization of its quantifier structure, requiring those conventions to be checked against the cited source.

Under the convention that graphs are finite, simple and undirected, write $V(G)$ and $E(G)$ for the vertex and edge sets. For disjoint sets $A,B\subseteq V(G)$, call $(A,B)$ a pure pair if either \[ \{a,b\}\in E(G)\quad\text{for every }a\in A,\ b\in B, \] or \[ \{a,b\}\notin E(G)\quad\text{for every }a\in A,\ b\in B. \] These alternatives mean that $A$ is complete or anticomplete to $B$, respectively. Disjointness is an explicit provisional convention here, not an explicit hypothesis in the supplied statement.

Let $\mathsf{Sparse}_{\varepsilon}(G)$ denote the source's unresolved sparsity predicate, and let $\mathsf{Free}_{H}(G)$ denote its unresolved forbidden-graph predicate. In particular, the record does not determine whether sparsity bounds maximum degree or edge density, whether the bound is strict, or whether the forbidden copy of $H$ must be induced.

For each graph $H$, consider the property $\mathcal P(H)$ given by the following assertion: there exists a real number $\varepsilon>0$ such that, for every integer $n>1$ and every graph $G$ satisfying \[ |V(G)|=n,\qquad \mathsf{Sparse}_{\varepsilon}(G),\qquad \mathsf{Free}_{H}(G), \] there exist disjoint sets $A,B\subseteq V(G)$ forming a pure pair and satisfying at least one of \begin{align*} \text{(a)}\quad &|A|\geq\varepsilon n\quad\text{and}\quad |B|\geq\varepsilon n;\\ \text{(b)}\quad &|A|\geq\varepsilon n\quad\text{and}\quad |B|\geq n^{\varepsilon};\\ \text{(c)}\quad &|A|\geq n^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad |B|\geq n^{\varepsilon}. \end{align*} The same $\varepsilon$ occurs in the sparsity hypothesis and in the size bounds; it may depend on $H$, but precedes the universal quantifiers over $n$ and $G$. The sets and the successful alternative may depend on $G$.

The literal classification question is: for which graphs $H$ does $\mathcal P(H)$ hold? The additional supplied sentence, "Must at least one of these three size conditions always hold?", does not specify an additional quantifier over $H$. It also leaves unresolved whether the intended task instead classifies $H$ separately for each of (a), (b), and (c). No such alternative interpretation is adopted here.

Short English statement

Classify the graphs $H$ for which one positive constant $\varepsilon$ guarantees a complete or anticomplete pair in every $\varepsilon$-sparse, $H$-free graph on $n>1$ vertices, with one of the three stated size bounds. The meanings of sparsity, forbidden copies, graph conventions, and the relationship between the three classification questions require source verification.
