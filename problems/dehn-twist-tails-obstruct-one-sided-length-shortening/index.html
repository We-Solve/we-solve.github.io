Math-1733

Dehn-twist tails obstruct one-sided length shortening

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma<PSL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ be an infinitely generated Fuchsian group of the first kind, meaning that its limit set is the whole ideal circle. In the surface interpretation take a torsion-free group and the complete hyperbolic quotient $X=\mathbb H^2/\Gamma$ of infinite area, with no boundary. The original phrase "closed hyperbolic surface of infinite area" cannot mean compact closed; its intended meaning is boundaryless. Boundarylessness alone is not silently asserted equivalent to first kind for every infinite-area surface.

The source asks whether one can quasiconformally deform such a structure so that every essential simple closed curve is nonlengthened. For a marked deformation $f:X\to Y$, the requested inequality is \[ \ell_Y(f(\gamma))\leq\ell_X(\gamma) \qquad\text{for every essential simple closed curve class }\gamma. \] A nontrivial deformation must be intended, since the identity satisfies the inequalities; the source does not state whether this asks for one example, for every base surface, for a whole path, or merely another marked point. It mentions recent results of Alessandrini and Guéritaud without a citation.

The supplied aid gives an obstruction for a specific natural family, on any complete hyperbolic surface where its curve hypotheses hold. Let $a$ be an essential simple closed geodesic with a simple transversal $b$, so $i(a,b)>0$. Let $X_t$ be a pure Fenchel--Nielsen twist along $a$, with $X_0=X$ and all other data unchanged. For every nonzero real twist parameter $t$, it reports infinitely many simple curve classes lengthened and infinitely many shortened relative to $X$.

More precisely, let $D_a$ be a Dehn twist and $b_n=D_a^{-n}(b)$ for $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$. The reported tail certificate says that for each $t\ne0$ there is $N$ such that, after possibly interchanging the positive and negative tails according to the twist-sign convention, \[ \ell_{X_t}(b_n)>\ell_X(b_n)\quad(n\geq N),\qquad \ell_{X_t}(b_n)<\ell_X(b_n)\quad(n\leq-N). \] The threshold may depend on $X,a,b,t$. The aid also reports the two-sign infinitesimal analogue for the length derivatives of these twist-orbit curves at $t=0$, without providing its estimates. Thus a nonzero pure twist is not an all-nonincreasing deformation, even though it is a standard quasiconformal deformation.

In the one-sided convention \[ K(X,Y)=\log\sup_{\gamma} \frac{\ell_Y(\gamma)}{\ell_X(\gamma)}, \] the lengthening tail implies $K(X,X_t)>0$ for every $t\ne0$. The shortening tail prevents the reversed comparison from satisfying all nonincrease inequalities as well. This is a pure-twist obstruction with an explicit transversal assumption; it does not rule out more general coordinated quasiconformal deformations or solve the source's full first-kind question. The dotted separator is layout, not a condition.

Short English statement

The source concerns nontrivial quasiconformal shortening on a complete infinite-area surface, with its quantifiers left partly open. The aid excludes pure twists: opposite tails of a Dehn-twist orbit supply lengthening and shortening curves. This special obstruction does not exclude every possible deformation.
