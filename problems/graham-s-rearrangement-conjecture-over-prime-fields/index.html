Math-4052

Graham’s Rearrangement Conjecture over Prime Fields

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime, and let ${\mathbb{F}}_p={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the field with $p$ elements, with its additive group structure. For a subset $S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_p\setminus\{0\}$, write $k=|S|$. An ordering of $S$ is a sequence $(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ containing each element of $S$ exactly once. Its nonempty successive partial sums are \[ t_i=\sum_{r=1}^{i}s_r\in{\mathbb{F}}_p,\qquad 1\leq i\leq k. \] The question in the supplied cleaned statement is whether \[ \forall p\text{ prime}\;\forall S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_p\setminus\{0\}\;\exists(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\text{ ordering }S\;\forall\,1\leq i<j\leq k,\quad t_i\ne t_j. \] For $S=\varnothing$, the empty ordering satisfies this condition vacuously. No restriction on $\sum_{s\in S}s$ is supplied.

Partial-sum convention requiring review. This formulation interprets successive partial sums as $t_1,\ldots,t_k$, without including the empty partial sum $t_0=0$. The supplied record does not explicitly settle that convention. Requiring $t_0,t_1,\ldots,t_k$ to be pairwise distinct would change the statement and would be incompatible with any nonempty allowed set whose total sum is zero.

Scope and supplied status. The extracted question concerns additive prime fields. The original passage also discusses a possible extension to every finite group, but does not supply the preceding question, its group-operation conventions, the statement of Theorem 1, or the bounds defining the intermediate regime. Those broader references do not determine an additional precise conjecture here. The passage's assertion that the intermediate regime remains open is qualified by the supplied dated assessment: as of its stated check date, 2026-08-22, the curation attributes a resolution for all sufficiently large primes to Pham and Sauermann, combining small- and large-subset results, while describing the broader arbitrary finite-group question as open. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

For each prime $p$, can every subset of the nonzero elements of ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ be ordered so that its nonempty successive partial sums are pairwise distinct? This draft excludes the empty partial sum; that convention needs source review. The supplied curation labels the problem partially solved and reports a result for sufficiently large primes, with a dated assessment of 2026-08-22.
