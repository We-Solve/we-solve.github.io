Let $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$ be quadratic forms in $n$ variables, with $Q_1$ nonnegative on the variety $Q_2=Q_3=0$. Literature and context check

The primary problem on printed p. 446 asks for homogeneous SOS multipliers and a dimension-free degree, but its displayed wording does not specify that the multiplier is nonzero or nonvanishing on the constraint variety. The preceding equality S-lemma assertion also omits a regularity condition. These are substantive source-scope issues. The calculations below diagnose them rather than declaring the intended research question solved by a vacuous certificate.

Research attempt: Multiplier degeneracy and an equality-boundary obstruction

The literal choice $P=R_2=R_3=0$ makes the displayed polynomial SOS for every input. Requiring only $P\ne0$ does not remove the defect. If $Q_2$ is a nonzero quadratic, choose \[ P=Q_2^2,\qquad R_2=-Q_1Q_2,\qquad R_3=0. \] All multipliers are homogeneous of degree four (with zero allowed in that homogeneous space), $P$ is a nonzero square, and $PQ_1+R_2Q_2+R_3Q_3=0$. This holds for arbitrary $Q_1$, including one negative on the constraint variety. For example, $Q_1=-x^2$, $Q_2=y^2$, $Q_3=z^2$ has $Q_1(1,0,0)=-1$, yet this formal identity exists. Its multiplier vanishes everywhere on the variety. If $Q_2=0$ and $Q_3\ne0$, interchange their roles. These identities show that mere nonzero-ness cannot express the intended positivity certificate.

Testing a stronger nonvanishing interpretation.
One might instead require $P$ positive at all nonzero real points of the variety. Without further assumptions even existence can then fail. In two variables take \[ Q_1(x,y)=2xy,\qquad Q_2(x,y)=x^2,\qquad Q_3=0. \] The variety is the line $x=0$, where $Q_1=0$. Suppose a polynomial identity $S=PQ_1+R_2Q_2$ is SOS and $P(0,1)>0$. At $a=(0,1)$ one has $S(a)=0$. Writing $S=\sum_j q_j^2$ forces every $q_j(a)=0$, and hence every partial derivative of $S$ at $a$ is zero, because $\partial S=2\sum q_j\partial q_j$. But differentiating the proposed identity with respect to $x$ gives \[ \partial_x S(0,1)=2P(0,1)>0, \] a contradiction. This excludes the stronger positive-multiplier convention for this degenerate input at every degree, not just at a small degree.

The same input tests the preceding scalar-multiplier assertion: $Q_1+\lambda Q_2$ has matrix \[ \begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \] whose determinant is $-1$, so it is never positive semidefinite. Directly, evaluating at $(x,y)=(1,-(|\lambda|+1))$ also gives a negative value. Nonnegativity on $x^2=0$ therefore does not imply the claimed scalar certificate without additional assumptions.

A nonvacuous theorem must specify its multiplier convention and regularity or ideal conditions together. For example, replacing the constraint polynomial $x^2$ by the radical equation $x=0$ changes the allowable ideal terms, despite leaving its real zero set unchanged. It cannot be silently treated as the same certificate problem.
