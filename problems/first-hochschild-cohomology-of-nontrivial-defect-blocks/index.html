First Hochschild Cohomology of Nontrivial-Defect Blocks

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns blocks of finite group algebras, but does not specify the coefficient field. The following formulation uses the conventional algebraically closed modular setting; this field hypothesis is an explicit interpretive assumption requiring source confirmation.

Let $p$ be a prime, let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and let $G$ be a finite group. A block algebra of $kG$ is $B=kGe$, where $e$ is a primitive central idempotent of $kG$. For a $p$-subgroup $P\leq G$, write \[ \operatorname{Br}_P:(kG)^P\longrightarrow kC_G(P),\qquad \operatorname{Br}_P\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)=\sum_{g\in C_G(P)}a_g g, \] where $(kG)^P$ denotes the subalgebra fixed by conjugation by $P$, and $C_G(P)$ is its centralizer in $G$. A defect group of $B$ is a $p$-subgroup $D\leq G$ maximal under inclusion subject to $\operatorname{Br}_D(e)\neq0$. Nontrivial defect means $D\neq\{1\}$.

Define the first Hochschild cohomology over $k$ by \[ HH^1_k(B)=\operatorname{Der}_k(B)/\operatorname{Inn}(B), \] where $\operatorname{Der}_k(B)$ consists of the $k$-linear maps $\delta:B\to B$ satisfying $\delta(xy)=\delta(x)y+x\delta(y)$, and $\operatorname{Inn}(B)$ consists of maps $x\mapsto ax-xa$ for $a\in B$.

Question, under the coefficient-field convention above. For every prime $p$, every algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p$, every finite group $G$, and every block algebra $B$ of $kG$ with nontrivial defect group, must \[ HH^1_k(B)\neq0 \] hold?

The original extract refers to "[9, Question 7.4]", whose bibliographic identity is not supplied. It also appends a corrupted formula introduced by $p\geq3$, containing an apparent summation from $j=0$ to $w-1$, $\dim_k HH^1(B)$, and a term rendered as $\texttt{dimFp (Z(Bpj ))}$. Short English statement

Does every block of a finite group algebra with nontrivial defect admit a $k$-linear derivation that is not inner? The displayed formulation assumes an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic, a convention absent from the supplied statement and requiring confirmation. The record labels the question open; this is supplied curation rather than an independently verified status.
