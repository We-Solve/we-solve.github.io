Math-3759

Virtual Nilpotence of Rational Log-Canonical Local Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is \[ \text{For every rational log-canonical singularity }(X;x),\quad \text{is }\pi_1(X;x)\text{ virtually nilpotent?} \] A group $G$ is virtually nilpotent if there is a subgroup $H\leq G$ with finite index $[G:H]<\infty$ such that its lower central series, defined by \[ \gamma_1(H)=H,\qquad \gamma_{j+1}(H)=[H,\gamma_j(H)], \] satisfies $\gamma_{c+1}(H)=\{1\}$ for some integer $c\geq 1$. Thus the requested conclusion is \[ \forall (X;x)\text{ rational and log-canonical},\quad \exists H\leq\pi_1(X;x)\ \exists c\geq1:\quad [\pi_1(X;x):H]<\infty,\qquad \gamma_{c+1}(H)=\{1\}. \] No uniform bound on the index or the nilpotency class is asserted.

Under the conventional interpretation as a normal complex variety germ, rationality means that, for a resolution $f:Y\to V$ of a representative neighborhood of $x$, the higher direct images $R^i f_*\mathcal O_Y$ vanish near $x$ for every $i>0$. Under the boundary-free interpretation, log canonicity means that $K_V$ is ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier and every prime divisor $E$ over $V$ with center near $x$ has discrepancy $a(E,V,0)\geq-1$; for exceptional divisors on a resolution these discrepancies occur in \[ K_Y=f^*K_V+\sum_{E\text{ exceptional}}a(E,V,0)E. \] These definitions describe a conventional interpretation, since the supplied record does not explicitly specify the ground field, the algebraic or analytic category, or whether a boundary is involved.

The notation $\pi_1(X;x)$ is also unresolved in the supplied record. In a complex setting, the fundamental group of a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood, $\pi_1(U\setminus\{x\},u)$, and that of its regular locus, $\pi_1(U\cap X_{\mathrm{reg}},u)$, are distinct possible targets when the singularity is not isolated. The record does not specify either convention, does not assert isolatedness, and does not explicitly distinguish a topological local group from an algebraic local fundamental group. The displayed question therefore retains the source notation pending verification of its intended definition.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether every rational log-canonical singularity has a local fundamental group containing a nilpotent subgroup of finite index. The meaning of the local fundamental group and the ambient geometric conventions require confirmation from the report's surrounding discussion. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
