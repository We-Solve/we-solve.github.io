Math-2282

Problem 5: — Develop a theory of geometrically finite subgroups of Mg,m which include the groups defined by Veech surfaces.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal M_{g,m}$ be the source's mapping class group of a genus-$g$ surface with $m$ marked points or punctures, with the precise marking and orientation conventions to be confirmed. A translation or half-translation structure determines a Teichmüller disk. Its orientation-preserving affine self-maps induce a subgroup of the mapping class group preserving that disk, and their derivative action gives the associated Veech group in $\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$ or $\mathrm{PSL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$, subject to the usual kernel and sign conventions. In the common lattice-surface terminology, a Veech surface has finite-covolume Veech group.

Problem 5. Develop a theory of geometrically finite subgroups of $\mathcal M_{g,m}$ that includes the mapping-class subgroups defined by Veech surfaces. In particular, a proposed geometric-finiteness notion must explain how the affine/Teichmüller-disk subgroups arising from the intended Veech surfaces qualify. The requirement is not merely to study the derivative Fuchsian groups abstractly; the subgroups live in $\mathcal M_{g,m}$.

The excerpt gives no existing definition of geometric finiteness to characterize, nor a fixed theorem or list of axioms. The task is to formulate a useful class and its theory subject to the specified inclusion requirement. It must not be silently replaced by convex cocompactness or a purely pseudo-Anosov requirement: the intended Veech examples can have cusp/parabolic behavior and associated reducible mapping classes. Whether all affine stabilizers, lattice Veech groups only, or another standard Veech-surface convention is intended remains to be checked.

Short English statement

Develop an appropriate geometric-finiteness theory for mapping-class subgroups that includes the affine groups associated with Veech surfaces, including their possible cusp behavior.
