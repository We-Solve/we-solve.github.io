Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.20

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be an infinite set and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ act transitively on $\Omega$, meaning that for every $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$ there exists $g\in G$ with $g\alpha=\beta$. For $H\leq G$, write $H\alpha=\{h\alpha:h\in H\}$. The permutation group is called barely transitive if \[ \forall H<G\ \forall\alpha\in\Omega,\qquad |H\alpha|<\infty, \] where $H<G$ means that $H$ is a proper subgroup. The group $G$ is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite. Its derived subgroup is \[ G'=[G,G]=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G\rangle. \] A group is perfect if $G=G'$.

The question in Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.20 is whether there exist such $\Omega$ and $G$ for which $G$ is locally finite, barely transitive, and perfect. Equivalently, does there exist an infinite transitive permutation group satisfying \[ \begin{gathered} \text{every finitely generated subgroup of }G\text{ is finite},\\ \forall H<G\ \forall\alpha\in\Omega,\quad |H\alpha|<\infty,\\ G=[G,G]? \end{gathered} \] The action is faithful because $G$ is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$. In this setting, requiring the permutation group to be infinite also forces $\Omega$ to be infinite.

The supplied record additionally reports the following background results; they are retained as supplied claims, without independent verification. There are no simple locally finite barely transitive groups. Every locally finite barely transitive group is a $p$-group for some prime $p$, meaning that each element has finite order equal to a power of that same prime. Moreover, if a point stabilizer \[ G_\alpha=\{g\in G:g\alpha=\alpha\} \] is soluble of derived length $d$, then $G$ is soluble of derived length bounded by a function of $d$. Here $K^{(0)}=K$, $K^{(n+1)}=[K^{(n)},K^{(n)}]$, and the derived length of a soluble group $K$ is the least nonnegative integer $n$ such that $K^{(n)}=\{1\}$. The record supplies no explicit bounding function. These background claims do not replace the existence question. The proposer field is truncated, and the exact attribution role of the opening parenthetical "B. Hartley" remains unresolved.

Short English statement

Does an infinite locally finite group admit a faithful transitive permutation action in which every proper subgroup has only finite orbits, while the group equals its own derived subgroup? The supplied registry labels this question open; that label and the accompanying background claims have not been independently verified.
