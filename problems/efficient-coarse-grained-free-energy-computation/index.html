Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The atomistic coordinates split as $X=(X_r,X_c)$, with $N_r$ retained representative atoms and $N_c$ eliminated atoms. $\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}>0$ and $V(X_r,X_c)$ is the potential energy. The reduced free energy is the negative log of the conditional configurational integral with the retained coordinates fixed. A chain-like one-dimensional interaction geometry can still have individual atom positions in $\mathbb R^d$. The quantity $A(X_r)/N_c$ normalizes by the number of removed particles.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source already presents a computational strategy for one-dimensional nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor models using Markov-chain and transfer-operator arguments. The broad efficient-computation question lacks an error tolerance and complexity definition; it should not be advertised as having no known method in the source's chain case. This report passage is closely repeated at ID 30001271.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For a one-dimensional atomistic system with repatom coordinates $X_r$ and eliminated coordinates $X_c$, define $A(X_r)=-\beta^{-1}\log\int\exp(-\beta V(X_r,X_c))\,dX_c$. Can $A(X_r)/N_c$ be computed efficiently as $N_c\to\infty$?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The question asks for an efficient large-Nc computation of the reduced-system free energy. The report supplies a formal expectation representation, but no general complexity or convergent computational theorem resolving the limit was found. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Compute the free energy per eliminated particle efficiently in the thermodynamic limit of a chain-like atomistic system.

An exactly soluble eliminated harmonic chain.
Fix endpoints $x_0=0,x_{n+1}=L$ and integrate $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb R$ with $V=\frac k2\sum_{j=0}^n(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2$, $k>0$. Put $x_j=jL/(n+1)+y_j$ with $y_0=y_{n+1}=0$. The cross term telescopes, giving \[ V=\frac{kL^2}{2(n+1)}+\frac k2 y^TT_ny, \] where $T_n$ is tridiagonal with diagonal two and adjacent entries minus one. Its determinant obeys $d_n=2d_{n-1}-d_{n-2}$, $d_0=1,d_1=2$, hence $d_n=n+1$. Gaussian integration therefore yields \[ A_n=\frac{kL^2}{2(n+1)}-\frac n{2\beta}\log\frac{2\pi}{\beta k}+\frac1{2\beta}\log(n+1). \] This computes the conditional free energy without an $n$-dimensional numerical integral.
The scaling of retained coordinates matters.
For fixed $L$, $A_n/n\to-(2\beta)^{-1}\log(2\pi/(\beta k))$. If instead $L=(n+1)\ell$, the limit is $k\ell^2/2-(2\beta)^{-1}\log(2\pi/(\beta k))$. Thus the large-$N_c$ question needs a specification of endpoint strain. The reference length implicit in Lebesgue measure also fixes the additive free-energy normalization.
Remaining scope.
This is elementary Gaussian elimination for a nearest-neighbor quadratic potential, consistent with the source's already known chain strategies. It does not furnish uniform quadrature errors, mixing bounds or complexity for nonlinear nearest/next-nearest interactions. Extending the transfer-operator approximation with a stated accuracy criterion is the missing step. The closely repeated target at 30001271 is not treated as a distinct new theorem.
