Econ-156

Liquidity backstops for nonbanks

LLM status Open unverified

E58 — Central Banks and Their Policies Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra ultra 30% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

Original subject group: Banking, credit and financial stability

Audit classification: Explicit published open question. The BIS primary open-question section explicitly addresses central-bank intervention design; the Bank agenda supplies related scope. Collateral haircuts, fiscal accounting and endogenous buffers are expanded editorially.

Definitions and precise research target

This is a formulation and structural-characterization agenda. Each proposed characterization must declare its primitive model or admissible model class and the quantifiers over that class; the displayed objective alone does not specify a unique economy, a numerical optimum or an algorithm. Specify the institution set, balance sheets, private objectives, buffer domains, markets, horizon, information, feasible policy rules and equilibrium conditions before evaluating the objective. State which restrictions are assumptions and which conclusions follow from them. The approximation criterion below is a success criterion, not an existence or computational theorem.

Fix nonbank institutions with assets, short-term funding, private liquidity buffers and potential access to central-bank lending. A facility $a$ specifies eligible institutions, collateral, haircuts, interest charges, borrowing caps and activation conditions. Institution $i$ chooses a pre-crisis buffer $b_i(a)$ anticipating the facility. Let $L(a,\omega;b(a))$ be crisis resource losses after this endogenous response, $F(a,\omega)$ the conditional expected public cost defined below, and $C(a)$ routine operating costs. The task is to characterize \[J(a)=E[L(a,\omega;b(a))+F(a,\omega)]+C(a),\qquad J_*=\inf_{a\in A}J(a).\] Here $A$ is the feasible facility set. Define shock state $\omega$, its distribution, the institution perimeter and fiscal-risk accounting explicitly. Impose incentive and eligibility constraints ensuring institutions cannot profit from manufacturing collateral or misreporting solvency. Relative to a declared benchmark facility under the same primitives, quantify signed changes in a defined liquidity-externality statistic and a defined private-buffer statistic; neither a reduction nor a welfare loss from lower buffers is presumed. Compare standing facilities with crisis-only lending while accounting for anticipated discretionary rescues. Distinguish access coupled to supervision for incentive feasibility or fiscal feasibility from access coupled to supervision for welfare improvement; state the criterion used for any claim of necessity. State which objects are identified from existing interventions and which require structural assumptions. A liquidity facility that prevents one historical fire sale does not establish an optimal access perimeter or demonstrate that moral hazard is negligible.

Additional formal definitions and scope (editorial). For collateral $j$, let $q_j\geq0$ be the eligible unencumbered quantity pledged and $P_j\geq0$ its price under a declared valuation date and rule. Haircut $h_j\in[0,1]$ permits at most $(1-h_j)P_j q_j$ of secured lending against that pledge, subject to any separate borrowing cap; specify portfolio aggregation and prevent reuse of the same pledge. Buffer $b_i(a)$ is an optimal private holding given anticipated access, with explicit private funding cost and losses in stress. Public fiscal cost is the net resource or distortionary-financing loss after collateral recovery within a declared welfare boundary; unrecovered principal is not automatically a social loss, and transfers and their financing consequences must be distinguished. Specify repayment priority, maturity, central-bank balance-sheet limits and whether monetary-policy costs are included. Use a common probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ for exogenous innovations across facilities; policy-dependent prices, defaults and recoveries are endogenous outcomes, not quantities held fixed by this convention. For each facility and declared equilibrium, let $f_a$ be the measurable integrable public cost after recovery and let $\mathcal G_a\subseteq\mathcal F$ be the explicitly declared information used to average remaining recovery uncertainty. Define $F(a,\omega)=\E_P[f_a\mid\mathcal G_a](\omega)$, with equilibrium dependence suppressed in this notation; taking $\mathcal G_a=\mathcal F$ includes the realized-cost representation. The outer expectation averages the complete innovation law, so $\E_P F=\E_P f_a$. Require integrable crisis losses and finite operating costs when reporting a finite expected objective. If private choices or crisis prices are multiple, define a nonempty correspondence $\mathcal E_m(a)$ of complete equilibrium strategies and contingent allocations in the declared model $m$. For each reported target, either declare a measurable equilibrium selector or use the robust cost $\sup_{e\in\mathcal E_m(a)}\{\E_P[L_m(a,e,\omega)+F_m(a,e,\omega)]+C_m(a)\}$, followed by the infimum over feasible facilities. The robust supremum is over complete plans outside the expectation, not independent statewise selections. These conventions define separate targets; do not combine their policy rankings. State how policies without equilibrium are treated rather than assigning them a finite cost. Standing-access and discretionary-rescue problems have different commitment assumptions. For a nonempty feasible set and a finite optimal value, the design target is an $\varepsilon$-optimal feasible rule for each specified $\varepsilon>0$: its cost is at most the infimum plus $\varepsilon$, or its welfare is at least the supremum minus $\varepsilon$. Report infeasibility or an unbounded value separately. An exact optimizer is requested only under additional attainment assumptions; it need not exist for the general rule class. Required formulation details. Declare the welfare boundary, numeraire, horizon, discounting and social weights. Provide a nonoverlapping accounting map for the costs included in $L,F,C$, and state explicitly whether social opportunity costs of private buffers and prudential compliance costs are included, represented elsewhere or excluded. Private funding costs need not equal social resource costs. The displayed objective must not be described as comprehensive welfare if relevant costs are excluded; expanding it to add such costs is a changed accounting target. Specify private types and signals, reporting and observation rules, feasible deviations, verification, enforcement and payoffs, and write the incentive inequalities for the claimed constraints. Interpret $b_i(a)$ as a contingent strategy when information is available before buffer choice. For discretionary rescue, separately specify the authority's continuation information, objective and feasible actions, institutions' anticipation, and the equilibrium and belief-consistency requirements. Feasibility of truthful reporting or nonmanufacturable collateral is not inferred from the names of these constraints.

For policy comparisons, declare the benchmark, buffer aggregation, equilibrium comparison convention and externality measure. Any direct-effect versus buffer-response decomposition must define its counterfactuals and treatment of interactions. For an empirical application, name the interventions, population, observable random object, observation and sampling laws, admissible model class and target functionals before using the identified-set convention below. Evidence selection and observation-model construction remain research tasks until supplied; no identification or estimation result follows from the agenda alone.

Variants and assumption changes

  1. Committed standing facility (source-motivated). Announce access and haircuts before buffer choice and jointly choose prudential constraints; quantify private self-insurance changes.
  2. Discretionary stress lending (editorial). Let authorities reoptimize after shocks with fiscal limits; distinguish the optimal crisis action from the ex ante effects of anticipating rescue.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

These conventions apply to each entry unless explicitly replaced.

Models and identification. A primitive model $m$ specifies all probability laws, feasible choices, information, equilibrium and measurement rules used by its target. The admissible class $\mathcal M$ is fixed independently of outcomes. If $P_O(m)$ is its observable probability law and $\tau(m)$ the target, its identified set at observable law $P$ is \[ \mathcal I_\tau(P)=\{\tau(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_O(m)=P\}. \] Point identification means that this set is a singleton. An empty set signals incompatibility of data and assumptions. Coordinate bounds need not describe the joint set. Bounds are sharp only if every retained target is attainable or arbitrarily approachable by compatible models. Finite bounds require explicit restrictions on unobserved quantities; unrestricted confounding, hidden wealth, extrapolation or arbitrary equilibrium selection can yield uninformative sets.

Causal interventions. A potential outcome $Y_i(p)$ is the outcome under a complete policy regime $p$, including timing, financing, information and market spillovers. The target population and units are fixed before treatment. With interference, use the complete assignment vector or a justified exposure mapping; individual no-interference notation alone cannot identify an economy-wide intervention. A difference of mean potential outcomes does not require the cross-world joint distribution. Conditioning on post-treatment migration, survival, enrollment or employment changes the estimand and needs separate justification.

Statistical statements. An estimator, confidence set, forecast or test specifies a sampling law, model class and loss/coverage criterion. Uniform $(1-\alpha)$-coverage means $\inf_{m\in\mathcal M}\Pr_m\{\tau(m)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha$, or an explicitly asymptotic version. The whole parameter space trivially has coverage, so informativeness requires a stated width, risk or power objective. A forecasting improvement is relative to a named benchmark, information set and evaluation population.

Equilibrium and optimization. An equilibrium comprises feasible plans, optimality, beliefs where needed, budget constraints and all market/resource clearing. The primitives or a stated benchmark define each correspondence $\mathcal E(p;m)$. Nonemptiness is assumed or proved, never inferred just from compact policy sets. A selected-equilibrium target uses a declared selection rule; a robust target quantifies over all permitted equilibria. Use suprema or infima unless attainment is established. An $\varepsilon$-optimal policy is feasible and within $\varepsilon$ of the finite optimum value. Report an empty feasible set separately.

Welfare and accounting. Normative utility, interpersonal normalization, social weights, numeraire, discounting and the use of public receipts are primitives. Behavioral utility need not coincide with the welfare criterion. Household welfare includes ownership income and rents once; adding profits again to compensating variation generally double-counts them. A monetary equivalent is defined by an explicit transfer and price convention, with monotonicity and range conditions. Average effects, mechanism contributions, transfers and welfare losses are different targets. Infinite sums require convergence and dynamic feasible plans require terminal or no-Ponzi conditions.

Source versions. A working-paper date, revision date and journal publication year are distinguished. A DOI for a journal version is not automatically the DOI of an earlier manuscript. Locators refer to the stated version and printed pages where specified. A metadata-only check does not verify a claim about a full-text conclusion. Every entry's source subsection narrows the provenance claim accordingly.
