Math-1515

Dimension-four Schouten-jet uniqueness and a two-obstruction reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge4$ be even, let $[g]$ be a smooth Riemannian conformal structure, and write \[ J=\frac{R}{2(n-1)},\qquad P_{ij}=\frac{\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}-Jg_{ij}}{n-2}. \] Here $P$ is the Schouten tensor, distinct from the GJMS operator $P_n$. Let $\mathcal E[w]$ denote conformal densities of weight $w$, and use the nonnegative Laplacian $\Delta=-\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}$. A linear natural operator $A:\mathcal E[0]\to\mathcal E[-n]$ is conformally invariant when \[ A_{e^{2\omega}g}f=e^{-n\omega}A_gf. \] Its normalized principal symbol is that of $\Delta^{n/2}$.

Problem 11. Is the critical GJMS operator $P_n$ uniquely characterized by that principal part, conformal invariance, and coefficients expressible purely using the Schouten jets $\nabla^\ell P$, $\ell\ge0$, together with metric contractions? The exact naturality class is not specified in the source; polynomiality, parity, homogeneity and permitted curvature identities need to be stated before uniqueness can be a definite classification. Formal self-adjointness is not included in the question.

Supplied dimension-four result. In the narrower class of parity-even polynomial homogeneous natural operators built by complete contractions of Schouten jets, the aid asserts uniqueness in dimension four without a self-adjointness assumption. With $\delta=d^*$, the resulting operator is \[ P_4=\Delta^2+\delta\left((2Jg-4P)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\right) =\Delta^2+\delta\left((\tfrac23Rg-2\operatorname{Ric})\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\right). \] This asserted coefficient audit is a supplied partial result, not a proof of uniqueness in all even dimensions or in every possible nonpolynomial/orientation-sensitive naturality class.

Two-obstruction reduction in general even dimension. For a competitor $A$, put $D=A-P_n$. Cancellation of principal symbols makes $D$ lower order. Since $P_n(1)=0$, define \[ s_g=D_g(1)=A_g(1),\qquad D_0f=Df-s_gf. \] The scalar $s$ is a natural conformal invariant of weight $-n$, and $D_0$ is a conformally invariant lower-order operator annihilating constants. Locally it factors through exact one-form jets: \[ Df=s_gf+B_g({\,\mathrm{d}} f), \] where $B$ is defined on jets of exact one-forms. The decomposition into $s$ and $D_0$ is canonical for a fixed $D$; extending $B$ to an operator on all one-forms need not be canonical. Thus absence of Schouten-jet scalar conformal invariants removes only the multiplication anomaly $s$. A residual operator on exact one-form jets must also be ruled out to establish uniqueness. Any Schouten-only claim for this residual must additionally check the allowed coefficient identities for the critical GJMS operator itself.

Truncation and cross-reference. The registry text ends "Combined with a negative answer to". Its supplied verification/aid says the printed continuation is genuinely "Combined with a negative answer to Problem 11", probably an erroneous self-reference. The preceding Problem 10 is reported to ask about weight-$-n$ scalar conformal invariants made purely from $\nabla^\ell P$, so replacing the second "11" by "10" is a plausible logical inference, not verified printed wording. Even that replacement concerns only the scalar obstruction in the reduction above and is not silently made into a proof that the residual obstruction vanishes. No missing continuation or general characterization of $Q$ is invented.

Short English statement

The question seeks uniqueness of the critical GJMS operator using only Schouten-jet coefficients and its conformal covariance. The aid asserts uniqueness in a precise polynomial parity-even class in dimension four. In general, both a scalar multiplication anomaly and a constant-annihilating residual operator must be excluded; the source's truncated, probably mistaken cross-reference is retained explicitly.
