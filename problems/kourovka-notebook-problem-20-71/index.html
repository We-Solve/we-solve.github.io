Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.71

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph of order $n=|V|\geq 1$. For $v\in V$, the card associated with $v$ is the induced subgraph \[ G-v:=G[V\setminus\{v\}]. \] Write $[H]_{\cong}$ for the isomorphism class of a graph $H$, and define \[ c(G):=\left|\{[G-v]_{\cong}:v\in V\}\right|. \] Let $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be the group of bijections $\alpha:V\to V$ satisfying \[ \{u,v\}\in E\iff\{\alpha(u),\alpha(v)\}\in E \qquad(u,v\in V, \ u\ne v). \] Its orbit through $v$ is $\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cdot v=\{\alpha(v):\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\}$. Define the number of vertex orbits by \[ o(G):=\left|\{\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cdot v:v\in V\}\right|. \] For each of the following values of $k$, determine whether \[ \exists\,G\text{ finite, simple, undirected and connected} \quad\text{such that}\quad c(G)=k\quad\text{and}\quad o(G)>k: \] \[ \text{(a) }k=2,\qquad \text{(b) }k=3,\qquad \text{(c) }k=4. \] The supplied source observes that $o(G)\geq c(G)$, and reports that $c(G)=1$ implies that $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ acts transitively on $V$. It also reports examples with $(c(G),o(G))=(5,6)$ and $(6,7)$; the passage describing these examples does not explicitly state whether they are connected.

The supplied registry labels the overall problem partially solved. Its dated curation reports an affirmative answer to part (c), attributed to J. Prajapati's 2026 preprint. This is a supplied literature assessment, not an independently verified result here. Parts (a) and (b) remain questions in the supplied record. The record lists V. Pannone as proposer and prefixes the statement with "(J. Lauri)"; the relationship between these attributions is unspecified.

Short English statement

For each $k\in\{2,3,4\}$, does there exist a finite connected simple undirected graph with exactly $k$ isomorphism types of vertex-deleted cards but more than $k$ vertex orbits under its automorphism group? The supplied dated curation reports an affirmative answer for $k=4$; it supplies no resolution for $k=2$ or $k=3$.
