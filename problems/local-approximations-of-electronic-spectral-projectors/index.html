Local Approximations of Electronic Spectral Projectors

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
After spatial discretization, the one-electron Hamiltonian $H$ is a selfadjoint matrix or operator with orthonormal eigenvectors $\psi_i$ and ordered eigenvalues $\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$. The occupied subspace is $\operatorname{span}\{\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{N_e}\}$ and its orthogonal projector is $P$. A spectral gap means $\lambda_{N_e}<\lambda_{N_e+1}$ when the next level exists; it isolates the occupied projector independently of the choice of basis inside that subspace. Locality means sparsity or decay in a basis localized in physical space. The report seeks methods to approximate $P$ without computing all occupied eigenpairs.
Source scope and qualifications.
The task is an approximation program, not a claimed universal complexity bound. The Hamiltonian class, approximation norm, accuracy, and behavior as the gap closes must be specified by any proposed method; no gap is silently assumed for every electronic system.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Given a discrete electronic Hamiltonian $H$ with occupied eigenvectors $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{N_e}$, develop effective approximations of the spectral projector $$P=\sum_{i=1}^{N_e}|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|$$ onto the $N_e$ lowest eigenstates, particularly approximations exploiting locality and a spectral gap.

Short English statement

Approximate the occupied electronic subspace directly and efficiently by using spatial locality and a gap in the electronic spectrum.

A gap-dependent local polynomial.
Shift and scale the Hamiltonian so that $\|H\|\leq1$ and its spectrum lies in $[-1,-\delta]\cup[\delta,1]$, $0<\delta\leq1$, with the negative part occupied. Put $c_n=\binom{2n}{n}/4^n$ and \[ P_m=\tfrac12\left(I-H\sum_{n=0}^m c_n(I-H^2)^n\right). \] The scalar binomial series $(1-z)^{-1/2}=\sum_{n\geq0}c_nz^n$ gives $\operatorname{sgn}(H)=H(H^2)^{-1/2}$. Since $0<c_n\leq1$ and $\|I-H^2\|\leq q=1-\delta^2<1$, the remainder satisfies \[ \|P-P_m\|\leq\frac{q^{m+1}}{2(1-q)}. \] This follows by diagonalizing the finite self-adjoint matrix and bounding the scalar tail geometrically.
Locality and its price.
If $H_{xy}=0$ whenever graph distance exceeds one, then $(H^k)_{xy}=0$ for distance greater than $k$, because every nonzero matrix-product term traces a walk of length at most $k$. Thus $P_m$ has range at most $2m+1$. The estimate gives a concrete range-versus-error guarantee without occupied eigenvectors. At $\delta=1$, already $P_0=(I-H)/2$ is exact. As $\delta\downarrow0$, the bound deteriorates and gives no uniform metallic approximation.
Scope.
This elementary polynomial filter is a known kind of restricted approximation, not a new general electronic-structure algorithm. Hamiltonian sparsity, spectral scaling and a certified gap were explicit hypotheses. Efficient treatment of closing gaps, continuum discretization errors and large-system storage remains outside the proof; those are the first gaps in the broader source program.
