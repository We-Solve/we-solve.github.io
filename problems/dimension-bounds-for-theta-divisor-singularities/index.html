Math-3761

Dimension Bounds for Theta Divisor Singularities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The complex-analytic language of the supplied excerpt indicates the following intended setting, although the base field is not explicitly specified in the problem statement. Let $(A,\Theta)$ be a complex principally polarized abelian variety, and put $g=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}A$. Here $\Theta$ is an effective ample divisor whose associated polarization $A\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)$ is an isomorphism. The polarized variety is indecomposable if it is not isomorphic, as a polarized abelian variety, to a product of two positive-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties.

For an integer $m\geq2$, define the multiplicity locus \[ \operatorname{Sing}_{m}(\Theta) :=\{x\in A:\operatorname{mult}_{x}(\Theta)\geq m\}. \] The multiplicity is the order of vanishing of a local defining equation of $\Theta$ at $x$. We take the dimension of this locus with its reduced structure and use $\dim\varnothing=-\infty$. These are the conventional interpretations used here; the supplied excerpt does not explicitly define its multiplicity-locus notation or its empty-set convention.

Question. Is it true that, for every indecomposable complex principally polarized abelian variety $(A,\Theta)$ and every integer $m\geq2$, \[ \dim\operatorname{Sing}_{m}(\Theta)\leq g-2m+1\,? \] In particular, when $g-2m+1<0$, this asks that the multiplicity locus be empty.

Supplied background and qualifications. The excerpt attributes to Kollár, reference [9, Theorem 17.13] in the report, the statement that the log canonical threshold of $\Theta$ is $1$, equivalently that $(A,\Theta)$ is log canonical, and the resulting bound $\dim\operatorname{Sing}_{m}(\Theta)\leq g-m$ for all integers $m\geq2$. It attributes to Ein and Lazarsfeld [7], under indecomposability, normality and rational singularities of $\Theta$, together with the bound $\dim\operatorname{Sing}_{m}(\Theta)\leq g-m-1$. It attributes to Saito [15] the equivalence between rational singularities of $\Theta$ and $\widetilde\alpha_{\Theta}>1$, where $\widetilde\alpha_{\Theta}$ denotes its minimal exponent. These reference labels are internal to the report; their full bibliographic entries are not supplied.

The excerpt reports that the proposed stronger bound holds for Prym varieties associated with étale double covers, by work of Casalaina-Martin [5], and states it as a corollary for $g\leq5$, with the supplied justification that every principally polarized abelian variety in those dimensions is a Prym variety. The precise scope of that Prym representation assertion is not explained in the record. It also attributes to Mustaţă and Popa [13] the proposed bound when $\Theta$ has only isolated singularities, using Hodge ideals.

The excerpt additionally reports a conditional theorem from joint work with Christian Schnell: if the "center of minimal exponent" is a one-dimensional scheme $Y$, then $Y$ is a smooth hyperelliptic curve and the proposed bound holds for every integer $m\geq2$. It further states that equality for some such $m$ implies either $A=\operatorname{Jac}(Y)$, or \[ g=2m,\qquad g(Y)=2m-1,\qquad \widetilde\alpha_{\Theta}=\frac{2m-1}{m}, \] with $\Theta$ having a unique singular point of multiplicity $m$. This is retained as a supplied source claim, with unresolved qualifications: the excerpt breaks off before defining the center, and it does not specify the sense of the Jacobian identification. Moreover, its second alternative gives $\dim\operatorname{Sing}_{m}(\Theta)=0$ while $g-2m+1=1$, so that alternative cannot itself realize equality under the multiplicity-locus interpretation above. The exact conditional equality statement therefore requires checking against the complete source. The general equality classification mentioned in the supplied assessment is not formulated precisely enough here to constitute an additional question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the points where a theta divisor has multiplicity at least $m$ always form a locus of dimension at most $\dim A-2m+1$, for every integer $m\geq2$, when the principally polarized abelian variety is indecomposable. The formulation uses the complex setting suggested by the excerpt. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved; its special cases, conditional results, and equality discussion remain attributed source claims requiring review.
