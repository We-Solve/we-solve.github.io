Math-7032

Poincaré-Function Limits Characterizing Exceptional Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p\in{\mathbb{R}}[z]$ have degree $d>1$ and $p'(0)=\lambda>1$. Let $f$ be the entire solution specified by \[ f(\lambda z)=p(f(z)),\qquad f(0)=0,\qquad f'(0)=1. \] Evaluation at zero requires $p(0)=0$; this is a compatibility consequence of the supplied equation, not an independently added restriction. Put \[ \rho=\log_\lambda d=\frac{\log d}{\log\lambda},\qquad N_f(x)=\#\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:f(\xi)=0,\ |\xi|<x\}\quad(x>0). \] The literal counting notation counts distinct complex zeros in the open disk; a multiplicity convention, if intended, must be checked. Let $\mu$ be the source's associated harmonic measure, whose domain and normalization are not supplied.

The conjectural characterization uses the two limits \[ \lim_{x\to\infty}x^{-\rho}N_f(x), \qquad \lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{-\rho}\mu(B(0,t)). \] Does existence of either limit characterize polynomials linearly conjugate to a Chebyshev polynomial or a pure power? On the reading that either one separately characterizes the exceptional class, each limit-existence condition is to be equivalent to membership in that class. If the source instead intends only the disjunction of the existence conditions, that is a weaker logical target and requires confirmation. No one-direction implication is substituted for the stated characterization.

The usual polynomial-dynamics meaning of linear conjugacy is affine conjugacy $p=\ell^{-1}\circ q\circ\ell$, with $\ell(z)=az+b$ and $a\ne0$. The source does not state real versus complex conjugacy, whether translations are allowed, the signed Chebyshev convention, or the exact pure-power normal form. These choices matter with the repelling fixed point normalized at zero. Chebyshev polynomials conventionally satisfy $T_d(\cos t)=\cos(dt)$, but no additional sign class is silently included.

The harmonic measure is often that associated with the polynomial's Julia set, viewed from infinity, but the source does not define it. The ball's metric and whether limit existence means a finite value, a finite strictly positive value, or an extended-real value are also unstated. These conventions must be recovered; no zero or infinite limit is silently accepted or excluded.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether normalized zero-counting or local harmonic-measure limits occur exactly for Chebyshev-type or pure-power polynomials. It retains both limit formulas, the normalized entire solution, and the unresolved conjugacy, measure, and limit-existence conventions.
