Linear Systems and Eigenvalue Problems — Problem 28

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$, and consider a nonnormal $n\times n$ matrix admitting an eigendecomposition \[ A=V\Lambda V^{-1},\qquad \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n), \] where $V$ is invertible. Thus the displayed assumption restricts the question to diagonalizable matrices. Write \[ \rho=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\mu_j|, \qquad \kappa_V=\|V\|\,\|V^{-1}\|<\infty. \] Here $\rho$ denotes the largest eigenvalue magnitude called $\lambda_1$ in the source. The scalar field and matrix norm are unspecified in the supplied record; in particular, whether real input matrices may use complex eigenvectors is not stated. The condition number refers to the displayed choice of $V$; no normalization or minimization over eigenvector matrices is specified.

For a positive accuracy parameter $\epsilon$, define the set of eigenvalues near the spectral radius by \[ S_\epsilon(A)=\{\mu_j:1\leq j\leq n,\ |\mu_j|\geq(1-\epsilon)\rho\}. \] The intended admissible range of $\epsilon$, beyond its role as an accuracy parameter, is not supplied.

The source asks whether there is an algorithm which, uniformly over the stated matrix class, uses \[ \operatorname{poly}\!\left(\log(n\kappa_V),\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right) \] matrix--vector products with $A$ and returns a scalar $\lambda$ approximating an eigenvalue in $S_\epsilon(A)$ to absolute error at most $\epsilon\rho$.

The quantifier in the source phrase "any eigenvalue" is unresolved. If it means an eigenvalue chosen by the algorithm, the requested output condition is \[ \exists\lambda'\in S_\epsilon(A) \quad |\lambda-\lambda'|\leq\epsilon\rho. \] If it means every qualifying eigenvalue simultaneously, the condition is instead \[ \forall\lambda'\in S_\epsilon(A) \quad |\lambda-\lambda'|\leq\epsilon\rho. \] These are distinct formulations; the supplied record does not unambiguously select one. It also does not specify whether the algorithm may be randomized, its required success probability, its arithmetic precision, or what information about $V$, $\kappa_V$, and $\rho$ is supplied. The stated resource bound counts matrix--vector products with $A$; other allowed operations and access to the matrix are not described. No hypothesis excludes $\rho=0$; in that boundary case the stated error bound is zero.

Short English statement

For a diagonalizable nonnormal matrix, can an algorithm use only $\operatorname{poly}(\log(n\kappa_V),1/\epsilon)$ products with the matrix to approximate an eigenvalue whose magnitude is at least $(1-\epsilon)$ times the spectral radius, with error at most $\epsilon$ times that radius? The source leaves unresolved whether the output must approximate one qualifying eigenvalue or every such eigenvalue, and leaves several computational and domain conventions unspecified.
