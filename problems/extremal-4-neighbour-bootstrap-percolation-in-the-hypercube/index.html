Math-1019

Extremal $4$-Neighbour Bootstrap Percolation in the Hypercube

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems openaigpt 6 astra ultra Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $d\ge4$, the hypercube graph $Q_d$ has vertex set $\{0,1\}^d$; two vertices are adjacent precisely when their binary strings differ in one coordinate. Write $N(v)$ for the $d$ neighbours of $v$. Choose an initial infected set $A\subseteq V(Q_d)$, and recursively define \[ A_0=A,\qquad A_{t+1}=A_t\cup\{v\in V(Q_d):|N(v)\cap A_t|\ge4\} \quad(t\ge0). \] Infections are permanent, and each step is simultaneous. Since the graph is finite and the sets increase, the process eventually stabilizes. The set $A$ percolates when $\bigcup_{t\ge0}A_t=V(Q_d)$.

Define the extremal number \[ m(Q_d,4)=\min\{|A|:A\subseteq V(Q_d),\ A\text{ percolates}\}. \] The problem is to determine this integer exactly as a function of $d$. A complete determination needs both a construction of an initial set of the claimed cardinality for each dimension and a lower bound excluding all smaller sets. No randomness is imposed on the initial set, and the percolation time is not the quantity being minimized.

The threshold is exactly four infected neighbours, regardless of dimension; therefore this is the four-neighbour process rather than a majority rule. For completeness, when $0\le d<4$ no initially healthy vertex can become infected, so the same definition gives $m(Q_d,4)=2^d$. The nontrivial range of the source problem is $d\ge4$.

Short English statement

How few initially infected vertices can eventually infect the entire $d$-dimensional cube when four infected neighbours are needed?

Research attempt

Scope and process.
This is a GPT-6 Astra Ultra research attempt dated 1 October 2026, using primary-source browsing and elementary mathematical checks. The canonical definition above is retained. Results below are restricted results or reductions, not a claim of a new solution to the entire catalogue question. No formal proof assistant or external mathematical referee checked this text.
Literature and scope.
Morrison and Noel [R1] establish strong asymptotic bounds for fixed threshold and an exact formula at threshold three. Those are different from the exact threshold-four function here. The present elementary attack uses the graph induced by the healthy vertices, settles $d=4$, and gives a checked two-point interval for $d=5$. These are small-case benchmarks, not improvements on the general literature.
An exact reduction to deletion of low-degree vertices.
Let $H_t=V(Q_d)\setminus A_t$. A vertex in $H_t$ has $d-\deg_{H_t}(v)$ infected neighbours. Hence \[ H_{t+1}=\{v\in H_t:\deg_{H_t}(v)\ge d-3\}. \] Thus percolation is equivalent to emptying the induced healthy graph by repeatedly deleting vertices of degree at most $d-4$. Equivalently, the initial healthy graph has no nonempty subgraph of minimum degree at least $d-3$. For one implication such a subgraph survives every round; for the other a nonempty terminal graph is such a subgraph. This also proves that deleting eligible vertices sequentially or simultaneously gives the same emptiness criterion. It is the usual core-deletion characterization, with the threshold checked explicitly.
Exact value in dimension four.
For $d=4$, only degree-zero healthy vertices are deleted. An initial healthy edge protects its two endpoints forever, while an independent healthy set is deleted in the first round. Thus a percolating set is precisely a vertex cover of $Q_4$. Pair each vertex with the vertex obtained by flipping its first coordinate: this is a matching of eight disjoint edges, so any vertex cover has at least eight vertices. The eight even-weight vertices form a vertex cover, since every cube edge changes weight parity. Therefore \[ m(Q_4,4)=8. \] This verifies both the lower bound and a construction.
Dimension five: forests and a lower bound.
For $d=5$, healthy deletion removes vertices of degree at most one. A finite graph is emptied by this procedure exactly when it is a forest: every nonempty forest has a vertex of degree at most one, whereas the vertices of a cycle survive forever. If $a=|A|$, the healthy induced graph has $32-a$ vertices. There are $80$ cube edges and at most $5a$ edges incident with $A$, so the healthy graph has at least $80-5a$ edges. If $a\le12$, a forest on $32-a$ vertices has at most $31-a$ edges, whereas $80-5a>31-a$ because $49>4a$. This is impossible. Hence $m(Q_5,4)\ge13$.
An explicit fourteen-vertex construction.
Represent vertices by five-bit strings. Infect all even-weight vertices except $00000$ and $01111$; the infected set has fourteen vertices. Initially the healthy set consists of all sixteen odd-weight vertices and those two exceptions. The exceptions are at Hamming distance four, so they have no common neighbour: a common neighbour would put them at distance at most two. The healthy graph is therefore two disjoint five-leaf stars plus six isolated vertices, which is a forest. More directly, every odd vertex has at most one healthy neighbour, so all odd vertices are infected in round one. The two exceptions then each have five infected neighbours and enter in round two. Thus \[ 13\le m(Q_5,4)\le14. \] Exact simulation in scripts/check_attempt_batch_algebra_2026_10_01.py checks these simultaneous rounds, with layer sizes $14,16,2$.
Verification and limits.
The degree threshold in the healthy graph depends on $d$, even though the infection threshold remains four. Substituting a forest condition in dimensions larger than five would therefore be incorrect. The edge count deliberately uses an inequality; edges within $A$ are double-counted in $5a$, which only weakens the lower bound and does not invalidate it. For $d<4$ no new infection is possible and the value is $2^d$, as already specified in the definition. The missing small-case step is exclusion or construction of a thirteen-vertex seed set in $Q_5$ by this argument, and the larger gap is an exact formula with matching constructions and lower bounds in every dimension.
Final status.
UNRESOLVED for general $d$. The healthy-core equivalence, exact dimension-four value and dimension-five bounds are proved; the displayed interval is a limitation of this elementary attempt, not a claim that the literature has no sharper small-case result.
