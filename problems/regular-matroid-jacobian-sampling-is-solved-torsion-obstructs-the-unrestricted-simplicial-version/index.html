Math-1353

Regular-matroid Jacobian sampling is solved; torsion obstructs the unrestricted simplicial version

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n\times m}$ have full row rank, let $L=DD^{\mathsf T}$, and define the finite group \[ J(D)={\mathbb{Z}}^n/L{\mathbb{Z}}^n. \] A column basis is an $n$-element index set $B$ with $\det D_B\ne0$.

Problem 2 and discussion. Jeremy Martin asks whether sampling a graph Jacobian and mapping to a uniform spanning tree generalizes to simplicial complexes, concretely whether uniform samples from $J(D)$ can efficiently yield uniform column bases. Farbod Shokrieh proposes regular matroids, for which the Jacobian has the right basis-count cardinality, and asks for an efficient bijection analogous to Dhar's burning algorithm. Matt Baker invokes Cauchy--Binet under unimodularity. Martin's final comment suggests the condition on maximal minors suffices. The distinction between total unimodularity and nonzero-maximal-minor unimodularity is made precise below; the extracted phrase "all maximal minors" should not silently exclude zero minors.

Reported regular-matroid resolution and matrix transport. Cauchy--Binet gives \[ |J(D)|=\det(DD^{\mathsf T})=\sum_{|B|=n}\det(D_B)^2. \] If every nonzero maximal minor is $\pm1$, this equals the number of column bases. Choose one basis $B_0$, put $U=D_{B_0}^{-1}\in\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{Z}})$ and $A=UD$. The aid proves $A$ is totally unimodular, meaning every square minor lies in $\{0,\pm1\}$, and \[ AA^{\mathsf T}=U(DD^{\mathsf T})U^{\mathsf T},\qquad [x]\longmapsto[Ux]:J(D)\xrightarrow{\sim}J(A). \] It credits Backman--Baker--Yuen (2019) with explicit regular-matroid Jacobian torsors and polynomial-time basis bijections, applicable after this transport. Their auxiliary choices and full algorithm are not supplied in the summary.

Reported direct projection sampler. For arbitrary full-row-rank $D$, the rank-$n$ projection kernel \[ K=D^{\mathsf T}(DD^{\mathsf T})^{-1}D \] yields a sequential Schur-complement sampler with \[ \Pr(B)=\det K_{B,B}=\frac{\det(D_B)^2}{\det(DD^{\mathsf T})}. \] This is uniform under the nonzero-maximal-minor-$\pm1$ condition. A qualification to the summary's phrase "uniform precisely in this unimodular regime" is needed: for the sampler alone, the exact criterion is that all nonzero maximal determinants have equal absolute value. For example $D=(2\ \ 2)$ samples its two bases uniformly but has $|J(D)|=8$, so no bijection to its two bases. Unimodularity is the regime giving both uniform determinant weights and the stated Jacobian cardinality, not a necessary condition for every uniform projection sample.

For the worked family $D=[I_n\mid v]$, $v\in\{0,\pm1\}^n$, let $s$ count its nonzero coordinates. The aid states that $[v]$ generates the cyclic group $J(D)$ of order $s+1$, and there are exactly $s+1$ bases: $I_n$ and each replacement of a column $i$ with $v_i\ne0$ by $v$. It reports a linear-time bijection in this family; the computational bit-cost convention is not given.

For general simplicial complexes, higher tree formulas retain torsion-squared weights. The report therefore does not solve the unrestricted unweighted problem. Cardinality/weight obstructions to this particular Jacobian-bijection approach are not a proof that every other uniform-sampling method is impossible.

Short English statement

The regular-matroid Jacobian-to-basis bijection is reported solved, and the aid transports the exact integer-matrix hypothesis to a totally unimodular representation. A projection sampler works more generally but weights bases by squared determinants. General simplicial torsion prevents simply treating those weighted samples as uniform; the summary's claimed necessity of unimodularity for sampler uniformity also needs qualification.
