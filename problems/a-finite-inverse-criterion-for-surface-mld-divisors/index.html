Math-1227

A finite inverse criterion for surface mld divisors

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a normal variety $X$ and an effective boundary $D$ with $K_X+D$ ${\mathbb{R}}$-Cartier, write \[ A_E(X,D)=1+\operatorname{ord}_E(K_Y-\mu^*(K_X+D)) \] for the log discrepancy of a prime divisor $E$ on a birational model $\mu:Y\to X$. At a specified center $x$, the minimal log discrepancy is the infimum of $A_F(X,D)$ over prime divisors $F$ centered at $x$. A divisor computes it when this infimum equals its discrepancy. Ishii's AIM questions ask which exceptional divisors over mildly singular $X$ compute a nonnegative mld for some pair $(X,D)$, and whether every exceptional component of a surface's minimal resolution can do so. The singularity class, center convention, field and permitted boundaries are not fully specified.

The report treats a singular klt surface germ $(X,x)$. Klt means positive log discrepancies for $(X,0)$. Let $\mu:Y\to X$ be its minimal resolution with exceptional components $E_1,\ldots,E_N$, and put \[ P_{ij}=-(E_i\cdot E_j),\qquad v_i=\text{valency of }E_i\text{ in the exceptional dual graph}. \] Its claimed finite criterion for a prescribed $E_k$ is existence of a real vector $A=(A_i)$ such that \[ A_i\geq0\ \text{for every }i,\qquad PA\leq2\mathbf1-v\ \text{componentwise},\qquad A_k=\min_iA_i. \] The report asserts equivalence between feasibility and existence of an effective ${\mathbb{R}}$-divisor boundary $B$ for which $E_k$ computes $\operatorname{mld}_x(X,B)\geq0$. The intended vector is $A_i=A_{E_i}(X,B)$. Conversely it claims that every feasible vector is realized by distributing $d=2\mathbf1-v-PA$ among general curvettes, smooth transverse curve germs meeting the exceptional components away from their intersections, with suitable boundary coefficients. This includes a claim about all divisors over $x$, rather than merely minimizing among the listed components; the curvette construction is needed to control further blowups.

As a negative surface example, the report uses the tetrahedral quotient singularity labeled $T_7$, with a central fork curve of self-intersection $-3$ and three arms of lengths $1,2,2$, all five arm curves having self-intersection $-2$. It claims the inequalities force uniformly positive discrepancy gaps from the fork to every arm component. Consequently none of those five arm components can compute a nonnegative mld for any effective boundary in the stated class. The actual gap constants and proof are not supplied.

The field and characteristic assumptions for the klt graph, minimal-resolution and general-curvette constructions are absent; the usual characteristic-zero quotient-surface interpretation requires confirmation. The criterion is a supplied partial surface result, not a classification of all exceptional divisors over arbitrary mildly singular varieties.

Short English statement

Determine which exceptional divisors can compute a nonnegative minimal log discrepancy after adding an effective boundary. The report turns the singular-klt-surface case into finite inequalities on the minimal-resolution graph and claims a curvette realization of every feasible vector. Its $T_7$ example reportedly excludes all arm components, giving a negative answer to unrestricted surface-component optimism.
