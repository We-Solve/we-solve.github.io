Boundary Corrections to Domain Lieb–Thirring Constants

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ denote the domain mentioned in the source. The source does not specify the dimension range, regularity or boundedness of $\Omega$, boundary conditions, potential class, or eigenvalue-moment exponent. Consequently, it does not determine a single quantified conjecture.

To describe the requested estimate conditionally, write $H_{\Omega,V}=-\Delta+V$ for a self-adjoint Schrödinger realization once its boundary conditions and hypotheses on the real potential $V$ have been specified. Set $V_-(x)=\max\{-V(x),0\}$. If the negative spectrum consists of discrete eigenvalues $\lambda_j(H_{\Omega,V})<0$, counted with multiplicity, define \[ M_\gamma(H_{\Omega,V})=\sum_{\lambda_j<0}|\lambda_j|^\gamma\quad(\gamma>0), \qquad M_0(H_{\Omega,V})=\#\{j:\lambda_j<0\}. \] These definitions supply notation; they do not impose hypotheses absent from the source.

Source question. Can Lieb--Thirring estimates for a Schrödinger operator on $\Omega$ be improved relative to the corresponding estimates on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, for example through a boundary correction? Schematically, in a setting where a whole-space constant $L_{\gamma,n}$ gives the relevant comparison, the question seeks an improvement of an estimate of the form \[ M_\gamma(H_{\Omega,V})\le L_{\gamma,n}\int_\Omega V_-(x)^{\gamma+n/2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \] possibly by reducing the constant or by including a nontrivial correction reflecting the boundary. This display describes the intended type of estimate, not an assertion that it holds for unspecified boundary conditions, dimensions, exponents, or potentials. The form, sign, size, and uniformity of a boundary correction, and the meaning of an improved constant, remain unspecified.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the geometry or boundary of a domain $\Omega$ permits stronger Lieb--Thirring bounds for negative Schrödinger eigenvalues than the corresponding whole-space bounds. Boundary conditions, admissible domains and potentials, exponents, and the proposed correction must be specified before this becomes a precise conjecture.
