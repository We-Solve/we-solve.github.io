Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For a unital coefficient algebra $B$, a Hilbert $B$-bimodule is a right Hilbert $B$-module with a compatible left action. $\mathcal B^a(E)$ denotes its adjointable operators. A pointed free product system $(E_t,\omega_t)$ has central reference vectors and associative identifications $E_s*E_t\simeq E_{s+t}$, using the source's reduced free product over $B$. A tensor product subsystem instead has $E_s\odot E_t\simeq E_{s+t}$ with $\odot$ the internal tensor product over $B$. An $E_0$-semigroup is a continuous semigroup of unital $*$-endomorphisms. A unit is a section whose values multiply according to the product-system structure. The time-indexed subalgebras embedded through the free left dilation give the free filtration. A unitary cocycle relative to an endomorphism semigroup $\vartheta_t$ obeys $U_{s+t}=U_s\vartheta_s(U_t)$, with a fixed cocycle convention. Adaptedness is intended to express localization in the available past filtration; finding the appropriate definition for this free setting is itself part of the question. Generation by a subsystem means that the closed free product subsystem it generates equals the original system.
Source scope and qualifications.
There are three retained requests: the adaptedness notion, the proposed Fock-case construction from units, and generation by the intersection tensor subsystem. The last request is repeated separately at ID 30001179.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
What is the correct notion of a cocycle adapted to the free filtration, and can it yield adapted unitary cocycles directly from units of the tensor-product system? Does the largest tensor-product subsystem contained in a free product system generate the whole free product system?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Adapted cocycles and related cohomological constructions have been developed, but a general construction or characterization for cocycles adapted to the free filtration remains unresolved. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Define adapted cocycles for the free filtration, construct them from tensor units, and determine whether the largest tensor subsystem generates the free system.

The tensor-Fock benchmark.
In symmetric Fock space over $L^2(\mathbb R_+)$, use exponential vectors $e(g)$ with $\langle e(g),e(h)\rangle=e^{\langle g,h\rangle}$ and define \[ W(f)e(g)=\exp(-\|f\|^2/2-\langle f,g\rangle)e(f+g). \] An inner-product calculation shows that this preserves inner products on the total exponential-vector span, and $W(-f)$ is its inverse; hence it extends to a unitary. For functions with disjoint supports, direct substitution gives $W(f)W(g)=W(f+g)$, since their inner product is zero.
An explicit adapted cocycle.
Fix $z\in\mathbb C$, put $f_t=z1_{(0,t)}$ and $U_t=W(f_t)$. If $S_s$ shifts functions by $s$ and the Fock shift endomorphism sends $W(f)$ to $W(S_sf)$, then $f_{s+t}=f_s+S_sf_t$ with disjoint supports. Consequently $U_{s+t}=U_s\vartheta_s(U_t)$. Moreover $U_t$ acts only on the past tensor factor and sends the vacuum to $e^{-\|f_t\|^2/2}e(f_t)$, a normalized unit. Strong continuity follows first from the displayed formula on exponential vectors and then from unitarity and density.
The free-system gap.
This is the familiar tensor-Fock construction, not a definition of adaptedness for the source's free filtration. Its proof uses orthogonal tensor splitting and commuting disjoint Weyl operators. In a free product there is no justified substitution of those tensor identities. Extending this construction while specifying the proper localization notion is unresolved, and the further claim that the largest intersection tensor subsystem generates the entire free system is independent and remains unproved, including the repeated question at 30001179.
