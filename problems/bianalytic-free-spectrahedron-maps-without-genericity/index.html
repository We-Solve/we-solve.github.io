Bianalytic Free Spectrahedron Maps Without Genericity

Definitions and mathematical statement

For terminology, under the standard complex monic-pencil convention, let $g,d\geq 1$ and let $A=(A_1,\ldots,A_g)\in M_d({\mathbb{C}})^g$. For $n\geq 1$ and $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_g)\in M_n({\mathbb{C}})^g$, set \[ L_A(X)=I_d\otimes I_n+\sum_{j=1}^g\left(A_j\otimes X_j+A_j^*\otimes X_j^*\right), \qquad \mathcal D_A(n)=\{X\in M_n({\mathbb{C}})^g:L_A(X)\succeq 0\}. \] Here $^*$ denotes conjugate transpose and $\succeq 0$ denotes positive semidefiniteness. The sequence $\mathcal D_A=(\mathcal D_A(n))_{n\geq 1}$ is the free spectrahedron determined by $A$. In this convention, boundedness means that there is a constant $R<\infty$ such that $\|X_j\|\leq R$ for every level $n$, every $X\in\mathcal D_A(n)$, and every $1\leq j\leq g$. Define $\mathcal D_B$ analogously for a coefficient tuple $B$, whose matrix size may differ from that of $A$.

A free analytic map is a level-preserving map that is holomorphic at each matrix level and respects direct sums and simultaneous similarities whenever the tuples involved belong to its domain. A free bianalytic map is a bijective free analytic map whose inverse is free analytic. Analytic maps between free spectrahedra are ordinarily considered on their levelwise interiors. These definitions provide terminology only: the supplied record does not specify the underlying pencil convention, scalar field, tuple lengths, or whether the referenced theorem imposes additional boundary conditions.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the partial converse classifying bianalytic maps between bounded free spectrahedra remains valid without assuming that the coefficient tuples $A$ and $B$ are generic.
