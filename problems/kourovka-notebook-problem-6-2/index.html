Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.2

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be an arbitrary group, with no finiteness assumption. The supplied record does not define the notation $C$ or the term $C$-closed. The following formulation uses the usual centralizer interpretation; this convention requires confirmation against the cited source.

For a subset $S\subseteq G$, define its centralizer by \[ C_G(S)=\{g\in G:gs=sg\text{ for every }s\in S\}. \] Under this interpretation, a subgroup $H\leq G$ is $C$-closed if \[ H=C_G(C_G(H)). \] Equivalently, $H$ is the centralizer of some subset of $G$. Write \[ \mathcal C(G)=\{H\leq G:H=C_G(C_G(H))\}. \] Ordered by inclusion, this is a complete lattice. Its binary operations, as given in the supplied statement, are \[ A\wedge_{\mathcal C(G)}B=A\cap B, \qquad A\vee_{\mathcal C(G)}B=C_G\left(C_G(A)\cap C_G(B)\right). \] Let $\operatorname{Sub}(G)$ denote the lattice of all subgroups of $G$, whose binary meet and join are intersection and generated subgroup, respectively.

Problem. Describe all groups $G$ for which $\mathcal C(G)$ is a sublattice of $\operatorname{Sub}(G)$. Explicitly, classify the groups satisfying \[ \forall A,B\in\mathcal C(G),\qquad \langle A\cup B\rangle =C_G\left(C_G(A)\cap C_G(B)\right). \] Equivalently, the subgroup generated by any two $C$-closed subgroups must again be $C$-closed. Here sublattice means preservation of binary meets and joins; preservation of arbitrary or empty joins in the ambient subgroup lattice is not imposed.

Short English statement

Using the usual centralizer meaning of $C$, classify all groups in which the subgroup generated by any two double-centralizer-closed subgroups is again double-centralizer-closed. The source does not explicitly define this terminology, so that interpretation requires review. The registry labels the problem open; this is supplied status information, not an independently verified assessment.
