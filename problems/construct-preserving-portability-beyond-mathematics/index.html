Attempt: transporting numerical assessment into a unit-bearing domain

The source leaves the target discipline unspecified. I tested one concrete extension to physical quantities: preserving dimensions when transporting numerical answer checks. Assign each input $x_i$ a dimension vector $d_i\in\mathbb Z^r$ relative to declared base units. In an expression tree, dimensionless constants have vector zero, multiplication adds vectors, division subtracts them, and addition or subtraction is permitted only for equal vectors. Divisions are evaluated only where denominators are nonzero.
Proposition: Every expression accepted by these typing rules transforms consistently under simultaneous rescaling $x_i\mapsto s^{d_i}x_i$, where $s\in(0,\infty)^r$ and $s^d=\prod_j s_j^{d_j}$. Its value is multiplied by $s^d$, where $d$ is its output dimension vector.
Proof: Induct on the expression tree. Inputs have the required transformation by definition and dimensionless constants are unchanged. If two subexpressions have scale factors $s^a,s^b$, their product has factor $s^{a+b}$ and their quotient factor $s^{a-b}$, matching the typing rules. For an accepted sum or difference, $a=b$, so both terms share the factor $s^a$, which can be factored out of their sum or difference. This establishes the result at every node and hence at the root. Positive scale factors also preserve whether any denominator is zero. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

For distance $x$ of dimension $(1,0)$ and time $t$ of dimension $(0,1)$, the quotient $x/t$ has dimension $(1,-1)$ and scales by $s_1/s_2$. The numerical calculation $2+2=4$ can conceal an invalid addition of two meters and two seconds, which this checker rejects. Conversely, the false physical relation $x=2x$ is dimensionally consistent, so passing the check does not prove physical truth. The failed inference is that numeric or syntactic portability preserves the target-domain construct. This restricted typing rule does not address all STEM learning, other disciplines or educational effectiveness.
