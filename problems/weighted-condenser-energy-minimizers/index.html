Weighted Condenser-Energy Minimizers

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $I$ be a countable index set and let $\mathbf A=(A_i)_{i\in I}$ be the condenser in the supplied record, where every $A_i\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ is nonempty and closed, and $A_i\cap A_j=\varnothing$ whenever $i\ne j$. Let $\mathbf a=(a_i)_{i\in I}$ satisfy \[ a_i>0\quad(i\in I),\qquad \sum_{i\in I}a_i<\infty. \] Let $g\in C({\mathbb{R}}^n)$, and suppose there exist constants $c_1,c_2$ such that \[ 0<c_1<g(x)<c_2<\infty\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n). \] Let $\mathbf f=(f_i)_{i\in I}$ be an admissible external field in the sense intended by the source.

The record denotes the constrained admissible class by $\mathcal E_{\mathbf f}^+(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g)$ and its energy functional by $G_{\mathbf f}$. Following its description of the target as an infimum, write \[ G_{\mathbf f}(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g) :=\inf_{\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf f}^+(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g)} G_{\mathbf f}(\boldsymbol\mu). \] The supplied question is whether, for every choice of data satisfying the stated hypotheses, \[ G_{\mathbf f}(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g)<\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\boldsymbol\lambda\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf f}^+(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g) \ \text{such that}\ G_{\mathbf f}(\boldsymbol\lambda) =G_{\mathbf f}(\mathbf A,\mathbf a,g). \] This is a conditional restatement: the supplied record does not specify the interaction kernel, the energy formula, admissibility of the external field, or the precise definition of the constrained class and its mass constraints. These missing definitions must be recovered before this becomes a fully specified mathematical problem. No additional condenser hypotheses or lower bound on the infimum are imposed here.

Short English statement

For the supplied countable condenser, positive summable masses, bounded positive continuous weight, and admissible external field, does the constrained energy infimum have a minimizer whenever it is less than $+\infty$? The energy and admissibility definitions are missing from the record, so the formulation remains conditional and requires human mathematical review.
