Iwahori-Hecke Algebras — Problem 2

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt omits the preceding setup of the Iwahori--Hecke algebra. Throughout, $W$, its weight function $L$, and the algebra $H$ with basis $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ mean the objects intended in that omitted setup. Their precise hypotheses and the defining multiplication of $H$ must be recovered before this becomes a standalone conjecture. The quantifiers below concern that setup. Read $A=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$, the damaged notation $v\text{-}$ as $v^{-1}$, and the damaged subscript $w\iota$ as $w^{-1}$; these are contextual reconstructions of the extraction.

The source specifies an additive-group automorphism $h\mapsto\overline h$ of $H$, which is also a ring isomorphism, by \[ \overline{v^nT_w}\,T_{w^{-1}}=v^{-n}1_H \qquad(n\in\mathbb Z,\ w\in W). \] Set \[ H_{\leq0}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathbb Z[v^{-1}]T_w. \] For each $w\in W$, the source asserts that there is a unique $c_w\in H_{\leq0}$ satisfying \[ \overline{c_w}=c_w,\qquad c_w-T_w\in v^{-1}H_{\leq0}, \] and that these elements form an $A$-basis of $H$. Define structure constants by the finite expansions \[ T_xT_y=\sum_{z\in W}f_{x,y,z}T_z, \qquad c_xc_y=\sum_{z\in W}h_{x,y,z}c_z, \qquad f_{x,y,z},h_{x,y,z}\in A. \]

The first requested assertion, labelled (b) in the source, is uniform boundedness: \[ \exists N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\ \forall x,y,z\in W, \qquad v^{-N}f_{x,y,z}\in\mathbb Z[v^{-1}]. \tag{b} \] Thus one bound must work simultaneously for every triple. The source describes (b) as immediate when $W$ is finite and known when $W$ is an affine Weyl group; these are supplied source qualifications.

Assume (b) for all subsequent constructions. The source asserts that the same $N$ bounds the coefficients $h_{x,y,z}$, and that for each $z\in W$ there is a unique $a(z)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ such that \[ \begin{aligned} &h_{x,y,z}\in v^{a(z)}\mathbb Z[v^{-1}] &&\text{for every }x,y\in W,\\ &h_{x,y,z}\notin v^{a(z)-1}\mathbb Z[v^{-1}] &&\text{for at least one pair }x,y\in W. \end{aligned} \] Define $\gamma_{x,y,z^{-1}}\in\mathbb Z$ by \[ h_{x,y,z}-\gamma_{x,y,z^{-1}}v^{a(z)} \in v^{a(z)-1}\mathbb Z[v^{-1}]. \] Let $J=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathbb Zt_w$. Extend the rule \[ t_xt_y=\sum_{z\in W}\gamma_{x,y,z^{-1}}t_z \] $\mathbb Z$-bilinearly; the source states that each displayed sum is finite. The second requested assertion, labelled (c), is that this multiplication makes $J$ an associative ring, with no identity element required in general. Equivalently, for every $x,y,u,w\in W$, \[ \sum_{z\in W}\gamma_{x,y,z^{-1}}\gamma_{z,u,w^{-1}} = \sum_{z\in W}\gamma_{y,u,z^{-1}}\gamma_{x,z,w^{-1}}. \tag{c} \] The source states that when $W$ is a Weyl group or an affine Weyl group and $L$ is the length function, (c) is known and $J$ has an identity element. The interpretation of the extracted notation $L=||$ as the length function requires confirmation against the original setup.

The excerpt also proposes a further program for an affine Weyl group with $L\in\Sigma_W$, where $\Sigma_W$ and $H_q$ are defined in the omitted part (a). It describes a geometric description of the $c_w$, referenced as [L16, 3.11], as conjectural, and suggests that it should imply (c) and the well-definedness of the map referenced in [L03, 18.9] as a $\mathbb C$-algebra homomorphism \[ \phi_q:H_q\longrightarrow\underline J, \qquad \underline J=\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb Z}J, \] with $\underline J$ independent of $q$. No formula for this map or allowed values of $q$ are supplied. The source further expects that the isomorphism classes of irreducible finite-dimensional $\underline J$-modules, viewed as $H_q$-modules through $\phi_q$, give classes forming a basis of the Grothendieck group of $H_q$-modules. The intended module category defining that Grothendieck group is unspecified, so this last expectation cannot yet be stated precisely. The source says that it holds when $L$ is the length function and suggests that the proposed approach would construct the standard modules of $H_q$ without the dual-group geometry used in [L95] and [L02]. These statements retain the source's conditional and conjectural qualifications.

Short English statement

Lusztig asks for a uniform upper bound on the powers of $v$ in the standard-basis structure constants of an Iwahori--Hecke algebra. Assuming that bound, he asks whether the leading coefficients in its canonical basis define an associative ring $J$. The excerpt also proposes consequences for affine Hecke algebra modules. The omitted algebra setup and the unspecified Grothendieck-group category require clarification before a fully standalone statement is possible.
