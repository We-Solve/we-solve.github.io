Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.5

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a closed surface of genus $g\geq2$, let $V=S\times S$, and let \[ \Sigma=\{(x,x):x\in S\}\subset V. \] Let $p:\widetilde V\to V$ be a finite branched cover ramified nontrivially along $\Sigma$: away from $\Sigma$ it is an ordinary finite covering, and at least one ramification index over $\Sigma$ exceeds $1$. In the usual manifold branched-cover convention, the local model transverse to the branch surface is $z\mapsto z^e$ for an integer $e\geq1$. The source does not specify a more restrictive ramification profile.

Assertion requested by the source exercise. For every such $S$ and $\widetilde V$, there is no $C^2$ Riemannian metric $h$ on $\widetilde V$ for which \[ K_h(\tau)\leq0\quad\hbox{for every }x\in\widetilde V \quad\hbox{and every two-dimensional subspace }\tau\subset T_x\widetilde V. \] Here $K_h$ denotes sectional curvature. The source also supplies a natural piecewise hyperbolic nonpositively curved metric on $\widetilde V$. Its original wording calls this a CAT(0) metric, whereas the research summary describes it as locally CAT(0); these are different global assertions, and only the local CAT(0) description is used as geometric background here. A locally CAT(0) metric has CAT(0) neighborhoods, meaning geodesic triangles in those neighborhoods satisfy the Euclidean comparison inequality.

The record reports that Stadler proved the asserted obstruction; the exact regularity and branched-cover hypotheses of that result are not verified by this draft.

Short English statement

A finite cover of $S\times S$ with nontrivial branching along its diagonal can carry the supplied locally CAT(0) piecewise hyperbolic metric, but the requested conclusion is that it admits no $C^2$ Riemannian metric of nonpositive sectional curvature.
