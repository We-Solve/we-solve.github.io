Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For an $\mathbb F_{q^m}$-linear code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_{q^m}^{n}$ and a Galois automorphism $\theta$ fixing $\mathbb F_q$, put $S_i^\theta(C)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\theta^j(C)$, $s_i^\theta(C)=\dim_{\mathbb F_{q^m}}S_i^\theta(C)$, and $\Delta_i^\theta(C)=s_{i+1}^\theta(C)-s_i^\theta(C)$. Rank distance is defined by expanding coordinates over $\mathbb F_q$. For $n\leq m$, a $k$-dimensional maximum-rank-distance (MRD) code has minimum rank distance $n-k+1$. Gabidulin codes are obtained by evaluating linearized polynomials at $\mathbb F_q$-linearly independent field elements.

The dimension profile needs a distance hypothesis

The primary source already proves properties of the theta profiles and gives a Gabidulin characterization with minimum rank distance greater than one. We test whether the profile alone can replace that distance hypothesis. Let $F=\mathbb F_2[\alpha]/(\alpha^3+\alpha+1)$; the cubic has no root in $\mathbb F_2$, hence is irreducible. Take $\theta(z)=z^2$ and the two $F$-linear length-three codes \[ C=\{x:x_1+\alpha x_2+\alpha^2x_3=0\},\qquad C'=\{x:x_1+\alpha x_2=0\}. \]
Proposition: Both codes have the same theta-dimension profile $(2,3,3,\ldots)$, but $C$ has minimum rank distance 2 and is MRD, while $C'$ has minimum rank distance 1.
Proof: Each is the kernel of one nonzero linear functional, so has dimension two. Its theta image has normal obtained by squaring each original normal coordinate. That new normal is not a scalar multiple of the original: their first coordinates are both 1, forcing any scalar multiple to use scalar 1, while $\alpha^2\ne\alpha$. Thus the code and its theta image are distinct two-dimensional subspaces of $F^3$, whose sum is all of $F^3$. This proves both profiles. A nonzero rank-one vector in $F^3$ is of the form $\lambda v$ with $\lambda\ne0$ and $v\in\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}$, because its coordinates span a one-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space. Such a vector cannot lie in $C$: $v_1+\alpha v_2+\alpha^2v_3\ne0$ by linear independence of $1,\alpha,\alpha^2$. The codeword $(\alpha,1,0)$ lies in $C$ and has rank weight 2, so its minimum is exactly 2, equal to $n-k+1$. In contrast $e_3\in C'$ has rank weight 1, proving the second claim. ∎
For completeness, the standard nonincreasing-increment property also has a short direct proof. With $S_i=\sum_{j=0}^i\theta^j(C)$, for $i\ge1$ the map induced by $\theta$ from $S_i/S_{i-1}$ onto $S_{i+1}/S_i$ is well defined, since $\theta(S_{i-1})\subseteq S_i$, and is surjective because $S_{i+1}=C+\theta(S_i)$ and $C\subseteq S_i$. This semilinear surjection cannot increase dimension, so $\Delta_i\le\Delta_{i-1}$. These necessary profile properties do not force a desired minimum distance, as the example shows.

Verification and remaining gaps

All 64 codewords of each code were enumerated over the explicitly represented field, their binary rank weights computed, and their theta-sum dimensions checked. The failed route is declaring a candidate MRD solely from this dimension profile. The example does not refute a characterization restricted in advance to MRD codes, nor the source's theorem with distance greater than one: $C'$ violates that hypothesis. No new MRD family or classification among already-MRD families is obtained.
