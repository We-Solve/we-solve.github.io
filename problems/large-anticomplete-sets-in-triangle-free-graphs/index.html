Math-3533

Large Anticomplete Sets in Triangle-Free Graphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph, and write $n=|V|$. The graph is triangle-free if no three distinct vertices are pairwise adjacent. Disjoint subsets $A,B\subseteq V$ are anticomplete if $\{a,b\}\notin E$ for every $a\in A$ and $b\in B$; no restriction is imposed on edges within either subset.

Under the lower-bound interpretation of the supplied phrase "of orders $cn^c$ and $cn$", the question is whether there exists a real constant $c>0$, independent of $n$ and $G$, such that every triangle-free graph $G$ with $n>2$ admits disjoint anticomplete subsets $A,B\subseteq V$ satisfying \[ |A|\geq cn^c\qquad\text{and}\qquad |B|\geq cn. \] Because cardinalities are integers, these inequalities are equivalent to $|A|\geq\lceil cn^c\rceil$ and $|B|\geq\lceil cn\rceil$. The same constant $c$ occurs in both coefficients and in the exponent. The source does not explicitly specify a rounding convention or say "at least"; thus this lower-bound interpretation requires confirmation. The finite simple undirected graph convention is also made explicit here rather than stated in the supplied wording.

Short English statement

Does one universal constant $c>0$ guarantee that every triangle-free graph on $n>2$ vertices contains two disjoint vertex sets, one of size at least $cn^c$ and the other of size at least $cn$, with no edges between them? This uses an explicitly qualified lower-bound interpretation of the source's size wording.
