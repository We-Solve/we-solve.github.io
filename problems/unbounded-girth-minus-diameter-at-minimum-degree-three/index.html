Math-4560

Unbounded Girth Minus Diameter at Minimum Degree Three

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether girth minus diameter is unbounded for graphs in which every vertex has degree at least three. It does not explicitly specify finiteness, simplicity, or connectedness. The following precise formulation uses the finite, simple, undirected, connected interpretation; these graph conventions require confirmation against the source.

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite, simple, undirected, connected graph. For $v\in V$, let $\deg_G(v)$ be the number of its neighbors, and define the minimum degree by \[ \delta(G)=\min_{v\in V}\deg_G(v). \] For vertices $u,v\in V$, let $d_G(u,v)$ be the minimum number of edges in a path joining them. Define \[ \operatorname{diam}(G)=\max_{u,v\in V}d_G(u,v). \] The girth $\operatorname{girth}(G)$ is the minimum number of edges in a cycle of $G$. Every finite graph with minimum degree at least three contains a cycle, so its girth is defined and finite.

Under the stated interpretation, the question is whether \[ \forall M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge 0}\quad \exists\,G\text{ finite, simple, undirected, and connected}: \qquad \delta(G)\ge 3 \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{girth}(G)-\operatorname{diam}(G)\ge M. \] The source does not supply conventions for disconnected or infinite graphs, or for graphs with loops or parallel edges; the displayed formulation is conditional on the graph conventions above.

Short English statement

Can the length of a shortest cycle exceed the largest distance between two vertices by arbitrarily large amounts in graphs whose vertices all have degree at least three? The displayed formulation interprets the graphs as finite, simple, undirected, and connected; the supplied record does not explicitly state these conventions.
