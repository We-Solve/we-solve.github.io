Math-1938

Wu's Higher-Dimensional Picard Conjecture

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $H_1,\ldots,H_{n+2}$ be projective hyperplanes in ${\mathbb{P}}^n({\mathbb{C}})$ in general position. Thus homogeneous defining linear forms for any $n+1$ of them are linearly independent; in particular no $n+1$ of these hyperplanes meet. Put \[ B=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n+2}H_j. \] Here subtraction of the source's set of hyperplanes means subtraction of their union.

The supplied higher-dimensional Picard assertion is that no nondegenerate holomorphic map \[ f:{\mathbb{C}}^n\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^n({\mathbb{C}})\setminus B \] exists. Under the usual maximal-rank meaning for maps between equidimensional complex manifolds, this can be written \[ \forall f:{\mathbb{C}}^n\to{\mathbb{P}}^n({\mathbb{C}})\text{ holomorphic},\qquad f({\mathbb{C}}^n)\cap B=\varnothing \ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{rank}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathrm df_z)<n \quad\text{for every }z\in{\mathbb{C}}^n. \] The record does not define nondegenerate. Linear nondegeneracy, meaning the image is not contained in any projective hyperplane, is a different condition. The intended condition must be confirmed rather than treating these two meanings as equivalent. The displayed rank formulation is explicitly conditional on the maximal-rank reading.

Short English statement

Can a nondegenerate holomorphic map from complex $n$-space omit $n+2$ hyperplanes in general position? The supplied assertion says it cannot. The registry labels this solved, and the research summary attributes an impossibility result to Carlson--Griffiths-type defect relations or a linear-degeneracy theorem, without exact references or hypotheses.

The source is Chern's 1970 presentation, with the title attributing the conjecture to Wu. Provenance records only its historical open status and says current status needs review. The later solved assessment is retained with the unresolved meaning of nondegeneracy and its missing theorem citation.
