Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can numerical methods correctly simulate polygonization in bent single-crystal beams, where dislocations align into polygonization patterns?

Source model and limits of the question

Blesgen's report, printed pages 3033--3034, specifies $D\varphi=R_eU_eF_p$, with $R_e\in SO(3)$, and the additive slip ansatz \[ F_p(\gamma)=I+\sum_{a=1}^I\gamma_a m_a n_a^T, \qquad |m_a|=|n_a|=1,\quad m_a^Tn_a=0. \] Its incremental energy contains $W_{\rm st}(R_e^TD\varphi F_p^{-1})$, angle-gradient energy, external work, and regularized slip dissipation. It suggests improved treatment of the micro-rotations because local minimization can select unwanted states. The catalogue's request for a physically correct polygonization pattern does not give a beam experiment, parameter values, convergence norm, or acceptance criterion. We attempt the suggested rotational descent route and check two prerequisites. This is not a numerical beam simulation.

An explicit admissibility obstruction for additive slips

One individual shear has determinant one: in a basis beginning with $m,n$, the matrix $I+\gamma mn^T$ is triangular with diagonal all ones. This does not extend to a sum of shears. Take $m_1=e_1,n_1=e_2,m_2=e_2,n_2=e_1$. Then \[ F_p=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma_1&0\\\gamma_2&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det F_p=1-\gamma_1\gamma_2. \] The two prescribed systems satisfy every displayed orthogonality and unit condition, but $\gamma_1=\gamma_2=1$ gives a singular matrix. Consequently the displayed inverse in the energy is undefined there. If orientation preservation is required, the admissible region in this example is $\gamma_1\gamma_2<1$. The report's ansatz by itself does not justify unconstrained slip iterates. This diagnoses a prerequisite for a solver; it does not show that an admissible continuum formulation is inconsistent.

A feasible rotational update and its precise descent guarantee

For a real skew-symmetric $K$, set $R(h)=R\exp(-hK)$ with $R\in SO(3)$. The power series gives $\exp(-hK)^T=\exp(hK)$, and multiplication of the commuting series gives $\exp(hK)\exp(-hK)=I$. Thus $R(h)$ is orthogonal. Its determinant is a continuous function of $h$ valued in $\{-1,1\}$; at zero it is one, so it is always one. This update preserves the pointwise rotation constraint exactly.

Let $E$ be a twice continuously differentiable, finite-dimensional discretized energy on a neighborhood of the trial curve. On the vector space of skew matrices use the Frobenius inner product. There is a unique skew $K$ representing the differential by $DE(R)[RA]=\langle K,A\rangle$ for every skew $A$: this follows by expanding that linear functional in an orthonormal basis. Hence $e(h)=E(R\exp(-hK))$ satisfies $e'(0)=-\|K\|_F^2$. If, on $[0,h]$, the additional bound $|e''(s)|\le L\|K\|_F^2$ holds, integration twice gives \[ e(h)\le e(0)-h\|K\|_F^2+\tfrac12Lh^2\|K\|_F^2. \] For $K\ne0$ this is strict descent whenever $0<h<2/L$ (all positive $h$ within the interval if $L=0$). No uniform $L$ for the original continuum energy is supplied by this calculation. Multiple nodal rotations use the direct sum of these tangent spaces; prescribed boundary nodes must be held fixed. Finite-element interpolation and slip admissibility are separate requirements, not consequences of nodal feasibility.

Why feasible descent does not select the correct state.
On the circle subgroup of rotations about $e_3$, consider the illustrative smooth energy \[ E(\theta)=1-\cos(2\theta)+\delta(1-\cos\theta),\qquad 0<\delta<4. \] Both summands are nonnegative, and $E(0)=0$ is a global minimum. But $E'(\pi)=0$, $E''(\pi)=4-\delta>0$, and $E(\pi)=2\delta>0$. Continuity of the second derivative makes $\pi$ a strict local minimum on that subgroup, so a gradient method started there stays there. The energy is a polynomial in $\cos\theta=R_{11}$ on this subgroup. This is a complete toy obstruction to the inference from descent to global selection; it is not a claim about the actual beam energy's wells.

Model-assisted audit and remaining scope

The matrix set $SO(3)$ is compact: it is closed by its defining polynomial equalities and bounded because each column has unit length. The source's discussion of failure of compactness must therefore not be paraphrased as noncompactness of this matrix group. Euler-angle representatives can be unbounded even for a fixed rotation, since adding $2\pi k$ leaves that rotation unchanged; fields require additional functional compactness arguments. None is established here. The audit checked the inverse in the source energy, the distinction between one slip and multiple slips, the sign of the tangent derivative, and the local nature of the toy minimum. These elementary calculations are not claimed novel. No physical data, numerical experiment, PDE convergence proof, or theorem prover was used. The proposed descent mechanism still lacks a global or physically justified selection principle and a validated polygonization error criterion.
