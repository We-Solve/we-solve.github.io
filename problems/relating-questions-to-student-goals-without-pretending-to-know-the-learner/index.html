Attempt: how much goal elicitation can exact matching require?

The source does not define learner goals or a mapping from tasks to them. In a deliberately finite model, assume exactly one of $m\ge2$ declared goals applies, each requiring a different task framing. A learner can answer truthful binary membership questions about this set of goals. I tested whether exact individual matching can avoid elicitation altogether.
Proposition: Every deterministic procedure guaranteed to select the right framing for all $m$ goals requires at least $\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ binary answers in the worst case. This bound is achievable when arbitrary goal-subset queries are allowed.
Proof: A procedure using at most $h$ binary answers is a binary decision tree of depth at most $h$, with at most $2^h$ leaves. Two different goals cannot terminate at the same leaf, because the output framing there is fixed and correct for at most one of them. Thus $2^h\ge m$. For achievability, assign each goal a distinct binary string of length $\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ and ask, for each coordinate, whether the goal belongs to the subset whose string has a one in that coordinate. The answer string identifies the goal and hence its prescribed framing. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

Five distinct goals require at least three binary answers in this model; two answers have only four possible transcripts. The construction uses three questions and five of its eight answer strings. The obstruction to zero-query matching is information, not a demographic prediction rule. Actual goals can be multiple, changing, uncertain or poorly represented by the proposed menu; truthful arbitrary-subset questions may also be impractical. Neither their relevance to a mathematical question nor the benefit of a framing is established. The source's unverified reconstruction and broad instructional aim remain unresolved.
