Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record describes a collection of research questions about cancer-cell migration, rather than a single specified mathematical conjecture. Its terms identify modeling topics: microscopic and mesoscopic models describe migration at different levels of resolution; a macroscopic limit is a limiting description at a larger scale; and an inverse recovery problem seeks an unknown model quantity from observations. These descriptions do not specify equations, admissible models, observations, or limiting procedures.

The research tasks recorded under permanent UnsolvedMath ID $30002572$, source code catalogue entry, are:

  1. Describe physical cell passage through obstacles.
  2. Determine the mechanics of collective motion and develop models of that motion.
  3. Optimize the methods identified in the source as DTI and Q-ball methods.
  4. Establish benchmarks for movement models.
  5. Formulate and analyze the moving-boundary problem for acid-mediated invasion.
  6. Compare amoeboid, mesenchymal, and collective migration strategies, including their invasion speeds.
  7. Establish global bounds for chemotaxis--haptotaxis models with tissue remodeling.
  8. Recover chemotaxis sensitivity from observations through an inverse problem.
  9. Develop simple microscopic or mesoscopic models of collective motion, including leaderless motion, and determine their macroscopic limits.
