Witten Spinor Proofs on Nonspin Asymptotically Flat Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ denote a Riemannian manifold. A spin structure is a lift of its oriented orthonormal frame bundle through the double covering $\operatorname{Spin}(n)\to\operatorname{SO}(n)$, where $n=\dim M$. In the oriented setting, nonspin means that no such lift exists. The record does not explicitly specify orientability or dimension.

Asymptotic flatness means that the geometry approaches Euclidean geometry on the relevant ends; the precise decay, differentiability, and integrability conditions are not supplied. Write $m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(E)$ for the ADM mass of an end $E$, whenever the hypotheses needed to define that mass hold.

For the proposed approach, a singular spin structure is understood provisionally as a spin structure on $M\setminus\Sigma$, where $\Sigma\subset M$ is an exceptional set across which that structure need not extend. The record specifies neither the permitted sets $\Sigma$ nor the required behavior near them. On this spin complement, let $D_g$ denote the spin Dirac operator. A Witten spinor is a spinor $\psi$ satisfying \[ D_g\psi=0\qquad\text{on }M\setminus\Sigma, \] with prescribed asymptotically constant behavior on the relevant ends. The admissible function space and conditions at $\Sigma$ remain unspecified.

Source question. Can Witten's spinor proof of the positive mass theorem be adapted to asymptotically flat nonspin manifolds by using singular spin structures and Witten spinors?

The conventional positivity target is \[ m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(E)\geq 0 \] under suitable positive mass theorem hypotheses, including nonnegative scalar curvature. This describes the intended theorem's usual positivity conclusion, rather than asserting that the supplied record states all necessary hypotheses. A rigorous adaptation would need to justify the spinor argument in the presence of $\Sigma$, including any additional boundary or singular contributions. The record does not determine a fully quantified theorem: the manifold class, end conditions, singular-set conditions, and whether an equality-rigidity conclusion is required all remain unresolved.

Short English statement

The question asks whether Witten's proof of nonnegative mass can be extended to asymptotically flat manifolds without a global spin structure, using spin structures and harmonic spinors away from a singular set. The supplied record leaves essential geometric and analytic hypotheses unspecified, so this is a qualified research question requiring mathematical review.
