Mixed Square-Tiled-Surface Talk Transition

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $g\geq 2$, and let $\mu=(m_1,\ldots,m_n)$ be a nonempty positive integral partition of $2g-2$, so that $m_i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n m_i=2g-2$. Let $C$ be a smooth connected projective complex curve of genus $g$, and let $z_1,\ldots,z_n$ be distinct marked points. With the zeros labeled, the stratum of holomorphic differentials with signature $\mu$ consists of isomorphism classes of tuples \[ (C,\omega,z_1,\ldots,z_n),\qquad 0\neq\omega\in H^0(C,K_C),\qquad \operatorname{div}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^n m_i z_i. \] Denote this stratum by $\mathcal H(\mu)$. Its quotient by the scaling action $\omega\mapsto\lambda\omega$, for $\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^*$, is the projectivized stratum $\mathbb P\mathcal H(\mu)$. It parametrizes the corresponding pointed curves for which $\sum_i m_i z_i$ is a canonical divisor. This distinguishes the differential stratum from its projectivization; the supplied extraction uses $\mathcal H(\mu)$ for both descriptions and has corrupted scaling notation.

Recoverable proposal, with unresolved construction. The supplied passage proposes decomposing the stratum according to isomorphism classes of singularities associated with these pointed curves. It describes a $\mu$-weighted blowup at the marked points in a central fiber $C_0$ of an isotrivial family written as $C\times C$, followed by contraction of the proper transform of $C_0$. The passage describes the resulting $s$ as an isolated Gorenstein curve singularity with $n$ rational branches and a ${\mathbb{C}}^*$-action. These are supplied descriptions, not conclusions established here. The family base, blowup weights, contraction, and the precise meaning of the resulting curve singularity are insufficiently specified to define a map assigning $s$ to each pointed curve. Consequently, even the proposed decomposition is conditional on recovering that construction and its intended isomorphism relation.

For the one-point notation $(C,z)$ appearing in the passage, the Weierstrass semigroup can be defined as \[ H(C,z)=\{0\}\cup\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\text{there exists a meromorphic function on }C\text{ whose pole divisor is }kz\}. \] The passage attributes to Pinkham a relationship between monomial curve singularities and loci with prescribed Weierstrass semigroup determined by monomial parametrization exponents. It does not supply the hypotheses, exponents, or a precise theorem. It also suggests that certain deformation spaces might illuminate the topology of $\mathcal H(\mu)$, with a connection to the Kontsevich--Zorich $K(\pi,1)$ conjecture, but specifies neither the deformation spaces nor the intended version of that conjecture.
