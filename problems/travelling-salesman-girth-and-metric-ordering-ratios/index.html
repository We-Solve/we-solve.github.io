Travelling-Salesman Girth and Metric Ordering Ratios

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space. Its Assouad--Nagata dimension is the least integer $n\geq 0$, if one exists, for which there is a constant $c>0$ such that, for every $r>0$, there is a cover $\mathcal U_r$ of $M$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{diam}(U)\leq cr\quad(U\in\mathcal U_r), \] and every subset of $M$ of diameter at most $r$ meets at most $n+1$ members of $\mathcal U_r$. If no such integer exists, write $\dim_{\mathrm{AN}}M=\infty$. The space is uniformly discrete if there exists $\delta>0$ such that $d(x,y)\geq\delta$ for all distinct $x,y\in M$.

The supplied record asks the following two questions, with the second introduced there as a consequence of the first.

  1. For every metric space $M$, does \[ \dim_{\mathrm{AN}}M=\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the travelling-salesman girth of }M\text{ is infinite} \] hold?
  2. For every uniformly discrete metric space $M$, does at least one of the following alternatives hold? \[ \begin{aligned} &\text{For every order }T\text{ on }M,\quad \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\quad \operatorname{OR}_{M,T}(k)=k-1;\\ &\text{There exist an order }T\text{ on }M\text{ and a constant }C>0 \text{ such that}\quad \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\quad \operatorname{OR}_{M,T}(k)\leq C\ln k. \end{aligned} \] Here $C$ may depend on $M$ and the chosen order $T$, but not on $k$. The original statement uses $\ln$; the cleaned statement uses $\log$.

Unresolved source qualifications. These expressions therefore retain their source-specific meanings as unresolved notation. In particular, the displayed range includes $k=1$: the first alternative requires $\operatorname{OR}_{M,T}(1)=0$, and the second requires $\operatorname{OR}_{M,T}(1)\leq0$. A convention making these assertions meaningful is not supplied. An ordinary quotient of a prescribed visiting-path length by a shortest visiting-path length is at least $1$ when its denominator is positive, while singleton sets may give a zero denominator; consequently, no such quotient definition or correction of the index is silently adopted here. The source also gives no exceptions or conventions for empty, singleton, or other finite spaces. Short English statement

The source asks whether infinite Assouad--Nagata dimension forces infinite travelling-salesman girth, and whether every uniformly discrete metric space satisfies the stated alternative between maximal ordering ratios for every order and a logarithmic bound for some order. Definitions of girth and ordering ratio, including the convention at $k=1$, are missing, so this statement-only draft requires clarification and human mathematical review.
