Math-7534

Bunyakovsky Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a nonconstant integer polynomial $f$, a fixed prime divisor is a prime $p$ dividing $f(n)$ for every integer $n$. Suppose $f$ is irreducible over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, primitive, and has positive leading coefficient, with \[ \forall p\in\mathcal P\ \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\ p\nmid f(n). \] The assertion is that the set $\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:f(n)\in\mathcal P\}$ is infinite. Positive leading coefficient ensures that signs cannot obstruct eventual positive prime values. No explicit count or density is required.

Formulation and concept references: [S1].

Short English statement

Must every irreducible positive-leading integer polynomial without a fixed prime divisor produce infinitely many primes?
