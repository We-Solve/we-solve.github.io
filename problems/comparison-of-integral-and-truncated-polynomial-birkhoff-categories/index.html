Math-14280

Comparison of Integral and Truncated-Polynomial Birkhoff Categories

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a ring $R$, use the convention that $\operatorname{mod}R$ denotes the category of finitely generated $R$-modules. Let $Q$ be the quiver with vertices $1,2$ and a single arrow $1\to2$. Define $\operatorname{mono}(Q,\operatorname{mod}R)$ to have objects the injective $R$-linear maps $u\colon M_1\hookrightarrow M_2$. A morphism from $u\colon M_1\hookrightarrow M_2$ to $v\colon N_1\hookrightarrow N_2$ is a pair of $R$-linear maps $(a,b)$, where $a\colon M_1\to N_1$, $b\colon M_2\to N_2$, and $b\circ u=v\circ a$; composition is componentwise.

With the conventional interpretation that $p$ is a prime and $k$ is a field, put \[ \mathcal C_p=\operatorname{mono}\left(Q,\operatorname{mod}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^6{\mathbb{Z}})\right),\qquad \mathcal D_k=\operatorname{mono}\left(Q,\operatorname{mod}(k[x]/(x^6))\right). \] The supplied excerpt does not explicitly specify these parameter domains, the permitted fields $k$, any relation between $p$ and $k$, or whether the comparison is intended for all parameters or for particular choices. The finite-generation convention above also requires confirmation against the complete source.

Comparison problem. Compare the representation theory of $\mathcal C_p$ and $\mathcal D_k$. In the wording of the supplied original excerpt, is their structure similar? The cleaned registry statement asks whether they are equivalent in an appropriate representation-theoretic sense. Neither wording specifies a precise relation to be established.

The excerpt separately defines, attributing the definition to Auslander, a representation equivalence to be a full, dense functor $F$ that reflects isomorphisms. Here dense means that every target object is isomorphic to an object in the image of $F$, and reflection of isomorphisms means \[ F(f)\text{ is an isomorphism}\ \Longrightarrow\ f\text{ is an isomorphism}. \] This supplies a possible comparison notion, but the excerpt does not identify it as the required relation between $\mathcal C_p$ and $\mathcal D_k$, specify a direction for such a functor, or state whether ordinary categories or injectively stable categories are intended.

The surrounding discussion assumes an abelian category $\mathcal A$ with enough injectives. It describes $\operatorname{mono}(Q,\mathcal A)$ as an extension-closed subcategory of $\operatorname{rep}(Q,\mathcal A)$, equipped with its induced exact structure and enough injective objects. It distinguishes these exact-category injectives from injectives in the ambient representation category. An injectively stable category uses the quotient by morphisms factoring through the relevant injective objects, so that distinction matters if a stable comparison is intended. The supplied excerpt loses notation distinguishing stable categories and ends mid-sentence; its contextual theorem cannot therefore resolve the intended comparison target reliably.

Consequently, this is a statement-only draft of the supplied comparison problem. A uniquely quantified equivalence assertion requires clarification of the parameters, comparison relation, and categories to which it applies.

Short English statement

Compare categories of embeddings of finitely generated modules over ${\mathbb{Z}}/p^6{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $k[x]/(x^6)$. The source asks whether their representation-theoretic structures are similar. It does not specify the relationship between $p$ and $k$, the parameter quantifiers, or the precise meaning of similarity or equivalence. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
