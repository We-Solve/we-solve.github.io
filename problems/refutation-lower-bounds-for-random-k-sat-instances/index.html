Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Does a random $k$-SAT instance with, for example, $m=n^{0.4k}$ clauses lack a polynomial-size refutation with high probability?

Which assertion is being attempted

The report, printed pages 3074--3075, explicitly separates efficient refutation algorithms from the stronger possibility of short nondeterministic certificates. The extracted question omits a proof system and the range of $k$. For the following auxiliary results define the usual model: independently draw $m=\lceil n^{0.4k}\rceil$ clauses with replacement, choosing a $k$-element variable set uniformly and each of its $2^k$ sign patterns uniformly. Variables within a clause are distinct, and $1\le k\le n$. We first take fixed $k\ge3$, then deliberately examine a different growing-$k$ boundary to show why the missing quantifier matters.

Unsatisfiability follows from a first moment, not proof hardness

For each fixed truth assignment, a random clause is falsified with probability $2^{-k}$, independently between clause draws. If $Z$ is the number of satisfying assignments, linearity of expectation gives \[ \mathbb E Z=2^n(1-2^{-k})^m \le\exp(n\log2-m2^{-k}). \] The inequality $1-u\le e^{-u}$ follows, for example, from the nonnegative derivative of $e^{-u}-(1-u)$ on $u\ge0$. For fixed $k\ge3$ we have $0.4k>1$, so the exponent tends to minus infinity. Since $Z$ is a nonnegative integer, $\Pr(Z\ge1)\le\mathbb EZ\to0$. This completely proves unsatisfiability with high probability in the stated auxiliary model. It gives no lower bound at all on the size of a proof of that fact.

A concrete short-refutation search usually fails

One very small contradiction consists of all $2^k$ possible sign clauses on a single $k$-element variable set. Put $C=2^k\binom nk$, the number of possible clauses. For a specified variable set, if every one of its sign clauses appears then there are distinct draw positions witnessing them. A union bound over these positions bounds its probability by $(m)_{2^k}/C^{2^k}\le(m/C)^{2^k}$. Thus the expected number of such complete cubes is at most \[ \binom nk\left(\frac{m}{2^k\binom nk}\right)^{2^k} =O_k\!\left(n^{\,k-0.6k2^k}\right)=o(1). \] For fixed $k$, $\binom nk$ is bounded above and below by positive $k$-dependent multiples of $n^k$, and $m=O_k(n^{0.4k})$, justifying the estimate. Markov's inequality shows that this particular short certificate is absent with high probability. The attempted extension "therefore every polynomial certificate is absent" has no basis: other refutations may combine clauses on many overlapping variable sets and need not contain this cube. Neither the first moment nor this certificate-specific calculation controls such proofs.

If k may equal n, polynomial refutations do occur

Now take $k=n$. There are $2^n$ possible clauses and each occurs with probability $2^{-n}$ at a draw. By a union bound, \[ \Pr(\text{some clause missing}) \le 2^n(1-2^{-n})^m \le\exp(n\log2-m/2^n)\longrightarrow0, \] because $m=\lceil n^{0.4n}\rceil$ and $\log(m/2^n)=0.4n\log n-n\log2\to\infty$ faster than $\log n$. When all clauses occur, pair clauses with the same first $n-1$ literals and opposite last literal and resolve that last variable. This produces every sign clause on the first $n-1$ variables. Repeat until the empty clause appears. There are $2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}+\cdots+1=2^n-1$ resolution inferences, each involving at most $n$ literals. Including the selected input clauses and their input indices uses $O(2^n(n+\log m))$ symbols/bits up to the ordinary variable-encoding factor. This is polynomial in the explicit input length, which is at least $m$, since eventually $2^n\le m$ and $n+\log m$ is itself polynomial in $m$.

This is a full counterexample to an unrestricted claim simultaneously covering all growing $k\le n$ under this sampling convention. It does not refute the intended fixed- or slowly growing-$k$ conjecture; the source does not supply the quantifier needed to identify the two. It illustrates that polynomial means polynomial in encoded formula length, not necessarily in the number of variables alone.

The proof-system gap remains substantial

If "no polynomial-size refutation" ranges over all polynomial-time verifiable sound certificate systems, a general proof would entail a major complexity separation. Indeed if $NP=coNP$, the language UNSAT belongs to NP, so there is one polynomial-time verifier and a polynomial bound on certificate length for every unsatisfiable formula. In the fixed-$k\ge3$ model just proved unsatisfiable with high probability, that system supplies polynomial certificates with high probability. This is incompatible with a universal lower bound over such systems. The implication uses the definition of NP and makes no claim that $NP=coNP$ holds. Restricting to resolution or sum of squares is a different target and must be stated explicitly.
