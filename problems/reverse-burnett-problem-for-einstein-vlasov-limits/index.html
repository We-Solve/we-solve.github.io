Reverse Burnett Problem for Einstein–Vlasov Limits

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A massless Vlasov field is a distribution on the null particle phase space transported along null geodesics; its momentum moments give the stress tensor sourcing Einstein's equations. The reverse Burnett problem asks whether this effective matter can arise from oscillatory vacuum geometry. Strong $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence of metrics is convergence on every compact coordinate region in square integral; an $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ bound on first derivatives permits oscillations that survive quadratically in the weak limit.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Given a solution $g_0$ of the Einstein equations coupled to a massless Vlasov field, can one construct vacuum Einstein metrics $g_\lambda$ converging to $g_0$ in a high-frequency weak limit with $g_\lambda\to g_0$ strongly in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and $\partial g_\lambda$ bounded in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$?
Source scope and qualification.
The convergence gauge, local existence region and source regularity conditions are indispensable. The requested high-frequency family must solve the vacuum equations, not merely reproduce the effective stress tensor in a formal expansion.

Short English statement

Can every indicated Einstein--massless-Vlasov spacetime be approximated by high-frequency vacuum Einstein geometries?

A single-phase weak-limit calculation.
In a fixed coordinate region let $k\ne0$ be a constant covector and $A$ a smooth compactly supported real function. Define $u_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon A(x)\cos(k\cdot x/\varepsilon)$. Then $u_\varepsilon\to0$ strongly in local $L^2$, while \[ \partial_\mu u_\varepsilon=-A k_\mu\sin(k\cdot x/\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon),\qquad \partial_\mu u_\varepsilon\partial_\nu u_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup\tfrac12 A^2k_\mu k_\nu. \] To justify the limit against a compact smooth test, use $\sin^2s=(1-\cos2s)/2$; integrate the oscillatory term by parts in any direction with nonzero component of $k$. The resulting integral is $O(\varepsilon)$. The derivative error is uniformly $O(\varepsilon)$ on the support.
Stress and missing geometry.
If $k$ is null for a constant Lorentz metric, the limiting tensor has zero trace and a single null direction, as a null-dust contribution should. Finite sums with nonresonant distinct phases can create sums of such tensors, but this scalar calculation supplies no transverse gravitational polarization, Einstein constraints or nonlinear error correction. A general massless Vlasov distribution may require a continuum of directions, not one phase.
