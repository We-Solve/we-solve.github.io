Attempt: choosing support packages under a fixed resource budget

The source supplies no training packages or measured benefits. I tested a limited selection model: package $i$ has a known nonnegative additive benefit $v_i$ and positive integer cost $c_i$, it may be purchased at most once, and total cost cannot exceed integer budget $B$. The tempting route is to rank packages by benefit per unit cost.
Proposition: The simple ratio-greedy rule can achieve an arbitrarily small fraction of optimal benefit. A dynamic program computes the exact optimum in $O(nB)$ arithmetic/comparison operations for $n$ packages.
Proof: For any integer $B>2$, offer two packages: $(c,v)=(B,B)$ and $(1,2)$. The second has ratio two, larger than the first's ratio one, so ratio-greedy selects it. The remaining budget $B-1$ cannot buy the first package; greedy benefit is two, whereas the optimum buys the first for benefit $B$. The ratio $2/B$ tends to zero.

Let $D_i(b)$ be the largest benefit obtainable from the first $i$ packages within budget $b$. Initialize $D_0(b)=0$. If $c_i>b$, set $D_i(b)=D_{i-1}(b)$; otherwise set \[ D_i(b)=\max\{D_{i-1}(b),\ v_i+D_{i-1}(b-c_i)\}. \] Every feasible selection either omits package $i$ or includes it and leaves a feasible selection of earlier packages at the reduced budget. Both alternatives in the recurrence are attainable, so induction on $i$ proves equality with the optimum. There are $(n+1)(B+1)$ states and constant work per transition. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At budget 100 the example gives greedy benefit two and optimal benefit 100, reproduced by the recurrence. The running time is pseudopolynomial because $B$ may have only $O(\log B)$ input bits. Additivity, known benefits, costs and indivisible purchases are modeling choices; real training and ongoing support can interact or affect different outcomes. The algorithm does not identify what support instructors need and does not validate the record's unverified reconstruction.
