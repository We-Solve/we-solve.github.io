The Converse Unitary Distinction Criterion

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for a converse to a unitary distinction criterion. The following formulation uses the customary ambient interpretation, which is not fully specified in the record. Let $p,q$ be signature parameters, put $n=p+q$, and set \[ G=\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad J_{p,q}=\operatorname{diag}(I_p,-I_q),\qquad H=U(p,q)=\{g\in G:\overline g^{\,t}J_{p,q}g=J_{p,q}\}. \] Here signature parameters are conventionally nonnegative integers; the supplied statement does not specify whether zero entries or rank zero are included.

Let $\boldsymbol\chi=(\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_n)$ be characters of ${\mathbb{C}}^\times$ forming Langlands standard data, and let $\pi$ be the corresponding irreducible Langlands quotient. Conventionally, this means the unique irreducible quotient of the normalized principal series induced from these characters, placed in Langlands order, from the upper triangular Borel subgroup of $G$. The precise character hypotheses and representation category are not supplied.

Write $\pi^c(g)=\pi(\overline g)$. Interpret conjugation invariance as $\pi\simeq\pi^c$, and interpret $H$-distinction as \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{H}(\pi,\mathbf 1)\ne\{0\}, \] where $\mathbf 1$ is the trivial representation and the continuity requirements for the invariant functional must be fixed by the intended representation category.

For a character $\chi$, write $\chi^c(z)=\chi(\overline z)$. Under the interpretation that conjugation acts on the Langlands character multiset, let $m(\chi)$ denote its multiplicity and let $S$ be its support. When this multiset is conjugation invariant, two possible readings of the orbit count are \[ k_{\mathrm{distinct}}=\#\{\{\chi,\chi^c\}:\chi\in S,\ \chi\ne\chi^c\}, \qquad k_{\mathrm{mult}}=\frac12\sum_{\substack{\chi\in S\\\chi\ne\chi^c}}m(\chi). \] The first counts distinct two-element character orbits; the second counts conjugate pairs with multiplicity. The supplied record does not identify the underlying orbit set or settle this multiplicity convention. Denote its intended count by $k_2(\boldsymbol\chi)$.

Requested converse, subject to the unresolved conventions above. For every permitted signature $(p,q)$ and every Langlands quotient $\pi$ of the indicated characters, does \[ \left(\pi\simeq\pi^c\ \text{ and }\ k_2(\boldsymbol\chi)\leq\min(p,q)\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Hom}_{U(p,q)}(\pi,\mathbf 1)\ne\{0\} \] hold without assuming that $\pi$ is generic? Here generic conventionally means admitting a nonzero Whittaker functional for a nondegenerate character of the maximal upper unipotent subgroup. The supplied source asserts the reverse implication as a necessary condition and presents the displayed converse as the conjecture. A fully unambiguous statement requires confirmation of the ambient conventions, admissible signatures, and orbit-count definition; the two counts displayed above are alternatives, not two separately supplied conjectures.

Short English statement

The question asks whether conjugation invariance and a bound of $\min(p,q)$ on the number of conjugate pairs in the Langlands character data guarantee a nonzero $U(p,q)$-invariant functional for every such Langlands quotient, including nongeneric ones. The precise orbit-count convention and representation setting need clarification. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
