Comparative Prime Number Theory — Problem 21

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer modulus $q$, an integer residue class $a\pmod q$, and a real number $t\geq 0$, define \[ \pi(t;q,a)=\#\{p\leq t:p\text{ is prime and }p\equiv a\pmod q\}, \qquad \phi(q)=\#(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times. \] Also let $\pi(t)$ denote the number of primes at most $t$. The source does not explicitly specify the range of $q$ or whether $a$ must satisfy $\gcd(a,q)=1$. The definitions above make sense for every residue class, but the intended domain of the requested classification remains to be confirmed.

Write $\operatorname{li}(t)$ for the logarithmic integral. For a precise conventional interpretation, take \[ \operatorname{li}(t)=\operatorname{PV}\int_0^t\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}} u}{\log u} \qquad(t>0,\ t\ne1), \] where the principal value is required when $t>1$. The source does not state this normalization explicitly. Integrals involving $\operatorname{li}(t)$ across $t=1$ are interpreted as improper integrals; its value at that single point does not affect them. Define \[ \mathfrak A_1^\pi(x;q,a) =\int_0^x\left(\phi(q)\pi(t;q,a)-\operatorname{li}(t)\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t \qquad(x>0). \] Assume the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH), meaning that every nontrivial zero of every Dirichlet $L$-function has real part $1/2$. The requested problem is to classify the intended pairs $(q,a)$ for which \[ \exists X=X(q,a)>2\quad\forall x\in\mathbb R, \qquad x\geq X\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathfrak A_1^\pi(x;q,a)<0. \] In particular, whether this classification is restricted to reduced residue classes is an unresolved source qualification.

For context, the supplied record attributes to $[\texttt{johnston2023average},\ \text{Theorem }1.1]$ the equivalence of the Riemann hypothesis (RH) with \[ \mathfrak A_1^\pi(x) :=\int_0^x\left(\pi(t)-\operatorname{li}(t)\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t<0 \qquad\text{for every real }x>2. \] This is a supplied theorem attribution, not an independently verified result here. The source informally explains the negativity under RH by comparing the bias with the explicit-formula contribution involving $\sum_{\rho}1/(\rho(\rho+1))$, where $\rho$ ranges over nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, counted with multiplicity. It attributes to Martin, in private communication, the observation that the analogous sum over zeros of Dirichlet $L$-functions grows faster than the bias as $q$ increases, motivating an expectation that negativity should fail for large moduli. This motivation supplies neither a precise threshold nor quantified conditions on $a$, and is not asserted here as a theorem.

Short English statement

Assuming GRH, determine which moduli and residue classes make the integral of the scaled prime-counting error strictly negative for every sufficiently large real $x$. The source leaves the intended residue-class domain and logarithmic-integral convention implicit. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
