Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns cohomological Hall algebras, denoted by $A_{Q,W}$, associated with a quiver $Q$ and a potential $W$. It does not specify the construction or conventions for these algebras. Its recoverable question is the following schematic existence problem: for the intended reference pair $(\widetilde Q,\widetilde W)$, does there exist a quiver $Q'$ with potential $W'$ such that \[ A_{Q',W'}\text{ ``partially fermionizes'' }A_{\widetilde Q,\widetilde W}? \] The reference pair and the phrase "partially fermionizes" are not defined in the supplied record. In particular, no required relation between the two algebras, their gradings, or their BPS cohomology is specified. Consequently, this expression cannot yet be made into a precise mathematical predicate.

The extracted background supplies the following intended motivation, with essential geometric data missing. For every $\alpha\in\mathfrak h$, including $\alpha=0$, it refers to a Cartesian construction of a smooth threefold $Y^\alpha$. The diagram defining this construction is corrupted. For a vertex $i\in\Gamma_0$, it states that a corresponding curve $C_i$ deforms along $\mathbb A^1$ if and only if $\alpha\in i^\perp$. Here $\Gamma$, $\mathfrak h$, and the hyperplanes $i^\perp$ are not defined in the excerpt. Writing $M_{i,n}$ for the moduli space of semistable coherent sheaves of the class denoted by $\mathcal O_{C_i}(n)$, the source reports \[ M_{i,n}\cong \begin{cases} \mathbb A^1,&\alpha\in i^\perp,\\ \mathrm{pt},&\alpha\notin i^\perp. \end{cases} \] The range of $n$, the meaning of the class, the stability condition, and the ambient moduli problem are not supplied. The source describes these moduli spaces as smooth and simply connected and writes their BPS cohomology as \[ \mathfrak g^{\mathrm{BPS}}_{i,n} =H\!\left(M_{i,n},\mathbb Q[\dim(M_{i,n})]\right)[-1]. \] It reports that this cohomology is one-dimensional, with parity depending on which hyperplanes $\alpha$ avoids. It also cautions that BPS cohomology for a specified class in a specified $3$-Calabi--Yau category can in general be difficult to calculate or even define. The displayed formula is therefore retained as source-specific background, without extending it to arbitrary such categories.

For its connection with the noncommutative conifold, the source specializes to $G=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, states $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $\dim\mathfrak h=1$, and refers to the extended quiver associated with $\Gamma$. It reports a derived equivalence between $\operatorname{Coh}(Y^0)$ and the category of $\Pi_Q[\omega]$-modules, where $Y^0$ is the specialization at $\alpha=0$. The intended derived categories and the algebra $\Pi_Q[\omega]$ are not defined. It further states that the BPS cohomology for $(\widetilde Q,\widetilde W)$ is entirely even. The sentence concerning every $\alpha\ne0$ and the resolved conifold is truncated, so its full conclusion cannot be recovered.

Short English statement

The recoverable question asks whether a quiver with potential can produce a cohomological Hall algebra that "partially fermionizes" the intended reference algebra. The meaning of this operation and the reference pair are missing. The registry identifies this corrupted, truncated record as a duplicate of OWR-10252931-001, so a precise independent statement cannot be recovered from the supplied evidence.
