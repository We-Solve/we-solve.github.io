Math-14412

Planar Regions Avoiding Prescribed Interior Lattice Point Counts

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal R$ denote the intended class of planar regions; the supplied record does not define this class. Write $\operatorname{Area}(R)$ for planar area, $\operatorname{int}(R)$ for the topological interior of $R\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$, and ${\mathbb{Z}}^2=\{(a,b):a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}$ for the standard integer lattice. For any set $S$, let $\#S$ denote its cardinality, which may be infinite. Boundary lattice points are excluded from the count.

Under the usual interpretation of congruence as an arbitrary Euclidean isometry, a congruent copy of $R$ has the form $QR+t$, where $Q\in O(2)=\{Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times2}:Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I_2\}$ and $t\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$. Thus reflections as well as rotations and translations are included.

The supplied question asks whether \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad \exists R\in\mathcal R\quad \left[\operatorname{Area}(R)=n\ \land\ \forall Q\in O(2)\ \forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}^2,\quad \#\left(\operatorname{int}(QR+t)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^2\right)\ne n\right]. \] Here each region may depend on $n$. The source does not specify whether $0\in{\mathbb{N}}$, or whether regions must be measurable, bounded, connected, open, closed, or satisfy boundary regularity conditions. Consequently, $\mathcal R$ and the meaning of area remain unresolved qualifications of this statement. If area is interpreted as Lebesgue measure, measurability must be specified. The standard lattice and the full Euclidean congruence group are conventional interpretations rather than explicitly supplied definitions.

Short English statement

For every natural number $n$, can one find a planar region of area $n$ such that every congruent placement has an interior lattice-point count different from $n$? Points on the boundary do not count. The admissible meaning of planar region and the convention concerning $n=0$ require clarification.
