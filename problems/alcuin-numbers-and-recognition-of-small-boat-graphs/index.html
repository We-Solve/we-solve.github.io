Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For a perfect graph, is there a polynomial-time algorithm for computing its Alcuin number? Is recognizing small-boat graphs in NP?

Source convention and attempted certificate

The report's capacity $b\ge1$ counts transported graph vertices; the operator does not consume one of those $b$ places. During each crossing, both banks must be independent sets, since the operator is in the boat. A small-boat graph has $\operatorname{Alcuin}(G)=\tau(G)$, where $\tau$ is the minimum vertex-cover size. We test whether an ordinary crossing certificate plus an explicitly supplied vertex cover proves this equality.
Complete standard two-value bound.
On the first trip the vertices left behind must be independent. Its boat load is consequently a vertex cover, proving every feasible capacity is at least $\tau(G)$. Conversely, keep a minimum vertex cover $W$ in the boat throughout, and transport vertices of the independent set $V\setminus W$ one at a time in one extra place, returning with $W$ between trips. Both banks always contain only vertices of that independent set, so the schedule is feasible. At the end all vertices, including the boat load $W$, are across. If the independent set is empty, simply take $W$ across once. Thus for a nonempty graph \[ \tau(G)\le\operatorname{Alcuin}(G)\le\tau(G)+1. \] For an edgeless nonempty graph, $\tau=0$ and the convention $b\ge1$ gives Alcuin number one by the same one-at-a-time schedule. The empty input can be handled separately according to whether its boat capacity is required to remain at least one; it does not affect the questions below.
Perfect graphs with the same cover number need different capacities.
For the path $a-v-b$, the seven successive boat loads $v,\varnothing,a,v,b,\varnothing,v$ (alternating directions) give a capacity-one schedule. Each unattended bank is an independent set; explicitly the central vertex is brought back before being left with a leaf. The first-trip bound gives equality $\operatorname{Alcuin}=\tau=1$. For the star with center $v$ and three leaves, $\tau=1$ but capacity one is impossible. Suppose there were a first trip that leaves at least two leaves on the right after arrival. It transports one leaf, so immediately before departure exactly one leaf is right and one other leaf remains left. The center cannot be in the boat together with the moving leaf. If it is on the left, the left bank contains the center and the remaining leaf during crossing; if it is on the right, the right bank contains the center and the already transported leaf. Both violate safety. No such first trip exists, contradicting final arrival of all leaves. The cover-plus-one construction gives capacity two, so the star is a big-boat graph. Both graphs are perfect: every induced subgraph is a forest, is two-colorable by distance parity when it has an edge, and has maximum clique size two; an edgeless induced subgraph needs one color and has clique size one (the empty subgraph uses zero). Thus knowing a perfect graph's cover number alone does not determine which of the two capacities occurs.
Why a cover plus a short schedule is not a small-boat certificate.
A supplied cover of size $b$ proves $\tau\le b$; a valid capacity-$b$ schedule proves $\operatorname{Alcuin}\le b$. These are two upper bounds. For the three-leaf star, choose the size-two cover consisting of its center and one leaf, and use a capacity-two schedule. Both certificates are valid, but $\tau=1<2=\operatorname{Alcuin}$. Hence the proposed inference of small-boat status is false, even on a perfect graph. A matching of $b$ disjoint edges would repair this particular certificate: every cover needs a different vertex for each matching edge, so $\tau\ge b$; together with a capacity-$b$ schedule and the first-trip lower bound, all three numbers equal $b$. This is a sound sufficient certificate but is incomplete. For a triangle, $\tau=2$ (every two vertices cover it, and one does not) and a capacity-two schedule with loads $\{a,b\},\{a\},\{a,c\}$ is feasible. Thus the triangle is small-boat, while its maximum matching has only one edge. It too is perfect, since all its induced subgraphs are cliques. A matching lower bound cannot certify all desired instances even in the perfect class.
An exact exponential search does not settle NP membership.
For $n$ vertices, one can enumerate every partition $(L,B,R)$ with independent banks and $|B|\le b$, together with a direction bit. There are at most $2\cdot3^n$ states. Connect two states precisely when the boat arrives on the proper bank and its contents can be repartitioned there, leaving the opposite bank unchanged, as in source condition (FS3). Start with right bank empty and finish with left bank empty after a forward trip. A graph-reachability search accepts exactly feasible crossing schedules, because every edge is one legal successive trip and every schedule yields such a path. A shortest state path repeats no state, so this is a finite exponential procedure. It provides neither a polynomial search nor a polynomial certificate for the equality with an unknown optimum cover size. The source in fact already gives a separate polynomial-size certificate for bounded Alcuin number; its unresolved recognition issue is the lower bound on the cover number, not merely writing a route.
