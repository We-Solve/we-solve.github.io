Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 1.4

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

For a nonconstant meromorphic function $f$ on $\mathbb C$, let $n(r,a,f)$ count the solutions of $f(z)=a$ in $|z|\le r$, with multiplicity; $a=\infty$ counts poles. Let $\bar n$ count each point once. The integrated count is \[ N(r,a,f)=n(0,a,f)\log r+\sum_{0<|z|\le r,\ f(z)=a}\log(r/|z|), \] where the sum repeats each point according to multiplicity; $\bar N$ has the same formula without repetitions. Write $\log^+x=\max(\log x,0)$ and \[ m(r,\infty,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|f(re^{it})|\,dt,\quad m(r,a,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+\frac1{|f(re^{it})-a|}\,dt\quad(a\in\mathbb C). \] The Nevanlinna characteristic is $T(r,f)=m(r,\infty,f)+N(r,\infty,f)$. Its order and lower order are \[ \rho(f)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log T(r,f)}{\log r},\qquad \lambda(f)=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log T(r,f)}{\log r}. \] The deficiency is $\delta(a,f)=1-\limsup_{r\to\infty}N(r,a,f)/T(r,f)$, and the reduced-count index is $\Theta(a,f)=1-\limsup_{r\to\infty}\bar N(r,a,f)/T(r,f)$. A value is deficient if $\delta(a,f)>0$. An entire function is holomorphic on all of $\mathbb C$; for an entire function, $M(r,f)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|$. Let $n_1(r,a,f)$ count only the simple zeros of $f-a$ in $|z|\le r$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every entire function of finite order $\rho$, does the existence of distinct $a,b\in\mathbb C$ and $c<\rho$ with \[ n_1(r,a,f)=O(r^c),\qquad n_1(r,b,f)=O(r^c)\quad(r\to\infty) \] imply $2\rho\in\mathbb Z$? The source separately asks the stronger proposed assertion: does $\Theta(a,f)=\Theta(b,f)=1/2$ for two distinct finite values imply $2\rho\in\mathbb Z$? [S2]

Short English statement

Must the order be a half-integer under either of the two proposed restrictions on two value counts? The 2018 source reports a counterexample to the simple-zero assertion. [S2]
