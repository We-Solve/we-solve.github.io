Question 4.10 — Let Γ be a lattice in SO(m, 1), m≥ 3 or SU(q, 1), q≥ 2 which is admissable for Γg.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a lattice in $SO(m,1)$, $m\geq3$, or $SU(q,1)$, $q\geq2$, satisfying the source's condition of being "admissible for $\Gamma_g$." A lattice is discrete of finite covolume; no cocompactness, arithmeticity or torsion-free assumption appears in this statement. The admissibility condition and exact target $\Gamma_g$, apparently the surface mapping class group of the surrounding chapter, are not defined in the excerpt.

The compound questions are whether there is an injection \[\iota:\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma_g,\] whether purely pseudo-Anosov representations can occur, and, for such an injection, whether there is a continuous map \[F:\partial_\infty\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb P\mathcal{ML}_0(\Sigma)\] with \[F(\gamma\xi)=\iota(\gamma)F(\xi)\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma,\ \xi\in\partial_\infty\Gamma).\] The source does not require $F$ to be injective or a boundary embedding. It does not say whether a purity requirement is on all nonidentity elements or only infinite-order elements, nor whether the second question allows noninjective representations; these conventions matter in the presence of torsion or kernel.

The boundary and target also need definition. For a cocompact rank-one lattice the Gromov boundary is naturally related to the ambient symmetric-space visual boundary. A noncocompact higher-dimensional rank-one lattice need not be word hyperbolic, so $\partial_\infty\Gamma$ cannot automatically be interpreted as its ordinary Gromov boundary; an ambient visual/relative-boundary convention may be intended. No cocompactness restriction is silently inserted to resolve this. Projective measured lamination space consists of nonzero measured laminations up to positive scaling, but the subscript-zero subset in $\mathbb P\mathcal{ML}_0(\Sigma)$ is absent from the definitions and is not replaced by all projective laminations.

The final sentence mentions fixing $\Sigma$, hence virtual cohomological dimension, and announces a further generalization; that subsequent problem is missing. Admissibility is not invented from an assumed dimension inequality, and the quantifier over allowable injections in the boundary-map request requires confirmation.

Short English statement

Ask whether admissible real- or complex-hyperbolic lattices embed in the specified mapping class group, whether purely pseudo-Anosov representations occur, and whether such embeddings admit continuous equivariant boundary maps. The admissibility, boundary and target-subset definitions are missing.
