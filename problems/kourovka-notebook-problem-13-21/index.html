Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.21

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. A $p$-group here means a group in which every element has finite order equal to a power of $p$. A group $G$ is residually finite if, for every $g\ne 1$, there is a homomorphism from $G$ to a finite group whose image of $g$ is nonidentity. Let $\mathcal C_p$ denote the class of infinite finitely generated residually finite $p$-groups.

For $G\in\mathcal C_p$, fix a finite generating set $S$. With the usual convention of allowing generators and their inverses, define \[ \delta_S(g)=\min\{k\geq 0:g=s_1\cdots s_k,\ s_i\in S\cup S^{-1}\}, \qquad \operatorname{ord}_G(g)=\min\{m\geq 1:g^m=1\}. \] For integers $n\geq 0$, put \[ \pi_{G,S}(n)=\max\{\operatorname{ord}_G(g):g\in G,\ \delta_S(g)\leq n\}. \] This maximum exists because each word ball is finite. The notation $|g|$ in the source denotes $\operatorname{ord}_G(g)$.

Part (b), the remaining question in the supplied record. What is the minimal possible asymptotic rate of growth of $\pi_{G,S}(n)$ for the class of groups indicated in part (a)? The underlying group hypotheses in that part are $G\in\mathcal C_p$. However, the reference to that class does not explicitly resolve whether the additional subpower bound asked for in part (a) is itself a restriction in part (b). The source also leaves the comparison convention for growth rates and the quantification over $p$ unspecified; no precise extremal ordering or assertion of an attained minimum is imposed here.

Part (a), retained as archived context. Does there exist $G\in\mathcal C_p$, a finite generating set $S$, and a function $f:\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\to[1,\infty)$ such that \[ \forall g\in G,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_G(g)\leq f(\delta_S(g)), \qquad \forall\lambda>0,\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^\lambda}=0? \] The domain and value at zero merely make the bound include the identity. This is the subpower interpretation of the source's phrase that $f$ grows more slowly than every positive power; no monotonicity condition is supplied. The source does not specify whether $p$ is prescribed or may be chosen.

The supplied archive reports an affirmative answer to part (a), attributed to A. V. Rozhkov. As background to part (b), the source attributes to R. I. Grigorchuk the existence of $p$-groups with a bound $\pi(n)\leq n^\lambda$ for some $\lambda>0$, and states that a result of E. I. Zel'manov implies that $\pi(n)$ is unbounded when $G$ is infinite in the indicated setting. These are supplied historical assertions, not independently verified results in this draft.

Short English statement

For an infinite finitely generated residually finite $p$-group, measure how large an element order can occur among words of length at most $n$. Part (b) asks how slowly this maximum can grow. The supplied archive reports that part (a), asking for growth below every positive power, was answered affirmatively. The precise growth comparison, prime quantification, and scope of the class in part (b) require clarification.
