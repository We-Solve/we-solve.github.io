Local noninteracting ensemble representation of Coulomb ground densities

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A three-electron two-nucleus molecule is claimed to have a density outside all noninteracting ground-state ensembles for a single real spin-independent local Kohn--Sham potential in the stated class.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Set $D=10^{200}$, $e_z=(0,0,1)$, and take the source's finitely specified positive integer charge $Z$. For the three-electron Coulomb molecule \[ H_C=\sum_{i=1}^3\left(-\tfrac12\Delta_i- \frac Z{|r_i-(D/Z)e_z|}-\frac Z{|r_i+(D/Z)e_z|}\right) +\sum_{i<j}|r_i-r_j|^{-1}, \] the source claims a normalized absolute ground state $\Psi$ in the $S_z=1/2$ sector. For every real scalar local potential $v\in L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, the noninteracting Hamiltonian $H_s(v)=\sum_i(-\Delta_i/2+v(r_i))$ has no trace-one ground-space ensemble $\Gamma$ with \[ n_\Gamma=n_\Psi\quad\text{almost everywhere}. \] The densities are spin-summed, with integral three, and the same scalar potential acts on both spin components.

Outline of the argument

A controlled small-coupling Coulomb limit isolates the two lowest spatial orbitals. Density and occupation constraints forced by a hypothetical noninteracting representation conflict with the interacting ground-state structure; exact finite charge selection makes the obstruction a genuine finite molecule.
