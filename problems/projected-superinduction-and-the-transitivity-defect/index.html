Projected superinduction and the transitivity defect

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 1.7. Define superinduction and restriction beyond algebra groups. The source suggests automorphism-induced theories and characteristic subgroups, and Johnson--Smith's association-scheme induction work. It does not require a particular compatibility or character-preservation axiom. The aid distinguishes a known coherent-theory extension from a canonical projected construction for arbitrary theories.

For a finite group $G$ with a supercharacter theory, let $\operatorname{SCF}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{CF}(G)$ be its superclass-function space, with the normalized character inner product, and let $P_G$ be orthogonal projection. Equivalently, $P_G$ averages over each superclass. For arbitrary theories on $H\leq G$, define \[ \operatorname{SRes}_H^G=P_H\operatorname{Res}_H^G, \qquad \operatorname{SInd}_H^G=P_G\operatorname{Ind}_H^G \] on the corresponding superclass spaces. The aid reports that these are adjoint. If $K_g$ is the superclass of $g$, the projected induction is exactly \[ \operatorname{SInd}_H^G f(g)= \frac{|G|}{|H|\,|K_g|}\sum_{h\in K_g\cap H}f(h). \] This construction always gives superclass functions, not automatically ordinary characters or a transitive functorial theory.

Reported positivity and integrality distinction. If $X$ is a block of irreducible characters, use the canonical supercharacter $\sigma_X=\sum_{\chi\in X}\chi(1)\chi$. For an ordinary character $\psi=\sum_\chi m_\chi\chi$, its projection is \[ P_G\psi=\sum_Xc_X\sigma_X,\qquad c_X=\frac{\sum_{\chi\in X}m_\chi\chi(1)} {\sum_{\chi\in X}\chi(1)^2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq0}. \] It remains an ordinary character precisely when $c_X\chi(1)$ is an integer for every $X$ and $\chi\in X$. Nonnegative rational supercharacter coefficients alone do not establish integrality.

Reported complete transitivity defect. For theories on $K\leq H\leq G$, the direct restriction minus its two-step projected version is, on $\operatorname{SCF}(G)$, \[ D_{K,H,G}=P_K\operatorname{Res}_K^H(I-P_H) \operatorname{Res}_H^G. \] Thus projected restriction is transitive for this triple exactly when this restricted operator vanishes. By adjunction, the corresponding induction defect is its adjoint, so induction has the same criterion. The domain restriction matters: vanishing on the relevant superclass space, not on every arbitrary class function, is required. The aid reports an explicit nonzero example for $C_8\supset C_4\supset C_2$, but does not supply the theories or test function in this summary; those data are not invented here.

Reported automorphism-compatible case. Let $\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, take $\operatorname{SCF}(G)=\operatorname{CF}(G)^\Gamma$, and give a $\Gamma$-invariant subgroup $H$ the theory induced by the restricted action. Ordinary restriction and induction preserve these invariant class-function spaces, so both projections act trivially on their outputs and the projected maps reduce to the ordinary ones. Characteristic subgroups satisfy this invariance requirement. A chain with compatible restricted actions is therefore transitive; unrelated automorphism choices are not covered.

The aid attributes an earlier generalization of superinduction for coherent supercharacter theories on arbitrary finite groups to de Stavola. Exact coherence hypotheses and a full citation are absent. The projected construction answers a flexible linear-algebraic version, while character preservation, categorical realization and stronger compatibility requirements need separate hypotheses. All reported statements require independent verification.

Short English statement

Superclass averaging gives a canonical adjoint superrestriction/superinduction pair for arbitrary finite-group theories. It may lose character integrality and transitivity; the aid gives an exact leakage operator detecting the latter. Compatible automorphism-induced theories on invariant subgroups recover ordinary induction and restriction.
