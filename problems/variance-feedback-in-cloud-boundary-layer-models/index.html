Can deterministic feedback create variance from zero?
Test a two-variable fluctuation model $\dot\xi=A(t)\xi$, where $A$ is a continuous real $2\times2$ matrix that may depend on deterministic mean fields. With mean-zero random initial fluctuations and covariance $C=\mathbb E[\xi\xi^T]$, differentiation gives \[ \dot C=AC+CA^T,\qquad C(t)=\Phi(t)C(0)\Phi(t)^T, \] where $\dot\Phi=A\Phi$, $\Phi(0)=I$. The formula is verified by differentiation; uniqueness follows from the linear matrix equation. It preserves positive semidefiniteness because $v^TC(t)v=(\Phi^Tv)^TC(0)(\Phi^Tv)\ge0$.
The zero-covariance obstruction.
In particular $C(0)=0$ implies $C(t)=0$ for every finite time for which the coefficients exist. Positive feedback through the means can amplify a pre-existing covariance but cannot seed it in this homogeneous closure. In the scalar constant case $\dot v=2av$, the exact solution $v=v_0e^{2at}$ makes both assertions explicit, including $a>0$.
