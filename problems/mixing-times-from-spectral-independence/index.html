Spectral independence bounds the operator norm or largest eigenvalue of the source's conditional influence matrices, uniformly after fixing subsets of coordinates. Mixing is measured by closeness to the stationary law, and $n$ is the number of coordinates.

A Markov chain evolves according to a transition kernel; mixing time measures convergence of its law to the stationary law, usually in total variation. Glauber dynamics updates a single coordinate at a time according to its conditional distribution.

A complete product-measure baseline

The primary definition includes the diagonal influence and calls a product law 0-spectrally independent. Its Glauber dynamics chooses a coordinate uniformly each step. We analyze that normalization exactly. Let $\mu$ be any product law on $\{-1,1\}^n$, with both probabilities at each coordinate positive. All of its conditionals remain product laws, so off-diagonal influences vanish and each remaining diagonal entry is one.
Proposition: The uniform-coordinate heat-bath chain satisfies, from every starting state, \[ \|\mathcal L(X_t)-\mu\|_{\rm TV}\le n(1-1/n)^t\le ne^{-t/n}. \] For the unbiased product law and $n\ge2$, put $a=n(1-1/n)^t$. If $a\ge4\sqrt n$, its total-variation distance from stationarity when started at all minus signs is at least $1/2$.
Proof: Couple a chain from the specified state with one started from $\mu$, using the same chosen coordinate and the same fresh draw there. Each coordinate agrees permanently after it has been selected at least once. A fixed coordinate is untouched with probability $(1-1/n)^t$; the union bound proves the first inequality. The stationary copy stays at $\mu$ because resampling a coordinate from its independent marginal preserves the product law. The exponential bound follows from $1-u\le e^{-u}$, with the trivial one-coordinate boundary interpreted directly. For the lower bound let $S_t=\sum_i X_{t,i}$. Each coordinate has expectation $-(1-1/n)^t$, so $\mathbb ES_t=-a$. For $i\ne j$, the product has expectation $(1-2/n)^t$: if either is refreshed, its last unbiased refresh has mean zero independently of the other coordinate. Since $1-2/n\le(1-1/n)^2$, pairwise covariances are nonpositive and $\operatorname{Var}(S_t)\le n$. At stationarity the sum has mean zero and variance $n$. Chebyshev gives probability at least $1-4n/a^2$ to the event $S_t\le-a/2$ for the started chain, and at most $4n/a^2$ for stationarity. The difference is at least $1-8n/a^2\ge1/2$. ∎
For example, taking $t=\lfloor n\log n/4\rfloor$ makes the lower-bound condition true for all sufficiently large $n$, because $a/\sqrt n=n^{1/4+o(1)}$. Thus the $n\log n$ scale is already necessary in this simple uniform-scan model. A separate normalization test shows why scan probabilities matter. On the unbiased two-bit product law, refresh coordinate one with probability $0<\varepsilon<1$ and start it at $-1$. Its marginal bias at time $t$ is $-(1-\varepsilon)^t$, giving total-variation distance at least $(1-\varepsilon)^t/2$. Spectral independence remains zero while mixing can be arbitrarily slow as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This is not a counterexample to the source's explicitly uniform scan.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact small transition matrices verify stationarity, total-variation bounds, magnetization moments and the nonuniform-scan marginal. The proof treats product laws only; there is no comparison controlling dependencies in a general spectrally independent law. Applying the coupon-collector argument there would fail because refreshing a coordinate need not couple it permanently. No improved general local-to-global theorem is obtained.
