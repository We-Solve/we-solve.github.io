Some Open Problems in Elasticity — A positive Jacobian bound

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source concerns an energy-minimizing deformation $y^*$ and the determinant of its deformation gradient $Dy^*$. In a standard Sobolev variational setting, one would specify a body $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$, an admissible class $\mathcal A$ with prescribed constraints, and an elastic energy such as \[ I(y)=\int_\Omega W(Dy(x))\,dx, \qquad y^*\in\mathcal A,\quad I(y^*)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal A}I(y). \] This is explanatory notation: the supplied record does not give the actual domain, dimension, energy functional, loads, boundary data, admissible class, or regularity assumptions.

Ball's Problem 6 asks to prove or disprove that, under "reasonable growth conditions" on $W$, an energy-minimizing deformation obeys a strictly positive spatially uniform Jacobian bound. In the almost-everywhere interpretation appropriate to a weak gradient, the requested conclusion is \[ \exists\varepsilon>0\quad \det Dy^*(x)\geq\varepsilon \quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Omega, \] or equivalently $\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in\Omega}\det Dy^*(x)>0$. The bound must be independent of $x$. Whether it may depend on the chosen minimizer, or must be controlled uniformly by specified problem data for all minimizers, is not stated. The source displays a pointwise-looking inequality without a regularity convention; a bound at every point would require a pointwise-defined gradient and is not silently identified with the Sobolev version.

The supplied summary says the available existence theory gives only $\det Dy^*>0$ almost everywhere and labels the uniform-positive-bound question OPEN-TRIAGE with moderate confidence. It says the assertion is believed not to hold in full generality without extra assumptions, but supplies no verified resolution. The unspecified phrase "reasonable growth conditions" prevents a single precise universal assertion about $W$; no coercivity exponent, blow-up rate near $\det F=0$, convexity condition, or injectivity hypothesis is invented here.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether growth assumptions on the elastic energy force the Jacobian of a minimizing deformation to stay uniformly away from zero. Positivity almost everywhere is weaker than the requested positive essential infimum. The admissible energies, deformations, and dependence of the bound are absent from the supplied statement.
