Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a countable discrete group acting by probability-measure-preserving transformations on a standard probability space $(X,\mu)$. The action is essentially free if almost every point has trivial stabilizer. Its orbit equivalence relation is \[ R_\Gamma=\{(x,\gamma x):x\in X,\ \gamma\in\Gamma\} \] modulo null sets. A graphing of this relation is a countable family of measure-preserving partial isomorphisms $\phi_i:D_i\to E_i$ whose graphs generate $R_\Gamma$ as an equivalence relation, modulo null sets. Set \[ \operatorname{cost}(R_\Gamma)=\inf_{\{\phi_i\}\text{ graphing }R_\Gamma}\sum_i\mu(D_i). \] The group has fixed price $1$ if every essentially free probability-measure-preserving action on a standard nonatomic probability space has orbit-relation cost exactly $1$. This is a statement about all such actions, not just the existence of one action with cost $1$.

The question is \[ \forall A,B\text{ countably infinite groups}\quad \forall\text{ essentially free p.m.p. actions }A\times B\curvearrowright(X,\mu), \qquad \operatorname{cost}(R_{A\times B})=1. \] No finite-generation, amenability, growth, or ergodicity hypothesis is imposed in the source. The fixed-price convention above quantifies over actions without an ergodicity restriction.

The supplied research summary reports an affirmative answer for all such $A,B$ in Khezeli's 2025 preprint, describing a Poisson-horoball weak limit of factors of independent identically distributed labels and a low-cost graphing construction. It separately reports parallel work by Seward and collaborators giving product results under growth conditions and the diagonal case $A=B$ in general. The latter qualifications are retained and are not silently promoted to an identical unrestricted theorem. The record labels the question solved, but no proof or independent verification is supplied here. Its final sentence about earlier special cases is truncated after "a factor contains an infinite", so the missing subgroup condition is not inferred.

Short English statement

Does every free probability-preserving action of a product of two countably infinite groups have cost exactly one? The supplied summary reports a 2025 affirmative result of Khezeli and qualified parallel results of Seward and collaborators. The claim concerns every free action, rather than merely the minimum cost among actions.
