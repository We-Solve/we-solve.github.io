Comparative Prime Number Theory — Problem 6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer modulus $q\geq 2$, an integer $a$, and real $x\geq 2$, define \[ \pi(x;q,a)=\#\{p\leq x:p\text{ is a rational prime and }p\equiv a\pmod q\}, \qquad D_{q;a,b}(x)=\pi(x;q,a)-\pi(x;q,b). \] One precise convention for the first strict sign change is \[ T(q;a,b)=\inf\{X\geq 2:\text{there exist }2\leq u<v\leq X\text{ with }D_{q;a,b}(u)D_{q;a,b}(v)<0\}, \] with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. This convention requires opposite strict signs and does not count a return to zero as a sign change. The supplied source does not specify its convention.

The principal request is to obtain an upper bound for the first sign change of $D_{q;a,b}$ in terms of $q$, with growth as slow as possible. Under the convention above, the desired form is \[ T(q;a,b)\leq B(q) \] for the intended class of triples $(q,a,b)$. That class is not specified in the supplied record: in particular, the source does not explicitly require distinct reduced residue classes, state whether the bound must be uniform in $a,b$, or state analytic hypotheses. Thus a fully quantified bound cannot be extracted without further qualification. The usual setting of distinct reduced residue classes would require $a\not\equiv b\pmod q$ and $\gcd(a,q)=\gcd(b,q)=1$, but these requirements are not explicitly supplied. If $a\equiv b\pmod q$, then $D_{q;a,b}\equiv0$ and the displayed convention gives $T(q;a,b)=+\infty$.

For a measurable set $E\subseteq[2,\infty)$, its logarithmic density, when the limit exists, is \[ \delta(E)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log X}\int_2^X\mathbf{1}_E(t)\,\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}{t}. \] As motivation, the source discusses the race between primes congruent to $2\pmod3$ and primes congruent to $1\pmod3$. It attributes to Rubinstein and Sarnak [RS] a corresponding logarithmic density written $\delta_{q;1,2}\approx1.0\times10^{-3}$, and attributes to Bays and Hudson the first sign change of $\pi(x;3,2)-\pi(x;3,1)$ at \[ x=608{,}981{,}813{,}029. \] These are supplied background reports. The notation $\delta_{q;1,2}$ is not defined in the record, including its event and the intended specialization of $q$ to $3$. The source suggests a correlation between race bias and the size of the associated "Skewes's number." It also states that classical race densities tend to $1/2$ as $q\to\infty$, and suggests that their first sign changes should become less extreme, while being bounded below by $q$. The families, hypotheses, and first-sign-change convention needed for these assertions are not supplied; they are retained as motivation rather than imposed as established properties of $T$.

The record additionally contains a number-field discussion. Let $\mathbb K$ be a number field, let $\mathcal O_{\mathbb K}$ be its ring of integers, and write \[ \kappa_{\mathbb K}=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{\mathbb K}(s), \qquad N(\mathfrak p)=\#(\mathcal O_{\mathbb K}/\mathfrak p), \] where $\zeta_{\mathbb K}$ is the Dedekind zeta-function and $\mathfrak p$ ranges over nonzero prime ideals. For real $x>1$, define \[ \Delta_{\mathbb K}(x)= \prod_{N(\mathfrak p)\leq x}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak p)}\right)^{-1} -e^{\gamma}\kappa_{\mathbb K}\log x, \] where $\gamma$ is the Euler--Mascheroni constant and an empty product is $1$. A precise threshold for a first negative value is \[ M_{\mathbb K}=\inf\{x>1:\Delta_{\mathbb K}(x)<0\}, \qquad \inf\varnothing=+\infty. \] The source connects this difference with Mertens's third theorem for number fields, describes a tendency toward positivity, and attributes to Büthe [buthe2015first] a first negative value for $\Delta_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ before $2\cdot10^{215}$. It attributes to [hathi2021mertens, Table 1] a weaker positivity bias, measured by logarithmic density, for ${\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt5)$ and ${\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{13})$ than for ${\mathbb{Q}}$. It explicitly leaves unclear whether their first negative values should occur earlier than those for ${\mathbb{Q}}$. The record gives no definite inequality to prove here and does not identify this paragraph as a separate problem or as part of the principal request. No comparison among the $M_{\mathbb K}$ is therefore asserted.

Short English statement

Seek a slowly growing upper bound, depending on the modulus, for the first sign change in a race between two prime-counting functions. The allowed residue classes, uniformity, hypotheses, and sign-change convention need clarification. An appended number-field discussion raises an unresolved comparison of first negative values without specifying a formal second target.
