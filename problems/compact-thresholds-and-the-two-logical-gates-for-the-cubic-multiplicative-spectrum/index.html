Math-329

Compact thresholds and the two logical gates for the cubic multiplicative spectrum

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 31, attributed to G. Margulis, asks that for every $\varepsilon>0$ the positive spectrum $[\varepsilon,\infty)\cap\{m(F)\}$ be finite. The local sentence does not define the forms $F$ or normalization of $m(F)$. The aid interprets it as the cubic multiplicative spectrum of unimodular lattices, not an assertion about arbitrary unnormalized functions.

In that interpretation take $B\in SL(3,{\mathbb{R}})$, $L_B=B{\mathbb{Z}}^3$ and $F_B(z)=(Bz)_1(Bz)_2(Bz)_3$. Set \[ m(F_B)=\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^3\setminus\{0\}}|F_B(z)| =\mu(L_B),\qquad \mu(L)=\inf_{0\ne v\in L}|v_1v_2v_3|. \] For an arbitrary invertible coefficient matrix, unimodular rescaling divides the product minimum by $|\det B|$; the original convention must be checked before using that extension. The reconstructed question is finiteness of the distinct values of $\mu(L)$ at least $\varepsilon$, not finiteness of the lattices themselves.

Let $A^+$ be the full positive determinant-one diagonal group and $\operatorname{sys}_\infty(L)=\inf_{0\ne v\in L}\|v\|_\infty$. The aid reports the exact identity \[ \mu(L)=\left(\inf_{a\in A^+}\operatorname{sys}_\infty(aL)\right)^3. \] With $K_r=\{L:\operatorname{sys}_\infty(L)\geq r\}$, its compact-threshold consequence is \[ \mathcal B_\varepsilon=\{L:\mu(L)\geq\varepsilon\} =\bigcap_{a\in A^+}a^{-1}K_{\varepsilon^{1/3}}. \] Mahler compactness and closedness make $\mathcal B_\varepsilon$ compact. This does not imply that its scalar minimum spectrum is finite: compactness alone permits infinitely many values.

The reported logical reduction separates orbit and spectrum information. If every bounded $A^+$ orbit is closed, every orbit in a positive threshold is compact. If, additionally, only finitely many compact closed orbits meet that threshold, the spectrum there is finite. Conversely, scalar-spectrum finiteness cannot alone give orbit finiteness without a finite-fiber statement for $\mu$ on the relevant orbit space. With such a finite-fiber hypothesis it yields finite orbit counts at each threshold; any further claimed closedness equivalence needs its own argument. No full equivalence is silently inferred from an invariant scalar.

For normalization the aid gives a Shanks cubic-order family of compact periodic orbits with exact minima \[ \mu(L_t)=\frac1{t^2+3t+9}. \] Its defining polynomial/order lattice and admissible integer range are not supplied. The summary also claims a closed threshold-count formula but does not print it. These remain reported family data and do not establish finiteness for all lattices.

The extraction then begins separate Section 5.4, defining, for $0\leq s\leq1$, \[ C_s=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^2: \inf_{q\geq1}\max\{q^s\|q\alpha\|, q^{1-s}\|q\beta\|\}>0\}, \] where $\|x\|$ is distance to the nearest integer. It identifies $C_{1/2}$ with simultaneous badly approximable vectors and ends with the fragment "Since." This weighted-approximation context is not an added clause of Question 31, and its missing continuation is not reconstructed. The aid leaves the full positive-spectrum question open.

Short English statement

The aid translates one normalized reading into multiplicative lattice minima and proves compactness of each positive threshold. Finiteness of values still needs additional orbit information; a compact set alone does not give it. The explicit cubic-order family calibrates the minimum but does not settle the full spectrum.
