Math-6900

Modular Isomorphism for Class-Two Exponent-p Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $k$ be an arbitrary field of characteristic $p$. A finite $p$-group is a finite group whose order is a power of $p$. A group $G$ has exponent $p$ if every element satisfies $g^p=1$ and some element has order $p$. Write $Z(G)$ for its center and $[G,G]$ for the subgroup generated by the commutators $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. Nilpotency class exactly two means \[ \{1\}\ne [G,G]\subseteq Z(G). \] The group algebra $kG$ is the $k$-vector space with basis $G$, with multiplication extending the group multiplication $k$-bilinearly.

Determine whether the following assertion holds: for every prime $p$, every field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and every pair of finite $p$-groups $G,H$, both of exponent $p$ and nilpotency class exactly two, \[ kG\cong_{k\text{-alg}}kH \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\cong H. \] Here the algebra isomorphism is taken to be a unital $k$-algebra isomorphism, hence fixes the scalar field. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify the type of algebra isomorphism; this interpretation follows the conventional formulation of the modular isomorphism problem and requires source confirmation.

The supplied report excerpt states that this group class has a positive solution over the prime field ${\mathbb{F}}_p$, dating to the 1970s and attributed there to reference [7], while the question over general fields remains open. The bibliographic identity of [7] is not supplied. These historical and status assertions are supplied source qualifications, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

For finite groups of exponent $p$ and nilpotency class exactly two, does the group algebra over every field of characteristic $p$ determine the group up to isomorphism? The supplied source reports a positive answer over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ and describes the arbitrary-field question as open. Algebra isomorphisms are interpreted as fixing the field, subject to source confirmation.
