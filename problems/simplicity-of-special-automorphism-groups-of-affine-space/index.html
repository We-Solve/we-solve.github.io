Simplicity of Special Automorphism Groups of Affine Space

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ denote the base field intended by the source, and let $\mathbb A_k^n$ be affine $n$-space over $k$. The supplied record does not specify $k$, its characteristic, or whether it is algebraically closed; these hypotheses remain unresolved. Write $\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb A_k^n)$ for the group of polynomial automorphisms over $k$. For $f=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$, its Jacobian determinant is a nonzero constant, giving a homomorphism \[ \operatorname{jac}\colon \operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb A_k^n)\longrightarrow k^\times, \qquad f\longmapsto \det\!\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}. \] In the source's notation, define \[ \operatorname{SAut}(\mathbb A_k^n):=\ker(\operatorname{jac}). \] A group $G$ is simple as an abstract group if $G\neq\{1\}$ and every normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ satisfies $N=\{1\}$ or $N=G$, without any restriction that $N$ be closed in a topology.

The supplied question is: for each integer $n\geq3$, is $\operatorname{SAut}(\mathbb A_k^n)$ simple as an abstract group? This formulation leaves the intended base-field hypotheses unresolved and does not assert that the question is meant uniformly over all fields.

The supplied excerpt attributes the failure of abstract simplicity in dimension $2$ to Danilov in 1974, with reference label [Dan74]. The supplied literature assessment also attributes topological simplicity in all dimensions to Blanc and describes the higher-dimensional abstract simplicity question as unresolved. These are supplied contextual claims, not independently verified conclusions; the topology and hypotheses of the reported topological result are not specified in the record.

Short English statement

For each $n\geq3$, ask whether the group of polynomial automorphisms of affine $n$-space having Jacobian determinant $1$ has any nontrivial proper normal subgroup. The intended base field and its hypotheses are missing. Abstract simplicity is the target; the supplied topological simplicity claim concerns a different condition.
