Erdős Problem #54: finite-prefix constraints on Ramsey completeness

Independent attempt, 23 September 2026.

1) Statement and current resolution

A set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ is Ramsey $2$-complete if every two-colouring of $A$ represents every sufficiently large integer as a monochromatic sum of distinct elements of $A$. The threshold may depend on the colouring. The problem asks to improve the classical bounds between logarithmic-square and logarithmic-cube counting functions. The current editorial attributes a construction with $|A\cap[1,N]|\ll(\log N)^2$ to Conlon, Fox, and Pham, resolving the requested improvement.

The distinct-summand convention is essential: arbitrary repetition of the element one would trivialize the density question. This note investigates elementary obstacles to constructing such a sparse set. It does not reprove the known logarithmic-square construction or improve its bound.

2) A global colouring supplies a rigorous prefix inequality

List $A$ increasingly and colour its elements alternately red and blue. This is one fixed colouring, rather than a different adversarial colouring for each $N$. If $A$ is Ramsey $2$-complete, there is an $N_0$ such that every integer in $[N_0,N]$ is a monochromatic sum for all $N\ge N_0$.

Write $m=|A\cap[1,N]|$. A sum at most $N$ can use only elements at most $N$, because all elements are positive. The two colours have $\lceil m/2\rceil$ and $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ elements in this prefix. Their nonempty subsets supply at most \[ (2^{\lceil m/2\rceil}-1)+(2^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}-1) \le2^{m/2+3/2} \] distinct monochromatic sums; collisions only reduce the count. Therefore \[ N-N_0+1\le2^{m/2+3/2},\qquad m\ge2\log_2(N-N_0+1)-3.\tag{1} \] This counts all possible subsets, including those summing above $N$, so its use as an upper bound is safe. The dependence of $N_0$ on the fixed colouring is also consistent with the definition.

Inequality $1$ is much weaker than the already known logarithmic-square lower bound. Its role here is to provide an elementary and fully justified test of proposed examples; it is not presented as an improvement of the published result.

3) Unique representations are incompatible with the requirement

Suppose an infinite positive set $A$ is dissociated: any equality between sums of two finite subsets forces the subsets to be equal. Colour it with two nonempty colours. One colour has infinitely many elements; fix an element $a$ of the other colour and choose an unbounded sequence $b_j$ in the infinite colour.

The integers $a+b_j$ tend to infinity. Their unique representations as subset sums of $A$ are the two-element mixed-colour subsets $\{a,b_j\}$. Consequently none has a monochromatic representation, and this colouring disproves Ramsey $2$-completeness of $A$.

The binary powers provide a concrete example: \[ A=\{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}. \] Every nonnegative integer is a sum of distinct powers of two, so ordinary completeness is perfect. But uniqueness of binary expansion makes every two-colouring with both colours nonempty fail Ramsey $2$-completeness by the preceding argument. Thus an ordinary sparse basis cannot be converted into a Ramsey basis merely by observing that it has many subset sums.

4) A necessary mixed-sum redundancy condition

The same proof isolates a more local obstruction. Suppose there is a fixed $a\in A$ and infinitely many $b\in A\setminus\{a\}$ such that $a+b$ has no subset-sum representation avoiding $a$. Colour $a$ red and every other element blue. The only red nonempty sum is $a$ itself. By the assumed absence of a representation avoiding $a$, none of those unbounded integers $a+b$ is a blue sum. Hence $A$ is not Ramsey $2$-complete.

Therefore any Ramsey $2$-complete set must satisfy: for each fixed $a\in A$, all but finitely many $b\in A$ allow a subset of $A\setminus\{a\}$ summing to $a+b$. This is a necessary condition only. It does not guarantee monochromatic representations under arbitrary colourings, since representations avoiding $a$ may still mix colours in another colouring.

5) Outcome and source

ATTEMPT UNRESOLVED. We have established a finite-prefix counting inequality and two exact representation obstructions. A construction with the required redundancy and logarithmic-square density has not been reconstructed here. The current problem is solved in the literature, and these observations are not claimed as new results or stronger bounds.

https://www.erdosproblems.com/54, current statement, bounds, and attribution to Conlon--Fox--Pham, checked 23 September 2026. All claims made about the elementary obstructions are proved in this note.

The subjective estimate gives limited credit for necessary conditions because the central sparse construction remains absent.
