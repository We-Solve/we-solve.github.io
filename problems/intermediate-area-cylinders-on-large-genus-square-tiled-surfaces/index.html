Intermediate-Area Cylinders on Large-Genus Square-Tiled Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $g$ denote the genus and $N$ the number of unit squares of a square-tiled flat surface $S$, so that its total flat area is $A(S)=N$. The permitted square identifications, stratum, and probability distribution on such surfaces must be specified before the random model is determined; these data are absent from the supplied excerpt.

A Euclidean cylinder on $S$ is an embedded flat annulus isometric to $({\mathbb{R}}/w{\mathbb{Z}})\times(0,h)$, with circumference $w>0$, height $h>0$, and area $wh$. Its normalized area is \[ a_S(C)=\frac{\operatorname{Area}(C)}{A(S)}. \] Let $\mathscr C(S)$ denote the collection of cylinders intended by the source. The source does not specify the directions to include or whether cylinders must be maximal. These choices are essential: allowing arbitrary subcylinders does not give the usual discrete cylinder count, and counting cylinders over unrestricted directions need not give a finite count.

Conditional on a specified cylinder collection, define its normalized-area counting measure by \[ \Xi_S(B)=\#\{C\in\mathscr C(S):a_S(C)\in B\}, \qquad B\subseteq(0,1]\text{ Borel}, \] allowing infinite values unless finiteness is established for the intended collection. An explicit version of the intermediate-area count, excluding endpoints, is \[ K_g^{\circ}(S)=\Xi_S\left((g^{-1/2},2g^{-1/2})\cap(0,1]\right). \] Including either endpoint adds the corresponding term $\Xi_S(\{g^{-1/2}\})$ or $\Xi_S(\{2g^{-1/2}\})$. The word "between" in the supplied record does not settle this boundary convention.

Problem, conditional on recovering the intended model. Determine the large-genus asymptotic behavior of the cylinder-area spectrum $\Xi_S$, in particular the number of cylinders with normalized area between $g^{-1/2}$ and $2g^{-1/2}$. The source describes macroscopic cylinders as having area proportional to $A(S)$, and microscopic cylinders as having area proportional to $A(S)/g$. It reports partial asymptotic descriptions in these two regimes. It does not specify whether the requested intermediate-area estimate concerns expectation, typical behavior, convergence in probability, or a limiting distribution, nor does it supply a candidate asymptotic formula.

The adjacent source text states that its Theorem 2 takes the limit $N\to\infty$ first, followed by $g\to\infty$. This is the supplied contextual limit order; Theorem 2 and its hypotheses are unavailable here. The excerpt further reports conjectural generalizations of Theorem 2 to all strata of quadratic and Abelian differentials, while saying that the proof uses an explicit description of a quantity transcribed as $\nu_{k,\mu}$, available only in a special case. Neither that quantity nor its parameters are defined. The special-stratum notation is corrupted in the supplied text, appearing as Ω(2) Mg,4g−4 (14g−4 ); no precise stratum can be recovered from this transcription alone. The associated reference label $[2]$ has no supplied bibliographic identification.

Adjacent question preserved separately. The original excerpt also includes a distinct "Problem 3" asking about random square-tiled surfaces when \[ N=\lfloor\alpha g\rfloor,\qquad g\to\infty. \] The parameter range for $\alpha$, admissibility conditions, probability law, and observable whose behavior is sought are not specified. This diagonal-limit question is not identified as the target of the cleaned intermediate-area record and is therefore retained as adjacent context rather than incorporated as a hypothesis of that problem.

Short English statement

For a random square-tiled surface of large genus $g$, describe how cylinder counts depend on cylinder area, especially for cylinders occupying between $1/\sqrt g$ and $2/\sqrt g$ of the surface. A precise probabilistic statement still requires the sampling law, stratum, cylinder directions and maximality convention, endpoint convention, and intended type of asymptotic estimate. The source also includes a separate question with $N=\lfloor\alpha g\rfloor$.
