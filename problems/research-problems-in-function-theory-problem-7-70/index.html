Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source uses a Clifford-algebra model of Möbius transformations of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, writing \[ \phi(x)=(ax+b)(cx+d)^{-1}, \] where $a,b,c,d\in A_n$ obey constraints not reproduced in the record. The inverse and all expressions are restricted to points where defined. In a specified Euclidean Clifford convention, the Dirac operator is $D=\sum_{j=1}^n e_j\partial_{x_j}$; the precise algebra, vector embedding, and generator signs must be fixed in that convention.

The source reports a linear conformally invariant class in the printed form \[ D^k f_k\left((ax+b)(cx+d)^{-1}\right)=0,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}, \] with associated weights \[ J_k(cx+d)= \begin{cases} (cx+d)^*|cx+d|^{-n-1+k},&k=2p-1,\\ |cx+d|^{-n+k},&k=2p. \end{cases} \] Here $*$ denotes a Clifford involution referred to by the source but not defined; it is not identified silently with conjugation or reversion. The coefficient constraints and norm convention are also absent. The record does not explain how the associated weight is inserted into the printed transformed equation, so the formula is retained as supplied rather than promoted to a fully specified covariance identity.

J. Ryan asks:

  1. Which nonlinear differential equations have conformally invariant solution spaces?
  2. Can their conformal weights likewise be expressed in terms of $cx+d$, and what relations do their solutions have to those of the linear conformally invariant equations?
In a precise formulation, conformal invariance would require a specified transformation law, often a weighted pullback such as $u\mapsto J_\phi(u\circ\phi)$, to carry solutions on the target domain to solutions on the source domain. In the noncommutative algebra the multiplication order is part of that law. The problem does not fix the admissible nonlinearities, differential orders, dependent-variable type, regularity, or meaning of classification and relation to linear solutions. Thus it is a source-specific classification program, not one universal theorem with supplied hypotheses. The summary reports it open as of 2018, with no definitive resolution located.

Short English statement

Ryan asks for nonlinear analogues of Clifford-Dirac equations whose solution spaces transform conformally, together with their weights and relation to linear solutions. The source omits the Clifford constraints, involution, and precise nonlinear class and transformation law, so these cannot be invented from the linear weight formula.
