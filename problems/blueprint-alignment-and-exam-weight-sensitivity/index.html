Attempt: whether exam scores contain the intended information

The record's reconstruction is unverified and gives no grading or alignment model. I tested an explicit linear version: a learner has a skill vector $k\in[0,1]^d$, the recorded exam scores are $Ak$, and a desired content summary is $t^{\mathsf T}k$. The question is whether the latter can be recovered exactly for all skill profiles from the recorded scores.
Proposition: Exact recovery is possible if and only if $t^{\mathsf T}$ lies in the row space of $A$.
Proof: If $t^{\mathsf T}=c^{\mathsf T}A$, apply the linear rule $c^{\mathsf T}$ to the recorded score vector to recover the target. Conversely, decompose $t=u+v$ into its orthogonal projections onto the row space of $A$ and its orthogonal complement. If $t$ is not in that row space, then $v\ne0$, $Av=0$, and $t^{\mathsf T}v=\|v\|^2>0$. For a sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, both \[ k_+=\tfrac12\mathbf1+\epsilon v,\qquad k_-=\tfrac12\mathbf1-\epsilon v \] belong to $[0,1]^d$. Their recorded scores agree because $Av=0$, but their target summaries differ by $2\epsilon\|v\|^2$. No function of the recorded score can assign both required answers. This proves necessity even allowing nonlinear recovery rules. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

For $A=(1\ 0)$ and $t=(0,1)$, profiles $(1/2,0)$ and $(1/2,1)$ have identical exam score $1/2$ and opposite target extremes. No change of weight applied solely to that one score repairs the missing information. The result gives a conditional algebraic alignment diagnostic, not a model of actual exam cognition. It does not establish causal learning gains, choose exam weights, or validate the skill representation. The broad alignment agenda and its source qualifications remain unresolved.
