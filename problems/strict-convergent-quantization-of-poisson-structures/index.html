Definitions and mathematical statement

A Poisson algebra over a field $\mathbf{k}$ is a commutative associative $\mathbf{k}$-algebra $A$ with a bilinear bracket $\{-,-\}:A\times A\to A$ that is antisymmetric, satisfies the Jacobi identity, and obeys the Leibniz rule \[ \{a,bc\}=\{a,b\}c+b\{a,c\}\qquad(a,b,c\in A). \] This supplies algebraic terminology; the source does not specify the underlying field, algebra, geometric space, or regularity class of its Poisson structures.

A formal star product is conventionally written \[ a\star b=ab+\sum_{r\geq 1}\hbar^r C_r(a,b), \] with bilinear maps $C_r:A\times A\to A$, and is associative on $A[[\hbar]]$. In one standard normalization its first-order commutator satisfies \[ C_1(a,b)-C_1(b,a)=\{a,b\}. \] This normalization is explanatory only: the excerpt does not specify its parameter or commutator normalization. Formal existence alone does not specify convergence after assigning numerical values to $\hbar$.
