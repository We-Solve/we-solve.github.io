Math-3358

Uniform Torsion Bounds for Chow Groups in Families

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$. The supplied question concerns a family $\pi:X\to S$, with a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-structure implicit in its use of number fields and $S(k)$. The question does not explicitly specify the admissible class of families or the geometric hypotheses on $X$, $S$, and $\pi$.

For a number field $k/{\mathbb{Q}}$ and a point $s\in S(k)$, write \[ X_s=X\times_{S,s}\operatorname{Spec}k. \] Under the arithmetic-fiber interpretation, $\operatorname{CH}^i(X_s)$ denotes the group of codimension-$i$ algebraic cycles on this $k$-fiber modulo rational equivalence, and \[ \operatorname{CH}^i(X_s)_{\mathrm{tors}} =\{\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^i(X_s):\text{there exists }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\text{ with }n\alpha=0\}. \] The supplied notation does not settle whether Chow groups of these arithmetic fibers or of their geometric base changes are intended; the display below uses the former interpretation explicitly.

Question as supplied, with unresolved family hypotheses. For each admissible family $\pi$ and each $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$, does there exist an integer $C=C(\pi,d)>0$ such that \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every number field }k/{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ with }[k:{\mathbb{Q}}]\le d,\\ \text{every }s\in S(k),\quad\text{every }i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\quad \text{and every }\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}^i(X_s)_{\mathrm{tors}},\\ C\alpha=0? \end{gathered} \] Thus a single integer must annihilate all the indicated torsion groups, independently of $k$, $s$, and $i$. This asks for a uniform annihilator, rather than a bound on group cardinalities.

Source qualifications. The surrounding excerpt discusses uniform bounds for cubic surfaces, attributes earlier questions in various forms to Manin, and says that results of Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc, and Swinnerton-Dyer imply that the $2$-torsion part of codimension-$2$ Chow groups may be unbounded even in one-parameter families. It also says that uniform boundedness has a negative answer in general. The meaning of "unbounded" and the hypotheses of those results are not supplied, so their relationship to the displayed annihilator question is unresolved.

The excerpt separately states a theorem, labelled Theorem 3 and attributed there to reference [1], for a family of smooth projective varieties over a smooth quasi-projective curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. For a fixed prime $\ell$ and positive integer $d$, it asserts a bound, written $C=C(\pi,d)$, on the cardinality of an $\ell$-primary Brauer torsion subgroup for all number fields of degree at most $d$ and all $s\in S(k)$ whose fibers satisfy the Tate conjecture for divisors. The group is printed as $\operatorname{Br}(X_s(k))$, whose intended meaning is unclear. The excerpt additionally asserts an equivalence between finiteness of the $\ell$-primary torsion subgroup of $\operatorname{Br}(X)$ and the $\ell$-adic Tate conjecture for divisors, without supplying the necessary conventions or hypotheses. These are contextual source assertions, not hypotheses silently imposed on the Chow-group question or independently verified mathematical claims.

Short English statement

The question asks whether one positive integer, depending only on the family and a bound on number-field degree, kills every torsion Chow class in every positive codimension on every indicated fiber. The family hypotheses and the arithmetic versus geometric interpretation require clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; its contextual claims about unbounded $2$-torsion and conditional Brauer-group bounds do not resolve these formulation issues.
