Math-1476

A boundary-transfer obstruction for 3-Sasakian Kirwan surjectivity

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a complete compact 3-Sasakian manifold; its metric cone is hyperkähler. Let a connected compact Lie group $G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ preserve the 3-Sasakian structure. For contact forms $\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3$, write the contact moment map as \[ \mu:S\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^*\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^3, \qquad \langle\mu_i(s),A\rangle=\eta_i(A^\#)_s, \qquad Z=\mu^{-1}(0),\quad S_{\mathrm{red}}=Z/G. \] If zero is regular and the $G$-action on $Z$ is locally free, use ordinary rational or real cohomology of the quotient orbifold. Restriction and the rational/real identification $H_G^*(Z;\mathbb K)\cong H^*(Z/G;\mathbb K)$ define \[ \kappa_S:H_G^*(S;\mathbb K)\longrightarrow H^*(S_{\mathrm{red}};\mathbb K), \qquad \mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}}\}. \] Here $H_G^*$ denotes Borel equivariant cohomology. For singular quotients, an alternative target theory is not specified by the original conjecture.

Conjecture 2.1, C. Boyer. This map should be surjective up to the middle dimension of the reduced space. For an odd-dimensional quotient of dimension $4m-1$, the inclusive lower-middle interpretation is \[ \kappa_S^q\text{ is onto for }0\le q\le2m-1. \] The source does not formally resolve endpoint conventions or general regularity assumptions. Boyer's Comment 2.2 reports the conjecture for circle reductions of spheres and says that in odd dimension only a middle-dimensional range can be expected. It cites examples of Boyer, Galicki and Piccinni [BGP02] whose cohomology computations motivate the question.

Sphere case and proposed hyperkähler route. In Comment 2.3, Boyer and T. Hausel warn that the nonabelian case may need extra hypotheses and discuss abelian reductions. For $G\subset\operatorname{Sp}(n+1)$ acting on $S^{4n+3}$, they conjecture the same range when $L=S^{4n+3}\mathbin{///}G$ is an orbifold. In the regular locally free case, $\dim L=4(n+1-\dim G)-1$, so $m=n+1-\dim G$. Their tentative strategy uses the corresponding hyperkähler reduction of $\mathbb H^{n+1}$ at a suitable central parameter with orbifold quotient and a hypercompactness hypothesis ensuring a middle-dimensional core. More precisely a moment-map parameter has type $\xi\in(\mathfrak g^*\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^3)^G$, not an element of the group center itself. Definition 6.9 of hypercompactness is not supplied. The proposed transfer is not assumed automatic.

Supplied conditional boundary-transfer statement. Suppose $L$ is the boundary of a compact oriented filling $W$ of dimension $4m$ for which Poincaré--Lefschetz duality over $\mathbb K$ holds. Assume $W$ retracts onto a core of dimension at most $2m$, and there is a graded map \[ \kappa_W:H_G^*(S^{4n+3};\mathbb K)\longrightarrow H^*(W;\mathbb K) \] onto in degrees at most $2m-1$, satisfying $i^*\kappa_W=\kappa_S$ for $i:L\hookrightarrow W$. This explicitly states the aid's Kirwan-compatible filling assumption. Define \[ I:H_{2m}(W;\mathbb K)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{PD}^{-1}} H^{2m}(W,L;\mathbb K)\longrightarrow H^{2m}(W;\mathbb K). \] The aid asserts surjectivity of $\kappa_S^q$ for $q\le2m-2$ and a natural identification \[ \operatorname{coker}\kappa_S^{2m-1}\cong\ker I. \] Thus the inclusive endpoint $2m-1$ additionally requires injectivity of the middle intersection map. A middle-dimensional core alone does not provide it. The aid's abstract example $W=C\times D^{2m}$, for a closed connected oriented $2m$-manifold $C$, has such a core but a zero middle intersection map with nonzero domain. This is an obstruction to the abstract boundary-transfer argument; it is not asserted to be an actual 3-Sasakian reduction or a counterexample to Boyer's conjecture. Existence of a compatible filling with these hypotheses remains separate from hypercompactness and from hyperkähler Kirwan surjectivity.

Short English statement

Boyer conjectures that 3-Sasakian Kirwan maps are surjective through the lower middle degree. The supplied aid gives a conditional transfer from a compatible filling and identifies the endpoint obstruction as a middle intersection kernel. A middle-dimensional core alone does not remove that obstruction or settle the conjecture.
