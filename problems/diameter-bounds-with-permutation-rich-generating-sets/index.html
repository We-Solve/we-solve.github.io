Math-6828

Diameter Bounds with Permutation-Rich Generating Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F$ be a finite field, let $n$ be a positive integer, and write \[ H=\operatorname{SL}_n(F)=\{g\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(F):\det g=1\}. \] For $\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)$, let $P_\sigma$ be its permutation matrix, defined by $P_\sigma e_i=e_{\sigma(i)}$. The supplied hypothesis is that $A\subseteq H$, $\langle A\rangle=H$, and $P_\sigma\in A$ for every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)$.

There is an unresolved compatibility issue in this hypothesis: $\det P_\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$ in $F$. For $n\geq2$ in odd characteristic, odd permutation matrices do not belong to $H$, so no such $A$ exists. In characteristic two, all permutation matrices do belong to $H$. The record does not specify whether characteristic two was intended, whether only even permutation matrices were intended, or whether a different embedding or ambient group was intended. These alternatives are not interchangeable and are not silently substituted here.

The notation $\Gamma(H,A)$ also leaves the graph convention unspecified. To expose this issue, define the undirected word diameter by \[ D_{\pm}(H,A)=\max_{h\in H}\min\{\ell\geq0:h=a_1\cdots a_\ell,\ a_i\in A\cup A^{-1}\}, \] and define the directed word diameter by \[ D_+(H,A)=\max_{h\in H}\min\{\ell\geq0:h=a_1\cdots a_\ell,\ a_i\in A\}. \] The empty product is the identity. Since $H$ is finite and $A$ generates $H$, both quantities are finite. They are the diameters of, respectively, the undirected Cayley graph with edges $\{x,xa\}$ and the directed Cayley graph with arcs $x\to xa$, for $x\in H$ and $a\in A$.

Problem, with unresolved qualifications. Establish a strong upper bound for the intended diameter of $\Gamma(H,A)$ under the supplied hypotheses, after resolving the permutation-matrix and graph-convention issues. The source tentatively suggests a polylogarithmic bound. A precise uniform version of that suggested target is: there exist absolute constants $C,c>0$ such that, for every admissible $n,F,A$, \[ \operatorname{diam}\Gamma(H,A)\leq C\left(\log|H|\right)^c. \] Here $C$ and $c$ would be independent of $n$, $F$, and $A$; the logarithm may be taken to be natural. The trivial case $|H|=1$ has diameter zero. This display formalizes the suggested ideal target, not an established bound or a fully specified conjecture in the source: the original request is for a "good bound" and presents the polylogarithmic expression as a possibility.

The original excerpt additionally suggests assuming only that $A$ contains a couple of permutation matrices generating $\operatorname{Alt}(n)$ or $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$, described there as subgroups of $\operatorname{SL}_n$, and then applying an unidentified result labelled $(2)$. The symmetric-group version has the same determinant obstruction for the standard permutation representation in odd characteristic. The excerpt supplies neither result $(2)$ nor sufficient details to formalize this proposed reduction. Its surrounding discussion of flag stabilizers and relatively large subsets, written as $A>|F|$, does not clearly impose an additional cardinality hypothesis on this problem.

Short English statement

For a finite special linear group generated by a set containing all permutation matrices, seek a strong Cayley graph diameter bound, ideally a power of the logarithm of the group order with absolute constants. The literal hypothesis is impossible in odd characteristic when $n\geq2$, and the intended permutation subgroup and directed or undirected graph convention require clarification. The supplied open status and literature assessment are dated curation, not independently verified facts.
