Math-3983

Log-Concave Busemann-Petty Problems

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be origin-symmetric convex bodies, meaning compact convex sets with nonempty interior satisfying $K=-K$ and $L=-L$. Write \[ S^{n-1}=\{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:|\theta|=1\},\qquad \theta^\perp=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\langle x,\theta\rangle=0\},\qquad tK=\{tx:x\in K\}. \] For a measure with specified density $d\mu(x)=f(x)\,dx$, define its induced hyperplane measure by \[ \mu_H(A)=\int_A f(x)\,d\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,H}(x),\qquad A\subset H, \] where $\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,H}$ is Euclidean Lebesgue measure on the hyperplane $H$. The section notation in the supplied statement is interpreted here as this induced measure. This interpretation requires confirmation: ordinary ambient $\mu(A)$ is zero for every hyperplane subset $A$ when $\mu$ has a Lebesgue density.

A nonnegative density $f$ is log-concave if \[ f((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq f(x)^{1-\lambda}f(y)^\lambda \quad(x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^n,\ 0<\lambda<1). \] Rotation invariance means that the specified density has the form $f(x)=g(|x|)$. The phrase "strictly decreasing density" is understood to refer to the radial profile $g$; the supplied record does not specify whether strict decrease is required on all radii or only on its positive support.

Question (1). For each $n\in\{5,6\}$, let $\mu$ be a rotation-invariant log-concave measure with strictly decreasing radial density, and let $K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be origin-symmetric convex bodies. Is the implication \[ \left[ \forall t>0\ \forall\theta\in S^{n-1},\quad \mu_{\theta^\perp}(tK\cap\theta^\perp) \leq \mu_{\theta^\perp}(tL\cap\theta^\perp) \right] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu(K)\leq\mu(L) \] valid?

For the second question, the Minkowski functional of an origin-symmetric star body $M$, with continuous positive radial function and the origin in its interior, is \[ \|x\|_M=\inf\{a>0:x\in aM\}. \] This functional need not be a norm unless $M$ is convex. In dimensions $n\geq2$, a classical intersection body $M=IB$ of an origin-symmetric star body $B$ satisfies \[ \rho_M(\theta)=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,\theta^\perp}(B\cap\theta^\perp), \qquad \rho_M(\theta)=\sup\{r\geq0:r\theta\in M\}. \] The term "intersection body" can also denote the broader class obtained by radial limits of such bodies. The supplied record does not specify which convention is intended.

Question (2), with unresolved scope. In arbitrary dimension, let $\mu$ be log-concave with specified density \[ d\mu(x)=\exp\left(-\phi(\|x\|_M)\right)\,dx, \] where $M$ is an intersection body. For every pair of origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,L$, does the same universally quantified section comparison displayed in Question (1) imply $\mu(K)\leq\mu(L)$? Here the section comparison and the conditions on $K,L$ are inherited from the supplied reconstructed statement. The supplied original excerpt does not state these hypotheses independently. It also does not specify the domain, allowed values, monotonicity, or strict monotonicity of $\phi$, nor whether strict radial decrease from Question (1) is retained. Log-concavity of $\mu$ is explicitly required by that excerpt. Euclidean rotation invariance is not imposed in this displayed formulation, since the density is expressed using $M$; whether any additional invariance was intended remains unresolved. The admissible dimension range and the intended intersection-body convention also require confirmation. These qualifications prevent a unique fully specified formulation of Question (2).

Short English statement

The first question asks whether comparison of all central hyperplane sections at every common positive dilation forces comparison of the full measures in dimensions $5$ and $6$. The second asks for an analogous implication for log-concave densities expressed through an intersection body's Minkowski functional in arbitrary dimension. The section-measure convention and several hypotheses of the second question require confirmation. Both questions are supplied as open; this draft does not independently verify their status.
