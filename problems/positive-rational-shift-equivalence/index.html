Math-1907

Positive rational shift equivalence

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write ${\mathbb{Q}}_+=\{q\in{\mathbb{Q}}:q\ge0\}$, the nonnegative rational semiring. Positive matrices here have every entry strictly greater than zero. Let $A\in M_m({\mathbb{Q}}_+)$ and $B\in M_n({\mathbb{Q}}_+)$ be positive square matrices, where $m,n\ge1$.

They are shift equivalent over ${\mathbb{Q}}_+$ if there are a lag $\ell\ge1$ and rectangular nonnegative rational matrices $R\in M_{m\times n}({\mathbb{Q}}_+)$, $S\in M_{n\times m}({\mathbb{Q}}_+)$ such that \[ AR=RB,\qquad SA=BS,\qquad A^\ell=RS,\qquad B^\ell=SR. \] They are elementary strong shift equivalent when $A=RS$ and $B=SR$ for rectangular matrices over ${\mathbb{Q}}_+$. They are strong shift equivalent over ${\mathbb{Q}}_+$ when there is a finite chain \[ A=A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_k=B \] of square nonnegative rational matrices, of possibly different sizes, with each consecutive pair elementary strong shift equivalent over ${\mathbb{Q}}_+$. Intermediate matrices and factors need not have strictly positive entries.

The statement to prove is \[ \forall m,n\ge1\ \forall A\in M_m({\mathbb{Q}}_+),B\in M_n({\mathbb{Q}}_+) \text{ positive},\qquad A\sim_{\mathrm{SE},{\mathbb{Q}}_+}B\ \Longrightarrow\ A\sim_{\mathrm{SSE},{\mathbb{Q}}_+}B, \] where the displayed relation symbols abbreviate exactly the definitions above. The supplied research summary records this positive rational assertion as solved by Kim--Roush (1990). It also reports real/rational variants and path results; the present target retains rational endpoints and rational nonnegative factors, without extending the conclusion to unrestricted real endpoint matrices.

Short English statement

Positive square rational matrices that are shift equivalent using nonnegative rational matrices should be connected by a finite chain of elementary strong shift equivalences over the same semiring. The supplied registry records this as a literature theorem.
