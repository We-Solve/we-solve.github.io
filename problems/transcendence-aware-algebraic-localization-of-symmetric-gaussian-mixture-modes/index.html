Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 18. Can algebra be used to better approximate the location of the modes in a mixture?

A defined mode-location problem

The source does not say whether modes are modes of a mixture density or of a parameter likelihood. This attempt treats density modes in one explicit family: equal weights, common unit variance, component means $\pm\mu$ with $\mu\ge0$. No claim of a general improved approximation criterion is inferred from this restriction. Let $\varphi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}$ and $f_\mu(x)=(\varphi(x-\mu)+\varphi(x+\mu))/2$. Factoring the two exponentials gives \[ \log f_\mu(x)=C_\mu-x^2/2+\log\cosh(\mu x),\qquad (\log f_\mu)'(x)=g_\mu(x):=\mu\tanh(\mu x)-x. \] Thus the stationary-point problem reduces exactly to a scalar transcendental equation. Algebraic rearrangement alone has not converted it into a polynomial equation.
Complete classification in this family.
For $\mu=0$, $g_0(x)=-x$ and the unique mode is zero. For $0<\mu\le1$ and $x>0$, the inequality $\tanh y<y$ for $y>0$ gives $g_\mu(x)<(\mu^2-1)x\le0$. To prove the inequality, differentiate $y-\tanh y$ to obtain $\tanh^2y>0$ and use its value zero at the origin. Evenness of $f_\mu$ now shows strict increase to zero and strict decrease afterward, so zero is the unique global mode, including $\mu=1$.

For $\mu>1$, $g_\mu'(0)=\mu^2-1>0$ and \[ g_\mu''(x)=-2\mu^3\operatorname{sech}^2(\mu x)\tanh(\mu x)<0 \quad(x>0). \] Thus $g_\mu$ is strictly concave on the positive half-line, begins at zero with positive slope, and satisfies $g_\mu(\mu)<0$. There is a positive root $x_*\in(0,\mu)$ by continuity. It is unique: strict concavity implies $g_\mu(x)/x$ is strictly decreasing for $x>0$ (by applying strict concavity to $x$ as a convex combination of zero and a larger argument). Its sign therefore changes exactly once. Consequently $f_\mu$ increases on $(0,x_*)$ and decreases on $(x_*,\infty)$; evenness gives exactly two global modes $\pm x_*$ and a strict local minimum at zero. These conclusions cover all real $x$ and do not rely on a local root finder.

A certified approximation route and its limit.
For example, at $\mu=2$, $g_2(1)=2\tanh2-1>0$ because $e^4>3$, while $g_2(2)<0$. The elementary bound $e^4\ge1+4>3$ proves the first inequality. Hence the positive mode is in $(1,2)$. Repeated interval bisection with verified signs localizes the unique positive root; after $k$ accepted bisections its interval width is $2^{-k}$. The statement is conditional on verified signs at each midpoint. Exact equality would itself finish the root computation; finite-precision sign certification near a zero needs an error bound for the exponential or hyperbolic tangent and is not granted for free. No numerical bisection run is claimed here.

An attempted uniform Newton/simple-root argument fails at the actual bifurcation: for $\mu=1$, $g_1(0)=g_1'(0)=0$, and also $g_1''(0)=0$ while $g_1^{(3)}(0)=-2$ by differentiation. The stationary root is not simple, even though it is the unique mode. Therefore an inverse derivative bound valid across all $\mu$ cannot be assumed. The classification supplies a useful family-specific bracket, but no error or performance comparison for arbitrary mixtures follows.

Audit and gap

The AIM Question 18 was checked. The audit separated density modes from likelihood modes, stationary points from maxima, the transition case from regular cases, and a symbolic bracket from an executed numerical experiment. The displayed reasoning is an elementary known Gaussian mixture calculation, with no claim of novelty. General weights, variances, dimensions, numbers of components and an agreed meaning of better approximation remain outside the proof. The original broad question is therefore unresolved in this attempt.
