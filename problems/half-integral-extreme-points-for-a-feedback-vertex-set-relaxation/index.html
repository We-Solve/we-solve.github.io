The actual relaxation and a symmetric candidate

The primary paper specifies the strong-density relaxation \[ P_{\rm SD}(G)=\left\{x\ge0: \sum_{v\in S}(d_S(v)-1)x_v\ge |E[S]|-|S|+1 \quad\text{for every }S\subseteq V\text{ with }E[S]\ne\varnothing\right\}. \] The question is the existence of a coordinate at least $1/2$ at every extreme point when the graph has a cycle. It does not say that every coordinate is half-integral. We tried the complete graph $K_n$, $n\ge3$, where symmetry suggests an all-small candidate.
Proposition: The vector $x_v=(n-1)/(2n)$ belongs to $P_{\rm SD}(K_n)$ and has every coordinate below $1/2$, but is not an extreme point.
Proof: For a set of size $s\ge2$, every degree is $s-1$ and the inequality is \[ (s-2)\sum_{v\in S}x_v\ge (s-1)(s-2)/2. \] For $s=2$ this is the zero inequality. For $s\ge3$ the displayed uniform point is feasible because $(n-1)/(2n)\ge(s-1)/(2s)$, with equality only when $s=n$. To verify nonextremality explicitly, set $\varepsilon=1/(4n)$ and form $x^\pm=x\pm\varepsilon(e_1-e_2)$. They are distinct and positive, and their coordinates remain below $1/2$. The full-set sum is unchanged. For every proper subset with $s\ge3$, the original slack is $(s-2)(n-s)/(2n)\ge(s-2)/(2n)$, whereas the perturbation changes the left side by at most $(s-2)/(4n)$. Therefore both perturbed vectors are feasible and $x=(x^++x^-)/2$. ∎
The attempted symmetric counterexample therefore fails at precisely the extreme-point hypothesis. On $K_4$ the simple cycle-cover candidate $(1/3)^4$ also fails the full strong-density constraint, since $2\sum_vx_v=8/3<3$; replacing the named relaxation by cycle-cover inequalities would invalidate the attempt.

Verification and remaining gaps

All nonempty-edge subsets of $K_4$ were checked for the vector $(3/8)^4$ and its $\pm(1/16)(e_1-e_2)$ perturbations. The example explains why feasibility alone is insufficient. No proof controlling a full-rank family of tight strong-density constraints on general graphs has been found.
