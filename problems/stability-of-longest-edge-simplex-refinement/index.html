Math-7175

Stability of Longest Edge Simplex Refinement

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual Euclidean interpretation of the source, let $d\geq 1$ be an integer and let $S=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a nondegenerate simplex, meaning that its vertices are affinely independent. The source does not explicitly specify the ambient metric; the following construction uses Euclidean distance.

A longest-edge bisection of $S$ chooses indices $i\ne j$ such that \[ \lVert v_i-v_j\rVert=\max_{0\leq a<b\leq d}\lVert v_a-v_b\rVert, \] sets $m=(v_i+v_j)/2$, and replaces $S$ by \[ S_i=\operatorname{conv}\left(\{m\}\cup\{v_k:k\ne i\}\right),\qquad S_j=\operatorname{conv}\left(\{m\}\cup\{v_k:k\ne j\}\right). \] These children have disjoint interiors, their union is $S$, and each has half its $d$-dimensional volume. A descendant is a simplex obtained by finitely many such bisections. For a nondegenerate Euclidean simplex $T$, let $r(T)$ be its inradius and $R(T)$ its circumradius, and define its shape ratio by \[ q(T)=\frac{r(T)}{R(T)}. \]

The supplied question asks whether, starting from a sufficiently generic $d$-dimensional simplex and repeatedly applying longest-edge bisection, the resulting subdivision stabilizes after finitely many iterations for every starting simplex in every dimension, or whether simplices produced from some fixed starting simplex can have shape ratios tending to zero.

Under an interpretation that recursively refines every child, the degeneration alternative can be expressed as the existence of a dimension $d$, an admissible initial simplex $S$, and descendants $T_n$ of that same $S$ such that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r(T_n)}{R(T_n)}=0. \] Here admissibility remains subject to the source's unspecified genericity requirement. The source does not say whether the $T_n$ must lie along one nested branch of descendants or may be selected from different branches.

A complete quantified stabilization assertion cannot be recovered from this record: "sufficiently generic" and "stabilize" are not defined, the refinement schedule and treatment of tied longest edges are unspecified, and the opening genericity restriction is not reconciled with the later phrase "every starting simplex." In particular, stabilization must not silently be identified with finitely many similarity classes or with a positive lower bound for $q(T)$. The supplied record also does not establish that failure of its intended stabilization property is equivalent to the stated degeneration alternative.

Short English statement

Repeatedly split a simplex at the midpoint of a longest edge, producing two equal-volume children. The source asks whether this refinement eventually stabilizes in every dimension, or whether descendants of one starting simplex can have inradius-to-circumradius ratio tending to $0$. The meanings of stabilization, sufficient genericity, and the refinement schedule require clarification before the question has a fully precise formulation.
