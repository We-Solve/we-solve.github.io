Math-3844

Ordinary Degree-d Curves Through Finite Point Sets

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})$ be the real projective plane, let $P\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})$ be finite, and let $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$. Put \[ V_d={\mathbb{R}}[X,Y,Z]_d,\qquad N_d=\binom{d+2}{2}-1=\frac{d^2+3d}{2}, \] where $V_d$ is the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $d$. For $0\ne F\in V_d$, define \[ C_F=\{[x:y:z]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}):F(x,y,z)=0\}. \] For a finite set $S\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})$, define \[ I_d(S)=\{F\in V_d:F(p)=0\text{ for every }p\in S\}. \] Vanishing is independent of the choice of homogeneous coordinates for $p$.

Supplied conjecture, with an explicit conditional interpretation. If a degree-$d$ curve means a nonzero degree-$d$ homogeneous equation up to multiplication by a nonzero real scalar, with reducible and nonreduced equations permitted, the supplied assertion becomes: for every finite $P\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})$ and every integer $d\geq1$, either \[ I_d(P)\ne\{0\}, \] or there exist $Q\subseteq P$ and $0\ne F\in V_d$ such that \[ |Q|=N_d,\qquad P\cap C_F=Q,\qquad I_d(Q)={\mathbb{R}} F. \] Thus the second alternative requires exactly $N_d$ points of $P$ on the curve, and those points determine its equation uniquely up to nonzero scalar multiplication.

Unresolved qualifications. The source record does not specify whether curves must be reduced or irreducible, whether nonreduced equations are admitted, or whether uniqueness concerns equations, algebraic curves, or their real point sets. The displayed formulation makes the coefficient-space interpretation precise but requires confirmation against the intended source convention. These interpretations need not coincide. The record gives no lower bound on $|P|$, no general-position hypothesis, and no exceptions in $d$; none have been added.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether, for every finite set of real projective points and every positive degree $d$, either all the points lie on one degree-$d$ curve or there is a degree-$d$ curve containing exactly $(d^2+3d)/2$ of them and uniquely determined by those points. The meaning of curve and uniqueness needs confirmation; the mathematical formulation above explicitly uses homogeneous equations up to scalar.
