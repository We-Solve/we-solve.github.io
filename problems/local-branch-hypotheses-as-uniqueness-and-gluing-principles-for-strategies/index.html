Fix a base premouse/model and a specified collection of relevant limit-length iteration trees, with all normality and admissibility conventions fixed. A branch-correct partial selector assigns to every tree in its domain a cofinal branch whose associated branch model is well-founded, and is compatible with its prescribed earlier trace. The record does not reproduce the full "minimal branch-correct" framework, so this is not silently identified with a total winning iteration strategy.

For such a domain $\mathcal D$, the local unique branch hypothesis is \[ \forall\mathcal T\in\mathcal D,\quad \#\{b:b\text{ is cofinal through }\mathcal T, \ M_b^{\mathcal T}\text{ is well-founded}\}\le1. \] The aid reports that two branch-correct partial selectors $\Sigma,\Theta$ agree on their common domain whenever this local hypothesis applies there. Hence their union is a well-defined selector, with branch correctness and the relevant trace compatibility preserved in the specified framework. It is not asserted to be total or to acquire additional game/iterability properties by taking a union.

Conversely, if two such selectors have a least disagreement at a commonly admissible tree $\mathcal T$, their distinct selected cofinal well-founded branches witness failure of the local unique-branch hypothesis at $\mathcal T$. The aid's trace-realizable converse assumes a tree with two well-founded cofinal branches whose proper initial-stage choices admit a common valid trace. In that minimal framework the common trace can be extended by either branch, yielding two partial selectors whose first disagreement is at that tree. No extension to total winning strategies is concluded.

Short English statement

The source is a biography rather than a problem. The aid adds a conditional local observation: unique well-founded branches force compatible partial selectors to agree, while a first disagreement exhibits two such branches. Its converse concerns trace-realizable partial selectors and does not construct total iteration strategies.
