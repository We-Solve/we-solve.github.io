Connectivity Under Weakened Bridge Addability

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the conventional setting of finite simple undirected graphs, a class $\mathcal{G}$ is bridge-addable if, for every $G\in\mathcal{G}$ and every pair of vertices $u,v\in V(G)$ in distinct connected components, the graph $G+uv$ obtained by adding the edge $uv$ also belongs to $\mathcal{G}$. The added edge is a bridge: deleting it increases the number of connected components. The supplied record does not specify whether this conventional graph setting, or a labelled or unlabelled version of it, is intended; these conventions require confirmation.

A condition $\mathsf{C}$ on graph classes is weaker than bridge-addability if every bridge-addable class satisfies $\mathsf{C}$; it is strictly weaker if the converse fails.

Source question. Which connectivity bounds and related asymptotic properties established for bridge-addable graph classes remain valid under specified weaker closure conditions?
