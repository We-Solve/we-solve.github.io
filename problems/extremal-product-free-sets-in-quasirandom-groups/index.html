Math-3934

Extremal Product-Free Sets in Quasirandom Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. A subset $A\subseteq G$ is product-free if \[ \forall a,b\in A,\qquad ab\notin A. \] Define its density to be $|A|/|G|$, and write \[ \alpha_{\mathrm{pf}}(G)=\max\left\{\frac{|A|}{|G|}:A\subseteq G\text{ is product-free}\right\}. \] For an action of $G$ on a finite set $X$, a point $x\in X$, and a subset $I\subseteq X$, define \[ K_{x,I}=\{g\in G:g\cdot x\in I\text{ and }g\cdot I\subseteq X\setminus I\}. \] The source presents these sets as examples of product-free sets. It imposes no explicit faithfulness or transitivity condition on the action and no additional restriction on $x$ or $I$.

The precise question attributed to N. Lifshitz is Question 10 of the supplied excerpt. Its representation-theoretic hypothesis requires clarification: literally, every finite group has a one-dimensional trivial irreducible representation, so a minimum over all irreducible representations cannot exceed one. Under the conventional interpretation that the minimum is taken over nontrivial irreducible complex representations, put \[ D(G)=\min\{\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}V:V\text{ is a nontrivial irreducible complex representation of }G\}. \] The exclusion of the trivial representation and the choice of ${\mathbb{C}}$ are interpretive qualifications, not explicit hypotheses in the supplied excerpt.

Under that interpretation, the intended question is whether there exist absolute constants $C,c>0$ such that, for every finite group $G$ for which $D(G)$ is defined and $D(G)>1$, there exist a finite $G$-set $X$, a point $x\in X$, and a subset $I\subseteq X$ satisfying \[ |X|\leq C D(G)^c, \qquad \frac{|K_{x,I}|}{|G|}=\alpha_{\mathrm{pf}}(G). \] Here the bound expresses the source notation $D^{O(1)}$; its intended uniformity should be checked against the report. The assertion concerns the existence of a maximum-cardinality product-free set of this form, rather than an explicit classification of all maximizers. The registry summary additionally asks to determine maximum density and structure in finite quasirandom groups, which is broader than the displayed existence question.

The excerpt contains a detached continuation involving an undefined function $f(n)$. It attributes to reference [7, Corollary 15] the existence of an unspecified set for $f(n)<\exp((\log n)^k)$, for any fixed $k$; asserts nonexistence for $f(n)\gg n!$, citing an unbounded minimal number of generators; and suggests the possibility $f(n)\approx\exp(n^{0.001})$. Neither $f$, $n$, the set, nor their connection with Question 10 is supplied. These fragments cannot be incorporated as hypotheses or established consequences of the question above.

Short English statement

For a finite group whose nontrivial irreducible complex representations all have large dimension, the intended question asks whether some largest product-free subset comes from a group action on a set of size polynomial in the minimum such dimension. The representation convention and the separation of Question 10 from adjacent text require review. The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved; that assessment is not independently verified here.
