Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.26

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $n$, define the Euler totient function by \[ \phi(n)=\left|\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:1\leq k\leq n,\ \gcd(k,n)=1\}\right|. \] In particular, $\phi(1)=1$. For a finite group $G$, let $|G|$ denote its order and let $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be the group of all automorphisms of $G$. A group $G$ is nilpotent if it admits a finite central series \[ \{1\}=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_c=G, \qquad G_i\trianglelefteq G,\qquad G_i/G_{i-1}\leq Z(G/G_{i-1})\quad(1\leq i\leq c), \] where $Z(H)$ denotes the center of a group $H$; the trivial group is included.

The two questions are:

  1. (a) Does there exist a real constant $a>0$, independent of $G$, such that \[ |\operatorname{Aut}(G)|\geq \phi(|G|)^a \qquad\text{for every finite group }G? \]
  2. (b) Does there exist a real constant $a>0$, independent of $G$, such that \[ |\operatorname{Aut}(G)|\geq \phi(|G|)^a \qquad\text{for every finite nilpotent group }G? \]
The existence quantifier is separate in each question; the constants need not be chosen to have the same value.

The supplied source additionally states that, if such a constant exists, then $a\leq 40/41$, attributing this restriction to J. González-Sánchez and A. Jaikin-Zapirain (2015). Its wording does not explicitly distinguish the scope of this restriction between parts (a) and (b); that qualification requires source review. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this status and the quoted restriction are recorded as source information, not independently verified conclusions.

Short English statement

Is there a fixed positive exponent such that every finite group has at least the corresponding power of the Euler totient of its order many automorphisms? Ask the same question for finite nilpotent groups. The source also reports the necessary upper bound $a\leq 40/41$, with its scope between the two parts requiring clarification.
