Definitions and conventions.
A point interaction is a zero-range interaction specified by contact boundary conditions or an extension of a free differential operator, rather than a naive bounded multiplication potential. A Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on an explicitly stated dense domain; boundedness below means $\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle\geq-C\|\psi\|^2$ on its quadratic-form domain for some finite $C$. Pairwise contact conditions must be jointly compatible with the statistics, mass ratios and many-particle collision geometry.

Why a naive contact quadratic form fails.
I test the simplest proposed construction before addressing the fermionic mass regimes specified by [S2]. In three relative spatial dimensions consider the formal two-body form \[ q(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla\psi|^2\,dx-g|\psi(0)|^2, \qquad g>0,\quad\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3). \] Choose $\phi$ in this domain with $\|\phi\|_2=1$ and $\phi(0)\ne0$, and set $\psi_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-3/2}\phi(x/\varepsilon)$. A change of variables yields \[ \|\psi_\varepsilon\|_2=1,\qquad q(\psi_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-2}\|\nabla\phi\|_2^2-g\varepsilon^{-3}|\phi(0)|^2\longrightarrow-\infty. \] Thus this unrenormalized candidate cannot be the desired lower-bounded form. Even point evaluation is not continuous in the free energy norm: with $\chi_\varepsilon(x)=\phi(x/\varepsilon)$, its squared $H^1$ norm is $\varepsilon^3\|\phi\|_2^2+\varepsilon\|\nabla\phi\|_2^2\to0$, while its value at zero stays nonzero.
