Recomposition and Cabling of Set-Theoretic Yang–Baxter Solutions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A set-theoretic solution of the Yang--Baxter equation consists of a set $X$ and a map $r:X\times X\to X\times X$ satisfying \[ (r\times\operatorname{id}_X)(\operatorname{id}_X\times r)(r\times\operatorname{id}_X) = (\operatorname{id}_X\times r)(r\times\operatorname{id}_X)(\operatorname{id}_X\times r) \quad\text{on }X^3. \] Write $r(x,y)=(\sigma_x(y),\tau_y(x))$. Nondegeneracy means that every $\sigma_x$ and every $\tau_y$ is a permutation of $X$; bijectivity of $r$ is a separate condition. In the usual nonempty, bijective, nondegenerate setting, indecomposability means that the subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$ generated by all $\sigma_x$ and $\tau_x$ acts transitively on $X$. The supplied excerpt does not specify whether this setting is intended, or whether finiteness or involutivity, $r^2=\operatorname{id}_{X\times X}$, is required. These conditions must therefore remain unresolved qualifications.

The supplied record contains the following research questions.

  1. Is there a systematic construction that recomposes, or stitches together, indecomposable set-theoretic solutions into new solutions? The permitted input families, compatibility data, meaning of recomposition, and required properties of the output are not specified. Thus no particular construction or universal existence assertion is determined by the excerpt.
  2. Can solutions be classified up to cabling, or can invariants distinguish cabling-equivalence classes? The cleaned statement additionally asks for computable invariants. Formally, after specifying an intended class $\mathcal C$ of solutions and a cabling equivalence relation $\sim_{\mathrm{cab}}$, an invariant with values in a set $S$ would be a map $I:\mathcal C\to S$ satisfying \[ A\sim_{\mathrm{cab}}B\ \Longrightarrow\ I(A)=I(B). \] A complete invariant would additionally satisfy the converse. The source does not define cabling, the resulting equivalence relation, or whether complete distinction of all classes is requested. Computability also requires an effective presentation of the solutions and invariant values, which is not supplied. It is unclear whether the classification concerns all intended solutions or only indecomposable ones.
  3. The original excerpt also asks, under "Structure algebras of set-theoretic solutions": what can be said about the integral unit group of the multiplicative group of $B$? This question is absent from the cleaned statement but is retained here as a source qualification. Neither $B$ nor its construction is defined. If the intended object is the unit group of the integral group ring of a group $G$, its notation would be $(\mathbb Z[G])^\times$; the excerpt does not establish this interpretation or specify whether a subgroup of units is intended. Consequently this part cannot be made into a definite mathematical statement from the supplied evidence.
These are statement-only research targets with unresolved hypotheses and operations, rather than a uniquely specified conjecture.

Short English statement

The record asks how to assemble indecomposable Yang--Baxter solutions and how to classify solutions using cabling or computable invariants. Its original excerpt also includes a question about an integral unit group associated with an undefined object $B$. The intended solution class, assembly operation, cabling relation, and algebraic object require clarification before a precise conjecture can be extracted.
