Open Problems in Mathematical Logic — Problem 56 (Halmos, 1956)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\lambda$ denote Lebesgue probability measure on $[0,1]$. Under the usual measure-theoretic convention, $G=\operatorname{Aut}([0,1],\lambda)$ is the group of invertible measure-preserving transformations modulo equality almost everywhere; inverses and compositions are understood modulo null sets. Define \[ R=\{T\in G:\exists S\in G\ (T=S\circ S)\}. \] Thus $T\in R$ means that some invertible measure-preserving transformation $S$ satisfies $T(x)=S(S(x))$ for $\lambda$-almost every $x\in[0,1]$.

The source asks for a characterization of the transformations $T\in R$ and states the conjecture \[ R\text{ is }\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}\text{-complete}. \] Here $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}$ denotes the class of analytic sets. The source record does not specify the topology on $G$, the reducibility used for completeness, or explicitly whether transformations are identified modulo null sets.

One conventional precise interpretation equips $G$ with its weak Polish topology, characterized by \[ T_n\longrightarrow T \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lambda\left(T_n(A)\mathbin{\triangle}T(A)\right)\longrightarrow0 \quad\text{for every Lebesgue-measurable }A\subseteq[0,1], \] where $\triangle$ denotes symmetric difference. Under continuous reductions from Baire space, the conjecture would say that $R$ is analytic and that, for every analytic set $B\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}$, there exists a continuous map $f:{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to G$ such that \[ \forall x\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\qquad x\in B\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f(x)\in R. \] These topology, reduction, and null-set conventions are explicit interpretive choices requiring confirmation against the intended source formulation; they are not specified in the supplied record.

Short English statement

Determine which invertible Lebesgue-measure-preserving transformations of $[0,1]$ admit an invertible measure-preserving square root. The supplied source conjectures that this collection is $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}$-complete. Its precise descriptive-set-theoretic meaning requires confirmation of the topology and reduction convention. The registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
