Conjecture 12.10 — (D.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $s(M)$ for the shadow number of a $3$-manifold $M$, as intended in the source. Its precise definition and the admissible shadows are not included in the supplied excerpt. Write $\|M\|$ for the Gromov norm. For a closed oriented manifold, the usual simplicial-volume convention is \[ \|M\|=\inf\left\{\sum_i|a_i|: \sum_i a_i\sigma_i\text{ is a real singular cycle representing }[M]\right\}. \] A relative or other convention would need to be specified for manifolds outside that class.

Conjecture (D. Thurston). The shadow number is quasi-linear in the Gromov norm, in the explicit multiplicative sense stated in the source: there exist constants $c_1,c_2$, independent of $M$, such that \[ c_1\|M\|\leq s(M)\leq c_2\|M\| \qquad\text{for every }3\text{-manifold }M \] in the intended domain. The natural nonvacuous reading of the comparison requires $0<c_1\leq c_2<\infty$; the excerpt itself says only "constants" and does not state their signs. It also does not fix compactness, connectedness, orientation, or boundary hypotheses for "any $3$-manifold."

The displayed inequalities are preserved exactly as multiplicative bounds, with no additive error. In particular, in any domain where $s(M)\geq0$, the upper bound forces $s(M)=0$ whenever $\|M\|=0$. This zero-norm consequence is part of the supplied formula and must be checked against the intended shadow-number definition; a restriction to hyperbolic manifolds or an additive correction is not silently introduced.

Short English statement

Thurston conjectures universal multiplicative upper and lower bounds comparing shadow number with Gromov norm. The source formula has no additive term and includes every manifold in its unspecified domain, so zero Gromov norm would force zero shadow number.
