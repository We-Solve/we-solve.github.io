Local Discrete Holomorphic Dynamics — Open Problem OP6

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$, let $O$ denote the origin of ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, and let $\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{C}}^n,O)$ denote the holomorphic map germs $f:({\mathbb{C}}^n,O)\to({\mathbb{C}}^n,O)$ fixing $O$. A germ is dominant if its complex Jacobian determinant is not identically zero, and is non-invertible if it has no holomorphic inverse germ; equivalently, $\det Df(O)=0$. Write $f^k$ for the $k$-fold iterate of $f$.

The supplied excerpt does not define $c$. Under the standard order-of-vanishing interpretation, for a nonzero holomorphic germ $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$ fixing $O$, set \[ c(g)=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\operatorname{ord}_O(g_i), \qquad \operatorname{ord}_O(h)=\min\{\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_n:a_\alpha\neq0\}, \] where $h(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}^n}a_\alpha z_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots z_n^{\alpha_n}$, with $\operatorname{ord}_O(0)=+\infty$. Dominance ensures that this order is finite for every iterate.

With this interpretation of $c$, Abate's Problem OP6 asks: for every non-invertible dominant germ $f\in\operatorname{End}({\mathbb{C}}^n,O)$, does the sequence $\{c(f^k)\}_{k\geq1}$ eventually satisfy a linear recurrence with integer coefficients? In the usual homogeneous, constant-coefficient, monic convention, this means that there exist integers $r\geq1$, $K\geq1$, and $b_0,\ldots,b_{r-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, which may depend on $f$, such that \[ \forall k\geq K,\qquad c(f^{k+r})=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}b_j\,c(f^{k+j}). \] The definition of $c$ and the precise recurrence convention require confirmation against the source: neither is specified in the supplied excerpt.

Short English statement

Abate asks whether the orders of vanishing of the iterates of every non-invertible dominant holomorphic germ fixing the origin eventually obey a fixed linear recurrence with integer coefficients. This formulation uses an explicitly provisional interpretation of the undefined notation $c$ and of the recurrence convention.
