10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Sum of Squares approximation ratio for Max-Cut

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E,w)$ be a finite graph with nonnegative rational edge weights of positive total sum. For signs $x_v\in\{-1,1\}$, its cut weight is $f_G(x)=\sum_{uv\in E}w_{uv}(1-x_ux_v)/2$; write $\operatorname{OPT}(G)=\max_x f_G(x)$.

A degree-four pseudoexpectation is a linear functional $L$ on real polynomials of total degree at most four satisfying \[ L(1)=1,\qquad L(q^2)\ge0\quad(\deg q\le2),\qquad L((x_v^2-1)q)=0\quad(\deg q\le2, v\in V). \] Define $\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)=\max_L L(f_G)$. These conditions are a semidefinite relaxation of expectations over actual cuts, so $\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)\ge\operatorname{OPT}(G)$.

Determine its worst-case integrality ratio \[ \alpha_4=\inf_G \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(G)}{\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)}, \] and the best polynomial-time rounding guarantee obtainable from this relaxation. The ratio convention is stated explicitly: larger values mean a tighter relaxation. An algorithm attaining a proposed bound and examples obstructing improvement play different roles.

The source also asks about any other fixed even degree $2r$, obtained by replacing the degree bounds by $2r$, $r$, and $2r-2$, respectively. The degree is fixed independently of $|V|$. In the near-bipartite regime, where a cut contains a $1-\varepsilon$ fraction of total weight, can degree four improve the degree-two guarantee $1-O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$? Merely adding constraints and changing some instances' optimum does not determine the worst-case ratio.

Short English statement

Find the worst-case strength and rounding guarantees of degree-four, and other fixed-degree, sum-of-squares relaxations for Max-Cut.

OpenAI claim summary for CS-105

The question includes the performance of polynomial-time rounding. A deterministic rounding rule that guaranteed a factor above $\alpha_{\rm GW}$ relative to $\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)$ would also achieve that factor relative to $\operatorname{OPT}(G)$, because $\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)\ge\operatorname{OPT}(G)$. Manuscript 1 therefore supplies a conditional obstruction to such a rule when $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$. This conclusion concerns deterministic guarantees; it does not by itself rule out a randomized algorithm under that assumption. Neither linked theorem computes $\inf_G\operatorname{OPT}(G)/\operatorname{SOS}_4(G)$ or answers the near-bipartite rounding question. The match remains partial.

OpenAI's claim

The two manuscripts give computational hardness results. The first claims that a deterministic polynomial-time Max-Cut approximation with any fixed factor above $\alpha_{\rm GW}$ would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. The second claims the Unique Games gap theorem, which supplies hardness consequences for approximation problems. Their relevance here is an obstruction to improving deterministic polynomial-time rounding; neither manuscript identifies the unconditional degree-four sum-of-squares ratio.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)$ denote the largest weight crossing a vertex bipartition, and set \[ \alpha_{\rm GW}=\min_{-1\le\rho<1}\frac{2\arccos\rho}{\pi(1-\rho)}. \] For each fixed $\alpha\in(\alpha_{\rm GW},1]$, the claim is NP-hardness of returning a cut of weight at least $\alpha\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)$, even on simple unweighted graphs. Its more explicit weighted gap uses any rational $0<t<1$, $B(t)=2\arcsin(t)/\pi$, and $0<\varepsilon<(t-B(t))/4$: distinguishing the two promises \[ \operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\ge(1+t)/2-\varepsilon \quad\hbox{and}\quad \operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\le(1+B(t))/2+\varepsilon \] is NP-hard when the rational edge weights sum to one.
Manuscript 2.
For every fixed $\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1/2)$, the claim gives $s\ge1$ and a deterministic polynomial-time $3$SAT reduction to explicit unweighted Unique Games over $K=\mathbb F_2^s$, on a simple bipartite graph with translation constraints $a(v_e)=a(u_e)+c_e$, satisfying \[ \varphi\text{ satisfiable}\Rightarrow\operatorname{val}(G_{\varphi})\ge1-\varepsilon, \qquad\varphi\text{ unsatisfiable}\Rightarrow\operatorname{val}(G_{\varphi})\le\delta. \] Alphabet size and polynomial constants depend only on the two fixed errors. For any fixed constraint-language instance $J$ with $A=\operatorname{OPT}(J)<B=\operatorname{SDP}_{R}(J)$, the associated consequence claims NP-hardness of distinguishing value at least $B-\eta$ from value at most $A+\eta$ for any fixed $0<\eta<(B-A)/2$.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 102; 2026-09-23), an affine game is composed directly with folded Gaussian sign tables. Excess stability reveals a hidden coordinate through restriction Fourier mass and vector-valued hashing, yielding endpoint labelers that contradict the outer game's low value.

For manuscript 2 (family 102; 2026-09-23), a latent quadratic alphabet and matrix-slice tests supply an outer permutation game. High acceptance decodes agreeing advice strategies, whereas clean-coordinate estimates from the unsatisfiable parity instance force their agreement to decay, giving the soundness contradiction.
