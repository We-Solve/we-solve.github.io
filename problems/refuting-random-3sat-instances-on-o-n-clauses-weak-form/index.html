Refuting random 3SAT-instances on $O(n)$ clauses (weak form)

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A Boolean literal is a variable or its negation; a 3CNF formula is a conjunction of clauses, each the disjunction of three literals. A clause is satisfied if at least one of its literals is true. For a formula $I$ with $m$ clauses, define \[ \operatorname{OPT}(I)=\max_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}\#\{\text{clauses of }I\text{ satisfied by }x\}. \] For the random model, a fixed nonnegative rational density $\Delta$ determines $m=\lfloor\Delta n\rfloor$. One may sample each clause by choosing three distinct variables uniformly and choosing their signs independently, with independent clauses. "Typical" is an algorithm's output label, not an assertion that each such instance is unsatisfiable. Its soundness requirement is that it never labels an input typical when $\operatorname{OPT}(I)\ge(1-\epsilon)m$; its success requirement is probability at least $1/2$ on the random-instance distribution. Polynomial time is measured in the formula's encoding length, with the fixed parameters held constant.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Conjecture For every rational $\epsilon > 0$ and every rational $\Delta$, there is no polynomial-time algorithm for the following problem.

Given is a 3SAT (3CNF) formula $I$ on $n$ variables, for some $n$, and $m = \lfloor\Delta n\rfloor$ clauses drawn uniformly at random from the set of formulas on $n$ variables. Return with probability at least 0.5 (over the instances) that $I$ is typical without returning typical for any instance with at least $(1 - \epsilon)m$ simultaneously satisfiable clauses. [S2]

Scope qualification.
The catalogue reproduces the Open Problem Garden weak form, including its universal density quantifier. The clause distribution must be fixed; the problem page explicitly discusses sampling with versus without replacement. The source wording allows a rational $\Delta$ without explicitly stating its nonnegativity; negative clause counts have no meaning. No randomized-algorithm soundness convention is supplied in the quoted question, so its probability over instances is not silently replaced by an average-error guarantee. This is a conjectural computational lower bound, not an established consequence of $\mathsf{P}\ne\mathsf{NP}$. Short English statement

Can a polynomial-time algorithm reliably recognize random formulas with only a constant number of clauses per variable while never accepting a formula whose clauses are almost all simultaneously satisfiable?
