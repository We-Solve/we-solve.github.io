Colored Transversals for Translates of Convex Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a compact convex set, and let $\mathcal T(K)=\{K+t:t\in{\mathbb{R}}^2\}$, where $K+t=\{x+t:x\in K\}$. Let $F_1,F_2,F_3\subseteq\mathcal T(K)$. Under the point-transversal interpretation, a $3$-transversal of a family $F$ is a set $P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ with $|P|\leq 3$ such that $P\cap A\neq\varnothing$ for every $A\in F$. Write this property as \[ T_3(F)\iff\exists P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2\;\left(|P|\leq3\ \land\ \forall A\in F,\ P\cap A\neq\varnothing\right). \] The supplied question asks whether the stated pairwise-intersection hypothesis forces \[ \exists i\in\{1,2,3\}\;T_3(F_i). \] However, the supplied record does not define either "pairwise intersecting families" or "$3$-transversal." Two distinct possible intersection hypotheses are \[ \begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{cross}}&:\quad \forall i\neq j\;\forall A\in F_i\;\forall B\in F_j, \quad A\cap B\neq\varnothing,\\ H_{\mathrm{within}}&:\quad \forall i\in\{1,2,3\}\;\forall A,B\in F_i, \quad A\neq B\Longrightarrow A\cap B\neq\varnothing. \end{aligned} \] For each choice $H$ among these hypotheses, a precise candidate question is whether, for every compact convex $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$ and every three families $F_1,F_2,F_3\subseteq\mathcal T(K)$, \[ H\Longrightarrow\exists i\in\{1,2,3\}\;T_3(F_i). \] These are alternative formulations requiring source clarification, not an assertion that either is the intended formulation. The point-transversal interpretation also requires confirmation. The record does not specify whether the families must be finite, whether $K$ must be nonempty or have nonempty interior, or whether empty families are permitted; the intended quantified domain therefore remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether three suitably intersecting families of translates of one compact convex planar set must include a family that can be met by three points, under the point-transversal interpretation. The meanings of the intersection condition and transversal, and the permitted sets and families, require clarification before a unique precise statement can be selected.
