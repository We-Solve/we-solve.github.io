Density of Finite-Order Absolutely Periodic Billiard Orbits

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not determine a fully specified mathematical problem. Its retained assertion is the following source claim:
For every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and every strictly convex domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ of the source's stated regularity $\Omega\in C^r({\mathbb{R}}^2)$, there exists a strictly convex domain $\widetilde\Omega\in C^{r-1}({\mathbb{R}}^2)$, arbitrarily close to $\Omega$ in the $C^{r-1}$ topology, having an absolutely periodic orbit of order $n$.
Here "arbitrarily close" expresses a neighborhood quantifier: every neighborhood of $\Omega$ in the intended $C^{r-1}$ topology must contain such a $\widetilde\Omega$. A domain conventionally means a nonempty connected open set. However, the source does not specify boundedness, the admissible values of $r$, the meaning of regularity for these domains, or the topology used to compare them. The notation $\Omega\in C^r({\mathbb{R}}^2)$ is retained as source notation; a domain is not itself a function, and interpreting this as boundary regularity requires clarification.

The billiard dynamics, the term "absolutely periodic orbit," and the meaning of its "order" are not defined in the supplied record. In particular, order $n$ must not silently be identified with least period $n$, number of reflections, or an order of vanishing. The convention for ${\mathbb{N}}$, including whether it contains zero, is also unspecified.

The supplied statement-verification field says that the official report presents this density construction as Theorem 1, rather than as an open problem. It marks the statement as unrecoverable and supplies no replacement open question. Accordingly, the displayed assertion is preserved for provenance and is not promoted here to an open conjecture or independently verified theorem.

Short English statement

The retained claim says that every strictly convex planar domain of the stated regularity can be approximated in $C^{r-1}$ by strictly convex domains carrying an absolutely periodic billiard orbit of any prescribed order. The orbit terminology and regularity hypotheses remain unspecified. Although the registry labels the entry open, its supplied verification identifies this assertion as Theorem 1 and provides no recoverable open question.
