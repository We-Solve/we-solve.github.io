CAM Actions of Residually Finite Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{e\}$, there exist a finite group $F$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ such that $\varphi(g)\ne e_F$. Equivalently, \[ \bigcap_{\substack{N\trianglelefteq G\\ [G:N]<\infty}}N=\{e\}. \] The supplied record uses the term chaotic almost minimal action, abbreviated CAM, without defining it. Write $\mathcal{C}(G)$ provisionally for the assertion that $G$ admits an action of the type called CAM in the cited report; this notation is a placeholder for the missing definition.

The recorded existence question is:

Does every residually finite group $G$ admit a chaotic almost minimal action?
Its logical form is \[ \forall G\;\left(G\text{ is a residually finite group}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{C}(G)\right). \] The supplied report passage also asserts the necessary implication \[ \mathcal{C}(G)\Longrightarrow G\text{ is residually finite}. \] This implication is reported source context, not a theorem verified in this draft.

Unresolved qualifications. The record does not supply the definition of CAM, the class of underlying spaces, or the requirements on the action. It also does not state whether the groups must be infinite, countable, or finitely generated. The literal universal wording includes finite groups and the trivial group; whether these are intended must be checked against the defining source. Thus the displayed question preserves the supplied quantifier, but a fully specified mathematical formulation requires the missing definition and scope restrictions.

Short English statement

The question asks whether residual finiteness is sufficient for a group to admit a chaotic almost minimal action. The supplied passage states that residual finiteness is necessary. A precise interpretation still requires the definition of CAM and clarification of the permitted groups and action spaces.
