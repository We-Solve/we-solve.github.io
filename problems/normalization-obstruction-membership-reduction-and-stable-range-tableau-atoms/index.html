Math-1708

Normalization obstruction, membership reduction, and stable-range tableau atoms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether the historical tableau $k$-atoms from the first definition in Morse's lecture notes form a basis of $\operatorname{span}\{J_\lambda:\lambda_1\le k\}$ and whether their expansion coefficients refine the $q,t$-Kostka polynomials. The atom construction and the normalization of $J$ are not included. The aid explicitly warns against replacing these original atoms by a later definition of $k$-Schur functions.

Work over ${\mathbb{Q}}(q,t)$ with independent parameters. The ordinary Macdonald integral form is $J_\lambda=c_\lambda(q,t)P_\lambda$. The aid's intended transformed normalization is instead \[ H_\lambda[X;q,t]=J_\lambda[X/(1-t);q,t], \qquad V_{k,n}=\operatorname{span}\{H_\lambda:|\lambda|=n,\lambda_1\le k\}. \] This plethystic transform is not silently identified with another modified-Macdonald convention. The unmodified interpretation already fails at $(k,n)=(1,2)$: \[ J_{(1,1)}=(1-t)(1-t^2)s_{(1,1)},\qquad H_{(1,1)}=s_{(1,1)}+t s_{(2)}. \] Thus its unmodified line is different from the historical transformed line underlying the atom claim. A normalization must be chosen before the original basis assertion can be evaluated.

Let $A_\mu^{(k)}(X;t)$ denote the original atoms for $\mu_1\le k$. The aid uses their Schur-unitriangularity, with leading term $s_\mu$, to prove independence. Since $\dim V_{k,n}$ equals the number of $k$-bounded partitions of $n$, the corrected degree-$n$ basis problem is exactly \[ A_\mu^{(k)}\in V_{k,n}\quad\text{for every }|\mu|=n,\ \mu_1\le k, \] or equivalently the vanishing of these finitely many classes in the quotient of degree-$n$ symmetric functions by $V_{k,n}$. Independence does not itself prove that membership.

When this basis exists, write for $\lambda_1\le k$ \[ H_\lambda=\sum_{\mu_1\le k}K^{(k)}_{\mu\lambda}(q,t)A_\mu^{(k)}, \qquad A_\mu^{(k)}=\sum_\nu a_{\nu\mu}^{(k)}(t)s_\nu. \] A precise natural refinement target is polynomial nonnegativity of $K^{(k)}$ together with \[ K_{\nu\lambda}(q,t)=\sum_\mu a_{\nu\mu}^{(k)}(t)K^{(k)}_{\mu\lambda}(q,t), \] where $K_{\nu\lambda}$ are the Schur coefficients in the stated $H$ convention. The source does not spell out what refinement means, and cannot mean expansion of every unbounded-index $H_\lambda$ in this smaller space without additional hypotheses.

For $n\le k$ the aid reports stabilization $A_\mu^{(k)}=s_\mu$. Then $V_{k,n}$ is the full degree-$n$ space, the atoms form a basis, and the refined coefficients equal ordinary $q,t$-Kostka polynomials. It leaves the general $n>k$ membership and positivity statements open for the original tableau atoms in its checked literature. The stable-range result does not settle those assertions.

Short English statement

The basis claim depends on Macdonald normalization. The aid rejects the literal unmodified integral-form reading, reduces the transformed problem to atom membership, and reports a complete stable sector when the degree is at most $k$. It does not replace the historical atoms or solve the general membership and refinement problem.
