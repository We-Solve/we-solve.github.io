Math-3966

Classification of Static Near-Horizon Geometries

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ with $n\geq3$, and let $\Lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}$ with $\Lambda\geq0$. For an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ and a vector field $X$ on $M$, define its metric-dual one-form $\alpha=X^\flat$ by $\alpha(Y)=g(X,Y)$ for every vector field $Y$. The closedness condition is \[ {\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha=0. \] Closedness must not be replaced by the stronger requirement that $\alpha$ be globally exact.

Classification problem. For every such $n$ and $\Lambda$, classify all compact horizon data $(M,g,X)$ satisfying the near-horizon quasi-Einstein equation referred to as equation (1) in the source, together with ${\,\mathrm{d}} X^\flat=0$. The supplied statement describes these data as equivalently the compact static near-horizon geometries.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. Equation (1) is not reproduced in the supplied record. Consequently, its tensor equation, constants, sign conventions, and normalization of $\Lambda$ cannot be specified from the supplied evidence. The record also does not specify the boundary convention, connectedness assumptions, or equivalence relation for the classification. The stated equivalence with static near-horizon geometries is supplied source information, not independently established here. These omissions prevent a fully self-contained mathematical formulation.

Surrounding source context. The original excerpt also discusses whether spherical topology of $M$, together with equation (1), forces the existence of a Killing vector field. This is contextual material, not an additional spherical-topology hypothesis on the classification problem above. The excerpt then attributes to Theorem 1, reference [13], the assertion that any $n$-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ with a non-gradient vector field $X$ satisfying equation (1) admits a Killing vector field $K$ such that \[ \mathcal L_Kg=0,\qquad [K,X]=0. \] Here $\mathcal L_Kg=0$ defines the Killing condition, and $[K,X]$ is the Lie bracket. In the usual global sense, non-gradient means that no globally defined function $f$ satisfies $X=\nabla_g f$. The supplied excerpt does not identify reference [13], specify whether the asserted Killing field must be nonzero, or clarify the precise meaning of spherical topology. This contextual theorem is retained as an attributed source assertion and does not resolve the classification problem.

Short English statement

For every $\Lambda\geq0$ and integer horizon dimension $n\geq3$, classify the compact static near-horizon geometries, described in the supplied record by near-horizon quasi-Einstein data with closed metric-dual one-form. The defining equation and several classification conventions are missing, so this remains a qualified statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
