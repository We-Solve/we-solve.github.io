Topographic Rossby Waves and Inertial-Wave Coexistence

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A rotating fluid supports inertial waves due to Coriolis restoring forces. A sloping boundary or varying depth gives an effective topographic potential-vorticity gradient and supports topographic Rossby waves. The report compares these waves in a rectangular rotating basin with a sloping boundary and examines an asymptotic series whose leading term is a topographic Rossby wave.
Source scope and qualifications.
The basin geometry, boundary slope, and asymptotic expansion of the contribution are part of the context. The source does not ask whether every Rossby wave in every geometry diverges; the two alternatives concern the particular paradox described there.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
How is the apparent paradox between topographic Rossby waves and inertial waves resolved: does the series whose zeroth-order term is the topographic Rossby wave diverge locally, or does it coexist with the inertial wave?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The report presents this as an open problem, conjecture, or unresolved research direction; targeted literature checking found no authoritative resolution of the extracted claim as of 2026-08-21. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Explain whether the topographic-Rossby expansion breaks down locally or describes waves coexisting with the exact inertial waves.
