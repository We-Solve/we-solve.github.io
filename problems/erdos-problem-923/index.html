Erdős Problem #923: triangle-free subgraphs of high chromatic number

Statement and result

For every positive integer $k$, does there exist $f(k)$ such that $\chi(G)\geq f(k)$ forces a triangle-free subgraph with chromatic number at least $k$? Yes. We reconstruct Rödl's argument and supply the auxiliary triangle-free construction explicitly. All subgraphs here may omit edges; the assertion does not require an induced subgraph.

A product bound for edge partitions

If $E(G)=E_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup E_q$ and $G_i=(V(G),E_i)$, then \[ \chi(G)\leq\prod_{i=1}^q\chi(G_i). \tag{1} \] Indeed, give each vertex the tuple of its colours in proper colourings of the $G_i$. Every edge belongs to one $E_i$, so its endpoints differ in that coordinate.

Clique or triangle-free subgraph

Fix $k\geq3$, and define \[ T_2=2,\qquad T_m=(k-1)^{T_{m-1}-1}+1\quad(m\geq3). \] We prove by induction on $m$ that $\chi(G)\geq T_m$ implies either $K_m\subseteq G$, or a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number at least $k$. For $m=2$, a graph of chromatic number at least two has an edge.

Suppose the assertion holds for $m-1$, and $G$ contains no $K_m$. Order its vertices. For each $v$, let $L_v$ be the graph induced by its neighbours preceding $v$. This graph contains no $K_{m-1}$. If $\chi(L_v)\geq T_{m-1}$ for some $v$, the induction hypothesis gives the required triangle-free subgraph inside $L_v$.

Otherwise put $q=T_{m-1}-1$, and choose a proper $q$-colouring $c_v$ of each $L_v$, allowing unused colours. Assign the edge $uv$, where $u<v$, to class $c_v(u)$. Each edge-class graph is triangle-free: in a triangle $u<w<v$, its two edges at the largest vertex $v$ have different labels, since $u,w$ are adjacent in $L_v$. If all $q$ classes had chromatic number at most $k-1$, $1$ would give $\chi(G)\leq(k-1)^q=T_m-1$, a contradiction. This completes the induction.

An explicit triangle-free graph with chromatic number $k$

Starting from $M_2=K_2$, apply the Mycielski construction. For a graph $H$ on vertices $v_1,\ldots,v_s$, introduce shadows $u_1,\ldots,u_s$ and one vertex $w$. Retain the edges of $H$; join $u_i$ to $v_j$ whenever $v_iv_j\in E(H)$; join $w$ to every shadow, and add no other edges.

If $H$ is triangle-free, so is the new graph. A triangle through $w$ would require an edge between shadows. A triangle with exactly one shadow projects to a triangle of $H$, and a triangle with two shadows is impossible.

If $\chi(H)=h$, copying the original colours to the shadows and using a new colour on $w$ gives $h+1$ colours. To show $h$ colours impossible, suppose such a colouring exists, with $w$ assigned colour $h$. Every shadow then avoids colour $h$. Recolour each original vertex $v_i$ of colour $h$ with the colour of $u_i$. The recoloured vertices form an independent set before recolouring, and the edges $u_iv_j$ ensure that no new conflict occurs with an unchanged neighbour $v_j$. This would colour $H$ with $h-1$ colours, a contradiction.

Consequently $M_k$ is triangle-free, has chromatic number $k$, and has \[ s_k=3\cdot2^{k-2}-1 \] vertices, by the recurrence $s_{h+1}=2s_h+1$.

Completion of the proof

Take $f(k)=T_{s_k}$. A graph of chromatic number at least $f(k)$ either already contains the desired triangle-free subgraph, or contains $K_{s_k}$. In the latter case retain inside that clique precisely the edges of $M_k$. This is the required subgraph. The cases $k=1,2$ use $f(1)=1,f(2)=2$. If chromatic number exactly $k$ is desired, delete vertices until a minimal subgraph with chromatic number at least $k$ remains; deleting one vertex reduces chromatic number by at most one.

Attribution and scope

Rödl, On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a given graph, Proceedings of the AMS 64 $1977$, 370--371, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1977-0469806-4, with the Mycielski input also proved.
