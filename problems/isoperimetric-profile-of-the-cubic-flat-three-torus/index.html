Isoperimetric profile of the cubic flat three-torus

Status OpenLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

On $\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3$, let $V\in(0,1)$ be the prescribed volume and put $v=\min(V,1-V)$. Is the least perimeter \[\min\left\{(36\pi)^{1/3}v^{2/3},\;2\sqrt{\pi v},\;2\right\}?\] Are all minimizers, modulo isometry and null sets, balls for $v\leq4\pi/81$, round tubes along shortest geodesics for $4\pi/81\leq v\leq1/\pi$, coordinate slabs for $v\geq1/\pi$, and their complements for $V>1/2$, with exactly both adjacent types at transition volumes?

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
On the unit cubic flat torus $\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3$, for $0<V<1$ set $v=\min(V,1-V)$. The claimed finite-perimeter isoperimetric profile is \[I(V)=\min\{(36\pi)^{1/3}v^{2/3},\ 2\sqrt{\pi v},\ 2\}.\] For $V\le1/2$ every minimizer, up to isometry and a null set, is a radius-$(3V/(4\pi))^{1/3}$ ball when $V\le4\pi/81$, a radius-$\sqrt{V/\pi}$ tube about a shortest closed geodesic when $4\pi/81\le V\le1/\pi$, or a coordinate-torus slab of width $V$ when $1/\pi\le V\le1/2$. Adjacent types both minimize at common endpoints; no other equality types occur. Larger-volume minimizers are complements.

Outline of the argument

Cubic three-torus isoperimetry: A reduction leaves nonstandard competitors to be excluded at two critical volumes. Geometric pattern classification and slice inequalities eliminate the three- and four-vertex possibilities except one parameterized case. Nested-area estimates reduce that case to positivity of an explicitly checked scalar expression, and the two-volume reduction recovers every volume and equality case.
