Math-4041

Class-Breadth Inequalities for Odd-Prime Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $G$ be a finite group of order $p^n$, where $n\geq 0$. For $g\in G$, define its conjugacy class and breadth by \[ g^G=\{x^{-1}gx:x\in G\},\qquad p^{\operatorname{br}(g)}=|g^G|. \] Thus $\operatorname{br}(g)$ is a nonnegative integer. Define the breadth of $G$ by \[ \operatorname{br}(G)=\max_{g\in G}\operatorname{br}(g). \] For subgroups $H,K\leq G$, let $[H,K]$ denote the subgroup generated by all commutators $[h,k]=h^{-1}k^{-1}hk$, with $h\in H$ and $k\in K$. Define the lower central series by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_i(G),G]$. The nilpotency class $\operatorname{cl}(G)$ is the least integer $c\geq 0$ such that $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=\{1\}$; in particular, $\operatorname{cl}(\{1\})=0$.

Question. Is it true that, for every odd prime $p$ and every finite $p$-group $G$, \[ \operatorname{cl}(G)\leq\operatorname{br}(G)+1? \] The supplied registry labels this question open. Its dated curation reports counterexamples for $p=2$ and an unresolved odd-prime case; these literature claims have not been independently verified here. The displayed question follows the supplied corrected statement. The supplied original excerpt does not itself display the inequality, so its reconstruction depends on the registry's correction and verification claim.

Short English statement

For every finite group whose order is a power of an odd prime $p$, must the nilpotency class be at most one more than the largest exponent of $p$ occurring as a conjugacy-class size? The supplied record labels this question open; this is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
