Why raw tensor modes contain false instabilities.
Write the geometric Einstein operator as $\mathcal E(g)=\operatorname{Ric}(g)+\Lambda g$ in the convention of the source. For any smooth diffeomorphism $\Phi$, tensor covariance gives $\mathcal E(\Phi^*g)=\Phi^*\mathcal E(g)$. If $\mathcal E(g)=0$ and $\Phi_s$ is the local flow of a vector field $X$, differentiating at $s=0$ gives \[ D\mathcal E_g(\mathcal L_Xg)=\mathcal L_X\mathcal E(g)=0. \] Thus every infinitesimal coordinate change supplies a linearized tensor solution.
Separated growing gauge fields.
On a stationary coordinate patch choose $X=e^{\sigma t}Y(x)$, $\sigma>0$, with smooth spatially supported $Y$. Since the background is time independent, $\mathcal L_Xg$ is also proportional to $e^{\sigma t}$ and generically nonzero. Taking the spatial support away from horizon and asymptotic boundaries avoids artificial local boundary singularities. This produces apparent growing solutions of the ungauged coordinate equation, but they represent zero geometric perturbation after quotienting by infinitesimal diffeomorphisms.
