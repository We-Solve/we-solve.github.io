Math-6663

Attainable Zero Counts of Nonnegative Bivariate Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a real polynomial $P\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]$, let \[ Z_{{\mathbb{R}}}(P)=\{(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:P(a,b)=0\}. \] The polynomial is globally nonnegative if $P(a,b)\geq0$ for every $(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$. Its real zeros are isolated if, for every $z\in Z_{{\mathbb{R}}}(P)$, there exists $r>0$ such that \[ Z_{{\mathbb{R}}}(P)\cap\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:\|w-z\|_2<r\}=\{z\}. \] Zeros are counted as distinct points, without multiplicity.

For a degree parameter $n$, let $\mathcal P_n$ denote the intended class of globally nonnegative real bivariate polynomials of degree $n$ with isolated real zeros. The supplied record does not specify the degree convention or the admissible values of $n$; these remain unresolved parts of this definition.

The record asserts an upper bound $\#Z_{{\mathbb{R}}}(P)\leq n^2$ and reports examples with at least $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ real zeros. These are supplied source claims, not independently verified bounds. The literal attainability question is: for each admissible $n$, is it true that \[ \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad \left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor\leq k\leq n^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists P\in\mathcal P_n\text{ such that }\#Z_{{\mathbb{R}}}(P)=k? \] The interval is read inclusively. A definitive mathematical formulation requires confirmation of the intended polynomial class, degree normalization, admissible degrees, and numerical bounds from the report. In particular, the existence of examples with at least a given number of zeros does not by itself establish attainability of that exact endpoint.

Short English statement

For each intended degree $n$, ask whether every integer from $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ through $n^2$ occurs as the number of distinct isolated real zeros of a globally nonnegative real bivariate polynomial in the intended class. The degree convention, admissible degrees, and stated bounds require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
