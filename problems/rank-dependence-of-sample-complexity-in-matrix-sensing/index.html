Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Is the quadratic dependence of matrix-sensing sample complexity $m$ on the rank $r_\star$ necessary, or is it an artifact of the proof?

The source algorithm and later context

The primary report, printed pages3017--3018, concerns Gaussian matrix measurements and factorized gradient descent from a small Gaussian initialization, including overparameterized factors. The question concerns the rank dependence in that analysis. A later Stoger--Zhu primary abstract reports linear rank dependence with spectral initialization, and the March2026 Li--Huang abstract reports improved sampling for scaled gradient descent. Those abstracts were inspected, not their complete proofs; their initialization or algorithm cannot be silently substituted for the original one. We attempt an information-counting argument, then check what it fails to say about the original dynamics.

An elementary linear-rank necessary scale

Let $\mathcal A$ be a linear map from symmetric $n$ by $n$ matrices to $\mathbb R^m$, required to distinguish every pair of rank-$r$ PSD matrices, where $1\le r\le n$. First restrict matrices to a fixed $r$-dimensional coordinate subspace. If $m<r(r+1)/2$, linear algebra gives a nonzero symmetric $r$ by $r$ matrix $H$ in the kernel of that restriction. For small enough $t>0$, both $I_r+tH$ and $I_r-tH$ are positive definite: $x^T(I_r\pm tH)x\ge(1-t\|H\|_{\rm op})\|x\|^2>0$. Embedding them into the fixed subspace gives two distinct rank-$r$ PSD matrices with equal measurements. Thus $m\ge r(r+1)/2$ is necessary.

A second bound is $m\ge r(n-r)$. If it fails, there is a nonzero $(n-r)$ by $r$ matrix $Z$ such that \[ \mathcal A\begin{pmatrix}0&Z^T\\Z&0\end{pmatrix}=0. \] But the distinct matrices \[ X_\pm=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&\pm Z^T\\\pm Z&ZZ^T\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}I_r\\\pm Z\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}I_r&\pm Z^T\end{pmatrix} \] are both PSD of rank $r$, and their difference is twice the kernel matrix. Therefore every such injective measurement map obeys \[ m\ge\max\{r(n-r),r(r+1)/2\}\ge nr/3. \] For the last bound, if $r\le2n/3$ use $n-r\ge n/3$; otherwise use $(r+1)/2\ge n/3$. This is an information-theoretic necessary scale, not a sample guarantee for any random ensemble.

An explicit linear-rank recovery patch

Suppose a rank-$r$ PSD target has block form \[ X=\begin{pmatrix}B&C^T\\C&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad B\succ0, \quad B\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}. \] Measure every entry of symmetric $B$ and every entry of $C$. This uses $r(r+1)/2+(n-r)r=nr-r(r-1)/2$ scalar linear measurements. They determine the whole matrix by \[ D=CB^{-1}C^T. \] To prove the identity, multiplication on the left and right by invertible block triangular matrices gives a congruence from $X$ to $\operatorname{diag}(B,D-CB^{-1}C^T)$. Congruence preserves PSD and rank. Since $B$ already has rank $r$ and $X$ has rank $r$, the second block has rank zero and is zero. This is a complete recovery algorithm on the specified patch.

The patch assumption cannot be dropped. If $r<n$, a nonzero PSD matrix supported in the unmeasured lower-right block has $B=C=0$ and is invisible to all these measurements. Also, $B^{-1}$ can be ill-conditioned, so the noiseless identity supplies no uniform stability bound. The construction shows why a universal information-theoretic demand of order $nr^2$ cannot be inferred just from a count of target parameters; it is not Gaussian sensing or the source gradient algorithm.

A dynamics check even with full observations

With full Frobenius observations the loss is $\tfrac14\|UU^T-X_*\|_{\rm F}^2$ and its gradient is $(UU^T-X_*)U$, as expansion of the directional derivative and the trace cyclic identity show. For diagonal $X_*=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)$, $\lambda_i\ge0$, and $U_0=\alpha I_n$, the iterate stays diagonal and each diagonal entry satisfies \[ v_{t+1}=v_t\{1+\mu(\lambda-v_t^2)\}. \] If $\lambda>0$, $0<\alpha\le\sqrt\lambda$, and $0<\mu\lambda\le1/2$, this scalar iteration increases to $\sqrt\lambda$. Indeed its map has derivative $1+\mu\lambda-3\mu v^2\ge1-2\mu\lambda\ge0$ on $[0,\sqrt\lambda]$, fixes the upper endpoint, and exceeds $v$ below it. Monotone bounded iterates converge, and their positive limit solves $\lambda-v^2=0$. For $\lambda=0$ and $0<\mu\alpha^2<1$, the iterates remain positive and decrease. Moreover \[ \frac1{v_{t+1}^2} =\frac1{v_t^2}(1-\mu v_t^2)^{-2} \ge\frac1{v_t^2}+2\mu, \qquad v_t^2\le(\alpha^{-2}+2\mu t)^{-1}, \] using $(1-z)^{-2}\ge1+2z$ for $0\le z<1$, obtained by multiplying by $(1-z)^2$ and simplifying or differentiating. Thus the zero-target coordinates converge to zero as well.

Even this favourable example retains initialization dependence. For $\lambda>0$, always $v_t\le\alpha(1+\mu\lambda)^t$ along the positive iteration. Reaching $v_t\ge\sqrt\lambda/2$ therefore requires \[ t\ge\frac{\log(\sqrt\lambda/(2\alpha))}{\log(1+\mu\lambda)} \] when the numerator is positive. At exactly $\alpha=0$ the iterate is stationary forever. These calculations do not model Gaussian random shapes, but they show why an information count alone cannot replace trajectory and initialization analysis.
