Numerical Convergence for General Monge–Ampère Boundary Problems

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for a convergence theory for numerical approximations of viscosity solutions of Monge--Ampère boundary problems. It does not specify a class of equations, boundary conditions, or numerical schemes sufficient to formulate a single quantified convergence theorem.

A schematic representation of the intended boundary problem is \[ F(x,u(x),Du(x),D^2u(x))=0\quad\text{in }\Omega, \qquad \mathcal B[u]=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega, \] where $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ denotes an as yet unspecified domain, $F$ an as yet unspecified Monge--Ampère type operator, and $\mathcal B$ unspecified boundary data or a boundary constraint. This display is a template, not a specification of the admissible operators or boundary conditions. In particular, the elliptic branch and the admissible solution class have not been supplied.

For a specified continuous degenerate elliptic operator $F$, the usual interior viscosity interpretation tests a continuous function $u$ against $C^2$ functions $\phi$. With the sign convention that $F$ is nonincreasing in its symmetric matrix argument, a local maximum of $u-\phi$ at an interior point $x$ requires \[ F(x,u(x),D\phi(x),D^2\phi(x))\leq 0, \] and a local minimum requires the reverse inequality. A viscosity solution satisfies both requirements. Applying this definition to the intended Monge--Ampère problems requires specifying the operator, its elliptic interpretation, any convexity or other admissibility restriction, and the treatment of boundary conditions; the record supplies none of these choices.

In the classical differentiable convex potential setting, the second boundary-value condition prescribes the gradient image, \[ Du(\Omega)=\Omega^*, \] for a prescribed target domain $\Omega^*\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$. This explains the terminology in a standard setting only. The record does not specify source and target domains, their regularity or geometry, or the interpretation of this condition for nonsmooth potentials or more general transport costs.

Requested research objective. Establish hypotheses on more general Monge--Ampère equations, boundary conditions, and numerical schemes under which the numerical approximations converge to viscosity solutions, including problems with the second boundary-value condition. For a discretization parameter $h\to0$, the intended conclusion has the schematic form \[ u_h\longrightarrow u, \qquad u\text{ solves the specified boundary problem in the specified viscosity sense}. \] The admissible data, quantifiers over schemes, reconstruction of discrete approximations, convergence topology, and whether convergence concerns the whole family or subsequences remain unspecified. Thus this is a statement of a research objective with unresolved formulation requirements, not a complete convergence theorem.

The supplied dated assessment labels the problem partially solved: it reports a convergence framework for the quadratic-cost second boundary-value problem and several discretizations, while describing the corresponding theory for fully general equations and boundary data as remaining open. This is supplied curation, not an independently verified mathematical or literature conclusion.

Short English statement

Develop a convergence theory showing when numerical approximations of more general Monge--Ampère boundary problems approach viscosity solutions, including the second boundary-value problem. The source leaves the equation class, boundary interpretation, numerical schemes, and convergence notion unspecified. Its supplied partially solved label and reported quadratic-cost progress require independent review.
