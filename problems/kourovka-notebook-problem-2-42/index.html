Definitions and mathematical statement

For a fixed finitary algebraic signature $L$, a quasi-identity is a universally quantified implication \[ \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\;\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}s_i(\mathbf{x})=t_i(\mathbf{x})\;\Longrightarrow\;s(\mathbf{x})=t(\mathbf{x})\right), \] where all displayed expressions are $L$-terms and $m\geq 0$. The empty conjunction is interpreted as true. In the usual universal-algebraic terminology, a quasivariety is a class of algebras axiomatized by a set of quasi-identities. Once an ambient class $\mathcal U$ and its signature have been specified, the corresponding relative notion is \[ \mathcal Q_L(\mathcal U)=\left\{\{A\in\mathcal U:A\models\Sigma\}:\Sigma\text{ is a set of }L\text{-quasi-identities}\right\}. \] This notation supplies a possible object collection; it does not supply a groupoid structure.

Source question (A. I. Mal'cev, 1967). Determine the structure of the groupoid of quasivarieties in each of the following cases:

  1. (a) quasivarieties of semigroups;
  2. (b) quasivarieties of rings;
  3. (c) quasivarieties of associative rings.
A semigroup has an associative binary multiplication. The source does not specify the ring signatures or conventions, including whether rings have a multiplicative identity and, if so, whether that identity is a named constant. The separate listing of rings and associative rings suggests that part (b) may permit nonassociative multiplication, but this interpretation is not explicitly supplied.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied record does not define its use of groupoid. In the binary-operation sense, the operation on quasivarieties is missing; in the categorical sense, the morphisms and composition are missing. The record also does not explicitly resolve the absolute versus relative meaning of quasivariety or conventions concerning empty algebras. Consequently, the three source questions can be preserved, but a uniquely specified mathematical structure to be determined cannot be reconstructed from the supplied evidence alone.

Short English statement

Mal'cev asks for the structure of a groupoid formed from quasivarieties of semigroups, rings, and associative rings. The supplied text leaves the groupoid structure and some algebraic conventions undefined, so the intended classification target requires clarification from the cited sources.
