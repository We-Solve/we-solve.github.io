Math-7333

Weighted Min–Max Theorems for Disjoint Terminal Paths

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an undirected graph, let $S\subseteq V$ be its set of terminals, and let $w:\binom{S}{2}\to{\mathbb{N}}$, where $\binom{S}{2}=\{\{s,t\}:s,t\in S,\ s\ne t\}$. The supplied record does not specify whether $0\in{\mathbb{N}}$.

For a conventional interpretation of the optimization problem, assume that $G$ is finite. Interpret an $S$-path as a simple path with two distinct endpoints in $S$ and all internal vertices in $V\setminus S$. Write $s(P),t(P)$ for its endpoints. A family $\mathcal P$ of such paths is node-disjoint if $V(P)\cap V(Q)=\varnothing$ for every two distinct members $P,Q\in\mathcal P$, including their endpoints. Define \[ \nu_w(G,S)=\max_{\mathcal P}\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}w\left(\{s(P),t(P)\}\right), \] where the maximum ranges over all node-disjoint families of $S$-paths and the empty family has weight $0$. Finiteness and these path conventions are explicit interpretive assumptions; the supplied statement does not itself specify them.

Recorded question. For every such input $(G,S,w)$, does $\nu_w(G,S)$ admit a Mader-type min--max characterization, and can an optimal family be found in polynomial time?

Short English statement

The recorded question asks whether the greatest total endpoint-pair weight of vertex-disjoint terminal paths has a Mader-type min--max formula and a polynomial-time algorithm. Under explicitly stated conventional path assumptions, the objective is defined above. The intended weighted theorem remains incomplete because the supplied assessment reports a truncated extraction and leaves the minimum side and possible weight restrictions unspecified.
