Counting-hierarchy upper bound for existential-universal real sentences

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 141; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that existential real feasibility, and more generally existential--universal real sentences with circuit-given integer polynomials, lie in a fixed counting-hierarchy level.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $\mathcal T_{\exists\forall}$ consist of true real sentences \[ \exists x\in\mathbb R^r\ \forall y\in\mathbb R^s:\Phi(x,y), \] where $r,s\ge0$, $\Phi$ is an explicitly encoded Boolean combination of $p=0$ and $p>0$, and each $p$ is an acyclic arithmetic circuit with binary integer constants and $+,-,\times$ gates, with no division or exponentiation gates. Malformed encodings are rejected. Define $\mathsf C_0=\mathrm P$, $\mathsf C_{j+1}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathsf C_j}\mathrm{PP}^A$. The claim is \[ \exists j\in\mathbb N:\quad\mathcal T_{\exists\forall}\in\mathsf C_j, \] with one absolute level independent of input size, variable counts, degrees and coefficient sizes. These are uniform bit-time complexity classes.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 141; 2026-10-04), critical-point deformation turns quantified fibers into short algebraic witnesses.
