Math-7140

Equivalence of Graphs in Strong Ramsey Games

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not define $q$-equivalence or the graph sum $+$. The following is a conditional formulation using the conventional Ramsey interpretation; these conventions require confirmation against the cited report.

Work with finite simple undirected graphs. For an integer $q\ge 2$, write $G\to_q H$ if every edge-colouring $c:E(G)\to\{1,\ldots,q\}$ contains a monochromatic subgraph isomorphic to $H$. Copies are not required to be induced, and the colouring need not use every colour. Define \[ H\sim_q H'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{for every finite simple graph }G,\quad (G\to_q H\iff G\to_q H'). \] Let $K_k$ denote the complete graph on $k$ vertices, let $K_{1,t}$ denote the star with $t$ leaves and hence $t+1$ vertices, and interpret $H+H'$ as the disjoint union of $H$ and $H'$. A tree is a nonempty connected graph with no cycle.

For a positive integer $k$, define the candidate sets \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal S_k&=\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}:K_k\sim_2(K_k+K_{1,t})\},\\ \mathcal T_k&=\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}:\text{there exists a tree }T\text{ with }|V(T)|=t \text{ and }K_k\sim_2(K_k+T)\}. \end{aligned} \] Determine the largest elements of $\mathcal S_k$ and $\mathcal T_k$, as functions of $k$, wherever such largest elements exist. The two functions requested by the source are separate functions, although the source denotes both by $t(k)$. If either set is empty or unbounded, its requested largest element does not exist; the supplied record does not specify a convention for those cases.

Furthermore, determine whether \[ \forall q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge2}\ \exists H,H'\quad \left(H,H'\text{ are connected finite simple graphs},\quad H\not\cong H',\quad H\sim_q H'\right). \] Here the graphs may depend on $q$.

The positive-integer ranges above specify a provisional rendering: the source gives no lower bound on $k$, does not state whether $t=0$ is allowed for the star question, and does not address degenerate cases. Its title refers to strong Ramsey games, whereas its statement supplies no game rules or game-based definition of equivalence. Consequently, the edge-colouring formulation above must be reviewed before it is treated as the intended statement.

Short English statement

Under the provisional interpretation of equivalence as having exactly the same $q$-colour Ramsey graphs, determine how large a disjoint star, or some disjoint tree, can be added to $K_k$ without changing its two-colour Ramsey graphs. Also ask whether, for every integer $q\ge2$, two non-isomorphic connected graphs can have exactly the same $q$-colour Ramsey graphs. The equivalence convention and parameter boundaries require source confirmation.
