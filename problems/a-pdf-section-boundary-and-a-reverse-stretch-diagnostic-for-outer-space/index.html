The text attributes to Juan Souto a discussion comparing Teichmüller and Weil--Petersson geodesic analogies with Lipschitz geodesics in Outer Space, and announces a multipart question that is not contained here. The aid gives a distinct mathematical supplement about reverse costs. Let $d$ be an asymmetric metric, let $\gamma:I\to X$ satisfy $d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=t-s$ for $s<t$, and define \[ d_{\max}(x,y)=\max\{d(x,y),d(y,x)\},\qquad d_{\Sigma}(x,y)=d(x,y)+d(y,x). \] A quasi-geodesic for one of these symmetrizations means constants $K\ge1,C\ge0$ give the usual two-sided linear distance bounds in $|t-s|$. The aid's equivalence is that this unit forward path is a quasi-geodesic for either symmetrization exactly when \[ \exists A,B\ge0\ \forall s<t\text{ in }I,\qquad d(\gamma(t),\gamma(s))\le A(t-s)+B. \] The forward distance already gives the lower quasi-geodesic bound; the reverse-cost condition supplies the missing upper bound. Constants must be uniform over all pairs of times.

For volume-one marked Outer Space $\mathrm{CV}_N$, $N\ge2$, points are finite marked metric graphs with fundamental group $F_N$. Let $\ell_X(\alpha)$ be the length of a conjugacy class in $X$, and let $d_L(X,Y)$ be the logarithm of the optimal marking-preserving Lipschitz constant. The finite-candidate length-ratio formula gives, with $X=\gamma(s)$, $Y=\gamma(t)$, \[ d_L(Y,X)=\log\max_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Cand}(Y)}\frac{\ell_X(\alpha)}{\ell_Y(\alpha)}. \] The finite source-graph candidate family consists of the standard simple-loop, figure-eight and barbell types. Thus reverse-cost control is equivalent to \[ \ell_{\gamma(s)}(\alpha)\le e^{A(t-s)+B}\ell_{\gamma(t)}(\alpha) \quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\operatorname{Cand}(\gamma(t)),\quad s<t. \] Candidates are taken at the later endpoint, which is the source of the reverse comparison, not automatically at the earlier endpoint.

The aid's sufficient budget criterion combines thick-part quasi-symmetry with control on thin pieces. On a fixed thick part, shortest nontrivial loop lengths have a uniform positive lower bound and known quasi-symmetry bounds reverse cost by a uniform multiple of forward duration. If each interval of the path can be subdivided so that the remaining thin pieces have aggregate reverse cost at most $A(t-s)+B$, uniformly over intervals, the asymmetric triangle inequality gives a linear global reverse budget. An additive allowance on each separate thin piece without controlling their aggregate is not the same hypothesis.

The rank-two thin-rose warning is explicit in compatible coordinates: take the same marking on roses with loop lengths $(1/2,1/2)$ and $(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)$, $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Their forward distance from the balanced rose to the thin rose is $\log(2(1-\varepsilon))<\log2$, while the reverse distance is $\log(1/(2\varepsilon))\to\infty$. Bounded forward duration alone therefore cannot provide uniform reverse control. This supplement is not the missing multipart source problem.

Short English statement

The stored text is an introduction at a PDF section boundary, with no complete question. The aid's separate supplement identifies the extra condition needed for a forward Lipschitz geodesic to remain a quasi-geodesic after symmetrization: a uniform linear reverse-cost bound, equivalently exponential reverse candidate-length bounds. It does not reconstruct the absent multipart problem.
