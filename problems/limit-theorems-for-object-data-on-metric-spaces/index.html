Limit Theorems for Object Data on Metric Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(S,d)$ be a metric space, and let $\mathcal B(S)$ denote its Borel $\sigma$-algebra. A metric measure space additionally carries a measure $\mu$; the supplied record does not specify its normalization or its relationship to the sampling law. For observations $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ taking values in $S$, define their empirical probability measure by \[ P_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}, \] where $\delta_x$ is the unit point mass at $x$.

Problem as supplied. For object-valued observations in a metric measure space, specify an appropriate population mean and its empirical counterpart, and determine hypotheses under which the empirical mean satisfies a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem.

For example, if the intended construction assigns a unique point $m(P)\in S$ to a probability law $P$, and $m(P_n)$ is defined, the consistency question can be expressed schematically as \[ d\left(m(P_n),m(P)\right)\longrightarrow 0 \qquad(n\longrightarrow\infty), \] with the mode of convergence and sampling assumptions still to be specified. This is a conditional template, not an identification of the source's intended mean or a claimed theorem.

Unresolved qualifications. The source extract does not identify the antecedent of "it," define a mean, or specify whether a mean is unique or set-valued. It supplies no independence, identical-distribution, moment, measurability, geometric, or selection hypotheses. It also does not specify the normalization, representation of fluctuations, limiting distribution, or topology needed for a central limit theorem. In a general metric space, subtraction of mean points has no prescribed meaning. Consequently, the supplied evidence supports this research question but does not determine a unique, fully quantified limit-theorem conjecture.

Short English statement

The problem asks how to define a mean for data taking values in a metric space, and when empirical means obey a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem. The supplied extract leaves the intended mean, sampling assumptions, convergence mode, and fluctuation framework unspecified, so a precise theorem requires further source clarification.
