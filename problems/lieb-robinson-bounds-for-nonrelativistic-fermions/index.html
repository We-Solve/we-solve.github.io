Testing a state-independent propagation cone.
In units where the one-particle Hamiltonian is $-\Delta$, let $\psi_0(0,\cdot)$ be normalized, smooth and compactly supported, and let $\psi_0(t,x)$ solve $i\partial_t\psi_0=-\Delta\psi_0$. For any momentum $p\in\mathbb R^d$, direct differentiation verifies \[ \psi_p(t,x)=e^{i(p\cdot x-|p|^2t)}\psi_0(t,x-2pt). \] All initial spatial densities agree, but their centers translate at velocities $2p$. For a fixed $t>0$, choose a bounded region containing most of $|\psi_0(t)|^2$; its translate by $2pt$ contains the same mass of $\psi_p(t)$. Arbitrarily large $p$ therefore defeats any state-uniform finite-speed localization claim with a fixed nontrivial tail tolerance.
What energy control buys.
With Fourier transform normalized unitarily, a state satisfying $\int |k|^2|\widehat\psi(k)|^2dk\leq K$ has \[ \int_{|k|>P}|\widehat\psi(k)|^2dk\leq K/P^2. \] This follows by $1\leq |k|^2/P^2$ on the integration region. It permits a velocity-tail decomposition, but any resulting estimate depends on a controlled kinetic moment. In a fermionic many-particle state the moment and local occupation must be compared with the high-density scaling.
