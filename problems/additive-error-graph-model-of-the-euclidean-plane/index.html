Math-4262

Additive-error graph model of the Euclidean plane

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a connected graph whose edges have unit length, and let $d_G$ be shortest-path distance. A map $f:V\to\mathbb R^2$ is an additive-error rough isometry if some $C<\infty$ satisfies \[\left|d_G(x,y)-\|f(x)-f(y)\|_2\right|<C\quad(x,y\in V),\] and every point of $\mathbb R^2$ lies at Euclidean distance less than $C$ from $f(V)$. A bounded-degree graph has $\sup_{v\in V}\deg(v)<\infty$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does such a graph and additive-error rough isometry exist? Can $G$ in such an example have bounded degree? [S2]

Short English statement

Can the whole Euclidean plane be represented by a unit-edge graph with uniformly bounded additive distance error, including with bounded degrees?
