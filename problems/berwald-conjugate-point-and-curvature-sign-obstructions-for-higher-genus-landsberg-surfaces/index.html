Math-1469

Berwald, conjugate-point, and curvature-sign obstructions for higher-genus Landsberg surfaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a closed oriented surface of genus at least two. A regular smooth strongly convex Finsler metric is a function $F:TS\to[0,\infty)$, positive off the zero section, smooth there, positively homogeneous of degree one, with positive-definite fundamental tensor \[ g_{ij}(x,y)=\frac12\frac{\partial^2F^2}{\partial y^i\partial y^j}(x,y). \] It is Riemannian precisely when this tensor is independent of $y$, equivalently its Cartan tensor $C_{ijk}=\tfrac12\partial g_{ij}/\partial y^k$ vanishes. Using the Chern horizontal covariant derivative, the Landsberg condition is \[ L_{ijk}=C_{ijk\mid s}y^s=0\quad\text{on }TS\setminus0. \] Question $10.1.4$ asks whether such a surface admits a non-Riemannian Landsberg metric. The closed oriented regular strongly convex setting is the aid's explicit interpretation of the shorter source sentence; generalized singular Finsler structures are not included.

The source refers to [Bao2007] for the Landsberg condition and explains that it supplies a geodesic-flow integral, motivating its placement in the integrability section. It cites [Gom-Rug2013] for a negative answer under absence of conjugate points. The supplied aid combines these and further restrictions into the following necessary conditions for any positive example:

  1. $F$ is non-Berwald. Berwald means that its geodesic spray coefficients are quadratic in the tangent variable, or equivalently that its Berwald curvature vanishes. Thus an example would be a regular non-Berwald Landsberg metric, often called a regular unicorn.
  2. $F$ is geodesically complete, in both time directions, as a regular metric on a closed manifold; no reversibility assumption $F(x,y)=F(x,-y)$ is made.
  3. $F$ has conjugate points, by the reported no-conjugate-points obstruction.
  4. If $K(p,v)$ is its surface flag curvature, there exist base points $p_+,p_-\in S$ such that \[ K(p_+,v)>0\quad\text{for every }v\in T_{p_+}S\setminus0, \qquad K(p_-,v)<0\quad\text{for every }v\in T_{p_-}S\setminus0. \] The all-directions quantifiers are stronger than merely finding two tangent directions with opposite signs.
These are reported obstructions for sufficiently smooth regular metrics, not an existence construction or a proof that the general question has a negative answer. The exact minimal differentiability and full theorem inputs for the basewise curvature assertion are not supplied. The extracted page header and the following Section $10.2$ heading, "Integrable geodesic flows with integrals of higher degree," are neighboring material rather than another question here.

Short English statement

The source asks for a regular non-Riemannian Landsberg metric on a higher-genus surface. The aid says any example must be non-Berwald, complete without reversibility, and have conjugate points. It also requires base points with flag curvature positive in every direction and negative in every direction, respectively; no example is supplied.
