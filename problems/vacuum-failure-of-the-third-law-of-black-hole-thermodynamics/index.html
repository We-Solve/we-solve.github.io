Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

In units $G=c=1$, a Kerr black hole has mass $M$ and angular momentum $J$, with rotation parameter $a=J/M$. Subextremal means $|a|<M$ and extremal means $|a|=M$. An apparent horizon is defined on a spatial slice by trapped-surface geometry; an event horizon is defined globally by visibility from future null infinity. Vacuum means $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.

Extremality as a parameter test.
For positive Kerr mass and nonnegative angular momentum define the deficit $D=M^2-J$. Subextremality means $D>0$; extremality means $D=0$. A finite change satisfying the illustrative relation $\delta J=2M\delta M$ gives \[ D_{\rm final}=D_{\rm initial}+2M\delta M+(\delta M)^2-\delta J =D_{\rm initial}+(\delta M)^2. \] Thus saturating this linear relation is insufficient to reach the nonlinear extremal surface from positive deficit. The relation is an algebraic test, not a vacuum gravitational-wave flux law.
Conversely, a parameter curve is too easy.
The curve $M(t)=1$, $J(t)=1-(1-t)^2$, $0\leq t\leq1$, stays subextremal before reaching extremality at finite parameter time. It satisfies the parameter inequality but does not define a spacetime metric or solve the vacuum constraints. Substituting variable parameters into the stationary Kerr metric is not justified by the stationary solution family.
