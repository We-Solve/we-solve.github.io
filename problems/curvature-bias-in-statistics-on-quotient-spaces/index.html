Curvature Bias in Statistics on Quotient Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a space with an equivalence relation $\sim$. Its quotient is $Q=X/{\sim}=\{[x]:x\in X\}$, where $[x]=\{y\in X:y\sim x\}$. The source does not specify $X$, the equivalence relation, a metric, a statistical model, or an estimator class.

For clarity, if a metric $d$ on $Q$ and a Borel probability measure $P$ satisfying $\int_Q d(q_0,y)^2\,P({\,\mathrm{d}} y)<\infty$ for some $q_0\in Q$ are specified, the Fréchet functional and its mean set are \[ F_P(q)=\int_Q d(q,y)^2\,P({\,\mathrm{d}} y),\qquad \mathcal M(P)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{q\in Q}F_P(q). \] This conditional definition explains the reference to a mean shape; it does not supply a metric or model omitted by the source. Neither existence nor uniqueness of a Fréchet mean is assumed. The source also does not specify whether its mean shape is a population mean, an empirical mean, or another estimator.

Problem as supplied. For statistical estimators on quotient spaces, determine:

  1. the geometric and statistical conditions under which curvature-induced bias occurs;
  2. whether this bias is confined to estimation of the mean shape or also affects other statistical analyses;
  3. its magnitude and practical importance in applications; and
  4. when correction is warranted and how such correction should be performed.

Unresolved formulation requirements. This is a research programme rather than a single fully quantified proposition. A precise instance must specify the quotient geometry and relevant notion of curvature, the data-generating model, the estimators and their targets, and the meaning of bias. In a general quotient space, subtraction and hence a vector expression such as $\mathbb E[\widehat\theta]-\theta$ need not be defined. The source does not choose an alternative definition, an asymptotic regime, a class of analyses beyond the mean, application-specific error scales, or a criterion for deciding whether correction is beneficial. No universal quantification over all quotient spaces or all estimators is asserted here.

Short English statement

The problem asks when quotient-space geometry biases statistical estimators, whether the effect extends beyond mean shapes, how much it matters in applications, and when and how to correct it. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved, but leaves the geometry, statistical model, definition of bias, and correction criterion unspecified.
