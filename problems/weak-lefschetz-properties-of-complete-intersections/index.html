Math-876

Weak Lefschetz Properties of Complete Intersections

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $r\geq 4$ be an integer. Give $S=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ its standard grading, with $\deg x_j=1$. Under the usual graded Artinian interpretation of the supplied question, consider \[ A=S/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}A_d, \] where the $f_j$ are homogeneous polynomials of positive degree forming a regular sequence, and $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$. Here regular sequence means that the generated ideal is proper and, for each $j$, the image of $f_j$ is a non-zero-divisor in $S/(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})$. Such an $A$ is a standard graded Artinian complete intersection.

The algebra $A$ has the weak Lefschetz property if there exists a single element $\ell\in A_1$ such that, for every integer $d\geq 0$, multiplication induces a map \[ \times\ell:A_d\longrightarrow A_{d+1} \] of maximal rank, meaning \[ \operatorname{rank}_k(\times\ell)=\min\{\dim_k A_d,\dim_k A_{d+1}\}. \] Question. For every field $k$ of characteristic zero, every integer $r\geq4$, and every homogeneous regular sequence as above, does $A$ have the weak Lefschetz property?

Qualification of the formulation. The supplied short statements omit characteristic, homogeneity, and Artinian hypotheses. The supplied literature assessment explicitly identifies the intended question as concerning characteristic-zero Artinian complete intersections; homogeneity and the standard grading are made explicit here to formulate the usual graded weak Lefschetz property. The source record does not explicitly specify the precise field scope or grading hypotheses, so these aspects require human confirmation against the report. The original extraction also asked what happens for more than $r+1$ forms. The supplied correction identifies that as a distinct question and removes it; it is not included in the quantified question above.

Short English statement

Does every standard graded Artinian complete intersection in at least four variables over a characteristic-zero field admit one linear form whose multiplication maps between all consecutive graded components have maximal rank? This formulation follows the supplied characteristic-zero Artinian qualification and makes the usual homogeneous grading explicit, subject to source review. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
