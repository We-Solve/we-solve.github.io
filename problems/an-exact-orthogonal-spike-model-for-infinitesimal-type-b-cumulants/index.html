An exact orthogonal-spike model for infinitesimal type-B cumulants

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

F. Goodman and P. Sniady's Problem 5.1 asks for a natural model of the type-B free probability introduced by Philippe Biane, Fred Goodman, and Alexandru Nica, and for analogues in other reflection types. Its historical assertion that no model was then known is not a current status claim. The accompanying remark stresses that usual free-probability formulas use the whole sequence $NC(A_{n-1})$, mentions Reiner's type-B multiplicative functions, and asks how an exceptional type without an infinite family could support the required all-arity structure.

The aid reports later identification of scalar first-order type-B content with infinitesimal free probability and finite-rank random-matrix perturbation models, without complete citations. An infinitesimal law here is $(\varphi,\varphi')$ with $\varphi$ a unital state and $\varphi'(1)=0$, arising as $\varphi_N=\varphi+N^{-1}\varphi'+o(N^{-1})$. The derivative $\varphi'$ need not itself be positive. Infinitesimal cumulants are derivatives of free cumulants; infinitesimal freeness means ordinary and first-order mixed free cumulants vanish. This specifies the scalar first-order interpretation, not every possible type-B bimodule construction.

The aid's explicit zero-bulk model fixes $r$ mutually orthogonal rank-one projections $P_{s,N}\in M_N({\mathbb{C}})$, $N\ge r$, real spike values $\theta_s$, and a partition of the spike labels into coordinate sets $I_j$. Put \[ A_{j,N}=\sum_{s\in I_j}\theta_sP_{s,N},\qquad\tau_N=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}. \] For every noncommutative polynomial $P$, the exact identity is \[ \tau_NP(A_{1,N},\ldots,A_{k,N}) =P(0)+\frac1N\sum_j\sum_{s\in I_j} \left(P(0,\ldots,\theta_s,\ldots,0)-P(0)\right), \] where the nonzero scalar slot is $j$. The families have ordinary zero laws; mixed words using different coordinates have zero first-order moments and cumulants. Thus they are infinitesimally free in this special model, although their finite matrices commute.

For one combined spike variable with all $r$ spikes, the empirical law and infinitesimal law are \[ \mu_N=(1-r/N)\delta_0+N^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^r\delta_{\theta_s},\quad \varphi=\delta_0,\quad\varphi'=\sum_s\delta_{\theta_s}-r\delta_0, \] and for every $n\ge1$, \[ \kappa_n'=\sum_{s=1}^r\theta_s^n. \] If $r$ is known, the first $r$ such cumulants determine the multiset of all spikes by Newton identities. Explicitly, for $p_j=\kappa_j'$, $e_0=1$, \[ ke_k=\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}e_{k-j}p_j, \] and the spikes are the roots, with multiplicity, of $z^r-e_1z^{r-1}+\cdots+(-1)^re_r$. The known-count hypothesis includes any zero spikes, which positive-order power sums alone cannot count if $r$ is unknown. Every finite-$N$ trace is a positive tracial state even though its first-order correction is signed. This special deterministic construction is not a universal random-matrix realization of all type-B laws, nor an answer for every exceptional type; the aid reports that broader all-arity issue unresolved.

Short English statement

The aid distinguishes the historical type-B modelling request from later infinitesimal interpretations. Its orthogonal-spike example gives an exact positive finite-matrix expansion, infinitesimal freeness, and power-sum cumulants recovering a known number of spikes. It does not resolve the all-arity exceptional-type question.
