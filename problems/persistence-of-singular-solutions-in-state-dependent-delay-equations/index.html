Math-15519

Persistence of Singular Solutions in State-Dependent Delay Equations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Consider the supplied family of state-dependent delay equations \[ \varepsilon\dot x(t)=g\left(x(t-r_1(x(t))),\ldots,x(t-r_m(x(t)))\right),\qquad \varepsilon>0, \] where $m$ is the number of delays, $x$ is the unknown state, $g$ is the prescribed response function, and each $r_i$ is a prescribed function of the current state. The equation is to hold at times for which the derivative and all delayed evaluations are defined. The source does not specify the state space, the time interval, the domains or regularity of these functions, or conditions ensuring that the delays are nonnegative and the delayed evaluations are available.

Formally setting $\varepsilon=0$ gives the outer constraint \[ 0=g\left(x(t-r_1(x(t))),\ldots,x(t-r_m(x(t)))\right). \] This formal constraint alone does not define the inner solutions or their matching to outer solutions.

The supplied persistence question is the following: given a singular solution $x_0$, composed of inner and outer solutions at $\varepsilon=0$, must there exist $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_0)$, there is a genuine solution $x_\varepsilon$ of the displayed delay equation corresponding to $x_0$? Here the requested correspondence is the persistence of the prescribed singular solution, rather than bare existence of an unrelated solution. However, the source supplies no definition of the singular composite, the inner equations, the matching conditions, or the approximation or convergence relation between $x_\varepsilon$ and $x_0$. It also supplies no initial history, boundary conditions, periodicity requirement, or intended solution class. Consequently this is a schematic persistence question requiring those specifications before it becomes a precise mathematical assertion.

Short English statement

Does a singular solution assembled from inner and outer pieces persist as a corresponding actual solution for every sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$ in the stated state-dependent delay equation? The supplied record leaves the hypotheses, matching conditions, and meaning of persistence unspecified.
