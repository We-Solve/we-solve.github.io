Statement

Abiogenesis hazard under observer selection

Domain. Origins of life; event-rate inference.   Retained status/source context. Open inference question with severe non-identifiability. One observed biosphere and its emergence time do not determine a universal abiogenesis probability.

Definitions and assumptions.
Specify a class of planetary chemical histories $E$, an operational emergence event $L$ (sustained compartmentalized heredity and selection under a stated assay), and a time window $[0,T]$. A finite-parameter model gives hazard $\lambda_\theta(t\mid E)$ per unit time. Earth supplies an interval for emergence time and observer-conditioned data; observer evolution requires a declared available time $t_o$. Independent laboratory trials sample restricted chemical environments and must not be assumed equivalent to entire planets. Prior assumptions and detection probabilities are explicit.
Problem statement.
Identify which rate functionals are constrained and estimate \[ P_\theta(L\mid E,T)=1-\exp\!\left[-\int_0^T\lambda_\theta(t\mid E)\,dt\right] \] with prior-sensitivity intervals. The formula assumes a first-event hazard; a constant-rate Poisson model is a special case, not an established law of life's emergence. Determine what additional evidence would narrow a declared probability functional by at least $\varepsilon$.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
The hazard formula is conditional on a specified chemical history and parameters, with survival up to each time included in the hazard definition. Random latent hazard histories require averaging the exponential, not exponentiating the mean integrated hazard. The observer-selection likelihood needs an explicit observation process. Laboratory environments constrain only the declared chemical submodel; planetary-rate inference additionally requires a separately justified scaling and environmental sampling law.
Alternative formulations.
(i) A laboratory formulation estimates the hazard of a specified hereditary chemical transition under repeatable conditions; it does not estimate all abiogenesis. (ii) A planetary hierarchical formulation models environment-specific hazards and observer-selection likelihoods. It may use astronomical non-detections with calibrated sensitivity. These become linked only through an explicit chemical-to-planet scaling model.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Spiegel and Turner show how emergence-time evidence and observer conditioning can leave strong prior dependence. Scharf and Cronin offer a heuristic accounting framework, not an empirical rate measurement. Malaterre and colleagues clarify that chemical possibility and actual Earth history are distinct explanatory aims. A broad posterior can be a correct result; asserting a precise probability from early emergence alone would overstate identification. Unlike Problem 299, this entry focuses on the event rate rather than its environmental map.

Probability laws and data.
Every expectation or probability refers to the specified stochastic law, including initial uncertainty, process noise, observation noise and sampling as appropriate. A deterministic equation is not a probability model without a declared random input or initial law. "Observed" targets in population-level formulas mean population observables $g_j^*$, not noisy estimates asserted to be exact. Vector/norm versions use corresponding valid simultaneous bounds. Training uncertainty must also be included when the claim averages over repeated training data. Evaluation on finitely many conditions does not establish a continuous-domain supremum without covering and regularity bounds.
Dynamics and well-posedness.
For ODEs and SDEs give coefficient domains, initial laws and existence/uniqueness conditions in the intended solution sense, or keep the entire admitted solution set. For stochastic jump models specify nonnegative off-diagonal rates, diagonal normalization, rate orientation and non-explosion. For PDEs specify spatial domains, boundary/initial conditions, conservation or source terms, and a weak or classical solution class. Moving-domain concentration equations include dilution/transport terms. A stationary law is used only for a time-homogeneous augmented model under existence assumptions; a stationary approximation to a driven system must be justified. Absorbing extinction states are retained. Survival-conditioned, quasi-stationary and unconditional laws are distinct and must not be substituted for one another.

Two parameter values are observationally equivalent when they induce the same full distribution under all allowed observation/intervention designs, not merely the same likelihood value for one sample. A prior or regularizer does not create data identification. Bounds intended to resolve a question must be informative at a predeclared width or decision threshold.

A causal contrast uses well-defined intervention versions and a stated causal model. Random assignment, or explicit consistency, overlap and exchangeability assumptions, is required for causal interpretation from observations. Interference and time-varying confounding are represented when present. Fixing a mediator by intervention differs from conditioning on its observed value. Marginal potential-outcome laws do not determine individual cross-world effects or an arbitrary joint coupling. Ethically admissible designs, rather than unconstrained interventions, define the experimental menu.

Attempted answer

Preliminary step: the following worked reduction has not been shown to instantiate all assumptions of the original target. Its applicability is an outstanding proof obligation.
Proposition: For a finite stochastic chemical-count model with an absorbing zero-replicator state, if every nonzero state can reach zero and there is no replenishment, eventual extinction occurs almost surely. A positive deterministic attractor does not remove this finite-state extinction conclusion.
Proof: Finiteness and reachability give a common finite horizon and positive lower bound on absorption probability from every transient state. Repeated application of the Markov property bounds nonabsorption by a geometric sequence tending to zero. ∎

What remains unresolved

Open reservoirs, growing state spaces, compartment reproduction or long quasi-stationary lifetimes can change the conclusion. This is a finite-model consistency check, not a reconstruction or chemical recipe for the origin of life.

Specify an observation map, parameter correspondence or dynamical reduction connecting this worked model to the original formulation; prove that correspondence under the original assumptions before claiming that the proposition resolves a clause of the target.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
