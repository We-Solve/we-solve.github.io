Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.29

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite group $H$, define its centre and its set of conjugacy class sizes by \[ Z(H)=\{z\in H:zh=hz\text{ for every }h\in H\},\qquad \operatorname{cs}(H)=\{|h^H|:h\in H\}, \] where $h^H=\{x^{-1}hx:x\in H\}$. Thus $\operatorname{cs}(H)$ is a set, with multiplicities discarded, and includes the size of the identity class. The group $H$ is centreless if $Z(H)=\{1\}$. A non-abelian finite simple group is a finite non-abelian group whose only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. For a positive integer $n$, let $S^n$ denote the direct product of $n$ copies of $S$.

The question is whether, for every non-abelian finite simple group $S$, every $n\in\mathbb N$, and every finite group $G$, \[ \left(Z(G)=\{1\}\ \text{and}\ \operatorname{cs}(G)=\operatorname{cs}(S^n)\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\cong S^n. \] The source does not specify whether $0\in\mathbb N$; if it is included, use the empty-product convention $S^0=\{1\}$.

The supplied record additionally reports that this is proved for $S=\operatorname{Alt}_5^2$, where $\operatorname{Alt}_5$ is the alternating group on five letters and the superscript denotes a direct square. This qualification is retained as supplied, but $\operatorname{Alt}_5^2$ is not simple and therefore does not satisfy the stated hypothesis on $S$. The intended special case cannot be determined from the record; in particular, it is not silently replaced by the case $S=\operatorname{Alt}_5$, $n=2$.

Short English statement

Does the set of conjugacy class sizes of a direct power of a non-abelian finite simple group determine that power up to isomorphism among centreless finite groups? The supplied special-case claim involving $S=\operatorname{Alt}_5^2$ conflicts with the simplicity hypothesis and requires clarification.
