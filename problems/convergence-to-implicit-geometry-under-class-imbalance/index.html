An orthogonal logistic model separates direction and rate

The source develops feature-learning geometries and asks about convergence under imbalance. We use a much smaller fixed-feature model whose gradient flow can be integrated exactly. Let $0<p<1$ be the fraction of one class. After multiplying each feature by its binary label, suppose the two types are $e_1$ and $e_2$. The logistic loss is \[ L(w)=p\log(1+e^{-w_1})+(1-p)\log(1+e^{-w_2}), \] and gradient flow starts at $w(0)=0$. Both types are separable; their different coefficients represent the class frequencies.
Proposition: Writing $p_1=p$, $p_2=1-p$, the exact trajectory satisfies \[ e^{w_j(t)}+w_j(t)=1+p_jt. \] For fixed $p\in(0,1)$, $w(t)/\|w(t)\|\to(1,1)/\sqrt2$. If $p\ne1/2$, the angle $\theta(t)$ to that direction satisfies \[ \tan\theta(t)\sim\frac{|\log(p/(1-p))|}{2\log t}. \]
Proof: Gradient flow gives $w'_j=p_j/(1+e^{w_j})$, so differentiating $e^{w_j}+w_j$ gives the constant $p_j$. Its initial value is one, proving the formula and uniqueness because $e^w+w$ is strictly increasing. Since $w_j\ge0$, the equation implies $w_j\le\log(1+p_jt)$. Therefore $w_j/t\to0$ and $e^{w_j}/(p_jt)=1+(1-w_j)/(p_jt)\to1$. Taking logarithms yields $w_j=\log t+\log p_j+o(1)$. The direction conclusion follows. The parallel and perpendicular coordinates relative to $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ are $(w_1+w_2)/\sqrt2$ and $(w_1-w_2)/\sqrt2$, so their absolute ratio is the tangent of the angle. Substitution proves the asymptotic when its numerator limit is nonzero. For $p=1/2$, the coordinates are identical for all $t$ and the direction is already balanced for $t>0$. ∎
The convergence is not uniform as the minority frequency goes to zero. Since $w'_j\le p_j$, reaching any minority margin $R>0$ needs at least $R/p_j$ time. Reweighting the two loss terms to have coefficients $1/2$ instead gives identical coordinate trajectories from this initialization, but that is a changed objective and does not prove improved population performance.

Verification and remaining gaps

The implicit equations were solved numerically by monotone bisection for several imbalances and times, verifying their residuals, angular relation and minority-margin bound. The limit takes time to infinity at fixed positive $p$; interchanging this with $p\downarrow0$ is unjustified. The model has fixed orthogonal features, not evolving embeddings or a SELI neural-collapse geometry. Thus a common limiting direction in this example does not establish architecture-independent geometry or rate.
