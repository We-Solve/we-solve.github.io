Math-4457

Exotic Group $C^*$-Algebras for Nonamenable Locally Compact Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a locally compact group, with the standard convention that locally compact groups are Hausdorff, and fix a left Haar measure ${\,\mathrm{d}} s$. No discreteness or countability hypothesis is supplied. The group $G$ is amenable if there exists a positive linear functional $m:L^\infty(G)\to{\mathbb{C}}$ such that $m(1)=1$ and $m(L_s f)=m(f)$ for every $s\in G$ and $f\in L^\infty(G)$, where $(L_s f)(t)=f(s^{-1}t)$. It is nonamenable if no such functional exists.

Equip $L^1(G)$ with its usual convolution and involution. For a strongly continuous unitary representation $\pi$ of $G$, write $\pi(f)=\int_G f(s)\pi(s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s$. Define the full and reduced group norms by \[ \|f\|_{\mathrm{u}}=\sup_{\pi}\|\pi(f)\|, \qquad \|f\|_{\mathrm{r}}=\|\lambda_G(f)\|, \] where the supremum ranges over strongly continuous unitary representations and $\lambda_G$ is the left regular representation on $L^2(G)$, given by $(\lambda_G(s)\xi)(t)=\xi(s^{-1}t)$. Their completions are the full group algebra $C^*(G)$ and the reduced group algebra $C^*_{\mathrm r}(G)$, respectively.

For this draft, an exotic group $C^*$-algebra means a completion $C^*_{\mu}(G)$ of $L^1(G)$ for a $C^*$-norm satisfying \[ \|f\|_{\mathrm r}\leq\|f\|_{\mu}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm u} \qquad(f\in L^1(G)), \] with $\|\cdot\|_{\mu}\neq\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm r}$ and $\|\cdot\|_{\mu}\neq\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm u}$. Equivalently, the canonical surjective homomorphisms \[ C^*(G)\longrightarrow C^*_{\mu}(G)\longrightarrow C^*_{\mathrm r}(G) \] are both noninjective. The two norm inequalities need not be strict on every element; each pair of norms must differ on some element.

Question. Does every nonamenable locally compact group $G$ admit such a norm $\|\cdot\|_{\mu}$, and hence an exotic group $C^*$-algebra?

Unresolved source qualification. The supplied record does not define "exotic" or specify additional requirements on the completion. Short English statement

Does every nonamenable locally compact group have a group $C^*$-completion strictly intermediate between its full and reduced completions, as measured by the canonical quotient maps? This uses the intermediate-completion meaning of "exotic"; the supplied record does not specify its definition or further hypotheses.
