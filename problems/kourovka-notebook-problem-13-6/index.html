Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is locally finite if every finitely generated subgroup of $G$ is finite. For $g\in G$, its centralizer is \[ C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}. \] For a prime $p$, the quasicyclic $p$-group is \[ C_{p^\infty}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^\times:z^{p^n}=1\text{ for some integer }n\geq 0\}, \] with multiplication inherited from ${\mathbb{C}}^\times$. A group $K$ is Chernikov if it has a normal subgroup $D$ of finite index such that \[ D\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}C_{p_i^\infty} \] for some integer $r\geq 0$ and primes $p_1,\ldots,p_r$, which need not be distinct. The empty product is the trivial group, so finite groups are included.

A group $G$ is almost soluble if it has a soluble subgroup $H$ of finite index. Here soluble means that the derived series, defined by $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(n+1)}=[H^{(n)},H^{(n)}]$, satisfies $H^{(d)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $d\geq 0$.

The question is whether, for every locally finite group $G$, \[ \left(\exists g\in G\; C_G(g)\text{ is Chernikov}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\exists H\leq G\; [G:H]<\infty\text{ and }H\text{ is soluble}\right). \] The supplied statement credits B. Hartley, whereas the proposer metadata names V. V. Belyaev; their respective roles are not explained in the record. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this is a source label, not an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

Must every locally finite group containing an element whose centralizer is Chernikov have a soluble subgroup of finite index? The supplied record labels this question open.
