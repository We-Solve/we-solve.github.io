Math-1505

Positive tropicalization, cluster fans, and a finite wall-detection criterion

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Lauren Williams's source asks how tropicalization of a totally positive variety relates to its cluster algebra structure. A positive part requires a specified positive atlas or coordinate convention; for a Grassmannian it means that the chosen Plücker coordinates are positive. Its positive tropicalization is obtained from valuations of points with positive leading coefficients over an appropriate real valued extension, with closure and lineality conventions specified. The question concerns both the tropical set and the polyhedral fan chosen on it, which are different data.

The source cites joint work with David Speyer on $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$, relating the $k=2$ fans to type $A$ associahedra and the $(3,6),(3,7)$ fans to types $D_4,E_6$. It relates these to Joshua Scott's cluster structures of the corresponding types. The aid refines the historical statement: after lineality, $\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)$ gives the Stanley--Pitman fan, combinatorially type $A$; the positive Plücker-coordinate fans for $\operatorname{Gr}(3,6)$ and $\operatorname{Gr}(3,7)$ are coarsenings, refined using non-Plücker cluster variables to the $D_4,E_6$ cluster fans. Combinatorial type is not automatically equality with a particular embedded g-vector fan. Full references and coordinate identifications are not supplied.

The aid's finite criterion is conditional on a known full fan. In a fixed positive Laurent chart, let \[ f_i(x)=\sum_{a\in A_i}c_{i,a}x^a,\quad c_{i,a}>0,\qquad \tau_i(w)=\min_{a\in A_i}\langle a,w\rangle, \quad1\leq i\leq m. \] Assume coefficient valuation zero, finite supports, a fixed quotient by common lineality, and that the common fan of linearity $\mathcal F$ of all $\tau_i$ is already known to equal the desired cluster g-vector fan in those coordinates. For a retained index subset $S\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}$, let $\mathcal F_S$ be the common linearity fan of $\tau_i$, $i\in S$; it coarsens $\mathcal F$. Then \[ \mathcal F_S=\mathcal F \] if and only if for every pair of adjacent maximal chambers $C,C'$ of $\mathcal F$, sharing a facet, at least one retained $\tau_i$ has different linear gradients on $C$ and $C'$. Equivalently, after identifying duplicate exponents and the lineality quotient, some retained coordinate changes its tropical minimizing exponent across every wall.

Failure has a finite local certificate: adjacent chambers on which every retained coordinate has the same linear formula. The result is a test inside a known full positive coordinate presentation; it does not establish the initial cluster-fan identification, produce a cluster structure for an arbitrary positive variety, or imply that Plücker coordinates alone detect every cluster wall.

Short English statement

The historical Grassmannian examples require distinguishing positive-coordinate fans from their cluster refinements. The aid gives a finite test: a coordinate subfamily preserves a known full cluster fan precisely when it detects every adjacent wall. The initial cluster identification remains an explicit hypothesis.
