Recursive Homological Filling and the Word Problem

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied clean statement concerns all finitely presented groups. The following gives a precise formulation under the integral cellular convention for homological filling; the supplied record does not specify the coefficient convention.

Let $H$ be a group with a finite presentation $\mathcal P=\langle A\mid R\rangle$. Let $X_{\mathcal P}$ be the presentation complex and let $\widetilde X_{\mathcal P}$ be its universal cover. For $i=1,2$, let $C_i(\widetilde X_{\mathcal P};{\mathbb{Z}})$ denote the finitely supported integral cellular chains, with cellular boundary operator $\partial_i$. For a chain $c=\sum_\sigma a_\sigma\sigma$, define $\lVert c\rVert_1=\sum_\sigma |a_\sigma|$. Write ${\mathbb{N}}_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. For an integral cellular $1$-cycle $z$, define \[ \operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal P}(z) =\min\left\{\lVert b\rVert_1:b\in C_2(\widetilde X_{\mathcal P};{\mathbb{Z}}),\ \partial_2b=z\right\}. \] Such a filling exists because the universal cover is simply connected. Define the integral homological filling function by \[ \operatorname{FA}_{\mathcal P}(n) =\sup\left\{\operatorname{Fill}_{\mathcal P}(z): z\in\ker\partial_1,\ \lVert z\rVert_1\le n\right\}, \qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}_0. \] Here $\operatorname{FA}_H$ is interpreted through a finite presentation as above. A recursive bound means a total computable function $f:{\mathbb{N}}_0\to{\mathbb{N}}_0$ satisfying $\operatorname{FA}_{\mathcal P}(n)\le f(n)$ for every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}_0$. The word problem for $H$ is solvable if an algorithm halts on every finite word in $A\cup A^{-1}$ and decides whether that word represents the identity of $H$.

Question under this convention. For every finitely presented group $H$, does the existence of a finite presentation $\mathcal P$ of $H$ and a total computable function $f:{\mathbb{N}}_0\to{\mathbb{N}}_0$ such that \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_0,\qquad \operatorname{FA}_{\mathcal P}(n)\le f(n) \] imply that $H$ has a solvable word problem?

Short English statement

Under the integral cellular interpretation of homological filling, the supplied clean question asks whether every finitely presented group with a computable upper bound on its homological filling function has a decidable word problem. The original extract leaves $H$ unidentified, so its equivalence to this universal question requires review.
