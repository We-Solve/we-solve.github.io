10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Mallat-Zeitouni Gaussian-basis problem

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $\Sigma$ be a positive-semidefinite real symmetric $n\times n$ matrix, and let $X\sim N(0,\Sigma)$. For an orthonormal basis $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$, put \[ N_B=\max_{1\le i\le n}|\langle X,b_i\rangle|,\qquad \Phi_\Sigma(B)=\mathbb E N_B. \] A Karhunen--Loève (KL) basis $B_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is an orthonormal eigenbasis of $\Sigma$. If its eigenvalues are $\lambda_i$, then its coordinates have the law $(\sqrt{\lambda_i}Z_i)_{i=1}^n$, with independent standard normal $Z_i$.

The stored statement asks whether $B_{\mathrm{KL}}$ minimizes $\Phi_\Sigma$, namely whether \[ \Phi_\Sigma(B_{\mathrm{KL}})\le\Phi_\Sigma(B)\quad\text{for every }B. \] The supplied research summary explicitly says the dataset has this extremum reversed and that the intended Mallat--Zeitouni conjecture is instead \[ \forall n,\Sigma,B,\qquad \Phi_\Sigma(B)\le\Phi_\Sigma(B_{\mathrm{KL}}), \] i.e. KL maximizes the expected largest absolute coordinate. Both formulations are displayed to preserve the material conflict; the maximum version is a supplied correction, not a silent replacement of the registry statement. Degenerate covariance matrices are not excluded.

The summary says the minimum version is already refuted in dimension $2$, claims an exact dimension-$2$ proof for the maximum version and a variance-equalizing minimizer, and refers to a "closed form above" that is absent from the record. Its numerical verification claim does not supply a proof artifact. It reports the exact maximum conjecture open in general, constant-factor approximate optimality attributed to Litvak--Tikhomirov (2018), and a $1+O(1/\sqrt d)$ factor attributed to Liu (2026). The parameter $d$, asymptotic regime, and precise hypotheses of that last estimate are not specified. These are unverified supplied progress claims.

Short English statement

Does an eigenbasis of a Gaussian covariance matrix maximize the expected largest coordinate magnitude? The stored question says minimize, while its research summary explicitly corrects that direction and reports partial progress.
