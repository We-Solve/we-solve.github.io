Problem 3.6 — (Generalized word problem).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix a finite generating set for $\operatorname{Mod}_g$. For a specified subgroup $H\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$, its generalized word problem is the membership decision problem: given an ambient-generator word $w$, decide whether its represented element lies in $H$.

Problem 3.6. Determine the subgroups $H$ for which this problem is solvable, give efficient algorithms for those subgroups, and find optimal time bounds. The source explicitly calls the full problem too broad and welcomes special cases. It also asks whether there exists a finitely presented subgroup of $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ with unsolvable membership problem. The genus range, representation of $H$, computational model, and fixed-subgroup versus uniform-input formulation are not specified.

The source cites Leininger--McReynolds [LM] for some solutions. It reports Bridson--Miller, by personal communication and extending Baumslag--Roseblade, as proving that a product of finite-rank free groups has solvable membership problem for each finitely presented subgroup. This is retained with its reported scope and provenance, not promoted to a statement about all mapping-class-group subgroups.

The adjoining refinement concerns distortion. Let $H\leq\Gamma$ be finitely generated, with finite generating sets fixed. Use the source convention $d_\Gamma(x,y)=|xy^{-1}|_\Gamma$, and similarly for $H$. It calls $f:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}$ a distortion bound if \[ |h|_H\leq f(|h|_\Gamma)\qquad(h\in H). \] To make growth-type assertions precise, the usual canonical function is \[ \Delta_H^\Gamma(n)=\max\{|h|_H:h\in H,\ |h|_\Gamma\leq n\}, \] up to the standard changes of scale from finite generating sets. An arbitrary upper bound $f$ is not unique in growth type: it may be increased freely. Thus the source claims about generator-independent growth and constant distortion exactly for finite $H$ should be interpreted for canonical distortion, or for existence of such bounds, not for every arbitrary chosen bound. The source says infinite subgroups have at least linear distortion and cites [Fa1] for the equivalence, when $\Gamma$ has solvable word problem, between recursive distortion bounds and solvable membership for finitely generated $H$.

Its examples are the center of the integral three-dimensional Heisenberg group with quadratic distortion, and $\langle b\rangle$ in \[ \langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}=b^2\rangle \] with exponential distortion. The compressed last identity is naturally read as $a^n b a^{-n}=b^{2^n}$, consistent with that defining relation; the flattened exponent in the record does not preserve its full typography. These are contextual examples, not algorithms for the general $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ problem.

Short English statement

Classify mapping-class-group subgroups with decidable membership, seek efficient and optimal algorithms in tractable cases, and ask whether a finitely presented subgroup can have undecidable membership. The source also relates membership to recursive distortion, with a needed distinction between canonical distortion and arbitrary upper bounds.
