Kourovka Notebook Problem 7.54

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finitely generated group $H$, let $d(H)$ be the minimum cardinality of a generating set of $H$, with $d(\{1\})=0$. Interpreting special rank in its standard Prüfer-rank sense, define \[ \operatorname{sr}(G)=\sup\{d(H):H\leq G\text{ is finitely generated}\}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}. \] Thus finite special rank means that a single finite bound applies to the numbers of generators required by all finitely generated subgroups. The supplied record does not explicitly define this terminology, so this interpretation requires confirmation against the cited source.

Does there exist a group $G$ and subgroups $A,B\leq G$ such that \[ G=AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\},\qquad \operatorname{sr}(A)<\infty,\qquad \operatorname{sr}(B)<\infty,\qquad \operatorname{sr}(G)=\infty? \] Here the last condition means that for every integer $n\geq0$, some finitely generated subgroup $H\leq G$ satisfies $d(H)>n$. The product is a setwise product; no normality assumption on either factor is stated in the supplied record.

Short English statement

Can a group whose finitely generated subgroups require arbitrarily many generators be the setwise product of two subgroups, each having a uniform finite bound on the number of generators required by its finitely generated subgroups?
