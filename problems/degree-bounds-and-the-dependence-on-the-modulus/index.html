Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 2. Consider Fp and d ∈ Z>0, and S ⊂ Fp with S = d. Let h(X) ∈

Fp[X] with h(s) 6 = 0 for all s ∈ S. Let G be the group generated by X − s for s ∈ S. In the original AKS paper, we have G ≥ 2d. There are techniques for getting G ≥ $5.82$ d

(using lattice point counting); there seems to be much more room for improvement (perhaps using ABC). (Bernstein, Voloch) Perhaps (log G)/d → ∞

as d → ∞ uniformly over h.

Ambient convention and original scope

The primary report's remarks identify the intended ambient ring as $\mathbb F_p[X]/(h)$. Its question does not specify the degree or factorization hypotheses on $h$ needed for the cited exponential bounds. We make one literal finite-ring interpretation and check its quantifiers. Let $h$ be nonconstant of degree $m$, let $S\subseteq \mathbb F_p$ have size $d\ge1$, and suppose $h(s)\ne0$ for every $s\in S$. Then $X-s$ is coprime to $h$: division by $X-s$ has remainder $h(s)$. Bezout's identity in the polynomial Euclidean algorithm makes each residue class a unit. Let $G$ be their generated subgroup of this finite unit group.
Unrestricted uniform divergence is false in this reading.
Choose any $a\in\mathbb F_p$, $h=X-a$, and $S=\mathbb F_p\setminus\{a\}$. Evaluation at $a$ identifies the quotient with $\mathbb F_p$, and the generators $a-s$ run through all nonzero elements. Hence $G=\mathbb F_p^*$ and $|G|=p-1=d$. Along arbitrarily large primes, $\log|G|/d=\log d/d\to0$, rather than infinity. There are arbitrarily large primes since the product of any finite list plus one has a prime divisor outside the list. The limit follows, for example, from $\log d\le\sqrt d$ for all sufficiently large $d$, verified by differentiating $\sqrt d-\log d$ beyond four. This refutes only the unrestricted degree-one-modulus version. It does not refute an unstated AKS condition on $h$, and is not advertised as a resolution of the intended strengthened group-size problem.
A complete degree-sensitive lower bound.
All monic products \[ P_e(X)=\prod_{s\in S}(X-s)^{e_s},\qquad e_s\ge0,\quad\sum_s e_s<m, \] have distinct residues modulo $h$. If two residues agreed, $h$ would divide their difference, a polynomial of degree below $m$; hence the difference would be zero. Unique factorization into the distinct linear factors $X-s$ then forces every exponent to agree. The latter can also be seen without a general factorization theorem by repeatedly dividing out $X-s$ and evaluating at $s$ to recover its multiplicity. Each product is a unit and lies in $G$. The number of exponent vectors is \[ |G|\ge\binom{d+m-1}{d}. \] To count them, introduce a slack exponent $e_0=m-1-\sum_s e_s\ge0$. Weak compositions of $m-1$ into $d+1$ parts correspond to strings of $m-1$ identical stars and $d$ separators, so their number is the displayed binomial coefficient. In particular \[ \binom{d+m-1}{d}=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{m+j}{j+1} \ge(m/d)^d. \] Thus the desired divergence does follow in the additional regime $m/d\to\infty$. This statement is conditional on that new regime; it is not asserted uniformly over the original unspecified moduli.
Where the direct counting route stops.
Once total degree reaches $m$, products can coincide modulo $h$ and the proof no longer gives injectivity. The extreme $m=1$ example shows that ignoring these collisions is genuinely invalid. In the difficult regime $m$ comparable to $d$, the bound proves only exponential growth with bounded base and therefore does not establish $\log|G|/d\to\infty$. No assertion about the historical base $5.82$ is made without its additional hypotheses. A new way to control higher-degree multiplicative collisions would be required.
