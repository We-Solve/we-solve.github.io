Radon Numbers for Unions of Convex Sets

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the following working formulation, take $d,s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$. The supplied record describes $r$-convex sets as unions of a bounded number of convex sets but does not give a formal definition. Interpret this as the family \[ \mathcal U_r(d)=\left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}K_i:0\leq m\leq r,\ K_i\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ is convex for every }i\right\}, \] where the union with $m=0$ is empty. Convexity means that $(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\in K_i$ whenever $x,y\in K_i$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$. No closedness, boundedness, or connectedness of the union is required.

For $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}$, let $\mathcal R_{d,s,t}(n)$ denote the property \[ \begin{aligned} &\forall P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ with }|P|=n,\ \exists A,B\subseteq P:\\ &\quad P=A\sqcup B,\quad A\ne\varnothing,\quad B\ne\varnothing,\\ &\quad \forall S\in\mathcal U_s(d)\ \forall T\in\mathcal U_t(d),\quad (A\subseteq S\ \land\ B\subseteq T)\Longrightarrow S\cap T\ne\varnothing. \end{aligned} \] Thus the partition is chosen before the universally quantified containing sets. There is no general-position hypothesis on $P$. Under the convention that the empty set belongs to each $\mathcal U_r(d)$, the intersection requirement itself excludes empty parts.

Determine the least integer \[ f(d,s,t)=\min\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:\mathcal R_{d,s,t}(n)\}, \] whenever this set is nonempty. The accompanying polynomial-growth question is interpreted as asking whether there exist constants $C>0$ and $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}$, independent of $d,s,t$, such that this minimum exists and \[ f(d,s,t)\leq C(d+s+t)^k \qquad\text{for every }d,s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}. \] The supplied Radon-theorem comparison gives $f(d,1,1)=d+2$, with sharpness witnessed by the vertices of a $d$-dimensional simplex.

The parameter ranges, the precise meaning of $r$-convexity, and the interpretation of polynomial growth above are explicit conventions requiring review against the complete mathematical source. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and attributes a near-optimal polynomial upper bound to Alon and Smorodinsky, while reporting that the exact least value remains unknown. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

Find the smallest number of points in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ that always permits a partition into two nonempty parts such that every union of at most $s$ convex sets containing the first part intersects every union of at most $t$ convex sets containing the second. The source also asks for polynomial growth in the parameters. Its supplied dated assessment reports a polynomial upper bound but leaves the exact minimum unresolved; the conventions and literature claims require review.
