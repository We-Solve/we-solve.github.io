Distinguishability versus polynomial reconstruction

We use the continuous-time Lie-derivative convention: for $\dot x=f(x)$ and $y=h(x)$, define $L_fh=h'f$ and iterate this operation. This is stated explicitly because the report also discusses discrete-time observations. The following one-dimensional examples test the inference that globally distinguishable real states must have polynomial reconstruction from finitely many output derivatives.
Proposition: The real polynomial system $\dot x=0,y=x^3$ distinguishes every pair of initial states but is not algebraically observable under this polynomial-reconstruction definition. Changing its dynamics to $\dot x=1$ while keeping $h=x^3$ makes it algebraically observable, with $x=y''/6$ along trajectories.
Proof: For the zero vector field, a trajectory starting at $x_0$ is constant, with output $x_0^3$. The cube map is injective on $\mathbb R$, so distinct initial states have different outputs already at time zero. All positive-order Lie derivatives vanish. Any proposed polynomial reconstruction from finitely many derivatives would therefore reduce to an identity $p(x^3)=x$ for a single-variable real polynomial $p$. A constant $p$ cannot work; if $p$ has positive degree $d$, the left side has degree $3d$, which cannot equal 1. Hence no such polynomial exists. For $f=1$, $L_fh=3x^2$ and $L_f^2h=6x$, so the linear polynomial taking the third observation to one sixth its value reconstructs the state. Along a trajectory these Lie derivatives are the corresponding time derivatives of the output by the chain rule. ∎
This shows that the observable/algebraically observable distinction is substantive and that dynamics can supply missing algebraic information. The exceptional subfamily $f=0$ is not evidence against genericity in a larger coefficient space. The authored Helmke--Martin--Zhou preprint states generic algebraic observability for bounded positive degree bounds in a nonempty Zariski-open family, using continuous-time Lie derivatives; that is attributed literature, not a result of this attempt. Its full genericity proof is not reproved here.

Verification and remaining gaps

The Lie derivative chain for $f=1$ was checked exactly as $x^3,3x^2,6x,6,0$; for $f=0$ all derivatives after the first vanish. The degree obstruction is a complete proof for this example.
