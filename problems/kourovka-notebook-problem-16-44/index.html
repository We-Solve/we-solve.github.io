Kourovka Notebook Problem 16.44

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$ with a topology $\tau$, being a topological group means that multiplication $G\times G\to G$ and inversion $G\to G$ are continuous, where the domain of multiplication has the product topology. The topology is non-discrete if not every singleton is open. A subset $D\subseteq G$ is discrete if, for every $d\in D$, there is an open set $U\subseteq G$ such that $U\cap D=\{d\}$. Define its set of accumulation points in the ambient group by \[ D'_G=\{x\in G: \text{every open neighbourhood }U\text{ of }x\text{ satisfies }U\cap(D\setminus\{x\})\ne\varnothing\}. \] The existence question attributed to E. G. Zelenyuk is whether ZFC proves that there exists a countable non-discrete topological group $(G,\tau)$ such that \[ \forall D\subseteq G,\qquad D\text{ discrete}\ \Longrightarrow\ \neg\exists p\in G\ (D'_G=\{p\}). \] Here countable means $|G|\leq\aleph_0$. The supplied record does not specify a separation axiom for topological groups. If the intended convention requires Hausdorffness, that hypothesis must be added; in that case countability and non-discreteness force $G$ to be countably infinite. The intended separation convention remains unresolved. Accumulation points in the displayed formulation are counted throughout $G$, including outside $D$.

The source additionally reports that such a group is known to exist under Martin's Axiom. This is retained as a supplied qualification, without independent verification.

Short English statement

Does ZFC prove the existence of a countable non-discrete topological group having no discrete subset with exactly one accumulation point in the group? The source reports existence under Martin's Axiom. The intended separation axiom for the group topology requires clarification.
