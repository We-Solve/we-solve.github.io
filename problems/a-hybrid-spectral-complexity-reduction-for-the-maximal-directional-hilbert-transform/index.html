Math-1291

A hybrid spectral-complexity reduction for the maximal directional Hilbert transform

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a unit vector $v\in S^1$, let \[ H_vf(x)=\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x-tv)\,\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}{t} \] for Schwartz functions, extended to $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^2)$ by its Fourier multiplier $-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\xi\cdot v)$. For a finite nonempty direction set $V$, put $T_Vf=\max_{v\in V}|H_vf|$. The weak norm is \[ \|h\|_{L^{2,\infty}}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\,|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:|h(x)|>\lambda\}|^{1/2}. \] Di Plinio's question asks whether there is an absolute constant $C$ such that, for every $N\geq2$, every choice $v_1,\ldots,v_N\in S^1$, and every $f\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^2)$, \[ \left\|\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|H_{v_j}f|\right\|_{L^{2,\infty}}\leq C\sqrt{\log N}\,\|f\|_2. \] The restriction $N\geq2$ avoids the literal zero on the right at $N=1$. Since $H_{-v}=-H_v$, the effective cardinality is the number $M$ of distinct projective directions; the equivalent uniformly nonzero formulation uses $\sqrt{\log(1+M)}$.

For the supplied partial estimate, take one representative of each projective direction. Let the angular sign cells be the connected components of \[ {\mathbb{R}}^2\setminus\bigcup_{v\in V}\{\xi:\xi\cdot v=0\}, \] so every multiplier sign is constant on each cell. There are $2M$ cells. For $j\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, set $A_j=\{\xi:2^j\leq|\xi|<2^{j+1}\}$. Choose $L\geq1$ such annuli and $K\geq1$ such cells, and let $S$ be the intersection of their respective unions. The aid reports an absolute-constant bound \[ \operatorname{supp}\widehat f\subset S\ \Longrightarrow\quad \|T_Vf\|_{L^{2,\infty}}\leq C\min\{\sqrt L,\log(K+1)\}\,\|f\|_2. \] Support containment is understood up to null sets. More generally, with $P_S$ the orthogonal Fourier projection $\widehat{P_Sf}=1_S\widehat f$, its reported stable form is \[ \|T_Vf\|_{L^{2,\infty}}\leq C\min\{\sqrt L,\log(K+1)\}\,\|P_Sf\|_2 +C\log(M+1)\,\|f-P_Sf\|_2. \] Consequently, if a nonzero input supported in such a finite-complexity region satisfies $\|T_Vf\|_{L^{2,\infty}}\geq c\sqrt{\log(1+M)}\,\|f\|_2$ for a fixed $c>0$, then \[ L\geq c_1\log(1+M),\qquad K+1\geq\exp\!\left(c_2\sqrt{\log(1+M)}\right), \] for positive constants depending on $c$ and the estimate's absolute constant. This is an obstruction for such inputs, not an existence assertion for near-extremizers or a bound at the conjectured scale for arbitrary inputs.

The original source reports that Katz proved a "maximal variant" and that the proposed estimate is known for lacunary and Vargas direction sets, with uniformly distributed directions as a typical Vargas example. The precise variant and structural hypotheses are not specified in this record.

Short English statement

The question is whether the maximum of $M$ directional Hilbert transforms has weak-$L^2$ norm growing only as $\sqrt{\log(1+M)}$. The supplied partial estimate bounds inputs with limited radial or angular Fourier complexity and quantifies a remainder. It forces any spectrally finite input reaching the conjectured scale to occupy many radial bands and angular cells.
