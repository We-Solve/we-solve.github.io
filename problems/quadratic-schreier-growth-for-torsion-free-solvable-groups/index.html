Math-5039

Quadratic Schreier Growth for Torsion-Free Solvable Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group, and fix a finite symmetric generating set $S$. Write $B_S(n)=\{g\in G:|g|_S\leq n\}$ for its word ball, where $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$. A $G$-set $X$ is a set equipped with an action of $G$; the action is faithful if $\{g\in G:gx=x\text{ for every }x\in X\}=\{1\}$. For a nonempty $G$-set define its uniform Schreier growth by \[ \operatorname{vol}_{G,X,S}(n)=\sup_{x\in X}\left|\{gx:g\in B_S(n)\}\right|. \] The source does not explicitly define its notation $\operatorname{vol}_{G,X}$. This draft interprets it as the uniform Schreier growth above, with the generating set suppressed. It interprets $\operatorname{vol}_{G,X}(n)\succeq n^2$ as the existence of constants $c>0$ and $n_0\geq 1$ such that $\operatorname{vol}_{G,X,S}(n)\geq cn^2$ for every integer $n\geq n_0$. This quadratic lower-bound property is independent of the chosen finite generating set. These interpretations require confirmation against the source.

The group $G$ is torsion-free if $g^m=1$, for $g\in G$ and an integer $m\geq1$, implies $g=1$. It is solvable if its derived series, defined by $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(j+1)}=[G^{(j)},G^{(j)}]$, reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps. It is virtually abelian if it has an abelian subgroup of finite index.

Conjectural statement. For every finitely generated torsion-free solvable group $G$, either $G$ is virtually abelian, or every faithful $G$-set $X$ satisfies a quadratic uniform Schreier-growth lower bound. More explicitly, if $G$ is not virtually abelian, then for every faithful action $G\curvearrowright X$ and every finite symmetric generating set $S$, there exist $c>0$ and an integer $n_0\geq1$, allowed to depend on the action and on $S$, such that \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad n\geq n_0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sup_{x\in X}|B_S(n)x|\geq cn^2. \] There is no restriction on the number of orbits or the cardinality of $X$. In this non-virtually-abelian case, faithfulness forces $X$ to be nonempty.

Supplied qualifications. The retained original passage refers to an unspecified "previous result" and reports that the conjecture holds for transitive actions and, more generally, actions with finitely many orbits. Here transitive means having exactly one orbit. The supplied dated assessment additionally reports results for metabelian groups and broad finite-rank or linear classes, without specifying the exact hypotheses of those classes. These are source-reported contextual claims, not independently verified results in this draft.

Short English statement

The conjecture says that every faithful action of a finitely generated torsion-free solvable group that is not virtually abelian has uniform Schreier growth at least quadratic. The bound takes the supremum over all starting points, and its constants may depend on the action. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved; the growth notation and the reconstruction from the original contextual passage require human review.
