Math-1656

Taylor coefficients and complex HCIZ free energy

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 20 asks whether convergent Taylor derivatives of an Itzykson--Zuber integral match a previously defined $\psi$, and whether the Guionnet--Zeitouni result extends to complex parameters. The prior integral and $\psi$ definitions are not included. The extraction also uses $n$ both for matrix size and derivative order and omits a normalization. The aid separates matrix dimension $N$ from a fixed derivative order $q$; it does not claim that fixed-order results apply to $q=N$.

For an explicit unitary plus-exponent convention, let $A_N,B_N$ be Hermitian, uniformly bounded in operator norm, with convergent normalized trace moments, and set \[ I_N(z)=\int_{U(N)}e^{Nz\operatorname{Tr}(A_NUB_NU^*)}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} U, \qquad F_N(z)=N^{-2}\log I_N(z),\qquad F_N(0)=0. \] Haar measure is normalized, and the logarithm is initially local at zero. The aid's normalization correction is substantive: an unnormalized derivative of $\log I_N$ generally scales like $N^2$, while $F_N^{(q)}(0)$ is the fixed-order quantity to compare. Non-Hermitian matrix parameters or a derivative order growing with $N$ require separate results.

The supplied analytic-continuation criterion assumes a radius $R>0$ such that $I_N$ has no zeros on $|z|<R$ for every $N$, and assumes the stated real-axis free-energy convergence on an interval through zero. Then the branches $F_N(0)=0$ converge compact-uniformly on that disc to a holomorphic $F$, and \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}F_N^{(q)}(0)=F^{(q)}(0)\qquad\text{for each fixed }q. \] Uniform matrix bounds give $\operatorname{Re}F_N(z)\leq |z|\sup_N\|A_N\|\sup_N\|B_N\|$; together with zero-free branches this supplies the local normal-family control used in the criterion. The common zero-free disc is a hypothesis, not a conclusion of real convergence alone.

Writing $a_{k,N}=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(A_N^k)$ and $b_{k,N}=N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(B_N^k)$, the aid gives the exact coefficients \[ F_N'(0)=a_{1,N}b_{1,N},\qquad F_N''(0)=\frac{N^2}{N^2-1} (a_{2,N}-a_{1,N}^2)(b_{2,N}-b_{1,N}^2)\quad(N\geq2). \] Under the criterion, the limiting fixed Collins coefficients are derivatives of $F$. If the missing $\psi$ convention uses the same integral with negative exponent, then $F_N^-(z)=F_N(-z)$ and its order-$q$ coefficient differs by $(-1)^q$. The exact match to the source's $\psi$ cannot be certified without that omitted definition and any other additive/sign normalizations.

Complex zeros obstruct a global logarithm even at finite size. In this convention, for $N=2$ and $A_2=B_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, \[ I_2(z)=\frac{\sinh(4z)}{4z}, \] with the removable value $1$ at zero and zeros $z=i\pi k/4$ for nonzero integers $k$. This rules out an unrestricted global holomorphic logarithm, not every local or selected-domain complex extension. The criterion does not establish all complex-parameter versions of the original question.

Short English statement

The source's derivative and normalization conventions are incomplete. The aid fixes dimension and derivative order separately, computes the first two normalized HCIZ coefficients, and shows that a common zero-free complex disc upgrades real convergence to holomorphic convergence. A finite-size example explains why a global complex logarithm cannot be assumed.
