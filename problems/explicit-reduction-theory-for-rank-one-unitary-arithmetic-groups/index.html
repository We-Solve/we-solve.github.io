Explicit Reduction Theory for Rank-One Unitary Arithmetic Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied cleaned formulation uses a positive square-free integer $d$ and the imaginary quadratic field $L={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})$. Write $\overline{x}$ for its nontrivial ${\mathbb{Q}}$-automorphism and $g^*=\overline{g}^{\,t}$ for conjugate transpose. The record specifies \[ G({\mathbb{Q}})=\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_3(L):g^*Jg=J\} \] and assumes that the corresponding algebraic group $G$ has ${\mathbb{Q}}$-rank one, meaning that a maximal ${\mathbb{Q}}$-split torus in $G$ has dimension one. For the displayed equation to define the asserted unitary group, the conventional interpretation requires $J\in\operatorname{GL}_3(L)$ with $J^*=J$. The record does not specify $J$; these conditions explain the unitary interpretation and do not identify an intended matrix.

Let $\Gamma\leq G({\mathbb{Q}})$ be an arithmetic subgroup, as required by the cleaned formulation. Here arithmetic means that, in a faithful algebraic representation $\rho:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_N$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, the subgroup $\rho(\Gamma)$ is commensurable with $\rho(G({\mathbb{Q}}))\cap\operatorname{GL}_N({\mathbb{Z}})$; two subgroups are commensurable when their intersection has finite index in each.

Source question, with unresolved qualifications. For groups and arithmetic subgroups satisfying these assumptions, can one carry out explicit reduction theory for $\Gamma$? The record does not define the required output of an explicit reduction theory, specify which choices of $J$ are intended, or say whether one uniform procedure for all such inputs is required. Consequently, the question cannot yet be expressed as a uniquely specified existence or algorithmic assertion.

There is also a substantive discrepancy between the supplied formulations: the original statement uses $L={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt d)$, with $d>0$ square-free, and merely calls $\Gamma$ a subgroup, whereas the cleaned statement uses $L={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})$ and requires $\Gamma$ to be arithmetic. The original also describes $G$ as a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-rank-one form of $\operatorname{SL}_3$. The imaginary quadratic and arithmetic assumptions above reproduce the cleaned formulation; the supplied evidence does not resolve their compatibility with the original.

Short English statement

The cleaned question asks for explicit reduction theory for an arithmetic subgroup of a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-rank-one unitary group in three variables over an imaginary quadratic field. The Hermitian form and the intended reduction outputs remain unspecified. The original wording instead uses a positive radicand and does not require the subgroup to be arithmetic; these differences require source review.
