Math-6952

Rational Periodic Points from Finite-Field Periodicity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]$ with $p(0,0)=q(0,0)=0$, and let $f=(p,q)$ be the induced polynomial self-map of the affine plane. Dominance is understood on the characteristic-zero generic fiber: the image of $f:\mathbb A^2_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to\mathbb A^2_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ is Zariski dense. Write $f^m$ for the $m$-fold iterate. A point $a$ is periodic if $f^m(a)=a$ for some integer $m\geq1$, including fixed points.

For each prime $\ell$, let $\overline f_\ell:{\mathbb{F}}_\ell^2\to{\mathbb{F}}_\ell^2$ be coefficientwise reduction. Preserve the exact finite-field hypothesis \[ \forall\ell\text{ prime}\ \exists a_\ell\in{\mathbb{F}}_\ell^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\ \exists m_\ell\geq1, \qquad \overline f_\ell^{\,m_\ell}(a_\ell)=a_\ell. \] The prime-dependent points and periods need not be compatible or uniformly bounded, and the hypothesis says every prime, not only almost all primes.

The literal requested conclusion is \[ \exists a\in{\mathbb{Q}}^2\ \exists m\geq1:\quad f^m(a)=a. \] However, the imposed equations already give $f(0,0)=(0,0)$, so the rational origin satisfies this conclusion without any finite-field or dominance hypothesis. The nonzero-point exclusion occurs only in the finite-field antecedent in both supplied formulations. A potentially substantive variant would require $a\ne(0,0)$ also over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, but that stronger conclusion is not written in the source and is not silently substituted. Clarification of this missing exclusion, or another intended nontriviality requirement, is essential to recover an open problem.

Short English statement

The source asks whether nonzero periodic points modulo every prime force a rational periodic point for a dominant plane map fixing the origin. As written, the rational origin is already periodic; a nonzero rational-point requirement would be a stronger, unstated question.
