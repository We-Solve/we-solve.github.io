Math-5381

Well-balanced reconstruction for hyperbolic chemotaxis

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Item A.5 is Francis Filbet's contributed-talk abstract, Approximation of Hyperbolic Models for Chemosensitive Movement, describing work with Chi-Wang Shu of Brown University. It is not an open-problem question. It reports first- and second-order well-balanced finite-volume schemes and a high-order finite-difference weighted essentially non-oscillatory (WENO) scheme adapted to preserve steady states while retaining high-order accuracy. Numerical tests concern accuracy, the well-balanced property, and network formation resembling experiments. The keywords concern chemotaxis, hyperbolic systems, finite-volume and finite-difference methods, WENO, and well-balanced schemes. No specific equations, discretization, accuracy theorem, or unresolved target occurs in the abstract.

The aid's separate structural statement concerns positive one-dimensional rest equilibria. For a pressure $p$ with $p'(n)>0$ and a chemical potential $\chi(c)$, define an enthalpy by \[ H'(n)=\frac{p'(n)}n\qquad(n>0). \] For the momentum balance $\partial_xp(n)=n\partial_x\chi(c)$ at zero velocity, the positive rest condition is \[ H(n)-\chi(c)=K \] for a constant $K$. Given a shared interface value $\theta$, reconstruct each positive state by \[ n^*=H^{-1}\left(H(n)+\theta-\chi(c)\right), \] whenever the argument lies in the range of $H$. States on the same equilibrium then reconstruct to the same interface density and zero velocity.

The aid reports exact balance for any consistent numerical flux, with the matching well-balanced source treatment understood. Explicitly, for the hyperbolic flux $(nv,nv^2+p(n))$, consistency gives interface flux $(0,p(n^*))$ on such equal rest states. The source discretization must supply exactly the corresponding momentum flux difference at equilibrium; for a cell of width $\Delta x$ this is \[ S_j=\left(0,\frac{p(n^*_{j+1/2})-p(n^*_{j-1/2})}{\Delta x}\right). \] Then the semidiscrete flux difference and source cancel. Matching reconstructed states without a compatible source term is not by itself a full well-balanced scheme. Any additional chemical-field equation must also be balanced separately; none is supplied here.

For the normalized isothermal case $p(n)=n$, $H(n)=\log n$, the aid proves a local multiplicative rigidity statement. Suppose $R(\theta,z)>0$, $n^*=nR(\theta,\chi(c))$, the reconstruction matches every pair with $\log n-\chi(c)=K$, and $R(z,z)=1$. On the admissible parameter domain where these comparisons are available, \[ R(\theta,z)=e^{\theta-z}. \] Thus the exponential Filbet--Shu factor is forced within this local multiplicative class and normalization. For a numerical flux that additionally detects unequal positive rest densities through a nonzero mass flux, a reconstruction mismatch creates nonzero face mass flux. That detection property is an extra condition, not implied by consistency of an arbitrary flux.

The aid says comparison with the published Filbet--Shu paper corrects the scope of the 2005 WENO claim, but does not state the correction or provide full bibliographic data. The abstract's broad high-order preservation language is therefore retained as a reported claim, without promoting it to an independently verified theorem for every equilibrium or WENO variant.

Short English statement

This is a numerical-methods talk abstract, not an open question. The accompanying aid studies positive rest equilibria and shows how shared enthalpy reconstruction can balance a compatible flux/source discretization. In a normalized isothermal multiplicative class, exact equilibrium matching uniquely gives the exponential reconstruction factor; the broader WENO claims still need their stated scope recovered.
