Math-464

5.9 (Cooper) — Thurston's Lego sets in dimensions at least four

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A hyperbolic $n$-simplex is a geodesic simplex in hyperbolic $n$-space, with the compact/ideal/other vertex conventions to be checked against the source. A finite set $\mathcal S$ specifies simplex shapes; finite gluings may use arbitrarily many copies of those shapes. In a facewise isometric gluing, the cone angle around a codimension-two face is the sum of its incident dihedral angles. The source requires the gluing to be a closed cone $n$-manifold, not merely any cell complex with assigned angles.

Cooper's Problem 5.9 asks whether \[ \forall R>0\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ n\ge4\ \exists\varepsilon>0\ \exists\mathcal S\text{ finite} \] with the following two properties:

  1. Every closed cone $n$-manifold $N$ obtained by gluing simplices from $\mathcal S$, with every codimension-two cone angle $\theta$ satisfying \[ 2\pi-\varepsilon<\theta<2\pi+\varepsilon, \] admits a hyperbolic metric.
  2. Every closed hyperbolic $n$-manifold $M$ with $\operatorname{inj}_x(M)>R$ at every point is obtained by such a gluing from the same $\mathcal S$.
Both $\varepsilon$ and $\mathcal S$ may depend on $R,n$, but must work uniformly for every $M,N$ in these clauses. The first clause asks for existence of a hyperbolic metric on $N$; it does not assert that the original cone metric is smooth hyperbolic. In the second clause "obtained this way" could mean realization of the underlying manifold by an allowed cone gluing, or realization of its given hyperbolic metric. That distinction and the precise simplex and cone-manifold conventions require source review; neither is silently fixed.

The source calls such a collection a Thurston's Lego set and reports existence in dimensions at most $3$. The supplied assessment reports no verified construction in dimensions at least $4$, with no complete modern primary-literature audit recorded.

Short English statement

For each radius threshold and dimension at least four, find finitely many hyperbolic simplex shapes that both model every sufficiently thick closed hyperbolic manifold and force any allowed near-$2\pi$-angle cone gluing to admit a hyperbolic metric. One common shape set and angle tolerance must serve all the manifolds in both requirements.
