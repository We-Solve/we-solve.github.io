Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let $L,N\ge1$, and write $\mathbb Z_L=\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z$. For $a\in\mathbb Z_L$, $R_a$ cyclically shifts the coordinates of $\mathbb R^L$. Observe $y_i=R_{\ell_i}x+\xi_i$, $1\le i\le N$, where $x\in\mathbb R^L$ and the shifts $\ell_i\in\mathbb Z_L$ are unknown and the $\xi_i$ are independent $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_L)$ random vectors. No prior distribution on the shifts is imposed. Define $C_{ia,jb}=\langle R_{-a}y_i,R_{-b}y_j\rangle$ for $a,b\in\mathbb Z_L$. The maximum-likelihood alignment objective is $\max_{a_1,\ldots,a_N}\sum_{i,j}C_{ia_i,ja_j}$. Its semidefinite relaxation maximizes $\operatorname{tr}(CV)$ over real symmetric $NL\times NL$ matrices satisfying \[V\succeq0,\quad V_{ia,jb}\ge0,\quad V_{ia,ib}=0\ (a\ne b),\quad \sum_{a,b\in\mathbb Z_L}V_{ia,jb}=1\ (i,j\in[N]).\] A shift assignment gives a feasible matrix $u u^\mathsf T$, where $u_{ia}=\mathbf1_{\{a=a_i\}}$; averaging its $L$ global cyclic translates is also feasible with the same objective. Tightness means that the optimal relaxed value equals the discrete alignment value, equivalently an optimal integral matrix exists. It does not mean the noisy maximum-likelihood shifts equal the true shifts.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Determine the noise levels $\sigma$ for which this multireference-alignment semidefinite relaxation is tight, including its dependence on the number of observations, dimension and underlying signal. In probabilistic asymptotic regimes, determine when tightness occurs with probability tending to one. [S2]

Short English statement

Determine when the multireference-alignment SDP exactly optimizes the discrete alignment objective, despite the noise.

Literature and context check

The original Bandeira--Charikar--Singer--Zhu paper was consulted, including the observation model and SDP formulation. It treats dependent pairwise noise and gives high-signal stability results. The argument below is an elementary all-pairs sufficient condition, not a claimed improvement of that literature.

Research attempt: A pairwise certificate and its sample-size bottleneck

Write $r=\|x\|$ and suppose initially that no nonzero cyclic shift fixes $x$. Define \[ \gamma=\min_{k\ne0}\{r^2-\langle x,R_kx\rangle\}>0. \] The positivity follows from $r^2-\langle x,R_kx\rangle=\tfrac12\|x-R_kx\|^2$ and finiteness of the shifts. Undo the true shifts, giving observations $x+e_i$. Suppose $\|e_i\|\le\eta$ for every $i$. For $i\ne j$ and $k\ne0$, expansion and Cauchy--Schwarz give \[ \langle x+e_i,x+e_j\rangle- \langle x+e_i,R_k(x+e_j)\rangle \ge\gamma-4r\eta-2\eta^2. \] There are two mixed terms bounded by $2r\eta$ each, and the difference of noise--noise terms is bounded by $2\eta^2$. If this lower bound is positive, each cross block $C_{ia,jb}$ is maximized by the consistent true relative shift. Every feasible SDP block consists of nonnegative weights with total one, so its contribution is at most that block maximum. The within-observation block contributes exactly $\|y_i\|^2$: its off-diagonal entries vanish and its diagonal weights sum to one. The integral true-shift assignment attains all these bounds simultaneously. This proves equality of the SDP and discrete values under this deterministic condition without using its positive-semidefinite constraint.

For comparison with a probability statement, $E\|e_i\|^2=L\sigma^2$. Markov's inequality and a union bound imply \[ \Pr\{\max_i\|e_i\|>\eta\}\le NL\sigma^2/\eta^2. \] Thus, for $0<\delta<1$, taking $\eta=\sigma\sqrt{NL/\delta}$ gives a proved, very weak sufficient high-probability regime whenever $\gamma>4r\eta+2\eta^2$. Markov here follows by $E Z\ge a\Pr(Z\ge a)$ for nonnegative $Z$; the union bound just sums indicators.

The route fundamentally demands the correctness of every pairwise comparison. For $L=2$, set $x=(a,-a)$ with $a>0$ and write the contrast coefficient of $y_i$ as $d_i=a+\zeta_i$, where $\zeta_i$ is a nondegenerate centered Gaussian when $\sigma>0$. The difference between the aligned and swapped inner products is $4d_id_j$. Let $q=\Pr(d_i>0)$, so $0<q<1$. All pairs prefer the true relative orientation precisely when all $d_i$ have the same sign, an event of probability $q^N+(1-q)^N$ for $N\ge2$. This tends to zero at fixed noise. Thus this all-pairs proof cannot exploit arbitrarily many samples at fixed positive noise, even in the simplest nontrivial signal length.

This is failure of the certificate, not of SDP tightness. In fact for any $N$ and $L=2$, after writing each observation as $(m_i+d_i,m_i-d_i)$, all pairwise maxima are simultaneously attained by choosing its swap so that the resulting contrast is $|d_i|$. The same blockwise upper bound proves tightness for all such data. For $N=2$ and arbitrary $L$, one can also attain the single cross-block maximum, proving tightness at every noise value. These elementary edge cases show why failure of exact recovery must not be equated with failure of tightness.

Verification and adversarial audit

The three perturbation terms and their constants were checked separately. The $L=2$ contrast formula verifies an explicit obstruction to using uniform pairwise truth recovery as a large-sample tightness argument. The $N=1$ case is constant objective, and $L=1$ has only one assignment. Periodic signals have $\gamma=0$, so the positive-margin argument is deliberately not applied to them.
Audit corrections.
Tightness was kept separate from recovery of the planted shifts. Zero-margin periodic signals and the constant-objective cases were excluded from the margin claim and handled separately.

Scope and remaining gaps

No necessary noise condition or sharp $N,L,x$ dependence is obtained. The next missing ingredient is a global certificate that tolerates inconsistent noisy pairwise maximizers. The finite-dimensional $L=2$ and $N\le2$ calculations and the small-noise sufficient bound are not presented as new original-target progress.
