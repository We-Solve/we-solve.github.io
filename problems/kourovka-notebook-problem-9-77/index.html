Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.77

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$ and elements $x,y\in G$, let $\langle x,y\rangle$ denote the subgroup they generate. Interpret the source term binary finite as \[ \forall x,y\in G,\qquad |\langle x,y\rangle|<\infty. \] The supplied record does not define this term, so this interpretation requires confirmation against the cited source. A group $G$ is finitely generated if $G=\langle S\rangle$ for some finite subset $S\subseteq G$. It is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$, there are a finite group $F$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ such that $\varphi(g)\ne 1$.

For a prime $p$, a $p$-subgroup means a subgroup each of whose elements has order $p^k$ for some integer $k\geq 0$. A Sylow $p$-subgroup is understood here to mean a $p$-subgroup maximal under inclusion among the $p$-subgroups of $G$. The supplied record does not specify its infinite-group Sylow convention; this convention also requires source confirmation.

Under these terminology conventions, the question attributed to V. P. Shunkov is: does there exist a group $G$ such that $G$ is infinite, finitely generated, residually finite, and binary finite, and \[ \forall p\text{ prime}\;\forall P\leq G,\qquad P\text{ a Sylow }p\text{-subgroup of }G \ \Longrightarrow\ |P|<\infty? \]

The source additionally reports that A. V. Rozhkov proved the corresponding existence assertion when every Sylow subgroup is required only to be locally finite, meaning that each of its finitely generated subgroups is finite. This is a supplied historical assertion, attributed to a 1997 doctoral dissertation, and is not independently verified here.

Short English statement

Does an infinite finitely generated residually finite group exist in which every two elements generate a finite subgroup and every Sylow subgroup is finite? The interpretations of "binary finite" and "Sylow subgroup" stated above require source confirmation. The record reports that Rozhkov obtained existence with locally finite Sylow subgroups instead.
