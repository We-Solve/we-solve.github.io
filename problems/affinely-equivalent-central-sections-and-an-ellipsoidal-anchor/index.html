Math-1390

Affinely equivalent central sections and an ellipsoidal anchor

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be a star body about $o=0$: it is compact, contains the origin in its interior, and has continuous positive radial function $\rho_K(u)=\max\{t\geq0:tu\in K\}$ for $u\in S^{n-1}$, with every ray intersection equal to $[0,\rho_K(u)]u$. Let $G(n,k)$ be the Grassmannian of $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. Two sections are affinely equivalent if there is a bijective affine map between their ambient $k$-planes taking one section onto the other. Such a map is not assumed to fix the origin.

The source asks whether \[ \left[\ \forall S,T\in G(n,k)\ \exists\text{ an affine bijection }a_{S,T}:S\to T, \quad a_{S,T}(K\cap S)=K\cap T\ \right] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\text{ is an ellipsoid}. \] Here an ellipsoid is an affine image of a Euclidean ball; its center is not prescribed to be $o$. The question is asked for star bodies and also for the narrower class of convex bodies. The supplied excerpt omits the section's introductory dimension assumptions. The nontrivial intended range should be checked against that missing paragraph; $2\leq k\leq n-1$ is the range used in the supplied partial result, rather than an explicitly recovered hypothesis of the original question. The cases $k=1$ and $k=n$ cannot be included in a universal affirmative conjecture: one-dimensional star-body sections are all intervals, while there is only one full-dimensional section.

The supplied machine research reports the following restricted statement. If $K=-K$ is an origin-symmetric star body, $2\leq k\leq n-1$, and $\mathcal F\subseteq G(n,k)$ satisfies \[ \forall L\in G(n,2)\ \exists S\in\mathcal F:\ L\subseteq S, \qquad \forall S\in\mathcal F:\ K\cap S\text{ is an ellipsoid}, \] then $K$ is an origin-centered ellipsoid. In particular, under origin symmetry, mutual affine equivalence of all central $k$-sections together with one ellipsoidal section implies the same conclusion. Bounded centrally symmetric sections have a unique center, so an affine equivalence between these sections fixes $o$ and is linear. This origin-symmetry assumption belongs to the partial result and is not imposed on the original star-body question.

The local summary describes the nonsymmetric question as remaining open, says many symmetric convex Banach-space cases are settled, and mentions a 2025--2026 preprint claiming the remaining finite-dimensional cases without a verified proof. No authors, precise hypotheses, or citation for that claim are supplied; these are attributed research-aid statements, not independently verified status assertions.

Short English statement

Must a star body whose central sections of a fixed dimension all have the same affine shape be an ellipsoid? The question also asks this for convex bodies, without assuming symmetry about the origin. The supplied partial result concerns an origin-symmetric body with enough ellipsoidal sections to cover every two-plane; the original dimension assumptions and the claimed recent literature need source review.
