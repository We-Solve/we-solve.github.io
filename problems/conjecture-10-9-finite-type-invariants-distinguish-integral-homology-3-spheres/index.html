Math-455

Conjecture 10.9 — Finite type invariants distinguish integral homology 3-spheres.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An integral homology $3$-sphere is a closed $3$-manifold $M$ with $H_*(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_*(S^3;{\mathbb{Z}})$. In the usual oriented category, let $\mathcal H$ be their equivalence classes. For the source's finite-type filtration $F_\bullet$ on the vector space generated by $\mathcal H$, an invariant $v$ of degree at most $d$ is a linear functional vanishing on $F_{d+1}$. The conjecture is that finite-type invariants separate these manifolds: \[ \forall M,N\in\mathcal H,\quad M\not\cong N\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists d<\infty\ \exists v\text{ of finite type of degree at most }d: v(M)\ne v(N). \] Equivalently, equality of all finite-type invariants implies equivalence of the integral homology spheres. The excerpt does not fix the filtration, coefficient ring, or whether equivalence preserves orientation; rational coefficients and oriented equivalence are the standard contextual reading, not additional printed hypotheses. It refers to Conjecture 11.2 without supplying that statement.

The subsequent supplied subsection gives background rather than an additional separation assertion. A finite-type invariant is primitive when \[ v(M_1\mathbin{\#}M_2)=v(M_1)+v(M_2) \] for all integral homology $3$-spheres $M_1,M_2$. Write $\mathcal A(\varnothing;R)$ for the Jacobi-diagram module over a coefficient ring $R$, with no external legs and the usual antisymmetry and IHX relations, and $\mathcal A(\varnothing;R)_{\mathrm{conn}}$ for the submodule spanned by connected trivalent diagrams. The source states the graded-vector-space identification \[ \mathcal A(\varnothing;{\mathbb{Q}}) \cong \operatorname{Sym}_{{\mathbb{Q}}} \left(\mathcal A(\varnothing;{\mathbb{Q}})_{\mathrm{conn}}\right). \] No explicit dimension problem is supplied by the intervening subsection heading.

Short English statement

Any two distinct integral homology $3$-spheres should be distinguished by some finite-type invariant of finite degree. The supplied primitive-invariant and connected-diagram definitions are accompanying background.
