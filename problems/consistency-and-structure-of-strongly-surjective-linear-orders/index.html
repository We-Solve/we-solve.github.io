Math-7019

Consistency and Structure of Strongly Surjective Linear Orders

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A linear order is a set equipped with a total order. Every subset is given the induced order. A map $f:L\to K$ is order-preserving if $a\le_L b$ implies $f(a)\le_K f(b)$. For a nonempty linear order $L$, write $\operatorname{SS}(L)$ if, for every nonempty subset $K\subseteq L$, there is an order-preserving surjection $f:L\twoheadrightarrow K$. This is the strong-surjectivity property used below. The empty-order convention is not supplied and is discussed explicitly below.

Write $\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}$ for Martin's axiom for families of at most $\aleph_1$ dense subsets of a partial order satisfying the countable chain condition. Write $\mathrm{PFA}$ for the proper forcing axiom: every family of at most $\aleph_1$ dense subsets of a proper partial order is met by a filter. A Countryman line is a linear order $C$ of cardinality $\aleph_1$ such that $C\times C$, ordered coordinatewise, is the union of countably many chains. These are standard terminology conventions; the supplied record does not itself give these definitions.

The record asks the following questions. For the consistency questions, $\mathrm{ZFC}$ is the conventional background theory adopted here; the record does not explicitly specify a base theory or relative-consistency strength.

  1. Is the theory \[ \mathrm{ZFC}+\exists L\,\left(\operatorname{SS}(L)\land |L|>\aleph_2\right) \] consistent, where $L$ ranges over nonempty linear orders?

  2. Is it consistent that a strongly surjective linear order contains no minimal strongly surjective suborder? The word "minimal" is not defined in the supplied record. In particular, the record does not specify the comparison relation, whether suborders must be uncountable, or whether minimality is taken up to order isomorphism. Consequently this question cannot be given a unique precise quantified formulation from the supplied evidence. If singleton suborders are allowed and minimality means minimality under inclusion among nonempty strongly surjective suborders, every nonempty order contains such a minimal suborder. This observation identifies a missing qualification; it does not identify the intended formulation.

  3. Does $\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}$ imply \[ \exists L\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}\,\left(|L|>\aleph_0\land\operatorname{SS}(L)\right), \] where $L$ has the usual real order restricted to $L$?

  4. For every nonempty strongly surjective linear order $L$ and every $x\in L$, must $L\setminus\{x\}$, with its induced order, be strongly surjective? With the nonempty-order definition above, the substantive assertion is \[ \forall L\,\left[\operatorname{SS}(L)\Longrightarrow \forall x\in L\,\left(L\setminus\{x\}\ne\varnothing \Longrightarrow\operatorname{SS}(L\setminus\{x\})\right)\right]. \] The source also quantifies over deletion from singleton orders, but does not specify whether the empty order is admitted or called strongly surjective. That boundary case remains unresolved in this draft.

  5. Separately for each $A\in\{\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1},\mathrm{PFA}\}$, does $\mathrm{ZFC}+A$ imply \[ \exists C\,\left(C\text{ is a Countryman line}\land\operatorname{SS}(C)\right)? \] The wording "exist under" is interpreted here as an implication from the stated axiom; the record does not explicitly distinguish this reading from mere consistency together with that axiom.

The supplied registry labels the collection of questions as partially solved. Its dated assessment mentions strongly surjective Countryman lines under Martin's axiom and says that size, minimality, deletion, and other consistency questions remain open. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical conclusions. In particular, the assessment does not specify which version of Martin's axiom supports the cited Countryman-line result or give an item-by-item resolution.

Short English statement

A strongly surjective order maps onto each of its nonempty suborders by an order-preserving surjection. The questions concern possible cardinalities, minimal suborders, uncountable examples inside ${\mathbb{R}}$, deletion of one point, and Countryman examples under forcing axioms. Minimality, the empty-order boundary case, and the exact scope of the supplied partial-resolution assessment require review.
