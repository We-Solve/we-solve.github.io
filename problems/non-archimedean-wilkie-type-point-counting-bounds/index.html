Non-Archimedean Wilkie-Type Point-Counting Bounds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns non-Archimedean point counting but does not contain a recoverable conjecture. Its opening phrase, "Conjecture (namely with polylogarithmic instead of subexponential upper bounds)", lacks the preceding formulation. No precise open mathematical assertion can therefore be extracted without additional source information.

For context, let $p$ be a prime and $n\geq 1$. The field ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$ is the field of $p$-adic numbers, and ${\mathbb{C}}((t))$ is the field of formal Laurent series over ${\mathbb{C}}$. For a subset $X\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_p^n$, rational-point counting concerns points in $X\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^n$ satisfying a height bound; the record supplies neither a height convention nor the precise counting function. For $X\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}((t))^n$ and an integer $d\geq 0$, the polynomial points of coordinate degree at most $d$ form \[ X[d]=X\cap\left({\mathbb{C}}[t]_{\leq d}\right)^n, \qquad {\mathbb{C}}[t]_{\leq d}=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_jt^j:a_j\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}. \] This set need not be finite. This notation describes the polynomial-point setting mentioned in the record; it does not resolve what its later phrase "rational points of bounded height" means.

The supplied excerpt distinguishes three settings over ${\mathbb{C}}((t))$:

  1. In the subanalytic setting, it reports finiteness of bounded-height rational points on the transcendental part of any subanalytic set, while allowing growth arbitrarily fast with the degree in $t$, which plays the role of height. It does not define the analytic language, the transcendental part, the counted points, or the precise quantifiers governing arbitrarily fast growth.
  2. In a non-Archimedean Pfaffian setting, described as new in the excerpt, it reports a proved analogue of Wilkie's conjecture in all dimensions. The Pfaffian class and the exact bound are absent.
  3. In the axiomatic Hensel minimal setting, described as the most general setting under discussion, it reports that finiteness can fail even for definable transcendental curves. It then proposes bounding dimension in place of infinite cardinality, without specifying the dimension notion, the object whose dimension is bounded, or the bound.
The opening reference to polylogarithmic rather than subexponential bounds supplies no counting parameter, exponent, constant dependencies, or hypotheses. In particular, these fragments do not determine a universal polylogarithmic counting conjecture.

The registry labels the record open, but also marks its statement unrecoverable. Its supplied statement-verification note says that the official contribution reports results in the listed settings, including a proved Pfaffian analogue of Wilkie's conjecture, rather than posing the extracted text as an open problem. These are supplied descriptions, not independently verified mathematical conclusions. This draft records the available mathematical context and the failure to recover a precise target; it asserts no new conjecture or theorem.

Short English statement

This record discusses rational-point counting over ${\mathbb{Q}}_p$ and polynomial-point counting over ${\mathbb{C}}((t))$. It reports different behavior in subanalytic, Pfaffian, and Hensel minimal settings, but the conjecture itself is truncated. The supplied open label does not identify a precise open assertion, and the supplied verification note describes reported results rather than an extracted open problem.
