Counting Rational Caustics in Convex Billiards

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question asks: How many $(m,n)$-rational caustics should a sufficiently smooth strictly convex billiard domain have?

The following definitions explain a usual planar interpretation; the source record does not explicitly specify the ambient dimension or all conventions needed to make this interpretation definitive. Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a bounded strictly convex domain with sufficiently smooth boundary. Its billiard trajectories consist of straight segments in $\Omega$, reflected at $\partial\Omega$ according to equality of the angles of incidence and reflection. Strict convexity is not here strengthened to everywhere positive boundary curvature.

In this setting, a regular closed curve $K\subset\Omega$ is called a caustic when reflection of a billiard segment tangent to $K$ produces another segment tangent to $K$. The admissible regularity, convexity, and possible degeneracy of $K$ require specification.

To describe a conventional meaning of the integer pair, orient $\partial\Omega$, let $L$ be its perimeter, and lift the successive boundary arclength coordinates of a trajectory to real numbers $s_j$, choosing $s_j<s_{j+1}<s_j+L$. A periodic trajectory returning to its initial billiard state after $n$ reflections has type $(m,n)$ when \[ s_n=s_0+mL. \] A commonly intended meaning of an $(m,n)$-rational caustic is a caustic carrying a family of periodic tangent trajectories of this type. The record does not define this term, specify whether every tangent trajectory must be periodic, or distinguish this condition from a condition stated only in terms of rational rotation number. It also does not specify whether $n$ is the least period or whether $m$ and $n$ must be coprime.

Once those conventions and an admissible class of caustics are fixed, the counting quantity can be written \[ N_{m,n}(\Omega) :=\#\{K:K\text{ is an admissible }(m,n)\text{-rational caustic of }\Omega\}, \] where curves are counted as geometric subsets and $\#$ denotes cardinality. This is a possible precise counting formulation, rather than an additional assertion supplied by the record.

Unresolved scope. The source does not say whether $(m,n)$ is fixed, which pairs are allowed, what differentiability threshold is intended, or whether the requested count concerns every domain, a generic domain, or an expectation under a probability distribution. Consequently, it does not determine a unique quantified mathematical assertion. No value, bound, finiteness claim, or genericity claim is asserted here.

Short English statement

The question asks how many rational caustics a sufficiently smooth strictly convex billiard table should possess. Under a usual planar interpretation, these are curves preserved by tangency under billiard reflection and associated with periodic trajectories of prescribed rotation type. The source leaves the precise caustic convention, smoothness threshold, admissible integer pairs, and meaning of the requested count unresolved.
