Math-7355

Independent Word Equations with Nonperiodic Solutions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X=\{x_1,x_2,x_3\}$ be three formal unknowns. Under the constant-free interpretation of word equations, an equation is a pair $U=V$ with $U,V\in X^*$, where $X^*$ is the free monoid with identity the empty word $\varepsilon$. For a target alphabet $A$, a substitution is a monoid homomorphism $h:X^*\to A^*$; thus empty images are permitted under this convention. For a system $S$, define \[ \operatorname{Sol}_A(S)=\{h:X^*\to A^*\text{ a monoid homomorphism}:h(U)=h(V)\text{ for every }(U=V)\in S\}. \] A three-equation system $S=\{E_1,E_2,E_3\}$ is independent if no proper subsystem has the same solution set, equivalently if \[ \operatorname{Sol}_A(S)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sol}_A(S\setminus\{E_i\})\qquad(i=1,2,3). \] In particular, for each $i$ there must be a substitution satisfying the other two equations and failing $E_i$; these witnesses need not be the same substitution.

Under the usual common-power convention, a substitution $h$ is periodic if there exist $p\in A^*$ and integers $n_1,n_2,n_3\geq0$ such that \[ h(x_j)=p^{n_j}\qquad(j=1,2,3). \] It is nonperiodic if no such common word and exponents exist.

The supplied question asks whether there exists an independent system of exactly three equations in these three unknowns that admits a nonperiodic solution. With the conventions above and a specified target alphabet $A$, this is the existence assertion \[ \exists S\subseteq X^*,\!\times X^*:\quad |S|=3,\quad \bigwedge_{i=1}^{3}\operatorname{Sol}_A(S)\subsetneq\operatorname{Sol}_A(S\setminus\{E_i\}),\quad \exists h\in\operatorname{Sol}_A(S)\ \forall p\in A^*\ \forall(n_1,n_2,n_3)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}^{3}\ \exists j\in\{1,2,3\}:h(x_j)\ne p^{n_j}, \] where $S=\{E_1,E_2,E_3\}$. Here the pair notation for equations means $S\subseteq X^*\times X^*$.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not specify the target alphabet or its quantification, whether constants are allowed in equations, whether substitutions must be nonerasing, or an explicit definition of nonperiodicity. The displayed formulation uses constant-free equations, possibly erasing substitutions, and the common-power definition; it is conditional on those being the intended conventions. If constants are intended, their alphabet and the requirement that substitutions fix them must be specified.

Short English statement

Does an independent system of three word equations in three unknowns have a solution whose three assigned words are not all powers of one common word? Independence means that removing any equation enlarges the solution set. The source leaves alphabet, constant, and substitution conventions implicit, so the formalization requires review.
