Math-1884

Entire solutions of higher-order Briot–Bouquet equations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a positive derivative order and let $F(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{C}}[X,Y]$ be irreducible. If its total degree is $d\geq1$, its highest-degree homogeneous part is the sum of its monomials of total degree $d$. The source considers the remaining case where that part has only one distinct linear factor, and gives the normalized form \[ F(X,Y)=(X-aY)^d+Q_{d-1}(X,Y),\qquad a\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad \deg Q_{d-1}\leq d-1, \] with irreducibility retained for the whole polynomial. All coefficients are constant; the degree bound is total degree.

An entire solution is a holomorphic function $y:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$ satisfying identically \[ (y^{(k)}(z)-ay(z))^d+Q_{d-1}(y^{(k)}(z),y(z))=0. \] Problems. Classify all entire solutions for the stated remaining equations. Are all of them exponential polynomials? Under the common constant-coefficient differential-equation meaning, this last assertion asks whether every solution has a representation \[ y(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_j(z)e^{\lambda_jz},\qquad p_j\in{\mathbb{C}}[z],\quad\lambda_j\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad m<\infty, \] with the zero solution allowed when it satisfies the equation. The excerpt does not define exponential polynomial; alternatives allowing polynomial exponents $e^{P_j(z)}$ give a different class and must not be silently identified with this convention.

The claimed equivalence of the linear-factor description and the displayed form also needs a qualification: a general linear factor is $\alpha X+\beta Y$, and the normalization $X-aY$ presupposes $\alpha\ne0$. The excerpt does not discuss a purely $Y$-factor. Its title says higher-order equations without an explicit lower bound on $k$, so the exact derivative-order range also requires confirmation.

The supplied record attributes this remaining 2024 case to Alexandre Eremenko and cited proposers. Its summary reports no published resolution found, while describing the surrounding Briot--Bouquet classification as well developed. The background says explicit solved/counterexample updates in the author index were applied through the reported access date. These are supplied provenance and status claims, not independent verification.

Short English statement

Classify entire solutions of irreducible autonomous equations whose top homogeneous part is a power of $y^{(k)}-ay$, and determine whether every solution is an exponential polynomial. The exact derivative-order range, exponential-polynomial convention, and normalization of the unique linear factor need checking.
