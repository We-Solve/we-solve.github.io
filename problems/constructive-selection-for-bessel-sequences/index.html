Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let $\{u_j\}_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{C}^n$ be a finite Bessel sequence with bound $1$ and $\lVert u_j\rVert^2\leq\varepsilon$. Given scalars $t_j\in[0,1]$, design a constructive algorithm that finds $J^\prime\subset J$ satisfying $\left\lVert\sum_{j\in J^\prime}u_ju_j^*-\sum_{j\in J}t_ju_ju_j^*\right\rVert=O(\varepsilon^{1/8})$.

Source scope and input convention

Iverson's report, printed pages 957--959, asks for a constructive method after stating a dimension-independent existence theorem. A search through all subsets, conditional on that unproved existence theorem, would not explain an efficient constructive mechanism. We analyze two explicit algorithms in exact arithmetic. For bit complexity, the input vectors' real and imaginary parts and the weights are assumed rational; arbitrary real inputs need an oracle or representation. Put $A_j=u_ju_j^*$, $S=\sum_jA_j\preceq I$, and $T=\sum_jt_jA_j$. Zero vectors may be discarded.

A deterministic second-moment algorithm

Let $X_j$ be independent Bernoulli variables of means $t_j$. For $D=\sum_j(X_j-t_j)A_j$, expanding the squared Frobenius norm and using zero means and independence gives \[ \mathbb E\|D\|_F^2=\sum_j t_j(1-t_j)\operatorname{tr}(A_j^2) \le\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\operatorname{tr}S\le\frac{\varepsilon n}{4}. \] Here $A_j^2=\|u_j\|^2A_j$, and $0\le t_j(1-t_j)\le1/4$. The trace bound follows from $S\preceq I$ by evaluating on an orthonormal basis and summing. Also $\|D\|\le\|D\|_F$: for an orthonormal eigenbasis of the Hermitian matrix, the largest absolute eigenvalue is bounded by the square root of the sum of their squares.

Derandomize explicitly. After some choices have been fixed, let $D_0=\sum_{j\text{ fixed}}(X_j-t_j)A_j$. The conditional expectation is \[ \|D_0\|_F^2+\sum_{j\text{ unfixed}}t_j(1-t_j)\operatorname{tr}(A_j^2). \] For the next index compute the two values obtained by choosing zero or one. Their weighted average, with weights $1-t_j,t_j$, is the current value, by expansion of the square. Choose the smaller; when $t_j=0$ or one choose that deterministic outcome. Each step cannot increase the conditional expectation. At the end this proves the constructive bound \[ \left\|\sum_{j\in J'}A_j-T\right\| \le\frac12\sqrt{\varepsilon\operatorname{tr}S} \le\frac12\sqrt{\varepsilon n}. \] Maintaining the dense $D_0$ and its inner products uses $O(|J|n^2)$ arithmetic operations after forming the rank-one matrices. All decisions are rational comparisons for rational complex data; sums and products of the given entries have polynomial bit length in their explicit total encoding. No eigenvalue approximation is needed for this certificate. This meets an $\varepsilon^{1/8}$ error bound whenever $\operatorname{tr}S\le4\varepsilon^{-3/4}$, but does not cover arbitrary trace or dimension.

The exact limitation of this potential.
Take $k$ copies of $e_i/\sqrt k$ in each coordinate $i=1,\ldots,n$, with $t_j=1/2$ and $\varepsilon=1/k$. Then $S=I$ and every equality in the second-moment bound holds: $\mathbb E\|D\|_F^2=n/(4k)$. Consequently a proof that controls operator error only through this initial Frobenius expectation retains a dimension factor. This is not a lower bound on attainable error: when $k$ is even, choosing half the copies in every coordinate gives zero error. The failed inference is treating the trace bound as though it were dimension independent.

A stronger complete algorithm for commuting rank-one matrices

Suppose additionally that all $A_j$ commute. Two nonzero rank-one matrices $uu^*,vv^*$ either have $u^*v=0$, or, if they commute and $u^*v\ne0$, their nonzero products have both range $\operatorname{span}(u)$ and range $\operatorname{span}(v)$; these ranges must coincide. Hence the vectors partition into parallel classes along mutually orthogonal lines. Choose a unit direction $q_\ell$ for each class. Within that class write $A_j=a_j q_\ell q_\ell^*$, $a_j=\|u_j\|^2\le\varepsilon$, and set $b_\ell=\sum t_j a_j$.

In any fixed ordering of the class, successive prefix sums start at zero and end at $\sum a_j\ge b_\ell$. If the target is an endpoint take it; otherwise choose the nearer of the two adjacent prefix sums bracketing $b_\ell$. Their distance is at most $\varepsilon$, so the error is at most $\varepsilon/2$. Make this choice independently in each class and take the union of the selected prefixes. The error operator is diagonal in the orthogonal directions $q_\ell$, zero on their orthogonal complement, and therefore has norm at most $\varepsilon/2$. One can detect the classes by exact parallelism and orthogonality tests and use the weights $\|u_j\|^2$ directly, without normalizing directions numerically. A simple pairwise implementation is polynomial. For $0<\varepsilon\le1$, the error is at most $\varepsilon^{1/8}/2$. For $\varepsilon\ge1$, even the empty subset has error $\|T\|\le1\le\varepsilon^{1/8}$, since $0\preceq T\preceq S\preceq I$. If $\varepsilon=0$, every vector is zero and the conclusion is exact.
