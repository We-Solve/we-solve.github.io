Math-511

Major problems 9 — Kervaire invariant one in dimension 126

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\pi_{126}^S=\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\pi_{126+j}(S^j)$ be the stable homotopy group of spheres. By Pontryagin--Thom, its elements are represented by closed smooth stably framed $126$-manifolds. Their Kervaire invariant lies in ${\mathbb{F}}_2$; in this dimension it is the Arf invariant of the framing-induced quadratic refinement of the middle-dimensional mod-two intersection form.

The question is whether there exists \[\theta_6\in\pi_{126}^S,\qquad\operatorname{Kervaire}(\theta_6)=1.\] Equivalently, in the mod-two Adams spectral sequence \[E_2^{s,t}=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal A}^{s,t}({\mathbb{F}}_2,{\mathbb{F}}_2)\Longrightarrow(\pi_{t-s}^S)^\wedge_2,\] where $\mathcal A$ is the mod-two Steenrod algebra, does \[h_6^2\in E_2^{2,128}\] survive nontrivially to $E_\infty$? Here $h_6$ has bidegree $(1,64)$, so the square has stem $128-2=126$. Permanent survival means a nonzero permanent class, not merely the absence of one selected differential.

The supplied research record reports an affirmative result by Lin--Wang--Xu, On the last Kervaire invariant problem (December 2024), proving this permanent survival and hence existence of $\theta_6$. It says that together with the earlier Browder, Mahowald--Tangora, Barratt--Jones--Mahowald and Hill--Hopkins--Ravenel results, framed Kervaire-invariant-one manifolds exist exactly in dimensions $2,6,14,30,62,126$. This is retained as the record's literature resolution, not a proof or independent verification here.

Short English statement

Does a framed Kervaire-invariant-one element exist in stable stem $126$, equivalently survive as $h_6^2$ in Adams filtration two? The record reports Lin--Wang--Xu's affirmative December 2024 result.
