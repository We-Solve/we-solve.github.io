Math-4072

Finiteness of R-Equivalence Classes in Nonabelian Cohomology

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field and $G$ a finite solvable group. Interpret $G$ as the constant finite group scheme over $K$; this convention makes explicit the usual interpretation of the supplied statement, which specifies no Galois action. Write $H^1(K,G)$ for the pointed set of isomorphism classes of $G$-torsors over $\operatorname{Spec}K$. Equivalently, if $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is a separable closure of $K$, then \[ H^1(K,G)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}\!\left(\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K),G\right)/G, \] where $G$ acts by conjugation; surjectivity of the homomorphisms is not required.

Use the following standard convention for $R$-equivalence. Two classes $\alpha,\beta\in H^1(K,G)$ are directly $R$-equivalent if there are a Zariski open subset $U\subseteq\mathbb A^1_K$ containing $0$ and $1$, and a $G$-torsor $\mathcal T\to U$, whose fibers at $0$ and $1$ represent $\alpha$ and $\beta$, respectively. Let $R$ be the equivalence relation generated by direct $R$-equivalence, and set \[ r(K,G):=\#\left(H^1(K,G)/R\right). \] The supplied extract does not itself define $R$-equivalence, so this convention requires confirmation against the source.

Finiteness question. Is it true that, for every number field $K$ and every finite solvable group $G$, \[ r(K,G)<\infty? \]

Additional requested relation, unresolved in the supplied evidence. If $r(K,G)$ is finite, the supplied statement asks for its relation to the minimum number of $G$-torsors over a purely transcendental extension $K(t_1,\ldots,t_d)$ needed to densely parametrize $H^1(K,G)$. Neither the meaning of dense parametrization nor the allowed specializations, the quantification of $d$, or the proposed relation is specified. Thus this additional request cannot be made into a precise assertion from the supplied record alone. The extract contains a preceding claim involving a group $A$, but omits its hypotheses and ends with the incomplete phrase "for all sufficiently large"; that claim cannot be transferred to $G$.

The supplied literature assessment also mentions preservation of finiteness under split extensions. It provides no exact formulation or hypotheses for that additional question.

Short English statement

For every number field $K$ and finite solvable constant group $G$, ask whether there are finitely many classes of $G$-torsors under the equivalence relation generated by one-parameter families joining their fibers. The requested connection with dense parametrization, and the reference to split extensions, need further source clarification. The supplied open status is dated curation.
