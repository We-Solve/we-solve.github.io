Even-dimensional knotted tori and two isotopy reductions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 1.20 has three separate Lagrangian-isotopy problems. Use the canonical cotangent symplectic structure on $T^*\Sigma_g$ and the standard symplectic form on ${\mathbb{R}}^{2n}={\mathbb{C}}^n$. Lagrangian isotopy means a smooth family of Lagrangian embeddings; it is not automatically Hamiltonian isotopy.

(a) For $g>1$, if a closed connected oriented embedded Lagrangian surface $L\subset T^*\Sigma_g$ satisfies \[ [L]=[\Sigma_g]\in H_2(T^*\Sigma_g;{\mathbb{Z}}), \] must it be Lagrangian isotopic to the zero-section? The source reports positive genus-zero and genus-one results of Richard Hind and Alex Ivrii, respectively. The aid supplies a reduction for the higher-genus setting: let $\lambda$ be the canonical one-form and $\pi:T^*\Sigma_g\to\Sigma_g$. The degree-one projection and Lagrangian self-intersection identity imply that $L$ has genus $g$, and \[ (\pi|_L)^*:H^1(\Sigma_g;{\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow H^1(L;{\mathbb{R}}) \] is an isomorphism. Hence choose a closed one-form $\alpha$ on $\Sigma_g$ with \[ (\pi|_L)^*[\alpha]=-[\lambda|_L]. \] The fibre translations $\tau_{s\alpha}(q,p)=(q,p+s\alpha_q)$ are symplectic and carry $L$ through Lagrangians to an exact Lagrangian, because $\tau_\alpha^*\lambda=\lambda+\pi^*\alpha$. The translation need not be Hamiltonian when $[\alpha]\ne0$. Reaching an exact representative does not prove isotopy to the zero-section; the aid leaves that stronger problem unresolved.

(b) For $n>2$, is there an embedded Lagrangian torus $T^n\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$ not Lagrangian isotopic to the Clifford torus $\{|z_1|=\cdots=|z_n|=1\}$? The source reports a negative answer for $n=2$ by Ivrii. The aid reports that the corrected 2024 Dimitroglou Rizell--Evans construction gives two monotone Lagrangian $n$-tori that are not smoothly isotopic for every even $n\geq4$. Here monotone means the symplectic-area homomorphism on relative discs is a positive multiple of the Maslov index. If both tori were Lagrangian isotopic to the Clifford torus they would be smoothly isotopic to each other; therefore at least one gives an affirmative answer to (b) in each such even dimension. A distinction only up to Hamiltonian isotopy would not suffice for this question. The record supplies no corresponding conclusion in all odd dimensions $n>2$.

(c) For $n>2$, can a Lagrangian submanifold asymptotic to a Lagrangian plane fail to be Lagrangian isotopic to that plane? The source reports a negative answer in dimension $n=2$ by Eliashberg--Polterovich. The meaning of asymptotic and allowed isotopies is not fixed. The aid's reduction concerns the strong flat-at-infinity version: properly embedded Lagrangian planes $L\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ equal to a fixed plane outside a compact set, and isotopies fixed outside one common compact set.

For $n>1$, any such fixed-end Lagrangian isotopy is, up to reparametrization of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, induced by a compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy of ${\mathbb{C}}^n$. Indeed its deformation one-forms \[ \beta_t=F_t^*(\iota_{\partial_tF_t}\omega) \] are closed and compactly supported, and $H_c^1({\mathbb{R}}^n;{\mathbb{R}})=0$ for $n>1$. Compactly supported primitives extend to ambient Hamiltonians, with a sign chosen to match the Hamiltonian-vector-field convention. This equates two notions of an existing fixed-end isotopy; it does not produce an isotopy or rule out knots. It also does not treat arbitrary decay-only asymptotic ends. The aid reports (a) and (c) as still unresolved in their natural strong forms; its account does not claim a new current literature assessment.

The original observation that contemporary holomorphic-curve techniques seemed unavailable in higher dimensions belongs to the 2003 workshop context and is not asserted as a present limitation.

Short English statement

The aid reports knotted tori in every even dimension at least four, answering part (b) there. For cotangent surfaces it reduces the homology condition to an exact representative. For flat-ended planes it upgrades any existing fixed-end Lagrangian isotopy to a compactly supported Hamiltonian one. Neither reduction solves parts (a) or (c).
