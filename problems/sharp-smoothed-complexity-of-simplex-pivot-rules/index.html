Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Smoothed analysis studies expected performance after random input perturbation. A projected polyhedron is the image under a linear map; its edges encode steps in the relevant shadow-vertex simplex analysis.

A linear program optimizes $c^Tx$ over a polyhedron $\{x:Ax\leq b\}$. In the simplex method a pivot moves between adjacent basic feasible solutions; a pivot rule chooses the next such move.

A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed affine half-spaces; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Its faces are its intersections with supporting hyperplanes.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For smoothed analysis of the simplex method, can the expected number of edges of a projected polyhedron be bounded sharply by $\Theta(d^{3/2}\log n)$ as $\sigma\to\infty$, with a lower bound depending on $\sigma$? What bounds hold if only a random $\varepsilon$-fraction of the entries of $(A,b)$ is perturbed, or if other pivot rules are used? [S2]

A source discrepancy and a sparse-perturbation obstruction

The preserved catalogue displays $\Theta(d^{3/2}\log n)$. Inspection of the actual primary PDF image on printed page 3230 shows $\Theta(d^{3/2}\sqrt{\ln n})$ in its fixed-$d$, centered Gaussian comparison. These are different targets; the original body is retained unchanged and neither sharp bound is proved here. The same primary page asks about perturbing only a random fraction of entries. We test whether full-Gaussian almost-sure nonsingularity can simply be carried over to independent sparse perturbations. Let $M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$. Independently for each of its four entries, with probability $\varepsilon\in[0,1]$ add a centered Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2>0$, and otherwise leave it unchanged. This Bernoulli selection model is one explicit meaning of sparse perturbation; it is not sampling an exactly fixed-size subset.
Proposition: The perturbed matrix is singular with probability exactly $(1-\varepsilon)^4$, for every $\sigma>0$. In particular this singular mass does not disappear as $\sigma$ tends to infinity when $\varepsilon<1$.
Proof: If no entry is selected, the original singular matrix remains, an event of probability $(1-\varepsilon)^4$. Conditional on any nonempty selection, the determinant is a nonzero polynomial in the selected independent Gaussian increments: set all increments but one selected entry to zero, and that entry changes the determinant linearly with coefficient 1 or $-1$. A nonzero polynomial evaluated at variables with a joint density vanishes with probability zero. To see this last fact, induct on the number of variables. In one variable there are finitely many roots. In more variables, write the polynomial as a polynomial in the last variable with coefficient polynomials in the others. At least one coefficient is nonzero; by induction the event that it vanishes has measure zero. Outside that event the last-variable polynomial has finitely many roots, and integration of the conditional zero probabilities finishes the argument. Every nonempty selection therefore contributes zero singular probability, proving the formula. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

All 16 selection masks were enumerated. For each nonempty mask, changing one selected entry by 1 while leaving the others at zero gave determinant $\pm1$, verifying nonidentity of its determinant polynomial. The atom probability was evaluated exactly at rational selection probabilities. This blocks an argument that assumes the sparse-perturbation distribution is fully absolutely continuous or automatically removes every degeneracy. It does not imply infinite simplex runtime: degeneracy can be handled by appropriate pivot conventions, and expected projected-edge bounds need a separate analysis. No sharp shadow-size estimate, sigma-dependent lower bound, or guarantee for other pivot rules is obtained.
