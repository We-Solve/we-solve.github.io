Math-14146

Borderline Sobolev Blowup Criteria for Hyperdissipative Flow

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $s=(d+2)/6$, where $d$ is the spatial dimension referred to in the source; the intended range of $d$ is not specified. For a vector field $v\colon{\mathbb{R}}^d\to{\mathbb{R}}^d$, use the standard inhomogeneous Sobolev space \[ H^s({\mathbb{R}}^d;{\mathbb{R}}^d)=\left\{v:\sum_{j=1}^d\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}(1+|\xi|^2)^s|\widehat v_j(\xi)|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\xi<\infty\right\}, \] with the displayed integral sum defining the squared norm. Here $\widehat v_j$ denotes the Fourier transform of the $j$-th component. This makes explicit the conventional interpretation of the source notation $H^s({\mathbb{R}}^d)$.

Source question, with unresolved hypotheses. Let $u$ be a solution of the intended $d$-dimensional hyperdissipative flow on ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, regular before a possible finite singular time $t_*$. Must every such finite-time blowup satisfy \[ \lim_{t\uparrow t_*}\|u(t)\|_{H^{(d+2)/6}({\mathbb{R}}^d)}^2=+\infty? \] The supplied background identifies the intended setting as hyperdissipative Navier--Stokes and calls this an endpoint lower-branch regularity criterion. However, the record does not specify the evolution equation, dissipation operator or exponent, admissible dimensions, initial-data assumptions, solution class, or meaning of regularity and blowup. Accordingly, the quantifier over solutions remains conditional on these missing hypotheses.

The source also asks whether finiteness of this norm on $[0,T]$ suffices for regularity, and describes this as equivalent. A possible precise interpretation is that, for every finite $T>0$, a solution regular on $[0,T)$ satisfying \[ \sup_{0\le t<T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{(d+2)/6}({\mathbb{R}}^d)}<\infty \] remains regular through $T$. This is an interpretation requiring review: the source does not distinguish uniform boundedness from pointwise finiteness, or specify whether a value at $T$ is available. Moreover, this boundedness criterion by itself excludes a finite bound near blowup; identifying it with the stated full limit to infinity requires additional solution-theoretic justification absent from the record.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every finite-time singularity of the intended hyperdissipative flow forces the $H^{(d+2)/6}({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ norm to tend to infinity. The equation, dissipation parameters, solution hypotheses, and interpretation of the proposed equivalent regularity criterion need clarification.
