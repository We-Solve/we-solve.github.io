Turing degrees of infinite-solution Hilbert's Tenth Problems

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Miller's Question 16 asks for the Turing degrees and relative reducibilities of \[ \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty}(\mathbb Q),\quad \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb Q),\quad \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty}(\mathbb Z),\quad \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb Z), \] and records $0'\leq_T\operatorname{HTP}_\infty(\mathbb Z)\leq_T0''$. The notation is not defined in the fragment. The aid explicitly reconstructs $\infty,1$ as infinitely many distinct first-coordinate values, not a one-variable polynomial problem.

Fix an effective encoding of integer-coefficient polynomials with a declared finite list of variables. For $R=\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Q$, write \[ S_R(f)=\{\mathbf x\in R^n:f(\mathbf x)=0\}. \] Define $\operatorname{HTP}(R)$ by nonemptiness of this set, $\operatorname{HTP}_\infty(R)$ by infinitude of the whole solution set, and $\operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(R)$ by infinitude of $\{x_1:\exists x_2,\ldots,x_n\ f(\mathbf x)=0\}$. Here $\leq_m,\leq_{tt},\leq_T$ mean many-one, truth-table and Turing reducibility, and $0',0''$ are the first two Turing jumps.

The aid reports \[ \operatorname{HTP}(R)\leq_m\operatorname{HTP}_\infty(R) \leq_{tt}\operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(R), \] and both infinitude sets are $\Pi^0_2$. The first reduction introduces a free solution coordinate; the second asks nonadaptively about each coordinate projection, since a set in a finite product is infinite exactly when some coordinate projection is infinite. Counting arbitrarily many distinct effective tuples, or first-coordinate values, gives the upper complexity bound.

Uniform MRDP is reported to yield \[ \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb Z) \text{ is }\Pi^0_2\text{-complete},\qquad \operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb Z)\equiv_T0''. \] It represents a uniformly enumerated c.e. set as the distinguished-coordinate projection of an integer Diophantine set; infinitude of c.e. sets is $\Pi^0_2$-complete. Infinite auxiliary witnesses do not affect that projected infinitude, but can affect total-solution infinitude.

The aid's finite-fiber transfer criterion is: for a surjective projection $\pi:T\to S$ preserving the distinguished coordinate, their first-coordinate projections agree regardless of fiber sizes. If in addition every fiber over a complete tuple $s\in S$ is finite, then $T$ is infinite exactly when $S$ is infinite. No uniform numerical fiber bound is required, but mere surjectivity is insufficient for this latter statement.

Accordingly a uniform finite-fold MRDP hypothesis would also give $\Pi^0_2$-completeness of total integer-solution infinitude. Separately, an existential Diophantine definition of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathbb Q$ would transfer the projected completeness result to $\operatorname{HTP}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb Q)$, of degree $0''$. Transferring total infinitude needs the additional finite-witness hypothesis; it is not a consequence of an unrestricted existential definition alone. These are conditional consequences, not supplied solutions of those open assumptions. The aid leaves the exact degree of $\operatorname{HTP}_\infty(\mathbb Z)$ and both rational variants unresolved.

Short English statement

The supplied reconstruction separates infinitely many tuples from infinitely many first-coordinate values. Uniform MRDP settles the latter integer problem at degree $0''$. Total infinitude is sensitive to infinitely many auxiliary witnesses, so finite-fold and rational definability consequences remain explicitly conditional; the other exact degrees are not supplied.
