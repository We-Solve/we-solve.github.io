Coercivity, collision support, and the identity braid for the class (1,0,-1)

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. For a planar collision-free periodic loop, identify the plane with $\mathbb C$ and define $k_{ij}=\deg(t\mapsto(q_i(t)-q_j(t))/|q_i(t)-q_j(t)|)\in\mathbb Z$. These pairwise winding numbers describe homology information; they do not determine the full braid homotopy class. A trivial braid is a loop null-homotopic in the ordered collision-free configuration space. It is a stronger constraint than having every pairwise winding number zero. The class $(1,0,-1)$ uses the source’s ordering of pairs.

An explicit admissible loop, without a dynamics claim.
Fix $q_1=(-2,0)$, $q_3=(2,0)$. Move $q_2$ along a smooth closed path that first makes one positive loop around $q_1$ without surrounding $q_3$, returns along the same connecting path, then makes one negative loop around $q_3$ without surrounding $q_1$, and returns. Choose all arcs a positive distance from both fixed points, with zero speed at concatenation points and smooth reparametrization. Degree additivity gives $(k_{12},k_{13},k_{23})=(1,0,-1)$: multiplying a relative vector by $-1$ does not change its winding, and retraced connecting arcs cancel. Subtracting the time-dependent center of mass fixes it at zero without changing any relative path. The resulting loop is smooth, collision free and has finite action for all positive masses.
What topology fails to provide.
This establishes nonemptiness of the variational class, but the loop does not satisfy Newton's equations. The nonzero edges 12 and 23 connect all bodies, so the elementary winding/oscillation argument bounds spatial size under bounded action. It does not exclude collision limits, at which winding information can disappear. The opposite signs alone provide no local collision-avoidance variation.
The trivial-braid clause.
Constant configurations are collision-free trivial-braid loops, but they cannot be Newtonian equilibria with positive masses. Indeed an equilibrium would have $\nabla U=0$, whereas Euler's homogeneity identity gives $\sum_iq_i\cdot\nabla_iU=-U<0$. Thus even this simplest topological representative is not a periodic solution. Existence of a collision-free Newtonian trivial-braid motion, existence in the mixed winding class and its minimization all remain unproved. The first gap is converting a topological loop into a collision-free critical point without changing the required class.
