Math-12774

A finite-period Levinson–Speiser identity for function-field numerators

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Brian Conrey asks to carry out "(the other) Levinson's method" for function fields. The original does not identify which Levinson argument, $L$-function family, mollifier, conductor/genus limit or intended zero-proportion conclusion is meant. The parenthetical is a genuine historical ambiguity.

The attached unreviewed aid treats a numerator-only analogue. Let $q>1$ be a prime power and $F(z)$ a nonconstant self-inversive complex polynomial, so $F(z)=\omega z^N\overline{F(1/\overline z)}$ with $|\omega|=1$, $N\ge1$, and nonzero constant term. Put $A(s)=F(q^{1/2-s})$ and choose the half-open period strip \[ S_q=\{s\in{\mathbb{C}}:0\le\operatorname{Im}s<2\pi/\log q\}. \] The aid attributes to Cohn's theorem the multiplicity-counted equality between zeros of $A$ with $\operatorname{Re}s>1/2$ and zeros of $A'$ with $\operatorname{Re}s<1/2$. Self-inversivity then gives \[ \#\{s\in S_q:A(s)=0,\ \operatorname{Re}s\ne1/2\} =2\#\{s\in S_q:A'(s)=0,\ \operatorname{Re}s<1/2\}. \] Critical-line zeros are omitted from both open-half-plane counts, and the half-open convention avoids periodic duplication.

For a smooth projective geometrically connected curve $C/{\mathbb{F}}_q$ of positive genus, write $Z_C(u)=P_C(u)/((1-u)(1-qu))$. Taking $F(z)=P_C(q^{-1/2}z)$ gives $A(s)=P_C(q^{-s})$. The aid calls the resulting equivalence a numerator Speiser criterion. It makes no claim for the derivative of the full rational zeta function, whose denominator changes the derivative. The genus-zero constant-numerator case must be excluded from a derivative-zero count, because its derivative is identically zero.

Separately the aid reports that a fixed-function, finite-mollifier height mean is just a finite Parseval norm. For a finite polynomial $B(s)=\sum_j b_jq^{-js}$ and real $\sigma$, its period mean is $\sum_j|b_j|^2q^{-2j\sigma}$; longer height intervals repeat the same period. It therefore says that a nontrivial asymptotic Levinson-proportion implementation must vary conductor, degree or genus. The source does not supply that growing-family implementation or prove a new general zero-proportion result.

Short English statement

Specify which Levinson method and growing function-field family are intended. The aid offers a finite-period numerator derivative criterion and a Parseval calculation, keeping both separate from the full-zeta derivative and the missing family asymptotics.
