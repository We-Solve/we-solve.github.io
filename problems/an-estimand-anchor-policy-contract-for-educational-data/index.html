Attempt: repeated errors as a diagnostic

The record is marked unrecoverable as a formal problem, and its literal questions about data and prior knowledge remain unchanged. I tested whether two repeated binary responses could separate a persistent latent cause from transient noise under an explicit model. Let $K\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ denote the persistent cause and $Y_j=K\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}E_j$, where $E_1,E_2$ are independent Bernoulli errors of rate $\epsilon<1/2$, independent of $K$.
Proposition: Population mean $m=E(Y_1)$ and disagreement rate $d=P(Y_1\ne Y_2)$ identify $\epsilon$ and $p$ in this model: \[ \epsilon=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2d}}2,\qquad p=\frac{m-\epsilon}{1-2\epsilon}. \] Persistent shared response-processing errors invalidate the intended causal interpretation.
Proof: The shared $K$ cancels from the disagreement event, which occurs exactly when $E_1\ne E_2$. Thus $d=2\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$. Of the two quadratic roots, only the displayed root is less than $1/2$. Also $m=p(1-\epsilon)+(1-p)\epsilon=\epsilon+p(1-2\epsilon)$, giving the second formula. At $\epsilon=1/2$, the denominator vanishes and $m=1/2$ for all $p$, so the excluded boundary is unidentifiable.

For a counterexample to causal labeling, compare a world with $K\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ and $E_1=E_2=0$ to a world with $K=0$ and a shared processing error $E_1=E_2=B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$. Both give $(Y_1,Y_2)=(B,B)$ in distribution. The second world violates independent transient errors, and its persistent observed failure cannot be distinguished from the first world's latent cause using these responses. ∎
