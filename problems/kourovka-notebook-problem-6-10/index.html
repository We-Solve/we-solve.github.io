Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.10

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $n\geq 1$ be an integer satisfying $p>2n+1$. Here positivity of $n$ is the usual matrix-size convention for $\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$; the source states only that $n$ is an integer. Write $\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$ for the group of invertible $n\times n$ complex matrices. A $p$-element is an element of finite order equal to a power of $p$, with the identity allowed. Let $x,y\in\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$ be $p$-elements, so that \[ \operatorname{ord}(x)=p^a,\qquad \operatorname{ord}(y)=p^b \quad\text{for some }a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}, \] and put $H=\langle x,y\rangle$, the subgroup generated by $x$ and $y$.

The source attributes to W. Feit and J. G. Thompson the following finite-case result: if $H$ is finite, then $H$ is an abelian $p$-group, meaning that it is abelian and has order $p^c$ for some $c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$.

Problem (J. D. Dixon, 1978). Under the same hypotheses on $p,n,x,y$, assume instead that $H$ is infinite. What structural conclusions can be drawn about $H$?

The supplied formulation is an open-ended request for structural information. It does not specify a particular property to prove, a classification to obtain, or a proposed analogue of the finite-case conclusion. Thus no more specific conjectural assertion is determined by the record.

Short English statement

J. D. Dixon asks what can be said about an infinite subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$ generated by two elements of finite $p$-power order when $p>2n+1$. The source contrasts this with an attributed theorem that every such finite subgroup is an abelian $p$-group. No particular conclusion for the infinite case is specified.
