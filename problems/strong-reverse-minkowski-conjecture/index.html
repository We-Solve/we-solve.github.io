Strong Reverse Minkowski Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n$ be a positive integer. A full-rank lattice in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a set $L=A{\mathbb{Z}}^n$, where $A$ is an invertible real $n\times n$ matrix. A convex body is a compact convex subset of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ with nonempty interior. Under the usual origin-symmetry convention, a symmetric convex body satisfies $K=-K$; the supplied record does not explicitly specify the center of symmetry. Define \[ B_2^n=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\leq 1\right\},\qquad tK=\{tx:x\in K\}\quad(t>0). \] For a finite set $S$, $|S|$ denotes its cardinality.

The supplied Strong Reverse Minkowski Conjecture asks, for every symmetric convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ and every full-rank lattice $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, whether \[ |K\cap L|\leq M\left(O(\log n)K,L\right), \qquad |B_2^n\cap L|\leq M\left(O(\sqrt{\log n})B_2^n,L\right). \] These are schematic source expressions, not fully defined mathematical assertions: the supplied complete record gives no definition of $M$.

Under the usual uniform asymptotic interpretation, the intended quantifiers would be the existence of constants $C_1,C_2>0$ and an integer $n_0\geq2$, independent of the body and lattice, such that for every integer $n\geq n_0$, every permitted $K$, and every full-rank $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, \[ |K\cap L|\leq M\left(C_1(\log n)K,L\right), \qquad |B_2^n\cap L|\leq M\left(C_2\sqrt{\log n}\,B_2^n,L\right). \] This interpretation is conditional and requires source confirmation. In particular, the definition of $M$, the intended uniformity of the constants, and the treatment of small dimensions remain unresolved. The source's wording includes $n=1$, where $\log n=0$; no replacement such as $\log(2n)$ is supplied.

Short English statement

The conjecture compares the number of lattice points in a symmetric convex body with an unspecified quantity $M$ evaluated on a logarithmic dilation; for the Euclidean unit ball, the proposed dilation has order $\sqrt{\log n}$. A precise complete statement requires the missing definition of $M$ and clarification of the asymptotic quantifiers and small dimensions.
