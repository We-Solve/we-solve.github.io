Math-3910

Almost-Disjoint Geometric Triangles in Three-Space

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For this draft, a geometric triangle means the filled, nondegenerate triangle \[ \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{a,b,c\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3, \] where $a,b,c$ are distinct noncollinear points; write $V(\Delta)=\{a,b,c\}$. This convention makes the intended formulation precise, but the supplied record does not explicitly distinguish filled triangles from their boundaries.

For each integer $n\geq 1$, define \[ t(n)=\max\left\{|\mathcal T|:\begin{array}{l} X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3,\quad |X|=n,\\ \mathcal T\text{ is a family of geometric triangles with }V(\Delta)\subseteq X,\\ \text{for all distinct }\Delta,\Delta'\in\mathcal T,\text{ either }\Delta\cap\Delta'=\varnothing,\\ \text{or }\Delta\cap\Delta'=\{v\}\text{ for some }v\in V(\Delta)\cap V(\Delta') \end{array}\right\}. \] Thus intersections along an edge, crossings, and contacts at points that are not vertices of both triangles are excluded. No general-position assumption on $X$ is stated, and the record does not require every point of $X$ to be used.

Question. Is \[ t(n)=o(n^2)\qquad(n\to\infty)? \] Explicitly, is it true that \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}, \qquad n\geq N\Longrightarrow t(n)\leq\varepsilon n^2? \] The supplied reconstructed statement is the basis for this question. The supplied original extraction contains only its concluding question followed by unrelated problems, so that extraction alone does not establish the definition of $t(n)$ or the triangle convention.

Short English statement

Among all sets of $n$ points in three-dimensional Euclidean space, consider the largest possible family of triangles whose pairwise intersections are empty or consist of one shared vertex. Must the number of triangles divided by $n^2$ tend to zero? This draft interprets triangles as filled and nondegenerate; that convention requires source confirmation.
