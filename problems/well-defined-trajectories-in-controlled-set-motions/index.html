Well-Defined Trajectories in Controlled Set Motions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega_0\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a bounded initial set. Write $\operatorname{meas}_2$ for planar Lebesgue measure. For the terminal time $T$ and effort bound $M$ appearing in the supplied model, consider a set motion $t\mapsto\Omega(t)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$, with $\Omega(0)=\Omega_0$, and a boundary control $\beta(t,x)$. Whenever the boundary integral is defined, set \[ E(t)=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\left(1+\beta(t,x)\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(x), \] where ${\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma$ denotes boundary arclength measure. The displayed optimization problem in the source is to minimize \[ J=\operatorname{meas}_2\left(\Omega(T)\right) \] subject to the initial condition and \[ \beta(t,x)\geq -1, \qquad E(t)\leq M\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,T]. \] The lower bound on $\beta$ is imposed on the boundary control; the extract does not specify whether it is interpreted pointwise or almost everywhere.

Question, conditional on the intended admissible evolution model. For an optimal motion satisfying these requirements, determine the regularity of the sets $\Omega(t)$ and their boundaries, and determine whether the trajectories described in the supplied clean statement as orthogonal to $\partial\Omega(t)$ can always be well defined. At a smooth boundary point, orthogonality means that a trajectory's tangent vector lies in the normal line to the boundary. This describes the geometric meaning only: the extract does not supply the trajectory equation, its parametrization, or the intended meaning of well-definedness.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The supplied extract does not give the evolution law linking $\beta$ to $\Omega(t)$, the admissible class of motions or controls, the boundary interpretation for nonsmooth sets, or the admissible ranges of $T$ and $M$. Consequently, the displayed constraints alone do not specify the optimization problem completely. In particular, no normal-velocity convention, additional regularity of $\Omega_0$, or existence or uniqueness requirement for trajectories is imposed here.

The extract also discusses a function $\phi$ in an unavailable cost formula labelled (1). It explicitly describes the hard-constraint choice \[ \phi(s)=\begin{cases} 0,&s\leq M,\\ +\infty,&s>M, \end{cases} \] on its unspecified argument domain, and explains that this enforces $E(t)\leq M$ at every time. Another alternative for $\phi$ is typographically corrupted in the supplied text and is not reconstructed here. The extract attributes the introduction and sharp-interface derivation of problems of the form (1) to its reference [2], and existence of optimal solutions and Pontryagin-type necessary conditions to its reference [3]. Their hypotheses and bibliographic identities are absent, so these statements do not supply missing hypotheses for the question above.

Short English statement

Starting from a bounded planar set, the supplied optimization problem minimizes terminal area while bounding total boundary-control effort at every time. It asks what regularity optimal evolving sets have and whether orthogonal trajectories can always be defined. The evolution law and trajectory construction are missing from the extract, so a complete precise formulation requires further source context. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
