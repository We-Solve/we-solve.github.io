Math-5081

Correcting the threshold and computing the singular locus

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Wooley's AIM problem considers, for positive integers $k,s$, the forms \[ \mathcal Q_{k,s}=\{F(y)=Q(y_1^k,\ldots,y_s^k):Q\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x_1,\ldots,x_s]\text{ a quadratic form}\}, \] of degree $2k$. In the nontrivial-zero interpretation, the Hasse implication means that a nonzero zero over every completion ${\mathbb{Q}}_v$ forces one over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. The printed definition is \[ h(k)=\inf\{s\in{\mathbb{N}}:\text{the Hasse principle holds for every }F\in\mathcal Q_{k,s}\}, \] and the challenge is $\log h(k)=o(k)$ as $k\to\infty$.

The report points out that this literal infimum is $1$ for every $k$: one-variable forms satisfy the implication, vacuously when they have no nontrivial local zero. A substantive threshold formulation would instead quantify over every variable count $s\geq s_0$, with all intended smoothness and local-solubility hypotheses specified. This repair is separate from the printed definition and is not silently substituted for it. The source does not distinguish ordinary from nonsingular local zeros, a distinction material to use of Birch's theorem.

The raw source attributes to Birch a bound written, after restoring its displaced exponent, as $h(k)\leq2k\cdot2^{2k}$, and compares the diagonal degree-$k$ case, obtained by taking $Q$ linear, with an expression $2k\log k$. Its OCR prints an asymptotic comparison symbol which the verification says was lost; no actual recovered symbol is supplied. Thus that comparison is not asserted here as a precise asymptotic equivalence for the same literal $h$.

The candidate structural theorem is independent of that defect. Let $k\geq2$, and let $A\ne0$ be a symmetric $s$-by-$s$ matrix over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Write \[ Q_A(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax,\qquad F_A(y)=Q_A(y_1^k,\ldots,y_s^k),\qquad \mu(A)=\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\operatorname{nullity}(A_I), \] where $A_I$ is the principal submatrix indexed by $I$. The report asserts \[ \dim\{y\in{\mathbb{A}}^s:\nabla F_A(y)=0\}=\mu(A), \] so the projective singular locus has dimension $\mu(A)-1$, with the empty-locus convention $\dim\varnothing=-1$. Equivalently the projective hypersurface is geometrically smooth exactly when every nonempty principal submatrix is nonsingular, or every nonempty principal minor is nonzero. The restriction $k\geq2$ and nonzero $A$ are retained.

For rational $A$, under nonsingular real and $p$-adic solubility at every prime, the report invokes Birch to give a nonsingular rational zero when \[ s-\mu(A)>(2k-1)2^{2k}. \] This is an explicit singular-locus-dependent sufficient condition, not a proof of the intended subexponential uniform threshold. Browning's appended suggestion is to replace $k$-th powers by norm forms from fixed extensions of degree $k$; its precise block-variable formulation is not supplied.

Short English statement

Seek a subexponential variable threshold for quadratic forms evaluated at $k$-th powers. The report flags a defective printed infimum and proposes an exact principal-submatrix formula for the singular locus, yielding a conditional Birch bound.
