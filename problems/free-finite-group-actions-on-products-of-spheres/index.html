Math-954

Free Finite-Group Actions on Products of Spheres

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a prime $p$, an elementary abelian $p$-group of rank $k$ is a group isomorphic to $({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^k$. An action of a finite group $G$ on a topological space $X$ is free if $gx=x$ implies $g=1$ for every $g\in G$ and $x\in X$. Actions are interpreted here as continuous actions. The notation $X\simeq Y$ denotes homotopy equivalence.

The supplied reconstructed statement asks for the following characterization, attributed in the supplied curation to Benson--Carlson. Under the explicit provisional convention that the number of sphere factors and all sphere dimensions are positive integers, for every finite group $G$ and every integer $r\geq 1$, prove that \[ \begin{gathered} \exists\, n_1,\ldots,n_r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\;\exists\, X\text{ a finite CW complex}:\\ X\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{r}S^{n_i} \quad\text{and}\quad G\text{ acts freely on }X \end{gathered} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \begin{gathered} \text{for every prime }p,\text{ there is no subgroup}\\ E\leq G\text{ with }E\cong({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{r+1}. \end{gathered} \] The source does not specify the allowed sphere dimensions or the range of $r$; consequently, these positivity conventions require confirmation. The dimensions in this display are existentially quantified and may depend on $G$ and $r$.

The particular question is whether, for every odd prime $p$, there exist positive integers $n,m$ and a finite CW complex $X\simeq S^n\times S^m$ admitting a free action of $Qd(p)$. The supplied record does not define $Qd(p)$. If the conventional notation is intended, it means \[ Qd(p)=({\mathbb{F}}_p)^2\rtimes\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p), \] where ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ is the field with $p$ elements, $({\mathbb{F}}_p)^2$ is its additive vector group, and $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p)$ acts by its natural matrix action. This identification, as well as the positivity convention for $n,m$, requires source confirmation.

The supplied original excerpt asks which finite groups act freely on a given product of spheres. This differs from the reconstructed statement, which permits an arbitrary finite CW complex of the indicated homotopy type and existentially chosen dimensions. The precise relationship between these targets is unresolved by the supplied excerpt.

Short English statement

The reconstructed question asks whether excluding elementary abelian subgroups of rank $r+1$, at every prime, exactly characterizes finite groups that act freely on some finite CW complex homotopy equivalent to a product of $r$ spheres. It singles out $Qd(p)$ for odd primes and two sphere factors. Sphere dimensions, the meaning of $Qd(p)$, and the difference from the original question about a given product require confirmation.
