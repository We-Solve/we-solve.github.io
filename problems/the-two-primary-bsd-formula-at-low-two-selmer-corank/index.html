Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every elliptic curve E over Q with full 2-power Selmer $Z_2-corank$ at most one, must analytic and Mordell-Weil ranks agree, must whole Sha be finite, and must the BSD-normalized leading L-value be a positive rational number whose 2-adic valuation equals that of the Sha order? No rational two-torsion or reduction-type hypothesis is imposed.

The two-primary BSD formula at low two-Selmer corank

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that the two-adic part of the BSD leading-coefficient identity holds for rational elliptic curves of two-power Selmer corank at most one, and for almost every quadratic twist.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For any elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$, let $s_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Z_2}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb Q)$ and assume $s_2(E)\le1$. The claim is \[ r=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=s_2(E), \qquad |\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)|<\infty. \] With $T_E=E(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}$, real Néron period $\Omega_E$, Néron--Tate regulator $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ and finite-prime Tamagawa numbers $c_{\ell}(E)$, it further claims \[ Q_E=\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)|T_E|^2}{r!\Omega_E\operatorname{Reg}_E\prod_{\ell} c_{\ell}(E)} \in\mathbb Q_{>0},\qquad v_2(Q_E)=v_2(|\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)|). \] There is no additional restriction on $E[2]$ or reduction at two. The formula determines the two-primary valuation; it does not state equality $Q_E=|\operatorname{Sha}|$ at all primes or address larger Selmer corank.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 002; 2026-09-24), the proof compares integral determinant lattices with Siegel-unit periods and central Kummer lattices. Separate residual and character-division arguments establish primitivity at two, and the rank-one comparison uses a squared Heegner logarithm rather than only rational period identities.
