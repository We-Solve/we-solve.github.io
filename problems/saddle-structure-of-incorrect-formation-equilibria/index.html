Math-6725

Saddle Structure of Incorrect Formation Equilibria

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $I=\{1,2,3,4\}$ and $p=(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)\in({\mathbb{R}}^2)^4$. The desired distances $\bar d_{ij}=\bar d_{ji}$, for $1\leq i<j\leq4$, are the pairwise Euclidean distances of a desired planar configuration $q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)$. The source describes this configuration as either a general quadrilateral or a triangle with the fourth agent in its interior. In the latter case, after relabelling, $q_1,q_2,q_3$ are noncollinear and $q_4\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$. The meaning of "general quadrilateral," including any convexity or genericity requirements, is not specified.

For distinct indices define \[ e_{ij}(p)=\lVert p_i-p_j\rVert^2-\bar d_{ij}^{\,2},\qquad F_i(p)=\sum_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}e_{ij}(p)(p_j-p_i). \] Consider the system $\dot p_i=F_i(p)$ for every $i\in I$. An equilibrium is a configuration $p^*$ satisfying $F_i(p^*)=0$ for all $i$; it is incorrect if $e_{ij}(p^*)\neq0$ for at least one pair $i<j$. No restriction excluding collisions or collinear configurations is supplied for the actual equilibrium $p^*$.

Source question. For every desired configuration of either stated type, and every incorrect equilibrium $p^*$ of the associated system, is $p^*$ a saddle point?

The term "saddle point" needs clarification. The system has the gradient representation \[ V(p)=\frac14\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}e_{ij}(p)^2, \qquad F(p)=-\nabla V(p). \] One possible precise interpretation of the requested conclusion is \[ \forall p^*\in({\mathbb{R}}^2)^4,\quad \left[F(p^*)=0\ \text{and}\ \exists i<j:\ e_{ij}(p^*)\neq0\right] \Longrightarrow \exists v\in{\mathbb{R}}^8\setminus\{0\}:\ v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2V(p^*)v<0. \] This is the negative-curvature interpretation, equivalently a positive eigenvalue of $DF(p^*)$. It is presented conditionally, not as a resolved interpretation of the source: requiring both positive and negative Hessian directions, or a nonlinear saddle property, can give different statements. Translations and rotations preserve the distance errors, so the treatment of symmetry directions also needs to be specified; hyperbolicity in the full configuration space must not be silently assumed.

Short English statement

Four agents in the plane move according to errors in all six desired pairwise distances. The question asks whether every equilibrium that fails to realize those distances is a saddle when the desired formation is a general quadrilateral or a triangle containing the fourth agent. The source leaves the precise quadrilateral class and the meaning of saddle unresolved. Its open status and literature assessment are supplied curation, not independently verified conclusions.
