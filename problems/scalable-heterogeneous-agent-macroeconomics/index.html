Statement

Original atlas ID: A6. Formulation: editorial specification of a research family.

Definitions and assumptions

Specify a heterogeneous-agent economy with idiosyncratic state $s\in S$, assets $a$ in a bounded interval with a borrowing limit, aggregate shock $z\in Z$, and a population probability measure $\mu\in\mathcal P(S\times A)$. The aggregate state is $(z,\mu)$; prices, household policies, and market-clearing conditions are equilibrium objects. Individual policies must solve the stated discounted optimization problems, and $\mu'$ must equal the distribution induced by those policies and the transition kernels. Let $\mathcal D$ be a declared region of aggregate states and $f$ collect equilibrium policy, price, and distribution-transition functions. An operator $\mathcal R(f)$ records equality residuals for feasibility, market clearing, and distributional evolution, positive parts of violated optimality inequalities, and complementarity errors at binding constraints; satisfied slack inequalities must contribute zero violation. Its norm, discretization, and treatment of binding constraints are specified.

Each instantiation must declare its population model explicitly rather than infer it from the common finite-agent default: specify whether $\mu$ is a finite population's empirical measure or a population law, its measurement time, agents' information and choice timing, and the conditional joint shock kernels, including dependence across agents. Specify whether distributional evolution is a realized random transition, a conditional law, or a deterministic population update under stated assumptions; an expected empirical measure must not replace a realized empirical measure without justification. The declared aggregate state must be sufficient for the equilibrium laws being computed; any additional state variables must be included explicitly.

Write $A=[\underline a,\overline a]$ with declared finite endpoints and borrowing limit $\underline a$. State whether the upper bound is an economic constraint or a computational truncation; in the latter case distinguish the truncated economy from the intended economy and certify the resulting error. For each primitive specification $h$, give the household objective and feasible choices, primitive spaces and kernels, and a candidate-function space $X_h$ with component domains and codomains. Declare the topology of $\mathcal D$ and either its adequacy for all required transitions and continuation values or the larger continuation domain and boundary data used. The displayed notation $\mathcal D$ denotes the declared domain for the relevant instance; any dependence on $h$ must be stated.

Define the exact operator $\mathcal R_h$ and residual norm, including component weights and units, separately from its numerically evaluated approximation. Establish on the declared candidate class that zero exact residual is equivalent to the full equilibrium conditions, including household optimality, distributional consistency and binding constraints. First-order and complementarity checks alone require hypotheses making them sufficient, or an appropriate Bellman verification argument, with any necessary boundary and transversality conditions. A certified full-domain bound must account for evaluation, discretization, interpolation, quadrature, distribution compression, truncation and roundoff errors as applicable; checking only grid points or simulated paths is insufficient without a justified enclosure of the full-domain residual.

Formal research question

An instantiation declares a nonempty restricted primitive class $\mathcal H$, its required economic features and parameter ranges, the representation of its inputs and outputs, and a computational cost model. These choices delimit the claim; success for a singleton or a simplified subclass does not establish scalability for a richer class. For the declared class, seek one algorithm taking $h\in\mathcal H$ and a tolerance $\varepsilon>0$ as inputs, with a stated cost bound as a function of tolerance and input size, charging any oracle, initialization and certification costs. State the admissible tolerance range and whether certification is deterministic or probabilistic; a probabilistic claim must specify its failure probability, randomness, and quantifiers over inputs, including whether it is pointwise with a uniform probability bound or simultaneous over the class. For a restricted primitive class $\mathcal H$, can one compute $\hat f$ with a certified bound \[ \sup_{h\in\mathcal H}\|\mathcal R_h(\hat f_h)\|_{\mathcal D}\le\varepsilon \] at a feasible computational cost, including states away from a steady state and transitions across binding constraints? Here feasibility of computational cost is assessed against the declared cost bound and benchmark budget, rather than an unspecified universal threshold.

Let $\mathcal E_h\subseteq X_h$ be the set of exact equilibria of the specified economy on the declared domains. Equivalence with the zero set of $\mathcal R_h$ must be established, not assumed from the residual's name. For a declared metric $d_h$ on $X_h$, including component scaling, define \[ \operatorname{dist}(g,\mathcal E_h)=\inf_{e\in\mathcal E_h}d_h(g,e). \] Establish equilibrium existence on the claimed class separately or state the conditions under which it is assumed. Identify stability conditions under which the residual controls distance to an equilibrium: \[ \operatorname{dist}(\hat f_h,\mathcal E_h)\le C_h\|\mathcal R_h(\hat f_h)\|_{\mathcal D}. \] State the candidate subset of $X_h$ on which the stability bound holds, whether it is local or global, and the allowed dependence of the finite constant $C_h$ on primitives, domains and normalizations. A uniform residual bound does not give a uniform equilibrium-error bound unless the relevant $C_h$ are uniformly bounded. Distance to the equilibrium set does not certify approximation of a particular selected equilibrium; selection-specific claims require the declared selector and additional justification.

For estimation, propagate numerical error into moments, likelihoods, and counterfactual uncertainty, so solver bias is distinguishable from sampling error. Benchmark methods on identical primitives, initial distributions, shocks, hardware budgets, and accuracy criteria; local linear accuracy and global state-domain accuracy must be reported separately.

Each estimation application must declare its parameter space, observation law and sample dependence, identified target or identified set, moment and likelihood functionals as applicable, estimator and counterfactual functional, and equilibrium-selection convention. Specify the norm or probability criterion and whether the error comparison is finite-sample or asymptotic, including the relation of solver tolerance and simulation or truncation horizons to sample size. Numerical approximation error, identification, estimator stability and statistical uncertainty require separate assumptions and claims; numerical residual control alone does not establish any of the latter three.

Interpretation and scope

The residual-to-error bound is a target requiring an explicit stability theorem; a small residual alone does not imply proximity to the correct equilibrium when the operator is ill conditioned or equilibria are multiple. Household Bellman contraction with fixed prices does not establish contraction of the coupled market-clearing economy. Asset truncation, distribution compression, quadrature, and tail losses all contribute numerical error. "Global" must name a state domain and norm, since an arbitrary probability-measure state space cannot be exhaustively validated by a few simulated paths. Occasionally binding inequalities require complementary optimality checks. A useful benchmark also reports how approximation error changes estimated parameters and distributional policy conclusions.

Source audit and status

Aiyagari (1994), Krusell--Smith (1998), and Kaplan--Moll--Violante (2018) are verified. Original link [18] names the 2016 NBER working-paper version of HANK, whereas the cited 2018 journal version is used here. Auclert--Bardóczy--Rognlie--Straub (2021), added as [S4], supplies an important efficient sequence-space solution and estimation method, preventing the impression that the computational field lacks established tools. Its scope includes nonlinear perfect-foresight transitions as well as steady-state sequence-space derivatives; computing such transitions is distinct from certifying a uniform global stochastic-equilibrium residual. The remaining question concerns verified nonlinear/global accuracy for explicitly rich models and estimation tasks. The operator and uniform guarantee above are editorial numerical-analysis specifications, not a claim that all heterogeneous-agent equilibria are unresolved or that local methods necessarily fail.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Unless an entry states otherwise, agent and firm sets are finite. State and action spaces are measurable, strategies and outcome maps are measurable, probability laws are specified, and expectations exist for the targets under discussion. Infinite-horizon discount factors lie in $(0,1)$ and discounted objectives are finite. Norms, tolerances and complexity input sizes must be specified for computational comparisons. Constants or primitives that appear locally are inputs, not empirical facts asserted by this catalogue.

Identification. A statistical model $m\in\mathcal M$ implies an observable law $P_m$ and target $\theta(m)$. At law $P$, its identified set is \[ \Theta(P;\mathcal M)=\{\theta(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_m=P\}. \] Point identification holds when this set is a singleton, or equivalently when $P_{m_1}=P_{m_2}$ implies $\theta(m_1)=\theta(m_2)$ for all compatible models. Sharp bounds mean bounds attained, or approached when the boundary is not attained, by compatible models. Writing this set is a definition, not a solution: the substantive task is to establish informative restrictions and derive or estimate the set. An unrestricted-confounding model may give only trivial bounds.

Inference. Identification, estimability and valid uncertainty quantification are separate questions. Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$. A confidence procedure $C_n$ over a declared law class $\mathcal P$ has asymptotic uniform coverage at level $1-\alpha$ when \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P}\Pr_P\{\theta(P)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha. \] For set-identified targets the coverage event must be defined separately, for example coverage of the full identified set. If an entry uses identification-indexed models instead of uniquely indexed laws, the same coverage convention is applied over those models. Pointwise limits do not establish uniform validity, and asymptotic validity does not guarantee finite-sample precision. Sample sizes, dependence structures and weak-identification sequences are part of each application.

Counterfactuals. $Y_i(a)$ is a potential outcome under a specified intervention, including its timing, implementation and versions. It is not $Y_i$ conditional on voluntarily choosing $a$. A full intervention vector permits interference; shorthand scalar treatment notation does not silently impose no interference. Assignment, selection, attrition, anticipation and measurement must be specified. A claim about an instrument's local effect is not automatically a population policy effect.

Economic equilibrium. A counterfactual model includes best responses, constraints, feasibility, market clearing, state transitions and expectations consistent with the stated information. When several equilibria exist, either a declared selector is an input or the target is set-valued. Comparisons across policy regimes must use the stated selection convention. Reduced-form relationships are not presumed invariant after an equilibrium-changing intervention.

Optimization and welfare. Optimization is over the stated feasible set. If attainment is not established, $\sup$ or $\inf$ and $\varepsilon$-optimal choices replace an asserted maximizer or minimizer. For example, with value $V=\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}W(a)<\infty$, an $\varepsilon$-optimal set is $\{a\in\mathcal A:W(a)\geq V-\varepsilon\}$ for $\varepsilon>0$. Benefits, costs, risk and health or environmental outcomes can be aggregated only using declared units and conversion weights. Interpersonal utility weights, the treatment of fiscal transfers and foreign profits, discounting, and the behavioral welfare criterion are explicit inputs. A comparative-static derivative additionally requires existence and appropriate differentiability of the selected counterfactual.
