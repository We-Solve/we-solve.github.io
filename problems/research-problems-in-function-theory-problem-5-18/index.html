Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.18

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$, fix a real $0<\lambda<1$, and write \[ f(z)=\lambda+\sum_{j\ge1}a_jz^j,\qquad F_f(r)=\lambda+\sum_{j\ge1}|a_j|r^j\quad(0\le r<1). \] The source asks for the best radius $B(\lambda)$ such that $F_f(B(\lambda))\le1$ for every function in its intended class.

The literal extract assumes only that $f$ is analytic in $\mathbb D$. That is insufficient for a positive universal radius, since the linear coefficient is unrestricted. In particular the quoted positive values cannot be statements for the literal unbounded analytic class. A bound on the functions is missing.

Under the usual bounded-analytic interpretation, which must be confirmed rather than silently inserted, define \[ \mathcal B_\lambda=\{f\text{ holomorphic on }\mathbb D:f(0)=\lambda,\ |f(z)|\le1\text{ on }\mathbb D\}, \] \[ B(\lambda)=\sup\{r\in[0,1):F_f(r)\le1\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal B_\lambda\}. \] This makes "best constant" the largest permissible radius, with the inequality at the endpoint to be checked in the intended statement. With this explicit conditional interpretation, the requested unresolved range is $0<\lambda<1/2$. The source reports \[ B(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\quad\left(\frac12\le\lambda<1\right),\qquad B(0)=\frac1{\sqrt2}, \] and \[ B(\lambda)>\sqrt{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}} \quad\left(0<\lambda<\frac12\right). \] The value at $\lambda=0$ is separately supplied endpoint information although the initial parameter range excludes zero. The strict lower bound is not a proposed exact value.

The supplied triage calls this Bombieri's $B(\lambda)$ problem and reports the full determination for $0<\lambda<1/2$ open as of the 2018 edition, with no later resolution located by that assessment. Its mention of de Branges and Bieberbach is contextual and supplies no reduction or theorem. No independent verification of the bounded-class correction, quoted formulas, or current status is claimed here.

Short English statement

Determine the largest radius at which the sum of absolute Taylor coefficients, weighted by that radius, stays at most one when the constant coefficient is fixed. The quoted positive radii require a missing bound on the analytic functions. Under the usual unit-bounded interpretation, the source leaves the interval $0<\lambda<1/2$ undetermined and supplies exact values elsewhere and a strict lower bound.
