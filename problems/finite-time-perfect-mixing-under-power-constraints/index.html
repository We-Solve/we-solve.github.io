Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: Determine whether finite-time perfect mixing can occur for a two-dimensional incompressible flow subject to a finite power constraint.

Let $\Omega$ denote the two-dimensional spatial domain, whose geometry and boundary conditions are not specified in the supplied record. Write $u(t,x)$ for the velocity field; incompressibility means \[ \nabla\cdot u=0, \] with the sense of this equation dependent on the unspecified regularity class.

The following notation describes the missing ingredients without selecting them. Let $q$ denote the state being mixed, let $\mathcal M(q)\geq 0$ denote a specified measure of its departure from perfect mixing, and let $\mathcal A_T(q_0)$ denote the class of admissible evolutions $(u,q)$ on $[0,T]$ with initial state $q_0$, incorporating the intended evolution equation, incompressibility, boundary conditions, regularity, and finite power constraint. If perfect mixing is defined by $\mathcal M(q)=0$, then the question has the schematic form \[ \text{Can there exist }T\in(0,\infty) \text{ and }(u,q)\in\mathcal A_T(q_0) \text{ such that }\mathcal M(q_0)>0 \text{ and }\mathcal M(q(T))=0? \] This is a formulation template, not a fully specified conjecture: the record does not define $q$, $\mathcal M$, $\mathcal A_T(q_0)$, or the intended quantifier over initial states. The initial nonzero mixing measure expresses the nontrivial interpretation of reaching perfect mixing; the source does not explicitly state an initial-data hypothesis.

In particular, the record does not specify whether the transported state obeys pure advection or an equation with diffusion, what norm or functional defines perfect mixing, what quantity constitutes power, or whether its constraint is instantaneous or integrated in time. Short English statement

Can an initially imperfectly mixed state become perfectly mixed in finite time under a two-dimensional incompressible flow with finite power? The supplied record leaves the domain, evolution equation, power constraint, mixing criterion, and initial-data quantifiers unspecified, so a unique precise conjecture cannot yet be stated. Its accompanying exponential lower-bound formulation also requires clarification.
