Math-1709

Modern k-(q,t)-Kostka positivity and a change-of-basis obstruction

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q,t$ be independent indeterminates and let ${\mathbb{N}}[q,t]$ mean polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients, including zero. The source asks for coefficientwise positivity of the $k$-$(q,t)$-Kostka polynomials. It does not define them in the quoted problem. The supplied aid identifies the intended 2005 convention as expansion of transformed Macdonald symmetric polynomials in the recursive $k$-split $k$-Schur basis. In that convention, for an integer $k\geq1$ and admissible $k$-bounded partitions $\lambda,\mu$ of the same degree $d$ (so $\lambda_1,\mu_1\leq k$), write \[ H^{\mathrm{tr}}_\mu(X;q,t)=\sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash d\\\lambda_1\leq k}} K^{(k)}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)\,s^{(k)}_\lambda(X;t). \] Here $s^{(k)}_\lambda$ denotes the recursive $k$-split basis, and $H^{\mathrm{tr}}_\mu$ must use the transformed-Macdonald normalization of that construction. The problem is \[ \forall k\geq1\ \forall\text{ admissible }\lambda,\mu,\qquad K^{(k)}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)\in{\mathbb{N}}[q,t]. \] The recursive definition and precise transformation of $H_\mu$ are not supplied, so an implementation must verify the convention and admissible indexing rather than substitute a different modified-Macdonald or atom basis.

According to the aid, Kato's Corollary 10.4 in arXiv:2505.23202v2, combined with the Blasiak--Morse--Pun--Summers equivalence of $k$-split, strong-tableau, and Catalan $k$-Schur functions, establishes this positivity in the stated modern $k$-Schur interpretation. The aid also reports the stable range $k\geq d$ directly, where the $k$-Schur basis specializes to the Schur basis. These are supplied literature and partial-result assertions, whose precise normalizations and theorem hypotheses require checking.

The aid distinguishes the original tableau-atom version by the following finite-degree cone criterion. Let $A=(a_j)_{j=1}^m$ and $B=(b_i)_{i=1}^m$ be two bases over ${\mathbb{Z}}[q,t]$, with an invertible unitriangular transition matrix $T$ defined by \[ a_j=\sum_{i=1}^m T_{ij}b_i. \] For coordinate columns $x=[v]_A$, $y=[v]_B$ one has $y=Tx$. Thus $T\in\operatorname{Mat}_m({\mathbb{N}}[q,t])$ transfers nonnegative $A$-coordinates to nonnegative $B$-coordinates. Universal transfer in the reverse direction holds exactly when $T^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_m({\mathbb{N}}[q,t])$. If both $T$ and $T^{-1}$ are coefficientwise nonnegative and $T$ is unitriangular in a fixed ordering, then $T=I$. In particular positivity in a modern basis transfers to an atom basis through a nonnegative modern-to-atom transition (or equality of bases); nonnegativity only of the opposite atom-to-modern transition is insufficient. This criterion does not establish the required transition or settle a distinct original tableau-atom conjecture.

Short English statement

The source asks for nonnegative coefficients in the $k$-$(q,t)$-Kostka polynomials. The supplied aid identifies a modern $k$-split interpretation and reports a recent theorem proving its positivity. It also keeps the original tableau-atom formulation separate: positivity transfers between bases only when the transition in the needed direction is nonnegative, and the record does not establish that atom transition.
