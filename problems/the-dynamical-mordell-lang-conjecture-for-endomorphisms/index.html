Math-4148

The Dynamical Mordell–Lang Conjecture for Endomorphisms

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a complex algebraic variety, let $f:A\to A$ be an algebraic endomorphism satisfying the source condition $\deg(f)>1$, let $V\subseteq A$ be a subvariety, and let $P\in A({\mathbb{C}})$. Define $f^0=\operatorname{id}_A$ and $f^{n+1}=f\circ f^n$. Using ${\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$ for the iteration indices, define the hitting-time set \[ S_f(P,V)=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}:f^n(P)\in V({\mathbb{C}})\}. \] The supplied question asks whether, for every such $A,f,V,P$, the set $S_f(P,V)$ is a finite union of one-sided arithmetic progressions. If progressions of step zero are allowed, this conclusion means that there exist $r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$ and integers $a_i,b_i\geq 0$, for $1\leq i\leq r$, such that \[ S_f(P,V)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\{a_i+b_i k:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}. \] Here $r=0$ represents the empty set, and $b_i=0$ represents a singleton. Equivalently, this convention permits a finite exceptional set together with finitely many progressions of positive step.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not specify the meaning of $\deg(f)$ for its class of varieties and endomorphisms. For an integral variety and a dominant generically finite endomorphism, one possible meaning is the function-field degree $[{\mathbb{C}}(A):f^*{\mathbb{C}}(A)]$; the source does not explicitly supply these hypotheses or select this degree convention. Thus the degree condition is retained pending clarification, rather than used to impose additional hypotheses. The source also does not specify whether varieties must be irreducible, whether subvarieties must be closed, or whether additional geometric assumptions are intended.

The displayed formulation explicitly uses nonnegative iteration indices and permits singleton progressions. The source writes ${\mathbb{N}}$ without specifying whether zero is included, and does not define its arithmetic-progression convention. If only positive steps are allowed, the displayed conclusion has an additional allowance for isolated times that requires confirmation. With singleton progressions permitted, changing whether the initial time zero is included does not change the finite-union property.

Short English statement

The question concerns the times when an iterated algebraic endomorphism of a complex variety sends a given point into a given subvariety. Must these times form finitely many one-sided arithmetic progressions, allowing isolated times under the convention stated above? The degree hypothesis and several geometric conventions require clarification from the source.
