Question 2.4 — Forn> 3, does Aut(Fn) have property (T)?

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F_n$ be the free group of rank $n$, and $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ its automorphism group. A group has Kazhdan's property $(T)$ if every unitary representation with almost invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector. Question 2.4 asks, for $n>3$, whether $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ has property $(T)$. The strict range is $n\ge4$; the separately discussed rank-three case is not the same question.

The supplied research summary labels the problem solved negatively for every $n\ge3$, without a primary citation, invoking unspecified unbounded actions, abelianization/free quotients and the surjection onto $\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})$. Those phrases do not establish the claim: arbitrary unbounded actions are not the unitary obstruction required by $(T)$, and a quotient map alone is not an obstruction unless the quotient is shown to fail $(T)$. No asserted rank-at-least-four free/abelian quotient is constructed. Thus the detailed negative assessment is preserved as unverified and is not promoted to a theorem.

The source gives the following context. A positive $(T)$ answer would, through Lubotzky--Pak [64], conceptually explain effectiveness of the Product Replacement Algorithm of Leedham-Green and others; no quantitative mixing statement is supplied. It also says the mapping-class analogue was open in the source era.

The source calls $F_A$ the property that every action on an ${\mathbb{R}}$-tree has a fixed point and reports it for $\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})$, $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$, $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ when $n\ge3$, and genus-at-least-three mapping class groups, reference [28]. Standard Serre $F_A$ concerns simplicial trees, whereas the all-${\mathbb{R}}$-tree version is commonly denoted $F_{\mathbb{R}}$; the intended convention and hypotheses must be checked. The source also states that $(T)$ implies its fixed-point property.

Finally it cites McCool [67] for a finite-index subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(F_3)$ with positive first Betti number and hence a quotient ${\mathbb{Z}}$. Translation on a line then gives a fixed-point-free action of that subgroup; since $(T)$ passes to finite-index subgroups, it concludes failure of $(T)$ for $\operatorname{Aut}(F_3)$. This rank-three argument does not justify the summary's claimed negative conclusion for all larger ranks.

Short English statement

For ranks strictly greater than three, ask whether the free-group automorphism group has property $(T)$. The source separately gives a rank-three obstruction. Its summary claims a negative solution in all ranks without a citation or valid supplied argument, so that status claim remains explicitly unresolved here.
