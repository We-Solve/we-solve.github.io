Math-456

Conjecture 11.2 — The LMO invariant distinguishes integral homology 3-spheres.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal H$ denote the integral homology $3$-spheres in the convention intended by the source: closed connected $3$-manifolds $M$ with $H_*(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_*(S^3;{\mathbb{Z}})$, taken up to the appropriate equivalence. In the usual oriented smooth formulation, that equivalence is orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. Let \[ Z_{\mathrm{LMO}}(M)\in\widehat{\mathcal A}(\varnothing;{\mathbb{Q}}) =\prod_{d\geq0}\mathcal A(\varnothing;{\mathbb{Q}})^{(d)} \] be the full LMO invariant, with a fixed consistent normalization. Here $\mathcal A(\varnothing;{\mathbb{Q}})$ is the graded rational space of closed vertex-oriented trivalent Jacobi diagrams modulo antisymmetry and IHX, and the hat denotes degree completion.

Conjecture. The LMO invariant distinguishes integral homology $3$-spheres. In the conventional oriented formulation, for every pair $M,N\in\mathcal H$, \[ Z_{\mathrm{LMO}}(M)=Z_{\mathrm{LMO}}(N) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\cong N\text{ by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism}. \] Equality means equality of the complete series in every degree, not of a single finite truncation. The source cross-refers to Conjecture 10.9; its statement is absent. The excerpt does not explicitly specify orientation, equivalence, or normalization conventions, so these must be checked against the source before adopting the conventional formulation as definitive.

Short English statement

The conjecture says that the complete LMO series is injective on integral homology $3$-spheres: two spheres with identical invariants in every degree should be equivalent. It does not assert a fixed finite degree sufficient to distinguish all pairs.
