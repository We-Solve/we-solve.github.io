Resolved Witt-Group Question on Anisotropic Modular Categories

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not determine a unique mathematical problem. Its clipped statement contains only "question: [1, Question 6.8]" and a reference to page 2425. The supplied literature assessment interprets this as a question about uniqueness of a $C^*$-norm on a group algebra, whereas the supplied statement-verification note says that the cited construction settles Question 6.8. The title instead refers to a resolved Witt-group question about anisotropic modular categories. These descriptions cannot be identified with one another from the supplied evidence.

To make the group-algebra interpretation explicit, let $G$ be an abstract group and interpret its group algebra as the complex algebra \[ {\mathbb{C}}[G]=\left\{\sum_{g\in G}a_g g : a_g\in{\mathbb{C}},\ a_g=0\text{ for all but finitely many }g\right\}, \] with multiplication induced by the group law and involution \[ \left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)^*=\sum_{g\in G}\overline{a_g}\,g^{-1}. \] A $C^*$-norm on this algebra means a norm, rather than merely a seminorm, satisfying $\|ab\|\leq\|a\|\|b\|$, $\|a^*\|=\|a\|$, and $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$. Uniqueness means that any two such norms agree on every element of ${\mathbb{C}}[G]$. The group $G$ is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite.

For a specified class $\mathcal G$ of groups, the question suggested by the supplied literature assessment would be whether \[ \forall G\in\mathcal G,\qquad \left({\mathbb{C}}[G]\text{ admits exactly one }C^*\text{-norm}\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\forall F\subseteq G\text{ finite},\ |\langle F\rangle|<\infty\right). \] This is only a conditional reconstruction. The class $\mathcal G$, any additional hypotheses, and the identification of this implication with the intended source problem remain unresolved. The supplied record does not provide enough information to formulate the alternative Witt-group target.

Short English statement

The supplied assessment suggests asking whether a unique $C^*$-norm on a complex group algebra forces every finitely generated subgroup to be finite. However, the fragment, title, and supplied verification note conflict, and the intended class of groups is unspecified. The displayed question is therefore a provisional interpretation requiring mathematical and source review.
