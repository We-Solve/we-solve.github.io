Math-5207

A two-vertex digon counterexample and an oriented local descent theorem

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite digraph $G$, let $d_G(u,v)$ be directed distance, with value $+\infty$ when $v$ is unreachable, and put \[ N^+(v)=\{w:v\to w\},\qquad N_2^+(v)=\{w:d_G(v,w)=2\},\qquad G^*=\{v:|N_2^+(v)|\geq|N^+(v)|\}. \] In particular, second neighbors are at exact directed distance two, not merely endpoints of two-step walks. Write $d_G(v,A)=\min_{a\in A}d_G(v,a)$, with minimum $+\infty$ for $A=\varnothing$. The literal source conjecture is \[ \text{for every finite digraph }G,\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\ v\in V(G),\quad |N^+(v)|=k\ \Longrightarrow\ d_G(v,G^*)\leq k. \] The supplied verification says that finiteness and the exact-distance convention are declared in the source list.

The accompanying aid reports a counterexample to the quantifier over all digraphs: for the loopless digon $a\to b\to a$, both first neighborhoods have size one and both exact second neighborhoods are empty, so $G^*=\varnothing$. It separately identifies the following repair, reported open: impose that $G$ is a finite oriented graph, meaning a loopless simple digraph with no antiparallel pair, and ask for the same bound.

For that repaired domain, the aid reports two partial results. First, suppose every induced digraph $G[N^+(x)]$, whenever $N^+(x)\ne\varnothing$, has a sink, namely a vertex with no outgoing arc within that induced digraph. For every $v_0$ of outdegree $k$, it reports a directed path \[ v_0\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_\ell\in G^*,\qquad \ell\leq k, \qquad |N^+(v_{i+1})|\leq|N^+(v_i)|-1\quad(i<\ell). \] If $d_G(v_0,G^*)=k$, the reported equality certificate for such a descent path is \[ \ell=k,\qquad |N^+(v_i)|=k-i,\qquad N^+(v_{i+1})=N_2^+(v_i),\quad |N_2^+(v_i)|=k-i-1\quad(0\leq i<k). \] Second, without the neighborhood-sink hypothesis, the aid reports $d_G(v,G^*)\leq |N^+(v)|$ for every starting vertex of outdegree at most three in a finite oriented graph. Neither statement is presented as a proof of the repair in general.

The source motivates the bound by a degree-one vertex $x$ with successor $y$: if $y$ has no successors it is good, and otherwise $x$ is asserted good. That motivation is compatible with the oriented interpretation, whereas a digon obstructs the literal unrestricted wording. Its sharpness illustration is a pyramid: rows $R_i$ of size $i$, $1\leq i\leq m$, with precisely all arcs from $R_i$ to $R_{i-1}$ for $i\geq2$. The apex is the unique good vertex; a vertex in $R_i$ has outdegree $i-1$ and distance $i-1$ to it. The source refers to the motivator only as "he", without supplying an identity in this record.

Short English statement

The literal finite-digraph conjecture fails for a digon when second neighbors mean exact distance two. The supplied aid distinguishes this from an open oriented-graph repair, and reports a local sink descent criterion, its equality case, and the unconditional degree-at-most-three case.
