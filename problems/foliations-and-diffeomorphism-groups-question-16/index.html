Foliations and Diffeomorphism Groups — Question 16

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth volume form $\eta$ on $S^3$, write \[ G_\eta=\operatorname{Diff}_0(S^3,\eta) =\left\{f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{\infty}(S^3):f^*\eta=\eta\right\}_0, \] where the subscript denotes the identity component, interpreted here using the $C^{\infty}$ topology. The source explicitly introduces a volume form only on $S^2$, although its two questions concern $S^3$. Thus $\eta$ supplies the missing three-dimensional setup; the smoothness convention, topology, and intended quantification over volume forms require confirmation. The source's assertion of independence from the volume form is stated for $S^2$, not explicitly for $S^3$.

For a group $G$, put $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. The group is uniformly perfect if \[ \exists N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\ \forall g\in G\ \exists k\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\ \exists a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_k,b_k\in G: \quad g=\prod_{i=1}^{k}[a_i,b_i], \] with the empty product equal to the identity. A real-valued quasimorphism on $G$ is a map $q:G\to{\mathbb{R}}$ with finite defect \[ D(q)=\sup_{g,h\in G}|q(gh)-q(g)-q(h)|<\infty. \] No continuity condition is imposed in this draft; the source does not specify one.

Questions attributed to Burago, Ivanov, and Poltervoich, as named in the supplied source.

  1. Is $G_\eta$ uniformly perfect?
  2. Does $G_\eta$ admit a non-trivial quasimorphism?
The second question retains an unresolved terminology issue: the source does not define "non-trivial." Two precise possible interpretations are existence of an unbounded quasimorphism, or existence of a quasimorphism that is not a bounded perturbation of a homomorphism. The latter asks for a quasimorphism $q$ satisfying \[ \forall \phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(G_\eta,{\mathbb{R}}),\qquad \sup_{g\in G_\eta}|q(g)-\phi(g)|=\infty. \] These interpretations are recorded as alternatives, without identifying either as the verified intended formulation.

Supplied background. For a volume form $\omega$ on $S^2$, the source states that the isomorphism type of $\operatorname{Diff}_0(S^2,\omega)$ is independent of $\omega$. It attributes simplicity of this group to Banyaga, reference label banyaga1978structure, and the existence of many non-trivial quasimorphisms, with resulting failure of uniform perfectness, to Ghys and Gambaudo, reference label gambaudo2004commutators. These are supplied background assertions, not independently verified results in this draft. The registry labels the questions open; this is a supplied status label rather than an independently verified literature assessment.

Short English statement

The source asks whether the identity component of the volume-preserving diffeomorphism group of $S^3$ has a uniform bound on the number of commutators needed to express any element, and whether it admits a non-trivial quasimorphism. The volume form on $S^3$, the precise meaning of non-triviality, and regularity conventions need confirmation. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
