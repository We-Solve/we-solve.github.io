Rare manifold components obstruct uniform support recovery

The source asks whether local entropy and correlation can identify a manifold and improve augmentation. No minimum sampling mass, topology or error criterion is fixed. We test support recovery in Hausdorff distance for a class allowing disconnected compact one-dimensional manifolds. Let $C$ be the unit circle centered at zero in $\mathbb R^2$, and $C'$ the unit circle centered at $(4,0)$. Let $P,Q$ be their uniform probability laws and $P_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon Q$, $0<\varepsilon<1$.
Proposition: For $n\ge1$, every possibly randomized estimator $\widehat C$ of a nonempty compact support from $n$ independent samples satisfies \[ \max\{\mathbb E_{P^n}d_H(\widehat C,C),\; \mathbb E_{P_\varepsilon^n}d_H(\widehat C,C\cup C')\} \ge2(1-\varepsilon)^n. \]
Proof: The Hausdorff distance between $C$ and $C\cup C'$ is four: every point on $C'$ has distance at most four from $C$, and $(5,0)\in C'$ has distance four. Couple a $P_\varepsilon$ sample by independently choosing its component with probability $\varepsilon$; use a corresponding $P$ draw whenever the component is $C$. Couple estimator randomness identically. On the event $E$ that all component choices are $C$, of probability $(1-\varepsilon)^n$, both data sets and outputs coincide. The $P$ data remain distributed as $P^n$ independently of $E$. For the common output, the Hausdorff triangle inequality gives \[ d_H(\widehat C,C)+d_H(\widehat C,C\cup C')\ge4. \] Integrating on $E$ and using nonnegativity of risks off $E$ gives a sum of the two full expected risks at least $4(1-\varepsilon)^n$. At least one risk is half that sum. ∎
For each $n$, choose $\varepsilon=1/(2n)$. A union bound gives $(1-\varepsilon)^n\ge1/2$, so the worst-case risk over this class is at least one at every sample size. This is a uniform impossibility caused by arbitrarily rare components; it does not prevent pointwise consistency for a fixed positive $\varepsilon$ or recovery under lower-mass and geometric assumptions. Even a known local tangent is only a first-order augmentation guide: at $(1,0)\in C$, the tangent step $(1,t)$ has distance $\sqrt{1+t^2}-1=t^2/(\sqrt{1+t^2}+1)$ from the circle. It leaves the manifold whenever $t\ne0$. Projection or curvature information is needed for exact on-manifold augmentation in this example.

Verification and remaining gaps

The exact no-component probability and tangent distances were checked, and the support separation was verified analytically and on circle grids. The example permits disconnected manifolds; it is not a counterexample to an unprovided connectedness or lower-density assumption. Neither local-entropy estimators nor nonlinear principal components are analyzed. Their useful recovery and augmentation guarantees require a specified model beyond the rhetorical source question.
