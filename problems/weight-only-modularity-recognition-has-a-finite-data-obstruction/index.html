Math-5129

Weight-only modularity recognition has a finite-data obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Given a $q$-series $f(q)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nq^n$ and its weight, the original problem asks for fast heuristic methods to recognize modularity without knowing the level or group. Its suggested directions are Zagier's method using asymptotics at a point, $p$-adic tests for more prime divisibility of coefficients than chance predicts, and comparison of the distribution of $a_p$ with Sato--Tate. These suggestions are not a specified decision algorithm or a uniform probabilistic guarantee.

In the scalar holomorphic congruence setting, the attached research aid asserts a finite-data obstruction. For every even integer $k\ge2$, every $M\ge1$ and every complex prefix $a_0,\ldots,a_M$, there is a holomorphic modular form $F$ of weight $k$ at level \[ N_k\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,M),\qquad N_2=11,\quad N_4=5,\quad N_k=11\ (k\ge6), \] whose expansion agrees through $q^M$. It describes an explicit triangular oldform construction, but does not supply its formula in this summary. For every integer $R>M$, it further asserts that $F(q)+q^R$ is a nonmodular continuation of the same prefix in the same fixed-weight holomorphic congruence interpretation. Thus a prescribed weight and finitely many unrestricted-level coefficients cannot decide modularity; this does not preclude useful heuristics with additional structure.

For normalized Hecke-eigenform candidates the aid separately reports level constraints from local Hecke defects. For example, for a proposed integral weight $k$ and nebentypus $\varepsilon$, the unramified relation is \[ a_{p^2}=a_p^2-\varepsilon(p)p^{k-1}\qquad(p\nmid N). \] A nonzero defect at $p$, under that proposed eigenform interpretation, forces $p\mid N$; distinct such primes therefore give a divisor and lower bound for any candidate level. These tests require the eigenform and character assumptions and are not necessary identities for arbitrary linear combinations of modular forms.

The aid also gives an exact calibration for multiplicativity tests on disjoint pairs of distinct primes. Under a null model in which all involved residues $a_p,a_q,a_{pq}$ are independent and uniform in ${\mathbb{F}}_\ell$, each test $a_{pq}=a_pa_q$ passes with probability $1/\ell$. If $m$ tests use disjoint coefficient data, their number of passes has distribution $\operatorname{Binomial}(m,1/\ell)$. This is a false-positive calculation for the stated residue model, not a probability that an arbitrary input series is modular. Appropriate eigenform, normalization and distributional hypotheses would also be needed to turn the original Sato--Tate suggestion into a test.

Short English statement

Find practical weight-only modularity heuristics. The supplied aid shows that finite coefficients cannot decide modularity when the level is unrestricted, but retains conditional eigenform tests that can constrain a candidate level and quantify random residue coincidences.
