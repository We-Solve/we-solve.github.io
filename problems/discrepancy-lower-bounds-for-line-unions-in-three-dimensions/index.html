Math-14313

Discrepancy Lower Bounds for Line Unions in Three Dimensions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $N\geq 1$ be an integer and let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be affine lines. Set \[ A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\ell_i\cap[0,1]^3),\qquad L=H^1(A), \] where $H^1$ denotes length and $H^3$ denotes three-dimensional volume, using the usual normalization of Hausdorff measure. The measure is that of the union as a set, without multiplicity.

The supplied statement does not specify the family of test sets over which $Q$ ranges. Denote that intended, but unresolved, family by $\mathcal Q$. For a specified nonempty family of measurable test sets and for $L>0$, the displayed discrepancy would be \[ D_N(A)=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\left|\frac{H^1(A\cap Q)}{L}-H^3(Q)\right|. \] The proposed question is whether there exists an absolute constant $c>0$, independent of $N$ and of the lines, such that for every such union with positive length, \[ D_N(A)\geq c\,\frac{\log L}{L}, \] where $\log$ is the natural logarithm.

This is a conditional formulation: the intended family $\mathcal Q$, including its shapes, location, anchoring, and boundary conventions, must be recovered before the question is fully specified. The supplied universal wording also permits unions of zero length, for which both displayed quotients are undefined. The restriction $L>0$ is necessary to interpret these formulas but is not explicitly supplied. For $0<L\leq 1$, the proposed lower bound is automatically satisfied because its right-hand side is nonpositive; its substantive range is $L>1$. No further length threshold is supplied.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the discrepancy of normalized length on every finite union of lines clipped to the unit cube has a universal lower bound of order $\log L/L$, where $L$ is the total length of the union. The test-set family is unspecified, and zero-length unions make the formulas undefined, so these qualifications require clarification.
