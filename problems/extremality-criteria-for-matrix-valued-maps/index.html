Math-1594

Extremality criteria for matrix-valued maps

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For positive integers $m,n$, call a matrix $Y\in M_{m\times n}({\mathbb{R}})$ very well approximable (VWA) if for some $\varepsilon>0$ there are integer vectors $q\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n\setminus\{0\}$ of arbitrarily large norm and $p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^m$ such that \[ \|Yq-p\|_\infty<\|q\|_\infty^{-n/m-\varepsilon}. \] Equivalent exponent conventions rescale $\varepsilon$. Kleinbock and Margulis ask for reasonable checkable hypotheses on a smooth map $f:U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^k\to M_{m\times n}({\mathbb{R}})$, generalizing nondegeneracy of vector-valued maps, that ensure almost-everywhere non-VWA behavior. The local question does not define the measure meant on $f(U)$; a precise statement uses the pushforward of a specified parameter measure, rather than ambient almost-everywhere language that could be vacuous on a lower-dimensional image.

The aid's elementary sufficient criterion assumes $k\geq mn$, a $\sigma$-finite parameter measure $\mu\ll\operatorname{Leb}_k$, and \[ \operatorname{rank}Df(x)=mn\qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in U. \] It reports that the coarea/submersion argument makes $f_*\mu$ absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on the full matrix space. Ambient VWA nullity then gives $f(x)$ non-VWA for $\mu$-almost every $x$. Full target rank almost everywhere is the hypothesis; merely one regular point or a nonempty regular locus does not suffice. This criterion does not address the main lower-dimensional nondegenerate-manifold regime by itself.

The complementary reported obstruction assumes a fixed nonzero $q_0\in{\mathbb{Q}}^n$ and $b\in{\mathbb{Q}}^m$ with \[ f(x)q_0=b\qquad\text{for every }x\in U. \] Clearing denominators gives an exact integer relation, and its unbounded integer multiples supply VWA approximations for every image matrix. Both rationality and the nonzero denominator vector are essential; a relation with arbitrary irrational $b$ is not silently treated the same way. The aid also reports a positive-measure critical-set example demonstrating the failure of replacing almost-everywhere rank coverage by existence of regular points, but supplies no formula for that example.

The summary mentions broad modern sufficient criteria and a sharp no-constraining-pencil criterion for connected analytic manifolds under suitable algebraic Zariski/Plücker-closure hypotheses. The pencil definition, algebraic hypotheses, theorem and references are absent, so this is retained as reported literature context rather than asserted for all smooth singular maps. No universal classification or necessary-and-sufficient smooth criterion is supplied.

The source continues into separate Section 5.2. It mentions a far-reaching Sprindžuk generalization [106], asks for Diophantine properties of generic points with respect to $f_*\mu$ for measures on ${\mathbb{R}}^k$, cites locally finite-measure results [198,107,111], and suggests Hausdorff measures. This is neighboring measure-theoretic context, not part of Question 25's exact request. Absolute continuity of the parameter measure in the elementary criterion does not resolve that general singular/Hausdorff-measure question. The malformed hyphens, duplicated "and" and pushforward symbol are normalized without supplying its missing continuation.

Short English statement

The aid supplies a full-rank pushforward criterion and an exact rational-relation obstruction. The first requires almost-everywhere coverage, not just some regular points, and does not solve the lower-dimensional or singular-measure problem. The next subsection’s Hausdorff-measure question is retained separately.
