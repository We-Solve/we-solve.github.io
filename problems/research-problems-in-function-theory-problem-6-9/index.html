Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.9

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}$, and let $S$ be the class of holomorphic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to{\mathbb{C}}$ normalized by $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$. A function in $S$ is convex if its image is a convex domain. For \[ f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nz^n,\qquad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_nz^n, \] define the Hadamard product (coefficientwise convolution) by \[ (f*g)(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nb_nz^n\qquad(z\in\mathbb D). \] The conjecture asks whether, for every pair of convex $f,g\in S$, the function $f*g$ also belongs to $S$ and has convex image. The supplied research summary records an affirmative resolution of this Pólya--Schoenberg conjecture by Ruscheweyh and Sheil-Small (1973). This is a supplied literature assessment, not a new proof.

Short English statement

Is the coefficientwise convolution of two normalized convex univalent disk functions again convex and univalent? The supplied summary reports that the conjecture was proved in 1973.
