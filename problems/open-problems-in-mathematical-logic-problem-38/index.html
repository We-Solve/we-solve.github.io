Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An oligomorphic permutation group on ${\mathbb{N}}$ is a subgroup $G\leq S_\infty$ such that, for every integer $n\geq 1$, the diagonal action of $G$ on ${\mathbb{N}}^n$ has finitely many orbits. Here $S_\infty$ is the group of all permutations of ${\mathbb{N}}$, with the topology of pointwise convergence, and each subgroup carries the induced topology.

For a precise conventional formulation, let \[ X=\{G\leq S_\infty:G\text{ is closed and oligomorphic}\}, \] with the Borel structure inherited from the Effros Borel space of closed subsets of $S_\infty$. Define an equivalence relation $E_{\mathrm{top}}$ on $X$ by \[ G\,E_{\mathrm{top}}\,H \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there exists a group isomorphism }\varphi:G\to H \text{ such that }\varphi\text{ and }\varphi^{-1}\text{ are continuous}. \] For equivalence relations $E$ and $F$ on standard Borel spaces $U$ and $V$, respectively, write $E\leq_B F$ if there is a Borel map $r:U\to V$ such that \[ \forall u,u'\in U\qquad u\,E\,u'\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r(u)\,F\,r(u'). \] Let $E_\infty$ denote a universal countable Borel equivalence relation, meaning that every countable Borel equivalence relation is Borel reducible to it. An equivalence relation on a standard Borel space is smooth if it is Borel reducible to equality on ${\mathbb{R}}$.

The supplied source attributes to Nies, Schlicht, and Tent (2019), under the reference label $[\mathrm{NST2}]$, the result $E_{\mathrm{top}}\leq_B E_\infty$. The question is whether $E_{\mathrm{top}}$ is smooth; explicitly, does there exist a Borel map $f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying \[ \forall G,H\in X\qquad G\,E_{\mathrm{top}}\,H \ \Longleftrightarrow\ f(G)=f(H)? \] The supplied excerpt does not explicitly impose closedness or specify a standard Borel coding of the groups. The formulation using $X$ above makes the conventional closed permutation group interpretation explicit and requires confirmation against the source; it does not settle the intended formulation if nonclosed oligomorphic subgroups were meant.

Short English statement

Can closed oligomorphic permutation groups, with their pointwise convergence topologies, be classified up to topological group isomorphism by real numbers assigned in a Borel way? The supplied record labels this question open and attributes a Borel reducibility upper bound to Nies, Schlicht, and Tent (2019). The closed group interpretation and coding require source confirmation.
