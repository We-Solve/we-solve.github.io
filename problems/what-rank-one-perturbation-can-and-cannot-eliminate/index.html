Math-1305

What rank-one perturbation can and cannot eliminate

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The AIM fragment asks whether polynomial machinery in Kadison--Singer-related proofs can be replaced by rank-one perturbation methods, recalling Bourgain--Tzafriri, or reproduced at a multivariate-polynomial level. It does not specify a theorem to reprove or what elimination means. The trailing "Adam Marcus" may be the next speaker heading, so attribution of this fragment is not fixed from that alone.

The report treats a Hermitian determinant pencil $M(z)=M_0+\sum_jz_jA_j$ in a region where $M(z)>0$, with $D(z)=\det M(z)$ and $R=M(z)^{-1}$. For a set $S$ of distinct derivative indices it claims the exact cycle expansion \[ \frac{\partial_S D}{D}=\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Sym}(S)} (-1)^{|S|-\#\operatorname{cycles}(\pi)} \prod_{(i_1\cdots i_a)\in\operatorname{cycles}(\pi)} \operatorname{tr}(RA_{i_1}\cdots RA_{i_a}). \] If every operated slot is rank one, $A_i=u_iu_i^*$, these derivatives become $D\det G_S$ for the resolvent Gram matrix $G_{ij}=u_i^*Ru_j$. The full mixed operator has the deterministic-shift identity \[ \prod_{i\in S}(1-t_i\partial_i)\det M(z) =\det\left(M(z)-\sum_{i\in S}t_iA_i\right). \] This is a polynomial identity even when intermediate shifted matrices are singular; resolvent evaluations then need adjugates or regularization. Slots outside $S$ need not be rank one.

The report's sharp fixed-base statement is: for fixed $M>0$ and Hermitian $A$, \[ \det(M-tA)=\det M\,(1-t\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A))\ \text{for every }t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{rank}A\leq1. \] The all-parameter and Hermitian hypotheses matter; no such equivalence is inferred from equality at a single shift.

For its determinant-tilted formulation, let $M_\xi(z)=H_\xi+\sum_jz_jA_j$ be a finite or suitably integrable random Hermitian pencil, positive definite for every ensemble member in a common region, with differentiation under expectation valid. Put $q(z)={\mathbb{E}}\det M_\xi(z)>0$ and tilt the probability by $\det M_\xi/q$. With $R_\xi=M_\xi^{-1}$ and $\Phi_q^j=\partial_j\log q$, the report claims \[ \partial_k\Phi_q^j=-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{tilt}}\operatorname{tr}(R_\xi A_kR_\xi A_j) +\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathrm{tilt}}(\operatorname{tr}(R_\xi A_j),\operatorname{tr}(R_\xi A_k)). \] Thus barrier monotonicity $\partial_k\Phi_q^j\leq0$ is equivalent there to domination of this covariance by the expected resolvent trace term. The identity does not prove that domination for every ensemble.

These formulas remove explicit mixed-polynomial computation in rank-one settings. The report expressly retains the real-stability, root-region, convexity and interlacing arguments required for the MSS Kadison--Singer bound; it does not supply a polynomial-free proof of that result.

Short English statement

Ask what rank-one perturbation methods can replace in polynomial-based Kadison--Singer arguments. The report gives exact resolvent and deterministic-shift identities, a sharp rank-one boundary, and a tilted-covariance test for barrier monotonicity. It distinguishes computational elimination of polynomials from eliminating the stability and interlacing logic of the proof.
