Periodic-orbit scars: a negative full-support result and quantitative transfer obstruction

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question 43 is attributed to A. Katok: do periodic orbits in a triangular billiard correspond to scars, more precisely to quantum limits supported on periodic orbits? The source says that [21] suggested a positive answer for rational billiards, meaning triangles whose angles are rational multiples of $\pi$.

To distinguish the meanings of "scar", let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a triangle and choose a fixed self-adjoint billiard Laplacian $P$ with specified boundary conditions. For normalized exact eigenfunctions \[ P u_j=\lambda_j u_j,\qquad \|u_j\|_{L^2(\Omega)}=1, \qquad \lambda_j\to\infty, \] a quantum limit is a subsequential semiclassical measure on the unit-energy billiard phase space; its position projection is a limit of $|u_j|^2\,dx$. A nonsingular periodic orbit $\gamma$ is a closed billiard trajectory avoiding the vertices. Full support on the orbit means $\mu(\gamma)=1$, which is stronger than a positive periodic component $\mu(\gamma)>0$. The original fragment does not specify Dirichlet versus Neumann boundary conditions, nor its quantifier over triangles and orbits; these must be fixed when applying a precise theorem.

The supplied aid reports that the Hassell--Hillairet--Marzuola vertex-control theorem excludes full support on a nonsingular periodic orbit for true eigenfunctions of polygonal billiards. Its claimed mechanism is a uniform bound $\int_U|u_j|^2\,dx\geq c(U)>0$ in suitably chosen vertex neighborhoods. If the closure $V=\overline U$ avoids the projected orbit, the closed-set limit argument gives $(\pi_*\mu)(V)\geq c(U)$ and hence \[ \mu(\gamma)\leq1-c(U),\qquad (\pi_*\mu)(O)\leq1-c(U) \] for any orbit tube $O$ disjoint from $V$. The constant and admissible boundary-condition hypotheses of the cited theorem are not supplied here. The conclusion excludes a fully orbit-supported quantum limit, not every possible partial scar.

The aid's abstract spectral-transfer obstruction is conditional on the stated vertex bound. Let $E$ be real, $v\in\operatorname{Dom}(P)$ satisfy $\|v\|=1$, and put \[ r=\|(P-E)v\|,\qquad \eta=\|1_Uv\|. \] Let $\mathcal E$ be a target eigenspace and assume $g=\operatorname{dist}(E,\sigma(P|_{\mathcal E^\perp}))>0$. If every normalized $u\in\mathcal E$ has $\|1_Uu\|^2\geq c>0$, then \[ \frac r g\geq\frac{(\sqrt c-\eta)_+}{\sqrt2}, \qquad t_+=\max(t,0). \] Consequently a sequence with $\eta\to0$ and a uniform $c>0$ cannot have $r/g\to0$. This distinguishes localized quasimodes from exact eigenfunctions or a single isolated exact eigenspace.

For the equilateral, right-isosceles, and $30$--$60$--$90$ reflection triangles, the aid reports a stronger torus-unfolding/$L^4$ argument: every quantum limit assigns zero mass to any finite union of individual periodic orbits. That special-family statement must not be generalized to all polygons. The registry says solved and the aid says counterexample/refuted; here these mean the supplied negative full-support conclusion, not an independent certification of all variants of scarring.

Short English statement

Katok asked whether triangular billiards admit exact-eigenfunction quantum limits entirely supported on periodic trajectories. The supplied aid reports a negative answer for nonsingular periodic orbits through vertex control, while distinguishing partial scars and localized quasimodes. It also reports zero periodic-orbit mass for three reflection-triangle families.
