Why is semidefinite programming computationally slow? For $$X(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\{(x_1,x_2,t_1,t_2,z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^4\times\{0,1\}^2:t_i\geq x_i^2,\ 0\leq\alpha x_1+\beta x_2\leq z_1,\ 0\leq\gamma x_2\leq z_2\},$$ find a scalable compact description of $\operatorname{conv}X(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$. What is the minimum number of auxiliary variables needed to represent a nonsemialgebraic convex hull semialgebraically? Develop randomized copositivity and complete-positivity tests, and recognize named structures in flat mathematical programs. Finally, if $m\geq12$ points in the shell between the unit sphere and its circumscribed dodecahedron are mutually at least unit distance apart, find concise proofs for $m>16$ of the conjectured Euclidean shell inequality and formulate its $\ell_1$ analogue. Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Why is semidefinite programming computationally slow? For $$X(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\{(x_1,x_2,t_1,t_2,z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^4\times\{0,1\}^2:t_i\geq x_i^2,\ 0\leq\alpha x_1+\beta x_2\leq z_1,\ 0\leq\gamma x_2\leq z_2\},$$ find a scalable compact description of $\operatorname{conv}X(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$. What is the minimum number of auxiliary variables needed to represent a nonsemialgebraic convex hull semialgebraically? Develop randomized copositivity and complete-positivity tests, and recognize named structures in flat mathematical programs. Finally, if $m\geq12$ points in the shell between the unit sphere and its circumscribed dodecahedron are mutually at least unit distance apart, find concise proofs for $m>16$ of the conjectured Euclidean shell inequality and formulate its $\ell_1$ analogue.

Source scope

The shell formula is absent from the catalogue sentence; the primary report does print a formula, but it is not a substitute for specifying which proof conventions and norm analogue the bundle asks to resolve. The questions about practical SDP speed, randomized tests, and structure recognition likewise have no single supplied quantitative success criterion. An exact lifted description for the displayed two-binary set

The following works for every fixed real $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$, including zero and negative values; it does not require division by those parameters. For each $b=(b_1,b_2)\in\{0,1\}^2$ introduce $\lambda_b\ge0$, $v^b=(v^b_1,v^b_2)\in\mathbb R^2$ and $s^b_1,s^b_2\ge0$. Consider the constraints \[ \sum_b\lambda_b=1,\quad x_i=\sum_bv^b_i,\quad t_i=\sum_bs^b_i,\quad z_i=\sum_b\lambda_b b_i\quad(i=1,2), \] \[ (v^b_i)^2\le\lambda_b s^b_i\quad(i=1,2),\qquad 0\le\alpha v^b_1+\beta v^b_2\le\lambda_b b_1,\qquad 0\le\gamma v^b_2\le\lambda_b b_2. \] Their projection is exactly $\operatorname{conv}X$, as a set of finite convex combinations, not merely its closure.

First take a finite convex combination of original points. Group its terms by the binary vector $b$. Let $\lambda_b$ be total group weight, and let $v^b_i$ and $s^b_i$ be the corresponding weighted sums of $x_i$ and $t_i$. All linear inequalities follow by summation. Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives $(v^b_i)^2\le\lambda_b\sum_{r\text{ in group}}\theta_r(x_i^r)^2 \le\lambda_b s^b_i$. Thus every hull point has a lift.

Conversely, a feasible lift with $\lambda_b=0$ forces $v^b_i=0$, but may still have $s^b_i>0$. Collect these vertical amounts as $r_i=\sum_{b:\lambda_b=0}s^b_i\ge0$. Choose any $b_*$ with positive $\lambda_{b_*}$, which exists because their sum is one, and add $r_i$ to $s^{b_*}_i$; discard the zero-weight groups. For every remaining $b$, put \[ \widehat x^b_i=v^b_i/\lambda_b,\qquad \widehat t^b_i=s^b_i/\lambda_b,\qquad\widehat z^b=b, \] using the increased $s^{b_*}$. Dividing the linear constraints by $\lambda_b$ shows the original on/off inequalities. The perspective inequality gives $\widehat t^b_i\ge(\widehat x^b_i)^2$. Increasing $s^{b_*}$ only helps this inequality. Therefore all these are points of $X$, and their convex combination with weights $\lambda_b$ is the projected point. This proves exactness, including the zero-weight boundary and unbounded $t$ directions.

Each quadratic constraint has a convex feasible set: for two points satisfying $v^2\le\lambda s$, $\lambda,s\ge0$, their convex combination satisfies it by $|\theta v+(1-\theta)v'| \le\theta\sqrt{\lambda s}+(1-\theta)\sqrt{\lambda's'} \le\sqrt{(\theta\lambda+(1-\theta)\lambda') (\theta s+(1-\theta)s')}$, using Cauchy--Schwarz. It is thus a finite convex quadratic lift with constant many variables for the literal two-binary set. All displayed inequalities are polynomial.

Why this does not settle scalability or the bundle

The route is disjunctive enumeration: each binary pattern receives a copy of the continuous variables. Applying it to an analogous model with $p$ binary variables generally creates $2^p$ groups. The two-variable calculation supplies no polynomial bound as $p$ grows. A scalable formulation would need a structural compression of these groups and a proof preserving the coupled inequalities; none is obtained here. Individual on/off perspective constraints cannot simply replace this proof when $\alpha x_1+\beta x_2$ couples both variables.

The separate request for a semialgebraic representation of a nonsemialgebraic hull also requires the allowed meaning of representation: our construction addresses only this particular semialgebraic hull. It does not supply a universal representation theorem. No stochastic certification guarantee, recognition grammar, SDP runtime explanation, or shell inequality proof is claimed by solving the four-state convexification component.
