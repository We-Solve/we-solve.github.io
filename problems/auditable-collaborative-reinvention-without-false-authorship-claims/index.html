Attempt: using agreement to validate a collaboratively developed argument

To study one narrow part of collaborative mathematical invention, I tested the inference that three agreeing reviewers supply independent confirmation. Suppose a fixed invalid argument is accepted by each reviewer with marginal probability $\epsilon\in(0,1/2)$, and the group uses majority acceptance. These are hypothetical error probabilities; no claim is made about a particular proof or classroom.
Proposition: Under independent reviewer errors, false majority acceptance has probability $3\epsilon^2-2\epsilon^3$. With the same individual marginal error rates but perfectly correlated errors, it has probability $\epsilon$, which is strictly larger.
Proof: With independence, a false majority consists of exactly two erroneous acceptances or all three. Its probability is $3\epsilon^2(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon^3=3\epsilon^2-2\epsilon^3$. For the correlated construction, draw one Bernoulli variable $B$ with probability $\epsilon$ and let every reviewer's acceptance indicator equal $B$. Each reviewer still has error rate $\epsilon$, but the majority accepts exactly when $B=1$. The difference between the correlated and independent risks is \[ \epsilon-(3\epsilon^2-2\epsilon^3)=\epsilon(1-\epsilon)(1-2\epsilon)>0. \] ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At $\epsilon=1/10$, the independent false-majority risk is $7/250=0.028$, whereas the correlated risk is $1/10$. Exact enumeration of the eight independent voting patterns confirms the first value. Shared reliance on an unverified lemma is a possible source of dependence, but its occurrence or rate is not established by this model. Distinct reviewer names alone do not justify independence. The result does not certify mathematical truth, historical authorship, original discovery, equal contribution or individual learning. The source's broad collaborative-invention agenda needs a mathematical task and empirical goals beyond this limited validation issue.
