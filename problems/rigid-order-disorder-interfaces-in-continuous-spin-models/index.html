Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
The sites are $i\in\mathbb Z^d$ and $\langle i,j\rangle$ denotes nearest-neighbor pairs. A circle spin is an angle modulo its chosen full-period convention, and $U$ is the strongly narrow-well interaction in the source. At order--disorder coexistence, mixed boundary conditions favor the ordered phase on one side and the disordered phase on the other. Rigidity means that the interface remains macroscopically localized; a Dobrushin state is an infinite-volume Gibbs state supporting this interface and breaking translation symmetry in the normal direction.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source writes S^1=R/Z but also writes cos(r), whose angular period is 2 pi. Those expressions require an explicit rescaling convention: a period-one representative uses cos(2 pi r), while a radian representative uses cos(r). The catalogue formula is retained; it cannot be used literally as a well-defined period-one potential without this convention. The report's toy and clock-model theorems do not settle the smooth continuous-spin model.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the continuous-spin model on $\mathbb Z^d$ with spins $\sigma_i\in S^1=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ and Hamiltonian $H(\sigma)=\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}U(|\sigma_i-\sigma_j|)$, where $U(r)=-((1+\cos r)/2)^p$ for large integer $p$, is the order–disorder interface rigid when $d\geq3$? Equivalently, does the model admit three-dimensional Dobrushin states?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Rigidity of the order--disorder interface is proved for related toy and clock models, but the conjectured rigidity for the continuous circle-spin model in dimensions at least three remains open. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Prove that the order--disorder interface stays rigid in the proposed continuous-spin model in dimension at least three.

A continuous-spin test of a discrete contour argument.
Fix an integer $p\geq1$ and use radian angles, so the source potential is $U(r)=-\cos^{2p}(r/2)$. Along a chain of $L$ bonds impose total angle change $\theta$ and distribute it evenly, with each difference $\theta/L$. Relative to the aligned state the energy cost is \[ \Delta E=L\left(1-\cos^{2p}(\theta/(2L))\right) \leq Lp\sin^2(\theta/(2L))\leq\frac{p\theta^2}{4L}. \] The first inequality is $1-(1-a)^p\leq pa$ for $0\leq a\leq1$, proved by expanding the geometric sum. Thus a fixed total twist can cost arbitrarily little when spread over a long chain, even at fixed large $p$.
The narrow well has a definite local scale.
For fixed $a$ and $p\to\infty$, $\cos^{2p}(a/(2\sqrt p))\to e^{-a^2/4}$, obtained from $\log\cos z=-z^2/2+O(z^4)$. The angular well width is therefore of order $p^{-1/2}$. Treating distinct angles as discrete labels with a uniform positive mismatch cost is incompatible with these two calculations.
Why rigidity is still undecided.
A gradual twist between ordered orientations is not an interface between an ordered and a disordered phase. The source's Dobrushin question concerns the latter and could survive these soft modes. The calculation only blocks a direct clock-model Peierls proof that assigns a fixed energetic penalty to every change of angle. One must instead control phase labels, entropy and spin waves together in the continuous model. The period-one convention would require replacing every angle by $2\pi$ times its coordinate; no inconsistent $\cos r$ on $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ is used.
