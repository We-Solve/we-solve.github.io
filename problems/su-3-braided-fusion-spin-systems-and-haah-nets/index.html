Local clock-and-shift algebra audit.
Put $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, $Xe_j=e_{j+1}$ and $Ze_j=\omega^je_j$, with indices modulo three. Direct evaluation on $e_j$ gives $ZX=\omega XZ$ and $X^3=Z^3=I$. On finitely many sites write $W(a,b)=\prod_jX_j^{a_j}Z_j^{b_j}$ for vectors $a,b$ over $\mathbb F_3$. Operators on distinct sites commute, so \[ W(a,b)W(a',b')=\omega^{b\cdot a'}W(a+a',b+b'), \] \[ W(a,b)W(a',b')=\omega^{b\cdot a'-b'\cdot a}W(a',b')W(a,b). \] These identities reduce any proposed local generator map to a test of its commutator form and linear relations.
Orientation and locality are separate checks.
Sending $X\mapsto X$, $Z\mapsto Z^{-1}$ reverses the Weyl phase. It cannot be a complex-linear automorphism of this single-site algebra with the same defining relation: applying an automorphism to $ZX=\omega XZ$ must keep the scalar $\omega$. Thus a braiding reversal cannot be silently implemented by this map without also tracking orientation or complex conjugation. Even a correct finite-region relation match would not prove a bounded-spread net isomorphism, since support radii must be bounded uniformly in region size for both map and inverse.
