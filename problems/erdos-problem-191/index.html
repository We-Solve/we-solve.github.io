Erdős Problem #191

1. FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Given $C>0$, does every red--blue colouring of the edges on $\{2,\ldots,n\}$, for all sufficiently large $n$, have a monochromatic clique $X$ with $\sum_{x\in X}1/\log x\ge C$?

2. LITERATURE AND SCOPE

Rödl proved the affirmative answer. Conlon, Fox and Sudakov proved the sharp order $\Theta(\log\log\log n)$ for the minimum, over colourings, of the maximum such weight. Their primary paper uses base-two logarithms. We likewise use $\log_2$ for the work below; changing to natural logarithms multiplies all vertex weights by $1/\log 2$ and does not change the question.

This attempt reconstructs the matching-order upper construction and the three-colour obstruction in full, and proves a constant two-colour lower bound. The unbounded two-colour lower bound remains an explicitly external result, not a theorem proved by the elementary argument below.

3. AN ELEMENTARY RANDOM-COLOURING FACT

For every integer $M\ge2$ there is an edge colouring of $K_M$ with no monochromatic clique of size $s=\lceil4\log_2 M\rceil+2$. If $s>M$, this is automatic. Otherwise the expected number of monochromatic $s$-cliques in a uniformly random edge colouring is at most \[ 2\binom Ms2^{-\binom s2}\le2^{1+s\log_2 M-s(s-1)/2}<1. \] Thus some colouring avoids them. In particular its largest monochromatic clique has size at most $4\log_2 M+2$. For $M=1$ the trivial one-vertex bound suffices.

4. A TWO-COLOUR UPPER CONSTRUCTION

Partition the vertices into the nonempty blocks \[ B_i=[2^{2^{i-1}},2^{2^i})\cap\{2,\ldots,n\},\qquad 1\le i\le t, \] where $t\le1+\log_2\log_2 n$. Colour each block by the preceding random-colouring fact. Every monochromatic clique inside it has at most $4\cdot2^i+2$ vertices, each with weight at most $2^{1-i}$. Hence its total weight is at most $12$.

Independently choose a red--blue colouring on the $t$ block indices with monochromatic cliques of size at most $4\log_2 t+2$ when $t\ge2$. Give every edge between $B_i$ and $B_j$ the colour of the index edge $ij$. If $X$ is monochromatic, its occupied block indices form a monochromatic clique of that same colour, and $X\cap B_i$ is monochromatic within its block. Thus \[ \sum_{x\in X}\frac1{\log_2 x} \le12(4\log_2 t+2)=O(1+\log\log\log n). \tag{1} \] This is Rödl's construction as explained in the Conlon--Fox--Sudakov paper, with deliberately generous constants and every colour-compatibility step spelled out.

5. WHY TWO COLOURS MATTER

Keep the red--blue colourings inside the blocks, but colour every between-block edge green. A red or blue clique lies in one block and has weight at most $12$. A green clique has at most one point from each block, so has weight at most \[ \sum_{i\ge1}2^{1-i}=2. \] This single block construction works on all integers $\ge2$ and disproves the analogous arbitrary-$C$ claim with three colours.

6. A CONSTANT LOWER BOUND AND ITS LIMIT

The elementary Ramsey recurrence $R(a,b)\le R(a-1,b)+R(a,b-1)$ follows by considering the red and blue neighbours of one vertex. Its boundary values $R(1,b)=R(a,1)=1$ give \[ R(s,s)\le\binom{2s-2}{s-1}\le4^{s-1}. \] Choose $s=1+\lfloor\log_4(n-1)\rfloor$. Every edge colouring on $n-1$ vertices has a monochromatic $s$-clique, whose weight is at least \[ \frac{s}{\log_2 n}=\frac12-o(1). \] This cannot make the weight arbitrarily large. The missing argument must force sufficiently many suitably located vertices; a large clique with no control of its distribution only yields a constant in this calculation.

7. FINAL

UNRESOLVED in this reconstruction; the literature proves the answer is yes. The sharp-order upper construction, the bounded three-colour example, and the constant lower bound are proved here. Conlon--Fox--Sudakov's Theorem 1.1 gives the missing two-colour lower bound $2^{-8}\log_2\log_2\log_2 n$, using dependent random choice and weighted Ramsey estimates. Those estimates are not reproved here, and no new resolution is claimed.
