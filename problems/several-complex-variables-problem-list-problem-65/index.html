Several Complex Variables Problem List — Problem 65

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f:{\mathbb{C}}^2\to{\mathbb{C}}^2$ be a polynomial automorphism, meaning a polynomial map with a polynomial inverse. Assume, as in the source, that $f$ is not conjugate to an elementary map. In the usual triangular convention, an elementary map has the form \[ (z,w)\longmapsto (az+p(w),bw+c),\qquad a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\},\quad c\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad p\in{\mathbb{C}}[w]. \] The source does not specify the category of conjugacy; this hypothesis retains that unresolved qualification.

Define the forward locally bounded set by \[ \mathcal F_b(f)=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^2:\ \text{there exist an open neighborhood }U\ni x\text{ and }M<\infty\text{ such that }\|f^j(y)\|\le M\text{ for every }y\in U\text{ and }j\ge1\right\}. \] The source calls this the Fatou set. Let $\Omega$ be a connected component of $\mathcal F_b(f)$, and suppose that $f^n(\Omega)=\Omega$ for some positive integer $n$. Taking $n>0$ is equivalent to the source's condition that a nonzero integer period exists, since $f$ is invertible. Put $G=f^n$. All boundaries below are taken in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$.

The source calls $\Omega$ recurrent if it contains a point whose orbit does not converge to $\partial\Omega$, and nonrecurrent otherwise. It does not specify the meaning of convergence to the boundary as a set or whether this criterion uses the full orbit under $f$ or the return orbit under $G$. These qualifications remain unresolved here.

For recurrent $\Omega$, the source asserts the alternatives: (a) a basin of attraction of a sink orbit, (b) a basin of attraction of a Siegel disk, or (c) a basin of a Herman ring. A sink orbit is a periodic orbit whose return derivative has all eigenvalues of modulus less than one. The terms Siegel disk and Herman ring refer to one-dimensional invariant rotation domains modeled, respectively, on a disk and an annulus, with return dynamics holomorphically conjugate to an irrational rotation. The source does not specify the relevant return iterate or the precise meaning of their basins in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$. It reports that examples of (a) and (b) are readily constructed.

  1. (i) Can alternative (c), a recurrent periodic component that is the basin of a Herman ring, occur under these hypotheses? The precise basin condition requires clarification from the source.
  2. (ii) For every nonrecurrent periodic component $\Omega$ as above, does there exist a single point $P\in\partial\Omega$ such that \[ \forall Q\in\Omega,\qquad \lim_{k\to\infty}G^k(Q)=P? \] Here the limit is ordinary convergence in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$, and the point $P$ must work for every $Q\in\Omega$.
  3. (iii) For every such nonrecurrent $\Omega$, does there exist $P\in\partial\Omega$ with $G(P)=P$ and a root-of-unity eigenvalue of the derivative denoted by $DF(P)$ in the source? The source does not define $F$. If its intended meaning is $F=f^n=G$, the resulting conditional formulation is \[ \exists P\in\partial\Omega\ \exists p\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ \exists q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\qquad G(P)=P,\qquad e^{2\pi i p/q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(DG(P)). \] This identification of $F$ is an unresolved interpretation, not an additional supplied hypothesis.

The source's statement that essentially nothing was known about nonrecurrent periodic domains is historical commentary from the 1995 list, not a verified assessment of subsequent literature.

Short English statement

For a polynomial automorphism of ${\mathbb{C}}^2$ outside the elementary class, consider periodic components where forward iterates are locally bounded. The questions ask whether a recurrent component can be a Herman-ring basin, and whether every nonrecurrent component has a common boundary limit under the return map and a boundary periodic point with a root-of-unity multiplier. The source leaves the conjugacy category, recurrence and basin conventions, and the derivative in the last question insufficiently specified.
