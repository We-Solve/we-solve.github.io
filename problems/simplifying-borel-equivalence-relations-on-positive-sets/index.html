Math-15705

Simplifying Borel Equivalence Relations on Positive Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a Polish space, meaning a separable topological space whose topology admits a complete metric. Let $E\subseteq X\times X$ be a Borel equivalence relation: $E$ is Borel in the product topology and is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Let $I\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$ be a $\sigma$-ideal, meaning that $\varnothing\in I$, every subset of a member of $I$ belongs to $I$, and every countable union of members of $I$ belongs to $I$. A set $B\subseteq X$ is $I$-positive if $B\notin I$. For such a set, define \[ E\upharpoonright B=E\cap(B\times B). \] The supplied question asks whether, for an arbitrary Polish space $X$, Borel equivalence relation $E$ on $X$, and $\sigma$-ideal $I$ on $X$, there exists a Borel $I$-positive set $B\subseteq X$ such that $E\upharpoonright B$ is "significantly simpler" than $E$ on $X$.

This is an unresolved formulation rather than a fully specified mathematical assertion: the source supplies neither a definition of "significantly simpler" nor a comparison relation or target class of equivalence relations. It also does not explicitly require $I$ to be proper, that is, $X\notin I$. If $X\in I$, downward closure precludes any $I$-positive subset. No properness assumption, additional restriction on $I$, or interpretation of simplification is imposed in this draft.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every Borel equivalence relation can be simplified by restricting it to a Borel set that is positive for a given $\sigma$-ideal. The intended meaning of simplification and the necessary hypotheses on the ideal are unspecified, so a precise quantified target still requires clarification.
