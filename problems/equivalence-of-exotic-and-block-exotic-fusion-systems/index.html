Math-925

Equivalence of Exotic and Block-Exotic Fusion Systems

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix a prime $p$ and a finite $p$-group $S$. A fusion system $\mathcal F$ on $S$ is a category whose objects are the subgroups of $S$, whose morphisms are injective group homomorphisms, which contains all homomorphisms induced by conjugation in $S$, and in which every morphism factors as an isomorphism in $\mathcal F$ followed by an inclusion.

We use the conventional domain of saturated fusion systems. Here $P\leq S$ is fully normalized, respectively fully centralized, if $|N_S(P)|$, respectively $|C_S(P)|$, is maximal among the corresponding orders for subgroups isomorphic to $P$ in $\mathcal F$. Saturation means that:

  1. If $P$ is fully normalized, then it is fully centralized and $\operatorname{Aut}_S(P)$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal F}(P)$.
  2. If $\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal F}(P,S)$ and $\varphi(P)$ is fully centralized, then $\varphi$ extends to a morphism $N_\varphi\to S$ in $\mathcal F$, where \[ N_\varphi=\{s\in N_S(P):\varphi\circ(c_s|_P)\circ\varphi^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}_S(\varphi(P))\}, \qquad c_s(x)=sxs^{-1}. \]

For a finite group $G$ with $S\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$, its group fusion system $\mathcal F_S(G)$ has morphisms \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal F_S(G)}(P,Q) =\{c_g|_P:g\in G,\ gPg^{-1}\leq Q\}. \] A saturated fusion system is exotic if it is not isomorphic to $\mathcal F_T(G)$ for any finite group $G$ and any $T\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$. An isomorphism of fusion systems means a group isomorphism of their underlying $p$-groups that transports all morphism sets onto one another.

For block realization, let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, let $G$ be finite, and let $b$ be a block of $kG$, viewed as a primitive central idempotent. In the standard theory of Brauer pairs, choose a maximal $b$-Brauer pair $(D,e_D)$; thus $D$ is a defect group of $b$. For each $P\leq D$, let $(P,e_P)$ be the unique $b$-Brauer pair below $(D,e_D)$ with first component $P$. The block fusion system $\mathcal F_{(D,e_D)}(G,b)$ has objects the subgroups of $D$ and morphisms \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal F_{(D,e_D)}(G,b)}(P,Q) =\{c_g|_P:g\in G,\ {}^g(P,e_P)\leq(Q,e_Q)\}, \] where $\leq$ denotes the standard Brauer-pair containment relation. A saturated fusion system is block-exotic if it is not isomorphic to any such block fusion system, allowing all finite groups $G$, all algebraically closed fields $k$ of characteristic $p$, and all blocks $b$ of $kG$.

Conjecture, under the conventional domain above. For every prime $p$, every finite $p$-group $S$, and every saturated fusion system $\mathcal F$ on $S$, \[ \mathcal F\text{ is exotic} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal F\text{ is block-exotic}. \] The supplied question does not explicitly specify saturation, finiteness, or the conventions for block realization. These are the conventional hypotheses used in this draft and require confirmation against the report. The supplied original-statement excerpt refers to two previously mentioned constructions but does not reproduce their definitions or the full text of Conjecture 1.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every saturated fusion system on a finite $p$-group that can be realized by a block of a finite group can also be realized by conjugation in a finite group on one of its Sylow $p$-subgroups. Equivalently, are exotic and block-exotic fusion systems exactly the same? The domain and realization conventions used here require source confirmation.
