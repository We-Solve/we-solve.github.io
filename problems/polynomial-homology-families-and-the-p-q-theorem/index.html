Polynomial-Homology Families and the p-q Theorem

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a space $X$ and a coefficient field $K$, write $\widetilde\beta_i(X;K)=\dim_K\widetilde H_i(X;K)$ for its reduced singular Betti number. The supplied record does not specify the coefficient field or homology theory; this notation explains a customary interpretation without resolving that omission.

The supplied conjecture fixes constants $c,t$ and considers a family $\mathcal F$ of subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ satisfying \[ \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\widetilde\beta_i\!\left(\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal G}S;K\right)\leq c\,|\mathcal G|^t \qquad\text{for every finite }\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F. \] Here $d$ is the ambient Euclidean dimension. The permitted ranges of $c,t$, conventions for $\mathcal G=\varnothing$, and any regularity assumptions on the sets are not supplied. In particular, the displayed sum starts at $i=1$; it does not include $\widetilde\beta_0$. When $\lfloor d/2\rfloor=0$, the sum is empty and equals zero.

For integers $p\geq q\geq1$, a finite family $\mathcal H$ has the $(p,q)$-property if every $p$-element subfamily $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal H$ contains a $q$-element subfamily $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A$ with \[ \bigcap_{S\in\mathcal B}S\neq\varnothing. \] Its piercing number is \[ \tau(\mathcal H)=\min\left\{|P|:P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ is finite and }P\cap S\neq\varnothing\text{ for every }S\in\mathcal H\right\}, \] with value $+\infty$ if no such finite set exists.

Requested conjectural conclusion. Such families satisfy a $(p,q)$-theorem for every pair of integers $p\geq q\geq d+1$. In the customary uniform finite-family interpretation, this asks for a finite bound $N=N(d,c,t,p,q)$, independent of $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal H$, such that every finite $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ with the $(p,q)$-property satisfies $\tau(\mathcal H)\leq N$. The homology coefficients must also be fixed when interpreting this assertion.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not explicitly define the intended $(p,q)$-theorem, its uniform dependence, or whether it concerns finite subfamilies or a single transversal for an infinite family. It also does not exclude empty members or specify how families with fewer than $p$ members are treated; for such families the displayed $(p,q)$-property is vacuous. These conventions require clarification. The absence of a degree-zero homology bound, especially in dimensions where the sum is empty, requires checking against the intended source hypotheses. No connectedness assumption, degree-zero term, or other missing hypothesis has been inserted.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether polynomial bounds on the indicated reduced Betti numbers of all finite intersections imply a uniform bound on piercing numbers whenever the $(p,q)$-property holds, for every $p\geq q\geq d+1$. The record labels the problem open. Homology conventions, possible missing hypotheses, and the precise finite or infinite piercing formulation remain unresolved; this is a statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
