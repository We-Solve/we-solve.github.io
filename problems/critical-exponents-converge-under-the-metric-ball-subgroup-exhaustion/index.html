Critical exponents converge under the metric-ball subgroup exhaustion

The following is the specific metric-ball subgroup interpretation recovered by the supplied aid.

Let $\Gamma_0$ be a torsion-free discrete subgroup of $\operatorname{Isom}^+(\mathbb H^2)$, let $S=\mathbb H^2/\Gamma_0$, and fix $x\in S$. Using the covering identification $\pi_1(S,x)\cong\Gamma_0$, define, for integers $n\geq1$, \[ \Gamma_n=\operatorname{im}\left(\pi_1(B(x,n),x)\longrightarrow\pi_1(S,x)\right). \] These are image subgroups; inclusion of the ball need not be $\pi_1$-injective. If torsion is allowed, an orbifold formulation is needed instead. The aid's exhaustion has \[ \Gamma_1\leq\Gamma_2\leq\cdots\leq\Gamma_0, \qquad\bigcup_{n\geq1}\Gamma_n=\Gamma_0, \] since each based loop has compact image in some finite-radius ball. The ambient notation $\Gamma_0$ is not an initial subgroup in the increasing stage sequence.

For a subgroup $H\leq\Gamma_0$, fix $o\in\mathbb H^2$ and define its Poincaré series and critical exponent by \[ P_H(s)=\sum_{h\in H}e^{-s\,d(o,ho)},\qquad \delta(H)=\inf\{s\geq0:P_H(s)<\infty\}. \] The aid invokes Sullivan's 1979 Corollary 6 for the increasing-union equality \[ \delta(\Gamma_0)=\sup_{n\geq1}\delta(\Gamma_n). \] Subgroup inclusion gives monotonicity of the stage exponents, so the reported conclusion is \[ \delta(\Gamma_n)\nearrow\delta(\Gamma_0). \] This conclusion uses the cited critical-exponent theorem, not just formal monotone convergence of positive series; the complete hypotheses and reference must be verified.

The supplementary formulation applies to any nested exhaustion by subsets whose carried image subgroups $H_n$ contain every compact based loop at some stage, so $\bigcup_nH_n=\Gamma_0$. It reports the same exponent limit. Moreover, at $\delta=\delta(\Gamma_0)$, monotone convergence of the sums gives \[ P_{\Gamma_0}(\delta)=\lim_nP_{H_n}(\delta). \] Therefore the ambient group is of divergence type, meaning $P_{\Gamma_0}(\delta)=\infty$, exactly when these stage sums are unbounded, allowing an individual stage sum already to be infinite. This does not assert convergence or divergence type from the exponents alone. The aid describes the recovered metric-ball question as solved affirmatively; without its missing source context, no general statement about an arbitrary sequence $\Gamma_n$ is intended.
