Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
In two spatial dimensions, anyons are excitations whose exchanges act through braid-group representations. Abelian statistics are scalar phases, while nonabelian statistics act nontrivially on a multidimensional fusion space. A microscopic loop operator is an observable or unitary associated with transporting an excitation around a closed path, constructed from a local many-body Hamiltonian. It must reproduce the relevant ground-state action, charge transport and braiding relations.

The admissible Hamiltonians, locality and gap assumptions must follow the source abelian construction. A formal topological field-theory braid representation alone does not produce operators for the requested microscopic models.

A nonabelian braid diagnostic.
On $\mathbb C^2$ define \[ B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix},\quad H=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad B_2=HB_1H. \] All three matrices are unitary. Direct multiplication gives \[ B_1B_2B_1=B_2B_1B_2=\frac{1+i}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}. \] Thus $B_1,B_2$ represent the two generators of the three-strand braid group. They do not commute: their upper-right entries in $B_1B_2$ and $B_2B_1$ are respectively $(1-i)/2$ and $(1+i)/2$. Replacing each transport by a scalar phase cannot retain this fusion-space action.
Attempted microscopic interpretation.
The algebra is a necessary diagnostic for any proposed nonabelian construction: transport operations must act on a space of dimension greater than one and their ordering matters. But these matrices specify no lattice support, local Hamiltonian, spectral gap or protected excitation subspace. An arbitrary embedding of $\mathbb C^2$ into a many-body Hilbert space would still not provide path-local loop operators or deformation invariance.
