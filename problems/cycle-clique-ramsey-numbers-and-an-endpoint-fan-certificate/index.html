Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Let $r(C_m,K_n)$ be the least number of vertices that forces a red cycle of length $m$ or a blue clique of size $n$ in every red-blue edge-coloring of a complete graph. Is \[r(C_m,K_n)=(m-1)(n-1)+1\] for all $m\geq n$?

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For all integers $m\ge n\ge3$, except $(m,n)=(3,3)$, the claimed cycle--clique Ramsey number is \[R(C_m,K_n)=(m-1)(n-1)+1.\] This is the least order such that every red-blue edge-coloring of the complete graph has a red $m$-cycle or a blue $n$-clique. The exceptional value is $R(C_3,K_3)=6$.
