Mixed and Nonsingular Identities in Finite Lie-Type Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group and let $X$ be a specified nonempty finite set of formal variables. Write $F(X)$ for the free group on $X$, and $G*F(X)$ for its free product with $G$. For each assignment $a:X\to G$, let $\operatorname{ev}_a:G*F(X)\to G$ be the homomorphism that fixes every element of $G$ and sends $x$ to $a(x)$. A mixed identity over $G$ is an element $w\in G*F(X)$ such that \[ w\ne 1\quad\text{in }G*F(X),\qquad \operatorname{ev}_a(w)=1_G\quad\text{for every }a:X\to G. \] Let $\pi:G*F(X)\to F(X)$ be the homomorphism that sends every element of $G$ to the identity and fixes the variables. Under the conventional interpretation used here, a mixed identity is nonsingular when $\pi(w)\ne 1$. The supplied excerpt does not explicitly define nonsingularity, so this interpretation requires confirmation against the report.

For a specified word-length convention $\ell$, define \[ \begin{aligned} m_{\mathrm{mix}}(G;X,\ell) &=\min\{\ell(w):w\ne1,\ \operatorname{ev}_a(w)=1_G\text{ for every }a:X\to G\},\\ m_{\mathrm{ns}}(G;X,\ell) &=\min\{\ell(w):w\ne1,\ \pi(w)\ne1,\ \operatorname{ev}_a(w)=1_G\text{ for every }a:X\to G\}. \end{aligned} \] Both minima are taken over words in $G*F(X)$; an empty minimum is assigned $+\infty$. These expressions make the dependencies explicit without selecting an unsupported length convention or number of variables.

The supplied problem asks for the lengths of shortest mixed identities and shortest nonsingular identities for the remaining families of finite groups of Lie type. Once the intended families, allowed variables, and length convention are specified, the requested quantities are the corresponding minima above, with minimization over variable sets as well if that is the intended convention. The supplied record does not identify those remaining families, distinguish simple groups from other forms of groups of Lie type, specify admissible rank and field parameters or exceptional small cases, define length, or say whether exact values or asymptotic orders are sought. Consequently, it does not determine a unique fully specified mathematical problem; these qualifications must remain unresolved in this statement-only draft.

Short English statement

Determine how short a nontrivial word with constants from a finite group of Lie type can be while evaluating to the identity for every substitution, and determine the corresponding shortest length when the word remains nontrivial after its constants are erased. The intended remaining families, variable convention, length convention, and meaning of the requested determination require confirmation from the source context.
