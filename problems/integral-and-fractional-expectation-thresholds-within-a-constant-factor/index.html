OpenAI claim summary for Math-2614

The theorem covers every nonempty proper increasing family on a finite ground set with both cover budgets equal to one half. Its explicit universal factor $25\cdot512^4$ answers the entire constant-factor question. The empty-ground-set case has no such family.

Integral and fractional expectation thresholds within a constant factor

OpenAI's claim

Integral versus fractional thresholds: The claimed comparison bounds the fractional expectation threshold by a universal multiple of its integral version. Both versions are defined with the same containment-cover budget.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For nonempty proper increasing $\mathcal F\subset2^X$, with finite nonempty $X$, let $q(\mathcal F)$ be the greatest $p\in[0,1]$ admitting a cover $\mathcal G$ such that every $H\in\mathcal F$ contains some $S\in\mathcal G$ and $\sum_{S\in\mathcal G}p^{|S|}\le1/2$. Define $q_f$ by allowing weights $g:2^X\to[0,1]$, requiring $\sum_{S\subseteq H}g(S)\ge1$ for each $H\in\mathcal F$ and $\sum_Sg(S)p^{|S|}\le1/2$. The exact claim is \[q(\mathcal F)\le q_f(\mathcal F)\le25\cdot512^4q(\mathcal F).\] The same budget $1/2$ is used on both sides, and the factor is independent of the ground-set size and minimal-member sizes.

Outline of the argument

Integral versus fractional thresholds: A multiscale random selector finds one member of the family that captures enough mass at every scale at once. Normalizing fractional cover weights on vertices gives a bounded average color for that member. Independent exponential color variables then force incompatible upper and lower expectations when the integral threshold is too small relative to the fractional threshold.
