Quadratic Toric Ideals and Unimodular Triangulations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $P$ be a lattice polytope, the convex hull of finitely many points in a lattice $M$. In its affine span use the full induced lattice. The source asks two distinct questions.
  1. If the projective toric variety $X_P$ is smooth, must its defining ideal $I_P$ be generated by quadrics? For the usual all-lattice-points embedding, over a field $k$ this ideal is \[ I_P=\ker\!\left(k[x_a:a\in P\cap M]\longrightarrow k[M][t], \quad x_a\longmapsto\chi^a t\right), \] with every $x_a$ of degree one. Quadratic generation means $I_P$ is generated as an ideal by its degree-two homogeneous elements. It does not require a quadratic Gröbner basis. The source does not specify the field or whether $X_P$ denotes this embedded variety or the normal toric variety associated to the normal fan. Those conventions, and the embedding intended by "defining ideal," must be matched; no projective-normality hypothesis is added silently.
  2. Does there exist, for each dimension $d$, a positive integer depending only on $d$ such that every $d$-dimensional lattice polytope admits a regular unimodular triangulation after that dilation? The quantifiers are \[ \forall d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\ \exists n(d)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\ \forall P\text{ of dimension }d:\quad n(d)P\text{ admits a regular unimodular triangulation}. \] Here $nP=\{nx:x\in P\}$. A lattice triangulation subdivides $nP$ into lattice simplices meeting in common faces. It is unimodular if the edge differences at a vertex of every top-dimensional simplex form a basis of the lattice in the affine span. It is regular if it is induced by lower faces of a lifting of the lattice points by real heights. The same $n(d)$ must work for every $P$ of dimension $d$; an integer depending on $P$ is not enough. Nor is the target merely a unimodular cover or a possibly nonregular triangulation.

Smoothness belongs to the first question only. The second ranges over all lattice polytopes of the stated dimension, without a smoothness assumption, and asks for one dilation factor rather than explicitly for every sufficiently large factor. The trivial zero-dimensional case can be treated separately; no dimension bound is otherwise supplied.

Short English statement

The first question asks whether smooth projective toric varieties in the intended polytope embedding have quadratically generated ideals. The second asks for a dilation factor depending only on dimension that gives every lattice polytope a regular unimodular triangulation. Their hypotheses and quantifiers are distinct.
