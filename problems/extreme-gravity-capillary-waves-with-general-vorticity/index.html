Extreme Gravity-Capillary Waves with General Vorticity

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $j\in{\mathbb{N}}$ index the sequence of periodic traveling water waves described in the supplied record, and let $\psi^j$ denote their stream functions, with $\psi_Y^j=\partial\psi^j/\partial Y$. The coordinate $Y$, the normalization of $\psi^j$, and the fluid domains are not specified in the record. Write $D_j$ for the unspecified set over which the source takes its maximum. The stated almost-extreme condition is \[ m_j:=\max_{D_j}\psi_Y^j\longrightarrow 0 \qquad (j\to\infty). \] This notation retains the source's maximum; it does not replace it by a supremum or by a maximum of an absolute value. The set $D_j$ and hypotheses ensuring that this maximum exists require clarification.

A stagnation point is a point where the fluid velocity relative to the traveling wave vanishes. For a planar stream-function formulation, this is equivalent to $\nabla\psi=0$. The Stokes $120^\circ$ corner condition means that two limiting free-surface branches meet with interior fluid angle $2\pi/3$, assuming such branches and their limiting tangent directions exist.

Source-level question. For periodic traveling water waves with general vorticity, does a sequence of almost-extreme, symmetric, monotone waves satisfying the displayed condition converge to an extreme wave satisfying the Stokes $120^\circ$ corner condition at stagnation points, rather than to a smooth wave with a bottom stagnation point?

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether almost-extreme periodic waves with general vorticity approach an extreme wave with a $120^\circ$ free-surface corner, instead of a smooth wave with stagnation at the bed. The wave model, limiting procedure, and several essential hypotheses remain unspecified. The supplied status is "partially solved"; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
