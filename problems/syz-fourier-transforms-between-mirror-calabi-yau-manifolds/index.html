SYZ Fourier Transforms between Mirror Calabi–Yau Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A symplectic structure on a smooth manifold $X$ is a closed, nondegenerate real differential $2$-form $\omega$. A complex structure is an integrable almost complex structure $J$. The source refers to mirror Calabi--Yau manifolds, but does not specify its convention for Calabi--Yau manifolds, their dimension, compactness, additional geometric data, or the criterion for being a mirror pair. These terms therefore remain source-dependent qualifications here.

SYZ denotes the Strominger--Yau--Zaslow approach to mirror symmetry, involving dual torus fibrations. A fiberwise Fourier transformation refers here to a Fourier-type operation along such fibers; the source does not specify the objects transformed, a kernel, or the resulting mathematical map. No existence of suitable global fibrations is assumed in this draft.

Source question. For a pair $(X,\check X)$ of mirror Calabi--Yau manifolds, is there an explicit SYZ or fiberwise Fourier transformation that interchanges their symplectic and complex geometries?

The question seeks a construction for mirror Calabi--Yau manifolds at the generality intended by the source. However, neither the admissible class of pairs nor the meaning of "interchanges" is specified. In particular, the source supplies no identity, equivalence, or compatibility condition that such a transformation must satisfy. It also supplies no scope condition concerning smooth fibers, singular fibers, or extension across a singular locus. Consequently, this is a statement of the supplied construction problem with its unresolved qualifications, rather than a uniquely specified quantified existence proposition.

Short English statement

The problem asks for an explicit SYZ or fiberwise Fourier transformation relating symplectic geometry on a Calabi--Yau manifold to complex geometry on its mirror. The source leaves the class of mirror pairs, the transformation's input and output, and the required compatibility unspecified, so a precise existence theorem cannot yet be formulated.
