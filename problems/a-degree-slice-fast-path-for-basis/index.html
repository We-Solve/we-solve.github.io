A degree-slice prototype with a proved basis contract

Official documentation distinguishes vector-space bases over a coefficient field from generating sets over more general coefficient rings. We restrict to a field $K$ and $R=K[x,y]/(x^a,y^b)$, where $a,b\ge1$, with the usual total grading. The goal of this prototype is the homogeneous degree-$D$ vector-space basis for $D\ge0$, not the general overloaded Macaulay2 basis method.
Proposition: Set $\ell=\max(0,D-b+1)$ and $u=\min(D,a-1)$. The monomials \[ \{x^i y^{D-i}:\ell\le i\le u\} \] form a basis of $R_D$, of cardinality $\max(0,u-\ell+1)$.
Proof: The ideal $(x^a,y^b)$ is spanned over $K$ by monomials with $x$ exponent at least $a$ or $y$ exponent at least $b$: multiples of either generator have this property, and every such monomial is a multiple of a generator. Since distinct monomials form a basis of the polynomial ring, the complementary monomials with $0\le i<a$ and $0\le j<b$ give a basis of the quotient: they span after deleting ideal monomials, and any linear combination in the ideal must have all complementary coefficients zero. Imposing $i+j=D$ yields $j=D-i\ge0$, $i\ge0$, $i\le a-1$, and $D-i\le b-1$, exactly $\ell\le i\le u$. Counting integers proves the formula. ∎
A direct generator computes the two bounds and emits only that interval of exponent pairs. For $(a,b,D)=(4,3,4)$ it returns $(i,D-i)=(2,2),(3,1)$, namely $x^2y^2,x^3y$. It never constructs the whole quotient basis. The formula requires only a constant number of integer bound operations plus one output operation per returned pair; this is not a bit-complexity or Macaulay2 runtime claim.
