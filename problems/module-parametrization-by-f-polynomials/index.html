Module Parametrization by $F$-Polynomials

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt introduces the additional assumption $k={\mathbb{C}}$, an algebra $\Lambda\cong kQ/I$, an admissible ideal $I$, and the number $n$ of vertices of $Q$. Here $kQ$ denotes the path algebra of the quiver $Q$. Under the usual convention, admissibility means that $J^m\subseteq I\subseteq J^2$ for some integer $m\geq2$, where $J$ is the ideal generated by the arrows. The word "moreover" in the excerpt indicates earlier hypotheses that are not supplied.

For a finite-dimensional $\Lambda$-module $M$, viewed as a representation of $Q$, and a dimension vector $\mathbf d=(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}^n$, let $\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbf d}(M)$ denote the quiver Grassmannian parametrizing submodules $N\subseteq M$ with $\dim_k N_i=d_i$ at each vertex $i$. Write $\chi$ for the topological Euler characteristic and $\mathbf y^{\mathbf d}=\prod_{i=1}^n y_i^{d_i}$. In this finite-dimensional setting, the displayed formula in the excerpt has the standard interpretation \[ F(M)=\sum_{\mathbf d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}^n}\chi\!\left(\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbf d}(M)\right)\mathbf y^{\mathbf d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y_1,\ldots,y_n]. \] The excerpt says "any $\Lambda$-module" without specifying finite dimensionality; the definition above explains the usual finite-dimensional interpretation and does not resolve the intended module domain.

Reported conjecture, with unresolved formulation. The original excerpt asserts that a map $\Phi$ is conjectured to be an isomorphism. The supplied clean statement describes it as a parametrization map associated with the $F$-polynomials above and asks whether \[ \Phi\text{ is an isomorphism}. \] However, the record gives neither the definition of $\Phi$, its source and target, nor the structures with respect to which it should be an isomorphism. Moreover, the conjecture sentence precedes the heading "4. Parametrization" and the assumption $k={\mathbb{C}}$; the excerpt does not establish that this later paragraph supplies the hypotheses or construction for that conjecture. Consequently, a precise quantified conjecture cannot be recovered from this record.

The record asks whether an unspecified map $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and associates it with module $F$-polynomials. It supplies an $F$-polynomial formula over ${\mathbb{C}}$, but omits the map, its domain, its codomain, and potentially earlier hypotheses.
