Wikipedia model theory and formal languages item 14: Assume K is the class of models of a countable first order theory omitting countably many types…

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T$ be a countable first-order theory in the intended countable language, and let $\Gamma=\{p_i:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}$ be a countable family of types. Under a standard finite-tuple, parameter-free convention, $p_i$ is a set of formulas in a fixed finite tuple of variables, and $M$ omits $p_i$ when no tuple in $M$ satisfies all formulas of $p_i$. The source does not state whether types are partial or complete, over parameters, or possibly in infinitely many variables.

Define \[ K=\{M:M\models T\text{ and }M\text{ omits every }p_i\in\Gamma\}. \] Write $\omega_1$ for the first uncountable ordinal, $\aleph_{\omega_1}$ for the corresponding aleph-indexed cardinal, and $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$ for the continuum.

The proposed transfer question is whether, for every such $T$ and $\Gamma$, \[ \left(\exists M\in K:\ |M|=\aleph_{\omega_1}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\exists N\in K:\ |N|=\mathfrak c\right). \] No equality between $\mathfrak c$ and a particular aleph, continuum-size inequality, cofinality assumption, or form of Martin's Axiom is imposed. The countable language and type conventions need matching to the intended source before a cardinal-transfer theorem is fully specified.

Short English statement

Does a countable theory with countably many omitted types transfer model existence from $\aleph_{\omega_1}$ to the continuum? The continuum is left as the actual set-theoretic cardinal $2^{\aleph_0}$.

The supplied summary says Shelah's Sh 522 gives failures under Martin's Axiom for certain cardinal/cofinality configurations and describes the behavior as set-theory dependent. It does not supply the exact hypotheses or a checked match to the displayed transfer. Consequently it is retained as reported partial progress, not as an unrestricted ZFC counterexample or a complete independence theorem. The pinned Wikipedia primary-source audit remains required; its unpublished background conflicts with published registry metadata.
