Zilber–Pink for Powers of the Modular Curve

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer and let $\mathbb H=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}:\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\}$. The modular curve $Y(1)$ has complex analytic realization $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\mathbb H$; the modular $j$-invariant identifies its underlying algebraic curve with ${\mathbb{A}}^1_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$. For the formulation below, use its complex base change $Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}$. This is an explicit interpretation of the analytic setting: the supplied clean statement does not specify a field of definition for $V$.

A singular modulus is a number $j(\tau)$ with $\tau\in\mathbb H$ imaginary quadratic over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. For $N\geq1$, let $\Phi_N(X,Y)$ be the classical modular polynomial, whose vanishing expresses the existence of a cyclic isogeny of degree $N$ between elliptic curves with the indicated $j$-invariants. A special subvariety of $Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}$ is an irreducible component of a locus defined by finitely many equations of the forms \[ x_i=c,\qquad \Phi_N(x_i,x_j)=0, \] where $c$ is a singular modulus and $N$ is a positive integer. Here special subvarieties, rather than arbitrary weakly special subvarieties, are intended. An irreducible algebraic subvariety $V\subseteq Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}$ is Hodge generic if it is contained in no proper special subvariety.

For a proper special subvariety $S\subsetneq Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}$, an irreducible component $W$ of the algebraic intersection $V\cap S$ is called atypical if \[ \dim W>\dim V+\dim S-n. \] Define \[ \mathcal A(V)= \bigcup_{\substack{S\subsetneq Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}\\S\text{ special}}} \ \bigcup_{\substack{W\text{ an irreducible component of }V\cap S\\ \dim W>\dim V+\dim S-n}}W. \] The supplied question is whether, for every $n\geq1$ and every irreducible Hodge-generic algebraic subvariety $V\subseteq Y(1)_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}$, \[ \overline{\mathcal A(V)}^{\,\mathrm{Zar},V}\subsetneq V. \] Equivalently, must there exist a proper Zariski-closed subset of $V$ containing every such atypical component?

The general target above comes from the supplied clean statement. The supplied original excerpt is truncated and discusses only an irreducible Hodge-generic curve $C$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, with an additional asymmetric hypothesis for a reported theorem. It neither defines asymmetric nor supplies a complete statement of that theorem. Consequently, the excerpt alone does not establish the intended field-of-definition scope of the general question or permit a precise formulation of the reported special case.

Short English statement

Must all unexpectedly large components of intersections between an irreducible Hodge-generic subvariety of a power of the modular curve and proper special subvarieties lie in one proper Zariski-closed subset? The displayed formulation uses complex varieties explicitly; the source leaves the intended field-of-definition restriction unresolved.
