Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied problem asks whether the equation $\bar\partial u=f$ can be solved with a supremum-norm estimate on every smooth bounded convex domain in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$. The record does not specify the degree or regularity of $f$, the solution class, or the precise estimate. Consequently, the following is an explicitly qualified conventional formulation, rather than a uniquely determined transcription.

Let $D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^2$ be a nonempty bounded convex domain. For this formulation, interpret smooth boundary as $C^\infty$ boundary. Write $z=(z_1,z_2)$ and let \[ f=f_1\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\bar z_1+f_2\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\bar z_2, \qquad f_1,f_2\in L^\infty(D). \] Define \[ \|f\|_{L^\infty(D)} =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{z\in D} \left(|f_1(z)|^2+|f_2(z)|^2\right)^{1/2}. \] The compatibility condition $\bar\partial f=0$ means \[ \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial\bar z_1} -\frac{\partial f_1}{\partial\bar z_2}=0 \] in the sense of distributions on $D$.

Under these conventions, the question is whether, for every such $D$, there exists a constant $C_D<\infty$ such that every $\bar\partial$-closed $f\in L^\infty_{(0,1)}(D)$ admits a scalar function $u\in L^\infty(D)$ satisfying \[ \bar\partial u=f\quad\text{in distributions on }D, \qquad \|u\|_{L^\infty(D)}\le C_D\|f\|_{L^\infty(D)}. \] Here $C_D$ may depend on $D$, but not on $f$. No boundary value for $u$ is prescribed in the supplied statement. The choice of bounded measurable data, distributional solutions, degree $(0,1)$, and a domain-dependent linear norm estimate requires confirmation against the intended source formulation; the record may instead concern smoother data or solutions. Its supplied status is open, which is a registry label rather than an independently verified mathematical assessment.

Short English statement

Does every smooth bounded convex domain in ${\mathbb{C}}^2$ admit supremum-norm control for solutions of $\bar\partial u=f$? A conventional formulation asks for a bounded solution for every bounded, $\bar\partial$-closed $(0,1)$-form, with its norm bounded by a domain-dependent constant times the norm of the data. The supplied statement leaves these functional and regularity conventions unresolved.
