A partition function is the amplitude or generating function assigned to a physical theory on its spacetime background. Testing the ordinary product-index shortcut.
The suggested loop-index test cannot be replaced without justification by an ordinary Dirac index. To see a concrete obstruction, take a closed even-dimensional spin manifold $X$ and a flat two-torus $T^2$, with a bundle pulled back from $X$ and product connections. For the graded product Dirac operator, \[ D_{X\times T^2}=D_X\otimes I+\Gamma_X\otimes D_{T^2},\qquad D_{X\times T^2}^2=D_X^2\otimes I+I\otimes D_{T^2}^2. \] Here $\Gamma_X$ is the chirality operator, which anticommutes with $D_X$. Taking the inner product with a zero mode shows that both nonnegative square terms vanish, so the kernel is the tensor product of the two kernels. Graded dimensions multiply.
The elliptic factor kills the ordinary index.
With the periodic spin structure on the flat torus, its positive and negative chiral spin bundles each have one constant zero mode; every nonzero Fourier mode has strictly positive squared eigenvalue. Their index difference is zero. With a nontrivial flat spin structure there is no zero Fourier mode and again the index is zero. Therefore the twisted product index in this setting is zero regardless of the index on $X$. The same conclusion holds for any finite sum or difference of twists pulled back from $X$.
Why this matters and what is missing.
This is a countercheck on a proposed method, not a disproof of the source's F-theory interpretation: loop indices carry additional graded information which an ordinary index forgets. It also does not define a twelve-dimensional path integral, flux quantization, or the proposed $E_8$ and Rarita--Schwinger loop operators. Defining those operators and showing what replaces the product-kernel calculation is the first gap. Both the partition-function comparison and its requested loop-index tests remain unsettled.
