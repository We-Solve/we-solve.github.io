Math-6606

Stationarity of a GARCH Recurrence

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $A_t:=\alpha_0 Z_t^2+\beta_0$. The supplied recurrence and logarithmic condition are \[ h_t=\omega_0+A_{t-1}h_{t-1},\qquad {\mathbb{E}}[\log A_t]<0. \] Here $\omega_0,\alpha_0,\beta_0$ are parameters and $(Z_t)$ is a stochastic sequence, but their domains, parameter restrictions, probability law, and time index set are not specified. The logarithmic condition requires a convention under which the expectation is defined; the source does not specify positivity, integrability, or treatment of $\log 0$.

Reported implication to be clarified. The record asks why this logarithmic condition implies the existence of a stationary ergodic solution and convergence of solutions from different initial conditions. For a two-sided interpretation, a solution would be a process $(H_t)_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}$, defined jointly with the driving sequence, satisfying \[ H_t=\omega_0+A_{t-1}H_{t-1}\quad\text{almost surely for every }t\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Strict stationarity means that every finite-dimensional distribution is unchanged by integer shifts of all its time indices. Ergodicity means that each shift-invariant event under the process law has probability zero or one. The source does not specify whether these properties concern $(H_t)$ alone or its joint process with $(Z_t)$, nor whether a two-sided time index is intended.

A possible interpretation of convergence from different initial conditions is the following: for each pair of admissible initial values $x,y$, solutions $(h_t^{(x)})$ and $(h_t^{(y)})$ driven by the same sequence satisfy \[ \lvert h_t^{(x)}-h_t^{(y)}\rvert\longrightarrow0\qquad(t\to\infty). \] This interpretation is provisional: admissible initial values, their dependence on the driving sequence, the coupling of solutions, and the mode of convergence are absent from the supplied formulation.

Unresolved qualifications. No independence, identical-distribution, stationarity, or ergodicity assumptions on $(Z_t)$ are supplied. Consequently, the displayed logarithmic condition and recurrence do not specify a complete implication with justified hypotheses. This draft records the intended explanatory question without asserting it as a theorem or as a verified open problem.

Short English statement

The record asks why a negative expected logarithmic multiplier yields a stationary ergodic solution of the stated recurrence and convergence from different initial conditions. Essential assumptions on the noise, parameters, logarithm, and convergence are missing. Supplied verification describes this as a standard explanatory step; the registry's open label is not an independently verified mathematical status.
