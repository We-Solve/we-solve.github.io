Random Geometric Graphs in the Hyperbolic Plane

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb H^2$ denote the hyperbolic plane, with hyperbolic distance $d_{\mathbb H}$ and area measure $A_{\mathbb H}$. The supplied record does not specify the curvature normalization. A point configuration $X\subseteq\mathbb H^2$ is locally finite if every compact subset contains finitely many points of $X$. A Poisson point process with locally finite intensity measure $\mu$ is a random locally finite configuration whose counts in pairwise disjoint bounded Borel sets are independent, with \[ \#(X\cap B)\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\mu(B)). \] The homogeneous choice $\mu=\lambda A_{\mathbb H}$, $\lambda>0$, is one possible specification; the record does not explicitly select it. In a finite-point model, $X$ is a random finite configuration, whose sampling region, point-count law, and joint location law must be specified.

For a specified random graph construction $G=(X,E)$, let $C_G(x)$ be the connected component containing $x\in X$, and let \[ N_k(G)=\#\{C:C\text{ is a connected component of }G,\ |C|=k\},\qquad k\geq1. \] For finite nonempty $X$, define \[ L(G)=\max_{x\in X}|C_G(x)|. \] Then $G$ is connected precisely when $L(G)=|X|$. For an infinite locally finite configuration, the graph percolation event is \[ \mathcal P_G=\{\exists x\in X:\ |C_G(x)|=\infty\}. \] If continuum percolation instead refers to an occupied random subset of $\mathbb H^2$, its construction must be given, and the corresponding event is the existence of an unbounded connected component of that subset. The record does not specify which continuum construction is intended.

Research question, conditional on specifying the models. Determine the component-size and connectivity behavior of finite random geometric graphs and continuum percolation in $\mathbb H^2$, when hyperbolic geometry replaces Euclidean planar geometry. For a chosen graph model, possible precise observables include the laws of $N_k(G)$, $L(G)$, and the connectivity event, and, for an infinite model, the probability of $\mathcal P_G$. These observables provide a framework for the stated question, rather than additional source-specified conjectures.

Short English statement

The question asks how component sizes and connectivity change when random geometric graphs and continuum percolation are studied in the hyperbolic plane. A precise conjecture requires further choices of point distribution, connection rule, boundary conditions, and limiting regime.
