Math-1451

The quarter-pinching endpoint and a spread obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's Question $5.4.1$ asks whether there exists a closed negatively curved Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, of real dimension greater than two, whose geodesic flow has $C^1$ stable and unstable bundles $E^s,E^u$, while $g$ is not $1/4$-pinched. Here the Anosov splitting on $SM$ is $T(SM)=E^s\oplus E^0\oplus E^u$, with $E^0$ tangent to the flow; $C^1$ refers to these bundles as distributions on all of $SM$.

The pinching endpoint is essential. For the global convention, put \[ k_{\min}=\min_{x,\sigma}(-K_g(\sigma))>0,\qquad k_{\max}=\max_{x,\sigma}(-K_g(\sigma)). \] Weak quarter pinching means $k_{\max}/k_{\min}\leq4$, whereas strict quarter pinching means $k_{\max}/k_{\min}<4$. Under the weak interpretation, the requested metric must have ratio greater than $4$; the aid reports that no such example with both bundles $C^1$ was found in literature checked through 8 August 2026. This is a dated open-status qualification, not a nonexistence theorem.

Under the strict interpretation, compact complex-hyperbolic quotients of complex dimension at least two already give the reported positive answer: their curvature ranges from $-4$ to $-1$, the ratio is exactly $4$, and the locally symmetric stable and unstable bundles are smooth. They fail strict quarter pinching but satisfy weak quarter pinching, so they do not answer the intended beyond-weak-endpoint question.

The appended source discussion motivates a perturbation program from [Has-Wil1999], Section $4$, especially Proposition $12$, seeking open metric sets with low-regularity Anosov splitting on large subsets. It requires a suitable base with widely separated unstable expansion rates and says that suitable geodesic-flow bases were not then known. It also points to Pollicott's question in the next subsection, whose statement is not part of this record.

For the aid's exact parallel two-rate symplectic Anosov model, let the slow and fast positive rates be $0<a\leq B$, with paired negative stable rates. "Exact parallel" means the invariant rate subbundles have the prescribed exponential rates without variable-rate errors. The aid's reduction of the Hasselblatt--Wilkinson $\alpha$-u-spread parameter feasibility is \[ \text{the specified spread inequalities are feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha B>2a. \] The full definition and auxiliary parameter inequalities of $\alpha$-u-spread are not supplied, so this equivalence is retained as a reported reduction in that exact model. Complex hyperbolic space has parallel curvature sectors $-1,-4$ and positive rates $a=1,B=2$. Thus it fails the strict $1$-u-spread condition at equality, but satisfies the $(1+\varepsilon)$-version for every $\varepsilon>0$. This endpoint obstruction does not prove that no other perturbation argument works or that a requested beyond-quarter-pinched example cannot exist.

Short English statement

The question depends on whether quarter pinching includes equality. Complex-hyperbolic examples answer the strict version, while the beyond-weak-bound version is reported open through 8 August 2026. The aid's two-rate reduction explains why complex-hyperbolic rates $1,2$ sit exactly at, and fail, the strict $1$-u-spread threshold.
