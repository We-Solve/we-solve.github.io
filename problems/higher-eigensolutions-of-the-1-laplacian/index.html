Higher Eigensolutions of the $1$-Laplacian

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For notation, if $n\geq 1$ and $\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ is open, then \[ BV(\Omega)=\{u\in L^1(\Omega):Du\text{ is a finite }{\mathbb{R}}^n\text{-valued Radon measure}\}, \] where $Du$ is the distributional derivative. Its total variation is \[ |Du|(\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_\Omega u\,\operatorname{div}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x: \varphi\in C_c^1(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^n),\ |\varphi(x)|\leq 1\text{ for every }x\in\Omega\right\}. \] These definitions explain the notation; the supplied record does not specify the dimension or hypotheses on the intended domain $\Omega$.

Problem as supplied. For the variational eigenproblem of the nonsmooth $1$-Laplace operator on $BV(\Omega)$, define and characterize higher eigensolutions beyond the first minimizers, accounting for the ambiguity between a single eigenvalue equation and multiple eigenvalue equations.

Unresolved formulation. The record supplies neither the variational functional and normalization constraint nor the boundary conditions, the notion of solution, or the competing single and multiple eigenvalue equations. Consequently, it does not determine a specific eigenproblem, the meaning of the first minimizers, or an indexing and multiplicity convention for higher eigensolutions. The total variation defined above alone does not specify these choices. A precise quantified characterization therefore requires additional source information; no particular completion of these missing hypotheses is asserted here.

The supplied literature assessment reports that a variational weak-slope construction gives a sequence of higher $1$-Laplacian eigensolutions, while the broader question of intrinsic characterization remains unresolved. This is supplied dated curation, not an independently verified mathematical or literature conclusion.

Short English statement

The problem asks how to define and intrinsically characterize higher eigensolutions of the nonsmooth $1$-Laplacian beyond the first minimizers, including how single and multiple eigenvalue equations should be interpreted. The supplied record leaves the domain assumptions, boundary conditions, normalization, and equations unspecified, so it does not determine a unique precise eigenproblem. Its supplied status is partially solved.
