Open Problems in Topological Algebra — Problem 8 (Protasov)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A topological group is a group $G$ equipped with a topology for which multiplication $G\times G\to G$ and inversion $G\to G$ are continuous. The supplied record does not specify a separation axiom; the intended convention requires review. The group is non-discrete if its topology is not discrete. A subset $A\subseteq G$ is nowhere dense if \[ \operatorname{int}_G\!\left(\overline{A}^{\,G}\right)=\varnothing. \] Its algebraically generated subgroup is \[ \langle A\rangle =\{a_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots a_n^{\varepsilon_n}: n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\ a_i\in A,\ \varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\}, \] where the empty product is the identity.

The question supplied as Problem 8, attributed to Protasov, is: does every non-discrete topological group $G$ admit a subset $A\subseteq G$ such that \[ \operatorname{int}_G\!\left(\overline{A}^{\,G}\right)=\varnothing \qquad\text{and}\qquad \langle A\rangle=G? \]

The record also includes a parenthetical assertion explicitly referring to Problem 6: its answer is reported as negative, with the following conditional example attributed to Proposition 13.8 of the Protasov--Banakh monograph cited below. Under the continuum hypothesis $2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1$, the product topological group $({\mathbb{R}}^{\omega_1},+)$, where $\omega_1$ is the first uncountable ordinal, is reported to contain a dense non-separable subgroup $H$ such that every meager subset $M\subseteq H$ generates a subgroup $\langle M\rangle$ that is metrizable and separable in the subspace topology. Here a meager subset is a countable union of nowhere dense subsets. This is a supplied source assertion, not an independently verified theorem in this draft. The reference to Problem 6, its relationship to the displayed Problem 8 question, and the registry's supplied open status remain unresolved.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every non-discrete topological group can be generated, using finite products and inverses, by a nowhere dense subset. The supplied record also reports a conditional negative example under the continuum hypothesis, but labels that discussion as concerning Problem 6. This numbering and status issue requires review.
