Problem 12.15 — (M.

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a knot $K\subset S^3$, its unknotting number $u(K)$ is the minimum, over all diagrams of $K$, of the number of crossing changes needed to obtain the unknot. In the usual Conway-mutation convention, a sphere meeting $K$ transversely in four points cuts it into two $2$-string tangles; rotating one tangle by an allowed half-turn and regluing gives a mutant knot $K'$.

M. Boileau asks whether \[ \exists K,K'\subset S^3:\quad K'\text{ is a mutant of }K\quad\text{and}\quad u(K')\ne u(K). \] Equivalently, is unknotting number nonconstant on some mutation class? No restriction to prime, alternating or hyperbolic knots, or to a prescribed unknotting number, is supplied. The excerpt does not define mutation; the displayed statement uses its standard Conway interpretation, subject to checking the convention in the cited problem.

Short English statement

Do there exist two mutant knots with different unknotting numbers?
