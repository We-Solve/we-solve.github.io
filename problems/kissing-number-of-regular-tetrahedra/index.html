Math-6466

Kissing Number of Regular Tetrahedra

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A regular tetrahedron in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ is the convex hull of four affinely independent points whose six pairwise distances are equal to some positive number. Its vertices are those four points, and $\operatorname{int}(T)$ denotes its interior in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$.

For a positive integer $n$, call a family $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ admissible if each $T_i$ is a regular tetrahedron, there exists $p\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$ that is a vertex of every $T_i$, and \[ \operatorname{int}(T_i)\cap\operatorname{int}(T_j)=\varnothing \qquad(1\leq i<j\leq n). \] Thus boundary contacts, including contacts beyond the common vertex, are permitted. The supplied statement does not explicitly require different tetrahedra to have equal edge lengths; this formulation imposes no such additional restriction.

Determine the maximum \[ \tau_3=\max\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:\text{there exists an admissible family of }n\text{ tetrahedra}\right\}. \] The target is the exact value of $\tau_3$, with a construction attaining it and an upper bound excluding larger admissible families.

The supplied dated curation states that an icosahedral construction gives $20$ tetrahedra and that the report improves the elementary upper bound $22$, while neither the exact value nor the optimality of $20$ is proved there. The improved numerical upper bound is not supplied. These are source-reported qualifications, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

Determine the largest number $\tau_3$ of regular tetrahedra in three-dimensional Euclidean space that can have one common vertex while their interiors remain pairwise disjoint. Boundary contacts are allowed. The supplied record labels the problem open; its dated assessment reports a construction with $20$ tetrahedra without asserting that this number is optimal.
