Math-3490

Parallel-Axiom Rigidity of Complete Riemannian Planes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A complete Riemannian plane is understood here to be a smooth surface $P$, without boundary and diffeomorphic to ${\mathbb{R}}^2$, equipped with a complete Riemannian metric $g$. Under the convention that a geodesic means the unparametrized image of a complete nonconstant geodesic, put \[ \mathcal G(P,g)=\{\gamma({\mathbb{R}}):\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to P\text{ is a unit-speed geodesic}\}. \] The parallel axiom is the assertion \[ \forall L\in\mathcal G(P,g)\;\forall p\in P\setminus L,\qquad \#\{M\in\mathcal G(P,g):p\in M\text{ and }M\cap L=\varnothing\}=1. \] Thus uniqueness refers to the geodesic image, independently of its parametrization or orientation.

Question. If a complete Riemannian plane $(P,g)$ satisfies this parallel axiom, must there exist a Riemannian isometry \[ F\colon(P,g)\longrightarrow({\mathbb{R}}^2,{\,\mathrm{d}} x^2+{\,\mathrm{d}} y^2)? \] No curvature condition or absence-of-conjugate-points hypothesis is stated in the supplied problem.

Interpretive qualification. The supplied record does not explicitly define "Riemannian plane" or specify whether "geodesic" means every complete geodesic or only a globally minimizing geodesic line. The formulation above uses all complete geodesic images; it does not impose global minimization. These conventions require confirmation against the source. The registry labels the question open; this is a supplied status, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

Short English statement

Must every complete Riemannian surface diffeomorphic to ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ be Euclidean if, through each point outside each complete geodesic, there passes exactly one complete geodesic disjoint from it? Here geodesics are counted by their images; the source's intended convention concerning globally minimizing lines requires review.
