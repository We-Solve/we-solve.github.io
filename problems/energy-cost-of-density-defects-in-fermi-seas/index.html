Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For a free Fermi sea in $\mathbb R^d$, chemical potential $\mu>0$ and spin degeneracy $q$ fix $\rho_0=q(2\pi)^{-d}|\{k:|k|^2\le\mu\}|$. Its one-particle density matrix is $\Pi_-=\mathbf1_{(-\infty,\mu)}(-\Delta)$. An admissible fermionic state has $0\le\gamma\le1$, density $\rho_\gamma$, and relative perturbation $Q=\gamma-\Pi_-$. The density defect $\delta\rho=\rho_\gamma-\rho_0$ can be negative but satisfies $\delta\rho\ge-\rho_0$. Relative kinetic energy is the properly defined relative trace of $(-\Delta-\mu)Q$, not the difference of two divergent absolute energies. A positive-density Lieb--Thirring bound compares that energy to an integral of the convex remainder $\rho_\gamma^{1+2/d}-\rho_0^{1+2/d}-(1+2/d)\rho_0^{2/d}\delta\rho$.
Source scope and qualifications.
The official report already proves a positive-density lower bound in d>=2. The remaining optimal-cost or sharp-constant task is more specific than the catalogue's title-only dated assessment. Dimensions, spin normalization, finite-relative-energy domain, and density admissibility must be retained; a trace of two infinite energies is not a valid definition.

The homogeneous density-cost benchmark.
Let $v_d$ be the volume of the unit ball and $q$ the spin degeneracy. Filling a momentum ball of radius $k_F$ gives $\rho=qv_dk_F^d/(2\pi)^d$. Direct radial integration of $|k|^2$ gives kinetic energy density \[ e(\rho)=K_{d,q}\rho^{1+2/d},\qquad K_{d,q}=\frac d{d+2}\left(\frac{(2\pi)^d}{qv_d}\right)^{2/d}. \] The derivative at $\rho_0$ is the Fermi chemical potential $\mu=k_{F,0}^2$. Hence the homogeneous grand-canonical cost of changing density by $s\geq-\rho_0$ is \[ e(\rho_0+s)-e(\rho_0)-\mu s =K_{d,q}\left((\rho_0+s)^p-\rho_0^p-p\rho_0^{p-1}s\right),\quad p=1+2/d. \] Strict convexity makes it nonnegative, with quadratic small-$s$ coefficient $K_{d,q}p(p-1)\rho_0^{p-2}/2$.
The complete-hole boundary.
At $s=-\rho_0$, the expression is $K_{d,q}(p-1)\rho_0^p>0$. Thus removing all local particles need not lower the relative energy: subtraction of the chemical-potential term matters. At $s=0$ it vanishes to second order as required.
Sharpness gap.
These are uniform momentum fillings, whose infinite-volume total relative energy is not finite; they provide a cost per volume benchmark only. Turning them into admissible localized defects requires boundary control, and proving a sharp lower bound for arbitrary $0\leq\gamma\leq1$ requires quantum spatial estimates. The source already proves a nonsharp positive-density bound in $d\geq2$. This elementary calculation neither replaces it nor establishes the optimal constant or optimal density-hole profile.
