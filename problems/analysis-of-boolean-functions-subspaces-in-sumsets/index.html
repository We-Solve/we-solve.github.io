CS-35 · Math-2516

Analysis of Boolean Functions — Subspaces in Sumsets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{F}}_2={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}$. For an integer $n\geq 1$, regard ${\mathbb{F}}_2^n$ as a vector space with coordinatewise addition. For $A\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$, define its density and sumset by \[ \mu(A)=\frac{|A|}{2^n},\qquad A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}. \] For a linear subspace $V\leq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$, set $\operatorname{codim}(V)=n-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_2}V$. Here a subspace means a linear subspace, containing the zero vector.

The question is whether, for every fixed real number $\alpha$ with $0<\alpha\leq 1$, there exists a constant $C_\alpha>0$ such that, for every integer $n\geq 1$ and every $A\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$, \[ |A|\geq\alpha 2^n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\,V\leq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n\;\left(V\subseteq A+A\ \text{ and }\ \operatorname{codim}(V)\leq C_\alpha\sqrt n\right). \] Thus the implicit constant in the source's $O(\sqrt n)$ is interpreted as allowed to depend on the fixed density parameter $\alpha$, but not on $n$ or $A$. The source states $\alpha>0$; values $\alpha>1$ admit no sets satisfying the density hypothesis and hence give only vacuous instances.

The following are supplied contextual claims and qualifications, not independently verified results or additional hypotheses of the question.

  • The source attributes the problem to [Gre05a]. It says that the analogous question for the cyclic group ${\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}$ dates to Bourgain [Bou90]; that analogy does not change the ambient group in the present question.
  • Writing $|x|$ for the number of nonzero coordinates of $x\in{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$, the source gives Hamming balls of the form \[ A=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_2^n:|x|\leq n/2-\Theta(\sqrt n)\} \] as showing that the proposed $O(\sqrt n)$ codimension order cannot be improved in general. The example is described as essentially due to Ruzsa [Ruz93], with [Gre05a] cited for discussion. The supplied record does not specify the constants in the radius or the associated density range.
  • The source attributes to Sanders [San10a] the bound that $A+A$ contains a subspace of codimension at most \[ \left\lceil\frac{n}{1+\log_2\!\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{1-2\alpha}\right)}\right\rceil. \] The displayed real-valued expression is meaningful for $0<\alpha<1/2$; the supplied remark does not explicitly state its parameter range or a separate endpoint formulation. The source describes this as giving dimension roughly $(\alpha/\ln 2)n$ for small $\alpha$, and codimension roughly $n/\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$ when $\alpha=1/2-\varepsilon$ with small positive $\varepsilon$. These are the source's informal asymptotic descriptions.
  • The same supplied remark attributes to Sanders [San10a] the assertion that if $\alpha\geq 1/2-0.001/\sqrt n$, then $A+A$ contains a subspace of codimension $1$.
  • The source also raises a tentative relaxed variant, referring to its remarks on the Polynomial Freiman--Ruzsa/Bogolyubov Conjectures: require only a linear subspace $V\leq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$ satisfying \[ |(A+A)\cap V|\geq 0.99|V|. \] It suggests that such a subspace might have codimension $O(\log(1/\alpha))$. This is a separate tentative question. The supplied text does not explicitly specify the uniformity of its implicit constant or the full density range for that proposed estimate.
The registry labels the main question open. This is a supplied status label, not an independently verified assessment of the literature.

Short English statement

For each fixed positive density $\alpha$, must every set $A\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$ of density at least $\alpha$ have a sumset $A+A$ containing a linear subspace of codimension at most $C_\alpha\sqrt n$, uniformly over $n$ and $A$? The supplied source also discusses a separate tentative variant in which the sumset need contain only $99\%$ of a subspace. The source's open status and contextual bounds require human mathematical and bibliographic review.
