Math-7377

High Influence Small Sets in Boolean Functions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n$ be a positive integer, let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and let $f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}$ be a Boolean function. For $S\subseteq[n]$, write $S^{c}=[n]\setminus S$. Given an assignment $y\in\{0,1\}^{S^{c}}$, define the restricted function $f_y:\{0,1\}^{S}\to\{0,1\}$ by $f_y(z)=f(x)$, where $x|_{S^{c}}=y$ and $x|_S=z$. The value of $f$ remains undetermined after assigning $y$ precisely when $f_y$ is nonconstant, equivalently when there exist $z,z'\in\{0,1\}^{S}$ with $f_y(z)=0$ and $f_y(z')=1$.

The supplied record does not specify the distribution used for random assignments or expectations. Under the explicitly provisional interpretation that all coordinates are independent and uniformly distributed on $\{0,1\}$, put \[ m(f)=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f(x),\qquad I_f(S)=2^{-|S^{c}|}\left|\{y\in\{0,1\}^{S^{c}}:f_y\text{ is nonconstant}\}\right|. \] Thus $I_f(S)$ is the coalition influence defined in the supplied statement.

Question, with unresolved qualifications. If $m(f)$ is bounded away from both $0$ and $1$, must there exist $S\subseteq[n]$ with $|S|\le n/3$ and \[ I_f(S)\ge 1-c^n \] for some absolute constant $c$ satisfying $0<c<1$? More explicitly, the requested implication has the form \[ \varepsilon\le m(f)\le1-\varepsilon \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists S\subseteq[n]\;\left(|S|\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor\ \text{ and }\ I_f(S)\ge1-c^n\right), \] where $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$ represents a fixed positive balance margin. The record does not specify that margin, whether $c$ must be independent of it, or whether the assertion is intended for every positive integer $n$ or only for sufficiently large $n$. Consequently, the displayed implication is a formulation template, not a fully specified quantified conjecture. The probability law, the quantifier order for $\varepsilon,c,n,f$, and any dimension threshold require clarification from the source.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a Boolean function whose mean stays away from $0$ and $1$ has a coalition of at most one third of its variables that can still change the output after all other variables are randomly fixed, with probability at least $1-c^n$. The probability law, uniformity of the constant, and intended range of $n$ remain unspecified in the supplied record.
