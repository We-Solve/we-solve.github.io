Semilattice Realizations by Wedge Semibraces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A semilattice is a set $S$ with a binary operation $\wedge\colon S\times S\to S$ satisfying, for all $x,y,z\in S$, \[ x\wedge y=y\wedge x,\qquad (x\wedge y)\wedge z=x\wedge(y\wedge z),\qquad x\wedge x=x. \] Its associated order is $x\leq y$ if and only if $x\wedge y=x$. An isomorphism of semilattices is a bijection preserving $\wedge$. The supplied record does not specify whether empty semilattices are permitted or whether bounds or other additional conditions are required.

Realization problem. Characterize, up to semilattice isomorphism, the semilattices $(S,\wedge)$ for which there exists a $\wedge$-semibrace whose underlying semilattice is isomorphic to $(S,\wedge)$. Characterize also those for which such a realizing $\wedge$-semibrace can be chosen to be an $L$-algebra. Thus the requested characterization must give necessary and sufficient conditions for each of these two existence assertions.

Unresolved definitions and source qualification. The supplied record gives neither the axioms of a $\wedge$-semibrace nor those of an $L$-algebra, and does not specify precisely which operations or distinguished elements constitute their common structure. Consequently, the assertions above retain these source terms and are conditional on recovering their intended definitions; this draft does not supply a complete axiomatic formulation. The extracted text also remarks that there is no obvious notion of a trivial $\wedge$-semibrace.

The field labelled original_statement does not contain the displayed realization question. Instead, it interleaves a passage labelled Problem 3 about self-similar closures with a corrupted definition of a right brick and questions labelled Problems 5 and 6 about underlying lattices of bricks. The closure passage attributes to Rump an embedding of each $L$-algebra $X$ into a self-similar $L$-algebra $S(X)$, generated as a monoid by $X$, unique up to isomorphism, and possessing a group of left fractions \[ G(X)=\{a^{-1}b:a,b\in S(X)\}, \] called the structure group of $X$. These are supplied contextual claims, not additional hypotheses of the realization problem.

The brick passage describes two $L$-algebra structures $(X,\cdot,e)$ and $(X,\circ,e)$, a further distinguished element $0$, and additional identities whose extraction is corrupted. It asserts that the two canonical orders coincide and form a lattice. Its separate lattice questions concern $L(K,n)$, the lattice of $K$-linear subspaces of the left vector space $K^n$, where $K$ is a possibly noncommutative field and $n\geq1$, and the subspace lattice of a non-desarguesian plane. The extraction calls a lattice isomorphism to such an $L(K,n)$ desarguesian and ends with an assertion that only positive answers are known, followed by an omitted list of cases. None of these separate questions or incomplete claims is substituted for the semilattice realization target.

Short English statement

Determine exactly which semilattices admit a realizing $\wedge$-semibrace, and which admit one that is also an $L$-algebra. The required algebraic axioms are absent, and the extracted original passage concerns different questions, so a fully precise formulation requires source reconciliation and human mathematical review.
