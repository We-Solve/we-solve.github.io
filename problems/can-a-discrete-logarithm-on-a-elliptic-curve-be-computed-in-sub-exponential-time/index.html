Math-431

Can a discrete logarithm on a elliptic curve be computed in sub-exponential time

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $E/\mathbb F_q$ be an elliptic curve, let $P\in E(\mathbb F_q)$ have order $r$, and let $Q\in\langle P\rangle$. The elliptic-curve discrete-logarithm problem asks for $a\in\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$ such that \[ [a]P=Q. \] The source asks whether it can be solved in subexponential time. On the usual classical, general-curve reading, seek an algorithm applicable uniformly to the intended unrestricted input family, with running time subexponential in the bit-length parameter, rather than only for a special curve family.

For example, a broad meaning with parameter $L$ is $\exp(o(L))$, where $L$ includes the field, curve and point encoding lengths. A commonly used narrower subexponential scale is \[ L_q(\alpha,c)=\exp\left((c+o(1))(\log q)^\alpha(\log\log q)^{1-\alpha}\right), \qquad0<\alpha<1. \] The excerpt does not choose a scale, expected versus worst-case bound, success convention, field regime, or classical versus quantum model. Those must be fixed before a single complexity assertion is stated.

The summary reports no general classical subexponential method and mentions Pollard rho's square-root generic complexity. Its informal $O(\sqrt p)$ is naturally expressed as $O(\sqrt r)$ group operations for subgroup order $r$, not automatically the field characteristic. Generic-group bounds do not establish lower bounds for all algorithms using curve representations. The summary also reports faster special-family methods, naming anomalous, supersingular and specially structured extension-field curves; these do not constitute a uniform general-curve algorithm.

The pinned Wikipedia open label and the later hardness assessment are source reports, not a proof of computational impossibility or an independently verified current status.

Short English statement

Can the general elliptic-curve discrete logarithm be solved by a classical algorithm in time subexponential in input size? The source's intended computation model and time scale need confirmation; square-root generic methods and special-curve algorithms are distinct from the general target.
