Talagrand discrete convexity conjecture

Status OpenLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does a universal integer $k$ exist such that, for every finite ground set $X$, every $p\in(0,1)$, and every family $D\subseteq 2^X$ with $p$-biased measure at least $1-1/k$, the family \[ E_k(D)=\{S\subseteq X:S\text{ is not contained in any union of }k\text{ members of }D\} \] has a cover $G$ satisfying \[ \sum_{I\in G}p^{|I|}\leq\frac12, \] where a cover means that every $S\in E_k(D)$ contains some $I\in G$?

Short English statement

Is there a universal integer $k$ such that, for every finite $X$, every $p\in(0,1)$, and every $D\subseteq2^X$ of $p$-biased measure at least $1-1/k$, the sets $S\subseteq X$ that are not contained in any union of $k$ members of $D$ have a cover $G$ with $\sum_{I\in G}p^{|I|}\leq1/2$, meaning that each such $S$ contains a member of $G$?

Talagrand discrete convexity conjecture

OpenAI's claim

Talagrand discrete convexity: The paper claims a universal number of family members whose unions leave only a small containment-cover obstruction, provided the original Bernoulli measure is sufficiently close to one. The cover cost is bounded at the original density.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $k=2^{75}$ and $\mu_p$ be product Bernoulli measure on $2^{[N]}$. For every $N\ge1$, $0<p<1$, and arbitrary family $\mathcal D$, define $E_k(\mathcal D)=\{S:S\nsubseteq D_1\cup\cdots\cup D_k\text{ for every }D_1,\ldots,D_k\in\mathcal D\}$. Repetitions among the $D_i$ are allowed. The theorem claims \[\mu_p(\mathcal D)\ge1-1/k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists\mathcal G:\ E_k(\mathcal D)\subseteq\{S:\exists I\in\mathcal G,\ I\subseteq S\},\quad\sum_{I\in\mathcal G}p^{|I|}\le1/2.\] No increasingness or disjointness hypothesis is imposed on $\mathcal D$.

Outline of the argument

Talagrand discrete convexity: A biased orthogonal expansion produces nonnegative subset weights with a global budget. A signed many-row identity turns failure of a union cover into a large higher-power sum of those weights. Large weights give cheap generators directly, while weighted-degree selection and random unions cover the remaining classes; a final density transfer gives the universal union count.
