Math-4283

Number of Simple Reeb Orbits on Three-Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a closed smooth three-manifold, where closed means compact and without boundary, and let $\lambda$ be a smooth contact form on $M$, meaning that $\lambda\wedge{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda$ is nowhere zero. Its Reeb vector field $R_\lambda$ is uniquely characterized by \[ \lambda(R_\lambda)=1,\qquad {\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda(R_\lambda,\cdot)=0. \] A simple closed Reeb orbit is a periodic trajectory $\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to M$ satisfying $\dot\gamma=R_\lambda\circ\gamma$, where $T>0$ is its least positive period. Count such orbits by their geometric images, so that reparametrizations and multiple covers do not count as additional simple orbits. Define \[ \mathcal P(M,\lambda)=\{\gamma({\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}):\gamma\text{ is a simple closed Reeb orbit}\},\qquad N(M,\lambda)=\#\mathcal P(M,\lambda). \] Source question. Determine what restrictions hold on $N(M,\lambda)$, and which finite or infinite cardinalities can occur, as $(M,\lambda)$ ranges over closed three-manifolds equipped with contact forms. No nondegeneracy or first Chern class assumption is imposed in the supplied question.

The supplied literature assessment identifies the two-or-infinity alternative \[ N(M,\lambda)=2\quad\text{or}\quad \mathcal P(M,\lambda)\text{ is infinite} \] as a particular focus. The original question is broader and does not itself state this alternative as a conjecture. Moreover, it does not specify connectedness. The alternative must therefore not be adopted as a universal formulation without clarifying the intended connectedness convention: orbit counts add over disjoint components. Here "infinite" does not require countability.

For interpreting the supplied assessment, write $\xi=\ker\lambda$. The form ${\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda|_\xi$ orients this plane bundle and defines its first Chern class $c_1(\xi)\in H^2(M;{\mathbb{Z}})$ using any compatible complex structure. This class is torsion if $k c_1(\xi)=0$ for some positive integer $k$. A contact form is nondegenerate if, for every periodic Reeb orbit, including multiple covers, the linearized return map on $\xi$ has no eigenvalue equal to $1$.

The supplied dated curation reports that the two-or-infinity alternative has been proved for nondegenerate contact forms and for all contact forms with torsion first Chern class, while the unrestricted degenerate case is not covered by those theorems. This is a supplied literature assessment, not an independently verified theorem statement; the exact hypotheses and connectedness conventions of the cited results require review.

Short English statement

Count the geometrically distinct simple periodic trajectories of the Reeb vector field of a contact form on a closed three-manifold. The source asks broadly what numbers can occur. Its supplied curation highlights the possibility of exactly two or infinitely many, but the intended connectedness convention and the precise hypotheses of the cited results require clarification. The supplied status is partially solved.
