Existential Theory of Polynomial Rings with Divisibility

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q=p^r$, where $p$ is prime and $r\geq 1$, and let $R_q=\mathbb F_q[t]$ be the polynomial ring in one indeterminate over the field with $q$ elements. Addition has its usual meaning, and divisibility is the binary relation \[ a\mid b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\exists c\in R_q)\ b=ac. \] Thus $0\mid b$ holds exactly when $b=0$.

The source asks whether the existential theory of $R_q$, with addition, divisibility, and a unary predicate for powers of $t$, is decidable. To describe the decision problem precisely under the minimal signature reading, put $L=\{+,\mid,P\}$, with equality available as a logical symbol. Interpret $+$ and $\mid$ as above. Under the nonnegative-exponent convention, interpret \[ P(f)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f=t^n\text{ for some integer }n\geq 0. \] An existential $L$-sentence is a sentence \[ \exists x_1\cdots\exists x_m\,\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_m), \] where $m\geq 0$ and $\varphi$ is quantifier-free; all variables range over $R_q$. Its quantifier-free part may use arbitrary Boolean combinations of atomic formulas. The existential theory is the set of these sentences that hold in the specified structure.

Question, subject to the qualifications below. For a fixed prime power $q$, is there an algorithm that, on every input existential sentence in the intended language, terminates and correctly decides whether that sentence holds in the intended structure on $\mathbb F_q[t]$?

Unresolved source qualifications. The supplied record does not explicitly specify any constant symbols or named parameters. Consequently, the minimal signature above is a conditional interpretation; adding names for $0$, $1$, $t$, or field elements must not be assumed without source clarification. Likewise, the record does not explicitly say whether the powers predicate includes $t^0=1$; if only positive powers are intended, its interpretation is instead $\{t^n:n\geq 1\}$. The record also does not distinguish decidability separately for each fixed $q$ from a uniform algorithm taking a description of the finite field as additional input. No equivalence between these alternative formulations is asserted here.

The supplied dated assessment labels the problem partially solved and reports decidability of the one-existential-quantifier fragment while describing the full existential theory as open. These are supplied literature claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

The question asks for an algorithm deciding existential statements about polynomials over a finite field using addition, divisibility, and a predicate selecting powers of $t$. The exact signature, whether the predicate includes $1=t^0$, and whether the algorithm must work uniformly across finite fields remain unspecified. The supplied partial-solution assessment requires independent review.
