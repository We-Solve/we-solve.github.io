Growing-Parameter Limits for Convex-Body Functionals

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $j\in{\mathbb{N}}$, and let $(d_j)_{j\geq1}$ and $(k_j)_{j\geq1}$ be sequences of integers such that, for every $j$, \[ 0\leq k_j\leq d_j-1,\qquad 2k_j>d_j+1, \qquad\text{and}\qquad d_j\longrightarrow\infty\quad(j\to\infty). \] The source denotes by $Y^*_{d,k}$ a family of standardized random variables. The supplied record does not define the underlying random variables, their probability laws, or the standardization. Thus $Y^*_{d,k}$ must remain an unresolved source-dependent symbol; these omissions prevent a self-contained mathematical statement.

Problem, conditional on supplying that definition. Determine the asymptotic behavior of $Y^*_{d_j,k_j}$ along admissible sequences. Identify conditions on $(d_j,k_j)$ under which there exists a limiting probability distribution $\mu$ with \[ Y^*_{d_j,k_j}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{d}}\mu \qquad(j\to\infty), \] and identify the possible limiting distributions, including whether they are Gaussian. In particular, investigate the additional condition \[ \frac{k_j}{d_j}\longrightarrow\frac12. \] This condition is proposed as a regime to investigate, not asserted to imply convergence.

The supplied excerpt says that the characteristic function can be calculated explicitly, while the supplied background reports divergent cumulants. Neither the characteristic-function formula nor the relevant cumulant orders, parameter regimes, or divergence statements are provided, so these remarks do not supply the missing definition or additional precise hypotheses.

Short English statement

For the source's standardized random variables $Y^*_{d,k}$, study distributional limits as $d\to\infty$ with $0\leq k\leq d-1$ and $2k>d+1$, particularly when $k/d\to1/2$, and determine whether any limits are Gaussian. The family and its standardization are undefined in the supplied record, so this is a conditional, statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
