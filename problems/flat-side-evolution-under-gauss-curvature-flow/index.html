Math-7470

Flat-Side Evolution Under Gauss Curvature Flow

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Determine the final shape of the shrinking flat side in the Gauss-curvature flow of a weakly convex surface with flat sides.

In the usual interpretation for surfaces in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$, a smooth portion of an evolving surface $M_t$ has normal velocity \[ \partial_t X=-K\nu, \] where $\nu$ is the outward unit normal and $K=\kappa_1\kappa_2$ is Gauss curvature. Weak convexity means nonnegative principal curvatures where these are defined. A flat side is a two-dimensional planar region $F_t\subset M_t$ with nonempty relative interior. The equation on nonsmooth portions must be understood in the solution class intended by the report; that class is not specified here. The ambient space and compactness assumptions are also not explicitly supplied.

If the flat region collapses at a time $T$, its unrescaled limit need not encode its shape. A precise version of the question must specify a normalization and ask for a limit of normalized planar regions, for example regions of the form $a_t(F_t-b_t)$ after identifying the supporting plane with ${\mathbb{R}}^2$, with $b_t$ a prescribed center and $a_t>0$ a prescribed scale. The record does not prescribe these choices or the topology of convergence, so this notation describes the missing formulation rather than selecting it.

The dated literature assessment refers to a conjectured ellipse as the final shape. In an appropriate normalization, this would ask whether the limiting region is an ellipse (equivalently, whether its boundary is an ellipse), together with existence of the asserted limit. The supplied statement itself asks to determine the shape and does not specify the ellipse conjecture, the normalization, or whether the limit is unique. No particular area, diameter, affine, or other normalization is imposed in this draft, and no universal convergence theorem is asserted.

Short English statement

The question concerns the shape of a planar face as it disappears during Gauss-curvature flow. The supplied assessment mentions an elliptical limiting shape, but a precise conjecture still needs initial-data assumptions, a solution notion, a rescaling, and a meaning of convergence.
