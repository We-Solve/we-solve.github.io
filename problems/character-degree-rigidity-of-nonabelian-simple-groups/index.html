Math-879

Character-Degree Rigidity of Nonabelian Simple Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite group $K$, let $\operatorname{Irr}(K)$ denote the set of irreducible complex characters of $K$, and define its set of character degrees by \[ \operatorname{cd}(K)=\{\chi(1):\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)\}. \] Here $\chi(1)$ is the dimension of the corresponding irreducible complex representation; degrees are recorded without multiplicity. A nonabelian simple group is a nonabelian group whose only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group.

The finite-group formulation suggested by the supplied workshop context and literature assessment is the following conjecture, identified in the supplied assessment as Huppert's character-degree conjecture: for every pair of finite groups $G,H$, if $H$ is nonabelian simple and \[ \operatorname{cd}(G)=\operatorname{cd}(H), \] then there exists a finite abelian group $A$ such that \[ G\cong H\times A. \] The trivial group is allowed for $A$.

The displayed source statement does not explicitly require $G$ or $H$ to be finite and does not define $\operatorname{cd}$. Thus finiteness and the interpretation as a set of complex character degrees are contextual interpretations requiring confirmation against the cited report; no formulation for infinite groups is supplied.

Short English statement

The intended finite-group conjecture says that a finite group having exactly the same irreducible complex character degrees as a nonabelian finite simple group must be the direct product of that simple group with a finite abelian group. The finite domains and the meaning of character degrees require source confirmation.
