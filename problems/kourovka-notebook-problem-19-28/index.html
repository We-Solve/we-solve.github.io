Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.28

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group and let $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. Define $\varphi$-conjugacy by \[ x\sim_\varphi y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists z\in G\;\left(x=z^{-1}y\varphi(z)\right). \] This is an equivalence relation. Its number of equivalence classes, possibly infinite, is the Reidemeister number \[ R(\varphi)=\lvert G/{\sim_\varphi}\rvert. \] The group $G$ is finitely generated if $G=\langle S\rangle$ for some finite subset $S\subseteq G$. It is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$, there exist a finite group $Q$ and a homomorphism $q:G\to Q$ with $q(g)\ne1$.

For a group $H$, define $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(n+1)}=[H^{(n)},H^{(n)}]$, where $[K,K]$ is generated by the commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a,b\in K$. The group $H$ is soluble (solvable) if $H^{(m)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $m\ge0$. A group is virtually soluble, also called almost solvable in the supplied source, if it has a soluble subgroup of finite index.

Conjecture (Fel'shtyn--Troitsky; Kourovka Notebook 19.28). For every finitely generated residually finite group $G$ and every $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, \[ R(\varphi)<\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists H\le G\;\exists m\ge0\;\left([G:H]<\infty\ \text{and}\ H^{(m)}=\{1\}\right). \]

Supplied background qualifications. The source reports that the conjecture is proved when $\varphi$ has prime order, citing Jabara (2008). It also reports that, if the finite generation hypothesis is dropped while retaining residual finiteness and the existence of an automorphism with finite Reidemeister number, virtual solubility need not follow, citing Dekimpe and Gonçalves (2014). It further reports a positive result for groups of finite upper rank, citing Troitsky (2025). In the usual terminology, finite upper rank means that there is an integer $r\ge0$ such that every subgroup of every finite quotient of $G$ can be generated by at most $r$ elements. The supplied record does not give the full theorem statement for this last result, so its precise scope and any additional hypotheses require checking the cited paper. These background results and the supplied open status are source reports, not independent literature verification.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether every finitely generated residually finite group admitting an automorphism with finitely many twisted conjugacy classes has a soluble subgroup of finite index. Proposed by A. L. Fel'shtyn and E. V. Troitsky in 2018, it is labelled open in the supplied registry record; the accompanying special cases and qualifications are supplied literature reports.
