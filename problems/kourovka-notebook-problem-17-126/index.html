Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.126

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For elements of a group, write $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and let $[G,G]=\langle [x,y]:x,y\in G\rangle$ be the derived subgroup. A group $G$ is residually finite if, for each $g\ne 1$, there is a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ to a finite group with $\varphi(g)\ne 1$. A group is locally finite if every finitely generated subgroup is finite. The identity $[x,y]^n=1$ means that this equation holds for all $x,y\in G$. We use the usual interpretation that $n$ is a positive integer; the supplied statement does not explicitly specify its range.

Main question. For every positive integer $n$ and every residually finite group $G$ satisfying $[x,y]^n=1$ for all $x,y\in G$, must $[G,G]$ be locally finite?

A group is soluble if its derived series, defined by $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$, reaches the trivial subgroup. For a finite soluble group $G$, its Fitting height $h(G)$ is the least integer $h\geq 0$ for which there is a series \[ 1=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_h=G, \] with every $G_i$ normal in $G$ and every factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$ nilpotent. A group is nilpotent if its lower central series $\gamma_1(H)=H$, $\gamma_{i+1}(H)=[\gamma_i(H),H]$ reaches the trivial subgroup, where $[A,B]=\langle[a,b]:a\in A,\ b\in B\rangle$. In particular, $h(1)=0$.

Alternative question, described as equivalent in the supplied source. For every positive integer $n$, does there exist an integer $H_n\geq 0$ such that every finite soluble group $G$ satisfying $[x,y]^n=1$ for all $x,y\in G$ has $h(G)\leq H_n$? Thus the bound may depend on $n$, but not on $G$. The equivalence is a supplied source assertion; no justification is included in the record. The source also says "Cf. Archive, 13.34", but the referenced archive entry is not supplied.

Short English statement

If all commutators in a residually finite group have orders dividing a fixed positive integer $n$, must every finitely generated subgroup of its derived subgroup be finite? The supplied source describes this as equivalent to asking for a bound, depending only on $n$, on the Fitting heights of finite soluble groups satisfying the same identity.
