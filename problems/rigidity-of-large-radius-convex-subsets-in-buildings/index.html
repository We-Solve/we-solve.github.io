Math-4882

Rigidity of Large-Radius Convex Subsets in Buildings

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $B$ be a spherical building equipped with its standard angular metric $d_B$; its apartments are unit spherical Coxeter complexes. A nonempty subset $C\subseteq B$ is called $\pi$-convex if, for every $x,y\in C$ with $d_B(x,y)<\pi$, the unique geodesic segment from $x$ to $y$ lies in $C$. No condition on geodesics joining points at distance exactly $\pi$ is imposed by this definition.

Two possible circumradius conventions are \[ \operatorname{rad}_{C}(C)=\inf_{c\in C}\sup_{x\in C}d_B(c,x), \qquad \operatorname{rad}_{B}(C)=\inf_{b\in B}\sup_{x\in C}d_B(b,x). \] The first restricts centers to $C$; the second allows centers anywhere in $B$. Neither definition requires the infimum to be attained.

Source question, with unresolved conventions. Are convex subsets of spherical buildings with circumradius strictly greater than $\pi/2$ necessarily subbuildings? Here "rigid" means precisely the asserted subbuilding conclusion, rather than an additional rigidity property.

Under the explicit conventions that convexity means $\pi$-convexity and circumradius means $\operatorname{rad}_{C}$, the proposed universal assertion takes the form \[ \begin{gathered} \text{For every spherical building }B\text{ and every nonempty }C\subseteq B,\\ C\text{ is }\pi\text{-convex and }\operatorname{rad}_{C}(C)>\frac{\pi}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C\text{ is a subbuilding of }B. \end{gathered} \] This is a conditional transcription of the intended question, not a resolution of its missing conventions. If ambient centers are intended, replace $\operatorname{rad}_{C}$ by $\operatorname{rad}_{B}$. The strict inequality excludes circumradius exactly $\pi/2$.

A subbuilding is understood informally here as a subset carrying a spherical-building structure compatible with that of $B$. The supplied record does not specify the permitted rank, type, or precise compatibility requirement for this conclusion. It also does not specify closedness of $C$, thickness or rank restrictions on $B$, or whether its use of "convex" includes a condition at antipodal pairs. These qualifications require clarification before the displayed assertion can be treated as a fully determined formalization. The supplied target concerns arbitrary convex subsets; it does not restrict them to convex subcomplexes.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every convex subset of a spherical building whose circumradius is strictly greater than $\pi/2$ must itself be a subbuilding. The radius convention, precise convexity convention, and intended meaning of subbuilding need clarification. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and reports that the unrestricted convex-subset question remains open; these are supplied dated assessments requiring independent review.
