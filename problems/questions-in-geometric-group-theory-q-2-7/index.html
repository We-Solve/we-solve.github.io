Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.7

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, define the source's power-alternative property by \[ \forall a,b\in G\ \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\quad \left(\langle a^n,b^n\rangle\text{ is a free group}\right) \ \lor\ \left(\langle a^n,b^n\rangle\text{ is abelian}\right). \] The same exponent $n$ is used for both elements and may depend on $G,a,b$. The literal source asks for a free subgroup, without explicitly requiring the displayed two generators to be a free basis of rank two. The abelian alternative means that all elements of that subgroup commute.

Wise asks whether every group $G$ acting properly discontinuously and cocompactly on a CAT(0) space has this property. In the usual geometric interpretation the action is by isometries, the space is geodesic and satisfies the CAT(0) comparison inequality, proper discontinuity requires that only finitely many translates of a compact set meet that set, and cocompactness means the orbit space is compact. The source does not explicitly specify its properness convention or any properness assumption on the metric space itself.

The parenthetical second question replaces this geometric hypothesis by the hypothesis that $G$ is automatic: in the usual sense, a finite generating set admits a regular language of representative words whose equality and multiplication-by-generator relations are recognized by finite automata reading padded word pairs synchronously. The question again asks for the displayed property for every pair $a,b\in G$.

The supplied research summary reports a negative answer to the CAT(0) question: Leary--Minasyan groups denoted $G_{k,m}$, with the stated restrictions \[ -2m<k<2m,\qquad k\notin\{0,m,-m\}, \] act properly and cocompactly on $\mathbb E^2\times T$ for a tree $T$ and contain $a,b$ with \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\qquad \langle a^n,b^n\rangle\text{ is neither free nor abelian}. \] This is an attributed literature resolution: the record gives neither a presentation of these groups nor the construction of $a,b$ or the tree. It expressly says that this counterexample does not settle the automatic-group variant. Thus the overall solved label must not be read as a supplied solution of both questions.

Short English statement

Can taking a common positive power of any two elements always make their generated subgroup free or abelian, first in a CAT(0) group and separately in an automatic group? The supplied summary reports CAT(0) counterexamples of Leary and Minasyan. It explicitly leaves the automatic variant untouched by those counterexamples, so that variant is not resolved by the supplied solved label.
