Math-4081

Structural Characterization of Exceptional Borel Graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the usual simple undirected graph convention, a Borel graph is a pair $G=(X,E)$, where $X$ is a standard Borel space and $E\subseteq X\times X$ is Borel, symmetric, and irreflexive. A standard Borel space is a measurable space isomorphic to a Borel subset of a Polish space. These graph conventions are not explicitly specified in the supplied excerpt and require confirmation against its omitted context.

For a positive integer $k$, a Borel proper $k$-coloring of $G$ is a Borel map $c:X\to\{1,\ldots,k\}$, with the finite target given its discrete Borel structure, such that \[ \forall x,y\in X,\qquad (x,y)\in E\ \Longrightarrow\ c(x)\ne c(y). \] The notation $\chi_B(G)\le k$ means that such a coloring exists. Define \[ \mathcal C_k=\{G:G\text{ is a Borel graph and }\chi_B(G)\le k\}. \]

Requested characterization. The source asks for a reasonable structural characterization of Borel graphs for which certain preceding general results fail, and asks whether an analogous characterization exists for $\mathcal C_k$, for each fixed $k\in\mathbb N$.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied excerpt does not identify the preceding general results, the proposed generalization, or what their failure means. Consequently, the exceptional class in the first question cannot be defined precisely from this record. The finite-coloring class above has a precise meaning under the stated conventions, but the source does not specify what counts as a reasonable structural characterization or what makes it analogous to the first characterization. Thus the second question remains a request for necessary and sufficient structural criteria, with the permitted form of those criteria unresolved. The source also leaves the convention for $\mathbb N$ unspecified; the definition above covers positive $k$. If zero is intended, a coloring into the empty color set exists exactly when $X=\varnothing$.

Short English statement

The source asks which structural features explain the failure of unspecified general results for Borel graphs, and whether similar criteria characterize graphs admitting a Borel proper coloring with at most a fixed $k$ colors. The omitted antecedents prevent a precise formulation of the first target class; the intended kind of structural criterion also remains unspecified.
