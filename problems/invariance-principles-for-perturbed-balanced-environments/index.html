Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks two related questions about random walks in random environments: whether an invariance principle extends to a slightly perturbed balanced environment, and whether it extends to a balanced environment subjected to a percolation perturbation. The motivating objective is to connect the balanced regime discussed in the source report with other regimes, particularly the perturbative regime. The supplied excerpt does not specify the underlying model or either perturbation, so it does not determine a uniquely quantified mathematical conjecture.

To explain the terminology conditionally, suppose a discrete-time random walk takes values in a countable set $S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$, with environment-dependent transition probabilities $p_\omega(x,y)$. Whenever the relevant first moment is finite, a local zero-drift balance condition would be \[ \sum_{y\in S}(y-x)p_\omega(x,y)=0\qquad(x\in S). \] If the intended model is nearest-neighbour motion on ${\mathbb{Z}}^d$, the coordinatewise balance condition is \[ p_\omega(x,x+e_i)=p_\omega(x,x-e_i) \qquad(x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^d,\ 1\leq i\leq d), \] where $e_i$ denotes the $i$-th coordinate vector. These are conditional explanations; the excerpt does not identify the intended state space, transition rules, or definition of balance.

For a specified environment law $\mathbb P$, let $P_\omega^x$ denote the walk law in environment $\omega$, started at $x$. A typical diffusive invariance-principle target, if this scaling and centering are intended, is \[ \left(\frac{X_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-X_0-\lfloor nt\rfloor v}{\sqrt n}\right)_{t\geq0} \Longrightarrow (B_t)_{t\geq0} \quad\text{in }D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^d), \] with the Skorokhod $J_1$ topology, where $v\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$ is a deterministic velocity and $B$ is a Brownian motion started at zero with covariance $\mathbb E[B_tB_t^{\mathsf T}]=t\Sigma$. A quenched formulation requires convergence under $P_\omega^x$ for $\mathbb P$-almost every environment; an annealed formulation requires convergence under the averaged law $\int P_\omega^x\,\mathbb P({\,\mathrm{d}}\omega)$. The source excerpt does not specify either formulation, the starting-point quantifier, the centering, the topology, or whether $\Sigma$ must be deterministic and positive definite. Thus this display is an explanatory template, not an assertion of the exact intended theorem.
