Math-7235

Real Rational Maps with Exclusively Real Periodic Points

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an integer $d\geq 2$. Let $\operatorname{Rat}_d({\mathbb{R}})$ denote the space of degree-$d$ morphisms $\phi:{\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{R}}}$. Concretely, its elements are pairs $[F:G]$ of homogeneous real polynomials of degree $d$ with no common zero in ${\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{C}})$, modulo multiplication of both polynomials by the same nonzero real scalar. Equip this open subset of ${\mathbb{P}}^{2d+1}({\mathbb{R}})$ with its usual real topology; its real manifold dimension is $2d+1$. The parameter space here consists of maps themselves, without quotienting by conjugacy.

For $\phi\in\operatorname{Rat}_d({\mathbb{R}})$, write $\phi_{{\mathbb{C}}}$ for its extension to ${\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{C}})$. A point $z\in{\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{C}})$ is periodic if $\phi_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\circ n}(z)=z$ for some integer $n\geq1$. Define \[ \mathcal R_d=\left\{\phi\in\operatorname{Rat}_d({\mathbb{R}}):\ \text{for every }n\geq1\text{ and every }z\in{\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{C}}),\quad \phi_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{\circ n}(z)=z\ \Longrightarrow\ z\in{\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{R}})\right\}. \] Thus periodic points of every period are included, and the point at infinity is included in both projective lines.

Remaining question. Is it true that, for every integer $d\geq2$, \[ \operatorname{int}_{\operatorname{Rat}_d({\mathbb{R}})}(\mathcal R_d)\neq\varnothing? \] Equivalently, for each such $d$, does there exist a nonempty open subset $U_d\subseteq\operatorname{Rat}_d({\mathbb{R}})$ such that every periodic point of every map in $U_d$ is real?

The original formulation also asks whether $\mathcal R_d$ has full dimension, and presents this as equivalent to nonempty interior. The supplied record does not specify the notion of dimension or justify this equivalence. Consequently, the interior question above is precise, while the claimed equivalence with the dimension formulation remains a source qualification requiring review.

The original question additionally asked whether $\mathcal R_d\neq\varnothing$ for each $d\geq2$. The supplied curation reports an affirmative answer via Chebyshev maps and labels the overall problem partially solved. It reports that the interior/full-dimension question remains open and that only lower-dimensional families are known. These are supplied literature assessments, not independent conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

For every degree $d\geq2$, ask whether there is a nonempty open family of real rational maps for which every complex periodic point lies on the real projective line. The supplied record attributes existence of individual examples to Chebyshev maps. Its asserted equivalence between full dimension and nonempty interior needs clarification.
