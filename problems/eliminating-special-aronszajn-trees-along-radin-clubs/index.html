Eliminating Special Aronszajn Trees Along Radin Clubs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\lambda$ be an infinite regular cardinal. A $\lambda$-tree is a partially ordered set $(T,<_{T})$ such that the predecessors of each node are well ordered, the tree has height $\lambda$, and each level $T_\alpha$, for $\alpha<\lambda$, has cardinality less than $\lambda$. A cofinal branch is a linearly ordered subset whose node heights are unbounded in $\lambda$. A $\lambda$-Aronszajn tree is a $\lambda$-tree with no cofinal branch. The tree property at $\lambda$, denoted here by $\operatorname{TP}(\lambda)$, is the assertion that no $\lambda$-Aronszajn tree exists.

For an infinite cardinal $\mu$, a $\mu^{+}$-Aronszajn tree $T$ is special if there is a function $f:T\to\mu$ such that \[ s<_{T}t\implies f(s)\ne f(t). \] Equivalently, $T$ is the union of at most $\mu$ antichains. Write $\operatorname{NSAT}(\mu^{+})$ for the assertion that no special $\mu^{+}$-Aronszajn tree exists. Thus $\operatorname{TP}(\mu^{+})$ implies $\operatorname{NSAT}(\mu^{+})$, whereas the two assertions express different targets.

For a limit ordinal $\kappa$, a subset $C\subseteq\kappa$ is closed unbounded if it is unbounded in $\kappa$ and, for every limit ordinal $\delta<\kappa$, \[ \sup(C\cap\delta)=\delta\implies\delta\in C. \]

The supplied record presents two formulations that must be retained separately. Its general question asks whether a cited consistency result excluding special Aronszajn trees at a prescribed collection of regular cardinals can be strengthened to give the tree property at every cardinal in that collection. The collection, the original consistency theorem, and its hypotheses are not supplied, so this does not specify a complete relative consistency statement.

Its explicit Radin-forcing question asks whether there is a Radin forcing $\mathbb P$ adding a club $C\subseteq\kappa$ such that every cardinal $\mu\in C$ has no special $\mu^{+}$-Aronszajn tree. Under the conventional interpretation that the cardinals under discussion are infinite, the requested extension satisfies \[ V[G]\models \text{$C\subseteq\kappa$ is closed unbounded} \quad\text{and}\quad \forall\mu\in C\, \left(\mu\text{ is an infinite cardinal} \implies\operatorname{NSAT}(\mu^{+})\right), \] where $G$ is $\mathbb P$-generic and cardinalhood and successors are computed in $V[G]$. The source says "every cardinal" without explicitly imposing infinitude; its treatment of finite cardinals is unspecified.

A corresponding club-wide formulation of the stronger target would replace $\operatorname{NSAT}(\mu^{+})$ by $\operatorname{TP}(\mu^{+})$. The explicit source question itself asks only for the absence of special trees. The record does not determine whether these are successive questions or competing descriptions of one intended target. It also does not specify the hypotheses on $\kappa$, the permitted Radin forcing, or the consistency strength. These omissions remain unresolved.

Short English statement

The record asks whether a consistency result excluding special Aronszajn trees can be strengthened to exclude all Aronszajn trees. Its concrete Radin-forcing formulation instead asks for a club $C\subseteq\kappa$ with no special $\mu^{+}$-Aronszajn tree at each cardinal $\mu\in C$. These targets differ, and the underlying consistency assumptions are missing. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
