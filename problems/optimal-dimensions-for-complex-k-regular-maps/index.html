Math-4422

Optimal Dimensions for Complex k-Regular Maps

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For positive integers $m,k,N$, equip ${\mathbb{C}}^m$ and ${\mathbb{C}}^N$ with their usual Euclidean topologies. Under the standard complex interpretation, a map $f:{\mathbb{C}}^m\to{\mathbb{C}}^N$ is $k$-regular if, for every ordered collection of pairwise distinct points $x_1,\ldots,x_k\in{\mathbb{C}}^m$, its images are linearly independent over ${\mathbb{C}}$; explicitly, \[ \sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i f(x_i)=0,\qquad a_1,\ldots,a_k\in{\mathbb{C}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_1=\cdots=a_k=0. \] Continuity is required of $f$; no holomorphic or polynomial condition is imposed in the supplied statement.

The proposed assertion is that, for every admissible triple $(m,k,N)$, \[ \left(\exists\text{ a continuous $k$-regular map }f:{\mathbb{C}}^m\to{\mathbb{C}}^N\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N\geq m(k-1)+1. \] Here the positive-integer parameter range and complex linear independence provide an explicit conventional interpretation: the supplied record does not itself define $k$-regularity or specify the parameter range. In particular, whether zero-dimensional spaces or restrictions on $k$ are intended requires source confirmation; no extension to those unspecified cases is asserted here. According to the supplied verification note, the report presents this assertion as Conjecture 5. It is recorded here as a conjectural equivalence, not as a theorem.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a continuous map from ${\mathbb{C}}^m$ to ${\mathbb{C}}^N$ can send every $k$ distinct points to complex-linearly independent vectors exactly when $N\geq m(k-1)+1$. The definition and parameter conventions require confirmation from the source. The supplied record labels the problem open.
