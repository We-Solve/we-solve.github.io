Artin’s Primitive-Root Density for Two

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 029; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that each integer other than $-1$ or a square is primitive modulo at least $c_a x/(\log x)^2$ primes between $x$ and $2x$ for large $x$; In manuscript 2 (family 029; 2026-10-04), the asserted result is that a fixed finite list of positive prime bases simultaneously has full multiplicative order for at least $cx/(\log x)^2$ primes in a dyadic interval, conditional on four specified companion inputs.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
An integer $a$ is admissible when $a\ne-1$ and it is not an integer square. For each such $a$, the claim supplies $c_a>0$, $x_a\ge2$ such that every real $x\ge x_a$ satisfies \[ |\{p\text{ prime}:x<p<2x,\ p\nmid a,\ \operatorname{ord}_p(a)=p-1\}| \ge c_a x/(\log x)^2. \] Here $\operatorname{ord}_p(a)$ is multiplicative order. The estimate yields infinitely many primitive-root primes for each admissible base, without asserting Artin's predicted positive-density asymptotic.
Manuscript 2.
For the conditional inputs, let $L=\log x$, $W=\exp(L^{0.24})$, $P^-(h)$ be the least prime factor with $P^-(1)=\infty$, and $V(y)=\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p)$. Fix $K\ge1$ and $0.1<a_1<\cdots<a_K<0.2$, and set \[ \mathcal P_i=\{p\text{ prime}:e^{L^{a_i}}\le p\le e^{2L^{a_i}}\},\quad V_i=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_i}1/p,\quad\omega_i(h)=|\{p\in\mathcal P_i:p\mid h\}|, \quad\mathcal W(h)=2^{K-\sum_i\omega_i(h)}\prod_i\frac{\omega_i(h)}{V_i}. \] For $w>\gamma>0$, the density in the inputs is the locally finite sum \[ D_{\gamma}(w)=\frac1w+ \sum_{j\ge2}\frac1{j!} \int_{\substack{t_i\ge\gamma\ (1\le i<j)\\\sum_{i<j}t_i\le w-\gamma}} \frac1{w-\sum_{i<j}t_i}\prod_{i<j}\frac{dt_i}{t_i}. \]

This simultaneous-prime-base theorem is conditional on four separate inputs: zero-freeness of every finite-order Hecke $L_F(s,\chi)$ over every cyclotomic $F\supseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_{12})$ in $\operatorname{Re}s>1-10^{-6}$ (with principal poles allowed); the marked Type-II coefficient-cancellation implication and rough-coefficient cancellation; a uniform block-sieve estimate; and the rough-number density estimate. For every fixed $\delta,C,D_*>0$, the Type-II premise uses coefficients $\alpha_m,\beta_n$, bounded by $L^C$, supported on $W$-rough integers in $[H_m,2H_m]$ and $[H_n,2H_n]$, with $\alpha$ further restricted to any interval $I$. For arbitrary fixed $A_0,B,A>0$, this premise imposes a uniform estimate on all Dirichlet characters $\chi$ with modulus $k\le L^{A_0}$, using frequencies in the range $|t|\le2XL^B$: \[ \left|H_m^{-1}\sum_m\alpha_m\chi(m)m^{it}\right|\ll_A L^{-A}. \] Characters are zero on nonunits. Its conclusion, for every fixed $D_*>0$, is \[ |\sum_{m,n}\alpha_m\beta_nF(mn-1)\mathcal W(mn-1)|\ll X(\log x)^{-D_*}, \] with $H_m,H_n\ge x^{\delta}$, $H_mH_n\asymp x$, $|F|\le1$ and $F(ph)=F(h)$ at group primes. The bound holds for sufficiently large fixed $K$, with the threshold and constants allowed to depend on fixed parameters and the $a_i$. The same scalar cancellation is also assumed for \[ \alpha_m=m^{iv}\left(\mathbf1_{P^-(m)>x^{\gamma}} -\frac{D_{\gamma}(\log m/L)}{LV(W)}\mathbf1_{P^-(m)>W}\right)\mathbf1_{m\in I}, \] for $|v|\le L^C$ and fixed $0<\gamma<w_-<w_+<1$, whenever the logarithmic dyad $[\log H_m/L,\log(2H_m)/L]$ lies in $[w_-,w_+]$.

For a finite ground set with nonnegative weights $\lambda_\nu$, designate primes in a set $\mathcal D\subseteq\{p:p\le z\}$ and corresponding bad subsets $\mathcal A_p$. For a designated squarefree product $\ell$, let $\mathcal A_\ell=\bigcap_{p\mid\ell}\mathcal A_p$, using the whole ground set at $\ell=1$. Require $\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal A_\ell}\lambda_\nu=Yg(\ell)+R(\ell)$, $Y\ge0$, with $g(1)=1$ and $g(p)=0$ outside $\mathcal D$. For these weighted bad-event intersections, the block input assumes $g$ squarefree-multiplicative, $0\le g(p)\le1-\eta_0$ and $\sum_{u<p\le u^2}g(p)\le C_0$. Writing $S=\sum_{\nu\notin\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal D}\mathcal A_p}\lambda_\nu$, the input asserts \[ S=Y\prod_{p\le z}(1-g(p))(1+O(e^{-H})) +O(\sum_{\ell\le z^{4H+2}}|R(\ell)|) \] for large even $H$, summing designated squarefree products including one. The implicit constants and the lower threshold on $H$ depend only on $\eta_0,C_0$, and remain uniform as $H$ increases. The density input requires, for all sufficiently small fixed $b>0$, \[ \int_b^{1/2}D_t(1-t)\,dt/t=D_b(1)-1, \qquad D_b(1)\le e^{-\gamma_E}b^{-1}(1+C_de^{-c_d/b}), \] where $D_b$ is defined above, $\gamma_E$ is Euler's constant, and $c_d,C_d$ and the allowed upper bound on $b$ are absolute. The block input also assumes the upper bound $S\ll Y\prod_{p\le z}(1-g(p))+\sum_{\ell\le z^{10}}|R(\ell)|$ for $H=2$, with the same designated squarefree-product convention. Subject to all four inputs, for any distinct positive prime bases $q_1,\ldots,q_k$, $k\ge1$, the claimed constants $c_{\mathbf q}>0,x_{\mathbf q}$ give \[ |\{p\in(x,2x):p\text{ prime},\ p\nmid\prod_iq_i,\ \operatorname{ord}_p(q_i)=p-1\ (1\le i\le k)\}| \ge c_{\mathbf q}x/(\log x)^2\quad(x\ge x_{\mathbf q}). \] The companion input statements are essential premises, rather than proofs supplied by this manuscript.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 029; 2026-10-04), cubic theta coefficients under Kummer twists give a Hecke zero-free comparison. Controlled prime predecessors in fixed progressions are then sieved against complete-splitting obstructions, retaining enough primes on which the base has full multiplicative order.

For manuscript 2 (family 029; 2026-10-04), uniform splitting estimates remove bad order divisors from a marked family in a progression. Rough-number counts and separation of the least prime factor eliminate balanced composites, leaving the simultaneous prime conclusion under those inputs.
