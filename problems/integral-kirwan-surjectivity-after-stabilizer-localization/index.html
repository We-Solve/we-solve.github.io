Math-1488

Integral Kirwan surjectivity after stabilizer localization

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 10.1, attributed to R. Goldin, S. Tolman, and J. Weitsman, asks for the conditions under which Kirwan surjectivity for symplectic reduction holds over ${\mathbb{Z}}$, and for the most general such conditions. It is a classification request; the original excerpt does not fix compactness, group connectedness, level regularity, freeness, or whether a nonfree target is Borel, orbifold, or coarse-space cohomology.

To state the supplied progress in a standard regular-level setting, let $(M,\omega)$ be a compact Hamiltonian manifold for a compact connected Lie group $G$, with equivariant moment map $\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*$. Assume $0$ is a regular value, set $Z=\mu^{-1}(0)$, and let $i:Z\hookrightarrow M$. The Borel Kirwan restriction with coefficients in a commutative ring $R$ is \[ \kappa_R^{\mathrm B}=i^*:H_G^*(M;R)\longrightarrow H_G^*(Z;R),\qquad H_G^*(Y;R)=H^*(EG\times_GY;R). \] If $G$ acts freely on $Z$, the usual comparison identifies the target with $H^*(Z/G;R)$. The aid attributes to Pomerleano--Teleman additive split surjectivity over ${\mathbb{Z}}$ in the free regular-level case. A splitting is additive, not an asserted multiplicative ring section.

If the level action is only locally free, let \[ \ell=\operatorname{lcm}\{|G_z|:z\in Z\} \] for its finite stabilizers. In the compact setting this uses finitely many stabilizer orders. The aid reports a stable complex-cobordism $MU[1/\ell]$-splitting and the resulting coefficient-localization statement: for every commutative ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/\ell]$-algebra $R$, $\kappa_R^{\mathrm B}$ is additively split surjective. With these coefficients, the finite-isotropy comparison \[ q^*:H^*(Z/G;R)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H_G^*(Z;R) \] identifies Borel and coarse targets, so $(q^*)^{-1}\kappa_R^{\mathrm B}$ is also split surjective. This is not an assertion of integral coarse-space surjectivity when stabilizers remain nontrivial.

For the integral Borel cokernel \[ C=\operatorname{coker}\left(\kappa_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm B}\right), \] the supplied localization audit records \[ C\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}[1/\ell]=0, \qquad \forall c\in C\ \exists N(c)\ge0:\ \ell^{N(c)}c=0. \] Thus $C\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}=0$ for every prime $p\nmid\ell$. Localization alone gives neither $\ell C=0$ nor one exponent $N$ valid for every element. In the nonfree integral setting the map $q^*$ need not be an isomorphism, so restriction by itself does not canonically define a map to integral coarse-space cohomology. The source reports these results as broad progress on the free/finite-stabilizer cases, not a classification of every possible symplectic reduction.

Short English statement

The problem asks when integral Kirwan surjectivity holds. The supplied aid reports a split-surjectivity theorem for free regular levels and a version for finite stabilizers after their orders are inverted. The remaining integral Borel cokernel is elementwise stabilizer-power torsion; this does not supply an integral coarse-space map or a uniform torsion bound.
