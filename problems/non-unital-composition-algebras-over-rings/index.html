Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a classification question, rather than a conjecture with a specified conclusion. Its original target is: describe the behavior over rings of non-unital composition algebras, including octonion isotopes. The extracted formulation additionally asks for a classification.

To explain the terminology conditionally, suppose that the base ring $R$ is commutative with identity. An $R$-algebra without an assumed identity consists of an $R$-module $A$ and an $R$-bilinear multiplication $A\times A\to A$, written $(x,y)\mapsto xy$; associativity is not assumed. A quadratic form on $A$ is a map $q\colon A\to R$ satisfying $q(rx)=r^2q(x)$ for all $r\in R$, $x\in A$, such that \[ b_q(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y) \] is $R$-bilinear. The composition identity is \[ q(xy)=q(x)q(y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in A. \] These identities explain a possible framework; they do not specify the intended class of composition algebras. The source does not state the required finiteness, projectivity, rank, or nondegeneracy conditions, nor does it explicitly restrict the base rings to commutative rings with identity. Thus these conditional definitions must not be read as additional hypotheses supplied by the problem.

In a usual algebraic sense, an isotopy between two $R$-algebras $A$ and $B$ is a triple of $R$-linear isomorphisms $f,g,h\colon A\to B$ satisfying \[ h(xy)=f(x)\mathbin{\cdot_B}g(y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in A. \] An octonion isotope is an algebra isotopic to an octonion algebra. The record does not specify whether its intended isotopies must satisfy additional conditions involving quadratic forms, or which definition of an octonion algebra over a ring is intended.

Statement of the research target. Describe and classify non-unital composition algebras over the intended base rings, including those arising as octonion isotopes. A precise quantified classification assertion cannot be recovered from the supplied record: the admissible rings and algebraic data, the equivalence relation for classification, and the requested invariants or structural conclusions are unspecified. In particular, "non-unital" could mean that an identity is not part of the data or that no identity exists; these interpretations give different classes.
