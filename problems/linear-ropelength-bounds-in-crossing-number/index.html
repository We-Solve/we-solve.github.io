Math-3535

Linear Ropelength Bounds in Crossing Number

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a standard geometric interpretation, let a knot type mean an ambient isotopy class of tame embeddings of the circle in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$. Define its crossing number $\operatorname{Cr}(K)$ as the minimum number of crossings in a regular planar diagram representing $K$. For an embedded closed $C^{1,1}$ curve $\gamma$, let $L(\gamma)$ be its length and let its thickness $\tau(\gamma)$ be its reach: the supremum of radii $r>0$ for which every point at distance less than $r$ from $\gamma$ has a unique nearest point on $\gamma$. Set \[ \operatorname{Rop}(K)=\inf_{\gamma\text{ representing }K,\ \tau(\gamma)>0}\frac{L(\gamma)}{\tau(\gamma)}. \] The tame-knot domain, regularity, and thickness convention make the questions precise but are not explicitly specified in the supplied record. A knot is alternating if it admits a diagram in which overcrossings and undercrossings alternate while traversing the knot.

The main question, interpreting the asymptotic constant uniformly over knot types, is whether \[ \exists C>0\ \exists n_0\in{\mathbb{N}},\ n_0\ge1\ \forall K:\quad \operatorname{Cr}(K)\ge n_0\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{Rop}(K)\le C\operatorname{Cr}(K). \] The crossing threshold expresses an asymptotic assertion and avoids imposing a zero upper bound on the unknot, whose crossing number is zero.

If this bound fails, the supplied record asks whether infinitely many knots have faster growth. A precise candidate interpretation is the existence of pairwise distinct knot types $(K_j)_{j\ge1}$, with positive crossing numbers, such that \[ \operatorname{Cr}(K_j)\longrightarrow\infty, \qquad \frac{\operatorname{Rop}(K_j)}{\operatorname{Cr}(K_j)}\longrightarrow\infty. \] The source does not specify a family, a growth rate, or whether this precise sequential interpretation is intended. Its original wording calls this question "equivalent" to the upper-bound question; the faster-growth alternative concerns failure of the upper bound, rather than its affirmative assertion.

The alternating-knot question, interpreted as a uniform lower bound over all alternating knot types, is whether \[ \exists c>0\ \exists n_1\in{\mathbb{N}},\ n_1\ge1\ \forall K\text{ alternating}:\quad \operatorname{Cr}(K)\ge n_1\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{Rop}(K)\ge c\operatorname{Cr}(K). \] No restriction to prime alternating knots is supplied. Here "linear-order lower bound" is interpreted as $\Omega(\operatorname{Cr}(K))$, without asserting a matching upper bound. These formulations are statement-only interpretations requiring mathematical review.

Short English statement

Ask whether a universal constant bounds knot ropelength above by crossing number, whether failure yields a sequence with unbounded ropelength-to-crossing-number ratio, and whether alternating knots satisfy a uniform linear lower bound. The domain, geometric conventions, and meaning of faster growth require confirmation. The supplied open status is dated curation, not independently verified.
