Efficient Extraction of Interval Summands

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for efficient detection and extraction of interval direct summands of a pointwise finite-dimensional representation of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$. It does not specify the coefficient field, the intended interval convention, or an effective presentation of the input.

Under the usual persistence interpretation, fix a field $k$ and a positive integer $d$, and equip ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ with the coordinatewise order: $x\leq y$ means $x_i\leq y_i$ for every $1\leq i\leq d$. A representation is a functor $M:({\mathbb{R}}^d,\leq)\to\operatorname{Vect}_k$, given by vector spaces $M_x$ and linear maps $M_{x,y}:M_x\to M_y$ for $x\leq y$, satisfying \[ M_{x,x}=\operatorname{id}_{M_x},\qquad M_{y,z}\circ M_{x,y}=M_{x,z}\quad(x\leq y\leq z). \] It is pointwise finite-dimensional if $\dim_k M_x<\infty$ for every $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$.

One standard convention calls a nonempty subset $I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ an interval if it is order-convex and connected by finite chains of comparable points. Order-convexity means that $x,z\in I$ and $x\leq y\leq z$ imply $y\in I$; the stated connectedness means that any two points of $I$ can be joined by a finite sequence in $I$ whose consecutive points are comparable. Under this convention the interval module $k_I$ is defined by \[ (k_I)_x=\begin{cases}k,&x\in I,\\0,&x\notin I,\end{cases} \qquad (k_I)_{x,y}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{id}_k,&x,y\in I,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad(x\leq y). \] This convention, including the connectedness requirement, is an explicit provisional interpretation; the supplied source record does not define intervals or restrict them to products of one-dimensional intervals. No openness, closedness, boundedness, or endpoint convention is supplied.

With these conventions, the direct-summand detection question is whether \[ \exists\, I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\ \exists\, N:({\mathbb{R}}^d,\leq)\to\operatorname{Vect}_k \quad\text{such that}\quad M\cong k_I\oplus N, \] where $I$ is an interval and the isomorphism is an isomorphism of representations. The requested task is to develop efficient procedures that decide this question and, when interval summands exist, extract them, preferably using local, readily checkable conditions without first computing a complete decomposition of $M$. Extraction may be witnessed by natural transformations $\iota:k_I\to M$ and $\pi:M\to k_I$ satisfying $\pi\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_{k_I}$.

The source leaves unresolved whether extraction should return one interval summand or all interval summands with multiplicities. It also leaves the dimension range, coefficient field, input encoding, meaning of locality, and complexity criterion unspecified. Pointwise finite dimensionality alone supplies neither a finite input description nor a complexity measure, so the algorithmic request is not yet a fully specified decision problem. Detecting an interval direct summand must also be distinguished from deciding whether the whole module is a direct sum of interval modules; the supplied literature discussion concerns the latter as well.

Short English statement

Given a pointwise finite-dimensional multiparameter persistence representation, detect and extract interval direct summands efficiently, preferably through local tests without a complete decomposition. The coefficient field, interval convention, input presentation, complexity standard, and required extent of extraction remain unspecified. The displayed formulation uses explicitly provisional persistence conventions and requires human mathematical review.
