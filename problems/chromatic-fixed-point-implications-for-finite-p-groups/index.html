Chromatic Fixed-Point Implications for Finite p-Groups

OpenAI's claim

For every subgroup of a finite p-group, the optimal chromatic loss under fixed points is claimed to equal its shortest subnormal-chain length with cyclic quotients, independently of height. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Fix a prime $p$, finite $p$-group $G$, subgroup $H$ and integer $n\ge0$. Let $r_n(G,H)$ be the least $r\ge0$ such that for every finite $p$-local genuine $G$-spectrum $X$, \[ K(n+r)_*(\Phi^HX)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K(n)_*(\Phi^GX)=0. \] Here $\Phi$ denotes geometric fixed points and $K(0)=H\mathbb Q$. Let $\ell(G,H)$ be the smallest number of steps among subnormal chains from $H$ to $G$ having a cyclic quotient at each step, and $\ell(G,G)=0$. OpenAI claims $r_n(G,H)=\ell(G,H)$ for all these parameters. Minimality includes finite witness spectra ruling out every smaller loss.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 314, 2026-09-24): Character evaluations construct a pro-p comparison group whose finite sections need few generators. Groupoid integration supplies subgroup-count congruences that force a sufficiently short closed series with Z_p factors. Finite chromatic fracture translates this arithmetic bound into fixed-point vanishing, and tensor-prime separation provides witnesses showing that no smaller loss can work.
