Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for a derived equivalence involving unipotent representations, but does not specify enough data to determine a mathematical proposition uniquely.

Under the usual interpretation, for a positive prime power $q$, $\operatorname{GU}_n(q)$ denotes the finite general unitary group of a nondegenerate Hermitian form on ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^2}^{\,n}$. The source does not specify the permitted values of $n$ and $q$. In ordinary characteristic-zero character theory, an irreducible character is called unipotent if it occurs in a Deligne--Lusztig virtual character induced from the trivial character of a rational maximal torus. This terminology alone does not specify the representation categories intended here: the coefficient field or ring, its characteristic, and the objects and morphisms of those categories are not supplied.

The expression $\operatorname{GL}_n(-q)$ has no ordinary finite-field interpretation for positive $q$, since a finite field cannot have negative cardinality. It may indicate a formal substitution in formulas, but the record does not define that substitution or an associated representation category. No replacement group or category can therefore be specified from the supplied evidence.

For clarity, if partitions of $n$ are intended as labels, a partition is a nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative integers with finite sum $n$. The dominance relation is \[ \lambda\unrhd\mu \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_i\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_i \quad\text{for every }r\geq1, \] where trailing parts are zero. The source does not specify the relevant labeling or how an equivalence must respect this order. It also does not define the relevant Lusztig $a$-function or identify the Deligne--Lusztig varieties and their geometric realizations. The original wording uses an "A-function," whereas the cleaned statement uses Lusztig's $a$-function; their intended relationship is unresolved.

The supplied question can consequently be retained only in the following schematic form. Once the missing categories $\mathcal C_U$ and $\mathcal C_L$, their derived-category conventions, and the compatibility conditions have been specified, does there exist a triangulated equivalence \[ \Phi:\mathcal D(\mathcal C_U)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal D(\mathcal C_L) \] that is compatible with the intended dominance order, the intended function, and the indicated Deligne--Lusztig geometric realizations on both sides? Here $\mathcal D$ is a placeholder for the unspecified derived-category construction, including its boundedness convention. No precise compatibility equations are supplied.

Short English statement

The record asks whether the stated unitary and linear unipotent representation categories admit a derived equivalence respecting dominance, a specified function, and Deligne--Lusztig geometry. The notation $\operatorname{GL}_n(-q)$, the categories, and the compatibility requirements are undefined. The supplied background also describes a different, incomplete fragment.
