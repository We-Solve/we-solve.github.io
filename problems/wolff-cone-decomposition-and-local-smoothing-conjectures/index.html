Wolff Cone-Decomposition and Local-Smoothing Conjectures

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For functions $f_k$ whose Fourier supports lie in $\delta$-plates of the truncated light cone in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$, does $\lVert\sum_k f_k\rVert_p\leq C_\varepsilon\delta^{-\alpha(p)-\varepsilon}(\sum_k\lVert f_k\rVert_p^p)^{1/p}$ hold for $p>2(d+1)/(d-1)$, where $\alpha(p)=d(1/2-1/p)-1/2$? Does the stronger mixed-norm version with $(\sum_k\lVert f_k\rVert_p^2)^{1/2}$ and exponent $\beta(p)=(d-1)/4-(d+1)/(2p)$ hold for $p>2+4/(d-1)$? Do the associated Sogge local-smoothing and cone-multiplier estimates hold in their conjectured ranges?

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

Wolff Cone-Decomposition and Local-Smoothing Conjectures

OpenAI's claim

Wave local smoothing: OpenAI claims the critical $L^3$ spacetime estimate for the wave equation in three dimensions with any positive Sobolev loss. This is the Euclidean three-dimensional smoothing assertion at that exponent.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $Uf(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\widehat f(\xi)\,d\xi$ and $J^\epsilon=(1-\Delta)^{\epsilon/2}$. The claimed three-dimensional critical local-smoothing estimate is \[\|Uf\|_{L^3(\mathbb R^3\times[1,2])}\le C_\epsilon\|J^\epsilon f\|_{L^3(\mathbb R^3)}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)).\] Every positive Sobolev loss is allowed, but zero loss is not asserted.

Outline of the argument

Wave local smoothing: Null coordinates put light rays into a quadratic velocity model, and packet synthesis reduces the physical estimate to a cubic mass sum. Entropy records retain changing shear frames as well as direction and time. Planar Furstenberg and cone-envelope inputs constrain those records, giving a frequency-annulus estimate that can be summed with arbitrarily small loss.
