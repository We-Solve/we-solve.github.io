Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 11. Given a (random) number n of 10000 digits, it may be impossible to find 5000 digit primes p, q such that n = pq. Much easier: find some 10000 digit primes

p, q such that n is the first 10000 digits of pq. Instead, find 7500 digit primes p, q such that

n is the first 10000 digits of pq. (Coppersmith) Therefore, do this for 6000 digit primes. (Bernstein)

Exact digit condition before choosing primes

Let $N$ have $D$ decimal digits and require two $A$-digit prime factors. For the source's $A=6000,7500,10000$ and $D=10000$, their product can have either $2A-1$ or $2A$ digits. Both possibilities must be kept: a statement excluding prefixes for only the longer product does not exclude the original leading-digit condition. For each possible product length $J\in\{2A-1,2A\}$ with $J\ge D$, put $W=10^{J-D}$ and $M=NW$. The exact branch condition is \[ M\le pq<M+W,\qquad 10^{A-1}\le p,q<10^A. \] This follows directly by dividing the product by $10^{J-D}$ and taking its integer part. Conversely the interval has $J$-digit endpoints in the correct decimal range because $N$ has exactly $D$ digits, so membership gives the desired prefix. The union of the two branches is the original requirement.
A fixed-factor residue test.
For any positive integer candidate $p$, the possible integers $q$ are \[ \left\lceil M/p\right\rceil\le q\le \left\lceil(M+W)/p\right\rceil-1, \] intersected with the $A$-digit range. If $W<p$, this interval contains at most one integer. Put $q_0=\lceil M/p\rceil$ and $\delta=pq_0-M\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. A candidate exists exactly when $\delta<W$ and $q_0$ has the required length. The residue $\delta$ is the least nonnegative residue of $-M$ modulo $p$. Thus even after choosing a prime $p$, the integer-existence step is a thin residue condition before the independent requirement that $q_0$ be prime. Assuming it holds merely because a real hyperbolic strip is nonempty is invalid. For $A=6000,D=10000$, the wider branch has $W=10^{2000}$ whereas $p\ge10^{5999}$; a row is much thinner than one integer. The shorter branch is ten times thinner still.
Exact perturbation equation.
Given integer starting values $p_0,q_0$, write $r=M-p_0q_0$. With corrections $x,y$, the prefix interval is exactly \[ r\le q_0x+p_0y+xy<r+W. \] Discarding $xy$ is not harmless without an error budget. If $|x|,|y|\le H$, its absolute value can be as large as $H^2$; for a method that uniformly ignores this term, $H^2\le W$ is one natural necessary scale requirement for an error allowance $W$. This requirement is about that ansatz, not about an algorithm retaining the exact product. There is a separate elementary coverage restriction on a linear residue search: if only $K$ candidate values of $x$ are used, then $q_0x+p_0y$ modulo $p_0$ has at most $K$ possible residues, regardless of $y$. Intervals of $W$ consecutive integer residues following those positions cover at most $KW$ residue classes. Covering every target residual class therefore requires $KW\ge p_0$. This counting fact does not prove the residues actually cover even when the inequality holds.

At the nominal balanced scale $U=10^A$, taking about $H$ choices for a correction and combining the putative residue-coverage scale $U/H\le W$ with the uniform linearization-error scale $H^2\le W$ yields $U^2\le W^3$. In the longer branch $W=10^{2A-D}$, this is $A\ge3D/4$. It explains the $7500$-digit threshold of this particular pair of scale requirements. For $A=6000$, the putative coverage scale $H=10^{D-A}=10^{4000}$ has $H^2/W=10^{6000}$. The discarded term exceeds the prefix-window scale by that factor. These equalities audit a suggested linearization; they do not prove impossibility for all methods or for nonuniform random-input strategies. Constants in the number of signed corrections also do not constitute a prime-distribution theorem.

Audit and remaining problem

The source Question 11 and its perturbation remark were inspected. The audit corrected a potential one-branch error: upper-product endpoint obstructions for $J=2A$ cannot be asserted for the whole problem because $J=2A-1$ is also allowed. It checks strict upper endpoints, factor digit ranges, residue versus real feasibility, the $xy$ term and primality. No large integers or primes were constructed, and no lattice reduction experiment was run. A proved construction or success probability for the source's random prefix, especially at $6000$ digits, remains missing.
