Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for a comparison between game-theoretic expectations and supermartingales and their measure-theoretic counterparts, but does not specify the underlying game or probability model.

For clarity, the standard measure-theoretic notions can be defined after choosing a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$. For an integrable real-valued random variable $X$, its expectation is \[ \mathbb E_P[X]=\int_\Omega X\,{\,\mathrm{d}} P. \] Given a linearly ordered time index set $T$ and a filtration $(\mathcal F_t)_{t\in T}$, a real-valued process $(M_t)_{t\in T}$ is a $P$-supermartingale if it is adapted, satisfies $\mathbb E_P[|M_t|]<\infty$ for every $t\in T$, and satisfies \[ \mathbb E_P[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]\leq M_s \quad P\text{-almost surely},\qquad s,t\in T,\ s\leq t. \] These definitions provide comparison terminology; the source does not prescribe this probability space, time index, or integrability class, and does not specify whether the intended measure-theoretic comparison uses one probability measure or a family of measures.
