Optimal Gaps Between Binary Quadratic-Form Values

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For this draft, interpret a primitive positive-definite binary quadratic form in the usual integral sense: \[ Q(x,y)=ax^2+bxy+cy^2,\qquad a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad \gcd(a,b,c)=1,\quad a>0,\quad b^2-4ac<0. \] Keep $Q$ fixed. Under the convention that representation allows every integer pair, define \[ \mathcal R_Q=\{Q(x,y):(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2\}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0} =\{s_1<s_2<s_3<\cdots\}. \] Thus distinct represented positive integers are listed once, irrespective of their numbers of representations. Set \[ g_n=s_{n+1}-s_n\qquad(n\geq1). \] To make the largest-gap question precise, adopt the explicit cutoff convention \[ G_Q(X)=\max\left(\{g_n:n\geq1,\ s_{n+1}\leq X\}\cup\{0\}\right),\qquad X\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}. \] Problem. For a fixed primitive positive-definite integral binary quadratic form $Q$, determine the optimal asymptotic order of $G_Q(X)$ as $X\to\infty$, specifying any dependence of bounds and constants on $Q$.

Unresolved qualifications. The integral coefficient domain, unrestricted integer representations, and cutoff convention above make explicit a standard interpretation of the reconstructed registry statement; the supplied original excerpt does not define these choices or the sequence $(s_n)$. In particular, primitive representations, meaning representations with $\gcd(x,y)=1$, are not required in this draft and would define a potentially different problem. The excerpt asks more broadly about both short and long gaps, whereas the reconstructed statement targets the largest gaps. No precise short-gap assertion, desired asymptotic formula, or requirement of uniformity over forms is supplied.

Short English statement

Fix a primitive positive-definite integral binary quadratic form and list its distinct positive values on integer pairs in increasing order. Determine how the largest difference between successive values up to $X$ grows as $X$ increases. The representation domain and cutoff are explicit draft conventions requiring confirmation; the original excerpt also asks about short gaps without specifying a precise target.
