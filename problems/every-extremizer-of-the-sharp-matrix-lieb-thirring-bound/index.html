Definitions and mathematical statement

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Fix $m\geq1$ and $1/2<\gamma<3/2$. Let $W:\mathbb R\to M_m(\mathbb C)$ be measurable, Hermitian, and nonnegative, with $\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{tr}(W^{\gamma+1/2})\,dx<\infty$. Define $H=-d^2/dx^2-W$ on $L^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb C^m)$, and put \[C_\gamma=\left(\frac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+1/2}\right)^{\gamma-1/2}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\gamma+3/2)}.\] Does equality in $\operatorname{Tr}(H_-^\gamma)\leq C_\gamma\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{tr}(W^{\gamma+1/2})\,dx$ hold exactly when a constant unitary change of basis makes $W(x)$ a diagonal collection of zero channels and channels \[(r+1)a_j^2\operatorname{sech}^2(r a_j(x-x_j)),\qquad r=(\gamma-1/2)^{-1},\quad a_j>0,\quad x_j\in\mathbb R?\] Each nonzero scalar channel is required to have exactly one negative eigenvalue, equal to $-a_j^2$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Fix a finite matrix size $m\ge1$, $1/2<\gamma<3/2$, and $p=\gamma+1/2$. For measurable Hermitian $W(x)\ge0$ with $\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{tr}(W^p)<\infty$, form $H_W=-d^2/dx^2-W$ by its $H^1$ quadratic form. The source claims \[ \sum_{\lambda_j(H_W)<0}|\lambda_j|^\gamma \le C_\gamma\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{tr}(W^p),\quad C_\gamma=\left(\frac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+1/2}\right)^{\gamma-1/2} \frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(\gamma+3/2)}. \] Writing $r=(\gamma-1/2)^{-1}$, equality occurs exactly for a constant unitary $U$, $0\le k\le m$, $a_j>0$, $x_j\in\mathbb R$, and \[ W(x)=U\operatorname{diag}(w_1(x),\ldots,w_k(x),0,\ldots,0)U^*,\quad w_j(x)=(r+1)a_j^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ra_j(x-x_j)) \] almost everywhere. Each nonzero channel has just the negative eigenvalue $-a_j^2$, and $k=0$ means the zero potential.
