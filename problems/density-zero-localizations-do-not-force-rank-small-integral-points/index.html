Math-5348

Density-zero localizations do not force rank-small integral points

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an integral affine Weierstrass model $U=E\setminus\{O\}$ of an elliptic curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. For a set $S$ of rational primes, let $R_S={\mathbb{Z}}[S^{-1}]$ and \[ U(R_S)=\{P\in U({\mathbb{Q}}):x(P),y(P)\in R_S\},\qquad H_S=\langle U(R_S)\rangle\le E({\mathbb{Q}}). \] The source's notation $E(R_S)$ is interpreted as affine coordinate integrality: the Siegel--Mahler finiteness assertion is not about all points on a proper projective model. Write $D(P)$ for the finite set of primes occurring in the denominators of $x(P)$ or $y(P)$, so $P\in U(R_S)$ iff $D(P)\subseteq S$.

Question 21, Shlapentokh. For a rank-two elliptic curve, can an infinite prime set $S$ be chosen with $\operatorname{rank}H_S=1$? If $S$ is infinite of density zero, must its affine integral points remain "small"? The source cites Siegel--Mahler finiteness for finite $S$, but finite cardinality of the point set is distinct from rank of the subgroup it generates. "Small" is not given a precise cardinality or height-growth meaning.

Supplied density-zero full-rank construction. For any fixed rank-$r$ curve/model, choose rational points $P_1,\ldots,P_r$ whose classes form a Mordell--Weil free basis, and let $S_0=\bigcup_iD(P_i)$. If $p_j$ is the $j$th prime, put \[ S=S_0\cup\{p_{2^n}:n\ge1\}. \] The aid asserts that $S$ is infinite and recursive and has both relative natural prime density and Dirichlet prime density zero, while $P_i\in U(R_S)$ and hence $\operatorname{rank}H_S=r$. Natural density here means $\#\{p\in S:p\le X\}/\pi(X)\to0$, not merely ordinary integer density of a prime subset. Recursiveness is relative to the fixed finite data; no uniform algorithm for finding a Mordell--Weil basis from an arbitrary curve equation is inferred. This construction concerns generated rank and does not prove that $U(R_S)$ itself is infinite or large by a height-counting criterion.

Supplied fixed-model obstruction. On the model labelled 389a1, \[ y^2+y=x^3+x^2-2x, \] the aid states that $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$ are independent Mordell--Weil generators. Both are integral and therefore lie in $U(R_S)$ for every $S$. Thus $\operatorname{rank}H_S=2$ for every prime set: no infinite $S$ can give rank one on this model. This refutes a universal assertion for every fixed integral rank-two model; it is not a claim about every alternative model or every refined existence question.

Supplied denominator-support criterion. Let $A=E({\mathbb{Q}})/E({\mathbb{Q}})_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^2$ and choose a primitive rank-one line $L\subset A$. Then $H_S$ has nonzero free rank contained in $L$ exactly when:

  1. some non-torsion $P$ with class in $L$ has $D(P)\subseteq S$;
  2. every $Q$ whose class is outside $L$ satisfies $D(Q)\cap(\mathcal P\setminus S)\ne\varnothing$, where $\mathcal P$ is the set of all primes.
This is a hitting-set problem for denominator supports of points transverse to $L$, together with an allowed nonzero point on $L$. An empty transverse support, supplied by an integral point outside $L$, obstructs it for every $S$. Infinite/density constraints on $S$ are additional requirements not solved by restating this criterion. The source's broad density-zero "smallness" question remains distinct from these generated-rank results.

Short English statement

Density zero alone does not force small generated Mordell--Weil rank: the aid gives sparse prime sets containing a finite basis-denominator seed. Its explicit rank-two model already has two integral independent points, so no prime set gives generated rank one there. The exact remaining rank-one selection problem is expressed through denominator supports; other meanings of smallness are unspecified.
