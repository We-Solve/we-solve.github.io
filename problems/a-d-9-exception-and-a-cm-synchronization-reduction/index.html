Math-1265

A d=9 exception and a CM-synchronization reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

D. Ulmer asks about CM fibers of the smooth projective models $C_{d,a}:y^d=x(x-1)(x-a)$, primarily over ${\mathbb{C}}$, with $d\ge2$ and $a\notin\{0,1\}$. The printed genus $d-1$ must be qualified: the attached aid gives \[ g(C_{d,a})=d-\frac{1+\gcd(d,3)}2, \] so it equals $d-1$ only when $3\nmid d$.

There is a separate definition issue. The original calls CM the condition $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{End}^0(J_{d,a})=2g$. Standard CM type instead requires a commutative semisimple ${\mathbb{Q}}$-subalgebra of dimension $2g$ inside $\operatorname{End}^0(J_{d,a})$, where $\operatorname{End}^0=\operatorname{End}_{\overline K}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}$. These conditions are not generally equivalent for non-simple abelian varieties. The aid expressly uses standard CM type, not the literal endomorphism-dimension test.

Using standard CM type, the intended question is whether there are infinitely many distinct $d$ admitting a parameter outside \[ \{0,1\}\cup\{-1,1/2,2,\zeta_6,\zeta_6^{-1}\}, \] for which the full Jacobian is CM; $\zeta_6$ is a primitive sixth root. The source presents the five latter parameters as known special examples. Its claim of finiteness for each fixed $d>7$ is corrected by the aid: $d=9$ gives Moonen's one-dimensional special family 19, of genus seven, with infinitely many Zariski-dense CM parameters. For each fixed $d>7$, $d\ne9$, it reports finiteness from Moonen's classification and Tsimerman's André--Oort theorem. This fixed-degree exception does not answer the infinitely-many-degrees question.

The aid also decomposes the Jacobian up to isogeny by exact-order cyclotomic factors $A_{e,a}$ for $e>1$ dividing $d$, arising from the deck action. With $S_d^{\mathrm{full}}$ the parameters for full-Jacobian CM and $S_e^{\mathrm{new}}$ those for CM of the exact-order-$e$ factor, it claims \[ J_{d,a}\sim\prod_{e\mid d,\ e>1}A_{e,a},\qquad S_d^{\mathrm{full}}=\bigcap_{e\mid d,\ e>1}S_e^{\mathrm{new}},\qquad e\mid d\Longrightarrow S_d^{\mathrm{full}}\subseteq S_e^{\mathrm{full}}. \] Thus CM of one new factor alone does not establish full CM at composite degree. The original also mentions $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ or finite fields, requiring their own characteristic and CM conventions; the cited classification concerns the characteristic-zero interpretation. Its elliptic-rank application is truncated after "rank $d+$" and no missing bound is inferred.

Short English statement

Seek nontrivial full-CM Jacobians for infinitely many degrees in this cyclic-cover family. The draft distinguishes standard CM type from the printed dimension test, corrects the genus, and retains the aid's exceptional degree nine and simultaneous-factor requirement.
