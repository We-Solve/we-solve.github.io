Three zeta-like products, degree-count inversion, and the factorization boundary

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Can one compute the zeta function of $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)$, \[\zeta_n(s)=\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-s})^{-1},\] without first factoring $n$? For a polynomial $f\in\mathbb F_q[x]$, consider the analogous product over its monic irreducible factors. From this zeta data, can one recover the factorization of $f$ in deterministic polynomial time?

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].
