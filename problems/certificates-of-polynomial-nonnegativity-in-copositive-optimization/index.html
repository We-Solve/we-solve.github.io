Research attempt: Exact certificates, closure, and a projection-invariant case

A precise exact-certificate test.
Write $\operatorname{Pos}(S)$ for the real polynomials nonnegative at every point of a closed set $S$. An exact certificate of the type investigated here is \[ p=q+hr,\qquad q\in\operatorname{Pos}(S),\quad r\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]. \] Such a certificate implies $p\ge0$ on $S\cap\{h=0\}$ by substitution. Its unrestricted converse fails even on a very simple unbounded zero set. Take $S=\mathbb R^2$, $h(x,y)=x^2$ and $p(x,y)=x$. The polynomial $p$ vanishes on $\{x=0\}$. If $p=q+x^2r$ with $q\ge0$ globally, then for every fixed $y$, $q(0,y)=0$ is a minimum of the differentiable function $x\mapsto q(x,y)$, so $\partial_xq(0,y)=0$. But $q=x-x^2r$ gives $\partial_xq(0,y)=1$, a contradiction.
Closure repairs this example but loses bounded coefficients.
For every $\delta>0$, \[ x+\delta=\frac{(x+2\delta)^2}{4\delta}-x^2\frac1{4\delta}. \] Thus $x$ is a coefficientwise limit of polynomials in $\operatorname{Pos}(\mathbb R^2)+(x^2)$, even using polynomials of degree at most two. This does not contradict a theorem formulated with closure. It also shows why compactness of certificate parameters cannot be inferred from a degree bound: the displayed multiplier coefficient diverges.

More generally, for any fixed degree bound on $r_\delta$, every exact family $x+\delta=q_\delta+x^2r_\delta$ with $q_\delta\ge0$ must have unbounded multiplier coefficients along $\delta\downarrow0$. Otherwise some sequence tending to zero would have a coefficientwise convergent subsequence of multipliers in the finite-dimensional bounded coefficient space. Then $q_\delta=x+\delta-x^2r_\delta$ would converge pointwise to a globally nonnegative polynomial $x-x^2r$, yielding the forbidden exact certificate. This proves the obstruction without assuming that the particular displayed family is optimal.

A sufficient hypothesis with an explicit formula.
In contrast, let $h(x,y)=x$, where $y$ may denote any finite tuple of other variables, and suppose $S$ is invariant under the projection $(x,y)\mapsto(0,y)$. If $p\ge0$ on $S\cap\{x=0\}$, set $q(x,y)=p(0,y)$. The projection hypothesis implies $q\ge0$ on all of $S$. Expanding $p$ in powers of $x$ shows that $p-p(0,y)$ is divisible by $x$, so \[ p(x,y)=p(0,y)+x r(x,y) \] is an exact certificate. If $p$ has degree $d\ge1$, the constructed multiplier has degree at most $d-1$; if $p$ is independent of $x$, take $r=0$. This is a sufficient condition, not a necessary one or an algorithm for testing arbitrary polynomial nonnegativity on $S$.

The examples $h=x$ and $h=x^2$ have the same zero set but different principal ideals and different exact-certificate behavior. Therefore any proposed characterization based solely on the underlying zero set, ignoring the generator/ideal or the role of closure, cannot justify the exact identity under discussion. Passing from this restricted formula to effective copositive optimization hierarchies requires additional degree, positivity and convergence arguments.
