Uniqueness in the Boltzmann–Nordheim Condensate Model

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In the isotropic Bose setting, $x=|v|^2/2$ is kinetic energy and $f_{\rm reg}(x,t)$ is the regular density in the measure $f_{\rm reg}(x,t)\sqrt{x}\,dx+n_c(t)\delta_0$. Its regular mass is $m[f]=\int_0^\infty f(x)\sqrt{x}\,dx$ in the source normalization, and mass conservation fixes $n_c(t)=m_{\rm tot}(0)-m[f_{\rm reg}(\cdot,t)]$. $C_4$ is the homogeneous Boltzmann--Nordheim collision functional; $C_3$ is obtained by inserting the condensate ansatz and extracting the condensate coupling. $\ell^1$-clustering is the summability condition on initial correlation functions used in the source's perturbative kinetic-limit argument.
Source scope and qualifications.
The primary equation uses C_3[f_reg], without the superscript (1) present in the curated formula. No separate f_reg^(1) is defined in the report's equation (1). The source's two open questions concern uniqueness once a condensate is generated and extension beyond clustering, rather than merely existence near an equilibrium with a condensate already present.
Primary-source collision term.
The report's equation (1) is $\partial_t f_{\rm reg}=C_4[f_{\rm reg}]+(m_{\rm tot}(0)-m[f_{\rm reg}])C_3[f_{\rm reg}]$. The unexplained superscript in the catalogue is preserved only in the catalogue target below and is not an additional source variable.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the regular part of the isotropic Boltzmann–Nordheim condensate model, consider the mass-conserving evolution equation $\partial_t f_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,t)=C_4[f_{\mathrm{reg}}(\cdot,t)](x)+(m_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)-m[f_{\mathrm{reg}}(\cdot,t)])C_3[f_{\mathrm{reg}}^{(1)}(\cdot,t)](x)$. Does this equation have a unique solution when a condensate is generated? Can the perturbative construction be extended to initial data that are not $\ell^1$-clustering, thereby deriving this equation rather than the standard Boltzmann–Nordheim equation?

The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Later analysis proves finite-time blow-up and condensate formation for broad classes of Nordheim solutions, but the extracted regularized continuation and its no-blow-up and zero-condensate-generation questions are not fully settled. Short English statement

Establish uniqueness of the condensate-generating mass-conserving evolution and derive it from more general correlated initial states.
