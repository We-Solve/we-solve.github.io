Scalar Curvature Question [?6]: that the surface-tangent-bundle condition in Llarull's scalar-curvature rigidity theorem is redundant

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X^n,g)$ be a closed orientable Riemannian manifold with \[ \operatorname{Sc}_g(x)\ge n(n-1)\qquad(x\in X), \] and give $S^n$ its unit round metric $g_{\mathrm{sph}}$. Let $f:X\to S^n$ be $C^1$ with nonzero topological degree. For $n\ge2$, the usual pointwise area-nonincreasing condition is \[ \|\Lambda^2\mathrm df_x\|_{g,g_{\mathrm{sph}}}\le1 \qquad(x\in X), \] expressing that the area of every tangent two-plane, hence every parametrized surface with multiplicity, does not increase. This is distinct from requiring the operator norm $\|\mathrm df_x\|\le1$ on tangent vectors.

The supplied conjecture asks whether these hypotheses alone force $f$ to be an isometry: \[ f^*g_{\mathrm{sph}}=g \quad\text{and }f\text{ is a bijective Riemannian isometry}, \] without the additional assumption that $TX$ restricts trivially to every closed surface. The original degree hypothesis is nonzero degree, not only degree one. No spin or tangent-bundle condition is restored as an assumption.

The source does not specify the dimension range or explicitly state connectedness. Both require checking for the proposed conclusion. In particular area control and length control differ in low dimensions, and the conclusion that this particular map is an isometry is stronger than saying only that $X$ is isometric to a round sphere. The exact intended low-dimensional scope and rigidity conclusion are not silently repaired. The omitted surface-bundle condition also lacks a convention on embedded versus mapped surfaces and actual versus stable triviality.

Short English statement

Is the surface-tangent-bundle hypothesis unnecessary in the stated scalar-curvature rigidity theorem for area-nonincreasing nonzero-degree maps to the round sphere? The original area, degree, and map-isometry assertions are retained, with dimension and bundle-convention issues flagged.

The supplied summary cites Llarull's 1998 theorem under its own hypotheses and describes weaker-hypothesis and non-spin results as partial progress, while saying the full removal remains unresolved. It alternates area and distance conditions, degree one and nonzero degree, and a "does not shrink" phrase; those variants are not merged into the source assertion. No exact later theorem or dimension range is supplied. Provenance marks current status for review.
