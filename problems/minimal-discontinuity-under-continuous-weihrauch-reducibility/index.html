Math-4678

Minimal Discontinuity Under Continuous Weihrauch Reducibility

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal B={\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ be Baire space with the product topology, where ${\mathbb{N}}$ has the discrete topology. A partial multivalued problem $f:\subseteq\mathcal B\rightrightarrows\mathcal B$ consists of a domain $\operatorname{dom}(f)\subseteq\mathcal B$ and a nonempty set $f(p)\subseteq\mathcal B$ for each $p\in\operatorname{dom}(f)$. A continuous selector for $f$ is a continuous map $F:\operatorname{dom}(f)\to\mathcal B$, with the domain carrying its subspace topology, such that $F(p)\in f(p)$ for every $p\in\operatorname{dom}(f)$. Call $f$ discontinuous if it has no continuous selector.

For two such problems, write $f\leq_{\mathrm W}^{\mathrm c}g$ if there are partial continuous maps \[ K:\subseteq\mathcal B\to\mathcal B, \qquad H:\subseteq\mathcal B\times\mathcal B\to\mathcal B \] satisfying the following requirements. For every $p\in\operatorname{dom}(f)$, the value $K(p)$ is defined and belongs to $\operatorname{dom}(g)$; for every such $p$ and every $q\in g(K(p))$, the value $H(p,q)$ is defined and belongs to $f(p)$. Thus the output processor may use both the original input and any valid output of $g$.

Interpreting "simplest" as a least continuous Weihrauch degree among all discontinuous problems, the question is whether \[ \exists f:\subseteq\mathcal B\rightrightarrows\mathcal B\quad \left[ f\text{ has no continuous selector} \ \land\ \forall g:\subseteq\mathcal B\rightrightarrows\mathcal B, \left(g\text{ has no continuous selector} \Longrightarrow f\leq_{\mathrm W}^{\mathrm c}g\right) \right]. \] The quantification imposes no effectiveness, definability, or descriptive complexity restriction on the domains or values. The least-degree interpretation is supported by the supplied literature assessment; the word "simplest" alone does not distinguish it from the weaker notion of a minimal degree.

The foundational theory for the unrestricted question is not specified. The supplied dated assessment says that Brattka proves that the problem denoted $\mathrm{DIS}$ is least for effectively discontinuous problems, and that under $\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{DC}+\mathrm{AD}$ it is least among all discontinuous problems. It also reports "non-effectively-discontinuous counterexamples" in $\mathrm{ZFC}$ and describes the unrestricted question as not settled unconditionally. These are supplied literature claims, not independently verified results of this draft. The record does not define $\mathrm{DIS}$, effective discontinuity, or the precise counterexample property; consequently these reported qualifications are retained in prose rather than expanded into additional formal claims.

Short English statement

Does there exist a discontinuous multivalued problem on Baire space that continuously Weihrauch reduces to every discontinuous multivalued problem on Baire space? Here discontinuity means the absence of a continuous selector, and "simplest" is interpreted as least in the reducibility order. The supplied partially solved assessment distinguishes effective discontinuity and results under $\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{DC}+\mathrm{AD}$; its claims require independent review.
