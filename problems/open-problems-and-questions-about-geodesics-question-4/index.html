Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Question 4

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ without boundary, write $\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)=\int_M {\,\mathrm{d}}\operatorname{vol}_g$ for its Riemannian volume. Completeness means geodesic completeness: every maximal geodesic is defined on all of ${\mathbb{R}}$. A closed geodesic means a nonconstant smooth periodic geodesic, equivalently a nonconstant smooth map $\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to M$, for some $T>0$, satisfying $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0$, where $\nabla$ is the Levi--Civita connection of $g$.

Question (attributed to Bangert). Does every complete Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ of finite volume admit a closed geodesic? In the boundaryless interpretation above, the requested implication is \[ \left((M,g)\text{ is geodesically complete and }\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)<\infty\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists T>0\ \exists\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to M\quad \left(\gamma\text{ is smooth and nonconstant, }\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0\right). \] The supplied statement specifies neither a dimension range nor connectedness, nonemptiness, or boundary conventions. The definitions above explicitly use the smooth boundaryless interpretation; the intended scope, particularly any positive-dimensional restriction, requires confirmation. No compactness assumption is supplied.

Short English statement

The question attributed to Bangert asks whether completeness and finite Riemannian volume force the existence of a nonconstant periodic geodesic. The intended dimension range and manifold conventions require clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this is not an independently verified status assessment.
