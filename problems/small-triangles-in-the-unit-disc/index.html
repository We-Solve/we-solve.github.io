Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Unless a section says otherwise, ${\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is the ring of integers, ${\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}$ are the rational, real, and complex fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity, $f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$; $f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$; $f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds. A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence. The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than $n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.

Use the closed unit disc in ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ and distinct points. For a triple $x,y,z$, its area is $\tfrac12|\det(y-x,z-x)|$, which is zero for collinear triples. If $\Delta(n)$ is the supremum, over $n$-point sets, of their smallest triangle area, the requested upper bound is $\Delta(n)\leq n^{-2+o(1)}$; equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually $\Delta(n)\leq n^{-2+\varepsilon}$. [S1] [S2]

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Given $n$ points in the unit disc, must there be a triangle of area at most $n^{-2+o(1)}$ determined by them? [S1]
Residual task reported by the archive.

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Approach or attain the exponent -2. [S1]

Short English statement

Must every large collection of points in a unit disc contain a triangle whose area is at most an almost inverse-square function of the number of points?

Small Triangles in the Unit Disc

OpenAI's claim

Heilbronn triangle power improvement: For every sufficiently large $n$, the paper claims $n$ points in the unit square with every triangle area at least $c n^{-2+\eta}$ for fixed positive $c,\eta$. This contradicts an upper bound with exponent arbitrarily close to minus two.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For $n\ge3$, define $\Delta(n)=\max_{P\subset[0,1]^2,\ |P|=n}\min_{\{p,q,r\}\subset P}\tfrac12|\det(q-p,r-p)|$. The claim gives absolute $\eta,c>0,n_0$ with \[\Delta(n)\ge cn^{-2+\eta}\quad(n\ge n_0).\] It therefore refutes the proposed upper estimate $\Delta(n)=O_\epsilon(n^{-2+\epsilon})$ for every $\epsilon>0$, by taking $\epsilon<\eta$. A fixed affine scaling transfers the construction to a unit disk.

Outline of the argument

Heilbronn triangle power improvement: Two congruence conditions govern randomly sampled integer columns. Norm-polynomial residues control small nonzero determinants, while an independent quadratic-surface condition and lattice counts handle zero determinants. The Chinese remainder construction combines them, repeated labels supply a deletion saving, and scaling transfers the retained determinant lower bound to triangle areas.
