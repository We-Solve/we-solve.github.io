Problem 2.6 — Let Γ be a finite index subgroup of Modg.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$ have finite index, in the source's surface convention. With trivial integral coefficients, \[ H^1(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}). \] The cohomological reading is supported by the source's explicit phrase "first cohomology" and its final equivalent homomorphism question. Vanishing does not assert that the integral abelianization $H_1(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})$ is zero; finite abelian quotients are not ruled out.

Problem 2.6. Is $H^1(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})=0$ for every finite-index subgroup of $\operatorname{Mod}_g$? The initial sentence does not state a genus restriction, but the source immediately gives counterexamples for $g=1,2$ and focuses on the unresolved higher-genus setting. Those exceptions must remain explicit rather than extending the vanishing question indiscriminately to all genera.

The source reports these qualifications:

  • For $g\geq3$, McCarthy [37] proves vanishing if $\Gamma$ contains the Torelli group.
  • For $g=2$, McCarthy [37] and Taherkhani [43] give finite-index subgroups with nonzero first cohomology.
  • For each $g\in\{1,2\}$ and integer $n\geq2$, [31] gives a finite-index $\Gamma_n\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g$ surjecting onto a rank-$n$ free group. In particular, $\operatorname{rank}H^1(\Gamma_n;{\mathbb{Z}})\geq n$.
  • A positive answer to the unprovided Problem 1(a) would imply a positive answer here. The source says the $g\geq3$ hypothesis in that Problem 1 is necessary, noting a finite-index subgroup of $\operatorname{Mod}_2$ mapping onto a finite-index subgroup of $\operatorname{Mod}_1$.
It asks separately the special case: for $g\geq3$, if a finite-index $\Gamma$ contains the Johnson kernel $\mathcal K_g$, generated by Dehn twists about separating simple closed curves, must every homomorphism $\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}$ vanish? Containing $\mathcal K_g$ is not replaced by the stronger requirement of containing the whole Torelli group.

The detached footnote records a later announcement by Behrstock and Margalit: an isomorphism between finite-index subgroups of $\operatorname{Mod}_{1,2}^*$ need not be the restriction of an inner automorphism. Its citation is empty and the star convention is undefined; it is adjacent historical context, not a solution of the cohomology question.

Short English statement

Ask whether finite-index mapping-class subgroups in the higher-genus setting have no nonzero homomorphisms to ${\mathbb{Z}}$, including the special case of subgroups containing the Johnson kernel. The source explicitly records genus-one/two counterexamples and a positive result when the full Torelli group is contained.
