A rate-delay abstraction certificate for filtered spiking circuits

Definitions and mathematical statement

The original item is Chapter C, "Breakthroughs in Brain Computing," a discussion after Steve Grossberg's talk, not a stated mathematical problem. Grossberg attributes his laminar architecture to robust grouping and analog coherence requiring an ordering of mechanisms, compactly reflected in anatomy, and reports ablation tests suggesting every part was necessary. He refers to a paper with "Razada" involving layer-6 stimulation and layer-4 observation. The exchange says lateral effects are "not superlinearly but faster," notes that one inhibitory neuron may not provide every normalization effect and that circuit variants range from bipole to modulatory, and leaves a center/flank experimental-prediction question without a recorded answer.

The transcript distinguishes models not requiring spikes from self-synchronizing networks and order-preserving limit cycles where they matter. Grossberg discusses nonattenuated long-distance transmission, axonal diameter and delays, developmental excitation--inhibition balance, instability and resynchronization; he mentions computer-vision applications without listing them in the record. A question about retaining spiking neurophysiology receives a rhetorical question about retaining all channels. Jeff offers a computer/quantum-mechanics abstraction analogy. Grossberg calls his equations mean values of stochastic differential equations and cites phonemic restoration as an example where spikes are not important, with a garbled speech/noise example. Pentti distinguishes synchronous from possibly asynchronous requirements. These are attributed discussion points, not established universal abstraction theorems.

The attached aid supplies two explicitly separate surrogate results. Let $N_i$ be $N$ independent Poisson random measures with common deterministic intensity $\lambda(s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s$, and let a causal linear filter $h$ have the required first and second integrability. For \[ Y_N(t)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\int_{-\infty}^t h(t-s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} N_i(s), \] the stated exact formulas are \[ {\mathbb{E}} Y_N(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t h(t-s)\lambda(s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s,\qquad \operatorname{Var}Y_N(t)=\frac1N\int_{-\infty}^t h(t-s)^2\lambda(s)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s. \] For $h(u)=\tau_s^{-1}e^{-u/\tau_s}\mathbf1_{u\ge0}$, $\tau_s>0$, and $0\le\lambda\le R$, this variance is at most $R/(2N\tau_s)$. An $L$-Lipschitz readout obeys ${\mathbb{E}}|F(Y_N)-F({\mathbb{E}} Y_N)|\le L\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y_N}$, while the square readout has exact bias ${\mathbb{E}} Y_N^2-({\mathbb{E}} Y_N)^2=\operatorname{Var}Y_N$, strictly positive when that variance is nonzero. Independence, linear filtering and deterministic rates are essential to this exact model; it does not automatically close nonlinear or correlated circuits.

Separately, for $T>0$, $D\ge0$ and $g\ge0$, consider the scalar delayed feedback equation \[ Tz'(t)=-z(t)-gz(t-D). \] The aid asserts asymptotic stability for every finite $D$ when $0\le g\le1$. For $g>1$, put \[ D_*=\frac{T\arccos(-1/g)}{\sqrt{g^2-1}}. \] It is asymptotically stable exactly for $0\le D<D_*$. At equality a conjugate pair has frequencies $\pm\sqrt{g^2-1}/T$, yielding nondecaying modes; as delay increases the pair crosses toward positive real part, and $D>D_*$ is unstable. Thus finite sampling, nonlinear-readout bias and delay instability are distinct potential failure modes of the chosen rate abstraction. They are not a full theorem about the original laminar circuit.
