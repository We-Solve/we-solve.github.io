Math-4625

Mean-Curvature Flow from Isolated Conical Singularities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq7$, and let $M_0\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$ be a hypersurface whose singularities are isolated points modeled on regular hypercones. A hypercone here means an $n$-dimensional cone invariant under positive dilations; the usual regularity convention is smoothness away from its vertex. The extraction does not state minimality, a precise asymptotic regularity condition, or a convergence topology for the conical models.

Let $(M_t)_{t\geq0}$ be a mean-curvature flow with initial data $M_0$, in the intended weak-flow sense at the singular time. Where smooth, mean-curvature flow has normal velocity equal to its mean-curvature vector. The question asks both:

  1. Near each singular point $p$, does the flow have asymptotically self-similarly expanding behavior as $t\downarrow0$? The expected parabolic scale is $t^{-1/2}(M_t-p)$; convergence to an expanding profile would need its topology, multiplicity, and selection conditions specified. A self-similar expanding profile produces the family $p+\sqrt t\,\Sigma$ for $t>0$ under the chosen time normalization.
  2. Does the flow become smooth for a short positive time, that is, is $M_t$ smooth for $0<t<\varepsilon$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ in the intended local or global sense?
The cleaned question restricts the remaining target to $n\geq7$. The original asks without this restriction and says that affirmative results hold for $n\leq6$. Which weak flow is meant, whether assertions concern every such flow or an appropriate selected flow, and whether smoothing is global or local near each singularity are not specified.

Short English statement

In hypersurface dimensions $n\geq7$, ask whether mean-curvature flow from isolated regular conical singularities immediately smooths and approaches expanding self-similar profiles near those points. The weak-flow choice and precise asymptotic and smoothing statements still require hypotheses.
