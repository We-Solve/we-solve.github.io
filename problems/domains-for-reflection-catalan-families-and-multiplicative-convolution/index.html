Math-1665

Domains for reflection-Catalan families and multiplicative convolution

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 1.2 asks for the largest natural domains of the reflection-Catalan families $NC_W$, $NN_W$, and $\Delta_W$, and their applications, including free probability beyond types A and B. These ordinarily denote noncrossing partitions, nonnesting objects, and a cluster complex, but the extraction omits their preceding definitions and ends mid-bullet. A single maximal domain is not specified by this open-ended request; different constructions can have different requirements.

For a finite complex reflection group $W\subset GL(V)$, a pseudoreflection fixes a hyperplane pointwise and has one nontrivial eigenvalue. In a unitary realization its eigenvalues are 1 with multiplicity $\dim V-1$ and a root of unity $\zeta\ne1$ once. The printed zero eigenvalues are impossible for a unitary operator, and the printed $-1$ is special to order-two reflections. This is an explicit mathematical repair, not an exact transcription. The source attributes to Shephard and Todd [43] that the invariant ring is polynomial precisely for finite groups generated by pseudoreflections, with a uniquely determined multiset of invariant degrees $d_1\le\cdots\le d_r$, in a fixed representation.

The proposed numerology, attributed to Bessis [7], is \[ \operatorname{Cat}(W)=\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{h+d_i}{d_i},\qquad h=d_r. \] The source suggests restricting to duality or well-generated groups because this product can fail to be integral. In the essential setting, well-generated means generated by $r$ reflections; any identification with duality must use its proper hypotheses. Reducible groups and nonessential representations require additional care rather than an automatic single common $h$.

The source records that $NC_W$ is defined for finite Coxeter groups and can be considered for finitely generated infinite Coxeter groups, where choices may affect uniqueness [11]. In the finite Coxeter case, one formulation is the absolute-order interval $[1,c]$, with reflections $T$, reflection length $\ell_T$, and \[ u\le_Tv\iff\ell_T(u)+\ell_T(u^{-1}v)=\ell_T(v), \] where $c$ is a Coxeter element. The source credits Bessis and Corran [8] with realizations for an infinite family of complex reflection groups and Bessis [7] with a proposed uniform well-generated complex definition. These are historical reports, not an independently updated classification. It also asks whether free probability, naturally expressed through type A noncrossing partitions [47] and already developed in type B [10], extends further, presumably through a generalization of Speicher's multiplicative functions [48].

Diagnostics in the supplied aid. A single highest degree is incompatible with reducible product numerology: $A_1\times A_2$ has degrees $(2,2,3)$, and the naive common $h=3$ gives \[ (5/2)^2(6/3)=25/2, \] whereas componentwise Coxeter numbers give $\operatorname{Cat}(A_1)\operatorname{Cat}(A_2)=2\cdot5=10$. Thus product-compatible numerology must not silently assign the largest component degree to every component.

For the imprimitive monomial group $G(m,p,n)$, with positive integers $p\mid m$ and $n\ge2$, use its degree list in the indicated $n$-dimensional representation, \[ m,2m,\ldots,(n-1)m,nm/p, \] and take their maximum as $h$. The aid's naive product evaluates to \[ \prod_i\frac{h+d_i}{d_i} =\begin{cases} \binom{2n}{n},&p=1,\\ ((p+1)n-p)C_{n-1},&p>1, \end{cases} \quad C_k=\frac1{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}. \] The restriction $n\ge2$ is necessary for the second formula: at $n=1$ the sole degree equals $h$, so the naive product is 2. For $m=p=1$, the permutation representation contains a fixed line; passing to the essential representation changes the degree list and must not be done invisibly. The aid observes integrality even for non-well-generated members of this family, so integrality is neither a characterization of well-generation nor sufficient evidence for a corresponding Catalan family.

For a multiplicative-function application, let a class of finite or locally finite poset intervals be stable under taking intervals and finite Cartesian products. If functions depend on interval isomorphism type and satisfy $f(P\times Q)=f(P)f(Q)$, then incidence convolution \[ (f*g)(x,z)=\sum_{x\le y\le z}f([x,y])g([y,z]) \] preserves multiplicativity by the product decomposition of intervals. The aid uses product compatibility and interval heredity as a structural gate for Speicher-style convolution. This combinatorial gate does not construct $NN_W$, $\Delta_W$, a positive probabilistic theory, or a notion of independence for all complex or infinite reflection groups. The general largest-domain and beyond-type-B questions remain broader than these diagnostics.

Short English statement

The source asks how far reflection-Catalan families and their free-probability applications extend. The aid separates numerical integrality from genuine constructions, detects a reducible-group obstruction, and gives explicit imprimitive-group products. Interval and product compatibility support multiplicative convolution, but do not by themselves define all Catalan families or a generalized probability theory.
