Minimum Distinct Distances Among Planar Point Sets

OpenAI's claim

Almost all planar pins: For any fixed positive $\varepsilon$, the claimed number of pins determining fewer than $n^{1-\varepsilon}$ nonzero distances is $o(n)$ uniformly over planar $n$-point sets. This is an almost-all-pins conclusion rather than an exact linear lower bound at every pin.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For finite $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ put $D_x(P)=\{|y-x|:y\in P\setminus\{x\}\}$ and $k_P(x,y)=|\{z\in P\setminus\{x\}:|z-x|=|y-x|\}|$. The claim states that for every $s>0$, \[\sup_{|P|=n}\frac{|\{(x,y)\in P^2:x\ne y,\ k_P(x,y)\ge n^s\}|}{n(n-1)}\longrightarrow0.\] Consequently, for every $\epsilon>0$, $\sup_{|P|=n}|\{x:|D_x(P)|<n^{1-\epsilon}\}|/n\to0$. Thus all but $o(n)$ pins determine at least $n^{1-\epsilon}$ distances, uniformly over the configuration; the sharp $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ global scale is not asserted.

Outline of the argument

Almost all planar pins: An extremal pair graph is extracted from large distance fibers while retaining nonconcentration within those fibers. Real-closed-field transfer moves finite configurations into number fields without altering their distance relations. Factorization into two complex coordinates and the product formula balance local closeness partitions across absolute values, giving an incompatibility with the assumed abundance of large fibers.
