General Quadrature for Isogeometric Galerkin Methods

Definitions and mathematical statement

Isogeometric analysis (IGA) uses spline-type representations of geometry and approximation spaces. In a Galerkin discretization, a discrete unknown $u_h$ in a trial space $V_h$ is required to satisfy \[ a(u_h,v_h)=\ell(v_h)\qquad\text{for every }v_h\in W_h, \] where $W_h$ is a test space and $a$ and $\ell$ denote the forms defining the variational problem. This notation describes the setting only; the source does not specify these spaces or forms, or restrict the problem to a particular differential equation.

For terminology, a quadrature rule approximates an integral by weighted evaluations: \[ I(f)=\int_{\Omega}f\,\mathrm d\mu, \qquad Q(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_j f(x_j). \] Here the integration domain $\Omega$, measure $\mu$, integrand class, nodes $x_j$, weights $w_j$, and number of evaluations $N$ are unspecified inputs or design choices, not additional hypotheses supplied by the record. In Galerkin IGA, quadrature is used to approximate integrals occurring in the discrete variational forms.
