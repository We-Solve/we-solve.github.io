Math-15113

Loss Thresholds in Two-Round Random Edge Coloring

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Consider the source's two-round random edge-coloring game on $n$ vertices. First the entire graph $G(n,p)$ is revealed and its edges are colored offline. Then $N$ additional random edges are revealed and colored online, with each color decision made when the edge is revealed. The number of colors and forbidden configuration defining a loss are not supplied, so no particular Ramsey-avoidance game is silently chosen.

The proposed implication is: for every $\alpha\in[0,1/6]$, if \[ p=\Omega(n^{-1/2-\alpha}),\qquad N=\omega(n^{8\alpha}), \] then the player loses. For a fixed $\alpha$, the first condition means there are $c>0$ and $n_0$ such that $p(n)\ge c n^{-1/2-\alpha}$ for all $n\ge n_0$; the second means $N(n)/n^{8\alpha}\to\infty$. Both endpoints of the stated interval are retained. A varying sequence $\alpha(n)$ is not asserted without source confirmation.

In a usual random-game threshold interpretation, the conclusion would say that every admissible strategy has loss probability tending to one, or that the optimal success probability tends to zero. The source does not state the probability quantifier, its uniformity over strategies, whether strategies may be randomized, or what information is available during the offline round. A single deterministic inevitable-loss assertion is not inferred from asymptotic notation alone.

The distribution of additional edges is also missing: sampling previously absent edges without replacement, independent sampling with repeats, and other models differ. Any feasibility condition on $N$, colors, recoloring rules, or stopping time must be recovered from the report. The exact exponents $-1/2-\alpha$ and $8\alpha$ are preserved, but the incomplete game rules prevent a self-contained theorem of loss.

Short English statement

The source proposes a loss threshold across the full interval $0\le\alpha\le1/6$ after an offline random-graph coloring round and an online extension. The powers of $n$ are explicit, but colors, the losing pattern, edge sampling, and the probabilistic strategy quantifier are missing.
