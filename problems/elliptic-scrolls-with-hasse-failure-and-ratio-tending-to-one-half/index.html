Math-1249

Elliptic scrolls with Hasse failure and ratio tending to one half

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Heath-Brown's main question is whether there is a sequence of smooth projective varieties $X_i\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N_i}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ with \[ X_i({\mathbb{Q}}_v)\ne\varnothing\text{ for every place }v\text{, including infinity},\qquad X_i({\mathbb{Q}})=\varnothing,\qquad \frac{\dim X_i}{\deg X_i}\longrightarrow\infty \] (or at least with the ratios unbounded). Degree is relative to the specified embedding. The supplied verification repairs missing fraction bars and reads the recorded Browning--Heath-Brown benchmark as ratio tending to $1/3$, with dimension tending to infinity.

The source discussion contains several further questions and qualifications. Wooley suggests dropping smoothness and using norm equations $N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\sum_{j=1}^d x_j\alpha_j)=ct^d$, with $d=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]$ and a basis $\alpha_j$. Heath-Brown separately asks for smooth hypersurface Hasse failures of dimension at least three. Colliot-Thélène attributes many general-type examples conditionally to Sarnak--Wang assuming Bombieri--Lang. Wooley proposes starting with a locally soluble smooth hypersurface of degree $d\gg N$ in ${\mathbb{P}}^N$, then combining determinant-method bounds and linear sections; Colliot-Thélène questions whether this merely forces large coefficients. These are suggestions and conditional claims, not supplied unconditional constructions.

Harari asks whether ratios tending to infinity force geometric rational connectedness, and remarks on difficulty of cohomological Hasse obstructions for geometrically rationally connected complete intersections. Browning states conditionally on a Hartshorne conjecture that a smooth nondegenerate $Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^N$ with $\dim Y\geq2\deg Y+1$ is a complete intersection and hence rationally connected, suggesting the range $1/3<\dim X_i/\deg X_i\leq2$. Finally Tschinkel asks whether Fano varieties over ${\mathbb{C}}$ with nonzero $\operatorname{Br}(X)=H^3(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ can occur in every dimension at least four; smoothness and the Brauer convention for that identification need clarification.

The supplied report improves only the bounded ratio benchmark. For every positive integer $m\equiv1\pmod3$, set $r=m^2$. It uses an order-three torsor self-cover of the Selmer cubic of degree $m^2$, followed by pushforward of a degree-$2m^2+1$ line bundle to a stable rank-$r$ bundle. The intended elliptic-scroll construction is its projectivization with tautological embedding. The report claims resulting smooth projective geometrically integral $X_m$ satisfy \[ X_m\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2r}_{{\mathbb{Q}}},\qquad \dim X_m=r,\qquad\deg X_m=2r+1, \] \[ X_m({\mathbb{Q}}_v)\ne\varnothing\ (\text{all }v),\qquad X_m({\mathbb{Q}})=\varnothing,\qquad \frac{\dim X_m}{\deg X_m}\longrightarrow\frac12. \] It expressly says these examples are not geometrically rationally connected and do not solve unboundedness. The cubic equation, self-cover, line-bundle data and embedding proof are not supplied, so the construction and its claimed sharp dimensions require independent verification.

Short English statement

Seek smooth projective Hasse-principle counterexamples with unbounded dimension-to-degree ratio. The report gives elliptic-scroll examples with exact ratio $m^2/(2m^2+1)$ tending to $1/2$, improving the historical $1/3$ benchmark but remaining bounded. The source's separate hypersurface, rational-connectedness and Fano-Brauer questions remain distinct.
