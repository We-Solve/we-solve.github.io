Math-3674

Shortest Billiard Trajectories in Constant Width Bodies

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\geq 2$. A Euclidean convex body is a compact convex set $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ with nonempty interior. For $u\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$, its support function is \[ h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle. \] The body has constant width $1$ if \[ h_K(u)+h_K(-u)=1\qquad\text{for every }u\in{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ with }\|u\|=1. \] No smoothness assumption is supplied.

To make the billiard terminology explicit, consider the following generalized reflection convention, whose agreement with the intended source formulation requires review. At $x\in\partial K$, define the outward normal cone by \[ N_K(x)=\{n\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\langle n,y-x\rangle\leq 0\text{ for every }y\in K\}. \] A nonconstant closed generalized billiard trajectory is a cyclic polygon $\gamma=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$, where $m\geq2$, each $x_i\in\partial K$, consecutive vertices are distinct, and, with indices read modulo $m$, \[ v_i=\frac{x_{i+1}-x_i}{\|x_{i+1}-x_i\|},\qquad v_{i-1}-v_i\in N_K(x_i)\setminus\{0\}. \] Its length is \[ L(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{i+1}-x_i\|. \] It is shortest if $L(\gamma)\leq L(\eta)$ for every nonconstant closed generalized billiard trajectory $\eta$ in $K$. Its period is the least number of reflections in one traversal of its primitive cyclic trajectory; period $2$ means travel from one boundary point to another and back along the same segment.

Question, using the universal interpretation in the supplied literature assessment. For every integer $d\geq3$, every Euclidean convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ of constant width $1$, and every shortest nonconstant closed billiard trajectory $\gamma$ in $K$, must $\gamma$ have period $2$?

The supplied record presents the planar period-two conclusion as known and asks for its extension to every higher dimension. The record does not specify a reflection convention at nonsmooth boundary points. Consequently, the generalized convention above is an explicit provisional interpretation, not an attributed definition from the report. There is also a quantifier issue: the statement says "a shortest" trajectory, whereas the supplied assessment explicitly asks whether every shortest trajectory has period two. The weaker alternative would ask only whether each such body admits a shortest trajectory of period two; these targets must be distinguished in mathematical review.

Short English statement

The question asks whether, in every Euclidean dimension $d\geq3$, every shortest closed billiard trajectory in every convex body of constant width $1$ travels between two boundary points and back. The supplied record reports the planar result as known. The treatment of nonsmooth boundaries and the distinction between existence of a period-two minimizer and the period-two property for every minimizer require confirmation.
