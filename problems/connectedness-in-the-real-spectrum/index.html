Math-967

Connectedness in the Real Spectrum

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a commutative ring $A$ with identity, write $\operatorname{Sper}A$ for its real spectrum. A point $\alpha$ consists of a prime ideal $\mathfrak p_\alpha$ and an ordering of $\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p_\alpha)$; write $a(\alpha)$ for the image of $a\in A$ in this ordered field. Use the usual spectral (Harrison) topology, generated by the sets on which finitely many specified elements are strictly positive. Define the separating ideal by \[I(\alpha,\beta)=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\big\langle a\in A:a(\alpha)\geq0,\ a(\beta)\leq0\big\rangle_A,\] and put $Z(g)=\{\delta\in\operatorname{Sper}A:g(\delta)=0\}$. The connectedness question in the supplied record is: given $\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Sper}A$ and a finite list $g_1,\ldots,g_s\in A\setminus I(\alpha,\beta)$, must there exist a connected subset \[C\subseteq\operatorname{Sper}A\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^s Z(g_i),\qquad \alpha,\beta\in C?\] Equivalently, must these two points belong to the same connected component of that complement? Connectedness is meant in the indicated topology and does not require path connectedness. The source does not specify the intended class of rings $A$; the commutative-ring setup above makes the notation meaningful but does not supply the missing hypotheses of the conjecture. In particular, this draft does not silently assert that the intended conjecture concerns every commutative ring. The supplied 2026-08-21 triage presents the connectedness conjecture as a strengthening implying Pierce--Birkhoff, reports partial dimension-two and regular-ring results, and labels the general question open; these are supplied literature qualifications.

Short English statement

Ask whether two real-spectrum points can remain in one connected component after removing finitely many zero sets of elements outside their separating ideal. The topology is the usual real-spectrum topology; the intended hypotheses on the ring are missing from the supplied record.
