Probabilities of Properties in Random-Knot Models

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathscr K$ denote the set of knot types in $S^3$ under conventions to be specified by the random-knot model. A knot property $\mathcal P$ is represented by a subset $A_{\mathcal P}\subseteq\mathscr K$. For each index $n$, let $(\Omega_n,\mathcal F_n,\Pr_n)$ be a probability space and let $K_n$ be a random knot taking values in $\mathscr K$, with $\{\omega\in\Omega_n:K_n(\omega)\in A_{\mathcal P}\}\in\mathcal F_n$. Define \[ p_n(\mathcal P)=\Pr_n\!\left(K_n\in A_{\mathcal P}\right). \] The spaces need not be coupled across different indices. The knot conventions, distributions, meaning of the size parameter $n$, and admissible indices are part of the model; the supplied record does not fully specify them.

Parametric problem. For a fixed property $\mathcal P$ and a specified random-knot model $(K_n)$, determine \[ L_{\mathcal P,(K_n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}p_n(\mathcal P) \] when this limit exists. Existence is a qualification of the question, not an assertion for every property and every model. The original excerpt asks for the probability that a random knot satisfies a property arising from preceding open problems; those preceding properties are not identified in the supplied excerpt. The asymptotic formulation above follows the supplied cleaned statement and the excerpt's discussion of increasing grid size. Neither a particular property nor a complete probability model is fixed, so this is a research question parameterized by those choices, rather than a single specified numerical target.

Grid-model context and unresolved qualifications. The excerpt describes choosing a random grid diagram of size $n$ and studying the represented knot as $n\to\infty$. It states that every knot in $S^3$ has a grid representation and gives the finite count $G(n)=n!(n-1)!$, while describing every counted diagram as representing a knot. These are supplied source claims: the precise diagram-counting conventions, treatment of diagrams representing links with multiple components, admissible small sizes, and sampling distribution are not supplied. Consequently, this draft does not adopt that count as a definition of a fully specified probability model, assume uniform sampling, or identify probabilities on diagrams with uniform probabilities on knot types. The excerpt also reports computer experiments on invariants related to smooth sliceness using GridPyM; it supplies neither a precise sliceness probability question nor a limiting conclusion. Its references to contact topology, knot Floer homology, and monotonic simplification of unknot grid diagrams provide background, not additional hypotheses.

Short English statement

For a specified way of sampling knots of increasing size and a fixed knot property, determine the limiting probability of that property, if the limit exists. The supplied record leaves the property and probability model open. Its grid-diagram example requires further counting and sampling conventions before it defines a precise instance of the question.
