Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.78

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let \[ \operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})=\{A\in M_2({\mathbb{Q}}):\det A=1\}, \] with matrix multiplication. A group $G$ is simple if $G\ne\{I_2\}$ and every normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ satisfies $N=\{I_2\}$ or $N=G$. Simplicity is understood in the abstract group sense.

Question. Does there exist a subgroup $G\leq\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})$ such that $G$ is infinite and simple? Equivalently, is the following statement true? \[ \exists G\leq\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})\quad \left[|G|=\infty\ \land\ \forall N\trianglelefteq G,\ (N=\{I_2\}\ \lor\ N=G)\right]. \]

The malformed matrix-group notation in the supplied record is interpreted as $\operatorname{SL}_2({\mathbb{Q}})$. The source describes this as a special case of a question Serge Cantat asked J.-P. Serre about "non-algebraic" simple subgroups of $\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{C}})$, and also as a special case of Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.57. Neither the meaning of "non-algebraic" nor the statement of Problem 15.57 is supplied; these contextual references impose no additional specified condition on the displayed question.

Short English statement

Does the group of determinant-one $2\times2$ rational matrices contain an infinite subgroup with no nontrivial proper normal subgroup? The supplied registry labels this question open; that label has not been independently verified.
