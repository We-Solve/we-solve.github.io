Math-1582

Division-ample sets outside finitely many unit orbits

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field, $\mathcal O_K$ its ring of integers, $d=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]$, and $\mathcal O_K^\times$ its unit group. Use $N(a)=|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(a)|$ for the absolute field norm; the original writes $N(a)$ without specifying the sign convention. A subset $A\subseteq\mathcal O_K$ is division-ample if it is Diophantine over $\mathcal O_K$, every $x\in\mathcal O_K$ divides some $a\in A$, and \[ \exists\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\ \forall a\in A\ \exists\widetilde a\in{\mathbb{Z}}: \quad \widetilde a\mid a\text{ in }\mathcal O_K,\qquad N(a)\leq|\widetilde a|^\ell. \] Here Diophantine means existentially definable by finitely many polynomial equations over $\mathcal O_K$, with fixed coefficients permitted. The source notes that for $A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}$ the norm condition follows by taking $\widetilde a=a$ and $\ell=d$.

Question 31 (Cornelissen) asks for such an $A$ satisfying \[ \forall S\subseteq\mathcal O_K\text{ finite},\qquad A\not\subseteq\mathcal O_K^\times\cdot({\mathbb{Z}}\cup S), \] where the product consists of all $u z$ with $u$ a unit and $z\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cup S$. The source attributes to Cornelissen--Pheidas--Zahidi the implication that existence of a division-ample set, together with an elliptic curve of rank one over $K$, yields a negative answer to Hilbert's tenth problem over $\mathcal O_K$. That is a conditional consequence, not an assumption that every $K$ has such an elliptic curve.

The aid reports the following classification assuming that ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is Diophantine over $\mathcal O_K$. If $d>1$, choose $x_0\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\mathcal O_K^\times{\mathbb{Z}}$, effectively enumerate $\mathcal O_K=(b_j)_{j\geq0}$, and put \[ n_j=\max\{1,N(b_j)\}. \] Choose distinct increasing positive integers $m_j\geq\max\{1,N(x_0)\}$, and set \[ A=\{n_jb_j:j\geq0\}\ \cup\ \{m_jx_0:j\geq0\}. \] The aid asserts this is computably enumerable and therefore Diophantine under the stated ${\mathbb{Z}}$-definability hypothesis, using MRDP in integral coordinates. Divisibility density follows from the first family. For its elements take $\widetilde a=n_j$; for the second family take $\widetilde a=m_j$. Both satisfy the norm bound with $\ell=d+1$. The increasing norms of $m_jx_0$ give infinitely many distinct unit orbits, none meeting ${\mathbb{Z}}$, so no finite $S$ covers $A$ as above. An enumeration including $b_j=0$ includes $0\in A$, accounting also for the literal divisibility demand at $x=0$. When $K={\mathbb{Q}}$, the required nonconcentration is impossible because $\mathcal O_K={\mathbb{Z}}$ already.

The summary calls the ${\mathbb{Z}}$-definability hypothesis a now-proved theorem and describes this as a full post-2025 classification, but supplies no author, theorem statement, or authoritative citation for that decisive input. Accordingly the construction and classification are stated conditionally here; the supplied solved labels are not independent verification of that literature claim.

Short English statement

Cornelissen asks for a division-ample set that cannot be covered by integer unit orbits and finitely many additional unit orbits. The supplied two-family construction gives such a set in every proper number field, conditional on a Diophantine definition of the integers in its ring of integers. The rational field cannot satisfy the nonconcentration requirement; the claimed universal definability input needs an authoritative reference.
