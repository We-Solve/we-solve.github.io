Square-Root-Scale Cancellation in Short Möbius Sums

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $n$, define the Möbius function by \[ \mu(n)=\begin{cases} 1,&n=1,\\ (-1)^k,&n\text{ is a product of }k\text{ distinct primes},\\ 0,&n\text{ is divisible by the square of a prime}. \end{cases} \] For real $x>e$ and $y>0$, put \[ M(x,y)=\sum_{\substack{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\x<n\le x+y}}\mu(n). \] The supplied problem asks for cancellation in $M(x,y)$ at the square-root interval scale or below. It singles out the range \[ \frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log\log x)^{1/4}}\ll y\le x^{1-\epsilon}, \] where $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm. The lower-bound notation means that $\sqrt{x}/(\log\log x)^{1/4}\le K y$ for a positive constant $K$ independent of $x$ and $y$; its permitted dependence on other parameters is not specified.

Unresolved formulation. The record does not define the required strength of cancellation, the quantification of $\epsilon$, or the uniformity in $y$. A possible precise interpretation, presented for review rather than as an established transcription, is: for every fixed $0<\epsilon<1/2$ and every fixed $c>0$, \[ \lim_{x\to\infty}\ \sup_{\displaystyle c\sqrt{x}/(\log\log x)^{1/4}\le y\le x^{1-\epsilon}} \frac{|M(x,y)|}{y}=0. \] This interprets cancellation as a uniform $o(y)$ bound. The supplied evidence does not determine whether this interpretation, a quantitative saving, or an assertion restricted to most intervals is intended. The displayed restriction on $\epsilon$ makes this illustrative range nonempty for all sufficiently large $x$; it is not an explicit source hypothesis.

The record attributes the concrete range to a proposed fractional-moment bound but supplies neither that bound nor its hypotheses. Its original quotation describes an unspecified conjecture as plausibly attackable, at least under standard conjectures such as the Riemann Hypothesis, and ends before the claimed consequence of Perron's formula. Consequently, neither an unconditional target nor a precise conditional implication can be recovered from that quotation.

Short English statement

The problem seeks cancellation in sums of the Möbius function over short intervals, including lengths near $\sqrt{x}/(\log\log x)^{1/4}$. The supplied record leaves the strength, uniformity, and hypotheses of the desired estimate unresolved. The displayed uniform $o(y)$ statement is an explicitly provisional interpretation requiring human mathematical review.
