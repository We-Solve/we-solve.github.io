Math-6862

Compatible Feigin-Odesskii Poisson Brackets

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks about coprime integers $d,r$ with $r>0$ and a fixed Feigin--Odesskii Poisson bracket of type $(d,r)$. The notation $\mathbb P^{d-1}$ requires $d\geq 1$. The record does not specify the base field, the complete admissibility conditions for this type, or the construction and parameters of the fixed bracket.

The following makes the question precise under the explicitly provisional interpretation that the brackets are regular algebraic Poisson brackets. Let $k$ be the intended base field, let $(d,r)$ be an admissible pair satisfying the supplied conditions, and put $X=\mathbb P^{d-1}_k$. Suppose that the specified Feigin--Odesskii bracket $\pi_0$ of type $(d,r)$ on $X$ has been fixed. Write \[ B(X)=H^0\!\left(X,\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_X}\!\left(\bigwedge^2\Omega^1_{X/k},\mathcal O_X\right)\right). \] An element $\pi\in B(X)$ gives an alternating $k$-bilinear bracket $\{f,g\}_{\pi}$ on local regular functions, satisfying the Leibniz rule in each argument. It is a Poisson bracket if, on every open set and for all local regular functions $f,g,h$, \[ \{f,\{g,h\}_{\pi}\}_{\pi} +\{g,\{h,f\}_{\pi}\}_{\pi} +\{h,\{f,g\}_{\pi}\}_{\pi}=0. \] A $k$-linear subspace $V\subseteq B(X)$ is a space of compatible Poisson brackets if every element of $V$ is Poisson; equivalently, every finite $k$-linear combination of elements of $V$ is Poisson.

Determine \[ M_k(X,\pi_0)=\max\left\{\dim_k V: V\subseteq B(X)\text{ is a }k\text{-linear subspace},\quad \pi_0\in V,\quad \text{every }\pi\in V\text{ is Poisson}\right\}. \] The question is asked for each fixed bracket $\pi_0$; no independence from its parameters is assumed. The base field, admissible type conventions, and interpretation as regular algebraic brackets must be confirmed before this conditional formulation can be treated as a definitive statement of the source problem.

Short English statement

Given a fixed Feigin--Odesskii bracket of type $(d,r)$, with $r>0$ and $\gcd(d,r)=1$, determine the largest dimension of a vector space of Poisson brackets on $\mathbb P^{d-1}$ containing it, such that every linear combination is again Poisson. The base field, admissible types, and class of brackets remain source qualifications requiring review.
