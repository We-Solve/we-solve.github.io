Regular Conformal Equations for Matter with Nonzero Trace

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A conformal compactification writes the physical metric as $\widetilde g=\Omega^{-2}g$, with a conformal boundary at $\Omega=0$. The trace of a physical energy--momentum tensor is $\widetilde g^{ab}T_{ab}$. A regular conformal system is an equivalent system in rescaled metric, curvature and matter variables whose coefficients and unknowns do not acquire singular divisions by $\Omega$ at the boundary. Matter equations and their transformation law are essential parts of such a system.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For matter with a nonzero-trace energy-momentum tensor, does there exist a choice of conformal variables yielding a regular conformal field-equation system with matter?
Source scope and qualification.
The question is a construction programme across matter models. It does not say that one fixed rescaling must work for every imaginable nonzero-trace tensor; admissible matter equations must be specified as in the source.

Short English statement

Can conformal field equations remain regular at the conformal boundary when the matter stress tensor has nonzero trace?

A traceful scalar test on a simple compactification.
In four spacetime dimensions, take a flat unphysical metric $g$ and a positive conformal factor $\Omega$ on an interior region, with $\widetilde g=\Omega^{-2}g$. The scalar divergence formula gives \[ \widetilde\Box f=\Omega^2\Box_gf-2\Omega\,\nabla\Omega\cdot\nabla f, \] because $|\widetilde g|^{1/2}=\Omega^{-4}|g|^{1/2}$ and $\widetilde g^{ab}=\Omega^2g^{ab}$. For the massive equation $(\widetilde\Box-m^2)f=0$, division by $\Omega^2$ produces the coefficient $m^2\Omega^{-2}$.
Why a power rescaling alone cannot generally remove it.
Set $f=\Omega^s u$. After substitution and division by $\Omega^s$, the zeroth-order coefficient is \[ s\Omega^{-1}\Box_g\Omega+s(s-3)\Omega^{-2}|\nabla\Omega|_g^2-m^2\Omega^{-2}. \] If $\Omega$ is a flat null linear coordinate, then $\Box_g\Omega=0$ and $|\nabla\Omega|_g^2=0$. No choice of constant $s$ cancels the massive pole in this example. First-derivative coefficients also require attention; eliminating the mass term was already impossible by this restricted ansatz.
Full-source limit.
The test does not show that every formulation of massive or traceful matter is singular. More variables, differentiated equations, special asymptotics or model-dependent cancellations may change the analysis. It is also a matter equation on a fixed conformal background, not the fully coupled Einstein system. The first gap is an equivalent coupled variable choice with controlled boundary behavior for a specified admissible matter model. No universal no-go theorem for all nonzero-trace tensors is claimed.
