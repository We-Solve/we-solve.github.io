Competing First-Passage Percolation in Hostile Environments

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an infinite graph. The supplied statement refers to first-passage percolation in a hostile environment, with competing types $F_1$ and $F_\lambda$, parameter $\lambda$, and initial red-particle density $\mu$. It does not specify the stochastic dynamics or probability law, so these types and parameters cannot be fully defined from the supplied record alone.

Conditional on a specification of that process, let $V_1$ and $V_\lambda$ denote the sets of vertices ultimately occupied by the respective types. A type cluster means a connected component of the induced subgraph $G[V_i]$, for $i\in\{1,\lambda\}$. Define the events \[ \mathcal I_i=\{G[V_i]\text{ has an infinite connected component}\}, \qquad \mathcal C=\mathcal I_1\cap\mathcal I_\lambda. \] This expresses coexistence as the simultaneous existence of an infinite cluster of each type in the same realization; the interpretation of the types as ultimate occupied vertex sets requires confirmation from the source.

The first question is to determine the graph and parameter conditions under which $\mathcal I_\lambda$ occurs. The original formulation restricts attention to hyperbolic graphs, whereas the supplied clean statement asks this on bounded-degree graphs. These scopes are retained as an unresolved distinction.

The second question concerns $G={\mathbb{Z}}^d$: for every $\lambda\in(0,1)$, does there exist an interval $I_\lambda$ of admissible initial red-particle densities such that coexistence occurs for every $\mu\in I_\lambda$? If coexistence is intended to mean positive probability, the proposed assertion would be \[ \forall\lambda\in(0,1)\quad \exists I_\lambda\quad \forall\mu\in I_\lambda:\qquad \mathbb P_{{\mathbb{Z}}^d,\lambda,\mu}(\mathcal C)>0. \] Here the probability law is a placeholder for the missing process specification. The record does not determine whether positive probability or probability one is intended, which dimensions $d$ are included, or whether the interval must have positive length and exclude endpoint densities. Consequently, this display records a conditional interpretation of the conjecture, not a fully specified assertion.

Short English statement

Determine when the hostile-environment process produces an infinite $F_\lambda$ cluster, and whether, on ${\mathbb{Z}}^d$, every $\lambda\in(0,1)$ admits an interval of red-particle densities supporting infinite clusters of both types simultaneously. The process law, dimension range, interval requirements, probability quantifier, and graph scope remain insufficiently specified.
