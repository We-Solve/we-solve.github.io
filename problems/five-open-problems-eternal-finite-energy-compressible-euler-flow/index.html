Five Open Problems — Eternal finite-energy compressible Euler flow

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An eternal flow is defined for every $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$, not merely for all forward times. The source asks whether compressible Euler in odd spatial dimension has a nontrivial smooth eternal solution with finite nonzero total mass and energy. It does not specify the equation of state or whether the full or isentropic system is intended.

For a full nonisentropic template on ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, density $\rho$, velocity $u$, specific internal energy $e$, pressure $p$, and total energy density $E=\rho e+\tfrac12\rho|u|^2$ satisfy \[ \partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0, \] \[ \partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0, \qquad \partial_tE+\nabla\cdot((E+p)u)=0, \] with a specified constitutive relation such as $p=p(\rho,e)$. An isentropic interpretation instead prescribes $p=p(\rho)$ and the corresponding internal-energy law. No particular law, exponent, or entropy condition is imposed by the supplied extract, so these equations are explanatory alternatives rather than a selected source model.

Under a whole-space, nonnegative-energy interpretation, the desired finiteness is \[ 0<M(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}\rho(x,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x<\infty, \qquad 0<\mathcal E(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}E(x,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x<\infty \quad(t\in{\mathbb{R}}). \] To require these quantities to be conserved also needs suitable integrability and flux-at-infinity conditions. The source does not state whether it means absolute or relative energy, the precise smooth variables at vacuum, strict positivity versus allowable vacuum, or the decay class. The finite absolute mass setting is distinct from perturbations of a nonzero constant density on all of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$.

The intended existence quantifier concerns an odd integer $d$ and a flow nontrivial in the source's sense. The extract does not specify a fixed odd $d$, all odd $d$, or whether $d=1$ is excluded, nor whether nontrivial means simply nonvacuum or excludes stationary or other special flows. These choices, together with the constitutive law, prevent a single fully specified existence theorem from being inferred.

The supplied summary reports no construction or impossibility proof in the intended odd-dimensional setting. It mentions local smooth theory, constant-background small-data and decay work labeled Klainerman--Majda-type and Christodoulou, and a one-dimensional entropy/rarefaction obstruction, but supplies no exact hypotheses or references and ends mid-sentence. Such local or different-background statements do not themselves address smooth existence for all positive and negative times with finite absolute mass; decay alone is not a definition of non-eternality. They remain unverified attributed context rather than established obstructions for the unspecified target problem.

Short English statement

Can an odd-dimensional compressible Euler flow remain smooth for all past and future time while carrying finite positive total mass and energy? The source leaves the constitutive law, odd-dimension quantifier, vacuum regularity, decay, and meaning of nontrivial unspecified. Its summary reports the intended problem unresolved, with incomplete contextual claims that do not define a precise model.
