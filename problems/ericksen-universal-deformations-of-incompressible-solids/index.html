Ericksen Universal Deformations of Incompressible Solids

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

For a deformation $y$, write $F=\nabla y$ and $B=FF^T$. Incompressibility requires $\det F=1$. The principal invariants are $I_1=\operatorname{tr}B$, $I_2=\tfrac12[(\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\operatorname{tr}(B^2)]$ and $I_3=\det B$. A universal deformation satisfies equilibrium for every constitutive law in the stated homogeneous isotropic incompressible class, with an appropriate pressure multiplier.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Classify all constant-principal-invariant universal deformations of homogeneous incompressible isotropic solids.
Scope and source qualification.
Constant principal invariants do not mean that $F$ itself is spatially constant. The target includes possible nonaffine deformations.

Short English statement

Classify every universal deformation whose principal invariants remain constant throughout the incompressible isotropic solid.

A differential reduction at constant invariants.
Restrict this test to a smooth three-dimensional incompressible deformation with spatial left Cauchy--Green tensor $B$, on a simply connected current domain. For the usual isotropic energy $W(I_1,I_2)$, the Cauchy stress has the form $T=-pI+2W_1B-2W_2B^{-1}$, where $W_i$ are derivatives of $W$; an isotropic term is absorbed in the pressure $p$. If both invariants are constant, $W_1,W_2$ are constants in space. Thus the equilibrium equation becomes \[ \nabla p=2W_1\operatorname{div}B-2W_2\operatorname{div}B^{-1}. \] Taking the two admissible test energies $W=I_1$ and $W=I_2$ shows that universality requires both vector fields $\operatorname{div}B$ and $\operatorname{div}B^{-1}$ to be gradients.
Converse within this smooth setting.
If $\operatorname{div}B=\nabla q$ and $\operatorname{div}B^{-1}=\nabla r$, then choosing $p=2W_1q-2W_2r$ proves equilibrium for every such $W$. Equivalently, in a simply connected domain the two curls must vanish: integrating a curl-free field along paths defines a potential because integrals around contractible loops vanish by Stokes' formula. Affine maps $y(X)=FX+c$ with $\det F=1$ pass the test because both divergences vanish.
Why classification remains.
The reduction does not classify compatible fields $B=\nabla y(\nabla y)^T$ with constant eigenvalues. Rotating their eigendirections arbitrarily generally destroys deformation compatibility or the two gradient conditions. On multiply connected domains vanishing curl also needs zero periods. The full source target includes nonaffine deformations and appropriate domains; solving this nonlinear compatibility system is the first unproved step, so the differential reformulation is not presented as a new classification.
