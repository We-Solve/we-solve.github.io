Math-310

Elementary equivalence versus isomorphism for finitely generated fields

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A field $K$ is finitely generated if it is generated as a field by finitely many elements over its prime field, ${\mathbb{Q}}$ or ${\mathbb{F}}_p$. Write $K\equiv L$ for elementary equivalence in the pure ring language without named generators or additional parameters, and $K\cong L$ for field isomorphism.

Question 10, Florian Pop. For finitely generated fields $K,L$, does \[ K\equiv L\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\cong L? \] Both fields are required to be finitely generated; this is not categoricity among all fields or a decision procedure for their theories.

Supplied Dittmann--Pop scope. The aid reports single-sentence characterizations among finitely generated fields: for each $K$ in the stated range there is a sentence $\varphi_K$ such that a finitely generated $L$ satisfies it precisely when $L\cong K$. It consequently reports an affirmative answer unconditionally if $\operatorname{char}K\ne2$, and also in characteristic two when the absolute transcendence degree over ${\mathbb{F}}_2$ is at most three. In characteristic two and transcendence degree greater than three, it reports the conclusion only under the authors' stated resolution-of-singularities hypothesis above ${\mathbb{F}}_2$. The exact form of that hypothesis and a proving citation are not supplied; no unconditional resolution of this residual range is asserted. The characterizing sentence may depend on $K$; no effective uniform construction or decidability follows merely from its existence.

Supplied positive-characteristic invariant package. For a finitely generated field $K$ of characteristic $p>0$, put $d=\operatorname{trdeg}_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}K$ and $K^p=\{x^p:x\in K\}$. Then $[K:K^p]=p^d$. A first-order sentence expressing a $p$-basis of length $d$ is \[ \exists b_1\cdots b_d\ \forall x\ \exists!\,(c_e)_{e\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^d} \quad x=\sum_{e\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^d} c_e^p\prod_{j=1}^db_j^{e_j}. \] For each fixed $p,d$ this is a finite formula; $d=0$ gives the perfect-field condition. The aid uses this to recover $d$ from the theory.

The relative algebraic closure of ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ in $K$ is a finite field $k_K={\mathbb{F}}_{p^r}$ for some $r\ge1$. For every $n\ge1$, the number of roots in $K$ of $X^{p^n}-X$ is \[ p^{\gcd(r,n)}. \] Each finite root count is first-order expressible, and their full sequence determines $r$. Thus the aid's explicit elementary-equivalence consequences are \[ \operatorname{trdeg}_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}K=\operatorname{trdeg}_{{\mathbb{F}}_p}L, \qquad k_K\cong k_L. \] These invariants do not by themselves determine a general function field up to isomorphism and do not replace the scope-qualified characterization theorem.

Short English statement

Pop asks whether finitely generated fields with the same first-order theory must be isomorphic. The aid reports an affirmative characterization theorem outside a remaining conditional high-transcendence-degree characteristic-two range. Its explicit partial calculation recovers transcendence degree and the finite constant field in positive characteristic, without claiming those invariants alone determine the field.
