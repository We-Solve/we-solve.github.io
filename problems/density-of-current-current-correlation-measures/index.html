Density of Current–Current Correlation Measures

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
$H_\omega$ is the report's covariant random Schrödinger operator, $E_{H_\omega}(B)$ its spectral projection on a Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb E$ expectation over the disorder. The lattice vector $|0\rangle$ is localized at the origin. The current operator $\nabla_iH_\omega$ is the derivation in position direction $i$ (with the convention fixed by the source). The summed diagonal current--current correlation defines a positive measure on the energy plane. Having a density means absolute continuity: $dm(E,E')=m(E,E')\,dE\,dE'$.
Source scope and qualifications.
The trace-per-unit-volume and spectral-product convention of the report is essential to the measure definition. The source proves analytic densities off a disorder-dependent neighborhood of the energy diagonal for a particular analytic single-site distribution; the question includes the diagonal and broader source setting.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the random Schrödinger operator $H_\omega$, let the current–current correlation measures be $$dm_{ij}(E,E')=\mathbb E\!\left[\langle0|\nabla_iH_\omega\,E_{H_\omega}(dE)\,\nabla_jH_\omega\,E_{H_\omega}(dE')|0\rangle\right],$$ and put $dm=\sum_i dm_{ii}$. Does the positive measure $dm$ have a density $m(E,E')$ with respect to Lebesgue measure?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The general density question is not settled, but Combes, Germinet, and Hislop prove density at coincident energies in a localization regime, with vanishing-rate bounds. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Determine whether the current--current energy correlation measure of the random Schrödinger model is absolutely continuous.

Why regular marginals are insufficient.
Let $\mu$ on $[0,1]^2$ be the pushforward of Lebesgue measure under $t\mapsto(t,t)$. Both marginals have density one, but $\mu$ gives mass one to the diagonal, whose two-dimensional Lebesgue measure is zero. Thus even sharp one-energy spectral regularity cannot alone imply a two-energy density.
The operator version of the same trap.
On $L^2([0,1])$ let $H$ multiply by $t$, let $J=I$, and use the vector state $\tau(B)=\langle1,B1\rangle$ on the commutative multiplication algebra. For Borel sets $B,C$, \[ \tau(J1_B(H)J1_C(H))=|B\cap C|. \] This reproduces $\mu(B\times C)$. However $J$ is not the current $i[H,X]$ of a random Schrödinger operator, so it is not a counterexample to the source question.
A current-specific check.
In finite dimension, if $Hv_a=E_av_a$ and $J=i[H,X]$, then $\langle v_a,Jv_b\rangle=i(E_a-E_b)\langle v_a,Xv_b\rangle$. Current matrix elements vanish within exactly degenerate energy spaces, eliminating the preceding simple diagonal mechanism. This does not prove absolute continuity: finite systems still yield point masses off the diagonal, and infinite-volume position operators are unbounded. The first missing estimate is joint two-energy regularity, uniform in volume and strong enough near $E=E'$ to survive the trace-per-volume limit. The source's off-diagonal results cannot be extended by marginal regularity alone.
