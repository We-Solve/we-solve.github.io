Math-230

Canonical indices, crepant centres, and an exact quotient-family calibration

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the intended characteristic-zero setting, let $x\in X$ be a point on a normal projective $n$-fold with $K_X$ ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier near $x$. Define its local canonical index by \[\operatorname{ind}_x(K_X)=\min\{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:rK_X\text{ is Cartier near }x\}.\] For a divisor $E$ over $X$, write $A_X(E)=1+\operatorname{coeff}_E(K_Y-f^*K_X)$; the exact-center invariant is $\operatorname{mld}_x(X,0)=\inf_{c_X(E)=x}A_X(E)$. Canonical and terminal singularities have respectively $A_X(E)\ge1$ and $A_X(E)>1$ for exceptional divisors. A point that is itself a crepant center is the center of a divisor of log discrepancy one.

Original questions (Shokurov). For fixed $n$, is the local index bounded for projective varieties with an "exact canonical" singularity of dimension $n$? In particular, what about $\dim X=3$? More generally, for fixed dimension $n$ and a fixed value $a$ of the exact-center minimal log discrepancy, is there a bound $R(n,a)$ such that \[\operatorname{mld}_x(X,0)=a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{ind}_x(K_X)\le R(n,a)\] for every point in the intended class?

The phrase "exact canonical" is undefined in the original. The research aid says it is a typo for "strictly canonical" in an earlier AIM draft, and interprets the meaningful local case as a canonical point that is itself a crepant center, equivalently exact-center log mld $1$. Being nonterminal somewhere in a neighborhood does not by itself specify that center. The earlier wording and intended equivalence have not been independently source-verified. The characteristic, precise singularity class for the general fixed-mld question, and point-versus-subvariety center conventions remain unstated.

Attached unreviewed research-aid account. The aid attributes to Kawakita the sharp index bound $6$ in dimension three for the indicated log-mld-one canonical interpretation, and fixed-mld index boundedness for canonical threefolds. It reports the general-dimensional conjecture as open and the quotient case as known, without exact hypotheses or citations.

It additionally gives a varying-mld family: for every $n\ge3$ and every integer $r>n-2$ with $\gcd(r,n-2)=1$, consider, over the intended characteristic-zero field containing the $r$th roots of unity, \[X_{n,r}={\mathbb{P}}^n/\mu_r(0,1^{n-1},r-(n-2)).\] Here the weights specify the diagonal action on homogeneous coordinates; the chart corresponding to the weight-zero coordinate contains the germ at the origin of \[U_{n,r}={\mathbb{A}}^n/\mu_r(1^{n-1},r-(n-2)).\] The report claims this germ is isolated terminal and \[\operatorname{mld}_0(U_{n,r})=1+\frac1r,\qquad\operatorname{ind}_0(K_{U_{n,r}})=r,\] with smooth index-one cover ${\mathbb{A}}^n$ of degree $r$. Its claimed calibration is \[\deg(\text{index-one cover})=\operatorname{ind}_0(K_{U_{n,r}})=\frac1{\operatorname{mld}_0(U_{n,r})-1}=r.\] Consequently any fixed-mld bounding function would satisfy $R(n,1+1/r)\ge r$ along these admissible parameters. The mld varies with $r$; this family concerns dependence of a bound on $a$, rather than being a counterexample with one fixed mld value or with log mld exactly one. All family and literature assertions are supplied research-aid claims requiring review.

Short English statement

Shokurov asks for local canonical-index bounds at a fixed dimension, first at an ambiguously named canonical singularity and then at fixed mld. The aid interprets the first as an exact-center mld-one question, reports threefold results, and supplies terminal quotient germs whose indices grow while their mlds approach one from above.
