Math-6741

Fiber Genus of Rational Maps from Newton Hypersurfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source question. Let $\Delta\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^n$ be a Newton polyhedron and let $H$ be a generic hypersurface with Newton polyhedron $\Delta$. Given a dominant rational map \[ f:H\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-2}, \] determine the genus of its generic fiber. The target requires an integer $n\geq 2$, although this restriction is not explicit in the source. The map $f$ is part of the given data; the record does not specify a particular map or assert that the genus depends only on $\Delta$.

Newton data. For a Laurent polynomial $P=\sum_{a\in A}c_a t_1^{a_1}\cdots t_n^{a_n}$, with finite support $A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^n$ and nonzero coefficients $c_a$, its Newton polytope is $\operatorname{conv}(A)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$. The source instead calls $\Delta\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^n$ a Newton polyhedron. It does not clarify whether this denotes the support, a lattice polytope, or another polyhedral convention. It also leaves the ground field, ambient variety, coefficient family, and meaning of generic unspecified.

Conditional interpretation of genus. If the intended setting is an integral hypersurface $H$ of dimension $n-1$ over a field $k$, set \[ K=k({\mathbb{P}}_k^{n-2}),\qquad \eta=\operatorname{Spec}K. \] On a dense open subset $U\subset H$ where $f$ is a morphism, its generic fiber is $U_\eta=U\times_{{\mathbb{P}}_k^{n-2}}\eta$, a curve over $K$. If this curve is geometrically integral, one possible intended invariant is the geometric genus \[ g(H,f)=\dim_{\overline K}H^1(\widetilde C,\mathcal O_{\widetilde C}), \] where $\widetilde C$ is the smooth projective model of $U_\eta\times_K\overline K$. Under this interpretation, the task is to determine $g(H,f)$ from the specified hypersurface and rational map.

These are conditional definitions, not additional hypotheses supplied by the source. Dominance alone does not ensure geometric integrality of the generic fiber. The record does not specify geometric versus arithmetic genus, a compactification, or a convention for geometrically reducible or nonreduced fibers. Its notation $f^{-1}(x)$, with $x\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n-2}$, also leaves unresolved whether it means the fiber over the generic point or a fiber over a sufficiently general geometric point. Consequently, the supplied question requires these qualifications before it has a unique precise formulation.

Short English statement

Given a generic hypersurface with prescribed Newton data and a dominant rational map to ${\mathbb{P}}^{n-2}$, determine the genus of the generic curve fiber. The field, Newton convention, meaning of generic, and intended genus require clarification. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
