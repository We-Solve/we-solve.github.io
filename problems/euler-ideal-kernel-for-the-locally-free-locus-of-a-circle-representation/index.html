Euler-ideal kernel for the locally-free locus of a circle representation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

R. Goldin's Question 7.3 asks, for a previously specified $G$-action on $V={\mathbb{C}}^n$ and a previously defined $X$, whether \[ H_G^*(V)\longrightarrow H^*(X/G)=H_G^*(X) \] is surjective, and asks for its kernel. The preceding definitions are absent. The aid reports an internal ambiguity between a locally-free locus before quotienting and its complexified quotient; the displayed equivariant restriction supports the former interpretation, which is adopted only explicitly and conditionally here: \[ X=V_{\mathrm{lf}}=\{v\in V:G_v\text{ is finite}\}. \] For a compact group the linear contraction gives $H_G^*(V;R)\cong H^*(BG;R)$. The Borel target is $H_G^*(X;R)=H^*(EG\times_GX;R)$. Its identification with ordinary cohomology of the coarse quotient is coefficient sensitive for finite stabilizers and cannot simply be assumed integrally.

The aid's specific result concerns every diagonal circle representation \[ V={\mathbb{C}}^{n_0}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^m{\mathbb{C}}_{w_i},\qquad n_0\ge0,\quad w_i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}, \] where $z\in S^1$ acts on ${\mathbb{C}}_{w_i}$ by multiplication by $z^{w_i}$ and acts trivially on ${\mathbb{C}}^{n_0}$. If $m=0$, the locally-free locus is empty. If $m\ge1$, it is \[ X={\mathbb{C}}^{n_0}\times({\mathbb{C}}^m\setminus\{0\}). \] Writing $H^*(BS^1;{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}[u]$, $\deg u=2$, and using the complex orientation, the moving equivariant Euler class is \[ e=\left(\prod_{i=1}^m w_i\right)u^m. \] The reported restriction is surjective over both ${\mathbb{Z}}$ and ${\mathbb{Q}}$, with \[ H_{S^1}^*(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[u]/(e),\qquad \ker\left({\mathbb{Z}}[u]\to H_{S^1}^*(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\right)=(e), \] and \[ H_{S^1}^*(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Q}}[u]/(u^m),\qquad \ker\left({\mathbb{Q}}[u]\to H_{S^1}^*(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\right)=(u^m). \] No effectiveness or primitivity of the weights is assumed. If the nonzero moving coordinates have index set $I$, the stabilizer has order $\gcd_{i\in I}|w_i|$; these are finite even when nontrivial. Rationally the usual locally-free comparison gives the coarse-quotient cohomology, but the displayed integral formula is for the Borel target. Already a single moving weight with $|w_1|>1$ gives finite-isotropy torsion in the Borel target absent from the contractible radial coarse quotient. For $m=0$, restriction to the empty set has zero target and kernel the entire coefficient polynomial ring, rather than the nonempty-locus formula.

M. Libine's Comment 7.4 recommends beginning with irreducible representations and warns that $X$ may be empty without further action hypotheses. The broad arbitrary-group question is not settled by this circle result; the aid says it remained unresolved in the literature it checked. The final Higgs-bundles heading starts the next section.

Short English statement

The aid answers the restriction-and-kernel question for the locally-free locus of any diagonal circle representation. The kernel is the moving Euler ideal, with finite-isotropy torsion retained integrally. The original definition of the target is missing and ambiguous, and rational coarse-quotient cohomology must be distinguished from integral Borel cohomology.
