Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A PDE-constrained variational problem optimizes a functional over functions satisfying a partial differential equation. Finite elements approximate those functions in finite-dimensional spaces; moment-SOS relaxations then replace finite polynomial constraints by increasingly strong positivity conditions.

A polynomial is a sum of squares (SOS) if it is $\sum_j q_j^2$ for polynomials $q_j$. Moment-SOS hierarchies impose positivity through increasingly large truncated moment matrices and corresponding degree-bounded SOS certificates.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Extend the convergence analysis of finite-element moment-sum-of-squares relaxations beyond variational problems with a unique global minimizer and boundary constraints, to variational problems subject to partial differential equation constraints. [S2]

The primary passage distinguishes discretization followed by moment relaxation from certified lower-bound approaches. It reports convergence to a minimizer under uniqueness and only boundary constraints, then asks about nonuniqueness and further PDE constraints. This attempt tests whether optimal relaxed moments can simply be averaged to remove uniqueness.

A PDE-constrained example with exact moments and failed mean extraction

On $(0,1)$ take $u\in H^1(0,1)$, weak constraint $u'=0$ and $|u|\le1$ almost everywhere, with no imposed boundary values, and minimize \[ F(u)=\int_0^1(u(x)^2-1)^2\,dx. \] The derivative constraint implies $u=c$ almost everywhere: an $H^1$ function has an absolutely continuous representative with $u(x)-u(0)=\int_0^xu'$. Thus the problem is exactly $\min_{-1\le c\le1}(c^2-1)^2$, with two minimizers $c=\pm1$ and optimum zero. Constant finite elements already represent both solutions, so no mesh error is involved.
Proposition: The order-two moment relaxation has an optimal feasible moment vector whose first-moment field is feasible but has objective one, strictly above the true optimum.
Proof: Set $(y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)=(1,0,1,0,1)$, the moments of $(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)/2$. Its moment and localizing matrices are \[ M_2(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq0, \qquad M_1((1-c^2)y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\succeq0. \] Indeed the first quadratic form is $(v_0+v_2)^2+v_1^2$. The relaxation minimizes $y_4-2y_2+y_0$, which is nonnegative for every feasible moment matrix because it is the moment functional applied to the square $(c^2-1)^2$. Our vector has value zero and is optimal. Its first moment is zero, so the mean reconstruction is $u(x)=0$, satisfying the PDE and bound but yielding $F(u)=1$. ∎
The same measure supplies compatible optimal moments at every higher order, so raising relaxation order alone does not cure this particular extraction rule. On the other hand, selecting either support atom gives a true minimizer. The obstruction is specifically to replacing a measure supported on minimizers by its barycenter for a nonconvex objective.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact rational checks verify the two matrices, the zero relaxed objective and the reconstructed objective one. This does not refute convergence in value, convergence to measures supported on minimizers, or a valid atom-selection theorem. General PDE discretization, compactness, relaxation exactness and a justified extraction rule without uniqueness are all still missing.
