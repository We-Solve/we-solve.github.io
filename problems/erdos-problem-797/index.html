Erdős Problem 797: acyclic colouring in subquadratic size

Statement and claim.
Every graph of maximum degree $d\geq1$ has a proper colouring with at most $\lceil100d^{4/3}\rceil$ colours in which no cycle uses only two colours. In particular $f(d)=o(d^2)$. We reconstruct the four-event argument of Alon, McDiarmid, and Reed, Acyclic coloring of graphs, Section 2: https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/Publications2/Acyclic%20colouring%20of%20graphs.pdf. See https://www.erdosproblems.com/797. The probabilistic external input is the asymmetric Lovász local lemma.
The random colouring and the forbidden events.
Put $q=\lceil100d^{4/3}\rceil$, and independently give each vertex a uniform colour from $[q]$. Call a nonadjacent pair special if it has more than $d^{2/3}$ common neighbours. Consider these four types of bad events:
  1. the ends of an edge receive the same colour;
  2. on a simple path $v_0v_1v_2v_3v_4$, the colours satisfy $c(v_0)=c(v_2)=c(v_4)$ and $c(v_1)=c(v_3)$;
  3. on a chordless four-cycle with neither opposite pair special, the colours agree on both opposite pairs;
  4. a special pair receives the same colour.
Their probabilities are, respectively, $q^{-1},q^{-3},q^{-2},q^{-1}$. Paths here need not be induced; this only makes avoidance a stronger sufficient condition.
How many events involve a given vertex?
A vertex belongs to at most $d$ edges and at most $5d^4$ of the specified path events, by choosing its position in an oriented path and then the four steps. There are at most $d^2$ length-two walks from a fixed vertex. Each special other endpoint consumes more than $d^{2/3}$ such walks, so there are at most $d^{4/3}$ special pairs containing it. Finally choose the first two steps of a four-cycle through the vertex, in at most $d^2$ ways. Its opposite vertices are nonadjacent. If that pair is not special, there are at most $d^{2/3}$ choices for the fourth vertex. Thus at most $d^{8/3}$ events of the third type contain the fixed vertex.
Checking the local lemma quantitatively.
Join two bad events in the dependency graph whenever they share a vertex. Each is independent of the entire collection of nonneighbours, since their colour variables are disjoint. Give each event the weight twice its probability. All weights are less than one. An event depends on at most five vertex colours, so the sum of its neighbours' weights is at most \[ 5\left(\frac{2d}{q} +\frac{10d^4}{q^3} +\frac{2d^{8/3}}{q^2} +\frac{2d^{4/3}}q\right) \leq \frac{20}{100}+\frac{50}{100^3}+\frac{10}{100^2} <\frac12. \] For numbers $0\leq x_i\leq1$, $\prod_i(1-x_i)\geq1-\sum_i x_i$. Therefore, for every bad event $E$, \[ \Pr(E)\leq (2\Pr(E)) \prod_{F\sim E}(1-2\Pr(F)). \] These are the hypotheses of the asymmetric local lemma, which gives a colouring avoiding every bad event.
Why the resulting colouring is acyclic.
Avoiding the first type makes it proper, so a two-coloured cycle would have even length. A two-coloured four-cycle has no diagonal, since such an edge would join equal colours in a proper colouring. It either has a special opposite pair, violating the fourth condition, or violates the third. A two-coloured cycle of length at least six contains five consecutive distinct vertices forming a path of length four, violating the second condition. Thus no two-coloured cycle remains. When $d=0$, one colour suffices.
Size and scope.
The bound just proved implies \[ 0\leq f(d)/d^2\leq100d^{-2/3}+d^{-2}\longrightarrow0. \] For context, Theorem 1.2 of the same primary paper establishes the stronger lower estimate $f(d)\gg d^{4/3}/(\log d)^{1/3}$. That lower theorem is an explicitly external additional estimate; it is not needed for our complete proof of the subquadratic assertion.
