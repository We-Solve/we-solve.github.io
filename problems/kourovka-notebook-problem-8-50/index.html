Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.50

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, let $G'=[G,G]$ and $G''=[G',G']$, where $[H,H]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in H$. The group $G$ is metabelian if $G''=\{1\}$, equivalently if $G'$ is abelian. It is finitely generated if some finite subset generates it as a group. The integral group ring ${\mathbb{Z}} G$ consists of finite formal sums $\sum_{g\in G}a_g g$, with $a_g\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, with multiplication extending the group operation distributively.

Use the convention of unital left ${\mathbb{Z}} G$-modules. A module $V$ is cyclic if $V=({\mathbb{Z}} G)v$ for some $v\in V$, and is nonzero if $V\ne\{0\}$. Equip $V\oplus V$ with the componentwise ${\mathbb{Z}} G$-action. The supplied source does not specify left or right modules; inversion in $G$ gives an anti-automorphism of ${\mathbb{Z}} G$, so the corresponding existence questions for left and right modules are equivalent.

Question (P. M. Neumann). Does there exist a finitely generated metabelian group $G$ and a nonzero cyclic ${\mathbb{Z}} G$-module $V$ such that \[ V\cong V\oplus V \qquad\text{as }{\mathbb{Z}} G\text{-modules}? \] Neumann's stated conjecture is that no such pair exists. Equivalently, the conjecture is \[ \forall G\;\forall V,\qquad \left. \begin{gathered} G\text{ is finitely generated},\qquad G''=\{1\},\\ V\text{ is a nonzero cyclic }{\mathbb{Z}} G\text{-module} \end{gathered} \right\} \Longrightarrow V\not\cong V\oplus V. \]

As motivation, the supplied source reports that Neumann exhibited at an Oberwolfach conference in 1979 a finitely generated soluble group of derived length exactly $3$, together with a nonzero cyclic integral group-ring module isomorphic to its direct sum with itself. Here derived length exactly $3$ means $G^{(3)}=\{1\}$ and $G^{(2)}\ne\{1\}$, with $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]$. This construction is supplied background, not independently verified here. The registry labels the metabelian question open; that label is not an independent assessment of its present status.

Short English statement

Neumann asks whether a finitely generated metabelian group can have a nonzero cyclic integral group-ring module isomorphic, as a module, to two copies of itself. He conjectures that this is impossible. The supplied source reports an example for a finitely generated soluble group of derived length $3$.
