Polyhedral Cones of Graph Profile Inequality Exponents

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $U$ be a finite collection of connected graphs. The supplied source asks about the graph profile $G_U$ and the exponents of binomial inequalities valid on $G_U$. Denote the intended set of exponents by $E_U$.

For a specified real coordinate space ${\mathbb{R}}^d$, a set $C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ is a polyhedral cone if there exist a nonnegative integer $r$ and a matrix $A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r\times d}$ such that \[ C=\{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:Av\geq 0\}, \] where the inequality is componentwise. It is a rational polyhedral cone if such a matrix can be chosen in ${\mathbb{Q}}^{r\times d}$.

Source question, subject to the unresolved definitions below. For every finite collection $U$ of connected graphs, is $E_U$ a polyhedral cone? If so, must $E_U$ be a rational polyhedral cone in its intended exponent coordinates?

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not define $G_U$, the class of graphs used to construct it, the normalization of its coordinates, or whether a closure is taken. It also does not specify the allowed binomial inequalities, their coefficients and exponent domains, or how their exponents are represented as vectors. In particular, it does not say whether an exponent vector records two monomial exponent lists or their difference. The ambient dimension and coordinate convention for $E_U$ therefore remain unspecified. If zero coordinates occur in the profile, the admissibility and evaluation of monomials at those coordinates must also be specified. These omissions prevent an unconditional precise construction of $E_U$ from this record; the question above preserves the supplied target without selecting those missing conventions.

The adjacent extracted discussion concerns sum-of-squares profiles and is truncated. It does not provide the missing definition of the true profile $G_U$ or establish an identification of its valid binomial inequalities with those of a sum-of-squares profile.

Short English statement

The source asks whether, for every finite collection of connected graphs, the exponents of all binomial inequalities valid on its graph profile form a polyhedral cone, and whether that cone must be rational polyhedral. The supplied record omits the profile and exponent conventions needed to make this fully precise. Its open status is supplied dated curation.
