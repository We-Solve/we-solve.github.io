Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 5. What is the algebro-geometric structure of passing from local to global odds ratios? Note that local odds ratio is obtained by looking at a 2 k subtable of a given table whereas a global odds ratio is obtained by looking at the odds-ratio in a 2 k collapsing of a given table.

Attempt and results

The source asks for a family of geometric descriptions, without fixing the table sizes, a selection of local ratios, or a chosen collapse. We work first with a positive two-way table and a specified dichotomization; this restriction is not identified with the entire source question.
A precise weighted-average identity.
Partition row indices into nonempty $I,I^c$ and column indices into nonempty $J,J^c$. The collapsed odds ratio is \[ \Theta=\frac{(\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}p_{ij}) (\sum_{k\in I^c,l\in J^c}p_{kl})} {(\sum_{i\in I,l\in J^c}p_{il}) (\sum_{k\in I^c,j\in J}p_{kj})}. \] For the same quadruples put $\theta_{ik;jl}=p_{ij}p_{kl}/(p_{il}p_{kj})$ and $w_{ik;jl}=p_{il}p_{kj}/\sum_{i,j,k,l}p_{il}p_{kj}$. Expanding numerator and denominator proves $\Theta=\sum w_{ik;jl}\theta_{ik;jl}$, with all $w>0$ and $\sum w=1$. Consequently $\Theta$ is strictly between the smallest and largest of these local ratios unless all of them agree. In particular, if all the relevant cross-block ratios agree, the collapse preserves their value. The weights depend on the actual table, not only on a list of local ratios.
A complete small positive fiber image.
For a three-row, two-column table, collapse rows one and two against row three. Fix their two ratios against row three at $\theta_1,\theta_2>0$. Writing row $i$ as $(a_i,b_i)$ gives $a_i=\theta_i b_i a_3/b_3$ for $i=1,2$, and hence \[ \Theta=\frac{b_1\theta_1+b_2\theta_2}{b_1+b_2}. \] Every $b_1/b_2>0$ is permitted: choose positive unnormalized entries with these relations and divide all entries by their total. The positive image is therefore the open interval between $\theta_1,\theta_2$, or their common singleton. This is an exact image calculation for this particular fiber.
Why conditional independence is not preserved by marginalization.
One proposed simplification is to replace every local ratio constraint by the same ratio after summing another factor. Consider the positive two layers \[ P_0=\begin{pmatrix}9&3\\3&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\3&9\end{pmatrix}. \] Each has odds ratio one. Normalize the whole three-way table by its total $32$; this leaves every ratio unchanged. Its sum over the layer factor is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}10&6\\6&10\end{smallmatrix}\right)/32$, whose odds ratio is $25/9$. Thus within-layer independence does not imply marginal independence. This operation differs from selecting all cross-block ratios in the first identity; confusing the two is exactly the failed step.
