Erdős Problem 197: a complete three-part construction and a two-part obstruction

1. Statement and scope

Can $\mathbb N$ be partitioned into two sets, each of which has an enumeration avoiding monotone three-term arithmetic progressions? The two-part question remains unresolved. We reconstruct the known three-part construction in full and prove a necessary condition that excludes periodic candidate partitions. Contrary to the earlier draft, the three-part version is established, not open.

2. Finite blocks can always be ordered safely

Every finite integer set can be ordered to avoid monotone triples. Split it into its even and odd classes, place one class before the other, and recursively apply the same procedure after scaling each class down by two and translating if needed. A three-term progression of odd difference has its endpoints in one class and its middle in the other, so its middle cannot occur between its endpoints. For even difference, all three terms lie in one class and division by two reduces the difference. Repeating reaches the odd case. This proves avoidance, and also gives a terminating construction by induction on the diameter of the finite set.

3. Three classes formed from consecutive intervals

Partition $\mathbb N$ into consecutive intervals $I_1,I_2,\ldots$ of lengths \[ L_1=100,\qquad L_{j+1}=\lfloor3L_j/2\rfloor. \] Put $b_j=L_1+\cdots+L_j$, so $I_j=[b_{j-1}+1,b_j]$, with $b_0=0$. Since all lengths are at least $100$, \[ \frac{149}{100}L_j\le L_{j+1}\le\frac32L_j.\tag{1} \] For each residue class of $j$ modulo three, take the union of the corresponding intervals, and enumerate its intervals in increasing index order, using within each interval an avoiding order from Section 2. Each class is infinite and every element occurs exactly once at a finite position.

We prove each enumeration avoids a monotone triple. A decreasing progression crossing distinct blocks is impossible, since every value in a later block is larger than every value in an earlier one. A triple lying entirely inside a block is excluded by construction. It remains to exclude an increasing progression $x<y<z$ crossing blocks, with common difference $d$.

If $x,y\in I_i$, then $d\le L_i-1$. The next permitted block of the same class is at least $I_{i+3}$, so \[ z-y\ge L_{i+1}+L_{i+2}+1>L_i, \] a contradiction.

If $y,z\in I_j$ and $x$ is in an earlier block of this class, then $j\ge4$ and \[ y-x\ge L_{j-2}+L_{j-1}+1 \ge\left(\frac49+\frac23\right)L_j+1>L_j, \] using the upper ratio bound in $1$ backwards. This contradicts $z-y\le L_j-1$.

Finally suppose all three lie in different blocks and $y\in I_j$. Since $x\ge1$, \[ d=y-x\le b_j-1 \le\frac{149}{49}L_j-1,\tag{2} \] where the last bound follows by summing the backwards geometric estimates from the lower ratio in $1$. But the next possible block for $z$ is at least $I_{j+3}$, whence \[ d=z-y\ge L_{j+1}+L_{j+2}+1 \ge\left(\frac{149}{100}+\left(\frac{149}{100}\right)^2\right)L_j+1. \tag{3} \] The coefficient in $3$ is $3.7101$, larger than $149/49<3.041$, contradicting $2$. All cases are excluded. Thus these three classes admit the required avoiding enumerations.

4. A necessary condition for either class in a two-part construction

An avoidable infinite set cannot contain an entire one-sided infinite arithmetic progression $a,a+d,a+2d,\ldots$, where $d>0$. Restrict its alleged avoiding enumeration to that progression and apply the affine map $a+jd\mapsto j+1$. The result would be an enumeration of $\mathbb N$ avoiding monotone triples.

Here is a short proof that this is impossible. If its first entry is $u$, choose the first later entry $v>u$. The number $2v-u$ is larger than $v$ and occurs later, because every entry before $v$ was at most $u$. These three entries form an increasing arithmetic progression. Restriction and affine rescaling preserve the property, proving the necessary condition.

In particular, a periodic partition cannot answer the two-part question: every nonempty periodic class contains all sufficiently large integers in one residue class modulo its period, hence an infinite arithmetic progression. Simple residue-class splits such as the even and odd integers therefore fail. This obstruction does not exclude nonperiodic classes such as the three unions of intervals constructed above.

5. Assessment and attribution

The three-class construction and all its cross-block inequalities are proved. It is the Davis--Entringer--Graham--Simmons construction, with the separation checks expanded. The necessary condition rules out a natural family of proposed two-class examples. It does not rule out every partition into two sets, and no such two-class construction is supplied.

Finite partition searches alone provide no evidence of an eventual obstruction: every finite set already admits an avoiding order by Section 2, even before splitting it into classes. An infinite solution must also ensure that every assigned integer appears at a finite position in its class's enumeration.

Sources: https://www.erdosproblems.com/197; J. A. Davis, R. C. Entringer, R. L. Graham and G. J. Simmons, On permutations containing no long arithmetic progressions, concluding remark 3, p. 89, https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-34-1-81-90. No novelty is claimed.

Erdős problem 197

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT

A set $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is said to be permutable to avoid monotone 3-APs if there exists a bijection $\pi:\mathbb{N}\to A$ such that the sequence $(\pi(1),\pi(2),\dots)$ contains no monotone 3-term arithmetic progression (i.e. no indices $i<j<k$ with $\pi(i),\pi(j),\pi(k)$ in arithmetic progression and monotone in that order). Question: Can $\mathbb{N}$ be partitioned into two such sets $A,B$? The problem text notes the analogous question for three sets is open.

2) QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

Problem text cites DEGS77 showing: in any permutation of $\mathbb{N}$ there is a monotone 3-AP; hence $\mathbb{N}$ itself is not permutable to avoid. Partition question remains.

3) ATTACK PLAN

Provide two core structural lemmas: $1$ heredity under subsets, $2$ affine invariance (shift/scale). Then perform a finite sanity-check: for $N\le 12$, exhaustive search found a partition of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ into two subsets each permutable to avoid (computed).

4) WORK

Lemma 4.1 (Heredity).
If a set $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is permutable to avoid monotone 3-APs, then every subset $A'\subseteq A$ is also permutable to avoid monotone 3-APs. Proof. Let $\pi$ be an avoiding permutation of $A$. Restrict $\pi$ to the indices where $\pi(i)\in A'$. This yields a permutation of $A'$. Any monotone 3-AP in the restricted sequence would also be a monotone 3-AP in the original sequence, contradiction. $\square$
Lemma 4.2 (Affine invariance).
If $A$ is permutable to avoid monotone 3-APs and $u\in\mathbb{Z}$, $v\in\mathbb{N}$, then \[ u+vA:=\{u+va:\ a\in A\} \] is permutable to avoid monotone 3-APs (when intersected with $\mathbb{N}$ as needed). Proof. Let $\pi$ be an avoiding permutation of $A$. Then $\pi'(i)=u+v\pi(i)$ is a permutation of $u+vA$. Three values form a 3-term arithmetic progression iff their affine images do, and monotonicity is preserved because $v>0$. Thus any monotone 3-AP in $\pi'$ would correspond to one in $\pi$, contradiction. $\square$

FAST REALITY CHECK (computed)

For finite sets $[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$, exhaustive backtracking verified: for each $N\le 12$ there exists a partition $[N]=A\sqcup B$ such that both $A$ and $B$ admit a permutation avoiding monotone 3-APs. This is only finite evidence (does not imply an infinite partition).

6) FINAL

UNRESOLVED

(i) Strongest proved partial results: heredity (Lemma 4.1) and affine invariance (Lemma 4.2). Finite partitions exist for $N\le 12$ by computation.

(ii) First gap: produce an infinite partition $\mathbb{N}=A\sqcup B$ with both sets permutable to avoid, or prove impossible.

(iii) Top 3 next moves: 1. Strengthen finite computations: find maximal $N$ where partitions exist and identify extension obstructions. 2. Try to build $A,B$ recursively using affine invariance gadgets (block concatenations). 3. Prove a compactness criterion: if every finite initial segment admits a partition with a coherent extension property, then an infinite partition exists (Kőnig-type).

(iv) Minimal counterexample structure: a proof that any partition $A\sqcup B$ forces at least one side to be "extension-rigid" so that every permutation of it eventually contains a monotone 3-AP.
