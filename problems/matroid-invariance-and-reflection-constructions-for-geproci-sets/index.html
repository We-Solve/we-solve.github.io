Matroid Invariance and Reflection Constructions for Geproci Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt does not specify the ambient space, ground field for the point configurations, or meaning of "nontrivial." The following gives a conventional formulation in complex projective three-space, conditional on that being the intended setting; the omitted restrictions require mathematical review.

Let $Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3({\mathbb{C}})$ be a finite set of distinct points, regarded as a reduced scheme. For positive integers $a,b$, call $Z$ $(a,b)$-geproci if there is a nonempty Zariski open set of projection centers $o\in{\mathbb{P}}^3({\mathbb{C}})\setminus Z$ such that projection $\pi_o$ to a projective plane is injective on $Z$ and its image is a scheme-theoretic complete intersection \[ \pi_o(Z)=V(F_o,G_o)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad \deg F_o=a,\quad\deg G_o=b, \] where the two homogeneous polynomials have no common curve component. Call $Z$ geproci if such $a,b$ exist. Necessarily $|Z|=ab$. This definition permits all positive degrees; the source does not specify which degrees or configurations its word "nontrivial" excludes.

The underlying matroid $M(Z)$ has ground set $Z$ and rank function \[ r_Z(S)=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\operatorname{span}\{v_p:p\in S\}, \] where $v_p\in{\mathbb{C}}^4\setminus\{0\}$ represents $p$. This is independent of the choices of representatives. Two configurations realize the same matroid if a bijection between their points preserves the rank of every subset. The uniform matroid $U_{r,n}$ has rank function $S\mapsto\min\{|S|,r\}$ on an $n$-element ground set. For a spanning configuration in ${\mathbb{P}}^3({\mathbb{C}})$, linear general position means that its matroid is $U_{4,n}$.

A finite complex reflection group is a finite subgroup $W\leq\operatorname{GL}(V)$, for a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$, generated by nonidentity elements fixing a hyperplane pointwise. It is irreducible if $V$ has no nonzero proper $W$-invariant subspace.

Subject to the unresolved scope qualifications above, the original excerpt poses these questions:

  1. Can the classification of irreducible complex reflection groups provide ideas toward a classification of nontrivial geproci sets? The excerpt specifies neither a construction associating point sets to reflection groups nor a proposed classification theorem.
  2. What necessary conditions on $M(Z)$, beyond elementary requirements such as a suitable factorization $|Z|=ab$, must hold when $Z$ is geproci?
  3. Does there exist a geproci realization of a uniform matroid, described in the source as a set of points in linear general position? In the spanning three-dimensional interpretation, this asks for a configuration with $M(Z)\cong U_{4,n}$; the intended restrictions on $n,a,b$ and on trivial examples are unspecified.
  4. Is the geproci property determined by the underlying matroid? For configurations in the intended class, is \[ M(Z)\cong M(Z')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(Z\text{ is geproci}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ Z'\text{ is geproci}\right) \] always true? Here "geproci" is existential in the degrees; the source does not ask explicitly for preservation of a fixed pair $(a,b)$.
The supplied shortened statement additionally asks which matroid properties are invariant under "the resulting reflection constructions." No such constructions are defined in the record, so this additional wording cannot yet be formulated as a precise invariance assertion.

The source compares the matroid question with Terao's freeness conjecture. It says that a direct connection between geproci and freeness is not expected, while suggesting projective duality, Matlis duality, or other algebraic considerations as possible ways for arrangement theory to inform the problem. These are motivations, not asserted implications.

Short English statement

The source asks whether complex reflection groups can guide a classification of nontrivial geproci point sets, which combinatorial restrictions geproci sets satisfy, whether uniform matroids admit geproci realizations, and whether the geproci property depends only on the underlying matroid. A conventional formulation in ${\mathbb{P}}^3({\mathbb{C}})$ is given, but the intended ambient setting, exclusions implicit in "nontrivial," and reflection constructions remain unspecified.
