Math-1036

Universal Decomposition of Complete Graphs into Bounded-Degree Factors

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the usual convention that graphs are finite, simple, and undirected; the supplied record does not explicitly state this convention. For a positive integer $n$, let $K_n$ denote the complete graph on $n$ vertices. A graph is $r$-regular if every vertex has degree $r$. A decomposition of $K_n$ into copies of $F_1,\ldots,F_t$ means that there are spanning subgraphs $H_1,\ldots,H_t$ of $K_n$ such that \[ H_i\cong F_i\quad(1\leq i\leq t),\qquad E(K_n)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{t}E(H_i). \] The copies may use different identifications of their vertices with $V(K_n)$, and the graphs $F_i$ need not be distinct or connected.

Question. Is it true that for every nonnegative integer $\Delta$ there exists a positive integer $n_0=n_0(\Delta)$ such that, for every integer $n\geq n_0$, every positive integer $t$, and every family of $n$-vertex graphs $F_1,\ldots,F_t$, if each $F_i$ is $r_i$-regular with \[ r_i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\qquad r_i\leq\Delta\quad(1\leq i\leq t),\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{t}r_i=n-1, \] then $K_n$ has a decomposition into copies of $F_1,\ldots,F_t$?

The integer domains above make explicit the usual interpretation of a degree bound. Zero-degree factors are allowed because the supplied statement does not exclude them. No additional parity restriction on $n$ is imposed: the hypotheses already require the existence of the specified regular graphs, and therefore require $nr_i$ to be even for every $i$. The supplied record labels this question open; this draft does not independently verify that status.

Short English statement

For each fixed degree bound, must every sufficiently large complete graph admit an edge partition into any prescribed family of regular spanning graphs whose degrees obey that bound and sum to $n-1$? The intended graph convention is taken to be finite, simple, and undirected, subject to source confirmation.
