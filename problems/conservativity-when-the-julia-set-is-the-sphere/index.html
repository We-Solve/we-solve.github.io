Math-1930

Conservativity when the Julia set is the sphere

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ be a rational map of degree at least $2$, the usual degree range for Julia-set dynamics. Its Julia set $J(f)$ is the complement of the open set on which the family of iterates is normal. Write \[ \omega_f(z)=\bigcap_{N\geq0}\overline{\{f^n(z):n\geq N\}} \] for the forward omega-limit set, with closure in the sphere. Let $m$ denote spherical area, normalized to total mass one; this gives the usual Lebesgue-null-set convention on the sphere.

The rational map is nonsingular for this measure class, but area preservation is not assumed. A standard conservativity condition is that every measurable $A$ with $m(A)>0$ has \[ m(A\cap f^{-n}A)>0\quad\text{for some integer }n\geq1. \] This is a nonsingular recurrence property, not an assertion that $m$ is an invariant probability.

Questions (Mikhail Lyubich). For every rational $f$ satisfying $J(f)=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$, is it true that \[ m\{z:\omega_f(z)=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\}=1, \] and is $f$ conservative with respect to Lebesgue/spherical area? The first question concerns almost-everywhere full forward accumulation; the second concerns recurrence of arbitrary positive-area measurable sets. Neither is restricted to an unstated special family of rational maps.

The supplied research summary marks both conclusions solved positively and attributes them to Lyubich's ergodic theory and Eremenko--Lyubich, calling them classical and saying they were verified through the literature. However, no article, theorem number, publication details, or exact hypotheses are provided. This draft records that claimed status without independently adopting the verification claim. The background separately says the source lists the item open and no comprehensive modern resolution check was available, so exact all-rational-map coverage requires source review.

Short English statement

If a rational map has Julia set equal to the whole sphere, must almost every point have the whole sphere as its omega-limit set, and must the map be conservative for spherical area? The registry claims positive solutions but supplies no precise theorem authenticating their full scope.
