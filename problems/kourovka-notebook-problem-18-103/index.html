Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.103

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is minimax if it has a finite subnormal series \[ 1=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_n=G \] such that each factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$ satisfies either the minimum condition on subgroups (every descending chain of subgroups stabilizes) or the maximum condition on subgroups (every ascending chain of subgroups stabilizes). The choice of condition may differ between factors. A group is nilpotent if its lower central series, defined by $\gamma_1(G)=G$ and $\gamma_{j+1}(G)=[\gamma_j(G),G]$, reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps.

A finitely generated field of characteristic zero is a field $K={\mathbb{Q}}(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ for some finite list of elements. For a nonzero $K$-vector space $V$, a representation $\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}_K(V)$ is faithful if $\ker\rho=\{1\}$, and irreducible if its only invariant $K$-linear subspaces are $0$ and $V$. Using the algebraic imprimitivity convention, it is primitive if there is no decomposition \[ V=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_i,\qquad |I|>1, \] into nonzero $K$-linear subspaces that $\rho(G)$ permutes transitively.

Question (A. V. Tushev; Kourovka Notebook 18.103). Is it true that for every nilpotent minimax group $G$, every finitely generated field $K$ of characteristic zero, and every nonzero $K$-vector space $V$, \[ \left(\exists\,\rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_K(V)\text{ faithful, irreducible, and primitive}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(\exists\,S\subseteq G\text{ finite with }G=\langle S\rangle\right)? \] Unresolved qualification. The supplied source does not specify the dimension of the representation space or define primitive representations. The displayed formulation allows arbitrary dimension and uses the algebraic direct-sum convention above; these conventions require confirmation against the original source.

Short English statement

Must a nilpotent minimax group admitting a faithful irreducible primitive representation over a finitely generated field of characteristic zero be finitely generated? The source leaves the representation dimension and the precise meaning of primitive unspecified; the formulation above makes its conventions explicit.
