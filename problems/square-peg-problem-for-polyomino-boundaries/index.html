Square-Peg Problem for Polyomino Boundaries

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the conventional finite square-grid definition of a polyomino: let $S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^2$ be finite and nonempty, with any two elements joined by a sequence of elements of $S$ whose successive differences belong to $\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)\}$. Set \[ P=\bigcup_{(m,n)\in S}[m,m+1]\times[n,n+1],\qquad J=\partial P. \] Require that $J$ be a Jordan curve, meaning that it is the image of a continuous injective map from the unit circle into ${\mathbb{R}}^2$.

The proposed assertion is that for every such $P$, there exist $x,u,v\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$ satisfying \[ \|u\|=\|v\|>0,\qquad u\cdot v=0,\qquad \{x,x+u,x+u+v,x+v\}\subseteq J. \] Thus the four distinct points are the vertices of a nondegenerate Euclidean square. They may lie anywhere on $J$; no lattice-point, boundary-corner, axis-alignment, or square-interior containment condition is imposed by the supplied statement.

The record does not explicitly define "polyomino." The conventional definition above is an interpretive convention requiring confirmation against the original report, particularly if a more restrictive grid variant was intended.

Short English statement

Does every polyomino whose entire boundary is a Jordan curve have four boundary points forming a nondegenerate square? The draft uses the conventional finite, edge-connected square-grid meaning of polyomino; the precise intended grid conventions require source review.
