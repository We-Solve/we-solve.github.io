Math-7940

Uniform Random Variables with Uniform Sum

Let $p_i=\Pr(X=i)$ and $q_j=\Pr(Y=j)$ have finite supports. Independence gives $\Pr(X+Y=s)=\sum_i p_iq_{s-i}$. A distribution is uniform on its support if all its nonzero probabilities are equal. The proposed implication is that a uniform convolution forces both input distributions to be uniform on their respective supports. [S1] [S2]

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Suppose $X, Y$ are finitely-supported independent random variables taking integer values such that $X + Y$ is uniformly distributed on its range. Are $X$ and $Y$ themselves uniformly distributed on their ranges? [S1]
Residual task reported by the archive.

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove the unfair 0-1 polynomial conjecture for arbitrary degree or construct a nonnegative-real counterexample. Computational verification cannot settle the unbounded-degree statement. [S1]

Short English statement

If the sum of two independent, finitely supported integer-valued random variables is uniform, must each original variable also be uniform?
