Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can we add simplicial resolutions to Macaulay2?

Attempt and results

The source snapshot labels the broad software request solved, and official Macaulay2 documentation lists labelled complexes and Taylor resolutions. We preserve that provenance; no newly solved open problem or package change is claimed. The following verifies the standard monomial interpretation and identifies an unsound shortcut toward smaller resolutions.
Construction with explicit augmentation.
Let $S=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and let $m_1,\ldots,m_r$ be nonzero monomials, $r\ge1$, with $I=(m_1,\ldots,m_r)$. For a subset $J$, let $m_J$ be the least common multiple of its labels, with $m_\varnothing=1$. Put $T_q=\bigoplus_{|J|=q}S e_J$ for $0\le q\le r$, where $e_J$ has multidegree $m_J$ and $T_0=S$. If $J=\{j_1<\cdots<j_q\}$, set \[ d(e_J)=\sum_{a=1}^q(-1)^{a-1}\frac{m_J}{m_{J\setminus\{j_a\}}} e_{J\setminus\{j_a\}}. \] Augment $T_0\to S/I$ by the quotient map. Every pair of removed vertices occurs twice in $d^2$, with opposite signs and the same coefficient $m_J/m_{J\setminus\{j_a,j_b\}}$, so $d^2=0$. The image of $d_1$ is exactly $I$, proving exactness at $T_0$ after augmentation.
Proof of exactness in higher degrees.
Fix any monomial $u$. The $u$-graded strand has one basis vector $(u/m_J)e_J$ precisely when $m_J\mid u$. Let $V_u=\{j:m_j\mid u\}$. Since $m_J\mid u$ exactly when $J\subseteq V_u$, the strand on the $T_q$ is the augmented simplicial chain complex of the full simplex on $V_u$, with $q$ vertices in homological degree $q$. If $V_u$ is empty, only $T_0$ and $(S/I)_u$ occur and their map is an isomorphism. If $V_u$ is nonempty, then $(S/I)_u=0$; choose a vertex $v$. Define $h(e_J)$ by adjoining $v$ in its ordered position with the sign making its deletion positive, and set $h(e_J)=0$ when $v\in J$. Direct expansion of the alternating boundary gives $dh+hd=e_J$: the term deleting $v$ is $e_J$, and every term deleting another vertex cancels with the corresponding term in $hd$. This includes $J=\varnothing$, where $h(1)=e_v$. Thus every nonempty-simplex strand is exact. Any polynomial vector has only finitely many homogeneous components, so strandwise preimages can be summed to prove exactness of the full graded complex. This proves a free resolution of $S/I$, traditionally the Taylor resolution.
An explicit failed compression.
For $I=(x,y,z)$, retain only vertices and edges of the labelled triangle and delete its two-dimensional face, keeping $T_0$ and the quotient augmentation. The resulting complex still has $d^2=0$, and every nonzero boundary entry is a nonconstant monomial. Nevertheless, at multidegree $xyz$ the three edges have the cycle \[ z e_{12}-y e_{13}+x e_{23}. \] Its boundary is zero: expanding gives $z(x e_2-y e_1)-y(x e_3-z e_1)+x(y e_3-z e_2)=0$. The vector is nonzero in the free edge module and there is no higher module mapping to it. Thus the compressed complex is not exact. No face has label $xyz$ after deleting the triangle, so a verifier inspecting only degrees that label surviving faces misses this homology. For a finite labelled simplicial subcomplex in general, all strand types are determined by the subsets $V_u$: the permitted faces are precisely those with vertices in $V_u$. If $V_u$ is nonempty, put $v=\operatorname{lcm}_{j\in V_u}m_j$. Then $v\mid u$, and $m_i\mid v$ if and only if $m_i\mid u$; hence the strand type already occurs at this join of vertex labels. Checking every such join is a finite exactness test, though potentially exponential and requiring homology over the actual field $k$.
