Measure-rigidity obstructions require an entropy and genuine-rank gate

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

D. Fisher's Question 6 asks whether measure-rigidity results for higher-rank abelian groups yield new obstructions to smooth or continuous actions of such groups on compact manifolds. For example, for $k\ge2$ one may consider actions $\alpha:{\mathbb{Z}}^k\to\operatorname{Diff}^\infty(M)$ or continuous actions of ${\mathbb{Z}}^k$ or ${\mathbb{R}}^k$ on compact $M$. An action is effective if only the identity group element acts identically. The source does not specify effectiveness, a genuine-rank condition, invariant measures, or positive entropy, so its target is a research direction rather than a single universal nonexistence statement.

The source notes existing obstructions for smooth volume-preserving actions due particularly to H. Hu and A. Katok. It explains why arithmetic-quotient arguments for semisimple groups and lattices do not transfer directly: those arguments use both Ratner's theorem and superrigidity for merely measurable cocycles. The paragraph invokes Dye's theorem [43] and generalizations [154, 37] as an amenable orbit-equivalence obstacle to the needed measurable-cocycle rigidity. This is the source's contextual limitation, not an assertion excluding every possible restricted cocycle theorem.

The attached aid reports affirmative topological restrictions, including exclusion of spheres, for maximal-rank actions under strong positive-entropy and nonuniform-rigidity hypotheses, attributed to Katok and Rodriguez Hertz. Those hypotheses and full citations are not supplied, so this cannot be stated as a restriction on all higher-rank abelian actions.

In the opposite direction, its reported universality statement is: for every positive-dimensional closed connected smooth manifold $M$ and every $k\ge2$, there exist effective smooth ${\mathbb{R}}^k$- and ${\mathbb{Z}}^k$-actions for which every element has zero entropy. The ${\mathbb{R}}^k$ orbits have dimension at most one, with the discrete examples given by restriction; for $\dim M\ge2$, the actions can preserve any prescribed smooth volume. Thus these examples do not supply genuine higher-dimensional orbit dynamics, but they rule out an obstruction to effective actions based on the underlying manifold alone.

The aid also reports this entropy gate. Let $\theta>0$, let $\alpha:{\mathbb{Z}}^k\to\operatorname{Diff}^{1+\theta}(M)$ preserve a smooth probability volume $\mu$, and let $f=\alpha(a)$ have a $Df$-invariant rank-$r$ bundle $E$, $r\ge1$, with \[ \|Df^j(v)\|\ge C\lambda^j\|v\| \quad(j\ge1,\ x\in M,\ v\in E_x), \qquad C>0,\quad\lambda>1. \] The reported Pesin-formula transfer gives \[ h_\mu(f)\ge r\log\lambda>0. \] In the full-action ergodic decomposition $\mu=\int\mu_\omega\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\eta(\omega)$, entropy affinity yields positive-entropy $f$-components on a set of positive $\eta$-measure. A measure-rigidity theorem can yield a topological obstruction only when its remaining rank, regularity, and dynamical hypotheses apply to the relevant action and measures.

The appended heading "3. Measure rigidity" and section 3.1, about $G/\Gamma$ and a subgroup generated by one-parameter unipotent subgroups, begin the next problem's context. They are not hypotheses of Fisher's question. All stated progress is from the supplied dated machine-generated aid and requires independent verification.

Short English statement

Can higher-rank abelian measure rigidity produce new restrictions on manifold actions? The supplied progress requires positive entropy and genuine rank: effective zero-entropy actions with at most one-dimensional orbits exist on every closed connected manifold of positive dimension, so no unconditional manifold obstruction follows.
