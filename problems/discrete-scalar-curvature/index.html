Math-13000

Discrete scalar curvature

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space with a probability transition law $m_x$ at each point. For $x\ne y$, Ollivier coarse Ricci curvature is \[ \kappa(x,y)=1-\frac{W_1(m_x,m_y)}{d(x,y)}, \] where $W_1$ is the minimum expected distance over probability couplings, assuming finite first moments. The proposed scalar-curvature candidate is \[ S(x)=\int_X\kappa(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}m_x(y), \] possibly replaced by a distance-weighted average \[ S_w(x)=\int_X w(d(x,y))\kappa(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}m_x(y). \] These formulas require integrability. The unweighted expression also requires a convention at $y=x$ if $m_x$ has an atom there, because the displayed coarse-curvature formula is undefined on the diagonal. The source gives no diagonal convention or particular weight $w$.

The problem asks whether this averaged quantity has useful geometric or probabilistic properties, with volume-growth control offered as an example. A volume-growth question would concern the mass of metric balls $B(x,r)=\{y:d(x,y)\leq r\}$ with respect to a specified global measure, such as counting measure on a locally finite graph. The source supplies neither that global measure nor hypotheses and a quantitative volume comparison. Accordingly this is a proposal to develop and test a scalar-curvature notion, not a stated universal volume bound.

Short English statement

Study the average of coarse Ricci curvature over a point's transition law, possibly weighted by distance. Does this proposed scalar curvature control useful geometry or probability, for example growth of metric balls?
