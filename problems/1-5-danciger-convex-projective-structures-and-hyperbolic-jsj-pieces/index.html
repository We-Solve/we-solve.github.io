1.5 (Danciger) — Convex projective structures and hyperbolic JSJ pieces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $N$ be a closed $3$-manifold whose JSJ decomposition consists entirely of hyperbolic pieces. In the standard setting, the decomposition cuts along JSJ tori, and each resulting piece has interior admitting a complete finite-volume metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$.

Danciger's question, item 1.5, is whether every such $N$ admits a convex real projective structure. In the customary properly convex interpretation, this asks for a properly convex open domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{RP}^3$ and a discrete group $\Gamma\subset\operatorname{PGL}_4({\mathbb{R}})$ preserving $\Omega$, acting freely and properly discontinuously with compact quotient, such that \[ N\cong\Omega/\Gamma. \] Proper convexity means that $\Omega$ is convex and bounded in some affine chart. This must not be strengthened to strict convexity: the source asks only for convexity. The excerpt does not specify whether proper convexity is part of its convention, nor explicitly state the usual connectedness, orientability and irreducibility hypotheses under which its JSJ terminology is being used. These conventions must be checked rather than extending the assertion to unrelated reducible cases.

The source identifies this as the more general version of item 1.4, whose statement is absent here. The registry reports gluing constructions in particular cases and says the general all-hyperbolic-JSJ statement remains unproved; this is supplied progress information.

Short English statement

Must a closed $3$-manifold built entirely from hyperbolic JSJ pieces carry a convex real projective structure? The exact convexity and JSJ conventions need source confirmation.
