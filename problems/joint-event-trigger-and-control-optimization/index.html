Math-6538

Joint Event-Trigger and Control Optimization

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An event-triggering policy $\gamma$ specifies when communication occurs, and a control policy $f$ specifies the control actions. The supplied record denotes the control-performance cost by $J_C$, the communication cost by $J_E$, and the average communication budget by $\bar m$. It does not define these costs or the underlying stochastic linear system.

For a fully specified model, let $\mathcal A$ denote its admissible pairs $(f,\gamma)$, including the information available to each policy. Write $J_C(f,\gamma)$ and $J_E(f,\gamma)$ for the resulting random costs, with expectations taken under the probability law induced by the model and the policies. These are placeholders for model data absent from the supplied record. Define, whenever these expectations are meaningful, \[ \mathcal A_{\bar m} =\left\{(f,\gamma)\in\mathcal A: \mathbb E[J_E(f,\gamma)]\leq\bar m\right\}. \] The requested joint optimization is to characterize a pair $(f^*,\gamma^*)\in\mathcal A_{\bar m}$ such that \[ \mathbb E[J_C(f^*,\gamma^*)] \leq \mathbb E[J_C(f,\gamma)] \qquad\text{for every }(f,\gamma)\in\mathcal A_{\bar m}. \] Existence of such a pair is not asserted by the supplied record.

This is only a conditional formulation of the stated target. A precise model-specific problem requires the state dynamics, time domain and horizon, noise and initial-state laws, quadratic cost weights and normalization, communication-cost definition, observation and communication structure, admissibility conditions, and allowed budget values. In particular, the phrase "average communication budget" does not determine how communication is averaged.

Short English statement

Jointly choose when to communicate and how to control a stochastic linear system so as to minimize expected quadratic control cost while keeping expected communication cost at most $\bar m$. The supplied record leaves the model, costs, and policy information unspecified, so it does not determine a complete optimization problem.
