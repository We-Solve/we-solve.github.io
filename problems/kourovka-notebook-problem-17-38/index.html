Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.38

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

All groups considered are finite, and classes of groups are understood to be closed under isomorphism. A formation is a nonempty class of finite groups closed under homomorphic images and finite subdirect products. Here a subdirect product of groups $G_1,\ldots,G_r$, with $r\geq 1$, is a subgroup of $G_1\times\cdots\times G_r$ whose projection onto every factor is surjective. A formation $\mathfrak H$ is hereditary if every subgroup of every member of $\mathfrak H$ also belongs to $\mathfrak H$. It is saturated if, for every finite group $G$, \[ G/\Phi(G)\in\mathfrak H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\in\mathfrak H, \] where $\Phi(G)$ is the intersection of the maximal proper subgroups of $G$, with $\Phi(1)=1$.

Let $\mathfrak U$ denote the formation of all finite supersoluble groups. Explicitly, a finite group $G$ is supersoluble if it has a series \[ 1=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_n=G \] in which every $G_i$ is normal in $G$ and every factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$, for $1\leq i\leq n$, is cyclic of prime order. The trivial group is included by allowing $n=0$.

A formation $\mathfrak F$ is radical in a class $\mathfrak H$ if $\mathfrak F\subseteq\mathfrak H$ and, for every $G\in\mathfrak H$ and every pair of normal subgroups $A,B\trianglelefteq G$, \[ A,B\in\mathfrak F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad AB\in\mathfrak F, \qquad AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}. \] Define \[ \mathfrak M=\left\{\mathfrak H:\mathfrak H\text{ is a saturated hereditary formation of finite groups and }\mathfrak U\text{ is radical in }\mathfrak H\right\}. \] The question is whether there exists $\mathfrak H_{\ast}\in\mathfrak M$ such that \[ \forall\mathfrak H\in\mathfrak M,\qquad \mathfrak H\subseteq\mathfrak H_{\ast}. \] Thus the requested element must contain every member of $\mathfrak M$. No restriction to soluble ambient groups is imposed. The supplied record labels this question open; that label and the supplied edition information have not been independently verified.

Short English statement

Among saturated hereditary formations of finite groups that contain all finite supersoluble groups and in which the product of any two normal supersoluble subgroups is supersoluble, does one formation contain every other such formation?
