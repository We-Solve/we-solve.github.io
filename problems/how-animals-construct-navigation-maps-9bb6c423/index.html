How animals construct navigation maps

Domain. Animal navigation; cue integration and maps.   Retained status/source context. Open comparative state-estimation question. Particular cue uses are experimentally and observationally supported; no common estimator is thereby established across taxa.

Definitions and assumptions.
For one species and route class let $X_t$ be position on a specified geographic surface and $V_t$ velocity in a local coordinate chart. The Markov state $Z_t=(X_t,V_t)$ has a specified transition law including movement uncertainty and transport by wind or current. Sensory vector $Y_t$ contains observed magnetic, celestial, olfactory, landmark and inertial cues with declared noise and missingness. Restrict internal estimators $\widehat\pi_t$ to a regular finite-dimensional state-space family, using only cues available by $t$. Heading and destination decisions have an explicit output law; a learned route memory is distinguished from a positional map.
Problem statement.
Infer an estimator and decision law that predict held-out movements: \[\pi_t(\,\mathrm{d} z)\propto p(Y_t\mid z) \int p(\,\mathrm{d} z\mid z')\pi_{t-1}(\,\mathrm{d} z'),\qquad W_1(\widehat\pi_t^X,\pi_t^X)\leq\varepsilon.\] The displayed filter is a specified Bayesian benchmark, not an assertion that animals implement it; $\pi_t^X$ is the position marginal and $W_1$ uses geographic distance. Test predictive divergence under declared cue conflicts or availability changes.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
The displayed Bayesian filtering recursion is an established benchmark under the specified hidden-Markov observation assumptions. Approximating that posterior does not by itself predict the animal's decisions, so add an independent movement/heading loss for the inferred decision law. Define movement states consistently across geographic charts and use the declared geodesic metric. Learning or cue-history dependence requires augmentation of the Markov state rather than future-information leakage.
Alternative formulations.
(i) A vector-navigation model encodes an inherited heading and a stopping cue without full two-dimensional localization. (ii) A landmark graph supports familiar-route navigation but may not generalize to displacement. (iii) A multimodal positional estimator combines cues with learned reliability. These alternatives are not interchangeable merely because they reach the same destination on ordinary routes.
Mathematical-status qualification.
The restricted calculation, identity, obstruction or construction identified in this entry is not itself a new open conjecture. The residual target is the explicitly delimited extension, identification, implementation or independent biological validation; an already known special case does not satisfy that residual target.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Reed-warbler analyses support use of magnetic inclination as an arrival signpost, with later experiments addressing positional information. This narrows specific navigation mechanisms without identifying every neural map. Natural cues covary spatially, so ordinary trajectories may not distinguish them. Unknown wind compensation, route selection and learned priors confound internal-state inference. The residual target is a restricted, testable estimator class with transparent behavioural observation laws and transfer across specified routes or cue conditions.
