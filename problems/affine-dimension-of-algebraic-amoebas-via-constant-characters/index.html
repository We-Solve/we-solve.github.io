Math-1509

Affine dimension of algebraic amoebas via constant characters

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Section C.3 is a collection of seven computational questions, not one precisely specified algorithmic problem. Its items are: (A) compute examples of amoebas and tropical varieties; (B) compute homology of amoeba complements and seek a Hermitian form whose signature gives the number of complement components; (C) determine expansiveness or membership of zero for classical varieties, listing Grassmannians, generalized flag manifolds, spherical varieties, determinantal varieties, Schubert varieties and Calabi--Yaus; (D) develop a theory of smooth curves; (E) determine the complexity of resultant vanishing; (F) decide when $\operatorname{Log}(X)$ lies in a lower-dimensional subspace; and (G) determine the complexity of distance to a discriminantal variety.

The aid makes item (F) precise in the following irreducible algebraic setting. Let \[ \varnothing\ne X\subset({\mathbb{C}}^*)^n \] be a closed irreducible subvariety and let $\operatorname{Log}(z)=(\log|z_1|,\ldots,\log|z_n|)$. Define its lattice of constant characters \[ L_X=\{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n:\exists c_u\in{\mathbb{C}}^*,\ z^u=c_u\text{ on }X\}. \] Equivalently, $x^u-c_u\in I(X)$. Irreducibility makes $L_X$ saturated: if a positive multiple of a character is constant, that character itself is constant. The map $u\mapsto c_u$ is multiplicative. The aid's affine-hull theorem is \[ \operatorname{Aff}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\operatorname{Log}(X)) =\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\langle u,a\rangle=\log|c_u| \text{ for every }u\in L_X\}. \] Its codimension is $\operatorname{rank}L_X$. Every real affine equation on the amoeba is a real linear combination of these character equations. Thus containment in a proper affine subspace is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero constant integral character. The direction space is rational; its offsets $\log|c_u|$ need not be rational numbers.

For $x_0\in X$, the corresponding algebraic set \[ x_0T_X=\{z\in({\mathbb{C}}^*)^n:z^u=c_u\ (u\in L_X)\} \] is a coset of a connected algebraic subtorus, by saturation, and is the smallest torus coset containing $X$. This gives the stated logarithmic shadow of the minimal coset. The theorem concerns affine-hull dimension, not the local real dimension of the amoeba; a small-dimensional amoeba can have full affine span. Nor does it automatically extend to reducible $X$ without taking the affine span of its component data.

If "subspace" in item (F) means a linear subspace through zero, there is a further distinction. Extend $u\mapsto\log|c_u|$ linearly to $\ell:L_X\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$. The homogeneous real equations are exactly \[ \langle v,a\rangle=0\quad\text{with }v\in L_X\otimes{\mathbb{R}}, \quad\ell(v)=0. \] A nonzero such $v$, rather than merely a nonzero $L_X$, is required for proper linear containment. The source does not specify affine versus through-origin meaning.

The other items remain open-ended in this record. For (C), the well-defined membership test in a fixed torus embedding is \[ 0\in\operatorname{Log}(X)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\cap(S^1)^n\ne\varnothing. \] The word expansive commonly refers in the associated algebraic-dynamical convention to avoidance of this unit torus, whereas the source's parenthesis asks whether zero is in the amoeba; the convention must be fixed. In every listed classical family the torus embedding and defining data matter, not just its abstract variety type. For (A)--(B), input class, coefficient field, homology coefficients and a nontrivial canonical Hermitian-form construction are unspecified. Item (D) does not state which algebraic or tropical smoothness notion is intended. For (E) and (G), coefficient encoding, dense/sparse presentation, variable/support bounds, computational model, norm, exact decision versus approximation and accuracy are absent. Therefore no definite complexity bound, random-model conclusion or classification of these six items is inferred from the affine-hull theorem.

Short English statement

For a nonempty irreducible torus subvariety, all affine equations on its amoeba come from constant integral characters. Their lattice determines the affine codimension and smallest containing torus coset. This resolves a precise affine version of item (F); the other computational questions still need their input and output conventions specified.
