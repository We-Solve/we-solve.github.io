Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.66

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. A pro-$p$ group is a topological group isomorphic to an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. Let $F$ be a free pro-$p$ group, with its prime and rank to be specified as discussed below.

Under the usual interpretation in the first-order language of groups $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$, define \[ \operatorname{Th}_{\mathrm{grp}}(F)=\{\varphi:\varphi\text{ is an }\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}\text{-sentence and }F\models\varphi\}. \] Here variables range over elements of the underlying group; the topology is not separately named in this language. This theory is decidable if there is an algorithm which, given any $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}$-sentence $\varphi$, halts and determines whether $F\models\varphi$.

Question (source formulation). Is the elementary theory of a free pro-$p$ group decidable?

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record does not specify the rank of the free pro-$p$ group, whether the rank is finite or infinite, or whether trivial and procyclic cases are excluded. It also does not state whether $p$ and the rank are fixed parameters, whether the question ranges over all such parameters, or whether a uniform decision algorithm is intended. The language above is the usual pure-group-language interpretation, which the source does not explicitly specify. These qualifications must be resolved before selecting a fully quantified version of the question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether an algorithm can decide every first-order sentence about a free pro-$p$ group.
