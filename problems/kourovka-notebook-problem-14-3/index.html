Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.3

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group, and let ${\mathbb{Z}}[G]$ be its integral group ring, whose elements are sums $a=\sum_{g\in G}a_g g$ with $a_g\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, with multiplication induced by the group multiplication and distributivity. Write ${\mathbb{Z}}[G]^\times$ for its group of units and $Z(G)$ for the center of $G$. A unit $u\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]^\times$ is central if $ua=au$ for every $a\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]$. Define the canonical antiinvolution by \[ \left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)^*=\sum_{g\in G}a_g g^{-1}. \] Thus $(ab)^*=b^*a^*$. A unit $v$ is symmetric if $v^*=v$.

The question is whether, for every finite group $G$ and every central unit $u\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]^\times$, there exist $z\in Z(G)$ and a central unit $v\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]^\times$ such that \[ u=zv\qquad\text{and}\qquad v^*=v, \] where $z$ is identified with its basis element in ${\mathbb{Z}}[G]$. No augmentation restriction on the units is stated in the supplied record.

Short English statement

Does every central unit of the integral group ring of every finite group factor as a central group element times a symmetric central unit, where symmetry means invariance under inversion of the group basis elements?
