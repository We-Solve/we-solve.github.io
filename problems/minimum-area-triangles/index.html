Minimum-area triangles

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$, $n\ge3$. For three distinct indices define \[ A(i,j,k)=\frac12\left|\det(p_j-p_i,p_k-p_i)\right|, \qquad A_{\min}(P)=\min_{1\le i<j<k\le n}A(i,j,k). \] The minimum-area-triangle task is to output a minimizing triple (or its area, if that is the required output). This formula permits collinear triples of area zero. The source does not state a general-position assumption or say whether only positive-area triangles are eligible, so that convention requires confirmation.

The algorithmic question is to find a worst-case subquadratic algorithm for all permitted $n$-point inputs, or prove a quadratic lower bound in a suitable specified computation model. Here a quadratic lower bound means a worst-case running-time lower bound $\Omega(n^2)$ for algorithms in that model. The record leaves the model and coordinate representation unspecified. It also does not say whether "subquadratic" means $o(n^2)$ or the stronger bound $O(n^{2-\varepsilon})$ for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$.

For the stated fixed-dimension generalization, let $d\ge2$ be fixed and let $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$, $n\ge d+1$. The analogous objective is \[ V_{\min,d}(P)= \min_{1\le i_0<\cdots<i_d\le n} \frac1{d!}\left|\det(p_{i_1}-p_{i_0},\ldots,p_{i_d}-p_{i_0})\right|. \] The question is to close the gap between algorithmic upper bounds and lower bounds for finding a minimum-volume simplex in each fixed dimension. The source gives no dimension-dependent target exponents or existing bounds. As for triangles, the eligibility of degenerate simplices and the computation model must be fixed.

Short English statement

Find the smallest-area triangle determined by planar input points faster than quadratic time, or establish a matching quadratic lower bound in an explicit computation model. More generally, determine the complexity of finding a minimum-volume simplex in fixed dimension.

The supplied summary reports 3SUM-hardness and says that the 3SUM hypothesis rules out an expected truly subquadratic worst-case algorithm, while an unconditional quadratic lower bound is not established. That conditional statement concerns a fixed exponent saving and must not be read as excluding every $o(n^2)$ improvement. The reduction model and hypothesis details are not supplied. The registry labels the item partial progress; the source index warns that its pages have not been systematically updated since 2001.
