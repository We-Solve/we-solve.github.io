Long Binary Blocks in Uniformly Random Sequences

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $N\in{\mathbb{N}}$ with $N\geq 1$, and let $X^{(N)}=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)$ be uniformly distributed on $\{0,1\}^N$; thus \[ \mathbb P\left(X^{(N)}=x\right)=2^{-N}\qquad(x\in\{0,1\}^N). \] Under the convention that a block occurrence is a contiguous occurrence contained entirely within the sampled sequence, define, for integers $1\leq k\leq N$ and words $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_k)\in\{0,1\}^k$, \[ C_{N,k}(w)=\#\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,N-k+1\}:(X_i,\ldots,X_{i+k-1})=w\right\}. \] This convention permits overlapping occurrences and does not identify the two ends of the sequence. It is an explicit framework for interpreting the question; these occurrence conventions are not specified in the supplied source.

Source question. What can be said about the occurrence of binary blocks whose length $k$ is larger than $\log N$ in a uniformly sampled binary sequence of length $N$? In the framework above, this asks for information about block occurrence counts $C_{N,k}(w)$, or occurrence events derived from them, in the stated length regime.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not specify the logarithm base. For a specified base $b>1$, the length condition would read $k>\log_b N$, but no particular $b$ is selected here. The source also does not specify whether a word is fixed in advance, varies with $N$, or is quantified over all words; whether the target concerns occurrence probabilities, counts, repeated blocks, or simultaneous coverage; or whether a finite-sample bound, limiting distribution, or other asymptotic characterization is sought. If an asymptotic interpretation is intended, the growth conditions on $k=k(N)$, beyond the stated logarithmic inequality, remain unspecified. Thus the supplied question is a research direction rather than a uniquely determined proposition. The displayed definitions do not resolve these missing choices.

Short English statement

The problem asks about the occurrence of binary words longer than $\log N$ in a uniformly random binary string of length $N$. Contiguous occurrence counts provide a possible precise framework, but the logarithm base, occurrence conventions, word quantifiers, and desired probabilistic or asymptotic conclusion remain unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
