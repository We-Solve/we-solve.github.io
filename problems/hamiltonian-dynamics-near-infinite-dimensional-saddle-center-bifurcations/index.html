Hamiltonian Dynamics Near Infinite-Dimensional Saddle-Center Bifurcations

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source requests two linked tasks: determine the dynamics of the intended infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system near a saddle-center bifurcation, and determine the nature of the homoclinic bifurcation induced there. No Hamiltonian, evolution equation, phase space, parameter or spectral hypothesis is supplied.

For a notation scheme, let $\mathcal X$ be the intended infinite-dimensional phase space and $\eta$ the bifurcation parameter. Write \[ \dot u=X_{H_\eta}(u) \] for its Hamiltonian vector field, when defined, and $u_\eta$ for a relevant equilibrium. In a suitable Hilbert-space realization one may have $X_{H_\eta}=J\nabla H_\eta$, but an unbounded or weak symplectic structure needs domains and regularity; no such realization is asserted from the source. The linearization would be $L_\eta=D X_{H_\eta}(u_\eta)$, with its actual operator domain and spectrum part of the missing data.

A saddle-center description usually concerns a critical Hamiltonian spectral or equilibrium transition involving saddle and center behavior, but the record gives no eigenvalue multiplicity, crossing or nondegeneracy condition. Different zero-eigenvalue, imaginary-mode and branch-collision configurations cannot be identified merely by the name. In infinite dimension, imaginary or continuous spectrum, resonance and loss of a spectral gap may affect both the local dynamics and the validity of a reduction. A finite-dimensional center-manifold or normal-form conclusion is not inserted automatically.

For an equilibrium-based homoclinic interpretation, a nonconstant orbit $u(t)$ would satisfy \[ u(t)\longrightarrow u_\eta\quad\text{as }t\to-\infty \quad\text{and as }t\to+\infty \] in the intended phase-space topology. The source does not specify whether the target is an equilibrium, a periodic orbit or another invariant set, or whether the requested nature concerns existence, splitting, multiplicity, scaling or stability. Those targets and the meaning of induced must be fixed before a particular homoclinic theorem can be formulated. The local saddle-center problem and the homoclinic/global connection question are retained separately.

The report title places the question in water waves, but the record supplies no water-wave PDE, boundary conditions, asymptotic regime or reversible symmetry to add. The literature triage checked 2026-08-21 reports no authoritative complete resolution found. The open label and reported exact-source verification are dated metadata, not independently checked current results.
