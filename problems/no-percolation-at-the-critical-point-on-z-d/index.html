Math-7900

No percolation at the critical point on $\mathbb{Z}^d$

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Consider nearest-neighbour Bernoulli bond percolation on $\mathbb Z^d$. Each edge is independently open with probability $p\in[0,1]$. Let \[ \theta(p)=\mathbb P_p(0\leftrightarrow\infty), \qquad p_c=\inf\{p:\theta(p)>0\}. \] The conjecture is \[ \boxed{\theta(p_c)=0.} \] Equivalently, at criticality there is almost surely no infinite open cluster.

This is often called the $\theta(p_c)=0$ conjecture, the critical percolation conjecture, the conjecture that there is no infinite cluster at criticality, or the continuity conjecture for the percolation probability at $p_c$. There is no single universally fixed eponymous name.

For nearest-neighbour Bernoulli bond percolation on $\mathbb Z^d$, the cited 2026 source records the conjecture as known in $d=2$ and in sufficiently high dimension, including $d\ge11$, and unresolved in dimensions $3\le d\le10$; see [S1].

Short English statement

At the exact threshold where percolation first becomes possible, is the probability that the origin belongs to an infinite open cluster still zero?
