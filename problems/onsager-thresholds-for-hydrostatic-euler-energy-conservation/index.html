Onsager Thresholds for Hydrostatic Euler Energy Conservation

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied registry labels this problem Onsager Thresholds for Hydrostatic Euler Energy Conservation. Its stated target is to determine critical anisotropic regularity thresholds for conservation of horizontal kinetic energy by weak solutions of the hydrostatic Euler equations, and to identify lower regularities at which anomalous dissipation can occur.

For purposes of explaining the energy terminology only, if a spatial domain $\Omega$, a time interval $I$, and a horizontal velocity field $u_h$ have been specified, with $u_h(t,\cdot)\in L^2(\Omega)$ for almost every $t\in I$, define the horizontal kinetic energy by \[ E_h(t)=\frac12\int_{\Omega}|u_h(t,x)|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] Conservation means that there exists a constant $E_0$ such that $E_h(t)=E_0$ for almost every $t\in I$. Here anisotropic regularity refers to regularity conditions that distinguish horizontal and vertical spatial directions; the record specifies neither the function spaces nor their exponents.

Requested mathematical target, with unresolved qualifications. Determine conditions on anisotropic regularity under which every weak solution in the intended class conserves horizontal kinetic energy, and determine the lower-regularity regime in which weak solutions exhibiting anomalous dissipation exist. To identify energy loss as anomalous dissipation requires specifying the energy balance, including any forcing and boundary fluxes.

Short English statement

The supplied target asks how much horizontal and vertical regularity guarantees horizontal kinetic-energy conservation for weak hydrostatic Euler solutions, and at what lower regularities dissipative solutions can exist. The supplied extract does not specify the equations, solution class, boundary conditions, or regularity spaces, so a precise threshold conjecture cannot be recovered.
