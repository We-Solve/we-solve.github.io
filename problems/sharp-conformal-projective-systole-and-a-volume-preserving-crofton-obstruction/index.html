Math-287

Sharp conformal projective systole and a volume-preserving Crofton obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $g_0$ be the canonical sectional-curvature-one metric on ${\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^n$, and put $V_0=\operatorname{Vol}({\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^n,g_0)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Vol}(S^n)$. Its prime closed geodesics have length $\pi$. The source asks whether every smooth Riemannian metric $g$ with $\operatorname{Vol}(g)=V_0$ has a nonconstant closed geodesic of length at most $\pi$. Equivalently, for the shortest nonconstant closed-geodesic length $L_{\min}(g)$, the scale-invariant question is \[ L_{\min}(g)^n\leq\frac{\pi^n}{V_0}\operatorname{Vol}(g). \] The question does not require the geodesic to be noncontractible. For $n\geq2$, the stronger noncontractible systole $\operatorname{sys}_{\pi_1}(g)$ is the infimum of lengths of noncontractible loops and is realized by a closed geodesic. The case $n=1$ is the circle-length normalization and is immediate; the source reports a positive answer for $n=2$.

The supplied research aid gives the sharp conformal special case. If $n\geq2$ and $g=f^2g_0$, where $f>0$ is smooth, then it reports \[ \operatorname{sys}_{\pi_1}(f^2g_0)^n \leq\frac{\pi^n}{V_0}\operatorname{Vol}(f^2g_0), \] with equality exactly when $f$ is constant. Its stated method uses the invariant probability measure $\mu$ on canonical projective lines, identified with two-planes in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$, and the incidence average \[ \int L_{f^2g_0}(\ell)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(\ell) =\frac\pi{V_0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^n}f\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_{g_0}, \] followed by Hölder's inequality.

The aid also reports, for every $n\geq2$, a smooth family $h_t$ with pointwise volume form ${\,\mathrm{d}} V_{h_t}={\,\mathrm{d}} V_{g_0}$ and \[ \int L_{h_t}(\ell)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(\ell)\longrightarrow\infty. \] No formula for this family is supplied in the record. These $\ell$ are the fixed canonical projective lines, not necessarily $h_t$-geodesics. Divergence of their average length does not bound the shortest $h_t$-geodesic from below and is not a counterexample to the source question. It only obstructs a naive extension of this particular average-length argument. The aid describes the unrestricted case $n\geq3$ as still open.

Short English statement

At the canonical volume, must a metric on real projective space have a closed geodesic of length at most $\pi$? The supplied aid reports a sharp conformal systolic estimate. Its volume-preserving family only makes the average length of fixed canonical lines large and does not disprove the general claim.
