Foliations and Diffeomorphism Groups — Question 56

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a regularity class $C^r$ with $r\geq 1$, or with $r=\omega$ denoting real-analytic regularity, consider local $C^r$ diffeomorphisms $f:U\to V$, where $U,V\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ are open neighborhoods of $0$ and $f(0)=0$. Two such maps represent the same germ if they agree on some neighborhood of $0$. Composition of representatives, after restriction to suitable neighborhoods, defines the group $\mathcal G^r$ of these germs.

For an abstract group $G$, an infinite-order element $g\in G$ is called a distortion element if there exist a finitely generated subgroup $H\leq G$ containing $g$ and a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of $H$ such that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|g^n|_S}{n}=0, \] where $|h|_S$ is the minimum number of generators from $S$ whose product is $h$.

Question (source Question 56). For each regularity class intended by the source, characterize the distortion elements of $\mathcal G^r$.

Unresolved conventions. The source does not specify whether germs must preserve orientation. The definition above includes both orientations; an orientation-preserving interpretation would instead use the subgroup represented by maps with $f'(0)>0$. The source also does not specify whether its range of $r$ includes $\infty$ or noninteger regularity, or whether finite-order elements are to be counted as distorted. The displayed definition uses the infinite-order convention; under the convention allowing finite-order elements, such elements satisfy the displayed sublinear word-length condition automatically.

The source excludes $r=0$, asserting that every one-dimensional homeomorphism germ is distorted and that every nontrivial such element is conjugate to its square. This is a supplied explanatory assertion, not a verified premise here. Its conjugacy claim cannot hold for the full group including orientation-reversing germs, since squaring yields an orientation-preserving germ whereas conjugation preserves orientation type. Thus the parenthetical explanation leaves the intended orientation convention unresolved.

Short English statement

Classify the germs at $0$ of $C^r$ diffeomorphisms fixing $0$ whose powers have sublinear word length in some finitely generated subgroup, including the real-analytic case $r=\omega$. The intended orientation restriction, precise regularity range, and treatment of finite-order elements require clarification.
