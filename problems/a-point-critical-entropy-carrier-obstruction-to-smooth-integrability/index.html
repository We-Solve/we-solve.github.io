Math-1467

A point-critical entropy-carrier obstruction to smooth integrability

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Bangert's Question 10.1.1 asks whether a closed surface of genus at least two admits a Riemannian metric with completely integrable geodesic flow. The source distinguishes independence of the additional integral on an open everywhere dense set from independence on a set additionally having full measure.

In the smooth interpretation used by the aid, let $H(x,p)=\tfrac12\|p\|_g^2$ on $T^*M$, and let $F$ be a smooth first integral, $\{F,H\}=0$. On the unit energy shell $S=H^{-1}(1/2)$, write $f=F|_S$ and \[ \mathcal R=\{v\in S:df_v\ne0\},\qquad \mathcal C=\operatorname{Crit}(f)=\{v\in S:df_v=0\}. \] Independence of $dF,dH$ is equivalent to this nonvanishing of the restricted differential. The point-critical set $\mathcal C$ is not the larger inverse image of all critical values. Let $\phi^t$ be the geodesic flow and let $m_L$ be normalized Liouville measure on $S$.

The paired aid reports that every ergodic $\phi^1$-invariant probability measure $\mu$ with $\mu(\mathcal C)=0$ has zero metric entropy. It consequently reports \[ h_{\mathrm{top}}(\phi^1|_S)= h_{\mathrm{top}}(\phi^1|_{\mathcal C}). \] For every metric on a closed genus-at-least-two surface, the aid uses positive topological entropy. Thus an integral whose regular set $\mathcal R$ is open and dense must have a closed nowhere-dense point-critical set carrying all that topological entropy. In the full-measure version, $m_L(\mathcal C)=0$ as well, and the reported conclusion is \[ h_{m_L}(\phi^1)=0. \] Positive topological entropy and zero Liouville entropy are not contradictory: other invariant measures may carry entropy on $\mathcal C$. These are necessary conditions, not an existence or nonexistence theorem for complete smooth integrability.

The original context attributes to Mañé [Mañé1996] a generic Anosov-or-zero-Liouville-exponents statement for Hamiltonian flows on surfaces. The topology, energy-level conventions and exact genericity statement are omitted; it is retained as source context rather than a fully specified theorem here. The text ends by introducing an "easier version" of Bangert's question, but that subsequent question is not included and is not guessed.

Short English statement

The source asks for smooth complete integrability on a higher-genus surface and distinguishes dense-open from full-measure regularity. The aid reports that any candidate's entropy must be concentrated on the actual critical points of its integral. In the full-measure version that entropy-carrying set is Liouville-null, while Liouville entropy is zero; the existence question remains unresolved.
