Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A semidirect product combines a group and an action by automorphisms. In a semidirect discrete-logarithm problem, a projected component of $(g,\phi)^n$ is given and the exponent n is sought; the corresponding Diffie--Hellman-type task combines projected secret powers. A composition series is a finite normal series with simple factors.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Cryptanalysis of SDLP

Various problems relating to the cryptanalysis, both quantum and classical, of SDLP remain open.

On the quantum side the question of quantum equivalence of SDLP and the CDH-type problem underpinning semidirect product key exchange (SCDH) remains unproven, though we have strong cause to suspect their equivalence given the quantum equivalence of the heavily related vectorisation and parallelisation problems. The decomposition attack of Imran and Ivanyos, providing in some cases a reduction of SDLP to the abelian hidden subgroup problem, relies on the quantum ability to quickly compute a composition series of a solvable group. Whether similar quantum speedups in the computation series exist for non-solvable groups remains open.

On the classical side, we would like to develop a kind of Sylow theory for the projected sets implied by the semidirect product structure. It would also be useful to develop a classical method of computing the size of the acting group when one considers the semigroup variant of SDLP (currently we have only quantum methods). [S2]

Scope qualification.
The source bundles several questions and includes a semigroup variant; the actual group encodings and action are not fixed, and no one uniform equivalence is assumed.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Cryptanalysis of SDLP

Various problems relating to the cryptanalysis, both quantum and classical, of SDLP remain open.

On the quantum side the question of quantum equivalence of SDLP and the CDH-type problem underpinning semidirect product key exchange (SCDH) remains unproven, though we have strong cause to suspect their equivalence given the quantum equivalence of the heavily related vectorisation and parallelisation problems. The decomposition attack of Imran and Ivanyos, providing in some cases a reduction of SDLP to the abelian hidden subgroup problem, relies on the quantum ability to quickly compute a composition series of a solvable group. Whether similar quantum speedups in the computation series exist for non-solvable groups remains open.

On the classical side, we would like to develop a kind of Sylow theory for the projected sets implied by the semidirect product structure. It would also be useful to develop a classical method of computing the size of the acting group when one considers the semigroup variant of SDLP (currently we have only quantum methods).

Attempt and results

The source bundles quantum reductions, composition series, a Sylow-like theory, and semigroup orbit sizes. We try the group-orbit route and calculate the one-dimensional affine case. These are elementary rederivations, not solutions to the whole agenda or independently checked quantum algorithms.
Projected products are permutation orbits.
Let $G$ be a finite group, $\phi$ an automorphism, and $s_0=1$, $s_t=g\phi(g)\cdots\phi^{t-1}(g)$ for $t\ge1$. The map $T(h)=g\phi(h)$ is bijective, with inverse $T^{-1}(h)=\phi^{-1}(g^{-1}h)$, and $s_t=T^t(1)$ by induction. Its finite orbit therefore has a least positive return time $N$. If $s_t=s_u$ with $t\ge u$, apply $T^{-u}$ to get $s_{t-u}=1$. Euclidean division by $N$ shows this occurs exactly when $N\mid t-u$: a smaller positive remainder would contradict minimality of $N$. Thus $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ acts freely and transitively on the orbit by $[a]\cdot s_b=s_{a+b}$. This is well defined by the proved equality criterion. An exponent solution $a$ for $s_a$ gives the combined state $s_{a+b}=s_a\phi^a(s_b)$. Efficient computation of this expression requires an efficient representation of $\phi^a$; the algebraic identity does not itself provide a polynomial-cost coherent oracle or a reverse reduction from SCDH.
A naive Sylow transfer already fails.
Take the additive group $G=\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$, $g=1$, and $\phi(h)=-h$. Then $T(h)=1-h$, so $s_0=0$, $s_1=1$, $s_2=0$ and $N=2$. The orbit has size two even though $|G|=3$, and its projected set $\{0,1\}$ is not closed under addition. Consequently the proposed shortcut of treating the projected set as a subgroup of $G$, with order dividing $|G|$ and the same Sylow primes, is false. This does not rule out a different structural theory of the acting permutation group.
A fully classified affine scalar family.
Let $q=p^f$ and $T(z)=az+v$ on $\mathbb F_q$, starting at $s_0=0$. If $v=0$, the orbit is constant. Assume $v\ne0$. If $a=0$, the sequence is $0,v,v,\ldots$, so its tail has length one and its cycle length one. If $a=1$, then $s_t=tv$, which has exact period $p$: $tv=0$ precisely when the characteristic $p$ divides the integer $t$. If $a\ne0,1$, induction on the recurrence gives \[ s_t=v\frac{a^t-1}{a-1}. \] Its return time is the multiplicative order of $a$, and an observed value $s_t$ determines $a^t=1+(a-1)s_t/v$ exactly. Thus this scalar instance of SDLP reduces to an ordinary discrete logarithm and its orbit size to an ordinary multiplicative order. The identity proves neither task easy for arbitrary fields under a bit-cost model. In particular, an orbit-size formula that invokes an unknown order is not a completed classical size algorithm. The noninvertible $a=0$ case demonstrates why a semigroup cannot simply be assumed to have a purely periodic orbit starting at the initial state.
