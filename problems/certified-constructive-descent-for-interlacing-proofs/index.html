Certified constructive descent for interlacing proofs

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Original request. Make existence proofs for Kadison--Singer (KS) constructive, particularly proofs using stable polynomials and interlacers. The workshop note supplies no specific witness problem, quantitative constants, input encoding, randomness requirement or runtime target. A real polynomial is stable when it has no zero with all variables in the open upper half-plane; interlacing methods compare roots of associated real-rooted polynomials. These terms identify a proof framework, not a complete algorithmic specification.

Attached unreviewed interval-descent claim. Consider a finite rooted interlacing tree: node polynomials are real rooted of a common degree with positive leading coefficients, each internal polynomial is an appropriate positive convex combination of its children, and the children have a common interlacer. Write $r_v$ for the largest root at node $v$. The needed descent property is that some child $c$ has $r_c\le r_v$.

Suppose every child has a certified root interval $[L_c,U_c]$ containing $r_c$. At each visited node choose a child minimizing $U_c$. With $\eta_v=\max_c(U_c-L_c)$ at that node, the aid's additive-width bound is represented by \[r_{\mathrm{leaf}}\le r_{\mathrm{root}}+\sum_{v\text{ on the chosen descent}}\eta_v.\] Thus depth at most $h$ and child widths at most $\eta$ give additive loss at most $h\eta$. The width control concerns children and requires the actual interlacing/parent-compatibility property; arbitrary parent labels are insufficient.

The aid additionally claims that, for every deterministic local selector receiving only fixed width-$\eta$ child intervals, there is a binary degree-one interlacing tree of depth $h$ on which its additive loss is at least $h\eta/2$. This lower bound is expressly in that restricted local interval-information model. It does not rule out inspecting exact polynomial coefficients, refining intervals, using additional global information, or obtaining a polynomial-time algorithm for KS by another method. It shows the upper bound's linear dependence on depth is necessary up to a factor of two within the stated model.

The aid mentions a "parent-label obstruction" without its precise statement. It also reports a greedy discrepancy bound $\alpha$ for pairwise commuting rank-one tight frames. In the standard sign-discrepancy convention this would concern commuting $A_i=v_iv_i^*$ with $\sum_iA_i=I$, $\|v_i\|^2\le\alpha$, and a signed operator sum bounded in norm by $\alpha$; the summary does not specify its normalization or algorithm, so this is an explicit interpretation requiring review, not a silently fixed theorem. The interval certification cost and a complete constructive KS procedure are not supplied. All claims require independent review.

Short English statement

The source asks for constructive Kadison--Singer proofs without defining a computational target. The aid gives certified approximate interlacing descent and a matching linear-depth obstruction for a restricted interval-only selector. It does not provide a complete efficient KS algorithm or a general computational lower bound.
