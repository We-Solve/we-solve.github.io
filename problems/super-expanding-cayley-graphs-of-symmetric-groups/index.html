Math-3928

Super-Expanding Cayley Graphs of Symmetric Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each integer $n\geq 2$, let $G_n=\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ be the group of all permutations of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A symmetric generating set is a set $X_n\subseteq G_n\setminus\{e\}$ satisfying $X_n^{-1}=X_n$ and $\langle X_n\rangle=G_n$. Its undirected Cayley graph has vertex set $G_n$, with $g$ adjacent to $gs$ for $s\in X_n$. Symmetry and exclusion of the identity are explicit graph conventions for this draft; a uniformly bounded generating set can be symmetrized without losing uniform boundedness. Omitting the trivial group at $n=1$ does not affect the family question.

A Banach space $E$ is uniformly convex if, for every $0<\varepsilon\leq 2$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that $\|u\|_E,\|v\|_E\leq 1$ and $\|u-v\|_E\geq\varepsilon$ imply $\|(u+v)/2\|_E\leq 1-\delta$.

The supplied record does not define super-expander. Under the uniform Banach-space Poincaré interpretation, the question is whether there exist an integer $D\geq 1$ and symmetric generating sets $(X_n)_{n\geq 2}$, with $|X_n|\leq D$ for every $n$, such that, for every uniformly convex Banach space $E$, there exists a constant $C_E\in(0,\infty)$ for which every $n\geq 2$ and every map $f:G_n\to E$ satisfy \[ \frac{1}{|G_n|^2}\sum_{g,h\in G_n}\|f(g)-f(h)\|_E^2 \leq \frac{C_E}{|G_n|\,|X_n|} \sum_{g\in G_n}\sum_{s\in X_n}\|f(g)-f(gs)\|_E^2. \] The generating sets must be chosen independently of $E$, whereas $C_E$ may depend on $E$ but not on $n$ or $f$. This gives uniformly bounded degrees and vertex counts $|G_n|=n!$ tending to infinity; taking $E={\mathbb{R}}$ includes the ordinary expander requirement.

This is an explicit interpretation requiring source confirmation. The supplied text does not determine whether its intended definition instead requires ordinary expansion together with failure of uniform coarse embedding into every uniformly convex Banach space. No equivalence between these formulations is asserted here. The source's sentence about whether "the converse holds" has no identifiable antecedent in the supplied excerpt and is not imposed as a further mathematical assertion.

Short English statement

Can one choose uniformly bounded generating sets for every symmetric group so that the resulting Cayley graphs form a family of super-expanders? The displayed formulation requires uniform Poincaré inequalities for maps into each uniformly convex Banach space. The source does not specify its definition of super-expansion, so this interpretation requires human review.
