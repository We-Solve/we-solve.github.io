Math-5066

Exact vertical tangent defects for canonical genus-four curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied verification field gives the evaluation map \[ \pi:\mathcal M_{g,n}(\mathbb P^r,\beta)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Conf}(\mathbb P^r,n),\qquad (f:C\to\mathbb P^r,p_1,\ldots,p_n)\longmapsto(f(p_1),\ldots,f(p_n)). \] The configuration target requires the open locus where marked images are distinct. The precise moduli component, map/embedding convention, and ground field are not reproduced. The verification field notes an initial source typo $\mathbb P^n$ where this displayed setup consistently uses $\mathbb P^r$.

AIM degeneration problem 1.2. Find understandable examples exhibiting behavior different from the expected one, especially unexpected tangent dimensions relevant to deciding dominance of $\pi$. The source expressly presents this as example hunting, not a request for a universal classification. It suggests using degeneration to propagate or analyze tangent-dimension defects; the exact hypotheses for any such deduction must be stated.

Original Remark 1.3 mentions attaching canonical curves and assumes nonnegative Brill--Noether number, conventionally $\rho(g,r,d)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)$. It says dominance is known to fail in the indicated canonical examples and that a special canonical-type divisor gives a larger defect. Its divisor typography and final "P3.12" fragment are garbled; the report below interprets the relevant divisor as bicanonical, but the remark cannot be quoted as a fully recovered formula.

Supplied research-aid context, not a verified solution. Let $C\subset\mathbb P^3$ be a general smooth canonical genus-four $(2,3)$ complete intersection, and let $D$ be a general reduced divisor of degree $n\ge7$. For embedded deformations its normal bundle is $N_C=\mathcal O_C(2)\oplus\mathcal O_C(3)$, and the report claims the evaluation-kernel dimension \[ h^0(N_C(-D))=(9-n)_++(15-n)_+, \qquad(x)_+=\max(x,0). \] The Euler-expected value is $\max(0,24-2n)$. Thus it identifies degrees $10,11,12,13,14$ as failures of normal-bundle interpolation.

For $n=10,11,12$, it reports differential cokernel dimension $n-9$, equal to the codimension of the locus of configurations lying on a quadric, giving nondominance even though source/target dimension permits dominance. The quadric-incidence argument is important: in positive characteristic, differential nonsurjectivity alone need not exclude an inseparable dominant map. Field and genericity assumptions are not specified in the attempt.

At degree $12$, the report contrasts a general divisor with a reduced $D\in|2K_C|$. It claims \[ N_C(-D)\cong\mathcal O_C\oplus K_C, \qquad(h^0,h^1)=(5,5), \] whereas a general degree-twelve divisor gives $(3,3)$. This is a jump from the already defective generic value, not from the Euler-expected zero. These precise examples are unreviewed partial claims and do not by themselves extend a dominance theorem to every degeneration.

Short English statement

Find explicit tangent-dimension defects and use degeneration to study evaluation-map dominance. The attached genus-four example supplies a normal-bundle formula, quadric-incidence coranks, and a further bicanonical jump under missing field/genericity hypotheses.
