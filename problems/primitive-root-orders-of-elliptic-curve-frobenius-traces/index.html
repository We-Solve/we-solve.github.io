Math-4071

Primitive-Root Orders of Elliptic-Curve Frobenius Traces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source fixes an elliptic curve $E$, but does not specify its ground field or any additional hypotheses on $E$. Its notation presupposes reductions over prime fields. The following formulation is therefore conditional on having a specified good reduction $E_p/{\mathbb{F}}_p$ at each rational prime under consideration; the intended class of elliptic curves remains unresolved.

Let $\mathcal P_E$ denote the set of those rational primes $p$ at which the specified reduction $E_p/{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is good. For $p\in\mathcal P_E$, define the Frobenius trace by \[ a_p=p+1-\#E_p({\mathbb{F}}_p), \] and let $\overline{a_p}$ be its residue class modulo $p$. Whenever $p\nmid a_p$, define \[ \operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)=\min\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}:a_p^n\equiv 1\pmod p\}. \] The residue class $\overline{a_p}$ is a primitive root modulo $p$ if it generates ${\mathbb{F}}_p^{\times}$, equivalently if $\operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)=p-1$.

For the fixed curve $E$, the two source questions are whether, respectively, \[ \#\{p\in\mathcal P_E:p\nmid a_p,\ \operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)=p-1\}=\infty \] and \[ \#\{p\in\mathcal P_E:p\nmid a_p,\ \operatorname{ord}_p(a_p)\geq 6\}=\infty. \] The source gives no restriction such as a specified ground field or a condition on the endomorphisms or torsion of $E$. Whether further hypotheses were intended must be checked before these questions can be treated as a fully specified assertion about every curve in a particular class.

The original passage also asserts, as context, that if $p$ does not divide the discriminant (or the conductor) of $E$, and $a_p\neq 0$, then the multiplicative order of $a_p$ modulo $p$ equals the degree of the extension of ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ generated by the $p$-torsion points of $E$ over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$. This contextual assertion is supplied source material, not a verified auxiliary theorem here. Its precise formulation requires a ground field, a suitable discriminant or conductor, clarification of the meaning of the $p$-torsion points in characteristic $p$, and an invertibility hypothesis: the integer condition $a_p\neq 0$ alone does not express $p\nmid a_p$. No small primes are silently discarded in the two displayed questions.

Short English statement

For a fixed elliptic curve with the prime-field reductions presupposed by the source, ask whether its Frobenius trace generates ${\mathbb{F}}_p^{\times}$ for infinitely many good primes $p$, and whether it has multiplicative order at least six for infinitely many such primes. The ground field and any additional curve hypotheses are unspecified. Supplied dated curation labels the pair partially solved and reports a sketch for the second question; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
