Math-280

Finite-time angular screening for the all-flat set

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented surface of genus at least two with Gaussian curvature $K\leq0$. Let $g_t:SM\to SM$ be its unit-speed geodesic flow and let $\mu_L$ be normalized Liouville measure. The source asks whether $g_t$ is ergodic for $\mu_L$. Define the invariant all-flat set and its complement by \[ \operatorname{Sing}=\{v\in SM:K(\pi(g_tv))=0\text{ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}, \qquad\operatorname{Reg}=SM\setminus\operatorname{Sing}. \] The source reports known ergodicity on $\operatorname{Reg}$, the vectors whose geodesics encounter negative curvature. Since genus at least two ensures a nonempty open negative-curvature region, $\mu_L(\operatorname{Reg})>0$. Under this known regular-set result, full Liouville ergodicity is equivalent to $\mu_L(\operatorname{Sing})=0$. This is not a claim that every basepoint with $K=0$ belongs to a wholly flat geodesic.

For $x\in M$ and $T\geq0$, let \[ A_T(x)=\{v\in S_xM:K(\gamma_v(t))=0\text{ for every }|t|\leq T\}, \qquad a_T(x)=\frac{\operatorname{angle}(A_T(x))}{2\pi}. \] Here $\operatorname{angle}$ is circle length in the unit tangent fibre and $\gamma_v$ is the complete geodesic with initial vector $v$. The closed measurable sets decrease as $T$ grows. Liouville disintegration and continuity from above give the aid's exact screening identity \[ \mu_L(\operatorname{Sing}) =\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{\operatorname{Area}(M)} \int_M a_T(x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A(x) =\frac1{\operatorname{Area}(M)}\int_M a_\infty(x)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} A(x), \] where $A_\infty(x)=\bigcap_{T\geq0}A_T(x)$ and $a_\infty(x)=\operatorname{angle}(A_\infty(x))/(2\pi)$. Thus positive singular Liouville measure requires a positive-area set of footpoints with positive angular measure of complete two-sided all-flat directions. Merely having infinitely many such directions is insufficient.

A quantitative sufficient screening condition is the following explicit form of angular escape. If there are times $T_j\to\infty$, measurable sets $B_j\subset M$, and numbers $\eta_j,\epsilon_j\to0$ with \[ \operatorname{Area}(B_j)/\operatorname{Area}(M)\leq\epsilon_j, \qquad a_{T_j}(x)\leq\eta_j\quad(x\notin B_j) \] almost everywhere, then the screening integral is at most $\epsilon_j+\eta_j$, so $\mu_L(\operatorname{Sing})=0$. The aid does not establish such estimates for every nonpositively curved surface.

Another sufficient criterion is countable ruling: outside a base set of area zero, suppose every element of $A_\infty(x)$ is among values of countably many measurable unit-direction fields, defined on measurable subsets of $M$. Then each such fibre is countable and has zero angular measure, hence the singular set is Liouville-null and the known regular-set ergodicity yields full ergodicity. Covering by countably many unoriented directions also suffices after taking both signs. This is a conditional criterion, not a proof that all all-flat sets have such a covering.

The source also asks the higher-dimensional analogue for a closed rank-one nonpositively curved manifold. A geodesic has rank one when it has no nonzero orthogonal parallel Jacobi field along its complete trajectory; the full rank includes the velocity field. It reports ergodicity on rank-one vectors but an unresolved null-measure problem for higher-rank vectors. It warns that several 1980s claims of a solution relied on an incorrect proof that this complement has measure zero. Separately, the source attributes to Knieper a unique ergodic measure of maximal entropy for rank-one geodesic flow. This distinct measure does not settle ergodicity for Liouville measure. The final heading "6.2. Zero curvature geodesics and flat strips" is a boundary fragment, not an additional supplied question.

Short English statement

The remaining obstruction to Liouville ergodicity is the measure of complete all-flat geodesics. The aid expresses that measure as a limit of finite-time angular averages. Countably many flat direction fields, or quantitative angular escape, would force it to vanish, but those conditions are not established in general.
