Math-1689

Constant-degree denominator removal and stable polynomial endpoints

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb B^n=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^n:\|z\|<1\}$, and let $f:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N$ be a proper rational holomorphic map. Properness means compact inverse images of compact sets. Write $f=P/q$, with a common polynomial denominator, the components of $P$ and $q$ having no common nonconstant factor, and set \[ \deg f=\max(\deg P_1,\ldots,\deg P_N,\deg q)=d. \] The source question, attributed to J. D'Angelo, is whether there exists a polynomial proper map $p:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N$ of degree exactly $d$, and a continuous family $(f_t)_{0\le t\le1}$ of proper holomorphic maps into the same target ball, such that \[ f_0=f,\qquad f_1=p. \] For definiteness, continuity may be read in the compact-open topology on holomorphic maps, equivalently locally uniform continuity in the parameter. The original wording specifies properness of every $f_t$, but does not specify a topology or explicitly require rational intermediate maps. It requires equal degrees of the endpoints; a path with $\deg f_t=d$ for every $t$ is a stronger conclusion.

The supplied research aid reports fixed-target rational homotopies of constant algebraic degree for maps spherically equivalent to a polynomial, where spherical equivalence means composition with automorphisms of the source and target balls. It reports in particular that degree-two maps from $\mathbb B^2$ satisfy the requested conclusion. It further describes an automorphism-slot denominator-removal lemma: an automorphism appearing in a factor multiplied by a polynomial $h$ can be contracted to a unitary map while preserving properness; constant degree requires the stated degree dominance and nondivisibility of $h$ by the affine denominator. The precise slot construction is not supplied here and cannot be taken as a lemma for arbitrary denominators.

As a further example the aid reports a constant-degree path $F_{(1-t)a}$ in the standard degree-three Faran--Huang--Ji--Zhang family, which is not spherically equivalent to a polynomial, ending at \[ p(z,w)=(z^2,\sqrt2\,zw,zw^2,w^3)\in{\mathbb{C}}^4. \] No formula or admissible parameter range for $F_a$ is supplied in the record. Finally the aid reports a separate stabilized result: after including $f$ by zero coordinates in a ball of target dimension \[ M=n+\max\{N,d(n-1)+1\}, \] a proper rational homotopy reaches a degree-$d$ Whitney polynomial, with intermediate degrees at most $d+1$. This statement permits a larger target and therefore does not answer the original fixed-target problem. The trailing source words "Squared norms" are the next heading, not a further hypothesis.

Short English statement

Can every rational proper ball map be deformed through proper maps in its fixed target to a polynomial of the same degree? The supplied aid gives special constant-degree homotopies and a result after increasing the target dimension; the stabilized result leaves the original requirement unresolved.
