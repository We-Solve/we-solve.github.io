Math-1428

The free-factor complex, recursive corank-one links, and a dynamical transfer test

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F_n$ be a free group of rank $n$, and $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)=\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)/\operatorname{Inn}(F_n)$. An iwip is fully irreducible: no positive power preserves the conjugacy class of a nontrivial proper free factor. Polynomial growth refers to polynomial growth of the lengths of iterated conjugacy classes in a fixed free basis. For an isometry $\gamma$ of a graph with its edge metric, write $\tau(\gamma)=\lim_{k\to\infty}d(x,\gamma^kx)/k$; ellipticity means bounded orbits, a stronger general condition than merely $\tau=0$.

The workshop asks for a $\delta$-hyperbolic graph with an $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$-action such that every iwip has positive translation length; polynomially growing elements are elliptic; the construction is elegant, beautiful, useful, or truthful; local structure supports induction; and the complex and links are highly connected. The last three requirements have no numerical or formal specification. Connectivity and links refer naturally to a simplicial complex with the graph as one-skeleton, since higher connectivity of a graph alone would be a different demand. The source's parenthetical equality between ellipticity and zero translation length is therefore recorded as an informal shorthand rather than a general equivalence.

The supplied aid reports, for $n\ge3$, a partial realization by the free-factor complex $\mathcal F_n$, whose vertices are conjugacy classes of nontrivial proper free factors and whose simplices are chains of inclusions up to conjugacy. It reports hyperbolicity of its one-skeleton, $\tau(\gamma)>0$ for every iwip, a finite vertex orbit for every polynomially growing element, and $(n-3)$-connectivity of the complex. It further reports, for every corank-one free factor $A$ of rank $n-1$, a natural simplicial identification \[ \operatorname{Lk}_{\mathcal F_n}([A])\cong\mathcal F(A)\cong\mathcal F_{n-1}. \] This is the conjugacy-class inclusion complex convention, including the rank-two link convention; other modified low-rank graphs are not silently substituted. These assertions do not specify all links or settle every qualitative workshop requirement.

A precise working version of the aid's two-arrow transfer test is: for equivariant Lipschitz maps $Z\to X\to Y$, positive stable lengths in $Y$ force positive stable lengths in $X$, while bounded orbits in $Z$ force bounded orbits in $X$. In particular an equivariant $L$-Lipschitz map $X\to Y$ has $\tau_Y(\gamma)\le L\tau_X(\gamma)$. The aid applies this to report, for even $n=2g\ge4$, that no $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$-equivariant Lipschitz map runs from the co-surface graph $CS_{2g}$ to $\mathcal F_{2g}$: geometric iwips are the stated dynamical obstruction. The source does not define the co-surface graph or give the full two-arrow theorem, so the displayed criterion is an explicit working formulation and the graph-specific conclusion remains an attributed claim.

The original discussion includes further goals, not conclusions of this partial realization. Lucas Sabalka asked for reasonable behavior of infinite-order elements, with block-upper-triangular or "non-hyperbolic elements with hyperbolic mapping torus" left as questions. Thierry Coulbois asked for naturality like Teichmüller space, and Martin Lustig discussed truth and beauty. Juan Souto stressed unforeseen curve-complex applications; Yair Minsky recalled applications motivating his and Masur's theorem. Proposed applications were bounded asymptotic dimension, uniformity in Dahmani--Guirardel--Osin, rigidity of lattice maps via bounded cohomology, and quasi-isometric rigidity. The notes describe the DGO statement as: for each iwip $g$, some positive integer $q$ makes every normal closure of $g^{qk}$, $k\ge1$, free and purely iwip. They attribute its graph input to Bestvina--Feighn; Vincent Guirardel questioned uniformity. No uniformity quantifier is specified here.

Martin Bridson's suggested application involving infinite-dimensional second cohomology of every infinite subgroup is retained as speculation with unspecified coefficients and ordinary-versus-bounded convention, not a theorem. Minsky suggested hyperbolic or $\operatorname{CAT}(0)$ links and global-to-local quasi-projections for induction, and Karen Vogtmann suggested high connectivity and cohomological applications. Candidate objects listed were the free-factor complex; the trivial-edge-group splitting complex, said quasi-isometric to the sphere complex; variants of splittings over ${\mathbb{Z}}^n$ or with rank cutoffs suggested by Matt Clay and related to length by Kasra Rafi; the poset of commensurability classes of abelian subgroups of linearly growing automorphisms, related by Souto and Bridson to Dehn multitwists and cut systems; and the Kapovich--Lustig graph. No definitions or proofs for these alternative candidates are supplied.

Short English statement

The aid reports the free-factor complex as a partial answer: iwips translate positively, polynomial growth gives finite vertex orbits, and connectivity plus corank-one links support some induction. Qualitative goals, alternative complexes, applications, and the even-rank co-surface obstruction remain separately qualified source claims.
