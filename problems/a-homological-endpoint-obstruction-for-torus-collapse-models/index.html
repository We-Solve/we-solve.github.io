A homological endpoint obstruction for torus collapse models

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether an $n$-torus can collapse to an interval and already records the answer "No," essentially settled at the workshop. The suppressed setting, made explicit by the supplied verification, is a sequence of closed smooth Riemannian tori $(T^n,g_i)$ with one uniform lower sectional-curvature bound $\sec_{g_i}\geq\kappa$, for fixed $n$, converging in Gromov--Hausdorff distance to a nondegenerate interval $[0,L]$, $L>0$. The reported theorem rules out such a sequence. No two-sided curvature bound is required in this statement. A point is not the nondegenerate interval in question.

The aid credits Zamora with the subsequent general proof and reports later full torus stability for compact limits by Bruè--Naber--Semola, without giving precise hypotheses or a full statement of that stronger theorem. The label solved is a supplied literature assessment; the detailed mathematical result in this particular attempt is a restricted cohomogeneity-one model obstruction.

For that obstruction let a connected closed $(k+1)$-manifold $M$, $k\geq1$, have an interval-type cohomogeneity-one $T^k$ group diagram with finite principal isotropy $H$, and endpoint isotropy groups $K_-,K_+$ satisfying $K_\pm/H\cong S^1$. The two disk-bundle pieces glue along the principal orbit $T^k/H$. Using this common orbit to identify rational first homology with ${\mathbb{Q}}^k$, let $L_-,L_+\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^k$ be the nonzero one-dimensional rational slope subspaces killed by the endpoint disk attachments. They must be transported through the chosen equivariant gluing before comparison. The aid's formula is \[ H_1(M;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Q}}^k/(L_-+L_+), \] \[ b_1(M;{\mathbb{Q}})=k-2+\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(L_-\cap L_+)\leq k-1. \] The intersection dimension is one for equal slope lines and zero otherwise. In particular this total space cannot be $T^{k+1}$, whose rational first Betti number is $k+1$. Finite isotropy may matter integrally; this assertion uses rational homology.

This excludes the specified global torus-action diagrams. It does not establish that every arbitrary lower-curvature collapsing sequence has such a global cohomogeneity-one model, and therefore is not itself the full proof of the general no-interval theorem.

Short English statement

Closed Riemannian tori with a uniform lower sectional-curvature bound cannot converge to a nondegenerate interval, according to the supplied historical answer and later literature assessment. The attempt proves a more restricted model obstruction: an interval-type torus action with circle endpoint isotropy kills too much rational first homology for its total space to be a torus.
