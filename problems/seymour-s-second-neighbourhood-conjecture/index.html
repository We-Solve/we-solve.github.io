Math-1116

Seymour's Second Neighbourhood Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D=(V,A)$ be a finite nonempty oriented graph: it has no loops, no parallel arcs, and never contains both $uv$ and $vu$. The first outneighbourhood of $v$ is $N^+(v)=\{u\in V:vu\in A\}$. Define its second outneighbourhood by \[ N^{++}(v)=\{w\in V\setminus(\{v\}\cup N^+(v)): \exists u\in V\text{ with }vu,uw\in A\}. \] Thus $N^{++}(v)$ consists of vertices at directed distance exactly two from $v$, not all vertices reachable by a two-step walk. A vertex already in $N^+(v)$ is excluded even if it also has such a walk. Both sets count distinct vertices, without multiplicity.

Seymour's second neighbourhood conjecture asks whether every finite nonempty oriented graph has at least one vertex $v$ satisfying \[ |N^+(v)|\le |N^{++}(v)|. \] The desired vertex may depend on the entire orientation. There is no claim that every vertex, or a prescribed vertex, satisfies the inequality. A sink has no outneighbours and therefore already satisfies it with both sides zero.

The orientation condition cannot simply be dropped: in the complete symmetric digraph every other vertex is a first outneighbour, leaving no second outneighbours in the exact-distance sense. The asserted bound has coefficient exactly one; a positive lower ratio strictly below one would be a different, weaker statement. All finite orders and all underlying simple graphs are included.

Short English statement

Must every oriented graph contain a vertex with at least as many vertices at outward distance two as at outward distance one?

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For a nonempty finite oriented graph $D$, with no loops or oppositely directed pair of edges, let $N_1^+(v)$ be the out-neighbors and $N_2^+(v)$ the vertices at directed distance exactly two. The claimed theorem gives \[\exists v\in V(D):\quad|N_1^+(v)|\le|N_2^+(v)|.\] For triangle-free $D$ a corollary yields a vertex with $d^+(v)\le|Z(v)|$ and $n-1\ge2d^+(v)+d^-(v)$, where $Z(v)$ consists of non-neighbors excluding $v$. Hence $\delta^+\ge n/3$ and $\delta^-\ge n/3$ force a directed triangle; a digraph regular in both directions with degree $r$ and girth four has $n\ge3r+1$. These consequences require the specified in-degree conditions and do not give the full out-degree-only Caccetta--Häggkvist assertion.

Outline of the argument

Seymour second neighborhoods: A smallest counterexample is pruned to yield strict neighborhood inequalities for all proper vertex sets. Replacing vertices by transitive tournaments strengthens these to a discrete concavity inequality. Extremal families of ordered pairs and an augmented bipartite matching are then controlled by two factorizations of one matrix, contradicting that inequality.
