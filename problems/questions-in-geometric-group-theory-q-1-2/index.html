Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.2

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A finite $K(G,1)$ is a finite connected CW complex with fundamental group $G$ and contractible universal cover. A group admitting such a space is said to be of type $F$. A finitely generated group is Gromov hyperbolic if a Cayley graph for a finite generating set has $\delta$-thin geodesic triangles for some $\delta\geq0$.

Let $G$ admit a finite $K(G,1)$. Assume that $G$ has no subgroup isomorphic to ${\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}$, and that it is balanced in the following precise sense: \[ \forall x\in G\ \left(\operatorname{ord}(x)=\infty\right),\quad \forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad \left(\exists g\in G:\ gx^mg^{-1}=x^n\right) \Longrightarrow |m|=|n|. \] Bestvina's Question 1.2 asks whether every such $G$ is Gromov hyperbolic. There is no assumption that $G$ is already a subgroup of a hyperbolic group; that is a property of the reported counterexample.

The supplied research summary reports a negative answer by Italiano--Martelli--Migliorini: a nonhyperbolic group $G$ of type $F$, with geometric and cohomological dimension $4$, that embeds in a hyperbolic group. It reports that this embedding implies the absence of ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$ and the displayed balanced property, the latter by translation lengths. It also reports a simultaneous negative answer to Bestvina's Question 1.1 concerning the absence of Baumslag--Solitar subgroups. The described construction uses circle-valued Morse theory and Bestvina--Brady-type finiteness arguments on fibering cusped hyperbolic $5$-manifolds, with a fiber-kernel after suitable filling. These are statements of the supplied literature assessment, whose exact construction and hypotheses have not been independently checked in this draft.

Short English statement

Bestvina asks whether a group with a finite classifying space must be hyperbolic if it contains no ${\mathbb{Z}}^2$ and conjugate powers of any infinite-order element have equal absolute exponents. The supplied research summary reports a nonhyperbolic type-$F$ subgroup of a hyperbolic group as a counterexample.
