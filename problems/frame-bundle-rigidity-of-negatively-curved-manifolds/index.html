Math-4233

Frame Bundle Rigidity of Negatively Curved Manifolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A closed manifold is a compact manifold without boundary. For a Riemannian manifold $(X,g_X)$, strictly negative sectional curvature means \[ K_{g_X}(\sigma)<0 \] for every two-dimensional tangent plane $\sigma\subseteq T_xX$ and every $x\in X$.

The supplied question is: for every pair of closed negatively curved Riemannian manifolds $M,N$ satisfying \[ \pi_1(M)\cong\pi_1(N), \] must their $k$-frame bundles be isomorphic, for every admissible $k$? The source does not specify the meaning of frame-bundle isomorphism or the precise range of $k$, so this question does not yet determine a unique mathematical assertion.

For clarity, one conventional meaning of the $k$-frame bundle of an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold $X$, for an integer $1\leq k\leq n$, is the orthonormal tangent-frame bundle \[ V_k(TX,g_X) = \left\{(x,v_1,\ldots,v_k): x\in X,\ v_i\in T_xX,\ g_X(v_i,v_j)=\delta_{ij}\text{ for }1\leq i,j\leq k \right\}, \] with projection $p_X(x,v_1,\ldots,v_k)=x$. Its fiber is the real Stiefel manifold $V_k({\mathbb{R}}^n)$. Another convention uses all ordered linearly independent tangent vectors instead of orthonormal ones. Neither convention is explicitly selected in the record.

Under the orthonormal convention and an additional common-dimension hypothesis $\dim M=\dim N=n$, a bundle-isomorphism interpretation would ask whether there exist maps \[ \Phi:V_k(TM,g_M)\longrightarrow V_k(TN,g_N), \qquad f:M\longrightarrow N, \qquad p_N\circ\Phi=f\circ p_M, \] that constitute an isomorphism in a specified bundle category. A topological bundle isomorphism requires appropriate homeomorphisms; a smooth bundle isomorphism requires appropriate diffeomorphisms. Any further structure to be preserved must also be specified. An isomorphism of total spaces alone is a different possible target. These are conditional interpretations, not hypotheses or conclusions supplied by the source. The source also does not require the maps to induce a prescribed isomorphism of fundamental groups.

The parenthetical assertion that the answer is known for $k=1,2$ is retained as a supplied claim, for values within the applicable frame range. Its precise category and hypotheses require verification alongside the intended general question.

Short English statement

The question asks whether isomorphic fundamental groups force closed negatively curved manifolds to have isomorphic $k$-frame bundles. The source reports affirmative results for $k=1,2$ and labels the general question open. The frame convention, admissible dimensions, and meaning of bundle isomorphism remain unspecified, so a definitive formal statement requires human review.
