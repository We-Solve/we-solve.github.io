Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Question 62 ([Has-Mor1996])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T$ be the abstract $\Theta$-graph: two distinct vertices joined by three edges. The supplied question asks whether every metric on $S^2$ admits a stationary realization of $T$. It does not specify the class of metrics or the admissible realizations.

For a precise conditional formulation, suppose that $g$ is a smooth Riemannian metric on $S^2$. A nondegenerate regular realization consists of distinct points $p,q\in S^2$ and three smooth regular curves $\gamma_i:[0,1]\to S^2$, for $i=1,2,3$, with $\gamma_i(0)=p$ and $\gamma_i(1)=q$. Its total length is \[ L_g(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_0^1\lVert\dot\gamma_i(t)\rVert_g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] Call this realization stationary if the first variation of $L_g$ vanishes for every smooth variation through such realizations, allowing both common endpoints to move. Equivalently, each edge is a geodesic up to reparametrization, and the outgoing unit tangent vectors balance at each vertex: \[ \sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\dot\gamma_i(0)}{\lVert\dot\gamma_i(0)\rVert_g}=0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{-\dot\gamma_i(1)}{\lVert\dot\gamma_i(1)\rVert_g}=0. \] Thus the three incident edges meet at pairwise angles of $2\pi/3$ at each vertex.

An embedded realization additionally requires each $\gamma_i$ to be injective and \[ \gamma_i([0,1])\cap\gamma_j([0,1])=\{p,q\}\qquad(i\ne j). \] For either the regular-realization convention or the embedded-realization convention, the corresponding precise question is: \[ \text{For every smooth Riemannian metric }g\text{ on }S^2, \text{ does there exist a stationary realization of }T \text{ satisfying that convention?} \] These are conditional formulations. The supplied record does not determine whether embeddedness is required, whether other degeneracies or intersections are allowed, or whether "metric" means a smooth Riemannian metric. Those qualifications must be resolved before selecting a definitive formulation.

Short English statement

Does every metric on the two-dimensional sphere admit a stationary $\Theta$-graph? Under a smooth Riemannian interpretation, stationarity means that the three edges are geodesic arcs whose outgoing unit tangents balance at both vertices. The supplied record leaves the metric class and the requirement of embeddedness unspecified.
