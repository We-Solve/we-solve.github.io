Math-1144

Soficity, Hyperlinearity, and Surjunctivity of Major Group Classes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

All groups below are regarded as discrete. The supplied record collects several questions rather than one fully specified conjecture.

For a positive integer $n$, let $\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ have normalized Hamming distance \[ d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma,\rho)=\frac{1}{n}\left|\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\sigma(i)\ne\rho(i)\}\right|. \] A group $G$ is sofic if, for every finite subset $E\subseteq G$ and every $0<\varepsilon<1$, there exist $n\geq1$ and a map $\sigma:G\to\operatorname{Sym}(n)$ such that $\sigma(1)=\operatorname{id}$, \[ d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma(gh),\sigma(g)\sigma(h))<\varepsilon\qquad(g,h\in E), \] and \[ d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma(g),\sigma(h))>1-\varepsilon\qquad(g,h\in E,\ g\ne h). \] For complex $n\times n$ matrices, write $\tau_n(A)=n^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(A)$ and $\|A\|_{2,n}=\tau_n(A^*A)^{1/2}$. A group $G$ is hyperlinear if, for every finite $E\subseteq G$ and every $0<\varepsilon<1$, there exist $n\geq1$ and a map $\varphi:G\to U(n)$ with $\varphi(1)=I_n$ such that \[ \|\varphi(gh)-\varphi(g)\varphi(h)\|_{2,n}<\varepsilon\qquad(g,h\in E), \] and \[ |\tau_n(\varphi(g))|<\varepsilon\qquad(g\in E\setminus\{1\}). \] A group $K$ is residually finite if, for every $k\ne1$, there exist a finite group $Q$ and a homomorphism $q:K\to Q$ with $q(k)\ne1$.

For a finite nonempty set $A$, equip $A^G$ with the product topology and the shift action \[ (g\cdot x)(h)=x(g^{-1}h). \] The group $G$ is surjunctive if every continuous $G$-equivariant injective map $A^G\to A^G$ is surjective, for every such $A$.

The questions supplied under permanent UnsolvedMath ID $30001795$, source code catalogue entry, are as follows.

  1. Determine soficity and, separately, hyperlinearity for Thompson's group $F$, one-relator groups, Burnside groups, Burger--Mozes groups, Gromov's monster groups, word-hyperbolic groups, and random finitely presented groups. Here Thompson's $F$ is the group of increasing piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of $[0,1]$ with finitely many dyadic breakpoints and slopes in $\{2^k:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}$. A one-relator group has a presentation $\langle X\mid r\rangle$ with a single defining relator. A word-hyperbolic group admits a finite generating set whose Cayley graph has $\delta$-thin geodesic triangles for some $\delta\geq0$.

    In particular, the universal word-hyperbolic questions are \[ \forall G\;\left(G\text{ word-hyperbolic}\Longrightarrow G\text{ sofic}\right) \] and \[ \forall G\;\left(G\text{ word-hyperbolic}\Longrightarrow G\text{ hyperlinear}\right). \] The original statement additionally says "equivalently as conjectured precisely when all such groups are residually finite." This qualification is retained as supplied source wording. Its intended logical relationship to the two displayed questions is unclear; no equivalence with universal residual finiteness is asserted here.

    The source does not specify the intended Burnside groups, the constructions or parameters of the Burger--Mozes and Gromov monster groups, or the probability model and limiting regime for random presentations. It also does not explicitly delimit the generating sets in the one-relator question. Those portions therefore remain a list of targets requiring clarification, rather than fully quantified assertions about specified families. In particular, "random" cannot be assigned an almost-sure or asymptotic-probability interpretation from this record alone.

  2. For each property $\mathcal P\in\{\text{soficity},\text{hyperlinearity}\}$, ask whether the following holds: for every pair of groups $G_1,G_2$ having $\mathcal P$, every residually finite group $K$, and every pair of injective homomorphisms $\iota_i:K\hookrightarrow G_i$, does the amalgamated free product \[ G_1*_{K}G_2 = (G_1*G_2)\big/\big\langle\!\big\langle \iota_1(k)\iota_2(k)^{-1}:k\in K \big\rangle\!\big\rangle \] have $\mathcal P$? The denominator is the normal closure in the free product. No finite-generation assumption or additional condition on the embeddings is supplied. This gives two closure questions with the same property imposed on both factors; the source does not clearly specify a separate mixed sofic/hyperlinear question.

  3. For every pair of finitely generated surjunctive groups $G_1,G_2$, must both $G_1\times G_2$ and $G_1*G_2$ be surjunctive?

  4. Classify the sofic approximations of a direct product of two nonabelian free groups. Writing $F(X)$ for the free group on a set $X$, the cleaned record describes products $F(X)\times F(Y)$ with $|X|,|Y|\geq2$, but does not specify the ranks. For a countable group $H$, a sequential sofic approximation can be described by maps $\sigma_j:H\to\operatorname{Sym}(n_j)$, with $n_j\to\infty$ and $\sigma_j(1)=\operatorname{id}$, satisfying \[ d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma_j(gh),\sigma_j(g)\sigma_j(h))\longrightarrow0 \quad(g,h\in H) \] and \[ d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma_j(g),\sigma_j(h))\longrightarrow1 \quad(g,h\in H,\ g\ne h). \] This explains one usual meaning of the objects mentioned; it does not impose a countability assumption missing from the source. The original wording asks whether an "analogous classification" holds for the direct product of two free groups, without supplying the antecedent classification. The cleaned wording specifies sofic approximations and nonabelian factors. Neither version specifies the equivalence relation or invariants for classification, so the intended classification problem remains incomplete.

Short English statement

This record asks about soficity and hyperlinearity of several group families, preservation under amalgamation over residually finite subgroups, preservation of surjunctivity under direct and free products, and classification of sofic approximations of products of free groups. Several family parameters and the classification target are unspecified. The supplied residual-finiteness qualification is ambiguous and is not asserted as an equivalence. The supplied open status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
