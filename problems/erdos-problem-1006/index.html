Erdős Problem #1006

Status Solved · erdosproblems.comLLM status Solved from source status

Erdős problems openaigpt 6 astra ultra 100% cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Statement and answer.
Does every graph of girth greater than four admit an acyclic orientation that remains acyclic after reversing any one edge? No. We reconstruct the Nešetřil--Rödl probabilistic method, proving that for every fixed $g\geq5$ there is a graph of girth exactly $g$ with no such orientation.
What an obstruction looks like.
In an acyclic orientation, reversing $uv$, originally directed from $u$ to $v$, creates a directed cycle precisely when there is another directed path from $u$ to $v$. Thus it suffices to force a cycle \[ v_1v_2\cdots v_gv_1 \] whose edges $v_iv_{i+1}$ all point forwards and whose closing edge points from $v_1$ to $v_g$.

Every finite acyclic digraph has a topological ordering: it has a vertex of indegree zero, since following incoming edges otherwise produces a directed cycle; remove such a vertex and induct. Therefore we will build an undirected graph such that every ordering of its vertices contains the displayed cycle with $v_1<\cdots<v_g$.

Sparse hypergraph containers for the cycles.
A Berge cycle of length $j\geq2$ in a hypergraph consists of distinct vertices $x_1,\ldots,x_j$ and distinct hyperedges $E_1,\ldots,E_j$, with $x_i,x_{i+1}\in E_i$, indices cyclically. In particular, forbidding Berge two-cycles makes different hyperedges intersect in at most one vertex.

For sufficiently large $N$, there is a $g$-uniform hypergraph on $N$ vertices with no Berge cycles of lengths $2,\ldots,g-1$ and with \[ M\geq c_gN^{1+1/g} \tag{1} \] hyperedges, where $c_g>0$ is fixed. To prove this, select each $g$-set independently with probability \[ \rho=N^{-(g-1)+1/g}. \] The expected number of selected hyperedges is $\binom Ng\rho=(1/g!+o(1))N^{1+1/g}$, and its variance is at most its mean. With probability tending to one, at least half that asymptotic number are selected.

For a possible Berge $j$-cycle, there are at most $N^j$ choices for its designated vertices and at most $N^{(g-2)j}$ choices for its distinct hyperedges containing the designated pairs. Its probability of being present is $\rho^j$. Consequently the expected total number $Z$ of forbidden Berge cycles is at most \[ \sum_{j=2}^{g-1}N^{(g-1)j}\rho^j =O_g(N^{(g-1)/g})=o(N^{1+1/g}). \] Counting a cycle multiple times only makes this upper bound larger. Markov's inequality and the preceding edge concentration give an outcome in which deleting one hyperedge from each forbidden cycle leaves $1$, for example with $c_g=1/(4g!)$ once $N$ is sufficiently large.

Choose one polygon inside each hyperedge.
Independently in each hyperedge, choose uniformly one of its \[ h=(g-1)!/2 \] unoriented Hamiltonian cycles, and let $G$ be the union of these graph edges. Linearity of the hypergraph means that no graph edge belongs to two different hyperedges.

The resulting graph has no cycle of length less than $g$. Here are details for mixed cycles. If all edges of a graph cycle lie in one hyperedge, they lie in its single chosen $g$-cycle, so that cycle has length $g$. Otherwise group cyclically consecutive edges that come from the same hyperedge. The transition vertices and the corresponding hyperedges form a closed walk in the hypergraph's incidence graph. It has no immediate reversal: successive hyperedge labels differ, and the two transition vertices of each run are distinct because the graph cycle is simple. Any closed walk without immediate reversals contains a simple cycle. A graph cycle of length $j<g$ would therefore give an incidence cycle of length at most $2j$, hence a forbidden Berge cycle of length at most $j$. This is impossible. Since $1$ ensures that there is at least one hyperedge, $G$ has girth exactly $g$.

Simultaneous control of every vertex ordering.
Fix an ordering of the $N$ vertices. In any hyperedge, list its vertices in increasing order as $v_1,\ldots,v_g$. Exactly one of its $h$ possible polygons is \[ v_1v_2,\ v_2v_3,\ldots,v_{g-1}v_g,\ v_gv_1. \] Thus the probability that no hyperedge supplies the desired ordered cycle is $(1-1/h)^M\leq e^{-M/h}$. There are $N!\leq N^N$ orderings, so the probability that any ordering fails is at most \[ N!\,e^{-M/h}\leq \exp\!\left(N\log N-(c_g/h)N^{1+1/g}\right)<1 \] for sufficiently large $N$. Some choice of polygons therefore works for all orderings.

If an orientation of this $G$ has a directed cycle, it already fails the requirement. Otherwise take a topological ordering and the ordered polygon forced above. Its path from $v_1$ to $v_g$ is directed forwards, as is the closing edge $v_1v_g$. Reversing that closing edge creates a directed cycle. This completes the counterexample.

Attribution and assessment.
The hypergraph placement and union bound over vertex orderings reconstruct the method of J. Nešetřil and V. Rödl, On a probabilistic graph-theoretical method, Theorem 2 and Corollaries 3--4, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1978-0507350-7. The author's publicly available copy is https://www.researchgate.net/publication/239285245_On_a_probabilistic_graph-theoretical_method. See https://www.erdosproblems.com/1006, checked 24 September 2026. All probabilistic and orientation steps needed for the negative answer are supplied here; no novelty or local formal verification is claimed.
