The repeated-label reduction and its sharp threshold

Definitions and mathematical statement

The extracted source is an implication from the preceding Seymour--Sullivan rainbow conjecture to the Caccetta--Häggkvist conjecture, not a new independent conjecture. Its broken ceiling expression is interpreted by the supplied aid as $\lceil n/\delta_H^+\rceil$.

Let $H$ be a finite simple loopless digraph on $n\ge2$ vertices, with minimum outdegree $\delta=\delta_H^+\ge1$. Directed cycle length counts arcs. The Caccetta--Häggkvist assertion is that $H$ has a directed cycle of length at most \[ \left\lceil n/\delta\right\rceil. \] For a list-labeled digraph $G$, specify arc sets $E_1,\ldots,E_k$, allowing overlap. An arc may receive label $i$ when it lies in $E_i$; a rainbow directed cycle admits distinct labels on all its arcs. Its total labeled outdegree at $v$ is \[ \Lambda^+(v)=\sum_{i=1}^k d^+_{E_i}(v), \] counting repeated membership in different label sets.

The load-threshold principle used in the supplied reduction is: \[ \Lambda^+(v)\ge n\quad\text{for every }v \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ has a rainbow directed cycle}. \] The aid invokes the Seymour--Sullivan reachability conjecture to imply this principle when reachability uses positive-length rainbow paths. The full preceding conjecture is not supplied, so the principle is stated conditionally rather than treated as an established general theorem.

For the complete-list lift, take $G=H$, $k=\lceil n/\delta\rceil$, and $E_i=A(H)$ for every $i$. Then $\Lambda^+(v)=k d_H^+(v)\ge n$. A rainbow cycle uses at most $k$ labels, hence gives the desired cycle in $H$. The aid reports that the load-threshold principle restricted to complete-list lifts is exactly equivalent to the Caccetta--Häggkvist assertion: conversely a cycle of length at most $\lceil n/\delta\rceil\le k$ can receive distinct labels whenever $k\delta\ge n$.

The aid highlights a reachability convention issue. Without a rainbow cycle, positive-length rainbow reachability from a vertex excludes the vertex itself and has size at most $n-1$. If the trivial path is counted, that cardinality ceiling becomes $n$; the same threshold argument then requires $k\delta>n$. The resulting choice \[ k=\lfloor n/\delta\rfloor+1 \] exceeds $\lceil n/\delta\rceil$ by one exactly when $\delta$ divides $n$. This is a loss in the proof under that convention, not a disproof of the desired cycle bound.

Separately, for functional digraphs, meaning exactly one outgoing arc per vertex, the aid reports that the threshold principle is unconditionally true for arbitrary arc-label lists and that $n$ is sharp. A directed $n$-cycle with the same $n-1$ labels available on every arc demonstrates why the smaller threshold $n-1$ cannot suffice. This restricted theorem does not prove the general rainbow principle or the general Caccetta--Häggkvist conjecture.

Short English statement

The source explains a conditional rainbow-label reduction to the general short-directed-cycle conjecture. The supplied aid makes the ceiling and positive-length reachability conventions explicit and reports a sharp threshold theorem only for functional digraphs.
