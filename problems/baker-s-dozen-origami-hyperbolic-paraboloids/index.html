Baker's Dozen — Origami hyperbolic paraboloids

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Consider the origami hyperbolic-paraboloid crease pattern referred to in Section 8 of Tabachnikov's source. In addition to its displayed creases, insert an "invisible" straight fold along a chosen diagonal of every elementary trapezoid. For a specified crease pattern, diagonal choices, and folding configuration, let $S$ denote the resulting piecewise-linear surface, with each face planar.
  1. Determine the shape of $S$ as the pattern folds. The source asks for the shape of the folded surface, rather than explicitly only for a refinement limit.
  2. Determine the surfaces produced by analogous constructions from other patterns of fold lines; the supplied research summary mentions circular pleats as an example.
The supplied statement does not give the crease coordinates, boundary, compatible folding constraints, parameterization of folding depth, or a mathematical meaning of "invisible." Thus it does not determine a unique surface from the text alone. The second question also leaves the class of alternative patterns and the intended notion of determining a shape unspecified.

The supplied later research summary reports a result for a more specified triangulated construction with one added diagonal per trapezoid: it is generically a one-degree-of-freedom origami, and as the pleats refine its folded shapes converge, at each folding depth, to a hyperbolic paraboloid of the form \[ z=k(x^2-y^2), \] where $k$ is a monotone function of the folding angle. The summary states independence of square spacing and of diagonal choices among the symmetric schemes. It supplies neither the exact admissible schemes nor a convergence topology or angle-to-$k$ formula. This limiting claim does not itself identify a finite piecewise-linear surface with the smooth quadratic graph. The summary also reports bistability with two mirror-symmetric stable states in the cited work, without supplying its mechanical stability model. It regards the first question as resolved but the second as only partially addressed by $N$-gon hypar extensions and framework remarks.

Short English statement

The problem asks for the shapes obtained by folding a diagonal-triangulated origami hypar pattern and analogous patterns. The supplied 2019 research summary reports a hyperbolic-paraboloid refinement limit for specified symmetric constructions, while the broader question about other crease patterns remains open. The precise finite folding model and limiting formulation need the original sources.
