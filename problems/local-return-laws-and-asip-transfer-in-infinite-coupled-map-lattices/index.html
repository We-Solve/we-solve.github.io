Local return laws and ASIP transfer in infinite coupled map lattices

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 19 asks about return times for infinite coupled map lattices and related almost sure invariance principles. The source specifies no local map, coupling, invariant measure, target sets, observable class or desired rate, so these are broad research questions rather than a single universal claim. Its final line, "Open Problems and Questions: Wednesday," is an ensuing heading, not a mathematical hypothesis.

The aid gives a finite-range transfer statement. Let a configuration be $x\in M^{{\mathbb{Z}}^d}$, with a measurable update $F$ whose coordinate $(Fx)_i$ depends only on $(x_{i+u})_{u\in D}$, where $D\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ is finite. Let a cylinder target $A$ and a bounded local observable $\psi$ depend on a finite set $W$ of coordinates. Write \[ K_n(W)=\bigcup_{0\le t\le n}(W+tD), \] where $tD$ is the $t$-fold Minkowski sum of $D$, with $0D=\{0\}$; it is not scalar multiplication of the offsets. The joint return-indicator path and local-observable path through time $n$, and hence their partial sums, depend only on the initial coordinates in $K_n(W)$.

Compare the infinite lattice with a periodic finite lattice carrying the same local rule, and assume the quotient map is injective on the entire relevant cone, so no periodic identifications change this path computation. Let $\mu$ and $\mu_L$ be their invariant laws, with the cone coordinates identified, and set \[ \delta=\|\mu|_{K_n(W)}-\mu_L|_{K_n(W)}\|_{\rm TV}, \] using $\|\nu-\nu'\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_B|\nu(B)-\nu'(B)|$. The complete joint path laws then differ in total variation by at most $\delta$.

For first return $\tau_A(x)=\inf\{t\ge1:F^tx\in A\}$, let $a=\mu(A)>0$ and $a_L=\mu_L(A_L)>0$, where $A_L$ is the corresponding periodic target. The aid's conditional-tail bound is \[ \left|\mu(\tau_A>n\mid A)-\mu_L(\tau_{A_L}>n\mid A_L)\right| \le\frac{2\delta}{\min(a,a_L)}. \] Thus small unconditional errors alone need not control a rare-event conditional law. For targets $A_\varepsilon$ with mass $a_\varepsilon\to0$, transfer on times $n$ of order $1/a_\varepsilon$ is certified when the torus contains the whole corresponding cone and its marginal discrepancy satisfies $\delta=o(a_\varepsilon)$. Since $|a_L-a|\le\delta$, the target masses then agree asymptotically. A finite-volume return limit is still an input; the estimate transfers that limit rather than proving an exponential law for every CML.

For local Birkhoff sums $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\psi\circ F^j$, an almost sure invariance principle means a coupling on an enlarged probability space to a Brownian motion with the stated covariance and an almost-sure error smaller than the central-limit scale, with a precise error rate depending on theorem hypotheses. The aid reports that a volume-uniform Gouezel characteristic-function condition (H) passes from periodic volumes to the infinite system through these finite-path comparisons and then yields an ASIP by Gouezel's theorem. The full condition (H), its parameters, the required cone-marginal convergence, all further theorem hypotheses and the quantitative ASIP rate are not printed in this record. No unconditional ASIP for arbitrary finite-range CML follows merely from finite dependence cones.

The research summary also reports existing CLT, local limit theorem and spectral ASIP results for important infinite expanding CML, and a 2026 theorem giving an exponential first-collision law and compound-Poisson collision counts for an infinite ${\mathbb{Z}}^d$ collision CML. These are model-specific supplied literature assessments; the authors, model hypotheses and full citations are absent, so they are not asserted as general statements or independently verified current literature.

Short English statement

The question concerns return laws and strong probabilistic approximations in infinite coupled map lattices. The aid transfers finite-time local paths from a periodic model when the whole dependency cone is represented correctly. Rare-event conditional laws require the approximation error to be smaller than the target mass; ASIP transfer additionally requires a uniform characteristic-function condition and its theorem hypotheses.
