Math-3352

Strict Majorant Properties at Fixed Noneven Exponents

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\geq 1$ be an integer and let $\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^d$. Write $e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$ and $n\cdot x=\sum_{j=1}^d n_jx_j$. For a finite positive real exponent $p$, put \[ \|f\|_{L^p([0,1]^d)}=\left(\int_{[0,1]^d}|f(x)|^p\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x\right)^{1/p}, \] where integration uses Lebesgue measure. For $0<p<1$, this expression is a quasi-norm. The source does not explicitly specify whether its intended exponent range includes $0<p<1$ or the endpoint $p=\infty$; the formulas here describe finite positive exponents, subject to that unresolved scope qualification.

Using finitely supported coefficient families, say that $\Gamma$ has the strict majorant property at exponent $p$ if, for every pair of real families $(a_n)_{n\in\Gamma}$ and $(A_n)_{n\in\Gamma}$ having finite support and satisfying $|a_n|\leq A_n$ for every $n\in\Gamma$, one has \[ \left\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma}a_ne(n\cdot x)\right\|_{L^p([0,1]^d)} \leq \left\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma}A_ne(n\cdot x)\right\|_{L^p([0,1]^d)}. \] Thus $A_n\geq0$. Despite the terminology, the defining inequality is non-strict. Finite support makes the sums unambiguous for arbitrary $\Gamma$; it is an explicit formulation convention requiring confirmation, since the supplied excerpt does not state a support or convergence hypothesis for infinite $\Gamma$.

Classification question. For each fixed admissible exponent $p\notin\{2,4,6,\ldots\}$, characterize all sets $\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ that satisfy the displayed majorant inequality. The target is a characterization at each fixed exponent, rather than solely a characterization of sets satisfying the property simultaneously at all exponents. The intended exponent range and the interpretation of infinite coefficient families remain unresolved as described above.

Supplied contextual qualifications. A set $\Gamma$ is affinely independent if, whenever $n_1,\ldots,n_k$ are distinct elements of $\Gamma$ and real numbers $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ satisfy $\sum_jc_j=0$ and $\sum_jc_jn_j=0$, all $c_j$ vanish. The supplied dated assessment reports that the all-exponent classification is solved, with the strict majorant property at every exponent holding exactly for affinely independent frequency sets, and that broad counterexample families are known at non-even exponents. It expressly states that this theorem does not supply a complete characterization for each fixed non-even $p$ and arbitrary $\Gamma$. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified assertions here; the phrase "every exponent" inherits the source's unspecified exponent range.

The original excerpt also reports that, for every infinite $\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^d$, there are infinitely many positive integers $m$ for which there exists a real coefficient family $(a_n)_{n\in\Gamma}$ such that, simultaneously for every $p\in(2m,2m+2)$, \[ \left\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma}|a_n|e(n\cdot x)\right\|_{L^p([0,1]^d)} < \left\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma}a_ne(n\cdot x)\right\|_{L^p([0,1]^d)}. \] The support or convergence conditions for these reported families are absent from the supplied excerpt. Finally, the excerpt contains the sentence "In particular, this holds for every set $\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ of cardinality at least $d+2$." Its antecedent is missing, so this sentence cannot be assigned a precise mathematical conclusion from the supplied record.

Short English statement

For a fixed non-even exponent $p$, determine which integer frequency sets $\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ have the property that replacing real Fourier coefficients by nonnegative coefficientwise majorants never decreases the $L^p$ size. The supplied assessment labels the problem partially solved: it distinguishes a reported all-exponent classification from the requested fixed-exponent characterization. The exponent range, support conditions for infinite sets, and one incomplete contextual assertion require review.
