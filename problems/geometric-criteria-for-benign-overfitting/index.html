Geometric Criteria for Benign Overfitting

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

In the linear interpolation setting, data form a matrix $X$ and response vector $y$. For a specified norm on the parameter space, a minimum-norm interpolator chooses $\widehat\beta\in\arg\min\{\lVert\beta\rVert:X\beta=y\}$. Interpolation means zero training residual, possibly fitting noise. Prediction risk is expected loss on a fresh observation from the specified data law; excess risk subtracts the population benchmark. Benign overfitting means that excess risk tends to zero along the source asymptotic sequence even though the observations are interpolated. Geometry includes the norm ball and its relation to the design and noise distributions.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Identify necessary and sufficient geometric conditions under which, for an arbitrary norm, the associated minimum-norm interpolating estimator exhibits benign overfitting. [S2]
Scope qualification.
Sample-size/dimension scaling, noise law, loss and tie-breaking among minimizers are part of the estimator model. A universal criterion is requested, rather than a claim that arbitrary norms automatically yield consistent interpolation.

A quadratic-norm calculation and the first arbitrary-norm obstruction

The primary source specifically seeks geometric understanding beyond Euclidean norms and flags dependence between noise and design as a difficulty. Bartlett--Long--Lugosi--Tsigler give covariance-based results in a specified Hilbert-space model; their assumptions are not a theorem for arbitrary norms. We first isolate an exact conditional calculation. Let $X\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ have full row rank, let $Q$ be positive definite, and use $\|\beta\|_Q^2=\beta^TQ\beta$. Conditional on this fixed design, suppose $y=X\beta_*+\xi$, $\mathbb E\xi=0$ and $\mathbb E\xi\xi^T=\sigma^2I_n$. Let the second-moment matrix of an independent test covariate be $\Sigma\succeq0$.
Proposition: Put $B=Q^{-1}X^T(XQ^{-1}X^T)^{-1}$. The unique minimum-$Q$-norm interpolator is $\widehat\beta=By$, and its expected excess squared prediction error conditional on $X$ is \[ \|\Sigma^{1/2}(BX-I)\beta_*\|_2^2+\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(B^T\Sigma B). \]
Proof: The matrix $XQ^{-1}X^T$ is positive definite: its quadratic form is positive for nonzero $v$ because full row rank implies $X^Tv\ne0$. Thus $XB=I$. If another interpolator is $By+z$ with $Xz=0$, then $z^TQBy=z^TX^T(XQ^{-1}X^T)^{-1}y=0$. Its squared norm is therefore $(By)^TQ(By)+z^TQz$, uniquely minimized at $z=0$. The estimation error is $(BX-I)\beta_*+B\xi$. Expanding its $\Sigma$-quadratic form, the cross term has zero expectation and the noise term equals $\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(B^T\Sigma B)$ by the covariance assumption. Independent mean-zero test noise cancels when excess risk is measured against the true linear predictor. ∎
Along any sequence in this conditional model, both nonnegative terms must vanish for the averaged excess risk to vanish. This exact identity is a known linear-algebra baseline, not the sought interpretable arbitrary-norm criterion. Its derivation relies on the quadratic objective producing a linear map $y\mapsto By$ and on the stated conditional noise moments. An attempted extension that disregards tie-breaking fails already for $\ell_1$. With $X=(1,1)$ and response $1$, every vector $(t,1-t)$ for $0\le t\le1$ has minimal $\ell_1$ norm one: the triangle inequality gives a lower bound $|\beta_1+\beta_2|=1$, attained by all these vectors. For test second moment $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)$ and zero target, their prediction errors are $t^2+4(1-t)^2$, including values $4,1,$ and $4/5$ at $t=0,1,4/5$. Thus the norm and interpolation constraint alone do not determine risk without the selection rule. This is a fixed-design diagnostic, not an i.i.d. asymptotic counterexample to a properly specified estimator.

Verification and remaining gaps

Rational full-row-rank designs and positive definite $Q$ were used to check $XB=I$, the orthogonality identity and the exact noise average over all sign-noise vectors. The $\ell_1$ risks were checked exactly. No distribution-free geometry theorem for nonquadratic norms or design-dependent noise is obtained; the displayed trace identity does not bypass those source difficulties.
