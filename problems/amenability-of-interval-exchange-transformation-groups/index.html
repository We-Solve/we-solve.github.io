Math-1113

Amenability of Interval-Exchange Transformation Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the standard orientation-preserving convention, let $\operatorname{IET}([0,1))$ be the group, under composition, of bijections $T:[0,1)\to[0,1)$ for which there exist a finite partition $0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_n=1$ and real numbers $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ such that \[ T(x)=x+c_i\qquad\text{for }x\in[a_{i-1},a_i),\quad 1\leq i\leq n. \] Thus each transformation exchanges finitely many half-open intervals by translations.

A group $G$, regarded as a discrete group, is amenable if there exists a linear functional $m:\ell^\infty(G;{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}$ on all bounded real-valued functions on $G$ satisfying \[ m(\mathbf{1})=1,\qquad m(f)\geq0\ \text{whenever }f\geq0,\qquad m(L_gf)=m(f)\ \text{for every }g\in G, \] where $(L_gf)(h)=f(g^{-1}h)$.

Question. Is every subgroup $G\leq\operatorname{IET}([0,1))$ amenable? No countability or finite-generation hypothesis is imposed. Equivalently, is $\operatorname{IET}([0,1))$ itself amenable, since amenability passes to subgroups?

Unresolved conventions. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify endpoint conventions, whether interval exchanges with flips are excluded, or whether any topology is intended. The formulation above uses half-open intervals, translations without flips, and discrete-group amenability; these conventions require confirmation against the source.

Short English statement

Must every subgroup of the interval-exchange transformation group be amenable, with no restriction on its size or generators? The draft uses the standard translation-only convention and discrete-group amenability. The supplied record labels the problem open; that status is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
