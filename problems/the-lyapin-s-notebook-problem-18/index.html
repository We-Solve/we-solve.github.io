The Lyapin's Notebook — Problem 18

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A commutative semigroup is a nonempty set $S$ equipped with an associative operation $\cdot$ satisfying $x\cdot y=y\cdot x$ for all $x,y\in S$. No identity element or finite generation hypothesis is imposed. An endomorphism of $S$ means a map $f:S\to S$ such that \[ f(x\cdot y)=f(x)\cdot f(y)\qquad(x,y\in S). \] Here endomorphisms are ordinary semigroup homomorphisms, with no additional identity-preservation requirement.

Problem 18 (A.A. Borisov). Describe, up to semigroup isomorphism, all commutative semigroups $S$ for which \[ \forall f\in\operatorname{End}(S)\;\forall x,y\in S, \qquad f(x)=f(y)\Longrightarrow x=y. \] Thus the requested description must give necessary and sufficient conditions for every endomorphism to be injective.

Supplied finite-generation qualification. The source record reports that [Borisov4] proves that the finitely generated commutative semigroups with this property are precisely subsemigroups of an infinite monogenic semigroup or finite ordinal sums of such semigroups. An infinite monogenic semigroup is $\{a,a^2,a^3,\ldots\}$, with distinct positive powers and multiplication $a^ma^n=a^{m+n}$. The record does not define its ordinal-sum construction or supply the bibliographic identity of [Borisov4]. The treatment of the one-element semigroup also requires clarification: it satisfies the stated endomorphism condition, but its inclusion in the reported classification is not explained. Consequently this classification is retained as a source-qualified report, not asserted here as a verified theorem or used to restrict the general problem. The nonempty-set convention above is explicit; the record does not state whether empty semigroups are admitted.

Short English statement

Classify all commutative semigroups whose every multiplication-preserving self-map is injective. The question is attributed to A.A. Borisov. The supplied record labels it open and reports a finitely generated classification, but that report has unresolved terminology, a missing reference, and a degenerate-case qualification requiring review.
