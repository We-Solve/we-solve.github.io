Mass-Constrained Neumann and Steklov Eigenvalue Maximization

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A membrane model must specify its geometric domain, its mass distribution, the relevant differential operator and boundary conditions, and the admissible variations. For such a model $X$, write $m(X)$ for its total mass. When its Neumann spectrum is discrete and has an ordered sequence of positive eigenvalues, write \[ 0<\mu_1(X)\leq\mu_2(X)\leq\cdots, \] listing eigenvalues with multiplicity and omitting all zero eigenvalues. Write $\sigma_k(X)$ for its $k$th Steklov eigenvalue once the Steklov problem and its indexing convention have been specified. The source explicitly says "nonzero" for the Neumann eigenvalue but does not specify whether the Steklov indexing includes the zero eigenvalue.

The supplied question is: for prescribed membrane mass, how large can $\mu_k$ and $\sigma_k$ be? Its conditional optimization formulation is the following. For a prescribed mass $M>0$ and index $k\in\{1,2,\ldots\}$, after choosing admissible membrane classes $\mathcal A_N$ and $\mathcal A_S$ on which the respective eigenvalues are defined, determine \[ \sup_{\substack{X\in\mathcal A_N\\m(X)=M}}\mu_k(X) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sup_{\substack{X\in\mathcal A_S\\m(X)=M}}\sigma_k(X). \] These classes are placeholders for missing source specifications, not additional hypotheses asserted by this draft. No finiteness or attainment of either supremum is asserted.

The supplied record does not specify the admissible domains or geometries, the mass functional, density assumptions, regularity, connectedness, or the precise weighted or unweighted Neumann and Steklov boundary value problems. In particular, it does not say whether mass is an interior quantity for the Neumann problem and a boundary quantity for the Steklov problem. Consequently, the displayed optimization problems cannot be made uniquely precise from the supplied record alone; their model, admissible classes, and Steklov indexing require clarification from the source.

Short English statement

For a membrane with prescribed mass, determine the largest possible $k$th positive Neumann eigenvalue and $k$th Steklov eigenvalue. The admissible membrane models, meaning of mass, and Steklov indexing are unspecified, so the optimization formulation remains conditional. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
