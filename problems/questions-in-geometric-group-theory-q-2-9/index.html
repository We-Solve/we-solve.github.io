Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.9

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a finite set and let $m_{st}=m_{ts}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ for distinct $s,t\in S$. The associated Artin group is \[ A=\langle S\mid \underbrace{stst\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ letters}} =\underbrace{tsts\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ letters}} \text{ whenever }m_{st}<\infty\rangle. \] An infinite label imposes no relation on that pair. No relations $s^2=1$ are included; imposing those would instead produce the associated Coxeter group.

A finite $K(A,1)$ is a connected CW complex $X$ with finitely many cells, fundamental group isomorphic to $A$, and contractible universal cover. Equivalently, $X$ is aspherical: its homotopy groups in every degree at least two vanish. Does every Artin group defined by finite data as above admit a finite $K(A,1)$?

Finiteness means a finite total number of cells, not merely finitely many cells in each dimension or a finite-dimensional complex. The desired complex need not be the standard presentation complex, whose higher homotopy may be nontrivial, nor any particular complex constructed from the defining diagram. Thus proving asphericity of a canonical candidate would suffice, but the question permits other constructions. The phrase Artin group here includes arbitrary finite defining matrices, not just those whose associated Coxeter group is finite. A solution for one diagram family therefore does not address the entire quantified class.

Short English statement

Does every Artin group with finitely many standard generators admit a finite aspherical classifying space?
