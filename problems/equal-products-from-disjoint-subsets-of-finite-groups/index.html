Math-6617

Equal Products from Disjoint Subsets of Finite Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k\geq 2$ be an integer, let $G$ be a finite group of order $n=|G|$, and let $S\subseteq G$. An ordering of a subset $T\subseteq S$ is a list $(t_1,\ldots,t_m)$ containing each element of $T$ exactly once, where $m=|T|$. Its ordered product is $t_1\cdots t_m$; in a noncommutative group this product may depend on the ordering. The empty ordered product is conventionally $1_G$.

The supplied question asks whether, for every integer $k\geq 2$, there exists a constant $c(k)>0$, depending only on $k$, such that for every finite group $G$ and every $S\subseteq G$, \[ |S|\geq c(k)\log |G| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \begin{gathered} \text{there exist }T_1,\ldots,T_k\subseteq S \text{ with }T_i\cap T_j=\varnothing\quad(i\neq j),\\ \text{orderings }(t_{i,1},\ldots,t_{i,m_i})\text{ of }T_i, \text{ and }g\in G\text{ such that}\\ t_{i,1}\cdots t_{i,m_i}=g\qquad(1\leq i\leq k). \end{gathered} \] Here $\log$ may be taken to be the natural logarithm, since changing to any fixed base greater than $1$ only rescales the constant.

Unresolved formulation issue. The supplied statement does not require the subsets $T_i$ to be nonempty or distinct. Under the conventional empty-product definition, taking every $T_i=\varnothing$ satisfies the displayed conclusion for every $S$, making the literal assertion trivial. Requiring each $m_i\geq 1$ would give a different, substantive question, but that requirement is absent from the supplied wording and is not adopted here as a verified correction. Moreover, with such a nonemptiness requirement, the stated quantification over all finite groups fails for $|G|=1$: the choice $S=\varnothing$ satisfies the size hypothesis because $\log 1=0$. An additional boundary condition or revised threshold would therefore also need clarification.

Short English statement

The source asks whether a subset of a finite group whose size is at least a constant times the logarithm of the group order contains $k$ pairwise disjoint subsets with equal products after suitable ordering. The wording permits empty subsets, making the literal question trivial; a substantive interpretation requires clarification of nonemptiness and the group of order $1$.
