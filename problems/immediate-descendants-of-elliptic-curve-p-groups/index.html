Immediate Descendants of Elliptic-Curve p-Groups

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. The source associates a finite group $G_{E,P}({\mathbb{F}}_p)$ of nilpotency class two to a pair $(E,P)$ involving an elliptic curve and a point. However, the supplied record does not define this construction, the permitted pairs, or the family denoted by $\mathcal G_p$ in the original statement. These are unresolved prerequisites to a complete formulation; no domain for $P$ or restriction on $p$ is imposed here without source evidence.

For a group $H$, define its lower central series by $\gamma_1(H)=H$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(H)=[\gamma_i(H),H]$. Thus $H$ has nilpotency class exactly three when $\gamma_4(H)=1$ and $\gamma_3(H)\ne1$. A central extension in the question means an exact sequence \[ 1\longrightarrow {\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\iota}H\xrightarrow{\pi}G_{E,P}({\mathbb{F}}_p)\longrightarrow1, \qquad \iota({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})\subseteq Z(H). \] The first requested classification is: among the pairs allowed by the source construction, determine exactly those $(E,P)$ for which such an extension exists with \[ |H|=p^{10},\qquad \gamma_4(H)=1,\qquad \gamma_3(H)\ne1. \] Exactness then necessarily gives $|G_{E,P}({\mathbb{F}}_p)|=p^9$. The source calls these extensions immediate descendants. Its precise convention for that term, and its relationship to any additional descendant conditions, are not supplied.

For an explicit interpretation of counting descendants as isomorphism classes of groups, define, for a finite group $K$, \[ d_p(K)=\#\left\{[H]\;\middle|\; \begin{gathered} |H|=p^{10},\quad \gamma_4(H)=1,\quad \gamma_3(H)\ne1,\\ \exists C\le Z(H):\ |C|=p,\quad H/C\cong K \end{gathered} \right\}. \] Here $[H]$ denotes the abstract group isomorphism class, so different choices of $C$ in the same group do not contribute additional terms. This definition makes the extension-counting interpretation explicit; agreement with the intended immediate-descendant convention requires review.

Once the missing family $\mathcal G_p$ is specified, the second question, under this counting interpretation, asks whether \[ D(p)=\sum_{[K]\in\mathcal G_p/\cong}d_p(K) \] is polynomial on residue classes, abbreviated PORC. For a function on the admissible primes, this means that there exist an integer $m\ge1$ and polynomials $f_0,\ldots,f_{m-1}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]$ such that \[ D(p)=f_r(p)\qquad\text{whenever }p\text{ is admissible and }p\equiv r\pmod m. \] The admissible primes, and whether finitely many exceptional primes may be omitted, remain unspecified. The sum counts each base-group isomorphism class once; it does not identify contributions from different base groups if they share a descendant.

As background, the original statement attributes to du Sautoy and Vaughan-Lee a non-PORC immediate-descendant count for the construction with $E:y^2=x^3-x$ and $P=(0,0)$. This is a supplied attribution, with its characteristic restrictions and counting conventions unavailable here. The original statement describes PORC behavior of the aggregate sum as a possibility of interest, not an established assertion.

Short English statement

Classify the permitted elliptic-curve pairs $(E,P)$ whose associated group has a central extension of order $p^{10}$ and nilpotency class exactly three. Then ask whether the sum of descendant counts over the intended base-group isomorphism classes is PORC in $p$. The construction, permitted pairs, prime restrictions, family $\mathcal G_p$, and precise counting convention are missing, so this is a conditional statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
