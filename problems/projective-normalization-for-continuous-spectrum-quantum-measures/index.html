Math-5366

Projective normalization for continuous-spectrum quantum measures

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The canonical record is the fragment "Problem 40 for continuous spectrum was proved in [89,126]. Results toward". It is a historical status sentence and unfinished transition, not the statement of Problem 40 or a new open problem. The aid identifies the context as continuous-spectrum quantum equidistribution: Luo--Sarnak's individual base-space compact-set ratio result for locally finite but globally infinite Eisenstein energy measures, and Jakobson's microlocal analogue using a positive Friedrichs-symmetrized distribution. The underlying space, spectral parameter and full theorem hypotheses are not supplied and are not reconstructed from the fragment.

The aid's independent normalization statement concerns a locally compact space $Z$, nonzero locally finite positive Radon measures $\mu_t$ and a nonzero target Radon measure $\nu$. Choose a nonnegative compactly supported continuous anchor $\phi_0$ with $\nu(\phi_0)>0$, and assume $\mu_t(\phi_0)>0$ eventually. Then the reported compact-test ratio convergence is \[ \frac{\mu_t(f)}{\mu_t(\phi_0)} \longrightarrow\frac{\nu(f)}{\nu(\phi_0)} \quad\text{for every }f\in C_c(Z). \] It is equivalent to vague convergence \[ \frac{\nu(\phi_0)}{\mu_t(\phi_0)}\,\mu_t\longrightarrow\nu. \] Every other compact nonnegative anchor with positive $\nu$ integral gives the same projective convergence notion. Positivity and nonzero anchor integrals are material. Multiplying an Eisenstein function by a nonzero scalar multiplies its energy measure by the scalar's squared modulus and cancels in these ratios. No normalization to a globally finite probability measure is implied when the energy measure has infinite total mass.

A cutoff estimate needs additional finite target data. Let $\nu$ now be a probability, let $0\leq\chi\leq1$ be compactly supported, and put $z=\nu(\chi)>0$. For bounded $f$, suppose the chosen scalar-normalized positive measure $\mu$ satisfies \[ |\mu(f\chi)-\nu(f\chi)|\leq\epsilon,\qquad |\mu(\chi)-z|\leq\delta<z. \] The reported error bound can be stated precisely as \[ \left|\frac{\mu(f\chi)}{\mu(\chi)}-\nu(f)\right| \leq\frac{\epsilon+\|f\|_\infty\delta}{z-\delta} +2\|f\|_\infty(1-z). \] The left side is a cutoff-normalized expectation versus the full target probability; it is not an expectation against the entire globally infinite source measure. Removing cutoffs requires control of numerator and denominator errors and of $1-z$.

The aid reports an explicit cusp half-line example in which fixed-cutoff limits recover the target volume while a diagonal growing cutoff loses a computable fraction of mass. Its actual measures, cutoff sequence and fraction are absent, so none is invented. The conclusion retained is that fixed-compact ratio convergence cannot be diagonalized without uniform control. Neither this normalization lemma nor the incomplete status sentence supplies a new proof of the full continuous-spectrum theorem or a formulation of the missing continuation.

Short English statement

This is historical context rather than a standalone question. The aid explains how one positive compact anchor normalizes projective vague convergence for possibly infinite energy measures. Its cutoff estimate needs explicit denominator control, and fixed-cutoff convergence alone does not justify a growing-cutoff limit.
