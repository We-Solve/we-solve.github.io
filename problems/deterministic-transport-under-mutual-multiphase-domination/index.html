Deterministic Transport Under Mutual Multiphase Domination

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write the supplied vector-valued measure spaces as $(X,\mathcal A,\sigma)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B,\eta)$, where $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ denote their measurable structures and $\sigma=(\sigma_i)_{i\in I}$ and $\eta=(\eta_i)_{i\in I}$ denote their component measures. The common component index set $I$, the permitted types of component measures, and the precise meaning of atomlessness are not specified in the supplied record. Retain the source's assumption that both vector-valued measure spaces are atomless, subject to these unresolved qualifications.

For a measurable map $T:X\to Y$, define its componentwise pushforward by \[ (T_\#\sigma_i)(B)=\sigma_i(T^{-1}(B))\qquad(B\in\mathcal B). \] For a measurable map $S:Y\to X$, similarly define \[ (S_\#\eta_i)(A)=\eta_i(S^{-1}(A))\qquad(A\in\mathcal A). \] Write $\preceq_{\mathrm{mp}}$ solely as a placeholder for the source's multiphase-transport order; the supplied record gives no definition of this relation.

The proposed question is whether, for every pair of atomless vector-valued measure spaces in the intended class, the mutual-domination assumptions \[ (X,\mathcal A,\sigma)\preceq_{\mathrm{mp}}(Y,\mathcal B,\eta) \quad\text{and}\quad (Y,\mathcal B,\eta)\preceq_{\mathrm{mp}}(X,\mathcal A,\sigma) \] imply the existence of measurable maps $T:X\to Y$ and $S:Y\to X$ such that \[ \forall i\in I,\qquad T_\#\sigma_i=\eta_i\quad\text{and}\quad S_\#\eta_i=\sigma_i. \] Equivalently, the required equalities are \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_i(T^{-1}(B))&=\eta_i(B)&& (i\in I,\ B\in\mathcal B),\\ \eta_i(S^{-1}(A))&=\sigma_i(A)&& (i\in I,\ A\in\mathcal A). \end{aligned} \] The same map $T$ must transport every component simultaneously, and likewise for $S$. The displayed source statement does not require $S\circ T$ or $T\circ S$ to be an identity, even almost everywhere. The background's phrase "deterministic inverse transports" leaves a possible stronger intended requirement unresolved. Without definitions of the order and the admissible measure spaces, this remains a qualified statement template requiring mathematical source review.

Short English statement

Does mutual domination in the intended multiphase-transport order between two atomless vector-valued measure spaces imply the existence of deterministic measurable transports in both directions, each preserving all component measures simultaneously? The supplied statement asks for maps in both directions; whether they must also be inverses is unclear from the background. The order, measure-space hypotheses, and atomlessness convention require clarification.
