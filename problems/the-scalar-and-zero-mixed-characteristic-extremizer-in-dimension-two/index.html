Math-1306

The scalar-and-zero mixed-characteristic extremizer in dimension two

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source is a fragment asking whether repeated identity copies with zero padding give the worst root bounds. It does not name a polynomial, matrix constraints, normalization, dimension or root statistic. The supplied report identifies it with an unnumbered Marcus--Spielman--Srivastava largest-root conjecture for mixed characteristic polynomials; that identification is a stated interpretation.

In this interpretation take positive semidefinite Hermitian $d$-by-$d$ matrices $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ satisfying \[ \sum_iA_i=I_d,\qquad \operatorname{tr}A_i\le\epsilon, \] and define \[ \mu[A_1,\ldots,A_m](x)= \left.\prod_{i=1}^m(1-\partial_{z_i}) \det\left(xI_d+\sum_i z_iA_i\right)\right|_{z_1=\cdots=z_m=0}. \] The interpreted conjecture says that capped scalar identity matrices, one residual scalar matrix if necessary, and zeros maximize the largest root among feasible families with the given parameters. It is not a coordinatewise maximization of every ordered root.

The supplied candidate theorem is restricted to $d=2$, with every fixed $m\ge1$, $\epsilon>0$ satisfying $m\epsilon\ge2$. Set \[ q=\lfloor2/\epsilon\rfloor,\qquad r=2-q\epsilon,\qquad t_i=\operatorname{tr}A_i. \] It asserts, without requiring commutativity, \[ \mu(x)=(x-1)^2-\sum_i\det A_i, \] so the largest root is $1+\sqrt{\sum_i\det A_i}$. Its exact deficit identity is \[ \frac{q\epsilon^2+r^2}{4}-\sum_i\det A_i =\frac{q\epsilon^2+r^2-\sum_i t_i^2}{4} +\frac12\sum_i\left\|A_i-\frac{t_i}{2}I_2\right\|_F^2\ge0, \] where $\|B\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(B^*B)$. The first term is the trace-packing loss and the second the anisotropy loss. The reported unique largest-root maximizer, up to permutation and zero padding to $m$ entries, consists of $q$ copies of $(\epsilon/2)I_2$ and, when $r>0$, one copy of $(r/2)I_2$. Feasibility supplies enough entries for these nonzero matrices. Its largest root is $1+\tfrac12\sqrt{q\epsilon^2+r^2}$.

The report leaves the exact extremizer problem in $d\ge3$ open in the literature it checked. It also reports a displayed counterexample to the stronger all-roots reading, but that example is not included in this record. The dimension-two largest-root theorem must therefore remain distinct from both the higher-dimensional conjecture and an all-ordered-roots claim.

Short English statement

The fragment is interpreted as a conjecture that scalar identity blocks and zeros maximize the largest mixed-characteristic root. The supplied report proves that interpretation for arbitrary positive semidefinite matrices in dimension two via an exact deficit identity, while retaining higher dimensions as open and rejecting the stronger all-roots interpretation.
