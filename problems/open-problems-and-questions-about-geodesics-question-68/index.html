Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Question 68

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^{3}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}:\sum_{i=1}^{4}x_i^{2}=1\}$ be the standard smooth three-dimensional sphere. A Lorentzian metric on $S^{3}$ is a smooth symmetric covariant $2$-tensor that is nondegenerate at every point and has one negative and two positive eigenvalues. The opposite overall sign convention may equivalently be used for both metrics.

Two metrics $g$ and $\bar g$ on the same manifold are projectively equivalent if they have the same unparameterized geodesics: every nonconstant $g$-geodesic can locally be reparameterized by a smooth change of parameter with nonzero derivative to become a $\bar g$-geodesic, and conversely. This requirement includes geodesics of every causal type. The metrics are non-proportional if there is no constant $c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}$ such that $\bar g=cg$ on all of $S^{3}$.

Question (supplied source, Question 68). Do there exist two smooth Lorentzian metrics $g$ and $\bar g$ on $S^{3}$ that are projectively equivalent and non-proportional? Equivalently, is the following existence statement true? \[ \exists\,g,\bar g\text{ Lorentzian metrics on }S^{3} \quad\text{such that}\quad \begin{cases} g\text{ and }\bar g\text{ have the same unparameterized geodesics},\\ \bar g\ne cg\quad\text{for every constant }c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}. \end{cases} \] Here smoothness and the constant-multiple meaning of proportionality are conventional interpretations; the supplied excerpt does not explicitly specify their definitions. No additional geodesic completeness hypothesis is stated.

The source presents this question as a simplest version of the broader problem of understanding the topology of closed manifolds admitting non-proportional projectively equivalent metrics of indefinite signature. Its surrounding discussion reports local and global examples, with early examples attributed to Lagrange, Beltrami, and Dini; a local Riemannian description attributed to Levi-Civita; and a local arbitrary-signature description attributed to the reference labeled [Bol-Mat2015]. It describes the global Riemannian situation as comparatively well understood, citing [Mat-Top1998, Matveev2003a, Matveev2003b], while describing the global arbitrary-signature situation as largely unknown. These are supplied historical literature assessments, not independently verified claims of this draft.

Short English statement

Can the smooth three-dimensional sphere carry two Lorentzian metrics with exactly the same geodesic paths, allowing changes of parameter, such that neither metric is a constant multiple of the other? The supplied registry labels this question open; that label is not an independently verified current status.
