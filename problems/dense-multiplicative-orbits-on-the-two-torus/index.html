Math-14991

Dense Multiplicative Orbits on the Two-Torus

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a real number $t$, let $\{t\}=t-\lfloor t\rfloor\in[0,1)$. For real $x,y$, positive integers $a,b$ satisfying $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and an exponent index set $I$, define \[ \mathcal O_I(a,b;x,y)=\left\{(\{a^n b^k x\},\{a^n b^k y\}):n,k\in I\right\}. \] The source writes $I=\mathbb N$, without specifying whether $0\in\mathbb N$. Its density conclusion means \[ \overline{\mathcal O_I(a,b;x,y)}^{\,[0,1]^2}=[0,1]^2, \] where closure is taken in the Euclidean topology of the closed square, including its boundary.

Source question, with unresolved hypothesis. The supplied record asks whether, for every pair $x,y$ satisfying the source condition "$1,x,y$ are multiplicatively independent over $\mathbb Q$", and every pair of relatively prime positive integers $a,b$, the set $\mathcal O_{\mathbb N}(a,b;x,y)$ is dense in $[0,1]^2$. Real coordinates are required for this interpretation of reduction modulo $1$, but the source does not explicitly state the domain of $x,y$. The stated multiplier range includes $a=1$ or $b=1$, including $a=b=1$; no additional lower bound is supplied.

Unresolved meaning of independence. Under the usual definition, elements $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ of a multiplicative group are multiplicatively independent if \[ \prod_{j=1}^{r}u_j^{m_j}=1,\quad (m_1,\ldots,m_r)\in\mathbb Z^r \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m_1=\cdots=m_r=0. \] A tuple containing $1$ cannot satisfy this definition, because the exponent vector $(1,0,0)$ already gives a relation. The qualification "over $\mathbb Q$" is not defined in the record and does not supply a clear alternative meaning. Consequently the literal hypothesis does not provide a meaningful nonvacuous formulation under the usual definition. Neither replacing multiplicative independence by linear independence over $\mathbb Q$, nor deleting $1$ from the tuple, is authorized by the supplied evidence. The intended independence hypothesis, coordinate domain, and multiplier restrictions require mathematical source review before a precise intended conjecture can be stated.

Short English statement

The source asks whether multiplying both coordinates repeatedly by two relatively prime positive integers produces a dense orbit in the unit square. Its independence assumption is unresolved: a tuple containing $1$ is never multiplicatively independent in the usual sense. The intended hypothesis and the allowed multipliers need review.
