Diffraction Scaling and Mahler-Function Asymptotics

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mu$ denote the diffraction measure of a substitution system and let $F$ denote its associated Mahler function. The supplied record does not specify the class of substitutions, the weights defining the diffraction measure, its ambient space, or the construction associating $F$ to the system. Consequently, these symbols describe the intended objects but do not yet determine a quantified mathematical conjecture.

To specify the scaling question, once a Euclidean ambient space and a diffraction measure have been fixed, let $B_\varepsilon(0)$ be the open ball of radius $\varepsilon>0$ about the origin and distinguish \[ M(\varepsilon)=\mu(B_\varepsilon(0)),\qquad M_*(\varepsilon)=\mu(B_\varepsilon(0)\setminus\{0\}). \] These quantities differ when there is an atom at zero. One possible measure of scaling, for either chosen mass function $H$, is \[ \alpha_H=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \frac{\log H(\varepsilon)}{\log\varepsilon}, \] provided the logarithms are defined and the limit exists. This is a possible formalization of "order of scaling," not a convention specified by the source; the source may require a finer asymptotic scale.

For the radial question, the supplied excerpt indicates approach to $1$ along the real radius, namely $z=r\to1^-$. A Mahler functional equation relates values of a function at arguments such as $z,z^q,z^{q^2},\ldots$, for an integer $q\ge2$; the equation, base, normalization, and relevant asymptotic quantity for $F$ are not supplied here.

Source problem, with unresolved qualifications. Determine whether, and under which hypotheses, the order of diffraction scaling near zero can be related to the radial asymptotics of the associated Mahler function as $r\uparrow1$. A precise version must specify the substitution systems under consideration, the correspondence $\mu\leftrightarrow F$, the treatment of any atom at zero, the meaning of scaling order, and the proposed relation between the two asymptotic quantities. The record supplies no equality, implication, or universal quantifier that can safely be asserted as the intended conjecture.

The supplied dated assessment describes matching exponents in selected Thue--Morse and Rudin--Shapiro cases. Short English statement

The question asks whether diffraction mass near zero and the radial behavior of an associated Mahler function encode related asymptotic information. The supplied record labels this question open and mentions selected Thue--Morse and Rudin--Shapiro examples. It does not specify enough hypotheses or a proposed relation to yield a complete conjecture. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
