A computable cut from one explicit lattice-free simplex

The report discusses two-row corner polyhedra, lattice-free cuts and higher-dimensional extensions. We work in a specified $k$-row corner model: $x=f+\sum_{j=1}^m r_js_j\in\mathbb Z^k$, $s_j\ge0$, where $k\ge2$, $f=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$ and $r_j\in\mathbb R^k$. Let \[ L=\{x\in\mathbb R^k:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le k\},\qquad \psi(r)=\max\left\{\max_i(-2r_i),\frac2k\sum_i r_i\right\}. \]
Proposition: The simplex $L$ has no integer point in its interior, contains $f$ in its interior, and gives the valid corner inequality $\sum_j\psi(r_j)s_j\ge1$.
Proof: An integer point in the interior would have every coordinate at least 1 and sum strictly less than $k$, which is impossible. The coordinates of $f$ are positive and its sum is $k/2<k$. In translated coordinates $r=x-f$, the strict interior inequalities are $-2r_i<1$ for every $i$ and $(2/k)\sum_i r_i<1$. Each associated linear form $\ell$ satisfies $\ell(r_j)\le\psi(r_j)$. If $s_j\ge0$ and $\sum_j\psi(r_j)s_j<1$, linearity gives \[ \ell\left(\sum_jr_js_j\right)=\sum_j\ell(r_j)s_j\le\sum_j\psi(r_j)s_j<1 \] for every facet form. Then $f+\sum_jr_js_j$ lies in the interior of $L$, contradicting its being integral. This proves validity. Also $\psi(r)\ge0$: if some coordinate of $r$ is nonpositive the corresponding $-2r_i$ is nonnegative; otherwise the sum form is positive. Thus the expression has the expected nonnegative gauge coefficients, including $\psi(0)=0$. ∎
For the positive coordinate rays $r_j=e_j$, $j=1,\ldots,k$, the coefficient is $2/k$ and the cut is $\sum_js_j\ge k/2$. It is tight at $s_j=1/2$, where $x=(1,\ldots,1)$ is integral. Computing all coefficients takes $O(km)$ arithmetic operations when the rays are explicitly given.

Verification and remaining gaps

The coefficients and tight point were checked for $k=2,3,4$, and a finite set of half-integer nonnegative $s$ values on coordinate rays was enumerated. This is a complete standard lattice-free-cut calculation for one chosen simplex. It does not prove that these cuts generate the whole corner polyhedron, identify maximal useful lattice-free sets, or establish practical effectiveness for the source's examples. The attempted step from one efficiently computable valid family to a complete finite description has no justification here; the source itself discusses obstructions to such completeness.
