Math-1466

Null completeness, the surface case, and a flat-holonomy obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a smooth compact manifold without boundary, of dimension $n\geq2$, and let $g$ be a Lorentz metric. "Globally conformal to constant curvature" means that, after a global identification if necessary, \[ g=e^{2u}g_0, \] where $u\in C^\infty(M,{\mathbb{R}})$ and $g_0$ is a Lorentz metric of constant sectional curvature. Question $9.3.2$, attributed to [Sanchez2013], asks whether every such $g$ is geodesically complete: every maximal affinely parametrized geodesic, of any causal type, is defined on all of ${\mathbb{R}}$. Compactness alone does not imply Lorentzian geodesic completeness.

The supplied aid reports these partial conclusions:

  1. $g$ is null-geodesically complete in every dimension. Along a null geodesic the conformal affine-parameter change is proportional to $e^{2u}$, which is bounded above and below by positive constants on $M$; this uses the completeness of the compact constant-curvature representative in the applicable Lorentz setting.
  2. In dimension $2$, $g$ is fully geodesically complete.
  3. In any dimension, $g$ is fully complete if a finite cover of $(M,g_0)$ admits an everywhere timelike Killing vector field. This hypothesis concerns the representative $g_0$, not a Killing field for every conformal metric. On the cover it becomes a timelike conformal Killing field for $g$.
  4. If $g_0$ is flat and its linear holonomy \[ \rho:\pi_1(M)\to\operatorname{O}(1,n-1) \] has compact closure, then $g$ is fully geodesically complete. The aid uses the resulting timelike parallel direction after a suitable finite cover.
Consequently, any geodesically incomplete example with a flat representative must have linear holonomy with noncompact closure, equivalently an unbounded linear-holonomy group in a fixed linear representation. Null completeness by itself leaves timelike and spacelike completeness to be addressed, and compact holonomy is a sufficient condition rather than a necessary condition for completeness. The aid reports the general higher-dimensional problem apparently open in the literature checked, without a complete search bibliography.

The original extraction also includes contextual commentary that noncompact homogeneous indefinite-signature manifolds need not be complete, referring to [Sanchez2013, Example $2$, Section $4$], and suggests asking which algebraic properties of the isometry group might imply completeness. The verified record boundary identifies this as commentary, not a hypothesis or answer to Question $9.3.2$. The subsequent Section $10$ and $10.1$ headings on integrability and ergodicity of higher-genus surface geodesic flows are further extraction spillover.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a compact Lorentz metric globally conformal to a constant-curvature metric must be complete. The aid proves null completeness, the full surface case, and completeness under a finite-cover timelike-Killing condition. In the flat case, an incomplete example would require unbounded linear holonomy; the general higher-dimensional question remains open in the supplied report.
