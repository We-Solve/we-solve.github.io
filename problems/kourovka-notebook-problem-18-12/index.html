Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.12

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group. For a finite symmetric generating set $S$, let $|g|_S$ be the word length of $g\in G$, and put \[ \gamma_{G,S}(n)=\left|\{g\in G:|g|_S\le n\}\right|\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}). \] Say that $G$ has intermediate growth if its growth is subexponential but is not bounded above by a polynomial: for every real $a>1$ there exists $C_a>0$ such that $\gamma_{G,S}(n)\le C_a a^n$ for every $n\ge0$, and there do not exist $C>0$ and $d\ge0$ such that $\gamma_{G,S}(n)\le C(1+n)^d$ for every $n\ge0$. This property is independent of the finite generating set.

For $x,y\in G$, write $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and define the derived subgroup by \[ G'=\langle [x,y]:x,y\in G\rangle. \] The commutators below are taken in $G$; their entries need not belong to $G'$.

Question (V. G. Bardakov, Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.12). For every finitely generated group $G$ of intermediate growth, does there exist an integer $m=m(G)\ge1$ such that \[ \forall g\in G'\quad \exists k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\quad \exists x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_k,y_k\in G \quad g=\prod_{i=1}^{k}[x_i,y_i]? \] The empty product is the identity. Thus the question asks whether every such group has finite commutator width; the bound may depend on $G$.

The supplied source states, as background, that the corresponding assertion holds for finitely generated groups of polynomial growth, invoking Gromov's theorem that these groups are almost nilpotent, meaning that they contain a nilpotent subgroup of finite index. It also states that the assertion fails for some groups of exponential growth, naming free groups as examples. That example requires a nonabelian free group; the source does not explicitly specify its rank. These background claims are supplied context and have not been independently verified here.

Short English statement

Does every finitely generated group of intermediate growth admit a bound, depending on the group, on the number of commutators needed to express any element of its derived subgroup? The supplied registry labels this problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
