Math-1670

Type-B extreme diameters and a dihedral meander determinant

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

H. T. Hall's problem, extracted with the split label "Problem 2. 4.," starts from classical meanders across $2n$ ordered bridges. Two noncrossing pairings of $[2n]$, one on each side, form a meandric system; a meander has one connected component. The source recalls a bijection with ordered pairs of type-$A_{n-1}$ noncrossing partitions at maximal distance in the undirected Hasse graph, and asks whether it yields new counting methods. It proposes type-$W$ meanders as ordered diameter pairs in $NC_W$, asks for a combinatorial model, and specifically asks about type $B$.

For a finite Coxeter system of rank $r$, fix a Coxeter element $c$, let $NC_c(W)=[1,c]$ in absolute order, and let $d_H$ be distance in its undirected Hasse graph. Its diameter is $r$; an ordered diameter is $(u,v)$ with $d_H(u,v)=r$, with endpoint order counted. The aid's type-$B$ result is: for every $n\geq3$, exactly \[ n^3(n+1) \] ordered diameters in $NC_c(B_n)$ have endpoint rank pairs $(1,n-1)$ or $(n-1,1)$, counted together. Adding $(1,c)$ and $(c,1)$ gives the lower bound $n^3(n+1)+2$ for all ordered diameters. For $B_3$, these are all possibilities, giving exactly $110$. For larger $n$ the statement does not count additional middle-rank diameter pairs.

The aid also gives a $B_3$ combinatorial interpretation: view a type-$B$ noncrossing partition as a centrally symmetric type-$A$ partition on the signed points, apply the standard doubling/fattening to obtain noncrossing pairings, and superpose the pairings for an ordered pair. The resulting doubled meandric system is centrally symmetric. The $B_3$ diameter pairs correspond precisely to those systems whose every connected component is fixed setwise by the half-turn. This permits several components and must not be replaced by the classical one-component condition. The supplied result is stated for $B_3$, not as a complete general-$B_n$ characterization.

The source remarks, attributed to A. Nica and J. Scott, ask about a type-$W$ meander determinant and its factorization, and about links to chromatic polynomials and Temperley--Lieb algebras. For an explicit distance convention consistent with the aid's formula, use \[ M_W(q)_{u,v}=q^{r+1-d_H(u,v)}. \] Different overall powers would change determinant factors. For the dihedral group $I_2(m)$, $m\geq3$, rank is two, the poset has $m$ middle elements and two bounds, and the aid's exact results are \[ \#\{\text{ordered diameters}\}=m(m-1)+2, \] \[ \det M_{I_2(m)}(q) =q^{m+2}(q^2-1)^{m+1}(q^2-(m-1)). \] Here $q$ is an indeterminate and the matrix entries are $q^{3-d_H}$. The irreducible dihedral range is explicit; extending the notation to the reducible $m=2$ case is a separate convention. The source's type-$A$ Chebyshev factorization and these dihedral formulas do not give a complete type-$W$ factorization or settle the chromatic/Temperley--Lieb significance question. The trailing "3. Reflection Groups" is a new section heading, not another assumption.

Short English statement

The source asks for Coxeter-type meander counts, models, and determinant factorizations. The aid counts all extreme-rank type-$B$ diameters, obtains the complete $B_3$ count and a symmetry model there, and factors the distance-meander determinant in every irreducible dihedral type. General counting, middle-rank type-$B$ behavior, and the broader algebraic links remain separate questions.
