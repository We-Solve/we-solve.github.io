Math-1074

Stokes Eigenfunctions with Constant Boundary Pressure

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a domain, let $u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a velocity field and $p:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}$ a pressure, and consider the Stokes eigenvalue equations \[ -\Delta u+\nabla p=\lambda u,\qquad \operatorname{div}u=0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega, \qquad u=0\quad\hbox{on }\partial\Omega. \] The source asks the following related questions.
  1. If $p$ is constant on the relevant portion $\Gamma\subseteq\partial\Omega$, under what intended domain and boundary hypotheses does it follow that $u\equiv0$ and $p$ is constant throughout $\Omega$? The record does not specify $\Gamma$, the domain restrictions, or the trace and solution regularity needed for this implication.
  2. Is the disk the only domain admitting a solution with $u\not\equiv0$ and $p\equiv0$? The mention of a disk indicates a planar classification question, but the source does not explicitly fix the dimension, boundedness, connectedness of the boundary, or boundary regularity. For any fixed solution the equation depends only on $\nabla p$, so a spatially constant pressure can be normalized to zero.
  3. Establish the related unique-continuation assertion for nonsmooth boundaries. The exact boundary class, observation set, hypotheses, and conclusion of this additional assertion are not supplied.
The first question is retained as a request to identify the intended rigidity statement, rather than an unconditional implication for every domain: the supplied assessment explicitly mentions a disk counterexample and known corner results. Their precise statements are absent, so neither the exception class nor a corner theorem is inferred here.

Short English statement

The problem concerns rigidity of Stokes eigenfunctions whose pressure is constant on part of the boundary, the proposed uniqueness of the disk among domains carrying a nonzero zero-pressure eigenfunction, and a nonsmooth-boundary extension. The missing geometric and regularity hypotheses prevent a fully quantified theorem statement.
