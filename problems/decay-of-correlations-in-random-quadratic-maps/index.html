Decay of Correlations in Random Quadratic Maps

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $P$ be a probability measure on $[1,4]$ that is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on that interval. Let $(\lambda_i)_{i\geq 1}$ be independent, identically distributed random variables with common law $P$. The supplied random quadratic maps have the formula \[ T_\lambda(x)=\lambda x(1-x),\qquad \lambda\in[1,4]. \] For a parameter realization, the successive compositions are \[ T_{\lambda_n}\circ\cdots\circ T_{\lambda_1},\qquad n\geq 1, \] wherever the intended phase space makes these compositions a dynamical system.

The supplied question asks whether this system has random decay of correlations, described in the record as equivalent to being relatively exact. This is an incompletely specified question: the record does not specify the phase space for $x$, the reference or invariant measures, the observables and limiting expression defining random decay of correlations, or the measured dynamical system and factor relative to which exactness is intended. It also does not specify whether the assertion is to hold for every absolutely continuous $P$, or whether further restrictions on $P$ were present in the omitted source context. No rate of correlation decay is stated. Consequently, the asserted equivalence is preserved as source wording, but cannot be formulated or justified from the supplied data, and a complete quantified proposition cannot be recovered without additional source context.

Short English statement

The question concerns correlation decay and relative exactness for compositions of quadratic maps with independent parameters drawn from an absolutely continuous law on $[1,4]$. The supplied extraction omits the dynamical and measure-theoretic definitions needed for a precise proposition. Its open status and claimed equivalence are supplied source descriptions requiring review.
