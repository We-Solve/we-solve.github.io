Convergence of Expected Graphon Chromatic Numbers

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard convention that a graphon is a symmetric Lebesgue-measurable function $W\colon[0,1]^2\to[0,1]$. For each integer $n\geq 2$, construct $G(n,W)$ on vertex set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ by sampling independent uniform random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ on $[0,1]$, and then, conditional on these variables, including each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$, $i<j$, independently with probability $W(X_i,X_j)$. There are no loops. For a finite graph $H$, its chromatic number $\chi(H)$ is the least number of colors in a vertex coloring in which adjacent vertices receive different colors. Expectation below is over both the sampled variables and the conditional edge choices, and $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm.

The supplied question asks whether, for every fixed graphon $W$, the sequence \[ a_n(W)=\frac{\log n}{n}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(G(n,W))\right] \] converges as $n\to\infty$.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied record does not specify whether convergence must have a finite limit or may include convergence to $+\infty$. The two possible targets are \[ \forall W\;\exists L_W\in[0,\infty)\quad a_n(W)\longrightarrow L_W \] and \[ \forall W\;\exists L_W\in[0,\infty]\quad a_n(W)\longrightarrow L_W, \] where the second display permits an infinite limit. These targets are distinct. No restriction such as an upper bound strictly below $1$ on $W$ is supplied. Accordingly, this draft preserves the convergence question while leaving its intended limit convention and any omitted graphon hypotheses for source review. The graphon and sampling definitions above explicate the standard meaning of the supplied notation; the record itself does not spell them out.

Short English statement

For each fixed graphon $W$, form its random graph on $n$ sampled vertices. Does its expected chromatic number, multiplied by $(\log n)/n$, converge as $n$ grows? The supplied record leaves unclear whether an infinite limit is allowed or further restrictions on $W$ are intended.
