Math-2040

Is the Borel monadic theory of the real order (BMTO) decidable? Is the monadic theory of well-ordering (MTWO) consistently decidable

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Monadic second-order logic of an order has variables for individual points and for unary subsets, with order and membership relations. The Borel monadic theory of the real order (BMTO) is the set of sentences true in $(\mathbb R,\leq)$ when every set quantifier ranges over Borel subsets of $\mathbb R$. Is this set of sentence codes decidable, meaning that a single algorithm halts on every sentence and correctly decides its truth?

The second question asks whether the monadic theory of well-ordering (MTWO), with unrestricted subset quantifiers, is consistently decidable. A usual class-theory reading takes \[ \operatorname{MTWO}=\{\varphi:(\alpha,<)\models_{\mathrm{MSO}}\varphi\text{ for every ordinal }\alpha\}, \] with full power-set semantics for each ordinal. Under that reading, the consistency question asks whether the intended background set theory can consistently hold that this theory is recursive. The source does not specify the background theory or distinguish the theory of all well-orders from that of particular well-orders; this interpretation must be checked rather than silently identifying the variants.

The supplied summary reports BMTO decidable in 2024--2025 work, while keeping MTWO's consistency-decidability question open. The two set-quantifier ranges and the direct versus consistency questions are separate.

Short English statement

Decide the Borel-set monadic theory of the real order, and separately ask whether the unrestricted monadic theory of well-ordering can consistently be decidable. The supplied summary reports the first part solved and leaves the second open.
