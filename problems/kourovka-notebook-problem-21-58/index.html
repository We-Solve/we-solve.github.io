Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.58

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For subsets $X,Y$ of a group $G$, let $XY=\{xy:x\in X,y\in Y\}$. The product is direct if the multiplication map $X\times Y\to G$ is injective, equivalently every element of $XY$ has a unique representation $xy$ with $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$. Directness does not require $XY=G$.

For a fixed $A\subseteq G$, let $\mathcal D_A=\{B\subseteq G:AB\text{ is direct}\}$. A member $B$ is maximal if there is no strictly larger subset $B'\supsetneq B$ in $\mathcal D_A$. This means maximal by inclusion, not maximum cardinality. The question is whether \[ \exists\text{ infinite group }G\ \forall A\subseteq G\ \forall B,C\in\mathcal D_A, \] \[ \left(B,C\text{ are inclusion-maximal in }\mathcal D_A\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ |B|=|C|. \] All subsets, not only subgroups or generating sets, are quantified.

For nonempty $A$ and a maximal $B$, also nonempty, the supplied discussion records the equivalent conditions \[ A^{-1}A\cap BB^{-1}=\{1\},\qquad A^{-1}AB=G, \] where $1$ is the group identity and inverse/product notation is setwise. The first expresses directness and the second inclusion-maximality. The discussion attributed to M. H. Hooshmand uses cardinal arithmetic to obtain $|G|=|A||B|$ and reduces a potential verification to balanced subsets with $|A|=|G\setminus A|$: if $|A|<|G\setminus A|$, every maximal $B$ has size $|G|$; if $|A|>|G\setminus A|$, one has $A^{-1}A=G$ and every maximal $B$ has size one. The empty case is separate: if $A=\varnothing$, every $AB$ is vacuously direct and the sole inclusion-maximal $B$ is $G$, so it causes no obstruction.

The supplied solution, attributed to Kabenyuk's 2026 preprint, reports the exact negative statement \[ \forall\text{ infinite groups }G\ \exists\varnothing\ne A\subseteq G\ \exists B,C\subseteq G, \] \[ B,C\text{ are maximal in }\mathcal D_A\quad\text{and}\quad |B|\ne|C|. \] It describes every infinite group as unstable in this direct-complement sense. This gives a nonempty witness and refutes the universal-subset property despite the permitted empty subset.

The registry and literature triage dated 2026-08-17 classify the existence question as solved negatively. The source retains peer review or publication of the recent exact-match preprint as a follow-up and qualifies the conclusion by the correctness of its theorem. These are supplied result and status reports, not an independent proof review in this draft.

Short English statement

Can an infinite group have the same cardinality for every inclusion-maximal direct right complement of each fixed subset? The supplied 2026 preprint says no: every infinite group has a nonempty subset with two such maximal complements of different cardinalities.
