Math-1940

Dually Flat Structures on Riemannian Manifolds

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth Riemannian manifold and $D$ its Levi--Civita connection. For a smooth totally symmetric covariant tensor $T\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^3T^*M)$, define $T^\sharp$ by \[ g(T^\sharp(X,Y),Z)=T(X,Y,Z), \] and choose the convention \[ \nabla_XY=D_XY-\tfrac12T^\sharp(X,Y),\qquad \nabla_X^*Y=D_XY+\tfrac12T^\sharp(X,Y). \] Both connections are torsion-free and dual with respect to $g$: \[ Xg(Y,Z)=g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_X^*Z). \] The structure is dually flat when both curvature tensors vanish, $R^\nabla=R^{\nabla^*}=0$. Reversing the sign convention on $T$ interchanges the two connections and does not change this existence question.

Define \[ \mathcal D(M,g)=\{T\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Sym}^3T^*M): R^{\nabla(T)}=R^{\nabla^*(T)}=0\}. \] Amari's first question is whether $\mathcal D(M,g)\ne\varnothing$ for every given Riemannian manifold. If there are multiple choices, the second asks to characterize the resulting dually flat structures. The source does not prescribe whether classification is of the tensors themselves, of marked connection pairs, or modulo structure-preserving isomorphisms.

Locally a dually flat structure has affine coordinates for $\nabla$ in which $g_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j\phi$ for a strictly convex potential $\phi$; this is the Hessian description. Global affine coordinates, global potentials, and completeness are stronger conditions and are not added to curvature flatness. The supplied research summary reports that not every Riemannian metric admits the construction and that local Hessian theory is substantially understood, while global and uniqueness issues are context-dependent. It provides no precise obstruction theorem or complete classification; its vague curvature-type comment is not converted into a necessary-and-sufficient curvature criterion.

Short English statement

Can every Riemannian metric support a pair of torsion-free flat dual connections obtained from a symmetric cubic tensor? When such tensors exist, classify the resulting structures with an explicitly chosen equivalence convention.
