Math-14096

Diameter Bounds for Connected Layer Families

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a finite alphabet with $|A|=n$. A $d$-element multiset on $A$ is a function $m:A\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$ satisfying \[ \sum_{a\in A}m(a)=d. \] Thus repetitions of symbols are allowed. The supplied record does not specify the admissible parameter range, define a connected layer family, or define the extremal quantity $\widetilde f(d,n)$. In particular, it does not specify the connectivity condition, the requirements on the layers, or the quantity being maximized. These definitions must be recovered from the cited source before the following question becomes a fully specified mathematical statement.

With those source-dependent definitions and their admissible parameter range retained, the proposed equality is \[ \widetilde f(d,n)=d(n-1). \] The record asks whether this equality holds for every admissible pair $(d,n)$, and specifically whether one can prove or refute \[ \widetilde f(3,n)=3(n-1) \] for every admissible $n$.

A further question concerns layer families arising from triangulated surfaces: are the stated upper bounds $2n$ for surfaces with boundary and $n$ for surfaces without boundary tight? The supplied record does not define the construction associating a layer family to a triangulated surface, identify the bounded quantity, or explicitly identify $n$ in this setting. It also leaves unspecified the allowed surfaces and triangulations and whether tightness means exact attainment for each admissible $n$, attainment for infinitely many $n$, or asymptotic sharpness. Consequently, this part cannot yet be expressed as a precise extremal assertion.

Short English statement

The source asks whether connected layer families of $d$-element multisets on $n$ symbols satisfy $\widetilde f(d,n)=d(n-1)$, especially for $d=3$, and whether the stated surface bounds $2n$ with boundary and $n$ without boundary are sharp. Essential definitions and the meaning of sharpness are missing from the supplied record.
