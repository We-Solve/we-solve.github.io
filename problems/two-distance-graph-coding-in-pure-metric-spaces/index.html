Math-12850

Two-distance graph coding in pure metric spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 14 asks to "understand the encoding of graphs in metric structures." This is a research program, without a specified class of graphs, signature, notion of encoding, or target theorem. The supplied aid addresses the following particular encoding, rather than resolving every interpretation of that program.

For a nonempty simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$, put \[ d_G(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&x=y,\\ \tfrac12&\{x,y\}\in E,\\1&x\ne y\text{ and }\{x,y\}\notin E.\end{cases} \] Then $(V,d_G)$ is a complete metric structure of diameter at most $1$ in the pure metric language. The predicate \[ A(x,y)=2\left|d_G(x,y)-\tfrac12\right| \] is $0$ precisely on adjacent pairs and $1$ on other pairs, including the diagonal; this uses the continuous-logic convention that truth has value $0$. Equality is represented by $\min(1,2d_G(x,y))$. With these atomic predicates, Boolean connectives and quantifiers translate by $\neg\mapsto1-\varphi$, $\wedge\mapsto\max$, $\vee\mapsto\min$, $\forall\mapsto\sup$, and $\exists\mapsto\inf$. Thus first-order graph formulas have exact $\{0,1\}$-valued continuous-metric translations on these codes. The displayed scalar function of distance is $2$-Lipschitz; coordinatewise uniform continuity follows from the metric triangle inequality. Induced graph embeddings are exactly isometric embeddings between codes, and graph isomorphisms are exactly their surjective isometries.

For a metric $\widetilde d$ on the same carrier $V$, if \[ \sup_{x,y\in V}|\widetilde d(x,y)-d_G(x,y)|<\tfrac14, \] then, for distinct $x,y$, \[ \{x,y\}\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\widetilde d(x,y)<\tfrac34. \] For bounded nonempty metric spaces $X,Y$, define the correspondence pseudodistance by \[ \delta(X,Y)=\tfrac12\inf_R\operatorname{dis}(R),\qquad \operatorname{dis}(R)=\sup_{(x,y),(x',y')\in R}|d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')|, \] where $R\subseteq X\times Y$ projects onto each factor. The aid asserts that nonisomorphic graph codes satisfy $\delta\geq\tfrac14$. The strict noise bound and the separation constant are sharp: the edge and nonedge graphs on two vertices have code distances $\tfrac12$ and $1$ respectively, share the midpoint metric with off-diagonal distance $\tfrac34$ at uniform error $\tfrac14$, and have correspondence pseudodistance $\tfrac14$. For infinite spaces, this definition is used as stated, without silently restricting to compact spaces or asserting that pseudodistance zero classifies arbitrary metric spaces.

Short English statement

The source asks broadly how graphs can be encoded in metric structures. The supplied partial result uses distances $\tfrac12$ and $1$ to encode edges and nonedges, preserving induced embeddings and first-order formulas, with sharp noise and correspondence-distance bounds. This explicit construction does not fix the scope of the original research program.
