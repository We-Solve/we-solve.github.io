Math-4522

Finite-Dimensional Uniform Embeddings of Compact Metric Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X,d)$ be a compact metric space and let $N$ be a fixed finite Euclidean dimension. Interpreting preservation of equality and inequality of distances as preservation of equality and disequality, a loose embedding into ${\mathbb{R}}^N$ is a continuous map $f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^N$ such that, for all $x,y,u,v\in X$, \[ d(x,y)=d(u,v) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \|f(x)-f(y)\|_2=\|f(u)-f(v)\|_2. \] This condition implies injectivity by comparison with a diagonal pair. The supplied wording does not explicitly specify whether strict ordering of distances must also be preserved; the displayed definition uses disequality, without imposing an order-preservation condition.

The finite-subspace hypothesis is \[ \text{for every finite }F\subseteq X,\quad \text{there exists a loose embedding } f_F:(F,d|_{F\times F})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^N. \] The dimension $N$ is the same for every $F$, but the maps $f_F$ may depend on $F$; no compatibility between these maps is assumed.

The question in the supplied clean statement is whether this hypothesis implies the existence of a loose embedding $f:(X,d)\to{\mathbb{R}}^N$, for every compact metric space $(X,d)$ and every fixed finite dimension $N$.

The accompanying manifold question, read according to the original statement's phrase "satisfy this hypothesis," is whether every compact Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, equipped with its geodesic distance $d_g$, admits a finite dimension $N=N(M,g)$ such that every finite subspace of $(M,d_g)$ loosely embeds into ${\mathbb{R}}^N$. Here geodesic distance means the infimum of the Riemannian lengths of curves joining the two points. The record does not specify connectedness or whether manifolds with boundary are included. In particular, the usual geodesic distance between different connected components is not finite, so the intended class must be clarified before this is a fully specified metric-space assertion. The clean statement's phrase "have this property" could instead refer to loose embeddability of the whole manifold; that alternative target remains unresolved.

There is also a substantive discrepancy between the supplied formulations: the original statement asks whether $X$ is uniformly embeddable into ${\mathbb{R}}^N$, whereas the clean statement asks whether it is loosely embeddable there. The record supplies no definition of uniform embeddability or explanation identifying these targets. The displayed question follows the clean statement, which the registry labels corrected_verified; the original target is retained as an unresolved alternative, and that registry label is not an independent verification.

Short English statement

If every finite subset of a compact metric space admits a continuous embedding into one fixed Euclidean dimension that preserves exactly which distances are equal, must the entire space admit such an embedding? Does every compact Riemannian manifold satisfy the finite-subset hypothesis for some fixed dimension? The source leaves unresolved the distinction between uniform and loose embeddability, the intended manifold conventions, and the referent of "this property."
