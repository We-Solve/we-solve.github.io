Restricted Isometries for Random Scattering Matrices

Definitions and mathematical statement

For terminology only, let $\mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}$, let $m,n\geq1$ be integers, and let $A\in\mathbb K^{m\times n}$. These choices are not specified for the scattering model in the supplied record. For an integer $1\leq s\leq n$, define \[ \Sigma_s(\mathbb K^n)=\{x\in\mathbb K^n:\#\{j:x_j\neq0\}\leq s\}. \] The matrix $A$ satisfies the restricted isometry property of order $s$ with constant $\delta\in[0,1)$ if \[ (1-\delta)\|x\|_2^2\leq\|Ax\|_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\|x\|_2^2 \qquad\text{for every }x\in\Sigma_s(\mathbb K^n). \] For a fixed matrix $A$, exact noiseless uniform recovery at sparsity $s$ means that there exists a single reconstruction map $\Delta_A:\mathbb K^m\to\mathbb K^n$ such that \[ \Delta_A(Ax)=x\qquad\text{for every }x\in\Sigma_s(\mathbb K^n). \] This definition explains one conventional meaning of uniform recovery; the source does not specify whether exact noiseless recovery, stable recovery, or recovery by a particular algorithm is intended. For a random matrix, uniformity requires the assertion to hold simultaneously for all relevant sparse signals on the same realization of the matrix; the required probability of such an event is not supplied.

Supplied open question. Does the random scattering matrix used for sparse recovery satisfy a restricted isometry property in a parameter regime strong enough to yield uniform recovery of all sparse signals?

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not define the scattering matrix, its dimensions, entry formula, probability law, normalization, underlying physical or geometric parameters, or admissible parameter regime. It also does not specify the signal field, sparsity range, required restricted isometry order and constant, recovery method, noise assumptions, or probability bound. Consequently, the question above cannot be converted from this record into a uniquely quantified conjecture about a specified random matrix ensemble. The definitions describe the terminology without selecting the missing model or recovery target. The supplied designation as open is a source label, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the intended random scattering matrices preserve the Euclidean lengths of sparse vectors sufficiently well to support recovery simultaneously for every signal of the prescribed sparsity. The record does not specify the matrix ensemble or the quantitative recovery target, so a precise conjecture remains unresolved and requires human mathematical review.
