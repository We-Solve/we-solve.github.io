Math-4373

Empty Regions in the Biembeddability Poset

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard interval-group conventions: let $I=[0,1]$, and let $\operatorname{PL}_o(I)$ be the group, under composition, of increasing piecewise-linear homeomorphisms of $I$ having finitely many linear pieces. Such homeomorphisms fix both endpoints. Let $F$ denote Thompson's group: the subgroup consisting of maps whose breakpoints lie in ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/2]\cap[0,1]$ and whose slopes are integral powers of $2$. These conventional meanings of $I$, $\operatorname{PL}_o(I)$, and $F$ are not explicitly defined in the supplied record and require confirmation against the cited report.

Write $G\hookrightarrow H$ when there exists an injective group homomorphism from $G$ to $H$. Embeddings here are abstract group embeddings; no compatibility with the given interval actions is imposed. Define biembeddability by \[ G\sim H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\hookrightarrow H\ \text{and}\ H\hookrightarrow G. \] Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of biembeddability classes $[G]$ of finitely generated subgroups $G\leq\operatorname{PL}_o(I)$, ordered by \[ [G]\leq[H]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\hookrightarrow H. \] Two classes are incomparable if neither is less than or equal to the other. A group is elementary amenable if it belongs to the smallest class containing all finite groups and all abelian groups and closed under taking subgroups, quotients, extensions, and directed unions. Elementary amenability is invariant under biembeddability, so it is meaningful to apply this property to a class in $\mathcal P$.

Define the following three regions: \begin{align*} \mathcal R_{\mathrm{EA}}={}&\{[G]\in\mathcal P:\ G\text{ is elementary amenable},\ G\not\hookrightarrow F,\ F\not\hookrightarrow G\},\\ \mathcal R_{\mathrm{NEA}}={}&\{[G]\in\mathcal P:\ G\text{ is not elementary amenable},\ G\not\hookrightarrow F,\ F\not\hookrightarrow G\},\\ \mathcal R_{<F}={}&\{[G]\in\mathcal P:\ G\hookrightarrow F,\ F\not\hookrightarrow G,\ G\text{ is not elementary amenable}\}. \end{align*} The third region can equivalently be represented by finitely generated, non-elementary-amenable subgroups $G\leq F$ into which $F$ does not embed: an embedding into $F$ identifies a representative with its image.

Question. Are all three regions empty; that is, does \[ \mathcal R_{\mathrm{EA}}=\mathcal R_{\mathrm{NEA}}=\mathcal R_{<F}=\varnothing \] hold? Finite generation applies throughout, including to the subgroups representing the third region. The supplied record labels this problem open; that label is a supplied curation claim, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

Short English statement

Among finitely generated subgroups of the interval piecewise-linear homeomorphism group, ask whether every group is comparable with Thompson's group $F$ under abstract embeddability, and whether every non-elementary-amenable subgroup of $F$ contains an isomorphic copy of $F$. The interval and group notation use explicitly stated standard conventions that require source confirmation.
