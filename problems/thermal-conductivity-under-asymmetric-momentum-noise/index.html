Thermal Conductivity Under Asymmetric Momentum Noise

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For oscillator coordinates $q_x$ and momenta $p_x$, the Hamiltonian dynamics has Liouville generator $A$. Define $D_x=p_{x+1}\partial_{p_x}-p_x\partial_{p_{x+1}}$; the source's asymmetric momentum-noise generator is $S=\sum_x(D_x^2+p_xp_{x+1}D_x)$. The combined dynamics adds this stochastic generator to the harmonic Hamiltonian generator. Thermal conductivity is the linear-response coefficient relating energy current to a small temperature gradient, or its source Green--Kubo thermodynamic-limit formulation.
Source scope and qualifications.
This noise does not conserve total momentum, as the report explicitly says. The catalogue literature assessment quotes results for a different energy-and-momentum-conserving noise. Those results do not answer this asymmetric-noise question; pinning, dimension, equilibrium, and limit conventions must remain those of the source model.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For harmonic oscillators, add to the Hamiltonian dynamics the asymmetric momentum noise $S=\sum_x[(p_{x+1}\partial_{p_x}-p_x\partial_{p_{x+1}})^2+p_xp_{x+1}(p_{x+1}\partial_{p_x}-p_x\partial_{p_{x+1}})]$. Is the resulting thermal conductivity finite?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The harmonic energy-momentum conserving model has an explicit answer: conductivity diverges in dimensions one and two and is finite in dimension at least three or with pinning; for general anharmonic interactions and disorder the question remains open. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Determine whether the specified asymmetric momentum noise makes the harmonic chain's thermal conductivity finite.

Exact generator checks.
Use a periodic chain with at least three sites, write $D_x=p_{x+1}\partial_{p_x}-p_x\partial_{p_{x+1}}$, and $K=\tfrac12\sum_xp_x^2$. Since $D_xK=0$, the stated noise satisfies $SK=0$. For total momentum $P=\sum_xp_x$, however, $D_xP=p_{x+1}-p_x$ and $D_x^2P=-(p_x+p_{x+1})$, so no corresponding conservation follows; for uniform nonzero momenta the drift contribution vanishes and $SP=-2P\ne0$.
Stationarity and a nonclosing hierarchy.
Under the centered product Gaussian measure, $D_x$ is skew-adjoint: its vector field is divergence-free and tangent to the quadratic Gaussian exponent. The adjoint of $a_xD_x$, $a_x=p_xp_{x+1}$, sends $1$ to $-D_xa_x=-(p_{x+1}^2-p_x^2)$. Summing around the ring telescopes to zero. Thus $S^*1=0$, confirming Gaussian invariance in this finite periodic setting. Yet \[ S(p_x^2)=2(p_{x-1}^2+p_{x+1}^2-2p_x^2) +2p_x^2(p_{x+1}^2-p_{x-1}^2). \] The cubic drift creates quartic observables from a quadratic one, so the usual closed quadratic covariance method for a linear harmonic-noise model does not apply directly.
First gap.
Energy conservation and Gaussian stationarity are not bounds on the time-integrated current correlation. One needs decay or resolvent estimates uniform in system size for this asymmetric generator. The distinct momentum-conserving noise discussed in the saved literature context cannot supply them without a new argument. The finite-conductivity question for the specified source model remains unresolved.
