Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.95

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $P$ be a finite $p$-group. Define its lower central series by $\gamma_1(P)=P$ and $\gamma_{i+1}(P)=[\gamma_i(P),P]$. Its nilpotency class $c(P)$ is the least integer $c\geq 0$ such that $\gamma_{c+1}(P)=1$; thus the trivial group has class $0$. For $x\in P$, let $x^P=\{g^{-1}xg:g\in P\}$. Define the breadth $b(P)$ by \[ p^{b(P)}=\max_{x\in P}|x^P|=\max_{x\in P}[P:C_P(x)], \] where $C_P(x)=\{g\in P:gx=xg\}$.

Class--Breadth Problem (catalogue entry). Is it true that, for every odd prime $p$ and every finite $p$-group $P$, \[ c(P)\leq b(P)+1? \]

Supplied contextual assertions. The record describes \[ c(P)<\frac{p}{p-1}b(P)+1 \] as the best known bound and attributes it to C. R. Leedham-Green, P. M. Neumann, and J. Wiegold (1969). This literature assessment is supplied source information, not independently verified here. The displayed strict bound requires an unresolved qualification: a nontrivial abelian group has $c(P)=1$ and $b(P)=0$, so the bound as written fails in that case. The supplied record does not specify the necessary restriction or correction.

For $p=2$, the record attributes to W. Felsch, J. Neubüser, and W. Plesken (1981) the assertion that for every positive integer $n$ there exists a finite $2$-group $T_n$ satisfying \[ c(T_n)\geq b(T_n)+n. \] The record writes $n\in\mathbb{N}$ without specifying whether $0$ is included; any additional case $n=0$ follows from the positive cases. These contextual assertions are retained as supplied, and are not assertions of independent literature verification.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every finite group of prime-power order for an odd prime has nilpotency class at most one more than its breadth, where breadth is the base-$p$ logarithm of the largest conjugacy-class size. The supplied record labels the problem open and reports counterexamples to an analogous bound for $2$-groups. Its quoted strict background bound has an unresolved abelian boundary case.
