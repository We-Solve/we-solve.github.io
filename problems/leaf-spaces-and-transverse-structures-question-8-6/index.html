Leaf spaces and transverse structures — Question 8.6

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is left-orderable if it admits a strict total order $<$ such that $g<h$ implies $kg<kh$ for every $k\in G$. Equivalently, there is a positive cone $P$ with $PP\subseteq P$ and \[ G=P\sqcup\{1\}\sqcup P^{-1}. \] For a finite symmetric generating set $S$, let $B_S(r)$ be the word ball. A customary finite-radius analogue asks for $P_r\subseteq B_S(r)\setminus\{1\}$ with \[ B_S(r)\setminus\{1\}=P_r\sqcup P_r^{-1},\qquad x,y\in P_r,\ xy\in B_S(r)\Longrightarrow xy\in P_r. \] Call this predicate $\mathsf{Ord}_r(G,S)$. The source does not define its finite-radius ordering convention, so this explicit positive-cone version must be checked.

With the intended hyperbolic $3$-manifold class and generating-set convention fixed, the first question is whether a single integer $c$ guarantees \[ \forall M,\qquad \mathsf{Ord}_c(\pi_1(M),S_M)\Longrightarrow\pi_1(M)\text{ left-orderable}. \] The weaker question asks for an effective procedure that, from the intended finite description of $M$, returns $c(M)$ with that implication for $M$. It need not return the least such radius. A radius computed separately for each manifold is weaker than a universal bound.

Word radius depends on $S_M$; a rule for choosing generators, or a quantifier over all generating sets, is essential. The source does not state closedness/finite-volume assumptions or an effective input representation. No such manifold or generator restrictions are silently inserted. For the positive-cone convention, global left orders restrict to every finite radius, and compactness relates consistent finite constraints to global orderability; that does not by itself provide a universal or computable detecting radius.

The supplied research summary mentions effective ordering and L-space connections but gives no verified radius result. Its undecidable-in-general/partially-open phrase lacks a precise group class or theorem and is not treated as undecidability for the hyperbolic $3$-manifold problem.

Short English statement

Can left-orderability of the intended hyperbolic $3$-manifold groups be detected at one universal finite radius, or at a radius effectively computed from the manifold? The local-order test, generators, and input category must be specified.
