Math-771

Gonek’s Conjecture on Shifted Negative Zeta Moments

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\zeta(s)$ denote the Riemann zeta function, defined for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ by $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}$ and elsewhere by meromorphic continuation. Fix a real number $k>0$. For real $T>e$ and real $0<\alpha\leq1$, consider \[ I_{-k}(\alpha,T)=\frac{1}{T}\int_T^{2T}\left|\zeta\!\left(\frac12+\alpha+it\right)\right|^{-2k}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] The supplied problem asks for the asymptotic order of $I_{-k}(\alpha,T)$ as $T\to\infty$, uniformly over the entire interval $0<\alpha\leq1$, allowing $\alpha$ to depend on $T$. The determination must include every transition regime near $\alpha=1/\log T$.

If "asymptotic order" means uniform two-sided comparability, the target would be an explicit positive scale $M_k(\alpha,T)$ and constants $c_k,C_k,T_0(k)>0$, independent of $\alpha$ and $T$, such that \[ c_kM_k(\alpha,T)\leq I_{-k}(\alpha,T)\leq C_kM_k(\alpha,T) \qquad(T\geq T_0(k),\ 0<\alpha\leq1), \] where the moment is finite. The supplied record does not specify whether this comparability or a sharper asymptotic equivalent is intended, and it does not supply Gonek's predicted scale or its transition formulas.

There is also an unresolved integrability qualification: the record states no Riemann hypothesis assumption and gives no convention for singularities if the integration segment contains a zero of $\zeta$. Thus finiteness throughout the stated domain is not an assumption that may be silently added. The treatment of any divergent moments, or any intended hypothesis ensuring finiteness, requires clarification before a finite uniform comparison on the full domain can be asserted.

Short English statement

For each fixed real $k>0$, determine how the averaged negative $2k$-th power of the shifted zeta function grows as $T\to\infty$, uniformly for real $0<\alpha\leq1$, including all transitions near $\alpha=1/\log T$. The precise predicted scale, the meaning of asymptotic order, and the treatment of possible zero singularities are not supplied.
