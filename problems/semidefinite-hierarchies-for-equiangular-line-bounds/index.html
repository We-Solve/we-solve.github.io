Semidefinite Hierarchies for Equiangular Line Bounds

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For $0<a<1$, the graph $G_{n,a}$ has vertex set $S^{n-1}$; distinct $x,y$ are adjacent unless $x\cdot y\in\{a,-a\}$. Independent sets encode equiangular lines with common angle $\arccos(a)$. Write $I_k$ for independent sets of size at most $k$ and $I_{=j}$ for those of size $j$. A feasible kernel $T(x,y,Q)$, $Q\in I_{k-2}$, is symmetric in $x,y$ and has positive-semidefinite finite Gram matrices for every $Q$. Put $(B_kT)(S)=\sum_{Q\subseteq S,\,|Q|\leq k-2}\sum_{x,y\in S:\,Q\cup\{x,y\}=S}T(x,y,Q)$. The source's bound is $\Delta_k(G)^*=\inf(1+\lambda)$ subject to $B_kT\leq\lambda\mathbf1_{I_{=1}}-2\mathbf1_{I_{=2}}$ on nonempty $I_k$, using the continuous kernel space stated in the source. Here $\alpha(G)$ is the independence number.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For the $k$-point semidefinite-programming bounds $\Delta_k(G_{n,a})^*$ for equiangular lines, explain their stabilization at $$(1/a^2-2)(1/a^2-1)/2,$$ determine whether $\Delta_k(G_{n,a})^*=\Theta(n^2)$ for fixed $k$ and $a$, and compare the corresponding level-$\alpha$ bound with the Lasserre hierarchy. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The parameter $a$ is the common inner-product magnitude; $\alpha$ denotes the graph independence number, not that magnitude. The stabilization target and comparison are those of the report's $k$-point hierarchy. The Lasserre hierarchy is the moment semidefinite hierarchy for the same independent-set problem; no equality between the hierarchies is asserted here.

The plateau number at one geometric endpoint

The source asks three questions about its particular block-diagonal $k$-point hierarchy. An upper bound for actual line sets does not automatically give a feasible dual kernel for that hierarchy. We examine why its displayed number appears at a familiar geometric endpoint, and identify the missing inference. Let $u_1,\ldots,u_N\in\mathbb R^n$ be unit representatives of distinct equiangular lines with $|u_i^Tu_j|=a$ for $i\ne j$, where $0<a<1$.
Proposition: One has $N\le n(n+1)/2$. If $na^2<1$, one also has \[ N\le\frac{n(1-a^2)}{1-na^2}. \] At an integer $n_0=a^{-2}-2\ge1$, the second bound equals $n_0(n_0+1)/2=(a^{-2}-2)(a^{-2}-1)/2$.
Proof: The symmetric rank-one matrices $P_i=u_iu_i^T$ have Frobenius Gram matrix $(1-a^2)I_N+a^2J_N$, which is positive definite since its quadratic form is $(1-a^2)\|x\|^2+a^2(\sum x_i)^2>0$ for $x\ne0$. Hence the $P_i$ are linearly independent in the vector space of symmetric $n\times n$ matrices, whose dimension is $n(n+1)/2$. For the second bound, the ordinary Gram matrix $G=(u_i^Tu_j)$ is PSD with rank at most $n$, trace $N$, and squared Frobenius norm $N+N(N-1)a^2$. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to its at most $n$ nonzero eigenvalues gives $N^2\le n[N+N(N-1)a^2]$. Divide by $N>0$ and rearrange, using $1-na^2>0$. The case $N=0$ is trivial. Substitution $a^2=1/(n_0+2)$ yields the last identity. ∎
For $a=1/3$, the endpoint $n_0=7$ is attained by 28 lines. For each two-element subset $\{i,j\}\subset\{1,\ldots,8\}$ set \[ v_{ij}=e_i+e_j-\tfrac14\mathbf1_8. \] These vectors have coordinate sum zero and squared norm $3/2$. Distinct pair vectors have inner product $|\{i,j\}\cap\{k,\ell\}|-1/2$, hence magnitude $1/2$. After normalization the absolute inner product is $1/3$. Differences $v_{i\ell}-v_{j\ell}=e_i-e_j$, with $\ell$ distinct from $i,j$, span the seven-dimensional sum-zero subspace, so these lines do span dimension seven. Their number $\binom82=28$ equals both proved bounds.

Verification and remaining gaps

The 28 rational vectors' norms, absolute inner products and exact rank were checked. The failed inference would be to replace a bound on $M_a(n)$ by the identical bound on $\Delta_k(G_{n,a})^*$. The source only guarantees $M_a(n)\le\Delta_k(G_{n,a})^*$; an upper bound on the smaller quantity says nothing by itself about an upper bound on the larger one. No feasible kernel $T$ at the desired hierarchy level is constructed here. Thus the endpoint identity does not explain stabilization over other dimensions, the fixed-$k$ asymptotic behavior, or finite-level exactness. These classical geometric bounds and the known 28-line example are not claimed as new research results.
