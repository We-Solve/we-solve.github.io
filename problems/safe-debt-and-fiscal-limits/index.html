Safe debt and fiscal limits

Statement

Original subject group: Fiscal policy and sovereign debt

Audit classification: Published research agenda. Debt absorption is a survey question; the horizon-specific bondholder safety criterion is editorial. Verification concerns the cited version, not first priority or proof that this exact editorial specification remains unsolved on October 8, 2026.

Definitions and precise research target

Editorial mathematical specification.
This is a family of research targets indexed by declared model primitives, rather than a claim of a single model-independent capacity. A specification must declare a primitive model or admissible model class, information and equilibrium-selection rules, and a comparison map assigning a complete initial economic state and issuance policy to each $b\ge0$. That map states which other initial quantities are held fixed or vary, including output, debt composition and maturity, and the use of issuance proceeds. A successful contribution identifies or bounds the capacity and benefit targets under its explicitly stated restrictions and reports their scope; an illustrative model does not establish a bound for an unrestricted class. Empirical identification additionally requires a declared observable law and compatibility class as in the common conventions; empirical identification is not mandatory for every theoretical contribution.

Fix a sovereign issuing debt in its own currency, a fiscal reaction rule, monetary institutions, and a stochastic process for tax capacity and expenditure commitments. Let $b$ be the initial debt-to-output ratio. Debt delivers an explicitly modeled convenience service to investors, but government bonds may also face rollover failure, restructuring, or inflation-related real losses. For horizon $H$, let $L_H(b)$ be the maximum cumulative real loss to a holder under the induced equilibrium and define a safety event $L_H(b)\le\ell$, with declared tolerance $\ell\ge0$. Declare the initial information $x$, either a specified initial state or a specified initial information event, and a probability law $P_b^x$ for each admissible debt ratio under the comparison map and selected equilibrium. Conditioning on a state requires a specified conditional probability kernel; conditioning on an event requires positive probability. The symbol $\Pr$ below abbreviates $P_b^x$, with $x$ held fixed across the comparison. Define a deterministic admissibility predicate $F_H(b;x)$ from the declared fiscal constraints: specify the information to which fiscal decisions are adapted, budget and resource constraints, adjustment limits, the terminal debt or continuation condition, and the quantifier over histories or probability criterion used for feasibility. Any probability tolerance for fiscal feasibility is a separate declared input. The phrase "the fiscal rule is feasible over $H$" below means $F_H(b;x)=1$. A debt ratio with no equilibrium under the declared selection convention is inadmissible and is excluded before evaluating the loss probability. Thus both capacity and its feasible set depend on these declared inputs; no particular stochastic feasibility quantifier is imposed across all specifications.

For risk tolerance $\alpha\in(0,1)$, define a conditional safe-debt capacity \[ b^{\mathrm{safe}}= \sup\{b\ge0:\Pr(L_H(b)>\ell)\le\alpha \text{ and the fiscal rule is feasible over }H\}. \] Take the supremum in the extended nonnegative reals, with value zero when the feasible set is empty; report emptiness separately from a nonempty feasible set with supremum zero. Identify or bound this capacity and the marginal convenience benefit of additional debt using explicitly justified restrictions on investor demand, fiscal adjustment, and equilibrium selection. Distinguish interest–growth arithmetic from feasible contingent primary surpluses and from self-fulfilling rollover equilibria. Report sensitivity to tail events and institutional credibility. The definition is intentionally horizon-, loss-, and institution-specific; there need not be a universal debt threshold. For the declared issuance comparison, specify a real-valued convenience-benefit functional $B(b;x)$ and a social-welfare functional $W(b;x)$, including the population, social weights, numeraire, discounting, use of receipts and treatment of liquidity services and fiscal or inflation risks. Declare whether the marginal benefit is a derivative, a directional derivative, or the finite-increment quotient $[B(b+\Delta b;x)-B(b;x)]/\Delta b$ for a stated $\Delta b>0$; use a derivative only where it exists. For proposed additional issuance, determine the sign of $W(b+\Delta b;x)-W(b;x)$, and check fiscal admissibility and the loss chance constraint separately at both compared debt ratios. Report whether the chance constraint is slack or binding at each admissible endpoint; being below the supremum alone does not establish safety, feasibility, or an interval of safe intervening issuance levels. If welfare along an issuance path is claimed, check admissibility and safety along that path as well.

Declare the horizon $H\in(0,\infty]$, its time units, and a monitoring set $T_H\subseteq[0,H]$ containing zero; for $H=\infty$, use finite monitoring times in $[0,\infty)$. The maximum below ranges over $h\in T_H$. Finite monitoring grids and continuous monitoring are distinct declared specifications. Require the displayed maximum to exist and to define a measurable random variable under each relevant law; a finite grid with measurable wealth and benchmark values suffices, while other monitoring sets require explicit path and measurability conditions. A specification lacking attainment cannot use this maximum without a separately disclosed change to a supremum criterion.

For a unit investment in a specified sovereign bond, let $V_h(b)$ be cumulative real distributions plus its ex-dividend resale value, all valued in a stated consumption numeraire. Specify the bond's contractual terms and purchase-price normalization, the rule for holding or accumulating earlier distributions to date $h$, and the treatment of recovery, replacement claims and resale after restructuring. Define the account after maturity or liquidation through the last monitoring time, without counting payments or recovery twice. Disclose the account's admissible range and any additional liabilities. The positive benchmark process $V_h^{\rm ref}$ must be defined on the same probability space, in compatible units and at the same monitoring dates; state its economic interpretation, whether it is deterministic or stochastic, and whether it varies with $b$ under the comparison map. Relative to this benchmark, define $L_H(b)=\max_{h\in T_H}[1-V_h(b)/V_h^{\rm ref}]_+$, where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$. This is a random maximum benchmark-relative shortfall along a path, not generally a peak-to-trough drawdown; it is not an essential supremum over states, which would make the chance constraint trivial or deterministic. Specify whether inflation compensation and convenience services enter $V$ or only welfare. Debt capacity can have a disconnected feasible set; its supremum does not imply every smaller debt ratio is safe. Robust safety requires the chance constraint for every model in a stated ambiguity set, using the same fiscal rule and equilibrium-selection convention. For a robust capacity, require fiscal admissibility and a selected equilibrium in every ambiguity-set model as well; declare the common initial information, comparison map and model-specific conditional laws. The ambiguity set is fixed independently of the realized outcomes.

Variants and assumption changes

The following are editorial variants, not additional published open conjectures.
  1. Credit loss only. Use a real indexed bond and define loss only from missed contractual payments and recovery. Compare with a nominal-bond criterion that includes inflation and market-value risk under the same fiscal institutions.
  2. Rollover-selection robustness. Allow several self-fulfilling refinancing equilibria at each debt ratio. Define safety under all admissible equilibrium selections and compare its capacity with that under one favorable selection; specify any lender-of-last-resort intervention.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

These conventions apply to each entry unless explicitly replaced.

Models and identification. A primitive model $m$ specifies all probability laws, feasible choices, information, equilibrium and measurement rules used by its target. The admissible class $\mathcal M$ is fixed independently of outcomes. If $P_O(m)$ is its observable probability law and $\tau(m)$ the target, its identified set at observable law $P$ is \[ \mathcal I_\tau(P)=\{\tau(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_O(m)=P\}. \] Point identification means that this set is a singleton. An empty set signals incompatibility of data and assumptions. Coordinate bounds need not describe the joint set. Bounds are sharp only if every retained target is attainable or arbitrarily approachable by compatible models. Finite bounds require explicit restrictions on unobserved quantities; unrestricted confounding, hidden wealth, extrapolation or arbitrary equilibrium selection can yield uninformative sets.

Causal interventions. A potential outcome $Y_i(p)$ is the outcome under a complete policy regime $p$, including timing, financing, information and market spillovers. The target population and units are fixed before treatment. With interference, use the complete assignment vector or a justified exposure mapping; individual no-interference notation alone cannot identify an economy-wide intervention. A difference of mean potential outcomes does not require the cross-world joint distribution. Conditioning on post-treatment migration, survival, enrollment or employment changes the estimand and needs separate justification.

Statistical statements. An estimator, confidence set, forecast or test specifies a sampling law, model class and loss/coverage criterion. Uniform $(1-\alpha)$-coverage means $\inf_{m\in\mathcal M}\Pr_m\{\tau(m)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha$, or an explicitly asymptotic version. The whole parameter space trivially has coverage, so informativeness requires a stated width, risk or power objective. A forecasting improvement is relative to a named benchmark, information set and evaluation population.

Equilibrium and optimization. An equilibrium comprises feasible plans, optimality, beliefs where needed, budget constraints and all market/resource clearing. The primitives or a stated benchmark define each correspondence $\mathcal E(p;m)$. Nonemptiness is assumed or proved, never inferred just from compact policy sets. A selected-equilibrium target uses a declared selection rule; a robust target quantifies over all permitted equilibria. Use suprema or infima unless attainment is established. An $\varepsilon$-optimal policy is feasible and within $\varepsilon$ of the finite optimum value. Report an empty feasible set separately.

Welfare and accounting. Normative utility, interpersonal normalization, social weights, numeraire, discounting and the use of public receipts are primitives. Behavioral utility need not coincide with the welfare criterion. Household welfare includes ownership income and rents once; adding profits again to compensating variation generally double-counts them. A monetary equivalent is defined by an explicit transfer and price convention, with monotonicity and range conditions. Average effects, mechanism contributions, transfers and welfare losses are different targets. Infinite sums require convergence and dynamic feasible plans require terminal or no-Ponzi conditions.

Source versions. A working-paper date, revision date and journal publication year are distinguished. A DOI for a journal version is not automatically the DOI of an earlier manuscript. Locators refer to the stated version and printed pages where specified. A metadata-only check does not verify a claim about a full-text conclusion.
