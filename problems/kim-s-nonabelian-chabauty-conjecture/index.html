Kim’s Nonabelian Chabauty Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the source's stated scope, let $X/{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a hyperbolic curve and let $p$ be a prime of good reduction. Under the usual convention, a hyperbolic curve is a smooth geometrically connected curve whose smooth projective compactification has geometric genus $g$ and whose geometric complement consists of $r$ points, with $2g-2+r>0$. The supplied excerpt does not specify whether the rational-point formulation assumes that $X$ is proper; this is an unresolved hypothesis.

Write $X({\mathbb{Q}}_p)_n\subseteq X({\mathbb{Q}}_p)$, for positive integers $n$, for the level-$n$ refined Chabauty--Kim sets intended by the source. The supplied record does not give their construction or the auxiliary choices and local conditions defining the refinement. These symbols therefore designate the source's sets rather than a fully specified construction in this draft. Identify $X({\mathbb{Q}})$ with its image in $X({\mathbb{Q}}_p)$.

The rational-point assertion attributed to Kim, labeled Conjecture 4 in the excerpt, is the following: for every curve $X$ and prime $p$ satisfying the intended hypotheses, there exists an integer $n_0\geq 1$ such that \[ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad n\geq n_0\ \Longrightarrow\ X({\mathbb{Q}}_p)_n=X({\mathbb{Q}}). \] The threshold may depend on $X$, $p$, and the data defining the refined sets; no uniform threshold is asserted. This is the supplied conjectural formulation, subject to the unresolved hypotheses and definition above.

The excerpt separately labels as Conjecture 3 the assertion \[ \exists n_1\geq 1\ \forall n\geq n_1,\qquad \#X({\mathbb{Q}}_p)_n<\infty. \] It states that this finiteness follows from the Bloch--Kato conjecture, citing its reference [4], and attributes eventual equality to [7, Conjecture 3.1]. Those references and the precise Bloch--Kato hypothesis are not supplied. The finiteness and equality assertions are recorded separately. In particular, the condition of being hyperbolic alone allows affine curves with infinitely many rational points, so the missing scope cannot be resolved by silently imposing both assertions on every affine hyperbolic curve.

The excerpt also gives an $S$-integral variant for affine hyperbolic curves. Under the conventional interpretation that $S$ is a finite set of rational primes, put \[ {\mathbb{Z}}_S={\mathbb{Z}}[\ell^{-1}:\ell\in S]. \] An integral model $\mathcal X/{\mathbb{Z}}_S$ with generic fiber $X$ is needed to interpret the source's notation $X({\mathbb{Z}}_S)$ and $X({\mathbb{Z}}_p)$; no such model is specified. For a prime $p\notin S$ of good reduction, the source describes refined sets satisfying \[ \mathcal X({\mathbb{Z}}_S)\subseteq X({\mathbb{Z}}_p)_{S,n}\subseteq\mathcal X({\mathbb{Z}}_p) \] and states the conjectural equality \[ \exists n_0\geq 1\ \forall n\geq n_0,\qquad X({\mathbb{Z}}_p)_{S,n}=\mathcal X({\mathbb{Z}}_S). \] Here the integral points are viewed through their natural map to $\mathcal X({\mathbb{Z}}_p)$. This variant remains dependent on the unspecified model and refined local conditions.

Short English statement

Kim's conjecture, as supplied, asks whether the refined Chabauty--Kim sets equal the rational points at every sufficiently large level, with a threshold depending on the curve and prime. The excerpt also states a separate finiteness conjecture and an affine $S$-integral analogue. The rational-point scope, the construction of the refined sets, and the integral model require clarification before this can be a fully self-contained statement.
