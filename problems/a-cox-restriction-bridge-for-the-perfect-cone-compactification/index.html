Math-235

A Cox-restriction bridge for the perfect-cone compactification

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A_g$ be the complex moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension $g$, and let $A_g^F$ denote its first Voronoi, or perfect-cone, compactification. Write $M$ for the weight-one modular-form or Hodge class and $D$ for the boundary divisor; on a coarse space these may require ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier interpretations. Shepherd-Barron's source problem has six parts.
  1. Run the MMP on $A_g^F$ for $g\leq10$, particularly flipping its stated extremal ray. The source additionally says $A_g^F$ is the canonical model of $A_g$ for $g\geq12$, and a minimal model for $g=11$; these are supplied background claims.
  2. For the natural inclusions $A_g^F\to A_{g+1}^F$ parameterized by the elliptic $j$-line, ask whether $H^*(A_g^F,{\mathbb{C}})$ stabilizes and identify the limit. A precise formulation needs degreewise ranges and the induced pullback maps, rather than unstated stabilization of the entire finite-dimensional graded group at once.
  3. Compute the intersections $M^aD^b$ in top degree $a+b=g(g+1)/2$, and the plurigenera of $A_g^F$, with coarse/stack and canonical-power conventions fixed.
  4. Determine whether its total coordinate ring is finitely generated.
  5. Determine whether $12M-D$ is semiample for $g\geq12$, meaning an appropriate positive Cartier multiple is globally generated.
  6. Find its effective cone, related in the source to possible slopes of Siegel cusp forms.

For $g\geq2$, the attached report proposes a bridge between the negative-boundary ring and a lattice Cox ring after finite-index Cartier regrading. A common interpretation, once $NM$ and $ND$ are Cartier, is the section ring with degrees $aNM-bND$, $a,b\geq0$, compared with the multisection ring for the lattice $\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}(NM)+{\mathbb{Z}}(ND)$. The report asserts that adjoining the canonical section of the effective Cartier boundary multiple generates that lattice Cox ring from the exact Shepherd-Barron negative-boundary bigraded ring. The precise definition of that "exact" ring, grading lattice, and relation to a full Weil-class Cox ring are not printed in the summary; these must be checked, rather than silently replacing it by a different ring.

The report claims that finite generation of this ring makes the compactification a Mori dream space, forces $12M-D$ to be semiample in genus $g$ and every lower genus, and makes the effective slope rational and attained. The usual effective slope is the infimum of $a/b$ over effective classes $aM-bD$ with $b>0$, in the specified modular-form convention. The Mori-dream-space conclusion needs particular care: the standard definition includes ${\mathbb{Q}}$-factoriality and other Picard hypotheses, and the source does not provide these for every perfect-cone compactification or specify a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-factorial model. This remains a reported implication with a missing geometric qualification, not an unconditional verified theorem.

The report further claims a fan-level primitive-ray proof of compatibility under a fixed-elliptic product inclusion $i_E:A_h^F\to A_{h+1}^F$. In the indicated Hodge/boundary normalization the asserted restriction behavior is \[ i_E^*M_{h+1}=M_h,\qquad i_E^*D_{h+1}=D_h,\qquad i_E^*(12M_{h+1}-D_{h+1})=12M_h-D_h. \] It uses this to propagate semiampleness to lower genus and says it obtains explicit coarse and stack product-locus intersection recurrences. Those recurrence formulas and their stabilizer/multiplicity normalizations are absent from the record. The candidate does not run the requested low-genus MMP, compute all plurigenera or intersections, or determine the stable cohomology.

Short English statement

The source asks six questions about the perfect-cone compactification: its MMP, stable cohomology, intersections, coordinate ring, semiampleness, and effective cone. The report proposes a finite-generation bridge with lower-genus restrictions, while leaving ring conventions and Mori-dream-space hypotheses unresolved.
