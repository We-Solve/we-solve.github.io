Exponential Degree Growth in Composita of Fibers

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks the following exponential degree-growth question. Some conventions needed for a fully precise formulation are not specified in the record and are identified below.

For a candidate formulation, interpret a curve over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ as a smooth, projective, geometrically integral curve $X$, of genus $g$. Let $t\in{\mathbb{Q}}(X)$ be nonconstant, defining a morphism \[ t:X\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{Q}}},\qquad \nu=[{\mathbb{Q}}(X):{\mathbb{Q}}(t)]\geq2. \] These regularity and geometric integrality conventions require confirmation against the original setup, which is referenced but not reproduced in the original statement.

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$. For each integer $n\geq1$, choose a geometric point $P_n\in X(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})$ with $t(P_n)=n$. Write ${\mathbb{Q}}(P_n)\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ for its field of definition and \[ K_N={\mathbb{Q}}(P_1)\cdots{\mathbb{Q}}(P_N) ={\mathbb{Q}}(P_1,\ldots,P_N) \] for the compositum. A finite critical value of $t$ means a point $b\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^1(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})$ such that some point above $b$ has ramification index greater than $1$.

Assume at least one of the following source conditions holds:

  1. $t$ has a finite critical value in $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}$.
  2. The geometric function-field extension $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)/\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}(t)$ is "nonabelian," in the terminology of the supplied statement.
The second condition requires clarification: no Galois hypothesis or definition of "nonabelian" is supplied. In particular, the record does not specify whether it refers to a Galois extension with nonabelian Galois group, to the Galois group of the normal closure, or to another convention.

The question is whether there exists a positive constant $c(g,\nu)$, depending only on $g$ and $\nu$, such that \[ [K_N:{\mathbb{Q}}]\geq\exp\!\left(c(g,\nu)N\right) \qquad\text{for all sufficiently large integers }N. \] The natural universal reading is that the same $c(g,\nu)$ works for every admissible pair $(X,t)$ and every sequence of choices $(P_n)_{n\geq1}$, with a threshold $N_0$ that may depend on $(X,t)$ and the sequence. The source does not explicitly settle the quantifier over point choices or the allowed dependence of $N_0$; this reading therefore remains provisional. No integers corresponding to ramified fibers are excluded in the supplied statement.

Short English statement

Under either of two stated conditions on a map $t:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^1$, must the field generated by one point above each of $1,\ldots,N$ have degree at least $\exp(c(g,\nu)N)$? The meaning of the nonabelian condition, the curve conventions, and the quantifiers over point choices need confirmation. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
