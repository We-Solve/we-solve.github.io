Fixed winding numbers: exact pair bounds, graph coercivity, and collision loss

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. For a planar collision-free periodic loop, identify the plane with $\mathbb C$ and define $k_{ij}=\deg(t\mapsto(q_i(t)-q_j(t))/|q_i(t)-q_j(t)|)\in\mathbb Z$. These pairwise winding numbers describe homology information; they do not determine the full braid homotopy class.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
In the planar three-body problem, can periodic solutions be obtained by minimizing action while fixing the three winding numbers $k_{ij}$, as in Poincaré's proposal? Also ask whether the answer is the same in the restricted case, as the source’s remark does.
Scope and source qualification.
The source distinguishes collision-free minimizers from collisional ones and records Lagrange equilateral minimizers when all windings are +1 or all −1. The restricted-case question is restored from its remark; no collision-free conclusion is assumed. Winding can prevent spatial escape.
Let $r:[0,P]\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ be an absolutely continuous closed loop with nonzero degree. Write $\bar r=P^{-1}\int r$. The oscillation estimate $\sup_t|r(t)-\bar r|\le\sqrt P\|r'\|_2$ follows by integrating $|r(t)-r(s)|$ and Cauchy--Schwarz. If $|\bar r|$ exceeded the right side, the entire loop would lie in a disk centered at $\bar r$ avoiding zero and would have degree zero, a contradiction. Hence \[\sup_t|r(t)|\le2\sqrt P\|r'\|_2. \] For a center-of-mass-zero n-body loop, apply this bound to every pair with nonzero winding. If the graph of those pairs is connected, path sums control all pair distances, and $q_i=M^{-1}\sum_jm_j(q_i-q_j)$, $M=\sum_jm_j$, controls every position. A bound on action bounds the kinetic energy and therefore all these derivative norms, yielding an H1 bound.
The unresolved collision boundary.
This coercivity does not make the collision-free class closed. The scalar collision profile $r(t)=|t|^{2/3}$ has $\int_0^\delta|r'|^2dt<\infty$ and $\int_0^\delta|r|^{-1}dt<\infty$, because both integrands are constant multiples of $t^{-2/3}$. Thus bounded Newtonian action permits collision limits, where winding numbers cease to be defined. Uniform convergence away from collision preserves degree, but the missing clearance is precisely what must be proved.
Scope.
The first gap is exclusion or analysis of collision minimizers for each prescribed winding class. Disconnected winding graphs can also allow cluster escape, so even coercivity is conditional. No general collision-free minimizing theorem or restricted-problem analogue follows, and fixed homology is not replaced by a full braid constraint. The source's equilateral cases remain context rather than newly established results here.
