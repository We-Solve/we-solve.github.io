Math-7469

Geometric Gradient Flows for Elastic Rods

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Given an initial rod configuration $\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$, the source asks whether geometric gradient flows can find critical points of a Kirchhoff elastic energy, called elasticae in the passage. A critical point is stationary under all variations admissible for the chosen rod model and constraints; it need not be a global minimum, and finding one is different from enumerating every critical point.

A centerline is a regular curve $\gamma$ with arclength parameter $s$, unit tangent $\tau=\partial_s\gamma$ and curvature $\kappa=|\partial_s\tau|$. A full Kirchhoff rod can also require a material orthonormal frame $(d_1,d_2,d_3)$ with $d_3=\tau$. Writing $\partial_sd_i=\Omega\times d_i$ defines bending/twist strains; the twist component is $\Omega\cdot d_3$ and need not equal the Frenet torsion. Thus an unframed point set $\Gamma$ alone does not determine a general twist energy.

For example, an isotropic framed model can have energy \[ E(q)=\frac12\int\left(a\kappa^2+c(\Omega\cdot d_3)^2\right)\,ds, \qquad a,c>0, \] where $q$ includes the curve and frame. This is an explicit illustrative model, not the report's recovered complete functional: intrinsic strain, anisotropy, forces, endpoint conditions, length and topology constraints are not specified in the extracted question.

After fixing a configuration space $\mathcal M$ and an appropriate metric, the desired constrained gradient evolution has the form \[ \partial_tq=-\operatorname{grad}_{\mathcal M}E(q),\qquad q(0)=q_0. \] For a sufficiently regular flow its intended descent identity is $\frac{d}{dt}E(q(t))=-\|\operatorname{grad}_{\mathcal M}E(q(t))\|^2$. The substantive request is to construct a well-defined flow that remains admissible for long time and converges, in an appropriate sense and possibly modulo rigid motion/reparametrization, to an equilibrium. Energy decrease alone is not an existence or convergence theorem. Whether one requires full-sequence convergence, subsequential convergence or a numerical algorithm with guarantees is not stated.

The supplied literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports long-time length-preserving flows and convergence to equilibrium for isotropic Kirchhoff models, including embedded-flow results. It distinguishes those theorems from an unavailable general algorithm for arbitrary three-dimensional configurations and energies. Their exact admissibility, framing, boundary and self-intersection hypotheses are not reproduced; no extension beyond their reported scope is asserted.

Short English statement

Can a geometric gradient flow starting from a spatial rod approach an equilibrium of a Kirchhoff elastic energy? The energy, constraints, frame and convergence notion must be fixed. The supplied assessment reports long-time and convergence results for isotropic length-preserving models, without a general arbitrary-energy algorithm.
