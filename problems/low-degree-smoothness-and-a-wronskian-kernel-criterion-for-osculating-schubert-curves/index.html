Math-1215

Low-degree smoothness and a Wronskian kernel criterion for osculating Schubert curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the supplied contextual reconstruction: let $E=H^0({\mathbb{P}}^1,\mathcal O(d))$, $n=d+1$, $k=r+1$, and $G=\operatorname{Gr}(k,E)$, of dimension $N=k(n-k)$. Let $F_\bullet(x)$ be the complete osculating flag defined by vanishing orders at $x\in{\mathbb{P}}^1$. For pairwise distinct marked points $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ and partitions $\lambda_i\subseteq k\times(n-k)$, form the scheme-theoretic intersection \[ S(\lambda_\bullet;x_\bullet)=\bigcap_{i=1}^s \Omega_{\lambda_i}(F_\bullet(x_i)),\qquad \sum_i|\lambda_i|=N-1. \] The supplied note attributes proper intersection to Eisenbud--Harris, making nonempty $S$ a projective Cohen--Macaulay curve. The question asks whether $S$ is smooth over ${\mathbb{C}}$, including the absence of nonreal singular points. The intended restriction to real versus arbitrary distinct complex $x_i$ is not completely recoverable: the contextual verification ends mid-sentence after "All $x_i$ are."

The source proposes tautological bundles on $M_{0,s}$ to describe the singular-fiber locus, possibly showing it avoids configurations with all $x_i$ real. It reports a check for $\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)$ with five single-box conditions, and suggests longer partitions or $\operatorname{Gr}(2,6)$ next. Its sum-of-squares example uses terms $(x_i-x_j)^2$, suggesting collisions at the boundary. Such a real sum-of-squares exclusion alone does not establish absence of complex singular points. Its final "B. Osserman suggests" has no following suggestion in this record.

The attached aid reports two partial smoothness results: every one-dimensional osculating intersection with at most two nonempty conditions is a normal Richardson curve, hence complex-smooth without a reality assumption; and every real osculating Schubert curve of Plücker degree at most two is complex-smooth. Its explicit longer-partition examples on $\operatorname{Gr}(2,6)$ are $(2,2)$ with three boxes and $(3,2)$ with two boxes, each of total codimension seven.

For the all-box case on $\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)$, there are $s=2n-5$ conditions. In a local coordinate at $x_i$, let \[ B_i(f,g)=f(x_i)g'(x_i)-f'(x_i)g(x_i) \] be the alternating Wronskian evaluation form, understood up to a nonzero fiber trivialization. The aid reports the exact singularity criterion for $U\in S$: \[ U\text{ singular in }S \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists[a_1:\cdots:a_s]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{s-1},\quad U\subseteq\operatorname{rad}\!\left(\sum_i a_iB_i\right). \] Here $\operatorname{rad}(B)=\{v\in E:B(v,w)=0\text{ for all }w\in E\}$. It reports gluing this local description via Wronskian evaluation line bundles to a global projective incidence and an equivalent Wronski-map transversality criterion. These descriptions reduce the hidden-singularity question; they do not prove general complex smoothness.

Short English statement

Determine whether one-dimensional osculating Schubert intersections are complex-smooth, with careful attention to whether their marked points are real. The attached aid reports two-condition and low-Plücker-degree cases and an exact Wronskian-kernel incidence for all-box curves, leaving the general smoothness question open.
