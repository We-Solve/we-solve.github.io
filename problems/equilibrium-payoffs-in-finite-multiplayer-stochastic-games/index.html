Equilibrium payoffs in finite multiplayer stochastic games

LLM status Open unverified

C73 — Stochastic and Dynamic Games - Evolutionary Games - Repeated Games Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra pro 25%35% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

Original sources: On Equilibrium Payoffs in Finite Multiplayer Stochastic Games and Uniform Equilibrium in Finite Stochastic Games, both dated 8 October 2026. Formulation: One merged problem with explicitly distinct payoff evaluations, equilibrium requirements, and initial-state quantifiers.

Definitions and assumptions

Fix a finite stochastic game \[ G=\left(I,S,(A_i(s))_{i,s},P,(g_i)_i\right), \] with finite player set $I$, finite states $S$, finite nonempty action sets $A_i(s)$, a transition kernel $P(\cdot\mid s,a)$, and bounded stage payoffs $g_i(s,a)$. States and realized action profiles are publicly observed after each simultaneous-move stage. Strategies are independently randomized behavioral strategies with perfect recall; no mediator, public sunspot signal, or additional correlating device is included. Write $\Sigma$ for the resulting profile space and $\tau_i$ for an arbitrary unilateral behavioral deviation.

For initial state $s$, profile $\sigma$, horizon $N\ge1$, and normalized discount rate $\lambda\in(0,1)$, define \[ \begin{aligned} \gamma_i^N(s,\sigma) &=\E_{s,\sigma}\!\left[\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}g_i(s_t,a_t)\right],\\ \gamma_i^\lambda(s,\sigma) &=\E_{s,\sigma}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^\infty \lambda(1-\lambda)^{t-1}g_i(s_t,a_t)\right]. \end{aligned} \] The following distinct undiscounted evaluations must not be identified without further assumptions: \[ \begin{aligned} u_i^{\mathrm{path}-}(s,\sigma) &=\E_{s,\sigma}\!\left[\liminf_{N\to\infty} \frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N g_i(s_t,a_t)\right],\\ u_i^{\mathrm{exp}-}(s,\sigma) &=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma),\\ u_i^{\mathrm{mean}}(s,\sigma) &=\lim_{N\to\infty}\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma) \quad\text{when this limit exists}. \end{aligned} \] For a well-defined evaluation $u$, an $\varepsilon$-Nash profile from $s$ satisfies \[ \sup_{\tau_i} \left[u_i(s,\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})-u_i(s,\sigma)\right] \le\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }i\in I. \] The existence of equilibria of each finite discounted game is logically separate from the existence questions below.

Formal research question

For every such game $G$, distinguish the following variants; the quantifiers and the allowable strategy profiles form part of each question.

  1. Uniform Cesàro equilibrium payoffs (principal question). Does there exist $v=(v_i(s))_{i,s}\in\R^{I\times S}$ such that \[ \begin{gathered} \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\sigma^\varepsilon\in\Sigma\ \exists N_\varepsilon\ge1\ \forall s\in S\ \forall i\in I\ \forall N\ge N_\varepsilon:\\[-2pt] \left|\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)-v_i(s)\right|\le\varepsilon,\\ \sup_{\tau_i}\left[\gamma_i^N(s,\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}^\varepsilon) -\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)\right] \le\varepsilon? \end{gathered} \] The same profile $\sigma^\varepsilon$ must work for all sufficiently large horizons and all specified initial states.
  2. Joint Cesàro--discounted uniformity (stronger formulation). Can $v$ and the same $\sigma^\varepsilon$ in (a) also be chosen with $\lambda_\varepsilon>0$ such that, for all $0<\lambda\le\lambda_\varepsilon$, all $s,i$, and every $\tau_i$, \[ \left|\gamma_i^\lambda(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)-v_i(s)\right| \le\varepsilon,\qquad \gamma_i^\lambda(s,\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}^\varepsilon) -\gamma_i^\lambda(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon? \] One must not replace this requirement by a different equilibrium profile for each $\lambda$.
  3. Liminf-average and limiting-average equilibrium payoffs. For each of the evaluations $u^{\mathrm{path}-}$ and $u^{\mathrm{exp}-}$ separately, does there exist a state-indexed vector $v$ such that $\forall\varepsilon>0$ one can choose one $\sigma^\varepsilon$ with \[ \begin{aligned} \left|u_i(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)-v_i(s)\right|&\le\varepsilon,\\ \sup_{\tau_i}\left[u_i(s,\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}^\varepsilon) -u_i(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)\right] &\le\varepsilon \end{aligned} \quad\text{for every }s,i? \] Separately formulate a limiting expected-average version on strategy/deviation classes for which the relevant $N\to\infty$ limits exist, or explicitly specify an upper-limit deviation criterion. These are alternative, not interchangeable, payoff definitions.
  4. Initial-state, refinement, and strategy-class variants. For each of (a)--(c), separately decide existence from one prescribed initial state $s_0$ versus a common state-indexed family for all $s$; ordinary Nash versus subgame-perfect Nash; and unrestricted history-dependent strategies versus stationary, Markov, or finite-memory strategies. Do not infer a stronger variant from a theorem covering only a weaker one.
  5. History-dependent finite-deviation selection criterion. For $u=u^{\mathrm{path}-}$, let $h$ range over finite public histories, $s_h$ be its current state, and \[ \begin{aligned} V_i^\sigma(h)&=\E_{h,\sigma} \left[\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}g_i(s_t,a_t)\right],\\ m_i(s)&=\inf_{\rho_{-i}}\sup_{\tau_i} u_i^{\mathrm{path}-}(s,\tau_i,\rho_{-i}),\\ A(\sigma)&=\sup_{i,h}[m_i(s_h)-V_i^\sigma(h)]_+. \end{aligned} \] Here the tail evaluation defining $V_i^\sigma(h)$ begins after the finite history $h$. Let $\tau_i\star_N\sigma_i$ deviate through the first $N$ stages and then return to $\sigma_i$; set \[ R_N(\sigma)=\max_i\sup_{\tau_i} \left[u_i^{\mathrm{path}-}(s_0, \tau_i\star_N\sigma_i,\sigma_{-i}) -u_i^{\mathrm{path}-}(s_0,\sigma)\right]. \] Does every $G,s_0$ and $\eta>0$ admit a single behavioral profile $\sigma$ satisfying \[ \boxed{\quad A(\sigma)\le\eta,\qquad \sup_{N\ge1}R_N(\sigma)\le\eta\ ?\quad} \] The deviations in $R_N$ have a finite active prefix but their payoffs remain infinite-horizon; the profile cannot depend on $N$.

Interpretation and scope

Variant (a) asks for a vector of approximate equilibrium payoffs, not an exact zero-error Nash equilibrium. Approximate Nash, correlated or sunspot equilibrium, and equilibrium from an unspecified subgame are distinct targets. Disregarding the choice between $\E[\liminf\bar g_N]$ and $\liminf\E[\bar g_N]$ would silently change variant (c). Variant (e) is a separate structural selection problem, not a purported necessary definition of uniform equilibrium. A vanishing-discount equilibrium-payoff limit or a selection of finite-prefix equilibria need not by itself satisfy the common-profile quantifiers in (a), (b), or (e).

Source audit and status

The two supplied stochastic-game notes treat the unrestricted finite-multiplayer uniform-equilibrium existence question as unresolved; no general proof or finite counterexample is presented. They distinguish two-player positive results, existence of discounted stationary Nash equilibria, and restricted absorbing-game results from the arbitrary multiplayer question. Their state-flat discounted branch and continuation-potential criteria are conditional devices, not unconditional existence theorems. The finite-deviation selection criterion in (e) is expressly identified as an unproved missing step. These are status claims of the supplied notes, not an independent literature or proof audit.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.
