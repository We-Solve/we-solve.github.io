Stability of Almost-Einstein Metrics

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual Riemannian interpretation, an Einstein metric on a smooth manifold $M$ is a Riemannian metric $g$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{Ric}(g)=\lambda g \] for a real constant $\lambda$, where $\operatorname{Ric}(g)$ denotes the Ricci curvature tensor. The supplied record does not explicitly specify the category of manifolds or metrics.

The source asks the following stability question: if a manifold $M$ admits a metric $\bar g$ that is almost Einstein in a suitable sense, does there exist an Einstein metric $g$ on the same manifold that is close to $\bar g$? Here both the almost-Einstein condition and the required closeness remain unspecified. The dimension, compactness, completeness, boundary conditions, normalization of the Einstein constant, geometric bounds, and topology or norm for comparing metrics are also unspecified. Consequently, the record does not determine a fully quantified implication with a prescribed error threshold and conclusion.

The original statement additionally contains a damaged formula labelled $(4)$. A tentative reading of its visible symbols is \[ \text{for every }x\in M,\qquad e^{b\,d(x,M_{\mathrm{thick}})} \int_M e^{-(2-\delta)d(x,y)} \left|\operatorname{Ric}(\bar g)+2\bar g\right|_{\bar g}^{2}(y) \,\mathrm{dvol}_{\bar g}(y) \leq \varepsilon^{2}. \tag{4} \] This is a provisional transcription, not an established hypothesis of the general question. Under this reading, $d$ would denote the distance induced by $\bar g$, $\mathrm{dvol}_{\bar g}$ its volume measure, and $|\cdot|_{\bar g}$ its tensor norm. The source does not define $M_{\mathrm{thick}}$, the parameters $b,\delta,\varepsilon$, their ranges or dependencies, or the other hypotheses accompanying $(4)$. The residual $\operatorname{Ric}(\bar g)+2\bar g$ uses Einstein constant $-2$, but the record does not establish that this normalization applies to the unrestricted question. No precise stability theorem or unrestricted affirmative assertion is supplied.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a metric that is sufficiently close to being Einstein guarantees an Einstein metric nearby on the same manifold. The source leaves the hypotheses and meaning of closeness unresolved, and an accompanying weighted curvature inequality is damaged and incomplete. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved under strong geometric hypotheses; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
