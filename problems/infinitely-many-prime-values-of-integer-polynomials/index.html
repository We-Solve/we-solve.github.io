Math-6543

Infinitely Many Prime Values of Integer Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbb P=\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}:p>1\text{ and the only positive divisors of }p\text{ are }1,p\}$. Let $P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]$ be irreducible in ${\mathbb{Z}}[X]$, with $\deg P\geq2$. Here irreducibility means that $P$ cannot be expressed as a product of two nonunits of ${\mathbb{Z}}[X]$; its units are $1$ and $-1$. Say that $P$ has no fixed prime divisor if \[ \forall p\in\mathbb P\;\exists m\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\quad p\nmid P(m). \] The supplied assertion is that every such polynomial satisfies \[ \#\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:P(n)\in\mathbb P\}=\infty. \] Unresolved sign qualification. The supplied statement imposes no sign condition on $P$ and does not replace $P(n)$ by $\lvert P(n)\rvert$. With positive primes as defined above, polynomials of even degree with negative leading coefficient are eventually negative in both integer directions, so the displayed assertion cannot hold for such polynomials. The intended formulation therefore requires clarification: a positivity hypothesis or an absolute-value convention would change the supplied assertion. Neither is silently imposed here. The domain $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ is retained exactly as supplied.

The original extraction also contains adjacent material on largest prime factors of quartic polynomial values in the cyclic and dihedral cases, attributed to Cécile Dartyge, in joint work with James Maynard. It attributes a general bound to Tenenbaum, reference [6], under the conditions that $f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]$ is irreducible, $\deg f\geq2$, $0<\alpha<2-\log4$, and $x\geq x_0(\alpha,f)$. The extracted product and largest-prime-factor notation are corrupted, so no precise auxiliary inequality is asserted here.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether every irreducible integer polynomial of degree at least two, with no prime dividing all its integer values, takes positive prime values at infinitely many integer arguments. A missing sign qualification prevents treating this as a precise universal conjecture as written. The supplied open label and reconstruction claim require independent review.
