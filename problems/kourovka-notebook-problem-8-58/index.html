Kourovka Notebook Problem 8.58

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a topological group with identity $1$. Say that $G$ is locally nilpotent if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is nilpotent, where a group $H$ is nilpotent if its lower central series $\gamma_1(H)=H$, $\gamma_{k+1}(H)=[\gamma_k(H),H]$ reaches $\{1\}$ after finitely many steps. Say that $G$ is torsion-free if, for every $g\in G$ and every integer $n\geq 1$, $g^n=1$ implies $g=1$. Here the source's phrase "without elements of finite order" is understood to exclude nonidentity elements of finite order.

A subgroup $N\leq G$ is isolated in $G$ if \[ \forall g\in G\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\qquad g^n\in N\Longrightarrow g\in N. \] Closedness refers to the topology of $G$, and normality means $gNg^{-1}=N$ for every $g\in G$.

The literal source question asks whether every locally compact, locally nilpotent, torsion-free, nonabelian topological group $G$ has a subgroup $N$ satisfying \[ N\trianglelefteq G,\qquad N\text{ is closed},\qquad N\neq G,\qquad \forall g\in G\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\quad g^n\in N\Longrightarrow g\in N. \] The source does not require $N\neq\{1\}$. Whether a nontrivial subgroup was intended is unresolved; that additional requirement has not been inserted. The source also does not explicitly specify a Hausdorff convention for locally compact groups.

Short English statement

Must every locally compact, locally nilpotent, torsion-free, nonabelian group contain a proper closed normal subgroup that contains every element having a positive power in that subgroup? The source leaves unclear whether the subgroup must also be nontrivial and whether Hausdorffness is assumed.
