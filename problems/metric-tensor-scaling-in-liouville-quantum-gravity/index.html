Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
A Liouville quantum gravity field $h$ is a random generalized function; its exponential is defined by regularization rather than by pointwise evaluation. The parameter $\gamma$ controls the field coupling. A random metric's Hausdorff dimension $\beta$ refers to covering behavior measured in that metric. A formal tensor $e^{2\gamma h/\beta}(dx^2+dy^2)$ describes a proposed conformal scaling prescription, rather than an ordinary smooth Riemannian tensor.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For $\gamma$-Liouville quantum gravity, is the scaling-limit metric tensor associated with the field $h$ given formally by

$$e^{2\gamma h/\beta}(dx^2+dy^2),$$

where $\beta$ is the Hausdorff dimension of the Liouville metric?

Source scope and qualification.
The displayed tensor is expressly formal. Because $h$ is distribution-valued, this is not an equality of pointwise metric coefficients; a regularization and limiting distance construction are required. No particular formula for $\beta$ is added.

Short English statement

Does the Liouville quantum gravity metric have the proposed formal conformal scaling with its Hausdorff dimension?

The constant-shift consistency test.
Fix positive $\gamma$ and $\beta$, and first replace the generalized field by a smooth function $h_\varepsilon$. The proposed regularized length of a piecewise smooth path $\zeta$ is \[ L_{\varepsilon,h}(\zeta)=\int e^{\gamma h_\varepsilon(\zeta(t))/\beta}|\zeta'(t)|\,dt, \qquad D_{\varepsilon,h}(x,y)=\inf_{\zeta:x\to y}L_{\varepsilon,h}(\zeta). \] Assume the regularization preserves constants. For every constant $c\in\mathbb R$, every path length is multiplied by the same factor, so taking the infimum gives exactly \[ D_{\varepsilon,h+c}(x,y)=e^{\gamma c/\beta}D_{\varepsilon,h}(x,y). \] Any normalized distance limit using a normalization independent of this additive constant would inherit this identity, provided the limit exists.

A regularized Liouville area measure with field-independent renormalization and exponential factor $e^{\gamma h_\varepsilon}$ scales instead by $e^{\gamma c}$. If such a measure were identified with a covariantly normalized $\beta$-dimensional measure of the limiting metric, scaling all distances by $a$ would scale that measure by $a^\beta$. These two transformation laws are consistent precisely with $a=e^{\gamma c/\beta}$. This is a compatibility calculation under stated measure hypotheses, not a deduction of $\beta$ from the field or a proof that the area measure is the metric Hausdorff measure.

A regularization countercheck.
For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, on a compact coordinate region the smooth positive weight $e^{\gamma h_\varepsilon/\beta}$ has bounds $0<c_\varepsilon\leq w\leq C_\varepsilon<\infty$. Writing $D^{\rm patch}_{\varepsilon,h}$ for the distance with paths restricted to a sufficiently small convex coordinate patch, we have \[ c_\varepsilon|x-y|\leq D^{\rm patch}_{\varepsilon,h}(x,y) \leq C_\varepsilon|x-y|. \] The upper bound uses the straight path, and the lower bound uses Euclidean path length. Locally the smooth metric therefore has Hausdorff dimension two, whatever numerical value of $\beta$ appears in the exponential. This does not force the limiting dimension to be two: the comparison constants depend on $\varepsilon$ and need not remain bounded after normalization.
The unproved limiting operation.
Neither calculation controls optimized paths as the field becomes rough. One still needs a specified normalization, tightness and nondegeneracy of the distance functions, identification of their limit with the intended LQG metric, and a proof of its dimension. In particular, the constant-shift identity cannot justify interchanging regularization with the infimum over paths. The source tensor remains a formal prescription; no pointwise exponential of a distribution or new formula for its Hausdorff dimension is asserted.
