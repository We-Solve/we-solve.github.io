Transported compact resolvent and a Tauberian entropy-dimension reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This damaged extraction joins a free-Gibbs compactness question and a separate free-entropy-dimension question. Matrix size and tuple length are denoted differently below to avoid the source's mixed indices.

1. Compactness of the limiting free heat semigroup. Consider a normalizable matrix model with density proportional to $\exp[-N\operatorname{Tr}P(A_1,A_1^*,\ldots,A_m,A_m^*)]$, and suppose a limiting tracial law exists. The source recalls the conjugate-variable relation $J_j=D_jP$, with $D_j$ the cyclic derivative, and asks whether \[ e^{-t\mathcal A},\qquad \mathcal A=\sum_{j=1}^m\partial_j^*\partial_j, \] is compact on the limiting $L^2$ space, for positive time. Here $\partial_j$ are Voiculescu free difference quotients with respect to the limiting generators; the operator must be defined from the closure of the quadratic form $q(\xi)=\sum_j\|\partial_j\xi\|_2^2$ with the coarse product-trace target. Closability, domains and the self-adjoint realization are necessary qualifications. The source suggests beginning with $P=\sum_iA_i^2+\sum_\ell t_\ell q_\ell(A)$, citing convergence work of Guionnet and Maurel-Segala, but gives no smallness or convergence hypotheses. The aid concerns the self-adjoint finite-tuple case, not every complex matrix model in the initial display.

For a nonnegative self-adjoint $\mathcal A$, compactness of $e^{-t\mathcal A}$ for any, equivalently every, $t>0$ is equivalent to compact resolvent. The aid reports this property for the finite variance-one semicircular tuple $S=(S_1,\ldots,S_m)$: its free-gradient Laplacian is the number operator $\mathcal N$, with eigenvalue $d$ on degree $d$ and multiplicity $m^d$, $d\ge0$. Its heat trace is finite exactly when $me^{-t}<1$, and then equals $(1-me^{-t})^{-1}$. Compactness at every positive time does not imply trace class at every positive time.

The aid's transfer criterion assumes a trace-preserving analytic free transport, a Jacobian boundedly invertible on the coarse $L^2$ bimodule, and compatible cores for the closed gradient forms. Equivalently for the comparison step, its induced $L^2$ unitary $U$ must give constants $0<a\le b<\infty$ such that \[ a q_0(\xi)\le q(U\xi)\le b q_0(\xi) \] on a common form core, hence on the corresponding closed form domains. The reported consequences are compact resolvent and eigenvalue comparisons \[ a\lambda_k(\mathcal N)\le\lambda_k(\mathcal A) \le b\lambda_k(\mathcal N), \] with multiplicities, and eventual heat trace class, for example when $at>\log m$. The aid applies this to sufficiently small analytic self-adjoint free-Gibbs perturbations for which those transport hypotheses hold; no quantitative smallness theorem is supplied in the record.

For a correlated quadratic semicircular law with positive covariance matrix $C$, the reported exact spectrum consists of all finite sums of precision eigenvalues $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_m$ of $C^{-1}$: \[ 0,\quad\nu_{i_1}+\cdots+\nu_{i_d},\qquad d\ge1, \] counted by words. In this normalization the heat trace is $(1-\sum_i e^{-t\nu_i})^{-1}$ when $\sum_i e^{-t\nu_i}<1$. The variance-one convention corresponds to potential $\tfrac12\sum_iA_i^2$; the source's $\sum_iA_i^2$ has a different precision normalization. Arbitrary free-Gibbs compactness is not settled by these conditional comparisons.

2. Reconstructed entropy-dimension question. Let $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$ be a bounded self-adjoint tuple and let $S$ be a variance-one semicircular tuple free from $X$. Put $X_t=X+\sqrt t\,S$. The extraction has a damaged second delta symbol, inconsistent indices, and printed limsup operations. The supplied verification explicitly replaces those displays by the following reconstructed standard formulas, rather than treating the OCR as exact: \[ \delta^*(X)=m-\liminf_{t\downarrow0} \frac{\chi^*(X_t)}{\log\sqrt t},\qquad \delta^\star(X)=m-\liminf_{t\downarrow0}t\Phi^*(X_t). \] Here $\chi^*$ is nonmicrostates free entropy and $\Phi^*$ is the total free Fisher information $\sum_j\|J_j\|_2^2$, not an additional repeated sum of that total. The question is whether $\delta^*(X)=\delta^\star(X)$ in general, in these explicitly reconstructed conventions.

Using the de Bruijn relation $\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}\chi^*(X_t)=\tfrac12\Phi^*(X_t)$, define $u=-\log t$ and $g(u)=e^{-u}\Phi^*(X_{e^{-u}})$. When the usual regularization and finite-reference-entropy hypotheses justify integration, the aid obtains \[ \delta^*(X)=m-\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^Tg(u)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} u, \qquad \delta^\star(X)=m-\liminf_{u\to\infty}g(u). \] Thus equality holds whenever $t\Phi^*(X_t)$ has a limit as $t\downarrow0$. The remaining issue is equality of the lower logarithmic Cesàro average and the pointwise lower limit; the aid does not remove this Tauberian gap in general. It is unrelated to proving compactness for all limiting matrix models.

Short English statement

The merged record asks about compact free-Gibbs heat semigroups and equality of two nonmicrostates entropy dimensions. The aid transfers compact resolvent from a semicircular number operator under explicit analytic-transport and form-core hypotheses. For the separately reconstructed dimension definitions, de Bruijn's identity reduces equality to a logarithmic averaging issue, settled when the Fisher-information scaling has a limit.
