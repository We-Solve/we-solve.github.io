Math-14497

Structural Explanation of the McKay Conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite group $H$, let $\operatorname{Irr}(H)$ denote the set of irreducible ordinary complex characters of $H$. For a prime $\ell$, define \[ \operatorname{Irr}_{\ell'}(H)=\{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(H):\ell\nmid\chi(1)\}. \] Let $G$ be a finite group, let $\ell$ be a prime, and let $P$ be a Sylow $\ell$-subgroup of $G$; thus $|P|=\ell^a$, where $\ell^a$ is the largest power of $\ell$ dividing $|G|$. Define its normalizer by \[ N_G(P)=\{g\in G:gPg^{-1}=P\}. \] The equality referred to as the McKay conjecture in the supplied record is \[ \left|\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell'}(G)\right| = \left|\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell'}(N_G(P))\right| \qquad\text{for every finite group }G,\text{ every prime }\ell,\text{ and every Sylow }\ell\text{-subgroup }P. \] This includes $\ell\nmid |G|$, when $P=\{1\}$ and $N_G(P)=G$.

Source question. Give a structural explanation of why this equality should hold. The finite-group setting and ordinary complex-character notation above make explicit the standard interpretation of the supplied formulation and report context. The source asks an explanatory question, rather than specifying a particular construction or a formally defined criterion for an explanation. Consequently, the equality precisely identifies the phenomenon to be explained, but the intended meaning of a satisfactory structural explanation remains unspecified. Its presentation here does not independently assess its current mathematical status.

Short English statement

The question asks for a structural reason why a finite group and the normalizer of a Sylow $\ell$-subgroup should have equally many irreducible complex characters whose degrees are not divisible by $\ell$. The supplied record labels this explanatory question open; it does not specify what would count as a satisfactory explanation.
