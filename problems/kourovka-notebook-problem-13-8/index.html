Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.8

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is periodic if every element has finite order, locally soluble if every finitely generated subgroup is soluble, and locally nilpotent if every finitely generated subgroup is nilpotent. For $g\in G$, its centralizer is \[ C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}. \] A group $H$ is Chernikov if it has a normal subgroup $A$ of finite index such that \[ A\cong\prod_{j=1}^{r}C_{p_j^\infty}, \] where $r\geq 0$, each $p_j$ is a prime, and $C_{p^\infty}$ is the Prüfer $p$-group, the union of an ascending chain of cyclic groups of orders $p,p^2,\ldots$. The empty product is trivial, so finite groups are Chernikov.

For each of the following hypotheses separately, the question asks whether every locally soluble periodic group $G$ satisfying that hypothesis admits a finite normal series \[ 1=G_0\trianglelefteq G_1\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_n=G \] for some integer $n\geq 0$, with $G_{i+1}/G_i$ locally nilpotent for every $0\leq i<n$:

  1. (a) There exists $g\in G$ such that $C_G(g)$ is finite.
  2. (b) There exists $g\in G$ such that $C_G(g)$ is Chernikov.
There is no prescribed uniform bound on the length $n$. The displayed chain specifies successive normality. The supplied wording does not explicitly settle whether every $G_i$ must additionally be normal in $G$; that convention remains a formulation issue for review.

The supplied registry labels the problem partially solved and reports an affirmative solution of part (a), attributed to E. Khukhro. This is a supplied source assessment, not an independently verified mathematical assertion here. No resolution of part (b) is supplied.

Short English statement

For a locally soluble group in which every element has finite order, does the existence of an element with finite centralizer, or more generally Chernikov centralizer, force a finite normal series with locally nilpotent factors? The supplied record reports an affirmative solution of part (a); it supplies no resolution of part (b). The intended normality convention for the series needs review.
