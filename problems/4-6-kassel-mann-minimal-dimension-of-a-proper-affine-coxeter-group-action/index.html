Math-2185

4.6 (Kassel, Mann) — Minimal dimension of a proper affine Coxeter-group action

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finite simplicial graph $\Lambda$, the associated right-angled Coxeter group is \[W_\Lambda=\langle s_v\ (v\in V\Lambda)\mid s_v^2=1,\ [s_v,s_w]=1\text{ when }\{v,w\}\in E\Lambda\rangle.\] The intended finite-generation convention is supported by the summary's number of generators but is not explicit in the short question. An affine action on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ has the form \[g\cdot x=L(g)x+b(g),\] where $L:W_\Lambda\to\operatorname{GL}_n({\mathbb{R}})$ is a representation and \[b(gh)=b(g)+L(g)b(h).\] It is proper if for every compact $C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, the set $\{g:gC\cap C\ne\varnothing\}$ is finite. Properness allows finite stabilizers and does not mean a free action; no cocompactness or preservation of a Euclidean metric is required.

Kassel and Mann ask to determine, for each given $W_\Lambda$, \[a(W_\Lambda)=\min\{n:W_\Lambda\text{ admits a proper affine action on }{\mathbb{R}}^n\},\] with $+\infty$ if there is no such dimension. Equivalently, find the sharp dimension admitting some pair $(L,b)$ with the above properness, and exclude every smaller allowed dimension. The convention whether $n=0$ is allowed matters for finite groups and is not specified. The task asks for the minimum for a general given group, rather than merely a uniform upper bound or existence in some dimension.

The supplied summary attributes proper-action existence and dimension reductions to Danciger--Guéritaud--Kassel. It specifically reports a $k$-dimensional bound, written ${\mathbb{R}}^{p+q+1}$ with $p+q+1=k$, for $k$ generators, and improvements for particular cohomological-dimension-two and -four examples. The exact dimension formula is a supplied claim requiring verification from that paper; a standard linear representation's dimension alone does not establish an affine proper-action dimension. The summary expressly says that these results do not determine the sharp minimum for general right-angled Coxeter groups.

Short English statement

Determine the smallest affine dimension in which a given right-angled Coxeter group acts properly. Existing existence and upper-bound claims in the record are distinct from this sharp minimization problem.
