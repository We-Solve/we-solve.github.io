Math-3466

Mass-Decreasing Flows and the Positive Mass Theorem

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M^3,g)$ be an asymptotically flat Riemannian three-manifold. At an asymptotically flat end with coordinates $x=(x^1,x^2,x^3)$, the ADM mass is conventionally written \[ m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(g)=\frac{1}{16\pi}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\{|x|=r\}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\left(\partial_j g_{ij}-\partial_i g_{jj}\right)\frac{x^i}{r}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} S, \] provided suitable decay and integrability hypotheses make this limit well defined and coordinate independent; here ${\,\mathrm{d}} S$ is Euclidean surface measure. The supplied record does not specify those hypotheses or the number of ends.

Source question. Can the mass-decreasing flow on asymptotically flat three-manifolds be used to give an independent proof of the positive mass theorem?

In its usual Riemannian interpretation, the nonnegativity conclusion sought is $m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(g)\geq 0$ for every admissible initial metric with nonnegative scalar curvature $R_g\geq 0$. This interpretation does not supply the missing admissibility hypotheses: the record does not state completeness, boundary conditions, decay rates, regularity, or treatment of multiple ends. It also does not specify whether the requested proof includes the rigidity conclusion concerning zero mass. Consequently, this is a question about a proof method, with the exact version of the positive mass theorem still to be fixed.

The term mass-decreasing flow refers to the construction discussed in the supplied Haslhofer references. Its evolution equations, admissible initial data, continuation procedure, and required estimates are not included in the record. An independent proof would need to establish the intended positive mass conclusion without assuming that conclusion in the flow argument. The supplied assessment says that elimination of mass was proved only under an a priori inequality, whose statement and hypotheses are not supplied. No unconditional mass-elimination assertion is made here.

Short English statement

Can Haslhofer's mass-decreasing flow yield an independent proof of the positive mass theorem for asymptotically flat three-manifolds? The precise flow, geometric hypotheses, and role of an additional a priori inequality require clarification from the cited sources.
