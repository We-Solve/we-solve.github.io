Math-6959

Cyclicity of Surface Quotients by Connected Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let a connected reductive complex algebraic group $G$ act on ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, and let its two-dimensional affine categorical quotient be \[ {\mathbb{C}}^n//G=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{C}}^n]^G \simeq{\mathbb{C}}^2/\Gamma \] for a finite algebraic action of $\Gamma$. The source concludes that $\Gamma$ should be cyclic, meaning generated by one element. Connectedness is already in its first hypothesis and is repeated in its concluding clause; it is retained.

The unrestricted presentation of $\Gamma$ is insufficient. Take $G=\mathbb G_m$ acting effectively on ${\mathbb{C}}^3$ by $a\cdot(x,y,z)=(x,y,az)$. Then ${\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{C}}^3]^G={\mathbb{C}}[x,y]$ and the quotient is ${\mathbb{C}}^2$. On the other hand the noncyclic group $S_3$ acts faithfully on \[ V=\{(u_1,u_2,u_3)\in{\mathbb{C}}^3:u_1+u_2+u_3=0\}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^2 \] by permutation. Its invariant ring is ${\mathbb{C}}[e_2,e_3]$, where $e_2,e_3$ are the elementary symmetric polynomials restricted to $V$, so $V/S_3\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^2$. This meets the extracted quotient hypotheses with a noncyclic finite group, even with faithful actions.

Thus a finite quotient presentation is not unique in a way that permits the literal cyclicity conclusion. One possible intended statement could concern a distinguished small/no-pseudoreflection quotient group, or existence of some cyclic quotient presentation rather than cyclicity of every given presentation. These are different assertions and neither missing restriction is supplied. The example uses complex reflections; no condition excluding them or requiring an isolated nontrivial quotient singularity occurs in the record.

The exact original claim is preserved for source review instead of silently replacing $\Gamma$ by a preferred local group. The literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports no authoritative complete resolution, but does not resolve this presentation ambiguity or elementary counterexample. That open label is not treated as certification of the literal assertion or as a status assessment of any repaired conjecture.

Short English statement

The source predicts a cyclic finite group for a two-dimensional quotient of affine space by a connected reductive group. As written, arbitrary finite presentations invalidate that conclusion: a faithful $S_3$ reflection quotient is already ${\mathbb{C}}^2$. A canonical/small-group convention or an existence-of-cyclic-presentation target is missing.
