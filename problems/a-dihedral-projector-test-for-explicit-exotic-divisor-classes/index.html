Math-1262

A dihedral projector test for explicit exotic divisor classes

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X/{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the Hilbert modular surface in the source discussion, and let ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ denote the maximal abelian extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$. For a smooth projective model and a prime $\ell$, a divisor Tate class is a class in $H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(1))$ fixed by the Galois group of some finite extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$. A divisor represents such a class if its cycle class is that class, allowing rational or coefficient-field linear combinations as appropriate. Being defined over a field for a cohomology class means Galois invariance over that field; an actual cycle representative has a separate descent question.

Ramakrishnan's 2007 problem reports that the Tate classes arising from modular curves on $X$ are defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$, while certain other classes are known to be algebraic and are defined over dihedral extensions, but not over ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. The task is to find specific algebraic representatives for those latter classes. This does not assert that every Hilbert modular surface has every type of class described. Non-congruence curves on $X$ are suggested as possible representatives. Intersecting representatives with modular curves is proposed as a possible way to obtain interesting non-Heegner points, not as a proven conclusion. The surface's level, compactification/model, classes, and meaning of an explicit representative are not specified further.

The supplied later report offers a sufficient criterion rather than a representative. In the actual finite dihedral Galois quotient $G$ governing a proposed divisor orbit, take an irreducible representation $\rho$ of dimension greater than one. Over a characteristic-zero splitting coefficient field $E$, the central projector on the corresponding isotypic component is \[ e_\rho=\frac{\dim\rho}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_\rho(\sigma^{-1})\sigma. \] In a divisor-class space carrying this Galois action and an invariant intersection form, put $D=e_\rho[C]$ for a proposed curve $C$ defined over the dihedral field. The reported character-weighted certificate is \[ \Theta_\rho(C)=\frac{\dim\rho}{|G|} \sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_\rho(\sigma^{-1})(C\mathbin{\cdot}\sigma C) =[C]\mathbin{\cdot}D=D\mathbin{\cdot}D. \] The last equality uses the self-adjoint idempotent in this dihedral intersection setting. Nonzero $\Theta_\rho(C)$ is sufficient to certify $D\ne0$; the converse is not asserted. The report states that divisors defined over abelian extensions have zero $\rho$-projection, and that $D$ has intersection degree zero with each abelian-defined modular-curve class. For a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-defined smooth projective modular curve $M$, a well-defined restriction of this divisor combination therefore has degree zero. If its projected class is nonzero in $\operatorname{Jac}(M)(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes E$, the report concludes that the projected combination is non-torsion and cannot descend to ${\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. This conditional conclusion neither guarantees a nonzero restriction nor supplies an individual non-Heegner point. Compactification, proper intersection and restriction conventions, and the actual Galois module must be fixed before applying the criterion.

Short English statement

Find concrete curves representing the algebraic Tate classes on a Hilbert modular surface whose fields of definition are dihedral rather than abelian. The supplied report gives a character-projector and intersection certificate for proposed curves, but produces no explicit representative and no guaranteed non-Heegner point.
