Resolved Root-Distribution Lead-In for Littlewood Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The recoverable displayed expression is \[ f_n(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_j z^j. \] For purposes of interpreting this expression, take $n$ to be a nonnegative integer and $z$ a complex variable. The symbols $\xi_j$ denote random coefficients, but the supplied statement does not specify their values, marginal distributions, dependence, or probability space. These omissions prevent recovery of a particular random-polynomial ensemble.

For any nonzero realization with complex coefficients, its roots can be represented by the multiset \[ \mathcal Z_n=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:f_n(z)=0\}, \] where roots are counted with algebraic multiplicity. Its cardinality equals the degree of that realization, which equals $n$ only if $\xi_n\ne0$. The supplied statement does not exclude the zero polynomial or specify how such a realization should be treated.

The source question is: What can be said about the distribution of the roots of $f_n$? It does not specify whether this means the joint law of $\mathcal Z_n$, an expected or empirical root measure, an asymptotic distribution as $n\to\infty$, or another root statistic. Although the title and background identify Littlewood polynomials, they do not recover the missing coefficient assumptions or a precise target assertion.
