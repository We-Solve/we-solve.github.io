Math-15268

Parametrization of Supersingular Cohomology Modules

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record uses $H_s^{(1)}$ for an algebraic object acting on modules and $B_s^b$ for an object whose cohomology contains modules described as supersingular. It does not define these objects, the indices $s,b$, the parameter $t$, or supersingularity in this setting.

Source question. Can the supersingular $H_s^{(1)}$-modules appearing in the cohomology of $B_s^b$ be parametrized by $t$?

Unresolved formulation. A precise parametrization statement requires the definition and coefficient ring of $H_s^{(1)}$, the construction of $B_s^b$, the cohomology theory and coefficients, the relevant degrees, and the meaning of a module appearing in cohomology. It also requires the domain of $t$, the permitted values and dependence of $s,b$, the equivalence relation on modules, and the intended meaning of parametrization. In particular, the supplied text does not specify whether parametrization means a bijection with isomorphism classes, a surjective indexing, or another correspondence. No such interpretation is imposed here.

Supplied qualification. The dated curation labels the problem partially solved and states that the report proves realization with multiplicity one for simple supersingular modules in dimensions $n\leq 3$, while no general parametrization by $t$ is established. The record does not define $n$, state the hypotheses of that realization result, or explain its precise relationship to the question above. This is a supplied literature assessment, not an independently verified theorem statement or a new assessment of the problem's status.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the supersingular $H_s^{(1)}$-modules occurring in the cohomology of $B_s^b$ can be indexed by $t$. The supplied record leaves the objects, parameter domain, and required correspondence undefined, so a fully precise formulation requires additional source context. Its dated curation reports partial progress for simple supersingular modules in dimensions $n\leq 3$.
