Attempt: a diagnostic task collection robust to response errors

The source does not define a good task. I tested one narrow criterion: $M$ declared learner profiles each predict a distinct binary response word to $n$ tasks, and at most $t$ answers may be corrupted arbitrarily. The purpose is exact profile discrimination, not teaching effectiveness. Let $C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ be the set of predicted words and $d_H$ Hamming distance.
Proposition: Every profile can be recovered uniquely after at most $t$ corruptions if and only if distinct words in $C$ have distance at least $2t+1$. Necessarily \[ M\sum_{j=0}^t\binom nj\le2^n \] when $0\le t\le n$.
Proof: If two words have distance $d\le2t$, form a received word by choosing approximately half of their differing coordinates from each. It is at distance at most $\lceil d/2\rceil\le t$ from both, so unique recovery is impossible. Conversely, if a received word were within $t$ of two codewords, the triangle inequality would put them within $2t$ of each other, contradicting the stated separation. Thus the radius-$t$ Hamming balls are disjoint exactly when unique recovery is possible. Each contains $\sum_{j=0}^t\binom nj$ words, obtained by choosing which $j$ coordinates to flip. Counting the disjoint balls inside the $2^n$ possible words proves the bound. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

For four profiles and one possible error, four tasks are insufficient because $4(1+4)=20>16$. Five tasks suffice in the abstract code model: use $00000,11100,10011,01111$, whose six pairwise distances are 3, 3, 4, 4, 3, 3. Exhaustive one-bit corruption checking gives unique recovery. These response patterns may not be realizable by useful mathematical tasks; profiles and errors also require validation. The failed inference is that distinct ideal answers alone guarantee robust diagnosis. This known coding argument is not a universal principle of good pedagogy or a claim of original progress.
