Math-4423

Simplicity of Differential-Operator Rings on Invariant Rings

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Under the usual algebraic interpretation of the supplied terminology, let $G$ be a linearly reductive affine algebraic group over ${\mathbb{C}}$: every finite-dimensional rational representation of $G$ is completely reducible. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional rational representation of $G$, and let $R=\operatorname{Sym}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V^*)$ be its polynomial coordinate ring with the induced linear action. Set \[ A=R^G=\{f\in R:g\cdot f=f\text{ for every }g\in G({\mathbb{C}})\}. \] For $a\in A$, let $\mu_a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A)$ denote multiplication by $a$. Define the differential operators on $A$ recursively by \[ D^{-1}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A)=\{0\},\qquad D^m_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A)=\{\delta\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A):[\delta,\mu_a]\in D^{m-1}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A)\text{ for every }a\in A\}\quad(m\geq0), \] where $[\delta,\mu_a]=\delta\circ\mu_a-\mu_a\circ\delta$, and set \[ D_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A)=\bigcup_{m\geq0}D^m_{{\mathbb{C}}}(A), \] with multiplication given by composition. A ring is simple if it is nonzero and has no two-sided ideals other than the zero ideal and the whole ring.

The question is whether, for every such $G$ and every such linear action, \[ D_{{\mathbb{C}}}(R^G)\text{ is simple}. \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify that the group is affine algebraic, the action is rational, or the polynomial ring has finitely many variables. These conventions make the displayed formulation precise, but their agreement with the intended source scope requires review; no broader interpretation is asserted here.

Short English statement

For every linear action of a linearly reductive complex group on a polynomial ring, must the ring of complex-linear differential operators on its invariant ring have no nonzero proper two-sided ideals? The precise formulation above uses the usual finite-dimensional rational algebraic interpretation, whose scope requires source confirmation.
