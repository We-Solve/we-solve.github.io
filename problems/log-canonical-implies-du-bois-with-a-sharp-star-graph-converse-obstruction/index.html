Math-229

Log canonical implies Du Bois, with a sharp star-graph converse obstruction

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the intended characteristic-zero setting, a normal ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Gorenstein variety $X$ is log canonical if every prime divisor $E$ over $X$ has log discrepancy $A_X(E)\ge0$, where $A_X(E)=1+\operatorname{coeff}_E(K_Y-f^*K_X)$ on a birational model $f:Y\to X$. It is klt if all these log discrepancies are positive. A reduced complex variety is Du Bois if the natural map $\mathcal O_X\to\underline\Omega_X^0$ to the degree-zero Deligne--Du Bois complex is a quasi-isomorphism. The original statement does not name the ground field; this characteristic-zero/complex framework must be part of its interpretation rather than an asserted arbitrary-characteristic theorem.

Original question (Schwede). If $X$ has log-canonical singularities, must $X$ have Du Bois singularities? The source directs readers to its labels [5] for historical discussion and progress, [4], introduction, for discussion, and [2], Chapter 12. The associated bibliography is not included.

Attached research-aid account, requiring verification. The aid attributes to Kollár and Kovács an affirmative result for characteristic-zero log-canonical pairs $(X,\Delta)$: the underlying $X$ is Du Bois, and every reduced closed union of log-canonical centers is Du Bois. It attributes extensions to quasi-log-canonical pairs and semi-log-canonical strata to Fujino and Liu, without exact statements or references. Its mentioned "formal Du Bois-pair refinement" is not specified in the summary and cannot be reconstructed uniquely as a new theorem here.

The aid additionally reports the following sharp worked family. For every pair of integers \[b\ge r\ge1,\qquad b\ge2,\] there are algebraic rational surface germs $X_{b,r}$ with resolution graph a star: the central exceptional rational curve $E_0$ has self-intersection $-b$, the $r$ exceptional rational leaves $E_1,\ldots,E_r$ have self-intersection $-2$, each leaf meets $E_0$ once, and distinct leaves do not meet. The report claims that every member is Cohen--Macaulay, Du Bois, and ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Gorenstein, and that the central log discrepancy is \[A_{X_{b,r}}(E_0)=\frac{4-r}{2b-r}.\] It gives the classification within this family: \[r\le3:\ \mathrm{klt};\qquad r=4:\ \mathrm{lc\ but\ not\ klt};\qquad r\ge5:\ \mathrm{not\ lc}.\] Here rational surface singularity means that the first higher direct image of $\mathcal O_Y$ under a resolution vanishes. This family is reported as a converse obstruction: Du Bois, Cohen--Macaulay and ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Gorenstein together do not force log canonicity when $r\ge5$. A graph and the central discrepancy alone are not a supplied algebraic construction or a verification of discrepancies on all divisors. The coefficient field, construction, and proof of the full asserted classification are absent from this record.

Short English statement

Schwede asks whether log-canonical singularities are Du Bois. The aid attributes the affirmative characteristic-zero theorem to Kollár and Kovács, with later extensions, and reports a rational surface family showing that the converse fails. All parameters, the central discrepancy, and the klt/lc transition are retained as unreviewed source claims.
