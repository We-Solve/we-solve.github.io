Math-5095

An explicit geometric Picard-rank-one quintic over F_2

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth projective surface $X/{\mathbb{F}}_2$, write $\rho_{\mathrm{geom}}(X)=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{NS}(X_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_2})$. The arithmetic Picard number concerns classes over ${\mathbb{F}}_2$, or equivalently their invariant rational Néron--Severi space; it need not equal the geometric number in general. Voloch's question is to exhibit a homogeneous quintic $f\in{\mathbb{F}}_2[x,y,z,w]$ such that $X=V(f)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3_{{\mathbb{F}}_2}$ is smooth and has Picard number one. The supplied solution uses the stronger geometric rank-one condition, which also gives arithmetic rank one because the hyperplane class is defined over ${\mathbb{F}}_2$.

The source's later report attributes to Abbott, Kedlaya and Roe the explicit polynomial \[ \begin{aligned} f={}&x^5+x^3yz+x^2y^2w+x^2yz^2+x^2z^3+xz^2w^2\\ &+y^5+y^3zw+y^2zw^2+yzw^3+z^5+z^2w^3+w^5. \end{aligned} \] It reports that $V(f)$ is smooth and $\rho_{\mathrm{geom}}(V(f))=1$, with the Picard conclusion supplied by their certified finite-precision Frobenius-corank computation. The report labels this a known solution, not a newly proved Picard-rank computation in the present draft.

For its separate candidate smoothness certificate let $R={\mathbb{F}}_2[x,y,z,w]$, let $R_m$ be the space of homogeneous degree-$m$ polynomials, and define the Jacobian Macaulay map \[ M_m:R_{m-4}^{\,4}\longrightarrow R_m,\quad (a_x,a_y,a_z,a_w)\longmapsto a_xf_x+a_yf_y+a_zf_z+a_wf_w. \] The supplied exact elimination results for this $f$ are \[ \operatorname{rank}M_{13}=560\quad(560\text{-by-}880\text{ matrix}),\qquad \operatorname{rank}M_{12}=454\quad(455\text{-by-}660\text{ matrix}). \] The candidate proposition further asserts that degree thirteen is the sharp uniform Jacobian-surjectivity threshold for smooth quintic surfaces in ${\mathbb{P}}^3$ in characteristic not dividing five. Its cited complete-intersection Hilbert series is \[ \frac{(1-t^4)^4}{(1-t)^4}=(1+t+t^2+t^3)^4, \] with top degree twelve. The matrices, elimination certificate and full proof are absent from the supplied record, so these are reported verification data requiring review.

The historical remarks connect the example to error-correcting codes, state second Betti number $53$, and describe zeta-function point counts to roughly ${\mathbb{F}}_{2^{28}}$ as costly. Their reference to "Tate's theorem" supplies no precise theorem or hypotheses, and is not used here to assert an unconditional equality of analytic and algebraic ranks. Raskind mentions Shioda's characteristic-zero rank-one examples as possible sources; Voloch says this had been tried once. These are historical suggestions, not a general preservation claim under reduction.

Short English statement

Find a smooth quintic surface over ${\mathbb{F}}_2$ with Picard number one. The supplied record reports a known explicit Abbott--Kedlaya--Roe example with geometric Picard number one, plus a separate exact Jacobian-matrix smoothness certificate. These source claims remain subject to mathematical review of the cited computation.
