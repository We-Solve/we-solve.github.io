Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.37

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. For subsets $A,B\subseteq G$, define their product set by \[ AB=\{xy:x\in A,\ y\in B\}. \] Interpret a factorization $|G|=ab$ as a factorization into positive integers $a,b$.

The question is whether \[ \forall\,G\text{ finite group}\quad \forall\,a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},\qquad |G|=ab\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists\,A,B\subseteq G\quad \left(|A|=a\ \land\ |B|=b\ \land\ AB=G\right). \] The sets $A$ and $B$ are arbitrary subsets; no subgroup condition is imposed.

The supplied Comment of 2025 reports that any minimal counterexample $G$ must be a simple group without prime-index subgroups, meaning that there is no subgroup $H\leq G$ for which $[G:H]$ is prime. The source excerpt does not specify the ordering used for minimality, so this contextual assertion is retained with that qualification rather than given a more specific interpretation. The source also refers to Archive problem 19.35, whose statement is not supplied.

Short English statement

Does every factorization of the order of a finite group into two positive integers arise as the sizes of two subsets whose product is the whole group? Proposed by M. H. Hooshmand in 2022. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that status and the accompanying 2025 comment have not been independently verified.
