Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.33

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the language of groups, with multiplication, inversion and identity element. Let \[ G=\langle a,b\mid a^{-1}ba=b^{-1}\rangle. \] A group quasi-identity is a universally quantified implication \[ \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_k\;\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}u_i(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=v_i(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\;\Longrightarrow\;u(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=v(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\right), \] where $k,m$ are nonnegative integers and all displayed terms are group terms; the empty conjunction is interpreted as true. Define the quasivariety generated by $G$ by \[ \mathcal Q(G)=\{H\mid H\text{ is a group satisfying every group quasi-identity valid in }G\}. \] Question (A. I. Budkin, Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.33). Does there exist an integer $n\geq 1$ and a set $\Sigma$ of group quasi-identities, each using only variables from $\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$, such that for every group $H$, \[ H\in\mathcal Q(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\models\Sigma? \] The set $\Sigma$ may be infinite: the requested finiteness is a uniform bound on the number of variables, not a finite number of axioms. No finiteness or finite generation assumption is imposed on $H$.

The supplied statement reports that the answer is known for all other groups with one defining relation, but supplies no supporting references or further scope qualifications for that assertion. The registry labels this problem open. Its background also records an explicitly unverified AI claim of a negative answer; that claim is not adopted here as a theorem or as a verified resolution.

Short English statement

For the group $\langle a,b\mid a^{-1}ba=b^{-1}\rangle$, ask whether its generated quasivariety can be axiomatized by possibly infinitely many quasi-identities with a common finite bound on their numbers of variables. The supplied open label and the explicitly unverified claim of a negative answer require separate treatment in mathematical review.
