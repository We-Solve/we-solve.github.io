Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

An $M\times N$ matrix $A$ has the $(s,\delta)$ restricted isometry property (RIP) if $(1-\delta)\lVert x\rVert_2^2\leq\lVert Ax\rVert_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\lVert x\rVert_2^2$ for every vector with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates. Its nullspace is $\ker A=\{x:Ax=0\}$. A nonzero $s$-sparse null vector is incompatible with RIP for $\delta<1$. A certifier must be sound for its positive certificates, while the probability of producing a certificate is evaluated over the source Gaussian matrix law. The Gaussian scaling, matrix encoding and time model must be specified for a complexity bound.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let $N = 2M$ . For which $s$ is there a polynomial time algorithm that is guaranteed to, with high probability, certify that a gaussian matrix $A$ is $\left(s,\frac13\right)$ -RIP? In particular, a $\left(s,\frac13\right)$ -RIP matrix has to not have $s$ sparse vectors in its nullspace. This motivates a second question: Let $N = 2M$ . for which $s$ is there a polynomial time algorithm that is guaranteed to, with high probability, certify that a gaussian matrix $A$ does not have $s$ -sparse vectors in its nullspace?

Attempt and results

Fix $N=2M$ and real entries $A_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,1/M)$ independently. Arithmetic on exact real inputs is an idealization; a practical implementation must use rational interval bounds on every tested inner product. Randomness belongs to the input, and the acceptance test below is sound on every matrix. Both RIP and absence of nonzero sparse null vectors are retained as targets.
A deterministic certificate.
Let $\gamma=\max_j|\|a_j\|^2-1|$ and $\mu=\max_{i\ne j}|a_i^Ta_j|$, where $a_j$ are columns. If $\gamma+(s-1)\mu\le1/3$, accept. For every vector supported on $t\le s$ columns, expansion of its quadratic form gives \[ |x^T(A^TA-I)x|\le\gamma\|x\|^2+ \mu\big((\sum_i|x_i|)^2-\|x\|^2\big) \le[\gamma+(s-1)\mu]\|x\|^2. \] This proves soundness, simultaneously on every support. Inner products can be computed in $O(MN^2)$ arithmetic operations. Accepted matrices have no nonzero $s$-sparse null vector because $\|Ax\|^2\ge(2/3)\|x\|^2$.
Success probability, with constants.
For independent standard Gaussians $g_1,\ldots,g_M$ and $0<t<1$, \[ \Pr\left(\left|M^{-1}\sum g_i^2-1\right|\ge t\right) \le2e^{-Mt^2/8}. \] Here is a direct derivation. The Gaussian integral gives $\mathbb E e^{\lambda(g^2-1)}=e^{-\lambda}(1-2\lambda)^{-1/2}$. For $|\lambda|\le1/4$, its logarithm, expanded into its absolutely convergent power series, has absolute upper bound $\sum_{k\ge2}2^{k-1}|\lambda|^k/k\le2\lambda^2$. Independence and Markov's inequality with $\lambda=\pm t/4$ give each tail at most $e^{-Mt^2/8}$. Apply this to each column and to each of $(a_i+a_j)/\sqrt2$ and $(a_i-a_j)/\sqrt2$. Every such vector has the same normalized Gaussian marginal law; independence between these tests is unnecessary. There are $N+2\binom N2=N^2$ tests. On their common good event, $\gamma\le t$ and, by polarization, $|a_i^Ta_j|\le t$. For $0<\eta<1$ put \[ t=\sqrt{\frac{8\log(2N^2/\eta)}M}. \] Whenever $t<1$ and integer $s\ge1$ satisfies $st\le1/3$, the certifier accepts with probability at least $1-\eta$. This rederives the familiar square-root sparsity scale with its logarithmic loss.
Attempted extrapolation and its first gap.
Almost-sure nonsingularity of every $M$-column Gaussian submatrix is not an efficient certificate: it concerns the input distribution, while soundness requires checking the actual matrix. Enumerating all supports has a number of cases that grows exponentially when $s$ is proportional to $M$. The proof above only bounds each Gram entry separately; summing magnitudes loses cancellation. No polynomial-time bound on every sparse quadratic form beyond this certificate is obtained.
