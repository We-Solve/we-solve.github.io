Open Problems in Mathematical Logic — Problem 19

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in Cantor space $2^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ with the fair-coin product measure $\mu$. Under the usual convention, a real $X\in 2^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ is Martin--Löf random relative to an oracle $A$ if, for every uniformly $A$-effectively open sequence $(U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ satisfying $\mu(U_n)\leq 2^{-n}$, one has $X\notin\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}U_n$. Write $\operatorname{MLR}^{A}$ for this class and $\operatorname{MLR}$ for randomness relative to a computable oracle. The unqualified randomness in the supplied question is interpreted as this unrelativized randomness.

For reals $A,B$, write $A\leq_T B$ when $A$ is computable from $B$, and use the standard LR-reducibility convention \[ A\leq_{\mathrm{LR}}B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{MLR}^{B}\subseteq\operatorname{MLR}^{A}. \] For $D\in\{T,\mathrm{LR}\}$, define $A\equiv_D B$ by mutual $D$-reducibility, and let $\deg_D(A)$ be the resulting equivalence class. The induced order on degrees is denoted by $\leq_D$, with $\mathbf a<_D\mathbf b$ meaning $\mathbf a\leq_D\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf a\ne\mathbf b$. A degree contains a Martin--Löf random real if some representative belongs to $\operatorname{MLR}$.

Consider the following interval property $P_D$: \[ \begin{aligned} \forall\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\in\mathcal D_D,\quad &\left[\mathbf a<_D\mathbf b<_D\mathbf c \ \land\ \exists X,Y\in\operatorname{MLR}\, (X\ne Y\ \land\ \deg_D(X)=\mathbf a \ \land\ \deg_D(Y)=\mathbf c)\right]\\ &\qquad\Longrightarrow \exists Z\in\operatorname{MLR}\;(\deg_D(Z)=\mathbf b), \end{aligned} \] where $\mathcal D_D$ is the set of $D$-degrees of reals in $2^{{\mathbb{N}}}$. The source phrase "different Martin--Löf randoms" is interpreted here as distinct random representatives; distinct endpoint degrees already force those representatives to differ.

The supplied source asserts that $P_T$ is false. Its follow-up asks whether "this" holds for a larger degree notion, specifically LR-degrees. Under the definitions above, the mathematical issue is to determine whether $P_{\mathrm{LR}}$ holds. Equivalently, determine whether there exist LR-degrees $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c$ such that \[ \mathbf a<_{\mathrm{LR}}\mathbf b<_{\mathrm{LR}}\mathbf c, \qquad \mathbf a\cap\operatorname{MLR}\ne\varnothing, \qquad \mathbf c\cap\operatorname{MLR}\ne\varnothing, \qquad \mathbf b\cap\operatorname{MLR}=\varnothing. \] Existence of such a triple would extend the source's negative answer for Turing degrees to LR-degrees; nonexistence would establish $P_{\mathrm{LR}}$. The antecedent of "this" is unresolved in the supplied wording, so no intended affirmative or negative answer is imposed. The source does not specify a formal claim for degree notions other than the LR example.

Short English statement

The source states that two comparable Turing degrees containing distinct Martin--Löf random reals can have an intermediate degree containing no such real. It then asks about LR-degrees. The draft makes the LR question explicit as deciding whether such an intermediate LR-degree can exist, while preserving the ambiguity in what the source means by "this." The supplied open label and the asserted Turing-degree answer have not been independently verified.
