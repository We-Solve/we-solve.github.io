Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.95

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$, where $\Omega$ is a finite set. A partition $\rho$ of $\Omega$ is a collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty blocks whose union is $\Omega$. A transversal of $\rho$ is a subset meeting each block in exactly one point. For $T\subseteq\Omega$, write $T^g=\{t^g:t\in T\}$. The partition $\rho$ is $G$-regular if \[ \exists T\subseteq\Omega\quad\forall g\in G\quad\forall B\in\rho,\qquad |T^g\cap B|=1. \] The two trivial partitions are the partition into singletons and the partition with just one block. Thus a non-trivial proper partition has $1<|\rho|<|\Omega|$. The group $G$ is synchronizing if $|\Omega|>2$ and there is no $G$-regular partition satisfying these inequalities. Non-synchronizing means that this definition fails; when $|\Omega|>2$, this is equivalent to the existence of such a partition.

(a) Determine whether the following groups are synchronizing in the primitive affine actions intended by the source: \[ 2^p.\mathrm{PSL}(2,2p+1), \qquad p\text{ and }2p+1\text{ prime},\quad p\equiv3\pmod4,\quad p>23, \] and \[ 2^{101}.He. \] Here the notation $2^d$ denotes an elementary abelian group of order $2^d$, identified with the additive group of $V={\mathbb{F}}_2^d$. To specify a primitive affine action with linear group $H$, one needs a faithful irreducible representation $\alpha:H\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)$; the associated affine permutation group consists of the maps \[ x\longmapsto v+\alpha(h)x\qquad(v\in V,\ h\in H). \] The intended pairs $(d,H)$ are $(p,\mathrm{PSL}(2,2p+1))$, subject to the displayed restrictions, and $(101,He)$. The supplied record does not specify the representations or embeddings. Consequently, these are questions about the intended actions, whose precise identification remains unresolved; the record does not justify replacing them by questions quantified over every faithful irreducible representation. The notation $2^{101}.He$, including its exponent, is retained exactly as supplied.

(b) For each finite simple group $S$, let \[ G_S=\{f_{g,h}:g,h\in S\}\leq\operatorname{Sym}(S), \qquad f_{g,h}(x)=g^{-1}xh. \] Classify the finite simple groups $S$ for which this action of $S\times S$ is non-synchronizing. Equivalently, for $|S|>2$, classify those $S$ for which there exist a partition $\rho$ of the underlying set of $S$ and a subset $T\subseteq S$ such that \[ 1<|\rho|<|S|, \qquad \forall g,h\in S\quad\forall B\in\rho,\qquad \left|g^{-1}Th\cap B\right|=1, \] where $g^{-1}Th=\{g^{-1}th:t\in T\}$. The source says finite simple groups without an explicit nonabelian restriction. Under that literal scope, the group of order $2$ is non-synchronizing because the degree condition fails. The notation $G_S$ denotes the induced permutation group, so no faithfulness assumption on the abstract $S\times S$-action is added.

Short English statement

P. M. Neumann asks whether two specified families of primitive affine permutation groups are synchronizing, and which finite simple groups yield a non-synchronizing left-right action on themselves. Synchronization excludes partitions admitting a subset whose every group translate is a transversal. The affine representations needed to identify the actions in part (a) are not supplied. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
