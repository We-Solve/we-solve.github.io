Bohr-Radius Characterization of $\ell^1$

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns a Banach sequence space $X$. Write $X_n$ for its first $n$ coordinate space and $B_{X_n}$ for its open unit ball. The exact convention for a Banach sequence space and the construction of $X_n$ are not specified in the supplied record. Under the inherited coordinate-subspace interpretation, if the coordinate vectors $e_j$ belong to $X$, then \[ X_n=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\},\qquad \|(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\|_{X_n} =\|(z_1,\ldots,z_n,0,\ldots)\|_X, \] with $B_{X_n}=\{z\in X_n:\|z\|_{X_n}<1\}$.

For clarity, a usual complex holomorphic interpretation of the Bohr radius is the following. Let $H^\infty(B_{X_n})$ be the bounded holomorphic functions on $B_{X_n}$, with \[ \|f\|_\infty=\sup_{z\in B_{X_n}}|f(z)|. \] For $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^n$, put $z^\alpha=\prod_{j=1}^n z_j^{\alpha_j}$, and let $a_\alpha(f)$ be the Taylor coefficients of $f$ at the origin. Under this convention, \[ K(B_{X_n})= \sup\left\{r\in[0,1]: \begin{array}{l} \displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^n} |a_\alpha(f)z^\alpha|\leq\|f\|_\infty\\[2pt] \text{for every }f\in H^\infty(B_{X_n}) \text{ and every }z\in rB_{X_n} \end{array}\right\}, \] where $rB_{X_n}=\{rw:w\in B_{X_n}\}$, including $0B_{X_n}=\{0\}$. This definition makes the customary interpretation explicit; the supplied record does not itself specify the scalar field or the defining Bohr inequality, so its agreement with the source convention requires review.

In the complex interpretation, define \[ \ell^1=\left\{x=(x_j)_{j\geq1}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\{1,2,\ldots\}}: \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_j|<\infty\right\}. \] The supplied mathematical question is whether, for every Banach sequence space $X$ in the intended source class, \[ \inf_{n\geq1}K(B_{X_n})>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X=\ell^1\ \text{as sets}. \] The hypothesis means that there exists $\delta>0$ such that $K(B_{X_n})\geq\delta$ for every integer $n\geq1$. The conclusion asks for equality of the underlying sequence sets, not equality of norms. The intended scalar field, sequence-space axioms, coordinate-space construction, and Bohr-radius convention remain source qualifications to be checked; no additional symmetry, solidity, or density hypothesis is asserted here.

Short English statement

Must every Banach sequence space whose finite coordinate unit balls have Bohr radii bounded below by one positive constant consist exactly of the absolutely summable sequences? Equality is requested only as sets. The supplied record labels this question open; the precise sequence-space and Bohr-radius conventions require review.
