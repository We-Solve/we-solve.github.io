Sections and Rational Points of Hyperbolic Curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k/{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a finitely generated field extension, let $\bar k$ be an algebraic closure, and put $G_k=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)$. We use the conventional interpretation of a hyperbolic curve as a smooth, geometrically connected curve $Y/k$ whose smooth proper compactification $\overline Y$ satisfies \[ 2g(\overline Y)-2+\#(\overline Y_{\bar k}\setminus Y_{\bar k})>0. \] In particular, a proper hyperbolic curve has genus at least $2$. These conventions make explicit hypotheses implicit in the supplied terminology.

For a geometric base point $\bar y$, the étale fundamental group has an exact sequence \[ 1\longrightarrow\pi_1(Y_{\bar k},\bar y)\longrightarrow\pi_1(Y,\bar y)\xrightarrow{p_Y}G_k\longrightarrow1. \] A section is a continuous homomorphism $s:G_k\to\pi_1(Y,\bar y)$ with $p_Y\circ s=\operatorname{id}_{G_k}$. Write $\operatorname{Sec}(Y/k)$ for sections modulo conjugation by $\pi_1(Y_{\bar k},\bar y)$. A point $y\in Y(k)$ induces a section class $[s_y]$, giving a map \[ \kappa_Y:Y(k)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sec}(Y/k). \] The use of conjugacy classes is necessary to make the point-induced section independent of choices of paths and base points; the supplied record does not explicitly specify this convention.

Proper-curve statement (section conjecture). For every finitely generated extension $k/{\mathbb{Q}}$ and every proper hyperbolic curve $X/k$, is $\kappa_X$ bijective? Equivalently, does every continuous section $s$ admit a unique $x\in X(k)$ such that $[s]=\kappa_X(x)$?

Nonproper statement and unresolved qualification. The source also asks whether, for every smooth nonproper hyperbolic curve $U/k$ with compactification $X$, every section of $p_U$ is induced by a unique point of $X(k)$. Here we interpret $X$ as the smooth proper compactification and write $j:U\hookrightarrow X$. For $x\in U(k)$, the point induces a section class of $U$ as above. For $x\in X(k)\setminus U(k)$, there is no point morphism $\operatorname{Spec}k\to U$, so the phrase "induced by" requires an additional definition at the boundary.

One precise possible interpretation, using the induced map $j_*:\operatorname{Sec}(U/k)\to\operatorname{Sec}(X/k)$, is \[ \forall\sigma\in\operatorname{Sec}(U/k),\quad \exists!\,x\in X(k)\quad j_*(\sigma)=\kappa_X(x). \] This is explicitly a possible interpretation, not an asserted equivalent reformulation of the supplied nonproper claim. It only identifies the image section on the compactification. If the source intends a stronger assertion involving sections associated with boundary decomposition or inertia groups, the required definition and conditions are not supplied. Also, the compactification of a nonproper hyperbolic curve need not itself be hyperbolic, so the proper-curve conjecture above does not by itself cover every such $X$. The intended meaning of the nonproper assertion therefore remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The proper section conjecture asks whether rational points of every proper hyperbolic curve over a finitely generated extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ correspond bijectively to continuous Galois sections modulo geometric conjugacy. The source also includes nonproper curves and rational points of their compactifications, but does not define how a boundary point induces a section. A possible formulation using the compactification map is displayed with this limitation explicitly retained.
