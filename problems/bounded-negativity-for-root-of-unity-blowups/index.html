Math-7212

Bounded Negativity for Root of Unity Blowups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $m\geq 1$ be an integer. The source does not specify a ground field. Conditional on fixing a field $k$ containing an element $\varepsilon\in k^{\times}$ of multiplicative order exactly $m$, define \[ Z(m)=\left\{(1:\varepsilon^{\alpha}:\varepsilon^{\beta})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{k}:1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m\right\}, \qquad X(m)=\operatorname{Bl}_{Z(m)}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{k}). \] Here $Z(m)$ is given its reduced structure and consists of $m^{2}$ distinct points; the blowup is at these points. The existence of $\varepsilon$ requires that the characteristic of $k$, if positive, does not divide $m$.

To make the question precise, adopt the usual bounded-negativity convention that curves are reduced, irreducible curves on the smooth projective surface $X(m)$, and let $C^{2}$ denote the self-intersection number of such a curve. Define \[ b(X(m))=\sup\left(\{0\}\cup\{-C^{2}:C\subset X(m)\text{ is a reduced, irreducible curve}\}\right) \in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}\cup\{+\infty\}. \] Negativity is bounded precisely when there exists $B\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}$ such that \[ \forall\, C\subset X(m)\text{ reduced and irreducible},\qquad C^{2}\geq-B. \] When finite, $b(X(m))$ is the least such $B$.

Question. For the root-of-unity blowups $X(m)$, is $b(X(m))$ finite for each positive integer $m$, and, whenever it is finite, what is its exact value? The bound may depend on $m$; the source does not explicitly ask for one bound independent of $m$.

Unresolved qualifications. The ground field, its characteristic, and whether curves are considered after passage to an algebraic closure are not specified in the supplied statement. The formulation above is conditional on a chosen field and uses curves over that field; it does not assert that the intended question ranges over all such fields. The reduced, irreducible curve convention and the normalization of $b(X(m))$ also require confirmation from the source.

Short English statement

Blow up the $m^{2}$ projective points whose last two coordinates are powers of a primitive $m$th root of unity. For each $m$, ask whether the self-intersection numbers of reduced, irreducible curves on the resulting surface have a lower bound, and determine the optimal bound. The ground field and the precise curve convention remain unspecified in the supplied source.
