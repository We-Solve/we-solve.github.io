Optimal Point Distributions on High-Dimensional Spheres

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For integers $n\geq2$ and $N\geq1$, let \[ S^{n-1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\|x\|=1\},\qquad \mathcal C_{N,n}=\{C\subset S^{n-1}:|C|=N\}. \] The source asks for the best way to distribute $N$ points on this sphere. "Best" is not defined in its one-sentence statement; finite energy minimization, maximal separation, covering, and discrepancy are different optimization problems. The case $N=1$ is degenerate for pair interactions; the usual nontrivial problem has $N\geq2$. No asymptotic limit in $N$ or $n$ is specified.

The supplied literature assessment interprets the question through spherical energy. For a chosen pair potential $f:(0,4]\to{\mathbb{R}}$, one can define \[ E_f(C)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset C}f(\|x-y\|^2),\qquad \text{and seek }C_*\in\mathcal C_{N,n}\text{ minimizing }E_f. \] The unordered-pair convention fixes the irrelevant factor of two. Distinct points avoid zero arguments, and the potential's regularity or blowup at zero controls possible limiting collisions. A packing formulation would instead maximize $\min_{x\ne y\in C}\|x-y\|$. Neither objective is silently asserted as the unique source meaning.

The cited universal-optimality perspective asks for configurations minimizing $E_f$ simultaneously for every completely monotone function of squared distance, meaning a smooth function with $(-1)^j f^{(j)}\geq0$ for all $j\geq0$ on the distance interval, with the needed endpoint convention. Such universal optimality is a stronger property of special configurations; it is not the assertion that one common optimizer exists for every $N,n$ and every unrelated criterion. Rotations and permutations do not change these energies, so classification should at least account for those symmetries if it is requested.

The supplied literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports many known optimal configurations, including sharp configurations and major lattice cases, while calling the general $N$-point spherical energy problem open. It gives no exhaustive parameter list or complete optimization criterion. This is dated partial-progress metadata rather than an independently verified classification or current literature survey.

Short English statement

How should $N$ points be placed on a unit sphere? A precise answer needs an objective, such as a chosen pair energy or maximal separation. The supplied assessment emphasizes energy and universal-optimality results for special configurations, while retaining the broad general problem.
