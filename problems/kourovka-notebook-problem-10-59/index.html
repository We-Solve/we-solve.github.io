Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.59

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $G$ be a group. For $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, write $g^{\varphi^i}=\varphi^i(g)$. An automorphism $\varphi$ of order exactly $p$ is called splitting if \[ \prod_{i=0}^{p-1}g^{\varphi^i}=1\qquad\text{for every }g\in G, \] where the factors are multiplied in increasing order of $i$. For each $g\in G$, define \[ H_g=\langle g,g^\varphi,\ldots,g^{\varphi^{p-1}}\rangle. \] For a group $H$, put $\gamma_1(H)=H$ and $\gamma_{n+1}(H)=[\gamma_n(H),H]$, where $[A,B]$ is generated by the commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$. The group $H$ is nilpotent if $\gamma_{c+1}(H)=\{1\}$ for some integer $c\geq1$. The group $G$ is locally nilpotent if every subgroup generated by a finite subset of $G$ is nilpotent.

The supplied source asks: if $G$ is a $p'$-group admitting a splitting automorphism $\varphi$ of order $p$, and \[ \forall g\in G\ \exists c_g\geq1:\quad \gamma_{c_g+1}(H_g)=\{1\}, \] must it follow that \[ \forall\text{ finite }S\subseteq G\ \exists c_S\geq1:\quad \gamma_{c_S+1}(\langle S\rangle)=\{1\}? \] No uniform bound on the nilpotency classes of the groups $H_g$ is assumed.

The source does not define $p'$-group. Under the periodic convention, this means that every element of $G$ has finite order relatively prime to $p$. A broader possible convention requires only that $G$ have no element of order $p$, allowing elements of infinite order. These give different domains for the displayed question. The intended convention requires source confirmation; the supplied record does not resolve it. In particular, no finiteness assumption on $G$ is supplied.

Short English statement

For a prime $p$, suppose a $p'$-group has a splitting automorphism of order $p$, and the subgroup generated by each individual element together with its automorphism orbit is nilpotent. Must every finitely generated subgroup be nilpotent? The source leaves the convention for $p'$-group unstated.
