Math-1034

Covariance Determinant Bounds for Polar Convex Bodies

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $n\geq 1$, a convex body in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ means a compact convex set with nonempty interior. For any such body $L$, let $|L|$ denote its $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure, and define its barycenter and covariance matrix with respect to normalized Lebesgue measure by \[ b_L=\frac{1}{|L|}\int_L x\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}(L)=\frac{1}{|L|}\int_L (x-b_L)(x-b_L)^{\mathsf T}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x. \] If $b_K=0$, then the origin lies in the interior of $K$, and its polar convex body is \[ K^\circ=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for every }x\in K\}. \] The covariance of $K^\circ$ is centered at its own barycenter $b_{K^\circ}$; no assumption that this barycenter vanishes is imposed.

The supplied determinant question is: does there exist a constant $C>0$, independent of both $n$ and $K$, such that for every integer $n\geq 1$ and every convex body $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ with $b_K=0$, \[ \det\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}(K)\operatorname{Cov}(K^\circ)\right) \leq \left(\frac{C}{n}\right)^{2n}? \] The supplied dated curation describes this determinant relation as established while labeling the overall record partially solved and referring to broader, only partially resolved trace-based duality relations. No precise trace-based question is supplied, so none is added here. These status descriptions are supplied assessments, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

Is there an absolute constant $C>0$ such that, in every dimension $n\geq 1$, the determinant of the product of the covariance matrices of a convex body with barycenter zero and its polar is at most $(C/n)^{2n}$? Each covariance uses the uniform probability measure and is centered at that body's own barycenter. The supplied curation calls this relation established, but that assessment requires independent verification.
