Math-3375

Minimal Sets and Lyapunov Positivity for Quasiperiodic Schrödinger Cocycles

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The record asks two questions about quasiperiodic Schrödinger cocycles. To explain a standard discrete model, let $V:\mathbb T^d\to{\mathbb{R}}$, let $\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$ satisfy rational independence of $1,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_d$, and write \[ (H_{\lambda,V,\theta}u)_j=u_{j+1}+u_{j-1} +\lambda V(\theta+j\omega)u_j,\qquad j\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] The associated transfer matrix is \[ A_{E,\lambda}(\theta)= \begin{pmatrix}E-\lambda V(\theta)&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \] With $A^{(n)}(\theta)=A(\theta+(n-1)\omega)\cdots A(\theta)$, its Lyapunov exponent is, under the usual assumptions ensuring the limit, \[ L_\lambda(E)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n \int_{\mathbb T^d}\log\|A_{E,\lambda}^{(n)}(\theta)\|\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\theta. \] This discrete formulation is explanatory: the source does not explicitly specify discrete versus continuous Schrödinger dynamics, base dimension, or its cocycle convention.
  1. Let $E_0$ be the lowest spectral energy in the intended model, and assume $L_\lambda(E_0)>0$. Under sufficient regularity of the potential and rational independence of the frequency, determine the structure of the cocycle's minimal set; in particular, can its fibers be Cantor sets? In the usual projective-cocycle interpretation, the map acts on $\mathbb T^d\times{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{P}}^1$ by \[ (\theta,[v])\longmapsto(\theta+\omega,[A_{E_0,\lambda}(\theta)v]). \] A minimal set is a nonempty compact invariant set containing no smaller nonempty compact invariant set; its fiber at $\theta$ is $\{[v]:(\theta,[v])\in K\}$. A Cantor fiber is a nonempty compact perfect totally disconnected fiber. The source does not identify a unique minimal set, its exact phase space, or whether Cantor fibers are asked for at one, every, or generic base point.
  2. When the potential is only finitely smooth, does sufficiently large coupling force positive Lyapunov exponent for every energy? Once the intended potential and frequency class is fixed, the natural quantifier pattern is \[ \exists\lambda_0>0\ \forall\lambda\ge\lambda_0\ \forall E\in{\mathbb{R}}: L_\lambda(E)>0. \] The source does not specify whether large negative coupling is included, the differentiability order, potential nondegeneracy assumptions, stronger arithmetic frequency conditions, or the dependence of $\lambda_0$. No positive lower bound uniform in $E$ is added to pointwise positivity for all energies.

The expressions "sufficient regularity" and "finitely smooth" do not determine admissible potential classes; in particular, they do not by themselves specify exclusions of constant or degenerate potentials. The questions must be read with missing report hypotheses still unresolved, rather than as a new universal theorem for every finitely smooth function. No analytic regularity or Diophantine hypothesis is silently imposed.

Short English statement

At the spectral bottom with positive Lyapunov exponent, the first question asks what minimal invariant sets look like and whether Cantor fibers occur. The second asks whether large coupling gives positivity at all energies for finitely smooth potentials. The precise model, regularity, and nondegeneracy hypotheses are missing.
