Open Problems on Central Simple Algebras — Problem 13

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a field $K$, let $\operatorname{Br}(K)$ denote its Brauer group. For a finite-dimensional central simple $K$-algebra $A$, its period $\operatorname{per}(A)$ is the order of its class in $\operatorname{Br}(K)$, and its index $\operatorname{ind}(A)$ is the degree of the central division algebra representing that class. To make explicit a convention that must be checked against the source, set \[ b(K)=\inf\left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}:\operatorname{ind}(A)\mid\operatorname{per}(A)^n\text{ for every finite-dimensional central simple }K\text{-algebra }A\right\}, \] with $b(K)=\infty$ if no such integer exists. The supplied excerpt does not define its notation $\operatorname{Br.\!dim}(K)$; in particular, it does not specify whether the period--index bound is imposed over $K$ alone, as in $b(K)$, or uniformly over its finite field extensions. This convention remains unresolved.

The requested construction is a notion of field dimension, on a class $\mathcal K$ of fields to be specified, satisfying, for every $F\in\mathcal K$, \[ \operatorname{Br.\!dim}(F)\leq \dim F-1, \qquad \dim(F(t))=\dim F+1, \] where $t$ is transcendental over $F$. To interpret both identities within the same domain of definition, $\mathcal K$ must also contain $F(t)$ whenever it contains $F$. The class $\mathcal K$, the value set of $\dim$, and the interpretation of the displayed arithmetic at any infinite values are not specified in the supplied record and are part of the unresolved formulation.

For an integer $i\geq 0$, a field $F$ is called a $C_i$ field if, for every integer $d\geq 1$, every integer $N>d^i$, and every homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$ in $N$ variables over $F$, the polynomial has a zero in $F^N\setminus\{0\}$. The source suggests assigning dimension $i$ to a $C_i$ field. Since the $C_i$ conditions are nested, this suggestion does not by itself define a unique dimension; using the least such $i$, and handling fields with no such $i$, would require additional specification.

As an obstruction to using cohomological dimension for this purpose, the supplied source attributes to Merkurjev an example of a field $F$ with cohomological dimension $2$ and $\operatorname{Br.\!dim}(F)=\infty$, citing $[\mathrm{M\!:\!simple}]$. This is retained as a source-reported example; the excerpt does not give the full reference or specify the cohomological-dimension convention.

Short English statement

Construct a notion of dimension for a specified class of fields that bounds Brauer dimension by one less than field dimension and increases by one upon adjoining a transcendental element. The source suggests using $C_i$ conditions and reports a Merkurjev example obstructing ordinary cohomological dimension. The field class, dimension values, Brauer-dimension convention, and precise meaning of the $C_i$ suggestion require clarification.
