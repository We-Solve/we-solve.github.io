Quadratically Enriched Tropical Curve Invariants

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$. The supplied record names two families of quadratically enriched tropical curve invariants, denoted by $N^{\mathbb A^1}_{u,v}$ and $N^{\mathbb A^1}_{\triangle,u,v}$. Its fragmentary original text refers to a corresponding toric surface and an ample divisor, but does not specify these geometric data or give definitions of the invariants. In particular, the base field, coefficient ring, admissible indices, curve classes, incidence conditions, and enrichment conventions are not supplied.

The stated conjecture is that, for every pair of integers $a,b\geq 0$, \[ N^{\mathbb A^1}_{a,a+b} = \sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{b+2k}{k} N^{\mathbb A^1}_{\triangle,a-k,b+2k}. \] Here the sum is indexed by nonnegative integers $k$, and \[ \binom{b+2k}{k}=\frac{(b+2k)!}{k!\,(b+k)!}. \] The task is to establish or disprove this identity with the intended geometric definitions and hypotheses.

Unresolved qualifications. For $k>a$, the first numerical index $a-k$ on the right is negative. The supplied record gives neither a definition at such indices nor a vanishing convention. Thus the displayed sum is retained exactly as supplied; replacing it by a sum over $0\leq k\leq a$ would require an additional justification or convention. The record also does not specify a finiteness or convergence interpretation for the sum, or the algebraic setting in which the integer coefficients act. These omissions prevent a fully specified mathematical statement without consulting the underlying definitions.

The extracted original text additionally says that an analogous conjecture for tropical refined invariants was formulated in its reference [5] and proved by Bousseau in its reference [2]. This is a supplied background claim about an analogous relation, not a supplied proof of the quadratically enriched identity displayed above.

Short English statement

The conjecture expresses a quadratically enriched tropical curve invariant as a binomially weighted sum of another family of such invariants, for every $a,b\geq 0$. The geometric definitions, coefficient setting, and treatment of negative indices are missing from the supplied record and require review.
