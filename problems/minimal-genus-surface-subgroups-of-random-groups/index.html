Math-6602

Minimal-Genus Surface Subgroups of Random Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied problem asks whether, for random groups at density $D<1/2$, the least genus of a homologically essential closed surface subgroup is $O(n/\log n)$, where $n$ is the relator length.

The following is a conditional precise formulation. Suppose that a random-group ensemble $(G_n)$ at a fixed admissible density $D<1/2$ has been specified. Adopt the conventions that surfaces are connected, closed and orientable, have genus at least two, and that homological essentiality uses integral group homology. Let $\Sigma_g$ denote such a surface of genus $g$. An injective homomorphism $\rho\colon\pi_1(\Sigma_g)\hookrightarrow G$ is homologically essential under these conventions if \[ \rho_*[\Sigma_g]\ne 0\quad\text{in }H_2(G;{\mathbb{Z}}), \] where $[\Sigma_g]\in H_2(\pi_1(\Sigma_g);{\mathbb{Z}})$ is the fundamental class under the identification of $\Sigma_g$ with a classifying space for its fundamental group. Define \[ m(G)=\min\left\{g\in{\mathbb{Z}}:g\ge2,\ \exists\rho\colon\pi_1(\Sigma_g)\hookrightarrow G\text{ with }\rho_*[\Sigma_g]\ne0\right\}, \] with $m(G)=+\infty$ if this set is empty.

Under these conventions, a possible precise interpretation of the question is: for every fixed admissible $D<1/2$ and every fixed choice of the remaining ensemble parameters, does there exist a constant $C>0$, independent of $n$, such that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left(m(G_n)\le C\frac{n}{\log n}\right)=1? \] The limit is taken along the relator lengths allowed by the chosen ensemble; $C$ may depend on $D$ and its other fixed parameters.

This formulation requires confirmation: the supplied record does not specify the random-group model, the number of generators, the probabilistic quantifier, the meaning of "simplest", the orientability or permitted genera of the surfaces, or the coefficient system and target homology used for essentiality. The displayed formulation records one explicit interpretation, rather than resolving those omissions. The stated question requests an upper bound; the supplied description of this as an optimal order does not supply a matching lower-bound assertion.

Short English statement

For random groups with density $D<1/2$, is the smallest genus of a homologically essential closed surface subgroup at most a constant times $n/\log n$, as the relator length $n$ grows? The ensemble, probability convention and precise meaning of homological essentiality require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
