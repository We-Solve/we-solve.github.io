Math-4078

Banach–Mazur Diameter of Nonsymmetric Convex Bodies

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer, and let $\mathcal K_n$ denote the class of compact convex subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ with nonempty interior. Under the standard affine interpretation of the supplied formula, define \[ d_{\mathrm{BM}}(K,L)=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 1:\ T_1(K)\subseteq T_2(L)\subseteq \lambda T_1(K),\quad T_1,T_2:{\mathbb{R}}^n\to{\mathbb{R}}^n\text{ invertible affine maps}\right\}. \] Here $\lambda T_1(K)=\{\lambda x:x\in T_1(K)\}$. The affine maps allow translations as well as invertible linear changes of coordinates. The affine Banach--Mazur compactum consists of the affine equivalence classes of these bodies; the distance above is expressed in its multiplicative convention.

Determine, as a function of $n$, the exact value of \[ D_n=\sup_{K,L\in\mathcal K_n}d_{\mathrm{BM}}(K,L), \] called the diameter in the supplied record, which describes this extremal value as a maximum.

There are unresolved source qualifications. The excerpt does not explicitly specify the admissible maps $T_1,T_2$; their invertible affine interpretation is supported by its description of comparison up to affine transformations. The formulation above includes all convex bodies without imposing central symmetry. The source's phrase "nonsymmetric convex bodies" may instead mean bodies that are strictly not centrally symmetric. Under that reading the domain would be \[ \mathcal K_n^{\mathrm{ns}}=\{K\in\mathcal K_n:\text{there is no }c\in{\mathbb{R}}^n\text{ with }K=2c-K\}, \] and the intended extremum and its attainment require clarification; in particular, this class is empty for $n=1$. The supplied record does not resolve these readings.

The background excerpt also discusses centrally symmetric bodies and attributes an ellipsoid comparison to John's theorem, labeled [3]. Its displayed dilation factor is corrupted and the bibliographic entry [3] is absent, so that comparison is not adopted as a precise additional assertion here.

Short English statement

For each dimension, determine the largest multiplicative Banach--Mazur distance between convex bodies when affine transformations are allowed. The intended meaning of "nonsymmetric" and the incomplete source formula require review. The supplied status is partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
