Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.117

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the first-order language of groups $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$. For a group $G$, its elementary theory is \[ \operatorname{Th}(G)=\{\varphi:\varphi\text{ is an }\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}\text{-sentence and }G\models\varphi\}. \] This theory is decidable if there exists an algorithm that, on input any $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}$-sentence $\varphi$, halts and correctly determines whether $G\models\varphi$.

For groups $A$ and $B$, their free product $A*B$ is the group equipped with canonical homomorphisms $i_A:A\to A*B$ and $i_B:B\to A*B$ such that, for every group $H$ and homomorphisms $f:A\to H$ and $g:B\to H$, there exists a unique homomorphism $h:A*B\to H$ satisfying $h\circ i_A=f$ and $h\circ i_B=g$.

Question (O. Kharlampovich; described in the source as a well-known problem). For every pair of groups $A,B$, does \[ \left(\operatorname{Th}(A)\text{ is decidable}\right)\ \land\ \left(\operatorname{Th}(B)\text{ is decidable}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Th}(A*B)\text{ is decidable} \] hold? No finiteness, generation, presentation, or nontriviality assumptions on the factors are stated in the supplied record.

Short English statement

If two arbitrary groups each have a decidable first-order theory in the language of groups, must their free product also have a decidable first-order theory?
