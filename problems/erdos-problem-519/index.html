FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #519; coefficient bounds for complex power sums, 2026-09-24. Let $z_1=1,z_2,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb C$ and $s_k=\sum_{i=1}^nz_i^k$. Prove that $\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|s_k|$ exceeds a positive absolute constant independent of $n$.

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

Atkinson established the requested absolute bound, initially with constant $1/6$; Erdős's 1961 original problem discussion records this result and earlier bounds of logarithmic size. The calculation below reconstructs an elementary logarithmic bound and completely solves the restricted case $|z_i|\geq1$ for every $i$. It does not reconstruct Atkinson's argument for arbitrary moduli.

OBJECTIVE AND ATTACK PLAN

Use the formal logarithm of $\prod_i(1-z_it)$ to control its coefficients from the first $n$ power sums. The root at $t=1$ forces a nontrivial sum of coefficients; a large constant term in the reciprocal-root polynomial gives a stronger obstruction when all moduli are at least one.

WORK

Put $M=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|s_k|$ and \[ Q(t)=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-z_it)=\sum_{j=0}^n b_jt^j, \qquad b_0=1. \] As an identity of formal power series, \[ Q(t)=\exp\left(-\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{s_k}{k}t^k\right). \] In particular, for $j\leq n$ its coefficient of $t^j$ depends only on $s_1,\ldots,s_j$. Expanding the exponential coefficient by coefficient and applying the triangle inequality yields \[ |b_j|\leq[t^j]\exp\left(M\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{t^k}{k}\right) \quad(0\leq j\leq n). \tag{1} \] This is a formal coefficient argument, so it makes no assumption that $|z_i|\leq1$ or that a power series converges at $t=1$.

A logarithmic bound for arbitrary complex numbers. Since $z_1=1$, $Q(1)=0$, hence $\sum_{j=1}^nb_j=-1$. The series on the right of $1$ has nonnegative coefficients and converges at $1$. With $H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n1/k$, \[ 1\leq\sum_{j=1}^n|b_j|\leq e^{MH_n}-1. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\displaystyle M\geq\frac{\log2}{H_n} \geq\frac{\log2}{1+\log n}.} \tag{2} \] This improves the elementary order-$1/n$ bound but still tends to zero.

The complete case of moduli at least one. In degree $n$, the majorant in $1$ has the same coefficient as \[ \exp\left(M\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{t^k}{k}\right) =(1-t)^{-M}. \] Thus \[ \prod_{i=1}^n|z_i|=|b_n| \leq\frac{M(M+1)\cdots(M+n-1)}{n!}. \tag{3} \] If every $|z_i|\geq1$, the left side is at least one. If $0\leq M<1$, each factor $(M+j)/(j+1)$ on the right is strictly below one, so their product is below one. This contradiction proves $M\geq1$ in the restricted case. It is sharp: for $n\geq2$, take all $(n+1)$st roots of unity except one root $\zeta\ne1$. The remaining $n$ numbers include $1$, and $s_k=-\zeta^k$ for $1\leq k\leq n$, so $M=1$. For $n=1$ equality is immediate.

Where the general obstacle lies. It suffices to prove the desired constant bound when all moduli are at most one and some number equals one. Indeed, choose $z_j$ of largest modulus $R\geq1$ and replace every $z_i$ by $w_i=z_i/z_j$. Then $w_j=1$, $|w_i|\leq1$, and $|s_k(z)|=R^k|s_k(w)|$. However, in this remaining regime the product in $3$ may be arbitrarily small or zero. The coefficient obstruction giving $M\geq1$ consequently no longer applies. Equation $2$ alone does not remove this dependence on $n$.

RESULT AND LIMITATIONS

Attempt status: unresolved. The general bound $2$ and the sharp bound $M\geq1$ when all $|z_i|\geq1$ are fully proved. A positive absolute constant for arbitrary complex numbers remains the missing step in this reconstruction; Atkinson's known resolution is acknowledged rather than asserted as a proof supplied here.
