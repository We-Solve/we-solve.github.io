Math-3661

Mass-Preserving Decomposition of Normal Currents

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A real $k$-current on ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ is a continuous linear functional on smooth compactly supported differential $k$-forms. Its mass is \[ M(T)=\sup\left\{|T(\omega)|:\omega\in C_c^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^d;\Lambda^k),\ \sup_x\|\omega(x)\|_*\leq 1\right\}, \] where $\|\cdot\|_*$ denotes the comass norm. For $k\geq1$, its boundary is defined by $\partial T(\eta)=T(\mathrm d\eta)$. For $k=0$, use the convention $\partial T=0$. A current is normal if both $M(T)$ and $M(\partial T)$ are finite; write $N_k({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ for this space. An integral current is an integer-multiplicity rectifiable current of finite mass whose boundary is also integer-multiplicity rectifiable and has finite mass; write $I_k({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ for these currents.

Decomposition question. For every normal current $T\in N_k({\mathbb{R}}^d)$, does there exist a positive measure space $(L,\mathcal A,\lambda)$ and a family $(R_\ell)_{\ell\in L}$ of integral currents in $I_k({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ such that \[ T=\int_L R_\ell\,\mathrm d\lambda(\ell),\qquad M(T)=\int_L M(R_\ell)\,\mathrm d\lambda(\ell),\qquad M(\partial T)=\int_L M(\partial R_\ell)\,\mathrm d\lambda(\ell)? \] Here the current-valued integral is interpreted weakly: for every smooth compactly supported $k$-form $\omega$, the function $\ell\mapsto R_\ell(\omega)$ is measurable and \[ T(\omega)=\int_L R_\ell(\omega)\,\mathrm d\lambda(\ell). \] The two mass functions are also required to be measurable. The stated mass identity makes each scalar integral above absolutely convergent.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The source does not specify the dimension range, coefficient convention, or meaning of the current-valued integral. The definitions above give the conventional real-current, integer-integral-current, weak-integral interpretation. The usual Euclidean dimension range is $d\geq1$ and $0\leq k\leq d$, but whether the source intends all these dimensions, including the endpoints, remains unspecified. No additional restriction on dimensions or on $(L,\mathcal A,\lambda)$ is supplied.

The supplied background describes a particular horizontal absolute-continuity question as answered positively by a later tangent-current theorem, while describing Morgan's broader normal-current decomposition and stronger distribution questions as not fully resolved. Those auxiliary questions and their hypotheses are not stated in the record, so no precise auxiliary assertion or implication for the displayed decomposition question can be extracted here. Short English statement

The question asks whether every normal current can be expressed as a measured family of integral currents while preserving both total mass and boundary mass exactly. The source leaves dimension and integration conventions unspecified. Its supplied dated assessment labels the topic partially solved, but does not supply enough detail to identify which cases of this decomposition question are settled.
