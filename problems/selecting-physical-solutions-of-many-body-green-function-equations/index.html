Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An interacting one-body Green function is a time-ordered or otherwise conventionally ordered two-point correlation function of creation and annihilation fields. The Dyson equation writes $G=G_0+G_0\Sigma G$, with convolution in the space, spin and time variables and self-energy $\Sigma$. A functional differential equation treats $G$ as a functional of an added external potential and differentiates with respect to that potential, thereby encoding higher correlations without assuming a known self-energy. A physical solution must satisfy the state, spectral, causality and boundary conditions appropriate to the chosen Green function, whereas a nonlinear algebraic or functional equation can also have extraneous branches. Strong coupling refers to interaction strengths for which expansion about the noninteracting branch need not be controlled.

Branch selection in a solvable algebraic diagnostic.
Consider the scalar closure $t^2G^2-zG+1=0$, with $t>0$ and complex $z$. Its two algebraic branches are \[ G_\pm(z)=\frac{z\pm\sqrt{z^2-4t^2}}{2t^2}. \] Choose the square root near infinity with expansion $z-2t^2/z+O(z^{-3})$. Then $G_-(z)=z^{-1}+O(z^{-3})$, while $G_+(z)=z/t^2-z^{-1}+O(z^{-3})$. A normalized scalar resolvent has leading coefficient $1/z$: for a probability measure of bounded support, expand $(z-x)^{-1}$ and integrate. Hence only the minus branch can meet that necessary physical condition near infinity.
Countercheck on phase-transition inference.
At $z=iR$, $R>0$, the continued root is $i\sqrt{R^2+4t^2}$, and $\operatorname{Im}G_-<0$, whereas $\operatorname{Im}G_+>0$. This agrees with the necessary sign of $\int(z-x)^{-1}d\mu(x)$. The branch points at $z=\pm2t$ are spectral-variable singularities of this algebraic model; they do not establish a thermodynamic phase transition in a many-body system.
