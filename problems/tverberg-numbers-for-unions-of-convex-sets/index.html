Tverberg Numbers for Unions of Convex Sets

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not define $s$-convex. Consequently, the following formulation is conditional on specifying the families $\mathcal C_s({\mathbb{R}}^d)$ of subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ that this term denotes. The title suggests a connection with unions of convex sets, but it does not supply a definition.

For a dimension $d$, a number $r$ of parts, and admissible parameters $s_1,\ldots,s_r$, define \[ \begin{aligned} f_r(d,s_1,\ldots,s_r)=\min\bigl\{N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}:{}&\ \text{for every }P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d\text{ with }|P|=N,\\ &\text{there exist }P_1,\ldots,P_r\subseteq P\text{ such that}\\ &P=\bigcup_{i=1}^r P_i,\qquad P_i\cap P_j=\varnothing\quad(i\ne j),\\ &\text{and for every }(C_1,\ldots,C_r)\in\prod_{i=1}^r\mathcal C_{s_i}({\mathbb{R}}^d),\\ &\qquad\left[(\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,r\})\ P_i\subseteq C_i\right] \Longrightarrow \bigcap_{i=1}^r C_i\ne\varnothing\bigr\}. \end{aligned} \] The partition is chosen before, and must work for every, qualifying family $(C_i)_{i=1}^r$. The question is to determine this least integer. If the displayed set of integers is empty, no such least integer exists; no alternative convention is supplied.

The displayed formulation follows the source's explicit disjoint-cover description and imposes no nonemptiness condition on the blocks $P_i$. Whether all blocks must be nonempty requires clarification. The admissible ranges of $d,r,s_1,\ldots,s_r$, including boundary cases, are also not stated. These omissions and the missing definition of $s$-convexity prevent an unconditional, self-contained numerical specification of the requested function.

As normalization checks, the supplied report identifies Radon's theorem with \[ f_2(d,1,1)=d+2 \] and Tverberg's theorem with \[ f_r(d,1,\ldots,1)=(r-1)(d+1)+1. \] These are reported source identifications, subject to the intended parameter ranges and definition of $1$-convexity.

The surrounding passage reports polynomial upper bounds, including \[ f_2(d,s,t)\leq O\!\left(dst\log(st+1)\right). \] A natural reconstruction of its damaged general-$r$ formula is \[ f_r(d,s_1,\ldots,s_r) \leq O\!\left(dr^2\log r\, \left(\prod_{i=1}^r s_i\right) \ln\!\left(1+\prod_{i=1}^r s_i\right)\right). \] Likewise, its flattened lower-bound expressions naturally read \[ f_2(2,s,s)\geq s^2, \qquad f_r(d,s,\ldots,s)\geq(d-2r+4)s^r \quad\text{for }d\geq2r-2. \] The reconstructed grouping and exponents require source review. The passage attributes the lower bounds to an unidentified follow-up reference labelled [3]; its identity and the bounds' full parameter hypotheses are absent. It also states that the mainly combinatorial proofs extend to abstract separable convexity spaces with bounded Radon numbers. That contextual extension is not a separately specified target here.

Short English statement

Determine the smallest number of points in ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ that guarantees a partition into $r$ parts such that every choice of qualifying $s_i$-convex supersets of those parts has a common point. The supplied record omits the definition of $s$-convexity and the full parameter and partition conventions, so the statement remains conditional pending mathematical review.
