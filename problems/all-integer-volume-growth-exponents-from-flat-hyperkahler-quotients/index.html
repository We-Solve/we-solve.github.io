Math-1490

All integer volume-growth exponents from flat hyperkähler quotients

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M^{4n},g,I,J,K)$ be a connected noncompact hyperkähler manifold: $I,J,K$ are parallel complex structures satisfying the quaternion relations and $g$ is Kähler for each. For $p\in M$, write $V_p(r)=\operatorname{Vol}_g B_g(p,r)$. Konno asks to describe its large-radius asymptotics and find examples with different volume growth. The statement does not explicitly impose geodesic completeness, irreducibility or simple connectedness, and does not assume that growth is a pure power. Its basepoint-independence remark concerns asymptotic behavior, not equality of ball-volume functions at different centers.

The supplied aid gives examples in every fixed dimension $4n$. For any integer $1\leq d\leq4n$, let $\Lambda$ be a full lattice in a $(4n-d)$-dimensional real subspace of $\mathbb H^n$, acting by translations. The quotient is a complete flat hyperkähler manifold isometric to \[ M_d={\mathbb{R}}^d\times T^{4n-d}. \] Translation invariance lets the quaternionic structures descend; no invariance of the lattice under quaternionic multiplication is required. Put $b_d=\operatorname{Vol}(B_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}(0,1))$, choose $q_0$ in the flat torus, and set $\rho(q)=d_T(q,q_0)$. The reported exact slice formula is \[ V(r)=b_d\int_T(r^2-\rho(q)^2)_+^{d/2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_T(q). \] Consequently, for $r$ larger than the torus diameter, \[ V(r)=b_d\operatorname{Vol}(T)r^d -\frac d2 b_d\left(\int_T\rho(q)^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V_T(q)\right)r^{d-2} +O(r^{d-4}). \] For a zero-dimensional torus take volume one and $\rho=0$, giving the Euclidean formula exactly. Thus all integer exponents from 1 to $4n$ occur under the source's unrestricted topological/holonomy conventions.

Under the literal wording, a nonempty bounded convex open subset $U\subset\mathbb H^n$, with its restricted flat hyperkähler metric, is an incomplete noncompact example. Its intrinsic distance equals Euclidean distance, so $B_U(p,r)=U$ for sufficiently large $r$, and $V_p(r)$ is eventually constant. This example is excluded if completeness was intended.

The aid also reports complete four-dimensional examples of Hattori with noninteger power growth between 3 and 4 and logarithmic corrections, without a detailed reference. These show why the flat product family is not a classification of possible asymptotics. The source does not specify whether a desired classification should be up to asymptotic equivalence, comparison bounds or growth exponents.

Short English statement

What volume-growth behaviors occur on noncompact hyperkähler manifolds? The supplied aid realizes every integer exponent in a fixed dimension through flat translation quotients and records a sharper expansion. Completeness is missing from the literal source, which also permits incomplete finite-volume examples with eventually constant ball volume.
