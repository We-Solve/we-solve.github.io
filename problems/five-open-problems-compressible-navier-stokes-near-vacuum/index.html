Definitions and mathematical statement

Denis Serre's Open Problem 3 concerns compressible Navier--Stokes flow with density-independent constant viscosities near vacuum. In a standard barotropic model the unknowns are density $\rho\geq0$ and velocity $u\in{\mathbb{R}}^d$, satisfying \[ \partial_t\rho+\operatorname{div}(\rho u)=0, \qquad \partial_t(\rho u)+\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p(\rho) =\operatorname{div}\mathbb S(u), \] with constant-coefficient viscous stress \[ \mathbb S(u)=\mu(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T}) +\lambda(\operatorname{div}u)I. \] This notation illustrates the constant-viscosity issue; the source does not specify whether the intended system is barotropic or includes energy/temperature, nor does it supply a pressure law, viscosity inequalities, dimension-dependent parameters, domain, boundary data, initial data, or solution class.

The question is whether the same unphysical one-dimensional consequence identified by Hoff and Serre has a counterpart in dimensions $d\geq2$. A rigorous analogue must identify that consequence and the one-dimensional hypotheses, then formulate the corresponding higher-dimensional property to prove or disprove. Neither the consequence nor its precise theorem is included in the supplied statement, so no particular vacuum-interface pathology, nonexistence result, or regularity failure is substituted for it. "Near vacuum" also requires a specified regime, such as vanishing density or a limiting low-density family.

The supplied research summary reports the one-dimensional constant-viscosity/vacuum incompatibility as established, but the multidimensional analogue unresolved. It describes the one-dimensional mechanism as forcing solutions into "only recursively weaker classes," without defining those classes or reproducing a result. It mentions later vacuum-interface work by Hoff--Serre and followers but supplies no precise citation settling or disproving the requested analogue. These are supplied assessments, not independently verified progress or a new failure example.

Short English statement

Serre asks whether a known one-dimensional pathology of constant-viscosity compressible flow near vacuum also occurs in higher dimensions. The record omits the exact Hoff--Serre consequence and its hypotheses, so the counterpart cannot yet be stated as a unique theorem or counterexample problem.
