Rigid Calabi--Yau intermediate Jacobians: periods, cycle-theoretic separation, and symmetry certificates

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks to use intermediate Jacobians arithmetically and, in dimension three, to study algebraic one-cycles modulo rational equivalence. Its concrete target is a smooth projective rigid Calabi--Yau threefold $X/{\mathbb{C}}$, with \[ K_X\sim0,\quad\pi_1(X)=1,\quad h^{1,0}=h^{2,0}=h^{2,1}=0,\quad h^{3,0}=1. \] Determine the elliptic curve \[ J^2(X)=H^3(X,{\mathbb{C}})/(F^2H^3(X,{\mathbb{C}})+H^3(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{free}}), \] in particular its $j$-invariant. Here $F^2=H^{3,0}$, so this complex torus has dimension one. The source does not specify an arithmetic field of definition or an effective period/lattice input, and determining this torus does not compute all of $\mathrm{CH}^2(X)$.

The attached aid reports the exact period expression. For a primitive integral basis $\alpha,\beta$ of torsion-free $H^3$, $0\ne\Omega\in H^{3,0}$, and cup-product pairing $Q$, choose orientation with \[ \tau=\frac{Q(\beta,\Omega)}{Q(\alpha,\Omega)}\in\mathbb H, \qquad j(J^2(X))=j(\tau). \] A rational basis or isogeny comparison alone need not determine this exact elliptic curve. The aid also reports $J_a^3(X)=0$, where this notation means the connected algebraic Abel--Jacobi part generated by algebraically trivial one-cycles. This does not assert the full Abel--Jacobi map on homologically trivial cycles or the Chow group is zero, and does not give an automatic arithmetic descent of all of $J^2(X)$.

For a genuine lattice-preserving automorphism acting on $H^{3,0}$ by a primitive third or sixth root, the aid reports $j=0$; a primitive fourth root gives $j=1728$. A primitive fifth-root scalar action is impossible on rational rank-two $H^3$. It consequently reports $j=0$ for the standard 27-fixed-point CM quotient example. The source's description using an elliptic curve with CM by ${\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_3)$ and kernels of $\zeta_3-1$ omits the quotient action: the product of finite kernels alone is not a threefold. The usual quotient/resolution interpretation is therefore explicit but not fully specified by the extracted text.

The source also gives the nodal quintic \[ \sum_{i=1}^5x_i^5-5\psi\prod_{i=1}^5x_i=0,\qquad\psi^5=1, \] with 125 nodes and a rigid resolution, attributed to Yui. The aid calls it the Schoen quintic and says its visible fifth-root symmetry does not determine $j$; a primitive integral lattice comparison is still needed. No $j$-value for it is supplied.

Its other remark concerns smooth intersections of two quadrics in ${\mathbb{P}}^5$: study the genus-two curve $y^2=\det(\lambda f_1+\mu f_2)$, its Jacobian, and the variety of lines as an abelian-variety torsor. It reports a line over finite fields from that torsor description, and asks about arithmetic applications, skew conjugate lines, ground-field conics and finding a line over a quadratic extension. The determinant model and finite-field claims need their smoothness/characteristic hypotheses; they are separate from the rigid Calabi--Yau computation.

Short English statement

Determine the elliptic intermediate Jacobian of a rigid Calabi--Yau threefold, ideally through its exact $j$-invariant, and explore arithmetic uses. The attached aid gives a primitive-lattice period formula and limited symmetry certificates, distinguishes the zero algebraic Abel--Jacobi part from the full torus, and leaves the quintic's exact lattice comparison unresolved.
