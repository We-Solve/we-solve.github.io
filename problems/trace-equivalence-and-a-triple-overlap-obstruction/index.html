Math-1364

Trace equivalence and a triple-overlap obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source identity and reconstruction. The canonical fragment is labeled "Conjecture 6.5" and starts at item 3. Supplied verification identifies it instead as items 3--4 of Conjecture 6.17, Section 6.3 "Matrices." The appended spectral-radius definition and page numeral belong to the next subsection, not this conjecture. The first two items and complete original matrix hypotheses are absent.

Under the aid's recovered convention, write $A_i=I+M_i\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$, $1\leq i\leq t$, with zero diagonal $M_i$ and each off-diagonal support oriented (no opposite arcs within one $M_i$). The stated colored-load condition is \[ \sum_{i=1}^t\sum_{w\ne v}(M_i)_{vw}\geq n\qquad\text{for every }v. \] The recovered strict-trace target is \[ \operatorname{tr}(A_1\cdots A_t)>n. \] The exact match to the missing parent hypotheses requires checking.

Layered and increasing-cycle formulations. Put $t+1$ copies of $V$ in order. Between layers $i$ and $i+1$, include each horizontal edge $v\to v$ and every arc of $M_i$. Product entries count layered paths, and the $n$ all-horizontal returns show trace at least $n$. The aid proves that strict trace excess is equivalent to a return from $u$ in layer one to the same $u$ in layer $t+1$ using an off-diagonal edge. After identifying layers and assigning color $i$ to $M_i$ arcs, this is equivalent to a rainbow directed cycle whose colors strictly increase when traversed from a suitable starting vertex. An arbitrary rainbow cycle without this ordering condition is not the asserted equivalent target.

Reported overlap obstruction. Suppose the load hypotheses hold but the trace is exactly $n$. Let $m(e)$ count the matrices containing off-diagonal arc $e$. The aid proves that the union support must be oriented and \[ \sum_e\max\{m(e)-2,0\}\geq n. \] Indeed, oppositely directed arcs with distinct layer times would create a layered return, while one-layer opposite arcs are prohibited by orientation. The resulting oriented union has at most $\binom n2$ arcs, so the load total at least $n^2$ forces at least $n$ multiplicity units beyond two.

Consequently the strict-trace conjecture holds when every off-diagonal arc appears at most twice. For $t>2$, a counterexample must have at least $\lceil n/(t-2)\rceil$ distinct arcs of multiplicity at least three; for $t=3$, at least $n$ arcs must appear in all three matrices. These are necessary conditions, not constructions of counterexamples.

Reported sharp two-matrix relaxation. At the weakened pointwise load threshold $n-1$, trace equality for two matrices is classified by duplicated regular tournaments: $M_1=M_2$ is the adjacency matrix of a regular tournament, so $n$ is odd, each load is $n-1$ and $\operatorname{tr}((I+M_1)^2)=n$. Conversely the aid reports that these are exactly the two-matrix trace-equality instances under that relaxed load condition. This does not settle the unrestricted many-matrix conjecture.

Short English statement

The fragment concerns layered matrix paths and increasing-color cycles, despite its wrong extracted conjecture number. The aid proves the trace/path/cycle equivalence and shows that a counterexample needs substantial triple overlap of arc supports. It settles multiplicity at most two and classifies the two-matrix one-unit-weaker threshold, leaving the general problem open.
