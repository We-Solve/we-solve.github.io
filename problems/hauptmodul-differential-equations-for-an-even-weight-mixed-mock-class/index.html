Changing the independent variable rigorously.
Let $t=t(\tau)$ be holomorphic with $t'(\tau)\ne0$ on a simply connected local chart, and suppose $f$ satisfies a known equation $f''+a(\tau)f'+b(\tau)f=0$. Write $f(\tau)=y(t(\tau))$. Chain-rule differentiation gives \[y_{tt}+\left(\frac{t''}{(t')^2}+\frac a{t'}\right)y_t+\frac b{(t')^2}y=0. \] This is a differential equation in the modular coordinate only if the two displayed coefficients are single-valued functions of $t$ in the intended global setting. A local inverse alone gives no such arithmetic or meromorphic coefficient property.
A branch countercheck.
Take $t=\tau^2$ away from zero and the equation $f''+f'=0$. Its transformed first-derivative coefficient is $1/(2t)+1/(2\tau)$. The final term changes sign between the two lifts $\tau$ and $-\tau$. Thus a perfectly regular differential equation upstairs need not descend to a scalar equation with coefficients on the $t$-plane. Critical points where $t'=0$ create further singularities and require separate local analysis.
Gap.
This example is not claimed to be a mock modular form; it isolates the actual descent obstruction in a tempting chain-rule argument. For mixed mock forms one must first derive a non-tautological differential system from their modular completion/shadow, then prove coefficient descent in a specified modular function field, including branch points and cusps. No such system is obtained here. Merely writing $dy/dt$ using a local inverse does not answer the source's structural question.
