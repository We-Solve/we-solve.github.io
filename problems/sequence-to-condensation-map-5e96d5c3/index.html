Statement

Sequence-to-condensation map

Domain. Biomolecular phase separation.   Retained status/source context. Open sequence-to-phase-behaviour prediction programme. The supplied crowding review is related background, not direct support for a universal condensation map; a primary restricted molecular-grammar study is added.

Definitions and assumptions.
Choose a family of disordered RNA-binding proteins with specified RNA species and modification states. Let $s$ be protein sequence, $T$ temperature, $I$ ionic strength and $\mu_R$ imposed RNA chemical potential. Protein concentration $c$ is in moles per litre. A finite-parameter free-energy density $f_\theta(c,c_R;s,T,I)$ describes a homogeneous mixture with internal RNA concentration $c_R$. At fixed reservoir $\mu_R$, define $g_\theta(c)=\min_{c_R}[f_\theta(c,c_R)-\mu_Rc_R]$; assume this minimization has a unique stable branch; coexistence protein concentrations $c_-,c_+$ satisfy common-tangent conditions for $g$ when the equilibrium approximation applies. Define an operational liquid condensate using reversibility and material-exchange assays, rather than visible puncta alone.
Problem statement.
Infer sequence-dependent interaction parameters and predict the coexistence boundary for a held-out variant panel: \[ \mu(c_-)=\mu(c_+),\quad g(c_+)-g(c_-)=\mu(c_-)(c_+-c_-),\quad \max_{s\in\mathcal V}|\widehat{\log c_-}-\log c_-^{\rm obs}|\leq\varepsilon, \] where $\mu=\partial g/\partial c$ at fixed reservoir RNA chemical potential. In words, predict how sequence changes move a measurable phase boundary. Logarithms use concentration divided by a fixed reference concentration.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
Require $c_-<c_+$, a globally minimizing RNA composition, and a supporting tangent: $g(c)\geq g(c_-)+\mu(c_-)(c-c_-)$ throughout the admissible concentration interval. Equality at two stationary points alone can select metastable rather than equilibrium coexistence. If several stable branches or phase pairs exist, predict the set of globally admissible pairs; do not silently choose one.

Alternative formulations.
(i) For a single purified protein, predict saturation concentration and its temperature dependence without RNA or active chemistry. (ii) For a closed mixture at fixed total RNA, impose equality of both component chemical potentials and osmotic pressure between phases; then predict composition-dependent partition coefficients and possible reentrant phase separation; a scalar saturation concentration is then insufficient. These are distinct targets; kinetic size selection is treated in Problem 6.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Residue-pattern rules work within some families, but aggregation, gelation and liquid coexistence are different phenomena. Finite-size cells and slow equilibration complicate thermodynamic extrapolation. Inferring every interaction from one phase boundary is non-identifiable, so orthogonal binding and material-property data constrain the model. No universally exact sequence map is claimed.

Attempted answer

Proposition: Let $H_1=g\prime(x)-g\prime(y)$ and $H_2=g(y)-g(x)-g\prime(x)(y-x)$. At coexistence $x<y$ with positive endpoint curvatures, $\det D_{x,y}H=-(y-x)g\prime\prime(x)g\prime\prime(y)\ne0$. The coexistence branch is locally differentiable in parameters.
Proof: Differentiate both constraints. The second Jacobian row is $(-(y-x)g\prime\prime(x),0)$ at coexistence. The implicit-function theorem gives derivative $-(D_{x,y}H)^{-1}D_\theta H$, and hence quantitative sensitivity wherever that inverse is bounded. ∎

What remains unresolved

Critical points, competing globally stable phases, sequence parameterization and validation are not controlled.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
