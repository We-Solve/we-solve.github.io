Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied excerpt does not specify the class of spaces under consideration or define its cover conventions. The following makes the usual open-cover interpretation explicit; its applicability to the intended class requires confirmation.

Let $X$ be a topological space and let ${\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$. An $\omega$-cover of $X$ is a family $\mathcal U$ of open subsets of $X$ such that $X\notin\mathcal U$ and every finite subset of $X$ is contained in some member of $\mathcal U$. A $\gamma$-cover is an infinite family of open subsets of $X$, none equal to $X$, such that each point of $X$ belongs to all but finitely many members. Under these conventions, the $\gamma$-property means that every open $\omega$-cover contains a $\gamma$-subcover.

For a sequence $(A_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of subsets of $X$, define \[ \liminf_{n\to\infty} A_n =\bigcup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigcap_{n\ge m}A_n. \] For $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$, let $L(\mathcal F)$ be the smallest family containing $\mathcal F$ and closed under this operation. Thus closure includes repeated applications, rather than only one application to sequences from $\mathcal F$. The space $X$ has the $\delta$-property, or is a $\delta$-set, if \[ \text{for every open }\omega\text{-cover }\mathcal U\text{ of }X, \qquad X\in L(\mathcal U). \]

Problem. In the class of spaces intended by the source, is the $\delta$-property equivalent to the $\gamma$-property? Equivalently, does every space in that class with the $\delta$-property have the $\gamma$-property?

The source records the implication from the $\gamma$-property to the $\delta$-property, observing that for a countably infinite $\gamma$-cover enumerated without repetitions as $\{U_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}$, one has $X=\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_n$. It presents the converse as open. The intended ambient class, and whether the source restricts $\omega$-covers to countable families, remain unresolved by the supplied excerpt; the question above is therefore qualified by those missing specifications.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every $\delta$-set must have the $\gamma$-property: if repeated sequential lower limits of each open $\omega$-cover can produce the whole space, must that cover contain a $\gamma$-subcover? The source states the reverse implication and labels the converse open. The intended class of spaces and cover conventions require confirmation.
