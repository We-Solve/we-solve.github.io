Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.31

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group with identity $1$. Its derived subgroup is \[ G'=[G,G]=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G\rangle. \] Define $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]$ for integers $n\geq 0$. The group $G$ is soluble if $G^{(n)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $n\geq 0$. It is right-orderable if there exists a strict total order $<$ on $G$ such that, for every $x,y,z\in G$, \[ x<y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad xz<yz. \] The quotient $G/G'$ is its abelianization.

The supplied question asks whether there exists a soluble right-orderable group $G$ such that \[ |G/G'|=[G:G']<\infty. \] No bound on the derived length or on this finite index is specified. Neither finite generation nor an order preserved by left multiplication is required in the supplied wording.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied statement does not require $G\neq\{1\}$, so its literal quantification includes the trivial group. Whether a nontrivial group is intended must be clarified before this can be treated as a precise formulation of the intended open problem. No nontriviality or infinitude hypothesis has been inserted here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a soluble group admitting a right-invariant total order can have finite abelianization. The source does not specify that the group must be nontrivial; this omission leaves the intended scope unresolved.
