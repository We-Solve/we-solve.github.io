Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns the following five settings: vortex patches, water waves, interface flows in porous media, Hele--Shaw cells, and atmospheric front dynamics. These names identify families of evolution problems; the record does not specify particular governing equations or admissible configurations.

A finite-time singularity concerns a failure of the required regularity or admissibility of an evolution at some time $T$ with $0<T<\infty$. Its precise meaning depends on the chosen equations, solution concept, regularity class, and geometric conditions. For example, once these choices are fixed, one possible formulation is that a solution defined for $0\leq t<T$ cannot be extended past $T$ within the specified solution class. This is only a possible formalization: the supplied record does not select this criterion or identify it with any particular norm blow-up, interface collision, or geometric degeneration.

Source-level problem. Investigate whether finite-time singularities form in each of the five settings listed above. The source describes singularity formation as a fundamental open question in these settings, but supplies a research theme rather than a single quantified mathematical proposition.

Unresolved formulation. No particular system of equations, spatial dimension, spatial domain, initial-data class, boundary conditions, physical parameters, or singularity criterion is supplied. The record also does not distinguish existence of singularity-forming data from singularity formation for all data, generic data, or a prescribed family of data. Consequently, no unique precise theorem or conjecture can be extracted without additional hypotheses and a specified target. In particular, the five settings must not be treated as one fully specified evolution problem.

Short English statement

The record asks about finite-time singularity formation in vortex patches, water waves, porous-media interfaces, Hele--Shaw cells, and atmospheric fronts. It states a broad research direction. A precise mathematical question requires a chosen model, admissible initial and boundary data, a solution class, a definition of singularity, and explicit quantifiers; these are absent from the supplied record.
