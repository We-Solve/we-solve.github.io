Attempted inference from negligible tail sampling to negligible training effect

The source asks about performance without fixing its metric. We separate one inference-time sampling metric from the training cross-entropy in a completely specified example. This does not assume that every distribution below is realizable by every fixed low-dimensional projection. Let $N=1001$, $\delta=10^{-6}$, and give the first 1000 tokens equal probabilities $(1-\delta)/1000$ under a data law $Q$, with $Q_{1001}=\delta$. For $M\ge0$, let a model law $P_M$ put probability $\delta e^{-M}$ on token 1001 and spread the remaining mass equally on the first 1000. Use nucleus threshold $\tau=0.9005$ and the same deterministic ordering to resolve equal-probability ties.
Proposition: Nucleus sampling from every $P_M$ and from $Q$ produces exactly the same uniform distribution on the first 901 tokens. Nevertheless the excess training cross-entropy $D_{\rm KL}(Q\|P_M)$ is unbounded as $M$ increases.
Proof: The tail token is always smaller than each head token. For either law, the total probability of the first 900 tokens is at most $0.9<\tau$, while that of the first 901 is at least $0.901(1-10^{-6})>\tau$. Thus the smallest probability-ranked prefix reaching $\tau$ consists of 901 equal-probability tokens; conditioning makes it uniform, independently of $M$. Direct substitution gives \[ D_{\rm KL}(Q\|P_M) =\delta M+(1-\delta)\log\frac{1-\delta}{1-\delta e^{-M}} \ge\delta M+(1-\delta)\log(1-\delta). \] The lower bound tends to infinity. This divergence is exactly the difference between cross-entropy against $P_M$ and cross-entropy against $Q$, by expanding the definition. ∎
Consequently equality of these truncated one-step output laws does not bound the cross-entropy cost of the excluded tail. Small tail mass requires an additional likelihood-ratio or loss bound to imply a small training effect. This is not evidence that any deployed model suffers the displayed degradation, nor a statement about all sequence-level performance metrics.

Verification and remaining gaps

Prefix inequalities were checked exactly. The divergence was evaluated using the stable log expression at $M=0,10^3,10^6$, avoiding underflow in the tiny tail probability. Architectural attainability, actual data frequencies, sequential sampling effects and the empirical importance of the bottleneck remain unestablished.
