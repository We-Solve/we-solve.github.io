Math-2164

Virtual-knot problem 44 — A complete invariant is used for proving that one theory is a part of another theory.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm v}$ be virtual knot diagrams and let $\sim_{\mathrm v}$ be equivalence generated by the usual classical Reidemeister and virtual detour moves, with knot orientation conventions fixed consistently. An invariant $I:\mathcal D_{\mathrm v}\to S$ is complete if, for all $D,D'\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm v}$, \[ I(D)=I(D')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D\sim_{\mathrm v}D'. \] Problem-list item 44 asks how to construct a complete invariant for virtual knots. The intended goal is a useful description of such an invariant and its target, beyond the tautological map sending each diagram to its own equivalence class. The excerpt does not require a particular algebraic target, algorithm, complexity bound, or effective equality test, so these must not be silently added as hypotheses.

The source motivates the request by inclusions of knot theories: it relates the embedding of classical knots into virtual knots to extending a quandle with peripheral structure, and gives the extension of Hurwitz's action for classical and virtual braids as an analogous example. These are supplied motivational assertions, not a definition of the requested virtual-knot invariant or a claim that the ordinary virtual knot group is complete.

Short English statement

Construct an explicitly described invariant that takes equal values exactly on equivalent virtual knots. The source does not specify the desired target or effectiveness requirements.
