Scaling kidney exchange

Statement

Original subject group: Mechanism design and market design

Audit classification: Published research agenda. Roth 2010 p.4 identifies center participation, and Ashlagi–Roth §6 item 2 explicitly asks about international collaboration. The weighted withholding mechanism is editorial.

Definitions and precise research target

Model and research target. Let countries or transplant centers $h=1,\ldots,H$ own sets $V_h$ of incompatible patient–donor pairs. A directed compatibility graph $G=(V,E)$, with $V=\bigcup_hV_h$, has edge $i\to j$ when donor $i$ can medically donate to patient $j$. Feasible exchanges are disjoint cycles of length at most $K$, together with permitted chains from nondirected donors, satisfying explicit cross-border shipping and surgery-capacity constraints. Each center may withhold pairs and conduct internal exchanges. Let $U_h(M)$ be its expected number of transplanted patients under mechanism $M$, and $U_h^{\mathrm{outside}}$ its attainable internal alternative. Fix nonnegative patient weights $w_i$ representing an openly stated equity objective. Determine mechanisms maximizing \[ E\left[\sum_{i\in V}w_i\mathbf1\{i\text{ receives a transplant}\}\right] \] while ensuring $U_h(M)\geq U_h^{\mathrm{outside}}$ and discouraging profitable pair withholding, under a specified stochastic compatibility and arrival model. Quantify separately gains from larger scale and gains from differences in biological compatibility distributions. Include exchange failures and the absence of unrestricted monetary transfers. Characterize the efficiency or equity loss needed for sustainable institutional participation and test mechanisms against realistic pool composition.

Definitions and maintained assumptions (editorial unless a source definition is named). This is a research agenda indexed by declared models, not an assertion of a common optimum over unspecified primitives. Each specialization must state its population and parameter domains, joint laws of arrivals, compatibility and failures, operational constraints, information and admissible policies, and whether its conclusions are pointwise or uniform over a declared model class. The strategic unit $h$ is a country or a center as declared in that model; these are alternative institutional partitions, not automatically a nested strategic structure.

Pair vertices remain $V=\bigsqcup_hV_h$. Write $D$ for donor-only vertices and $R$ for recipient-only terminal vertices, disjoint from $V$ and each other. The extended compatibility graph has vertex set $V\sqcup D\sqcup R$; its arcs go from a vertex in $V\cup D$ supplying a donor to a vertex in $V\cup R$ supplying a recipient, when medically compatible. Donor-only vertices have no incoming arcs and recipient-only vertices have no outgoing arcs. Cycles use pair vertices; chains start at donor-only vertices and may end at pair vertices or terminal recipients according to the declared chain rules. A plan is vertex-disjoint and obeys declared length, timing, surgery and shipping limits. The displayed objective counts patients of pairs in $V$, with weights $w_i$; it does not count donor-only vertices or recipient-only terminals. Institutional transplant-count payoffs likewise count patients of pairs in $V_h$, once each, including permitted internal transplants. Control, reporting rights and access to donor-only and terminal vertices must be declared. Including terminal recipients in welfare or institutional credit would require an explicitly changed accounting target.

Let $T_h$ denote the realized count for institution $h$ from the complete execution, including permitted internal exchanges and any recourse. Its expectation, conditional on a declared comparison information set $\mathcal I$, defines utility. For fixed model $m$, mechanism $M$, submissions $s=(S_h,S_{-h})$ and execution policy $d$, write \[ u_h^m(M;s,d\mid\mathcal I)=\mathbb E_m[T_h(M;s,d)\mid\mathcal I]. \] Here $d$ includes the institution's internal-exchange decisions, timing and any permitted reuse of submitted but unmatched pairs. Define $\mathcal D_h^m(M,S_h,S_{-h})$ to contain only admissible deviations whose internal and platform execution is jointly feasible under the same medical, donor-access and shared-resource constraints. Do not add separately optimized platform and internal values without establishing that joint feasibility. For a declared full-submission execution policy $d_h^0$, the full-submission comparison is \[ u_h^m(M;(V_h,S_{-h}),d_h^0\mid\mathcal I) \geq u_h^m(M;(S_h,S_{-h}),d_h\mid\mathcal I) \] for every institution, submitted subset, other submissions and admissible $d_h$, at the information states specified by the chosen incentive convention. Full-participation dominance requires this comparison against every other submission profile, rather than only its equilibrium distribution; the model must also state whether other institutions' realized private information is held fixed or integrated out. An interim comparison conditions on the institution's own information and integrates unobserved information under the specified conditional joint law and other institutions' specified reporting strategies. These conventions must not be interchanged.

Let $\mathcal A_h^m$ be the declared admissible internal policies when institution $h$ does not participate. Its outside value at the same comparison information is \[ o_h^m(\mathcal I)=\sup_{a\in\mathcal A_h^m}\mathbb E_m[T_h^{\rm internal}(a)\mid\mathcal I], \] with the supremum over policies measurable with respect to their permitted information. Participation compares the participating conditional utility to this value, using identical medical and resource constraints and failure treatment. In the introductory notation, $U_h(M)$ and $U_h^{\mathrm{outside}}$ refer to these expected values under the declared participation and information convention, not to realized counts inside another expectation.

For repeated decisions, a mechanism is a history-dependent policy, with its observed histories, execution after failures, rematching, bridge-donor availability and exit rules declared. State a horizon and payoff aggregation for which the comparisons are well defined, and impose continuation participation at the histories required by the model. A centralized benchmark must declare its information, timing, resources and recourse; complete information about the current graph does not imply knowledge of future failures or arrivals. State the loss measure and handle a zero benchmark explicitly before using ratios. The agenda does not impose a polynomial-time or approximation requirement. Patient weights are announced equity primitives; centers' transplant-count objectives need not share them. Quantitative scale and biological-composition comparisons must declare their pool laws, biological attributes, features held fixed, allocation rules and treatment of interactions. Matched pool size alone does not identify a unique decomposition or establish an empirical effect. A realistic-pool evaluation must state its population, evidence, metric and whether the claim is theoretical, simulated or empirical; no particular empirical protocol is prescribed here.

Variants and assumption changes

  1. One-shot center incentives (editorial). Fix graph distributions and cycle cap, prohibit money and require dominant full submission. Determine the attainable weighted-efficiency loss relative to a centralized complete-information benchmark.
  2. Dynamic international pool (source direction, editorial model). Allow arrivals, exchange failures and center exit with continuation outside options. Find participation-sustaining collaboration rules and compare matched-size pools to isolate biological heterogeneity gains.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

These conventions apply to each entry unless explicitly replaced.

Models and identification. A primitive model $m$ specifies all probability laws, feasible choices, information, equilibrium and measurement rules used by its target. The admissible class $\mathcal M$ is fixed independently of outcomes. If $P_O(m)$ is its observable probability law and $\tau(m)$ the target, its identified set at observable law $P$ is \[ \mathcal I_\tau(P)=\{\tau(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_O(m)=P\}. \] Point identification means that this set is a singleton. An empty set signals incompatibility of data and assumptions. Coordinate bounds need not describe the joint set. Bounds are sharp only if every retained target is attainable or arbitrarily approachable by compatible models. Finite bounds require explicit restrictions on unobserved quantities; unrestricted confounding, hidden wealth, extrapolation or arbitrary equilibrium selection can yield uninformative sets.

Causal interventions. A potential outcome $Y_i(p)$ is the outcome under a complete policy regime $p$, including timing, financing, information and market spillovers. The target population and units are fixed before treatment. With interference, use the complete assignment vector or a justified exposure mapping; individual no-interference notation alone cannot identify an economy-wide intervention. A difference of mean potential outcomes does not require the cross-world joint distribution. Conditioning on post-treatment migration, survival, enrollment or employment changes the estimand and needs separate justification.

Statistical statements. An estimator, confidence set, forecast or test specifies a sampling law, model class and loss/coverage criterion. Uniform $(1-\alpha)$-coverage means $\inf_{m\in\mathcal M}\Pr_m\{\tau(m)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha$, or an explicitly asymptotic version. The whole parameter space trivially has coverage, so informativeness requires a stated width, risk or power objective. A forecasting improvement is relative to a named benchmark, information set and evaluation population.

Equilibrium and optimization. An equilibrium comprises feasible plans, optimality, beliefs where needed, budget constraints and all market/resource clearing. The primitives or a stated benchmark define each correspondence $\mathcal E(p;m)$. Nonemptiness is assumed or proved, never inferred just from compact policy sets. A selected-equilibrium target uses a declared selection rule; a robust target quantifies over all permitted equilibria. Use suprema or infima unless attainment is established. An $\varepsilon$-optimal policy is feasible and within $\varepsilon$ of the finite optimum value. Report an empty feasible set separately.

Welfare and accounting. Normative utility, interpersonal normalization, social weights, numeraire, discounting and the use of public receipts are primitives. Behavioral utility need not coincide with the welfare criterion. Household welfare includes ownership income and rents once; adding profits again to compensating variation generally double-counts them. A monetary equivalent is defined by an explicit transfer and price convention, with monotonicity and range conditions. Average effects, mechanism contributions, transfers and welfare losses are different targets. Infinite sums require convergence and dynamic feasible plans require terminal or no-Ponzi conditions.

Source versions. A working-paper date, revision date and journal publication year are distinguished. A DOI for a journal version is not automatically the DOI of an earlier manuscript. Locators refer to the stated version and printed pages where specified. A metadata-only check does not verify a claim about a full-text conclusion.
