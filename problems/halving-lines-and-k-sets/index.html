Math-1646

Halving lines and k-sets

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S\subset\mathbb R^2$ consist of $n$ distinct points, with no three collinear. A $k$-set is a subset $A\subset S$ of size $k$ for which some line disjoint from $S$ strictly separates $A$ from $S\setminus A$. Count distinct subsets, not the infinitely many separating lines. Define \[ K_2(n,k)=\max_{|S|=n}\#\{A\subset S:A\text{ is a }k\text{-set}\}. \] Find sharp bounds as functions of both $n$ and $1\le k\le n-1$.

For even $n$, a halving line is a line through two points of $S$ with exactly $(n-2)/2$ of the remaining points in each open half-plane. Determine the largest number $H_2(n)$ of these lines. The halving-line count is a closely related extremal question; its objects are not the separating lines in the preceding definition.

In $\mathbb R^d$, take sets in affine general position, define $k$-sets by strict separation with a hyperplane, and determine the analogous $K_d(n,k)$. A halving hyperplane passes through $d$ points and has equally many remaining points on each side, so this equal-split version requires $n-d$ even. Count each resulting affine hyperplane once.

Short English statement

Determine how many subsets of a point configuration can be cut off by a line or hyperplane, with particular emphasis on equal-size splits.

OpenAI's claim

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For an even-sized planar point set $P$, with no three points collinear, let $h(P)$ count pairs whose line leaves $(|P|-2)/2$ points in each open halfplane. The claimed constants $\epsilon>0,C<\infty,n_0$ are absolute and satisfy \[h(P)\le C|P|^{4/3-\epsilon}\quad(|P|\ge n_0).\] The all-ranks theorem also bounds by $CN^{4/3-\epsilon}$ the number $S_k(P)$ of switches of the $k$ points minimizing $y-sx$ as $s$ ranges from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, for every rank $0\le k\le N$ of a generic $N$-point configuration. This power improvement does not give matching sharp bounds at every rank. Here the all-ranks genericity conditions prohibit three collinear points, repeated first coordinates, equal slopes from different point pairs, and any proper intersection of two point-pair segments lying on a third such segment. Its parameters range over $N\ge1$ and $0\le k\le N$.

Outline of the argument

Planar halving-line saving: Rotating rank sweeps track membership changes in the lower point set, including points that later return. Near saturation yields limiting rank-coordinate measures with an impossible concentration pattern; a little-oh packing improvement at one fixed scale then contracts the recurrence.
