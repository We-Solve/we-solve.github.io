Emerton's mod-p local-global factorization and a finite-level one-prime ratio law

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Conjecture 3 (Emerton), with source repairs marked. Let $\bar\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_2(k)$ be continuous, absolutely irreducible and odd, over a splitting field $k$ of characteristic $p$. In the usual $p>2$ convention, odd means determinant $-1$ at complex conjugation. Let the level Hecke ideal $\mathfrak m_N$ have the good-prime eigenvalues $T_\ell=\operatorname{tr}\bar\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_\ell)$. The precise excluded primes and any determinant/diamond conditions must be supplied. Restoring dropped overlines and subscripts, the conjectural display is of the form \[ \varinjlim_N H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X(N)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},k)[\mathfrak m_N] \ \cong\ \left(\bigotimes_\ell'\pi_\ell^{\mathrm{modified}} (\bar\rho|_{G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell}})\right)\otimes_k\bar\rho. \] The local factors are said to be finite-length admissible smooth $\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$-representations. Their actual modified definitions, restricted-tensor reference vectors and full action/level conventions are absent. Eigenspace annihilation by $\mathfrak m$, localization and generalized eigenspaces are different operations; the bracket notation is not silently changed among them.

Reported theorem qualification. The aid attributes the Galois multiplicity-space form to Emerton (2011), Theorem 1.2.6, for $p>2$ with two explicit exclusions on $\bar\rho|_{G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}}$. Those exclusions are not stated in this supplied record, so the theorem is cited only conditionally on its full hypotheses, not for all $p>2$ representations. A multiplicity-space theorem for $\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\bar\rho,-)$ is not automatically the full displayed tensor decomposition in the presence of Galois extensions. The aid mentions later improvements but does not establish unrestricted $p=2$ or reducible-global versions from its checked sources.

Reported finite-level product reduction. Assume the proven factorization applies to a multiplicity space $M$, together with the required comparison of finite-level cohomology with invariants at a factorizable neat level $K=\prod_\ell K_\ell$. Assume also that, almost everywhere, the restricted-tensor reference line is the full one-dimensional $K_\ell^0$-fixed space. Then the aid's finite-support tensor-invariants lemma gives \[ M^K\cong\bigotimes_\ell'\pi_\ell^{K_\ell},\qquad \mu(K):=\dim_kM^K=\prod_\ell d_\ell(K_\ell), \quad d_\ell(K_\ell)=\dim_k\pi_\ell^{K_\ell}. \] Admissibility gives finite local dimensions and almost all factors are one. The finite-level comparison is a separate hypothesis, especially in characteristic $p$; neatness or a direct-limit factorization alone does not justify exactness of fixed vectors. If a finite-level eigenspace is additionally known to equal $M^K\otimes\bar\rho$, its dimension is $2\mu(K)$; that extra identification is not built into the multiplicity definition.

For two such levels differing only at a prime $q$, the reported division-free diagnostic is \[ \mu(K')\,d_q(K_q)=\mu(K)\,d_q(K'_q). \] It remains valid when a local dimension or a common factor vanishes; a quotient formula would not. No explicit $p$-local invariant dimensions are computed from missing local factor data.

Preserved appended questions. Calegari asks to calculate finite-level Hecke-eigenspace multiplicities from the modified local factors; Emerton's workshop comment calls the away-from-$p$ calculation "easy" and the $p$-part a thesis question. Diamond asks for reducible representations and analogues for Shimura curves attached to quaternion algebras unramified at $p$, and whether these clarify the local $p$-behavior. The final "3.2. Berger. In the notation of Breuil--Berger" is an incomplete next-item heading, not a recoverable new theorem. These extensions are not answered by the conditional product lemma. All statements retain the source notes' transcription-error warning and require independent review.

Short English statement

The aid reports Emerton's multiplicity-space theorem only under its missing local hypotheses, then gives a conditional finite-level product of local fixed-vector dimensions. The cross-multiplied one-prime identity survives zero dimensions. The original reducible, Shimura-curve and explicit $p$-local questions remain separate, and eigenspaces are not silently identified with localized multiplicity spaces.
