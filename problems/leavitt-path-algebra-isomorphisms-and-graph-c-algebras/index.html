Leavitt Path Algebra Isomorphisms and Graph $C^*$-Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A directed graph is a quadruple $G=(G^0,G^1,s_G,r_G)$, consisting of sets of vertices and edges and source and range maps $s_G,r_G:G^1\to G^0$. A vertex $v$ is regular if $0<|s_G^{-1}(v)|<\infty$. The phrase "arbitrary graphs" is retained: no finiteness, countability, or row-finiteness hypothesis is imposed in this draft.

The complex Leavitt path algebra $L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(G)$ is the universal associative complex algebra generated by vertices $v\in G^0$, edges $x_e$, and formal ghost edges $y_e$, for $e\in G^1$, subject to \[ \begin{gathered} v w=\begin{cases}v,&v=w,\\0,&v\ne w,\end{cases}\\ s_G(e)x_e=x_e=x_e r_G(e),\qquad r_G(e)y_e=y_e=y_e s_G(e),\\ y_e x_f=\begin{cases}r_G(e),&e=f,\\0,&e\ne f,\end{cases}\\ v=\sum_{s_G(e)=v}x_e y_e\qquad(v\text{ regular}). \end{gathered} \] No common identity is adjoined by this definition. The last relation is imposed only at regular vertices, not at sinks or infinite emitters.

The graph $C^*$-algebra $C^*(G)$ is the universal complex $C^*$-algebra generated by mutually orthogonal projections $p_v$, for $v\in G^0$, and partial isometries $t_e$, for $e\in G^1$, satisfying \[ \begin{gathered} t_e^*t_f=\begin{cases}p_{r_G(e)},&e=f,\\0,&e\ne f,\end{cases} \qquad t_e t_e^*\le p_{s_G(e)},\\ p_v=\sum_{s_G(e)=v}t_e t_e^*\qquad(v\text{ regular}). \end{gathered} \]

For permanent UnsolvedMath problem $30002034$, source code $\texttt{catalogue entry}$, the question is whether, for every pair of directed graphs $E,F$, \[ \left(\exists\text{ a ring isomorphism }L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(E)\longrightarrow L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(F)\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(\exists\text{ a complex }{*}\text{-isomorphism }C^*(E)\longrightarrow C^*(F)\right). \] Both implications are requested. A ring isomorphism here means an additive and multiplicative bijection; complex linearity and preservation of an involution are not required. The right-hand isomorphism is interpreted in the usual category of complex $C^*$-algebras. The question concerns existence of isomorphisms, without asserting that a given isomorphism extends or restricts to one on the other side.

These definitions use standard directed-graph conventions. The supplied formulation does not explicitly specify graph cardinality conventions or the category of the right-hand isomorphism, so those conventions require source review. The supplied registry labels the problem open and its statement corrected_verified; these are supplied curation claims, not independent verification by this draft.

Short English statement

For arbitrary directed graphs, is the existence of an abstract ring isomorphism between their complex Leavitt path algebras equivalent to the existence of a ${*}$-isomorphism between their graph $C^*$-algebras? Both directions are part of the question. The graph and isomorphism conventions stated above require human source review.
