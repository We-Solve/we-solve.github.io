Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A phylogenetic model assigns transition parameters to edges of a tree and maps them to joint probabilities at its observed leaves. A phylogenetic invariant is a polynomial vanishing on all probability vectors in that model. Rate variation among sites can yield mixtures of phylogenetic probability distributions. Algebraically, mixtures are modeled by secant varieties: Zariski closures of spans of a fixed number of points of the original model variety. The task is to find equations for such rate-variable model families.

Research attempt

Literature and scope

Problem 34 leaves the mutation model, tree and rate classes unspecified. I use a binary symmetric (CFN) quartet on split $12\mid34$ as an explicit example, not as a replacement for that agenda. The attempted route is to retain a single-class quadratic invariant after mixing stochastic parameter points.
Proposition: A two-class stochastic mixture can violate the quadratic edge invariant that vanishes in each class.
Proof: Represent binary states by spins $\pm1$. Give the root an independent uniform spin and each edge an independent noise spin with mean $\theta_e\in(0,1)$; a leaf spin is the root spin times the noises along its path. The transition probabilities are $(1+\theta_e)/2$ for no flip and $(1-\theta_e)/2$ for a flip, both positive. For pendant-edge means $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_4$, set $a=\theta_1\theta_2$, $b=\theta_3\theta_4$. Cancellation of shared path noises gives \[ E(X_1X_2)=a,\quad E(X_3X_4)=b,\quad E(X_1X_2X_3X_4)=ab. \] Thus the Fourier block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\a&ab\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has determinant zero. The internal-edge noise cancels in these moments.

Mix classes 1 and 2 with weights $\lambda,1-\lambda$. Linearity of moments gives determinant \[ \lambda a_1b_1+(1-\lambda)a_2b_2- (\lambda a_1+(1-\lambda)a_2)(\lambda b_1+(1-\lambda)b_2) =\lambda(1-\lambda)(a_1-a_2)(b_1-b_2). \] Take $\lambda=1/2$, all four pendant means $1/5$ in class 1 and $4/5$ in class 2, and internal mean $1/2$ in both. Then $(a_1,b_1)=(1/25,1/25)$ and $(a_2,b_2)=(16/25,16/25)$, and the determinant is $9/100$. These are strictly positive stochastic models, so the violation is not caused by complex or inadmissible parameters. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact rational calculation verified $9/100$. The failure is the invalid inference that a polynomial vanishing at two endpoints vanishes on their mixture segment. A $2\times2$ block supplies no $3\times3$ minors to repair the rank condition for two classes. This particular example does not compute a secant ideal or address other rate models, and may not satisfy a more restrictive common-rate parameterization unless that parameterization is specified. No general solution or novelty is claimed.
