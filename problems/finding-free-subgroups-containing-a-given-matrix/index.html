Math-15875

Finding Free Subgroups Containing a Given Matrix

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For notation, a linear group is a subgroup $G\leq \mathrm{GL}_n(K)$, where $K$ is a field and $n\geq 1$. A free basis of a subgroup $H\leq G$ is a subset $B\subseteq H$ that generates $H$ and for which every nonempty reduced word in the formal letters $b^{\pm1}$, $b\in B$, represents a nonidentity element. A subgroup is free if it has such a basis.

The supplied problem asks for an algorithm that, given a linear group $G$ and a matrix $M\in G$, decides whether \[ \exists H\leq G:\qquad M\in H,\qquad H\neq\{1\},\qquad H\text{ is free}, \] and, in an affirmative case, constructs a free basis $B$ of a witnessing subgroup $H$. Membership of $M$ in $H$ is required; the statement does not require $M$ to be a basis element or exclude $M=1$.

Unresolved qualifications. The displayed formulation reads "nontrivial free subgroup" literally, allowing free groups of rank one. If the intended requirement is a nonabelian free subgroup, the condition must instead require a free basis with at least two elements. The supplied record does not resolve this distinction. It also does not specify the coefficient field, the permitted dimensions, how the group and its matrix entries are presented effectively, or how a basis is to be output. In particular, finite generation of the input group and finiteness of the output basis are not stated. These missing specifications prevent a fully determined algorithmic formulation.

Short English statement

Given a linear group and a matrix belonging to it, decide whether some nontrivial free subgroup contains that matrix and, if so, produce a free basis of such a subgroup. The meaning of nontrivial and the effective input and output conventions require clarification. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
