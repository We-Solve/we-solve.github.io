Math-14805

Improved Error Rates for QMC Slicing

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $m\in{\mathbb{N}}$ denote the number of directions used in a sliced approximation. For the sole purpose of describing the requested comparison, write $S$ for the kernel sum being approximated, $S_m^{\mathrm{QMC}}$ for its sliced approximation using a quasi-Monte Carlo design, and $S_m^{\mathrm{ind}}$ for the corresponding approximation using independent random directions. These are schematic labels: the supplied record does not define the kernel sum, the slicing construction, the direction space, the quasi-Monte Carlo designs, or the distributions of the independent directions.

Requested problem. Establish rigorous convergence-rate estimates, as $m\to\infty$, explaining the improved errors observed numerically for $S_m^{\mathrm{QMC}}$ compared with $S_m^{\mathrm{ind}}$.

Unresolved formulation. The record supplies neither a numerical rate nor an error criterion. In particular, it does not specify whether the comparison concerns deterministic error, expected error, mean-square error, a probability bound, or another quantity. It also omits the admissible kernels, data configurations, dimensions, regularity assumptions, and permitted dependence of constants. Consequently, this request cannot yet be expressed as a complete quantified theorem without additional source information. The notation above introduces no assumptions filling these gaps.

The supplied registry labels the problem open and reports that the primary account leaves the proof open, while later work presents the improvement as numerical evidence. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

The requested result is a proof of the numerically observed convergence improvement obtained by replacing independent random directions with quasi-Monte Carlo designs in sliced kernel-sum approximations. The supplied record leaves the construction, error measure, hypotheses, and target rates unspecified, so a precise theorem remains undetermined.
