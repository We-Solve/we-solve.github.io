Functional Differential Equations for Many-Body Green Functions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An interacting one-body Green function is a time-ordered or otherwise conventionally ordered two-point correlation function of creation and annihilation fields. The Dyson equation writes $G=G_0+G_0\Sigma G$, with convolution in the space, spin and time variables and self-energy $\Sigma$. A functional differential equation treats $G$ as a functional of an added external potential and differentiates with respect to that potential, thereby encoding higher correlations without assuming a known self-energy.

How can the interacting Green functions of many-body quantum systems be obtained from a functional differential equation rather than the usual Dyson integral equation?
Source scope and qualification.
The source's ordering, contour and external-field convention fix the functional equation. Producing formal perturbative derivatives alone does not construct the desired nonperturbative interacting Green function.

A nonperturbative scalar analogue.
For real $J$, $a>0$, $\lambda>0$, define \[ Z(J)=\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(-ax^2/2-\lambda x^4/4+Jx)\,dx. \] The quartic decay dominates every polynomial times the integrand on compact $J$-intervals, justifying differentiation and integration by parts. Integrating the derivative in $x$ gives the exact equation \[ \lambda Z^{(3)}(J)+aZ'(J)-JZ(J)=0. \] Since $Z>0$, its normalized one-point function $g=Z'/Z$ obeys \[ \lambda(g''+3gg'+g^3)+ag-J=0. \] This follows by twice differentiating $Z'=gZ$. Thus an external-source differential equation can encode interaction nonperturbatively without inserting a self-energy.
The selection gap.
The linear third-order equation requires three initial values; evenness gives $Z'(0)=0$, and normalization fixes one scale, but $Z''(0)/Z(0)$ still depends on the physical integral. The equation alone does not determine it. As $\lambda\to0$, the equation loses order and becomes $aZ'=JZ$, so perturbative continuity is a singular selection problem rather than an arbitrary initial-value prescription.
