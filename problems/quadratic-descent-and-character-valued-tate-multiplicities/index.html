Math-1263

Quadratic descent and character-valued Tate multiplicities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a variety $X$ over a number field $k$, a Tate class in a chosen degree and twist means a class in $H^{2r}_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{\overline k},{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(r))$ fixed after some finite extension of $k$; equivalently one considers the union of invariant spaces for open Galois subgroups. For a nonproper Hilbert modular variety one must specify ordinary, interior, intersection, or compactification cohomology and boundary contributions. The source does not do so.

Original Problem 3 (J. Getz). Let $X$ be a Hilbert modular variety, whose cohomology is described heuristically as resembling a product of modular-curve cohomologies. Under what circumstances does it have Tate classes not coming from modular subvarieties? In the automorphic submotive or Galois-representation interpretation, must these exotic classes arise from CM automorphic forms, as in the workshop's Question 2? The relevant modular subvarieties, their cycle-class span, and the content of Question 2 are not supplied. Thus "exotic" requires a specified comparison subspace, not merely the existence of Tate classes.

Attached unreviewed literature account. The aid attributes to Getz--Hahn results for a cohomological nondihedral eigensystem on a Galois Hilbert modular variety, with sufficiently large $\ell$. It says the eventual Tate Hecke rank is zero when the projective-conjugacy stabilizer has odd order $m$, and a Catalan power when $m$ is even; a Tate line forces quadratic base-change descent after twisting; and rank-at-most-one Tate spaces are generated by twisted Hirzebruch--Zagier cycles. The summary gives no exact rank exponent, stabilizer definition, field and level hypotheses, or publication locator, so this is not a complete theorem statement. It does not identify every nonmodular class with a CM automorphic form.

The aid separately proposes a character-valued exact-base-change lemma. Let $L/F$ be Galois of degree $2n$, let $\rho_0$ be a two-dimensional characteristic-zero Galois representation in the intended full-$\mathrm{SL}_2$ monodromy setting, and form the tensor-induced representation $\operatorname{As}_{L/F}(\rho_0|_{G_L})$. After the full determinant normalization, represented in the tensor model by twisting by $(\det\rho_0)^{-n}$, the aid identifies its Tate multiplicity space with \[\operatorname{Sp}^{(n,n)}\big|_{\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\hookrightarrow S_{2n}},\] where $\operatorname{Sp}^{(n,n)}$ is the characteristic-zero Specht module and the embedding is the regular permutation action on the $2n$ tensor factors. Its dimension is the Catalan number $\binom{2n}{n}/(n+1)$ in this tensor model. Precise monodromy, tensor-induction sign, Galois and normalization hypotheses require review.

For degree four, the stated restrictions are \[\operatorname{Sp}^{(2,2)}|_{C_4}\cong\mathbf1\oplus\chi_2,\qquad\operatorname{Sp}^{(2,2)}|_{V_4}\cong\mathbf1^{\oplus2},\] where $\chi_2$ is the unique nontrivial quadratic character of $C_4$. This refines rank to fixed-character multiplicities. The aid expressly constructs no new algebraic cycle and gives no general CM classification; representation-theoretic multiplicities alone do not supply those missing conclusions.

Short English statement

Getz asks when Hilbert modular varieties have Tate classes beyond modular-subvariety classes, and whether CM forms explain them. The aid reports qualified Getz--Hahn results and an exact-base-change Specht-module refinement, including the different cyclic and Klein-four character decompositions. It supplies neither new cycles nor a general answer to the CM question.
