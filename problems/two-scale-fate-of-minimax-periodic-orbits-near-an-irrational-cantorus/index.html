Math-5387

Two-scale fate of minimax periodic orbits near an irrational cantorus

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Walter Craig's question concerns an area-preserving annulus twist map: for rational rotation $p/q$ there are minimal and minimax periodic Birkhoff orbits; as $p_j/q_j\to\omega\notin{\mathbb{Q}}$, the source describes convergence of minimal orbits to the Mather set $\Pi_\omega$, and convergence of minimax orbits to the same set when it is an invariant circle. It asks for the fate of minimax orbits when $\Pi_\omega$ is a Cantor set. The topology of convergence and the selection/centering of minimax representatives are not specified.

For the aid's variational reconstruction, suppose the twist map admits a suitable smooth generating function $S(x,y)$, periodic under $(x,y)\mapsto(x+1,y+1)$, with the usual monotone-twist and coercivity hypotheses. A lifted $(p,q)$ configuration satisfies $x_{i+q}=x_i+p$ and has total action \[ A_{p,q}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}S(x_i,x_{i+1}). \] Minimal configurations minimize this action, while minimax configurations are the corresponding mountain-pass critical configurations; the precise minimax class is not given in the source. A Birkhoff configuration is ordered with each of its integer translates $(x_{i+k}+\ell)_i$.

The aid attributes to Mather's classical irrational minimax construction a gap orbit homoclinic to the cantorus: an orbit outside $\Pi_\omega$ whose distance to $\Pi_\omega$ tends to zero in both time directions. This is a gap defect, not an assertion that an additional minimizing Cantor set is produced. Full hypotheses of that construction are not supplied.

Its reported two-scale refinement assumes periods $q_j\to\infty$, rational rotations approaching $\omega$, and total action excess \[ \Delta A_j=A_{p_j,q_j}(x^{\mathrm{mm},j})- \min A_{p_j,q_j}=o(q_j). \] A bounded finite action barrier is a sufficient special case of this sublinear assumption. Under the needed orbit compactness and generating-function hypotheses, the empirical measures \[ \mu_j=\frac1{q_j}\sum_{i=0}^{q_j-1}\delta_{f^i(z_j)} \] converge weakly to the unique irrational Mather minimizing measure. Nevertheless defect-centered, integer-normalized lifted configurations can have subsequential limits that are stationary Birkhoff configurations outside the minimizing set. Stationarity means \[ \partial_2S(x_{i-1},x_i)+\partial_1S(x_i,x_{i+1})=0\quad(i\in{\mathbb{Z}}). \] Measure convergence therefore does not determine every pointed configuration limit.

If the rational minimax critical points are Morse of index one, the aid states that every finite-window, fixed-boundary Hessian of a pointed limit has negative index at most one. If, on one fixed finite window, normalized perturbations retain quadratic form at most $-\delta$ for some $\delta>0$ along the sequence, their limit supplies a negative direction. The pointed limit is then non-minimizing and lies outside the cantorus. No such persistent negative mode is asserted for every sequence; without it a pointed limit may be minimizing.

Short English statement

The source asks what replaces circle convergence of minimax orbits when the irrational Mather set is a cantorus. The aid distinguishes a gap homoclinic defect from the limiting empirical measure: a localized defect can survive after centering even while its relative weight in a long periodic orbit vanishes.
