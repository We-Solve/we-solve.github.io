Banach Spaces and Ramsey Theory — Problem 16

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write ${\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. For $f,g\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}$, put $f\leq^{*}g$ if $f(n)\leq g(n)$ for all but finitely many $n$. The bounding number is \[ \mathfrak b=\min\left\{|B|:B\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}},\ (\forall g\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}})(\exists f\in B)\ f\not\leq^{*}g\right\}. \] Here $\aleph_2$ denotes the second uncountable cardinal.

For a set $S$, let $[S]^{\leq\aleph_0}$ denote its finite and countably infinite subsets. An ideal $\mathcal I\subseteq[S]^{\leq\aleph_0}$ contains every finite subset of $S$ and is closed under taking subsets and finite unions. It is a $P$-ideal if every sequence $(A_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ in $\mathcal I$ has an $A\in\mathcal I$ such that $A_n\setminus A$ is finite for every $n$. The $P$-ideal dichotomy (PID) asserts that, for every set $S$ and every such $P$-ideal $\mathcal I$, at least one of the following holds:

  1. There is an uncountable $T\subseteq S$ such that $[T]^{\leq\aleph_0}\subseteq\mathcal I$.
  2. There are sets $S_n\subseteq S$, for $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$, with $S=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}S_n$, such that $S_n\cap A$ is finite for every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and every $A\in\mathcal I$.

Let $\mathbb K\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}$ be a scalar field. A Banach space over $\mathbb K$ is a complete normed vector space over $\mathbb K$; it is nonseparable if it has no countable norm-dense subset. Write $X^*$ for its continuous $\mathbb K$-linear dual. An uncountable biorthogonal system in $X$ is a family $(x_\alpha,x_\alpha^*)_{\alpha\in A}$, where $A$ is uncountable, $x_\alpha\in X$, $x_\alpha^*\in X^*$, and \[ x_\alpha^*(x_\beta)=\begin{cases}1,&\alpha=\beta,\\0,&\alpha\ne\beta\end{cases} \qquad(\alpha,\beta\in A). \] Let $\mathcal B_{\mathbb K}$ be the assertion that every nonseparable Banach space over $\mathbb K$ admits such a system.

The supplied Problem 16 asks: assuming PID, are the following assertions equivalent? \[ \mathcal B_{\mathbb K}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathfrak b=\aleph_2. \] The supplied source record does not specify whether its Banach spaces are real or complex. The parameter $\mathbb K$ makes this unresolved convention explicit; it does not assert that the source separately asks the question for both fields.

Short English statement

Assuming the $P$-ideal dichotomy, is the assertion that every nonseparable Banach space has an uncountable biorthogonal system equivalent to $\mathfrak b=\aleph_2$? The supplied record leaves the scalar field unspecified and labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
