Optimal Great-Subsphere Concentration for the Negative-$p$ Aleksandrov Problem

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p\in{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfy $p\leq -1$. Write $S^{n-1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\|x\|_2=1\}$. A proper great subsphere has the form $\xi_L=S^{n-1}\cap L$, where $L$ is a nonzero proper linear subspace of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. The supplied record does not specify the allowed ambient dimensions or whether every dimension of proper great subsphere is intended. Whenever $\mu$ is a measure defined on these sets and $0<\mu(S^{n-1})<\infty$, define its concentration on $\xi_L$ by \[ r_\mu(L)=\frac{\mu(S^{n-1}\cap L)}{\mu(S^{n-1})}. \] The positivity and finiteness condition here makes the displayed ratio meaningful; the full measure hypotheses of the cited theorem are not supplied.

Problem as supplied. For $p\leq -1$, determine the optimal upper restrictions on $r_\mu(L)$, over the intended great subspheres, that guarantee the existence of a convex body whose $L_p$ integral curvature measure is $\mu$. In particular, the original passage asks whether the measure concentration bound in Theorem 2 is optimal.

For notation only, if $J_p(K,\cdot)$ denotes the intended $L_p$ integral curvature measure, the requested sufficient condition is to imply \[ \exists\,K\quad J_p(K,\cdot)=\mu \] for every measure $\mu$ satisfying the intended hypotheses. Neither the definition and normalization of $J_p$, the admissible class of convex bodies $K$, nor the hypotheses and concentration bound of Theorem 2 are included in the supplied record. Consequently, this is an explicitly incomplete formulation: it does not specify a numerical candidate bound, its dependence on $n,p,\dim L$, or whether the concentration inequalities are strict. A fully quantified sharp-threshold statement requires the omitted theorem and its definitions.

Short English statement

The problem asks how much of a measure may lie on great subspheres while still guaranteeing that it is the $L_p$ integral curvature measure of a convex body, for $p\leq -1$. The original target is the optimality of a bound in Theorem 2, whose statement and supporting definitions are absent. The supplied open-status assessment is dated curation, not an independently verified conclusion.
