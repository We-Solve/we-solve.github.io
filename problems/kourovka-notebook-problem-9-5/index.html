Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.5

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the language of groups, with multiplication, inversion, and the identity element. A group identity is a universally quantified equation between group terms. A variety of groups is the class of all groups satisfying some set of group identities.

A group quasi-identity is a universally quantified implication of the form \[ \forall x_1,\ldots,x_n\quad \left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m} u_i(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=v_i(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\right) \Longrightarrow u(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=v(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \] where all displayed expressions are group terms and the conjunction is finite; the case $m=0$ is allowed. A quasivariety of groups is the class of all groups satisfying some set of group quasi-identities. A subquasivariety of a variety $\mathcal V$ is a quasivariety $\mathcal Q$ with $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal V$.

A variety $\mathcal V$ is called primitive if every subquasivariety $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal V$ is itself a variety of groups. A group is locally finite if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite. A variety is locally finite if each of its groups is locally finite.

Problem (V. A. Gorbunov, 1984; Kourovka Notebook 9.5). Describe all primitive varieties of groups. In particular, determine whether every primitive variety of groups is locally finite; equivalently, whether \[ \forall\mathcal V\;\left[\mathcal V\text{ is a primitive variety of groups}\ \Longrightarrow\ \forall G\in\mathcal V\;\forall S\subseteq G\; \left(|S|<\infty\Longrightarrow |\langle S\rangle|<\infty\right)\right]. \] The supplied registry labels this problem open; that label is supplied source metadata and is not an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

Classify the varieties of groups for which every contained quasivariety is a variety. Determine also whether every such variety consists entirely of groups whose finitely generated subgroups are finite.
