Compressing a leaf-root certificate

The historical report asks about recognition for fixed $k\ge5$. Subsequent primary literature states a polynomial-time algorithm for each fixed $k$, with a very large dependence on $k$, and separately rules out a finite forbidden-induced-subgraph characterization even within strongly chordal graphs for $k\ge5$. These are attributed literature results; neither proof is reproduced here. We investigate the simpler certificate-enumeration route and its precise limitation.
Proposition: For a graph on $n\ge2$ vertices and an integer $k\ge1$, existence of a unit-edge $k$-leaf root implies existence of a weighted-tree certificate with the same labeled leaves, at most $n-2$ other vertices, at most $2n-3$ edges, and integer edge lengths in $\{1,\ldots,k+1\}$. The converse also holds after subdividing weighted edges. The certificate uses $O(n\log n+n\log(k+1))$ bits and can be verified in polynomial time in this size.
Proof: In a unit-edge root, suppress every degree-two nonleaf vertex, replacing its incident path by one edge whose length is that path's positive integer length. Leaf distances are unchanged. Every remaining nonleaf has degree at least three. If there are $b$ nonleaves, the degree sum gives $n+3b\le2(n+b-1)$, hence $b\le n-2$ and the number of edges $n+b-1$ is at most $2n-3$. Replace each edge length $w$ by $\min(w,k+1)$. A leaf-to-leaf path containing a length greater than $k$ remains longer than $k$ afterward, because that edge alone still has length $k+1$. Every path containing only lengths at most $k$ is unchanged. Thus the distance-at-most-$k$ relation is preserved. Conversely subdividing each weighted edge into a path of its integer length realizes the same distances with unit edges and introduces no extra leaves. Encode the topology using $O(n)$ edges with vertex labels of $O(\log n)$ bits, and encode the $O(n)$ bounded lengths with $O(\log(k+1))$ bits each. Pairwise leaf distances can be summed in binary along the unique paths and compared with $k$; they have at most $O(\log n+\log(k+1))$ bits. Checking every pair and the tree structure is therefore polynomial in the certificate size, without expanding long edges. ∎
For $n=1$, the sole graph is handled separately using the conventional one-vertex tree as a trivial leaf root; this boundary case is not needed by the suppression count. The problem's actual $k\ge5$ lies inside the proposition's positive-integer range.

Verification and remaining gaps

For a weighted star with arm lengths $1,2,6,7$ and $k=5$, capping at 6 preserves exactly the same leaf adjacencies: only the first two leaves are adjacent. All six pair distances were checked. Enumeration of the compressed trees still permits exponentially many topologies and length assignments, so the step from a short verifiable witness to a polynomial recognition algorithm is invalid. The later fixed-$k$ algorithm is not derived by this argument. No new recognition bound, uniform parameter dependence or forbidden-structure theorem is established.
