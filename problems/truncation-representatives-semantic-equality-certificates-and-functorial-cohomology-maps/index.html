Verbatim supplied catalogue question

M2 currently has sheaves on projective varieties, but no morphisms between them, and no way to compute the associated maps on cohomology.

What is the best way to implement a "morphism of sheaves" data type? What's the best way to calculate the maps on sheaf cohomology induced by such a morphism on a projective variety?

Attempt and results

The original software request is historically superseded in the supplied record, official report, and current SheafMap documentation. We do not claim a new implementation. Instead we test two mathematical requirements of an interface: equality must respect sheafification, and induced maps must act on cohomology classes rather than arbitrary stored representatives.
A finite certificate for vanishing of a represented map.
Let $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/J$ with $J$ homogeneous, $B=(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$, and let $f:M\to N$ be a degree-zero map of finitely generated graded $S$-modules. Write $L=\operatorname{im}f$. On the standard open set $D_+(x_i)$, sheafification is obtained from the degree-zero part of localization. Thus the associated sheaf map is zero exactly when every localized homogeneous element of $L$ is zero. To justify the degree-zero qualification, if $l$ has degree $d$, then $x_i^{-d}l$ has degree zero in the localized module; multiplying by the unit $x_i^d$ recovers $l$. Consequently vanishing is equivalent to $L_{x_i}=0$ for every $i$. Choose finite homogeneous generators $l_1,\ldots,l_s$ of $L$. Localization vanishes precisely when, for every $i,j$, some power of $x_i$ kills $l_j$: this is the defining zero criterion for fractions. Taking a maximum over $j$ gives a positive integer $a_i$ with $x_i^{a_i}L=0$. Every monomial in $B^t$, for $t=1+\sum_{i=0}^n(a_i-1)$, has some exponent at least $a_i$ by the pigeonhole principle. Hence $B^t L=0$. Conversely $B^tL=0$ implies $x_i^t L=0$, so all those localizations vanish. We have proved \[ \widetilde f=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B^t\operatorname{im}f=0\text{ for some }t\ge0. \] For two maps on a common module presentation, apply this to their difference. For maps with truncated sources, first restrict to a common truncation. The inclusion $M_{\ge r}\subseteq M$ sheafifies to an isomorphism: after localizing at $x_i$, multiply any homogeneous element by enough powers of $x_i$ to put it in degree at least $r$, then divide by that unit. This proves that restriction is semantically legitimate. Maps with different module presentations additionally require explicit transports; matrix equality alone is not an invariant comparison.
A complete Cech calculation for a family of induced maps.
On $\mathbb P^1_k$ with homogeneous coordinates $[x:y]$, put $z=x/y$. Using the trivialization $y^d$ of $\mathcal O(d)$ on $y\ne0$, the two-chart Cech complex has degree-one space $k[z,z^{-1}]$ and degree-zero image \[ k[z]+z^d k[z^{-1}]. \] The exponent sets in these two summands are respectively $j\ge0$ and $j\le d$. Therefore its first cohomology has basis the classes of $z^j$ with $d<j<0$; for $d\ge-1$ it is zero. The kernel describing degree-zero cohomology is the intersection $k[z]\cap z^d k[z^{-1}]$, with basis $1,z,\ldots,z^d$ if $d\ge0$ and zero otherwise. These are direct computations of the displayed complex. Using the usual acyclicity of this affine cover identifies it with sheaf cohomology; that standard geometric identification is a stated dependency, not reproved here.

For a homogeneous polynomial $h(x,y)$ of degree $e\ge0$, multiplication $\mathcal O(d)\to\mathcal O(d+e)$ acts in the chosen trivialization by $H(z)=h(z,1)$. It respects the quotient above: $H(z)k[z]\subseteq k[z]$, and each monomial $z^a$, $0\le a\le e$, takes $z^d k[z^{-1}]$ into $z^{d+e}k[z^{-1}]$, since its resulting exponents are at most $d+a\le d+e$. Thus multiply a basis Laurent monomial by $H$, then discard exponents outside $d+e<j<0$ to compute the induced map. For $d=-3$, $e=1$, $h=x$, the source basis is $(z^{-2},z^{-1})$, the target basis is $(z^{-1})$, and the induced matrix is $(1\ \ 0)$: $z^{-2}\mapsto z^{-1}$ while $z^{-1}\mapsto1$, a boundary. Keeping the raw product $1$ as an extra target coordinate would be incorrect.
