Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.37

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group, with no finiteness assumption on $G$. Use the following standard group-theoretic conventions. The group $G$ is locally graded if every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup $H\leq G$ has a proper subgroup $U<H$ with $[H:U]<\infty$. It has finite rank if there exists an integer $r\geq 0$ such that every finitely generated subgroup of $G$ can be generated by at most $r$ elements.

For a group $H$, define its derived series by $H^{(0)}=H$ and $H^{(n+1)}=[H^{(n)},H^{(n)}]$, where $[K,K]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in K$. A group is soluble if its derived series reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps. A group $L$ is locally soluble if every finitely generated subgroup of $L$ is soluble. Interpret almost locally soluble as having a locally soluble subgroup of finite index.

Problem (M. Dixon; described in the supplied source as a well-known problem). Is every locally graded group of finite rank almost locally soluble? Explicitly, for every group $G$ satisfying the first two definitions, must there exist $L\leq G$ such that \[ [G:L]<\infty \quad\text{and}\quad \forall\,H\leq L\text{ finitely generated},\quad \exists\,d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\text{ such that }H^{(d)}=\{1\}? \] The integer $d$ may depend on $H$; no uniform bound on derived lengths is required. The supplied record does not define the technical terms, so the conventions stated above require confirmation against the original source. Its label open is supplied registry metadata, not an independently verified mathematical assessment.

Short English statement

Does every locally graded group whose finitely generated subgroups have a uniform bound on their numbers of generators contain a locally soluble subgroup of finite index? Here locally soluble means that each finitely generated subgroup is soluble, with its own possible derived length. The technical conventions and supplied open status require human review.
