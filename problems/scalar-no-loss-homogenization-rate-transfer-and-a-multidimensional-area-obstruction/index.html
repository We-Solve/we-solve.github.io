Scalar no-loss homogenization rate transfer and a multidimensional area obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 32 concerns deterministic homogenization for the reconstructed slow--fast equations \[ \dot x_\varepsilon=a(x_\varepsilon,y_\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{-1}b(x_\varepsilon)v(y_\varepsilon),\qquad \dot y_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}g(y_\varepsilon),\qquad \int v\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu=0. \] Here $\mu$ is interpreted as an invariant probability measure for the fast flow $\phi_s$ generated by $g$, and $y_\varepsilon(0)$ has law $\mu$. The source asks whether multiple-correlation estimates at a given rate imply convergence of $x_\varepsilon$ to a diffusion, written formally ${\,\mathrm{d}} X=\bar a(X){\,\mathrm{d}} t+b(X)*{\,\mathrm{d}}\omega$. The scale parameter, convergence notation and measure symbol are corrupted in the extraction, and the star integral, dimensions, regularity, initial law and correlation hypotheses are not specified. The following is the aid's conditional scalar product-noise result, not a general theorem derived from the unspecified correlation rate.

Take scalar $x$, scalar $v$, a fixed time horizon $[0,T]$ and deterministic initial point $x_0$. Let $b$ be sufficiently smooth, nowhere zero and bounded, with $b^*=\sup_x|b(x)|<\infty$, and assume the required equations are well posed. Define \[ \bar a(x)=\int a(x,y)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(y),\qquad h(x)=\int_{x_0}^x\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}} u}{b(u)},\qquad A(z)=\frac{\bar a(h^{-1}(z))}{b(h^{-1}(z))}. \] Assume $A$ is globally Lipschitz with constant $L$. Since continuous nonvanishing $b$ has fixed sign and is bounded, the Lamperti coordinate $h$ is a global monotone coordinate, and $h^{-1}$ is Lipschitz with constant $b^*$.

The integrated fast driver and feedback averaging remainder are \[ W_\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\int_0^{t/\varepsilon^2}v(\phi_sy_0)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s, \] \[ R_\varepsilon(t)=\int_0^t\frac{a(x_\varepsilon(s),y_\varepsilon(s))-\bar a(x_\varepsilon(s))}{b(x_\varepsilon(s))}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s. \] The transformed path satisfies the exact identity \[ h(x_\varepsilon(t))=h(x_0)+\int_0^tA(h(x_\varepsilon(s)))\,{\,\mathrm{d}} s+W_\varepsilon(t)+R_\varepsilon(t). \] The remainder depends on the slow path; it is not an independent externally averaged error.

Fix $p\ge1$. Let $\mathcal W_p$ be path-space Wasserstein distance for the uniform norm, and use the sum of the two uniform norms as the metric on pairs of paths. If \[ \mathcal W_p\left(\mathcal L(W_\varepsilon,R_\varepsilon),\mathcal L(\sigma B,0)\right)\le r_\varepsilon, \] where $B$ is standard Brownian motion and $\sigma^2\ge0$, then the aid's no-loss transfer is \[ \mathcal W_p(\mathcal L(x_\varepsilon),\mathcal L(X))\le b^*e^{LT}r_\varepsilon. \] Thus a given rate exponent for the joint driver and remainder is preserved. This is a joint-law input, not two unrelated marginal estimates, and finite Wasserstein moments are part of its meaning.

The limiting scalar diffusion has the Stratonovich form \[ {\,\mathrm{d}} X=\bar a(X)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t+\sigma b(X)\circ{\,\mathrm{d}} B, \] or equivalently the Itô form \[ {\,\mathrm{d}} X=\left(\bar a(X)+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}b(X)b'(X)\right){\,\mathrm{d}} t+\sigma b(X){\,\mathrm{d}} B. \] The correction fixes the meaning of the source's star only in this scalar setting.

For at least two noise components, the aid reports an opposite-area vanishing-loop obstruction. Smooth driving paths can converge uniformly to the same first-level path while their signed iterated areas converge with opposite signs. With noncommuting driving vector fields the resulting equations then acquire opposite Lie-bracket drifts. Therefore first-level path convergence alone does not determine the limiting integral or drift in the multidimensional problem; second-level iterated integrals and their convergence must also be specified. The particular loop formulas, area constants and vector fields are not printed in the supplied summary.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a quantitative diffusion limit from correlation estimates for a slow--fast system. In a scalar product-noise case, the aid transfers a joint driver-and-averaging-error Wasserstein rate directly to the slow path, with explicit constant $b^*e^{LT}$. It identifies the scalar limit as Stratonovich, while explaining why multidimensional noise also requires area information.
