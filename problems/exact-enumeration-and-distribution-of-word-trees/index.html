Exact Enumeration and Distribution of Word Trees

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks two questions about $w$-trees and random functions associated with words, but does not define these objects or their probability model. Consequently, the following is a schematic formulation requiring the missing definitions.

For a word $W$ and its associated function $w$, let $\mathcal T_{w,n}$ provisionally denote the intended class of $w$-trees of size $n$. This notation does not specify what a $w$-tree is, how size is measured, or whether objects are labelled or counted up to isomorphism. Whenever this class is finite, write \[ a_w(n)=\lvert\mathcal T_{w,n}\rvert. \] The enumeration question is to determine these numbers exactly, by an explicit formula, a recurrence with sufficient initial conditions, or a functional equation that determines them. The permitted words, the admissible sizes, and the dependence of the class on $W$ remain unspecified.

For the distribution question, suppose the intended random functions associated with two distinct words $W$ and $W'$, at size $n$, have laws $\mu_{W,n}$ and $\mu_{W',n}$ on a common measurable space $(\Omega_n,\mathcal F_n)$. Define their total-variation distance by \[ d_n(W,W')=\sup_{A\in\mathcal F_n} \left|\mu_{W,n}(A)-\mu_{W',n}(A)\right|. \] Determine conditions on $W$ and $W'$ under which, as the intended size parameter tends to infinity, \[ d_n(W,W')\longrightarrow 0, \qquad d_n(W,W')\longrightarrow 1, \] or neither limit holds. Here "neither" leaves open both convergence to another value and failure to converge; the source does not distinguish these possibilities. The record does not specify the alphabet, word-evaluation rule, distribution of the underlying random maps, their dependence, the function domains and codomains, or whether the words are fixed as $n$ varies. Thus the displayed laws and limits cannot yet be instantiated as a fully specified mathematical problem.

The original excerpt continues into a separately headed problem about words in random permutations, attributed there to Valentin Féray. Short English statement

Count the intended $w$-trees of each size exactly, and characterize when random functions associated with two distinct words become close in total variation, become asymptotically separated, or exhibit neither behavior.
