Curved-Waveguide Simplicity and Surface-Curvature Optimization

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record combines two separate problems. Its waveguide formulation is reconstructed, while the quoted original passage begins with remarks referring to an omitted preceding question.

For the waveguide problem, let $H$ denote the self-adjoint operator associated with an admissible curved waveguide generated by a curve $\Gamma$, with confinement by a potential $V$ in the soft case or by a Dirichlet boundary condition in the hard-wall case. The supplied record does not specify the differential expression, ambient dimension, operator domain, admissible geometry, or hypotheses on $V$. Consequently, the admissible family of operators remains unresolved. Write $\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(H)$ for the isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity. When $H$ has a ground-state eigenvalue $E_0=\inf\sigma(H)$, the question in the reconstructed statement is whether every admissible $H$ satisfies \[ \forall\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(H),\qquad \lambda>E_0\ \Longrightarrow\ \dim\ker(H-\lambda I)=1. \] This expresses simplicity of each discrete eigenvalue strictly above the ground state; it does not assert simplicity of the ground state. A precise universal conjecture requires the missing definition of the admissible waveguides. The supplied excerpt states that suitable choices of $\Gamma$ and $V$ can produce any finite number of eigenvalues, that the question remains open for hard-wall waveguides, and that the quasi-one-dimensional restriction is essential because replacing the generating curve by a graph can yield nonsimple spectrum. These are supplied source qualifications, not independently verified assertions.

For the surface problem, fix an area $A>0$ and an integer $g\geq1$. Let $\mathcal S_{A,g}$ denote the admissible connected compact smooth embedded surfaces $\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ with \[ \operatorname{Area}(\Sigma)=A,\qquad \operatorname{genus}(\Sigma)=g. \] The source does not explicitly specify whether surfaces must have empty boundary. That convention, and any conditions on an allowed boundary, must be fixed before $\mathcal S_{A,g}$ is fully defined. At a point $p\in\Sigma$, let $k_1(p),k_2(p)$ be the principal curvatures computed using a local unit normal, and define \[ H_\Sigma:=\|k_1-k_2\|_{L^\infty(\Sigma)} =\sup_{p\in\Sigma}|k_1(p)-k_2(p)|. \] The absolute difference is independent of the ordering of the principal curvatures and of reversal of the unit normal. Determine \[ m(A,g):=\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal S_{A,g}}H_\Sigma, \] obtain lower bounds for this infimum, decide whether it is attained, and characterize all attaining surfaces if they exist. The excerpt singles out the torus case $g=1$ as particularly interesting. It also says that $H_\Sigma$ occurs as the "size" of the essential spectrum of Dirac operators with special transmission conditions along $\Sigma$, referring to an unidentified reference [3]; neither those operators nor the meaning of "size" is supplied.

Short English statement

The reconstructed waveguide question asks whether every discrete eigenvalue above an existing ground state has multiplicity one, for both potential confinement and Dirichlet confinement. The admissible operators are not specified. Separately, for each fixed area and genus $g\geq1$, minimize the maximum absolute difference of the principal curvatures and describe minimizers, if any exist. The surface boundary convention remains unresolved.
