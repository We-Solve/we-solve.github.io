Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.68

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is a $2$-group if every element of $G$ has finite order equal to a power of $2$. It is finitely generated if $G=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_d\rangle$ for some positive integer $d$ and elements $g_1,\ldots,g_d\in G$. A group $H$ is locally finite if $\langle S\rangle$ is finite for every finite subset $S\subseteq H$. A $2$-group has finite exponent if there is an integer $e\geq 1$ such that $g^{2^e}=1$ for every $g\in G$.

The source asks whether there exists an infinite finitely generated $2$-group $G$ such that \[ \forall H\leq G,\qquad H\neq G\ \Longrightarrow\ \left(\forall S\subseteq H,\ |S|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ |\langle S\rangle|<\infty\right), \] with the parenthetical qualification "of finite exponent." Under the reading that this qualification is required, the question additionally requires \[ \exists e\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\ \forall g\in G,\qquad g^{2^e}=1. \] The supplied wording does not explicitly distinguish between requiring finite exponent throughout and asking both the unrestricted question and its finite-exponent strengthening. Both readings are therefore retained here for review.

Short English statement

Does an infinite finitely generated $2$-group exist whose every proper subgroup is locally finite? The source includes the parenthetical requirement "of finite exponent"; this may impose a uniform bound on element orders or indicate an additional strengthened question. Its intended scope requires review.
