In manuscript 1 (family 112; 2026-09-23), the asserted result is that one polynomial-time language eventually needs more than $2^{A\sqrt n}$ depth-three OR--AND--OR gates for every fixed $A$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
The claim constructs one language $L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ decidable by a fixed deterministic Turing machine in polynomial time. For its length-$n$ Boolean function $f_n$ and minimum size $S_3(f_n)$ of circuits with three successive unbounded-fan-in layers OR--AND--OR, literal inputs and optional bottom constants, and free input negations, it asserts \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2S_3(f_n)}{\sqrt n}=\infty, \quad\text{equivalently}\quad \forall A>0\ \exists N_A\ \forall n\ge N_A:\ S_3(f_n)>2^{A\sqrt n}. \] The circuit family may be nonuniform, and there is no bound on bottom fan-in. The size counts every AND or OR gate, including gates in the bottom layer and the output gate.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 112; 2026-09-23), a random restriction expresses moderately wide CNFs as signed combinations of constant-width CNFs with controlled total coefficient mass. A fixed hard-slice language has low correlation with those tests, so no bounded number of middle gates can cover its positive inputs.
