Math-6626

Smallest Solvable Group Excluded from Abelian-Type Skew Braces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A skew brace is a triple $(A,+,\circ)$ such that $(A,+)$ and $(A,\circ)$ are groups and \[ a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)+(-a)+(a\circ c) \qquad\text{for all }a,b,c\in A, \] where $-a$ denotes the inverse of $a$ in $(A,+)$. It is of abelian type if $(A,+)$ is abelian. Its multiplicative group is $(A,\circ)$.

A finite group $G$ is realizable if there exists a finite skew brace $(A,+,\circ)$ of abelian type with $(A,\circ)\cong G$. Thus realizability concerns group isomorphism, and necessarily $|A|=|G|$.

For a group $G$, define its derived series by $G^{(0)}=G$ and \[ G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}],\qquad n\geq 0, \] where $[H,H]$ is the subgroup generated by all $x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x,y\in H$. The group $G$ is solvable if $G^{(r)}=\{1\}$ for some integer $r\geq 0$.

Determine a finite solvable group $G$ such that $G$ is not realizable and \[ \text{for every finite solvable group }H,\qquad |H|<|G|\ \Longrightarrow\ H\text{ is realizable}. \] In particular, identifying a candidate requires establishing both its failure of realizability and the realizability of every smaller finite solvable group. The target is one group of minimum order, rather than a classification of all groups attaining that order. The original excerpt asks for a "minimal counterexample"; minimum order is the interpretation explicitly supplied by the cleaned statement.

Short English statement

Find a finite solvable group of the smallest possible order that cannot occur, up to isomorphism, as the multiplicative group of a skew brace whose additive group is abelian. Minimality requires every smaller finite solvable group to admit such a realization.
