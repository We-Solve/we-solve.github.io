Math-2248

Question I — s it possible that all nontrivial (i.e., ̸= 1 ) elements of a normal subgroup of ModS are pseudo-Anosov?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\operatorname{Mod}_S$ be the mapping class group of the surface $S$ in the source's convention. A pseudo-Anosov class preserves transverse measured foliations and scales them by reciprocal factors $\lambda>1$ and $\lambda^{-1}$. The intended existence question for a given $S$ is \[ \exists N\triangleleft\operatorname{Mod}_S:\quad N\ne\{1\}, \qquad\forall f\in N\setminus\{1\},\quad f\text{ is pseudo-Anosov}. \] Normality means $hNh^{-1}=N$ for every $h\in\operatorname{Mod}_S$. The nontriviality of $N$ is necessary to avoid the vacuous trivial-subgroup answer, but is not explicitly stated in the source. No finite-generation, freeness or finite-index condition is requested.

The source credits K. Whittlesey [Whi] with examples for spheres with at least five punctures and for closed genus-two surfaces, noting the latter's connection with the six-punctured sphere. It emphasizes the still-intended higher-genus closed-surface cases and expresses pessimism about extending that particular proof method; the pessimism is a source opinion, not a mathematical impossibility result. The exact surface range, orientation/puncture conventions and whether the question seeks existence for every remaining type are not specified. The known source examples must not be used to turn the remaining question into the already answered assertion that some surface admits such a group.

Short English statement

For the intended surface, does its mapping class group contain a nontrivial normal subgroup all of whose nonidentity elements are pseudo-Anosov, beyond the examples reported for punctured spheres and genus two?
