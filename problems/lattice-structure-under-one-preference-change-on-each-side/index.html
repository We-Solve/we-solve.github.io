Lattice structure under one preference change on each side

LLM status Open unverified

C78 — Bargaining Theory - Matching Theory Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra ultra 35% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

ID: GT-070. Category: Stable matching structure.  Kind: R. Status: Formalized structural agenda; the naive closure property is false.

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $W,F$ have the same finite cardinality. Let $A,B$ be strict complete preference profiles differing in at most one worker's ranking and at most one firm's ranking. A matching is stable when it has no pair whose two members strictly prefer each other to their assigned partners. Let \[ L=\mathcal M_A,\qquad R=\mathcal M_A\cap\mathcal M_B,\qquad D=\mathcal M_A\setminus\mathcal M_B. \] Order $L$ by unanimous weak worker preference in $A$, with a better matching smaller. Its meet assigns each worker the better of the two partners and its join the worse. The set $R$, when nonempty, is closed under both operations. For a subset $D\subseteq L$, define the closure conditions \[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{MeetClosed}(D)&:\ \forall x,y\in D,\quad x\wedge y\in D,\\ \mathrm{JoinClosed}(D)&:\ \forall x,y\in D,\quad x\vee y\in D. \end{aligned} \] A lattice isomorphism is a bijection preserving meet and join.
Formalized research target.
Characterize the finite pairs $(L,R)$, with $L$ a distributive lattice and $R\subseteq L$ either empty or a sublattice, that arise from such profiles, up to a lattice isomorphism carrying $R$ onto the designated subset. Seek intrinsic necessary and sufficient conditions, including conditions deciding when the complement $D=L\setminus R$ is meet-closed or join-closed.

Short English statement

What lattice structures can robust matchings and their complement have after one preference change on each side?

Variants and scope boundaries

This is a structural classification agenda, not a single conjectured closure theorem. The complement need not be a meet or join semisublattice. Computing a Birkhoff representation efficiently is the separate question in Entry game:GT-068.

Source relationship and status

[S1] gives a counterexample to the proposed semisublattice property and asks for a structural understanding. The intrinsic characterization above is an editorially chosen precise target, rather than a verbatim formal conjecture from the paper.

References

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction.
