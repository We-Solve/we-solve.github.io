Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.9

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems unknownunknown Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $g$ be a $C^\infty$ Riemannian metric on a neighborhood of a point $p$ in a smooth two-manifold. In local coordinates $(u^1,u^2)$ write $g=\sum_{i,j=1}^2g_{ij}\,du^i\,du^j$, with $(g_{ij})$ positive definite. A local smooth isometric embedding is a $C^\infty$ embedding $f:U\to\mathbb R^3$ on some open neighborhood $U$ of $p$ satisfying \[ \left\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial u^i}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial u^j}\right\rangle =g_{ij}\qquad(1\le i,j\le2). \] Does such a neighborhood and embedding exist for every smooth positive-definite metric and every point $p$?

The neighborhood may be shrunk as much as necessary; no global embedding is requested. There is no assumption that the Gauss curvature is nonzero, has a fixed sign, or vanishes to finite order. In particular, difficult behavior at flat points is part of the statement.

A theorem for analytic metrics or metrics with additional curvature hypotheses establishes only a special case. An immersion of rank two is locally an embedding after further restriction, so the local issue is the smooth solvability of the metric equations, not global self-intersection.

Short English statement

Can every smooth metric on a surface, including near degenerate zero-curvature points, be realized locally by a smooth surface in ordinary three-dimensional space?

Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.9

OpenAI's claim

A smooth three-space immersion obstruction: A smooth positive surface metric is claimed to have no isometric immersion into $\mathbb R^3$ on any neighborhood of the origin. All its derivatives at the origin nevertheless agree with the Euclidean metric.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
There exists a positive-definite smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $(-1,1)^2$ such that for every open neighborhood $U$ of $(0,0)$ there is no $C^\infty$ immersion $F:U\to\mathbb R^3$ satisfying \[F^*(dx_1^2+dx_2^2+dx_3^2)=g|_U.\] Consequently smooth local isometric embeddings also fail for this metric.

Outline of the argument

A smooth three-space immersion obstruction: The necessary Darboux equation for an immersion height is obstructed on a sequence of small square patches. A candidate height with an appropriate Hessian component would violate the patch estimate. The metric is arranged to produce such a height from any hypothetical local immersion, while the patch amplitudes vanish fast enough to preserve smooth flatness at the origin.
