Dynamics Near Generic Spacetime Singularities

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

The Belinski--Khalatnikov--Lifshitz picture describes local near-singularity evolution as successive approximately homogeneous Kasner epochs with transitions. Generic Einstein solutions may have spatial dependence and need not satisfy a symmetry reduction. A rigorous dynamical description must specify asymptotic variables, convergence and the class of singularities.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Give a rigorous dynamical description of generic solutions of the Einstein equations near spacetime singularities, including the extent to which Belinski–Khalatnikov–Lifshitz dynamics governs their asymptotic behavior.
Scope and source qualification.
This is a programme covering the extent and limitations of a heuristic, not a claim that every singularity obeys one prescribed BKL sequence.

Short English statement

Determine how well BKL dynamics describes generic Einstein solutions approaching spacetime singularities.

A homogeneous vacuum consistency calculation.
Consider the diagonal metric $g=-dt^2+\sum_{i=1}^3t^{2p_i}dx_i^2$, $t>0$, and write $S=\sum_i p_i$, $Q=\sum_i p_i^2$. Its nonzero basic connection coefficients are $\Gamma^t_{ii}=p_it^{2p_i-1}$ and $\Gamma^i_{ti}=p_i/t$. Substitution into the Ricci formula gives \[ R_{tt}=\frac{S-Q}{t^2},\qquad R_{ii}=p_i(S-1)t^{2p_i-2},\qquad R_{ij}=0\ (i\ne j). \] Thus the Kasner conditions $S=Q=1$ give a vacuum solution. If at least one $p_i\ne0$, the same equations force those conditions; the separate all-zero case is static flat spacetime.
Curvature countercheck.
In an orthonormal frame the squared curvature is \[ R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=\frac4{t^4}\left(\sum_i p_i^2(p_i-1)^2+\sum_{i<j}p_i^2p_j^2\right). \] The bracket vanishes for permutations of $(1,0,0)$, which are flat coordinate cases despite the degenerating metric coefficient. For $(-1/3,2/3,2/3)$ it is positive, proving a curvature singularity at $t=0$. These examples show why coordinate degeneration and curvature blowup must be distinguished.
BKL gap.
This family has no spatial derivative terms and no transitions between Kasner epochs. It therefore cannot establish generic BKL behavior. The first missing estimate is control of spatially inhomogeneous Einstein terms relative to velocity-dominated terms, including possible transition and exceptional regions. The source asks the extent and limitations of BKL asymptotics for a specified generic class, not merely existence of singular homogeneous examples.
