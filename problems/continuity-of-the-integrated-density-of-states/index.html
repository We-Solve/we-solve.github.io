Definitions and notation.
For finite-volume restrictions $H_\Lambda$ of a lattice or continuum Schrödinger operator, the integrated density of states is a thermodynamic limit of $|\Lambda|^{-1}$ times the number of eigenvalues at most $E$, with an appropriate volume normalization. Denote that distribution function by $k(E)$. Continuity means that the limiting spectral counting measure has no atom at any energy.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Prove continuity in energy of the integrated density of states $k(E)$ in the higher-dimensional continuum setting targeted by Simon. The source explicitly says that the one-dimensional and discrete cases were already known when this question was posed.

Free continuum density of states.
On a cube of side $L$ with periodic boundary conditions, $-\Delta$ has eigenvalues $|2\pi n/L|^2$, $n\in\mathbb Z^d$. For $E\ge0$, its counting function per volume is \[k_L(E)=L^{-d}\#\{n:|n|\le L\sqrt E/(2\pi)\}. \] Cover each lattice point by a unit cube. A ball of radius $R-\sqrt d/2$ is contained in the union of cubes whose centers lie in the radius-$R$ ball, and that union lies in the radius-$R+\sqrt d/2$ ball. Dividing their volumes by $L^d$ and taking $L\to\infty$ proves \[k(E)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_d)}{(2\pi)^d}(E_+)^{d/2},\quad E_+=\max(E,0). \] In particular the continuum benchmark is continuous, including at zero. The possible zero eigenvalue in each finite torus contributes only $L^{-d}$.
Why atom-freeness does not pass through a bare limit.
Each finite-volume counting measure is discrete. Even replacing it by a smooth approximation and proving atom-freeness would not suffice: the probability densities $f_n(E)=n\mathbf1_{[0,1/n]}(E)$ are absolutely continuous but converge weakly to $\delta_0$. Thus convergence of measures alone cannot establish continuity of the limiting distribution. A sufficient estimate would be a uniform modulus $k_L(E+\eta)-k_L(E-\eta)\le\omega(\eta)+o_L(1)$, where $\omega(\eta)\to0$. Taking the volume limit and then shrinking $\eta$ would exclude an atom at $E$.
Gap.
No such uniform small-interval estimate is proved for the higher-dimensional continuum potential class intended by Simon. The restored scope cannot be replaced by already-treated discrete or one-dimensional models, nor can unspecified operator hypotheses be ignored. The explicit free calculation and weak-limit counterexample identify a necessary type of argument but establish no new continuity theorem for the target class.
