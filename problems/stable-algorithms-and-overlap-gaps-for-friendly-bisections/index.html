Literature and context check

I read Gamarnik's primary contribution on pages 61--63 of the 2023 report. It defines the threshold as $(d_{\rm same}-d_{\rm opposite})/\sqrt n\geq h$ and distinguishes fully friendly from asymptotically friendly partitions. The report discusses an overlap gap for a stated interval of positive $h$ and leaves the broader transfer question open. The calculations below use this normalization, but do not define an absent universal model of "stable algorithm" or claim a barrier for every polynomial-time method.

Research attempt: Swap optimality, spectral necessity, and failure of an energy-only transfer

What local minimum bisection actually guarantees.
Let $n$ be even, let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of a finite simple graph, and let $\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}$ encode an exactly balanced partition. Put $D_i=\sum_jA_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$, the same-side degree minus the opposite-side degree. Swap opposite-side vertices $u,v$. Flipping one vertex changes the cut size by its $D_i$. Adding the two single-flip changes counts their shared crossing edge as a decrease twice, although after the simultaneous swap it still crosses. Thus the true change is \[ \Delta\operatorname{cut}=D_u+D_v+2A_{uv}. \] A partition locally minimizing cut under swaps therefore gives only $D_u+D_v+2A_{uv}\geq0$ for opposite-side pairs. It does not give $D_i\geq0$ separately, still less $D_i\geq h\sqrt n$. For an explicit example, consider the four-vertex star, with its center and one leaf on one side. The center has $D=-1$, its same-side leaf has $D=1$, and the other two leaves have $D=-1$. Every bisection of this graph cuts two edges, so this is a global minimum bisection, yet three vertices are unfriendly. Equivalently the swap formula is zero for every opposite-side pair. This does not refute a random-graph theorem; it refutes a deterministic inference used in a proposed greedy route.
A necessary spectral inequality, with the centering term retained.
Write $J$ for the all-ones matrix and $W=A-\frac12(J-I)$. Balancedness gives $\sigma^TJ\sigma=0$ and $\sigma^T\sigma=n$, hence \[ \sum_iD_i=\sigma^TA\sigma=\sigma^TW\sigma-\frac n2 \leq n\|W\|_{\rm op}-\frac n2. \] A fully $h$-friendly bisection must therefore satisfy \[ h\leq\frac{\|W\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n}-\frac1{2\sqrt n}. \] This is a finite-instance necessary condition only. A bound on the sum of local fields is not a lower bound on their minimum, and it says nothing about the pairwise geometry of the solution set needed for an overlap gap.
Why an energy transfer alone does not transfer friendliness.
Changing $q$ undirected edges changes $\sigma^TA\sigma$ by at most $2q$ for a fixed $\sigma$. With $q\leq n$, its normalization by $n\sqrt n$ changes by at most $2/\sqrt n$. But changes incident to one vertex can alter that vertex's normalized local field by order $\sqrt n$. For an explicit family, take two disjoint cliques of size $m\geq2$, partitioned by their components, so $n=2m$. Choose one vertex $u$ in the first clique, remove its $m-1$ edges within that clique, and add its $m$ edges to the other clique. Exactly $2m-1<n$ edges change. Its field changes from $m-1$ to $-m$, a difference $2m-1$, while the change in the normalized total energy is at most $2/\sqrt n$. Every other vertex has a nonnegative field for $m\geq2$, but $u$ violates every positive friendliness threshold. Thus a proof comparing only normalized total energies can lose a vertexwise constraint even when its energy error vanishes. For an asymptotic criterion allowing $o(n)$ bad vertices this one-vertex example is admissible instead; it must not be used as a counterexample to that weaker criterion.

Scope and remaining gaps

The route eliminates two unjustified sufficient conditions for full friendliness and gives a finite-instance necessary bound. It proves neither a stable random- graph algorithm nor an overlap gap for every positive $h$, and gives no transfer of an algorithmic barrier between random regular and Erdős--Rényi models. The original stability and asymptotic quantifiers remain essential gaps.
