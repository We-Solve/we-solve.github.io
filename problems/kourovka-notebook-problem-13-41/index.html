Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.41

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Lambda$ be a totally ordered abelian group, written additively, whose order is invariant under translation. A $\Lambda$-metric on a nonempty set $X$ is a function $d:X\times X\to\Lambda_{\geq 0}$ satisfying symmetry, the triangle inequality, and $d(x,y)=0$ if and only if $x=y$. A segment from $x$ to $y$ is the image of an isometric embedding of $[0,d(x,y)]_\Lambda$ with endpoints $x,y$. A $\Lambda$-tree is a $\Lambda$-metric space in which any two points are joined by a unique segment, the intersection of two segments with a common endpoint is a segment, and the union of two segments whose intersection consists of their common endpoint is a segment.

An isometric action of a group $G$ on $X$ is free on points if \[ \forall g\in G\ \forall x\in X,\qquad gx=x\Longrightarrow g=1. \] Some conventions for free actions on $\Lambda$-trees additionally exclude inversions, meaning that no group element exchanges the endpoints of a nondegenerate segment. The supplied record does not specify this convention; it can matter when $\Lambda$ is not divisible by $2$.

Use the first-order language of groups $L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$, without predicates for the tree or its action. For any specified class $\mathcal C$ of groups, its elementary theory is \[ \operatorname{Th}(\mathcal C)=\{\varphi:\varphi\text{ is an }L_{\mathrm{grp}}\text{-sentence and }G\models\varphi\text{ for every }G\in\mathcal C\}. \] This theory is decidable if an algorithm, on every input $L_{\mathrm{grp}}$-sentence $\varphi$, halts and correctly decides whether $\varphi\in\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal C)$.

Problem. Is $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal C)$ decidable, where $\mathcal C$ is the class of all groups admitting a free isometric action on a $\Lambda$-tree? No finite generation or other restriction on the groups is stated.

Unresolved scope. The source does not say whether $\Lambda$ is fixed or ranges over totally ordered abelian groups. For fixed $\Lambda$, the class would be \[ \mathcal C_\Lambda=\{G: G\text{ admits a free isometric action on some }\Lambda\text{-tree}\}. \] For varying $\Lambda$, the class would instead be \[ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{all}}=\{G:\text{there exist a totally ordered abelian group }\Lambda\text{ and a free isometric action of }G\text{ on a }\Lambda\text{-tree}\}. \] The corresponding questions concern $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal C_\Lambda)$ and $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{all}})$, respectively. The supplied wording does not resolve which scope, or which convention concerning inversions, is intended.

Short English statement

Does an algorithm decide which first-order group sentences hold in every group admitting a free action on a $\Lambda$-tree? The source leaves the scope of $\Lambda$ and the convention concerning inversions unspecified. The registry labels this problem open; that label is supplied source data, not an independently verified status assessment.
