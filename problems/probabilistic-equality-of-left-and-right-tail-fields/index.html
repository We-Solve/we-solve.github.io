Math-7233

Probabilistic Equality of Left and Right Tail Fields

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a finite nonempty set equipped with its discrete sigma-field, and let $(X_k)_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}$ be an $S$-valued process on a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$. Assume strict stationarity: for every $r\geq1$, every $k_1,\ldots,k_r\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, and every $h\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, \[ (X_{k_1+h},\ldots,X_{k_r+h})\overset{\mathrm d}= (X_{k_1},\ldots,X_{k_r}). \] Define the left and right tail sigma-fields by \[ \mathcal T^-:=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma(X_k:k\leq -n), \qquad \mathcal T^+:=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma(X_k:k\geq n). \] Equality modulo null sets means that for every $A\in\mathcal T^-$ there exists $B\in\mathcal T^+$ with $\mathbb P(A\triangle B)=0$, and conversely for every $B\in\mathcal T^+$ there exists such an $A\in\mathcal T^-$.

Requested statement. For every process satisfying these assumptions, prove, using only probability theory and without entropy theory, that \[ \mathcal T^-=\mathcal T^+\qquad\text{modulo }\mathbb P\text{-null sets}. \] Unresolved qualifications. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify the time index set or the meaning of stationarity. The formulation above uses the conventional interpretation of a bi-infinite discrete-time strictly stationary process; this interpretation requires source confirmation. The source also does not formally delimit the permitted probabilistic methods or the excluded entropy-theoretic methods. Its target is a proof subject to this methodological restriction.

Short English statement

For a stationary process taking values in a finite set, show that the events determined by the arbitrarily remote past coincide, up to probability-zero differences, with those determined by the arbitrarily remote future. The requested proof must use only probability theory and exclude entropy theory. Bi-infinite discrete time and strict stationarity are the explicit interpretations used here; the source leaves the precise methodological boundary unspecified.
