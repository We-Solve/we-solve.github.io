Math-226

Efficient generation and a completable-coordinate strong-lifting criterion

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finitely generated module $M$ over a ring $R$, let $\mu_R(M)$ be its minimum number of generators. The main question, attributed in the source to Satya Mandal, is whether an ideal $I$ in $R=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ satisfies \[ \mu_R(I)=\mu_{R/I}(I/I^2). \] The field is unspecified. The usual efficient-generation problem concerns proper ideals; the extracted statement does not say proper, and $I=R$ would give different sides. That missing qualification is recorded rather than silently removed. The source reports the equality known when $\mu(I/I^2)\geq\dim(R/I)+2$.

The related Bhatwadekar question takes a surjective ${\mathbb{C}}$-algebra map $\alpha:{\mathbb{C}}[X_1,\ldots,X_5]\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}[Y_1,Y_2]$ and $I=\ker\alpha$. It reports $\mu(I/I^2)=3$ and asks whether $\mu(I)=3$. Asok's remark suggests detecting the number of generators of a projective module through lifting its classifying map $\operatorname{Spec}R\to BGL_r$ to a suitable Grassmannian, without giving the required hypotheses. Fasel asks for a Chow-type computable cohomological obstruction to a smooth embedding $Y\subset X=\operatorname{Spec}R$ being a global complete intersection, and subsequently for a locally complete-intersection $Y$.

Fasel's universal setup in the damaged remarks is \[ R_{\mathrm{univ}}=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n,Y_1,\ldots,Y_n,Z]/ (\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^nX_iY_i-Z(1+Z)), \] \[ X_{\mathrm{univ}}=\operatorname{Spec}R_{\mathrm{univ}},\qquad Y_{\mathrm{univ}}=\operatorname{Spec}(R_{\mathrm{univ}}/(X_1,\ldots,X_n,Z)). \] It describes $X_{\mathrm{univ}}$ as an ${\mathbb{A}}^1$-sphere and asserts a classifying map of pairs $(X,Y)\to(X_{\mathrm{univ}},Y_{\mathrm{univ}})$ from conormal-generation data, but the value of the conormal-generator bound is lost. It reports a Chow--Witt decomposition $GW(k)\oplus GW(k)\alpha$, with $\alpha$ a form on $Y_{\mathrm{univ}}$, while its precise grading and source twist are damaged. The related question asks whether $f^*(\alpha)=0$ in the indicated Chow--Witt group of $X$ twisted by $\omega_{X/k}$ suffices for $Y$ to be a global complete intersection. Choices of orientation, grading, and classifying map must be recovered before this is a precise obstruction criterion.

The research aid reports the general and complex-kernel questions open and proposes a restricted strong-lifting criterion. For Segre data \[ I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r,s),\qquad s(1-s)=\sum_{i=1}^ra_ib_i, \] if the opposite-coordinate column $b=(b_i)$ can be completed to the first basis vector by an invertible change of basis, it claims the prescribed conormal orientation lifts to $r$ generators of $I$. A strong lift here means generators congruent to the prescribed $a_i$ modulo $I^2$. Over a polynomial ring over a field, it reports that $(b_1,\ldots,b_r)=R$ suffices by Quillen--Suslin. Its contrapositive candidate is that every nonliftable orientation has a proper opposite-coordinate ideal in every Segre presentation. This conditional locus is not a solution of the unrestricted efficient-generation or cohomological questions.

Short English statement

Can an ideal in a polynomial ring be generated by as few elements as its conormal module, including the stated complex kernel example? The source also asks for homotopical/cohomological complete-intersection obstructions. The report gives only a special strong-lifting criterion, while substantial OCR damage leaves some universal obstruction data unresolved.
