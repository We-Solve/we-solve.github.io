Math-124

Modularity of Calabi–Yau Cohomological $L$-Series

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The question asks whether the cohomological $L$-series of Calabi--Yau varieties are completely determined by suitable modular cusp forms. An arithmetic formulation must fix a variety $X$ over a number field $K$, a Calabi--Yau convention, a cohomological degree or motivic summand, and the class of automorphic forms. These are not specified in the source. Smooth projective varieties over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, arbitrary number fields and singular models are not silently identified.

For a chosen degree $i$, a standard object to compare is the $L$-function of $H^i_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\overline K},{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ or of a specified subrepresentation/motivic summand. A precise modularity assertion would identify its Euler factors with those of a specified cusp form or automorphic representation, with matching weight, coefficient field, level/conductor and twists. Agreement at almost all good places is different from a fully specified comparison at bad places; the word completely does not choose between them.

In the familiar rank-two setting over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, a pure piece of Hodge types $(i,0)$ and $(0,i)$ suggests a classical modular form of weight $i+1$, with the needed hypotheses and normalization. Higher-dimensional cohomology need not be represented by one classical $GL_2$ cusp form. Tate or character pieces also need their own factors rather than being silently counted as cusp-form pieces. Thus the general question requires a decomposition or a broader automorphic interpretation, neither supplied by the one-sentence formulation.

The dated literature assessment, checked 21 August 2026, reports modularity for elliptic curves and many rigid or special Calabi--Yau varieties, while a theorem for all Calabi--Yau cohomological $L$-series remains open. The supplied references do not include theorem statements or a uniform decomposition result. These positive cases are retained as reported partial progress, not promoted to a theorem that every cohomological degree of every Calabi--Yau variety is a classical modular-form $L$-function.

Short English statement

The question needs an arithmetic base, cohomological piece and meaning of modular form before a precise comparison is possible. The supplied review reports elliptic-curve and special Calabi–Yau cases, while leaving the general statement open. Higher-rank cohomology and noncuspidal factors require separate treatment.
