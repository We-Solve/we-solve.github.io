Topological Nilpotence Degree of Finite-Group Chow Rings

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group, and let $p$ denote the prime implicit in the source's use of mod-$p$ coefficients. Write $M=CH_G^*$ for the mod-$p$ Chow ring referred to in the supplied record. The record does not specify the base field, the construction of this Chow ring, or whether its grading is by codimension or by doubled codimension.

Write $\mathcal A_p$ for the Steenrod algebra intended by the source. An unstable module here means a graded $\mathcal A_p$-module satisfying the instability conditions for the intended algebra and grading. Once those conventions have been fixed, write $\Sigma^d N$ for the graded suspension, with $(\Sigma^d N)^n=N^{n-d}$ and the corresponding suspended action whenever this is defined in the intended category. Interpret "contains" as the existence of an injective, degree-preserving $\mathcal A_p$-linear map. Thus the suspension quantity in the source can be expressed as \[ D(M)=\left\{d\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\begin{array}{l} \text{there exists a nonzero unstable module }N,\\ \Sigma^dN\text{ is defined in the intended category, and}\\ \text{there is an injective graded }\mathcal A_p\text{-linear map } \Sigma^dN\hookrightarrow M \end{array}\right\}. \] The source calls $d_0(M)$ the topological nilpotence degree, and its literature assessment also calls it a detection bound. No definition of this invariant or its normalization is supplied.

Conjecture as recorded. For a finite group $G$, the proposed equality is \[ d_0(CH_G^*)=\max D(CH_G^*). \] This formulation includes the requirement that the indicated maximum exist. The source gives no restriction on $G$ and states no restriction on the prime $p$; the intended universal reading is therefore for every finite group and every prime for which the intended construction applies. However, the unspecified Chow-ring setting, Steenrod action, grading, allowable suspension indices, and definition of $d_0$ must be recovered from the cited material before this becomes a fully determined mathematical statement. In particular, the record does not explicitly say whether only nonnegative suspension indices are allowed.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether the topological nilpotence degree of a finite group's mod-$p$ Chow ring equals the greatest suspension degree of a nonzero unstable Steenrod module that embeds in that ring. The definition of the invariant and several category and grading conventions are missing from the supplied record, so the equality remains conditional on resolving them.
