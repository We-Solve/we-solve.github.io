Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is periodic if every element has finite order, that is, \[ \forall g\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\quad g^n=1_G. \] It is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are $\{1_G\}$ and $G$. A finite Frobenius group is a finite group $F$ having a subgroup $H$ such that $\{1_F\}<H<F$ and \[ H\cap xHx^{-1}=\{1_F\}\qquad\text{for every }x\in F\setminus H. \] Let $\mathcal F$ denote the class of finite Frobenius groups.

The source asks whether there exists an infinite periodic simple group saturated with groups from $\mathcal F$, with saturation understood exactly as in Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.101. To display the requested quantifiers, write $\operatorname{Sat}_{14.101}(G,\mathcal F)$ as a placeholder for that source-defined condition. The question is whether \[ \exists\text{ a group }G\quad \left[ \begin{gathered} |G|\text{ is infinite},\\ \forall g\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\quad g^n=1_G,\\ \forall N\trianglelefteq G\quad (N=\{1_G\}\ \lor\ N=G),\\ \operatorname{Sat}_{14.101}(G,\mathcal F) \end{gathered} \right]. \] The supplied record does not include the definition in Problem 14.101. Consequently, the saturation condition remains unresolved here, and this display is not a complete self-contained formulation. In particular, no quantification over finite subgroups or embeddings is substituted for the missing definition.

Short English statement

A. I. Sozutov asks whether an infinite simple group in which every element has finite order can be saturated with finite Frobenius groups. The intended meaning of saturation must be recovered from Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.101 before this draft can be made self-contained.
