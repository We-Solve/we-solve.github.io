Zero dimension exponent for well-conditioned sampling queries

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 139; 2026-09-26), the asserted result is that exact value-and-gradient sampling for known-minimizer potentials with Hessian between $I$ and $2I$ has worst-case query budget at most $C_\varepsilon d^\varepsilon$, with a logarithmic lower bound and unrestricted internal computation.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For $d\ge2$, let $V\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$ satisfy $V(0)=0$, $\nabla V(0)=0$ and $I_d\preceq\nabla^2V(x)\preceq2I_d$ everywhere. The target is $\pi_V(dx)\propto e^{-V(x)}dx$. Exact adaptive first-order queries return $(V(x),\nabla V(x))$; algorithms are measurable and have unrestricted real computation and randomness between queries. Let $Q(d)$ be the minimum worst-execution query budget for total-variation error at most $1/10$ uniformly over this class. The claim gives \[ \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_{\varepsilon}:\quad Q(d)\le C_{\varepsilon} d^{\varepsilon}, \qquad Q(d)\ge c\log d\quad(d\ge d_0) \] for absolute $c,d_0$. Thus the optimal dimension exponent is zero. These are oracle-query bounds, without a polynomial bit-time conclusion.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 139; 2026-09-26), transport-derived skew dynamics keep a stationary Gaussian-observation flow whose momentum records a conditional mean. Material-derivative bounds and nested noisy-center evaluations control the query count; stability and deterministic caps convert the analytic construction to a sampling procedure.
