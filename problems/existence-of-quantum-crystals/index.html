A symmetry test for the meaning of crystallization.
On a finite torus of volume $V$, suppose a neutral many-particle Hamiltonian commutes with simultaneous translations $U_a$, and let $\Gamma$ be any ground-state density matrix. Its translation average \[\overline\Gamma=V^{-1}\int U_a\Gamma U_a^*\,da\] is again positive, has trace one and has the same ground energy. If $\rho_\Gamma(x)$ is the one-particle density and its total particle number is $N$, averaging gives \[\rho_{\overline\Gamma}(x)=V^{-1}\int\rho_\Gamma(x-a)\,da=N/V. \] Thus finite-volume minimization alone does not force a visibly periodic nonconstant one-body density. This holds even when a particular untranslated state has pronounced density peaks.
A diagnostic that survives averaging.
For a reciprocal wavevector $k$, define the density mode $D_k=\sum_{j=1}^N e^{ik\cdot x_j}$. Translation changes it by $e^{ik\cdot a}$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(\overline\Gamma D_k)=0$ at nonzero $k$, whereas $D_k^*D_k$ is translation invariant. For a deterministic set of positions on a lattice compatible with the torus, $|D_k|^2=N^2$ when $k$ belongs to its reciprocal lattice. Randomizing the common translation kills the first moment but leaves this squared amplitude unchanged. This calculation illustrates why two-point observables or a symmetry-breaking limit can retain crystalline information lost by the averaged density.
First gap and scope.
No ground state of a Coulomb electron-nucleus system with such order has been constructed here. The deterministic lattice example prescribes the configuration and therefore cannot prove its energetic selection. One must choose an admissible neutral quantum system, a thermodynamic sequence and an observable/topology of periodic order, then prove that minimization enforces the required order. The source asks for some system, not all materials; this distinction does not permit inserting an external lattice that already assumes the desired structure. The symmetry calculation is an obstruction to an overly simple proof strategy, not a new crystal-existence result.
