Floquet Exponents and Invariant Measures of Schrödinger Operators

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Question. To what extent does the Floquet exponent of a Schrödinger operator characterize its invariant probability measure? The record supplies neither an operator model nor a definition of its Floquet exponent or invariant measure. In particular, it does not say whether the exponent is known at one spectral parameter or as an entire spectral-parameter function, or whether it means a real growth exponent or a complex Floquet quantity. These distinctions are essential to any injectivity statement.

A conditional way to organize the question is to fix a parameter space $\Omega$, its dynamics $\tau$ (a transformation or flow), a specified associated family of Schrödinger operators $H_\omega$, and a class $\mathcal M_\tau(\Omega)$ of invariant probability measures. Invariance means $\mu(\tau^{-1}B)=\mu(B)$ for a transformation, or the corresponding equality at every flow time. Once the source's exponent construction and spectral domain $D$ are fixed, denote the resulting data by $\mathcal F_\mu:D\to{\mathbb{C}}$ (or its specified real-valued version). Characterizing the measure from these data asks for a description of the fibers\[\{\nu\in\mathcal M_\tau(\Omega):\mathcal F_\nu(z)=\mathcal F_\mu(z)\text{ for every }z\in D\},\] and, in particular, hypotheses under which such a fiber consists of $\mu$ alone or of explicitly identified equivalent measures. This is a framework for the missing choices, not an assertion that the excerpt has fixed $\Omega,\tau,D$, ergodicity, or an exponent construction. No periodic, continuum, discrete, or random operator model is selected silently.

The supplied literature triage dated 2026-08-21 labels the question open. It says that a general injectivity theorem for the Floquet exponent was not located and that later work treats special invariant measures without resolving the stated general characterization. This is a dated report of a search, not a verified nonexistence theorem or an independently established current status.

Short English statement

Determine what information a Schrödinger operator's Floquet exponent retains about the underlying invariant probability measure, and when it determines that measure uniquely. The operator family, dynamics, definition of exponent, and range of spectral data are missing from the excerpt.
