Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Question 10. To speed up the elliptic curve factorization algorithm, pick E/ Q

with large torsion group so that its reduction modulo n is more likely to be smooth. Mazur's results show that this torsion subgroup can be no larger than 16. There is a result of Kamienny-Mazur-Merel: there is a bound on E(K)tors in terms of [ K: Q]. So try to find a number field K where p splits completely and such that E(K)tors large. (Pomerance)

A concrete construction to test the proposal

The source seeks a computational advantage from large torsion after passing to a number field in which an unknown factor prime splits. There is no specified torsion size, field-selection distribution or cost model. We test a minimal explicit construction and the proposed inference from forced torsion to smooth group order.
Full two-torsion in a cubic splitting field.
Let $f\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be monic squarefree cubic and $E:y^2=f(x)$. In characteristic zero this is an elliptic curve with the point at infinity $O$, since a repeated root would be exactly a singularity of the affine equation and the usual projective point at infinity is smooth. Let $K$ be its splitting field, with roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$. The inverse of $(x,y)$ in the Weierstrass group law is $(x,-y)$. Thus \[ T=\{O,(\alpha_1,0),(\alpha_2,0),(\alpha_3,0)\} \] is the full group killed by two: a nonzero point equals its inverse exactly when $y=0$. It has four elements. The field degree is at most six, since adjoining one cubic root costs at most three and the other two roots satisfy the remaining quadratic. This does not yield arbitrarily large torsion.

At an odd prime of good reduction where these roots reduce to three distinct elements of $\mathbb F_p$, the same four points remain four distinct two-torsion points on the reduced curve. In particular $4\mid\#E(\mathbb F_p)$ by partition into cosets of this subgroup. Complete splitting in $K$, away from the discriminant and two, is a sufficient setting: the integral roots reduce into its residue field $\mathbb F_p$ and distinctness is guaranteed by the nonzero reduced discriminant. This explicit argument uses no blanket assertion that all torsion, including residue-characteristic torsion, always injects.

Forced torsion does not force smoothness.
For $E:y^2=x^3-2$ over $\mathbb F_{127}$, the three roots are $32,100,122$, verified by cubing modulo $127$. They are distinct and the cubic is squarefree since its derivative is $3x^2$ and zero is not a root. Exact enumeration of the quadratic character of $x^3-2$ gives $72$ values $+1$, $52$ values $-1$, and three zeros. Hence \[ \#E(\mathbb F_{127})=127+1+72-52=148=4\cdot37. \] The count is reproducible by listing the nonzero squares modulo $127$ and testing the $127$ residues; no approximate curve software is used. Although the entire rational two-torsion subgroup is present, the group order is not $7$-smooth. In general if a known subgroup of order $t$ injects, the reduced group order is $t u$. Its prime factors are all at most $B$ exactly when both $t$ and $u$ have that property, by unique factorization of integers. Knowledge of a $B$-smooth $t$ leaves the cofactor $u$ to be controlled. The example demonstrates the gap without making a statistical claim about the distribution of cofactors.
A computable symbol does not identify splitting at unknown factors.
For a quadratic choice $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ and odd $p\nmid2D$, having a square root of $D$ in $\mathbb F_p$ is the local split condition for the defining quadratic. A Jacobi-symbol test modulo the composite input cannot ensure that condition for every prime factor. For example, $n=15$ and $D=2$ give $\left(\frac2{15}\right)= \left(\frac23\right)\left(\frac25\right)=(-1)(-1)=1$, but two is a nonsquare modulo both three and five: their nonzero square sets are $\{1\}$ and $\{1,4\}$. Thus a positive Jacobi symbol cannot be used as a certificate that the unknown factor splits in the chosen field. Passing to a splitting field of a torsion polynomial also does not by itself make an arbitrary unknown factor a split prime.
