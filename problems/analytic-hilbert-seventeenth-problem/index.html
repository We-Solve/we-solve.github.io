Analytic Hilbert Seventeenth Problem

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 1$ be an integer. A function $f:{\mathbb{R}}^n\to{\mathbb{R}}$ is real analytic if it is locally represented by convergent real power series, and it is positive semidefinite if $f(x)\geq 0$ for every $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$.

Under the local-quotient convention, a real-analytic meromorphic function on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a global meromorphic section locally represented by quotients $a/b$, where $a,b$ are real-analytic functions and the denominator has nonzero germ. Local representatives agree wherever their quotients are defined. An identity between such functions is understood as a meromorphic identity, checked locally after clearing denominators; poles of individual summands are permitted.

The supplied question, with "sum of squares" interpreted as a finite sum, is whether \[ \forall n\geq 1\;\forall f\in C^\omega({\mathbb{R}}^n,{\mathbb{R}}),\qquad \left(\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n,\ f(x)\geq 0\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\exists m\geq 1\;\exists g_1,\ldots,g_m\text{ real-analytic meromorphic on }{\mathbb{R}}^n,\quad f=\sum_{j=1}^m g_j^2\right). \] Here $m$ may depend on $n$ and $f$. No compactness assumption or bound on the number of squares is supplied.

The source does not define "real-analytic meromorphic." If it requires each $g_j$ to be a quotient of globally defined real-analytic functions, the corresponding precise formulation is: for every such $n$ and $f$, do there exist $m\geq 1$ and $h,a_1,\ldots,a_m\in C^\omega({\mathbb{R}}^n,{\mathbb{R}})$, with $h\not\equiv 0$, such that \[ h^2f=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j^2\quad\text{on }{\mathbb{R}}^n? \] This common-denominator formulation expresses the global-quotient convention. The supplied evidence does not establish its equivalence with the local-quotient formulation, so the intended convention requires review.

Short English statement

Does every everywhere nonnegative real-analytic function on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, for every positive integer $n$, admit a finite representation as a sum of squares of real-analytic meromorphic functions? The identity allows poles and is interpreted after clearing denominators. The source leaves the precise meromorphic convention unstated.
