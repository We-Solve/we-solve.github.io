Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Question 49 (\'Alvarez Paiva)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be either $S^3$ or $S^2\times S^1$, where $S^n$ denotes the smooth $n$-sphere. For a smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $M$, write $\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)$ for its total Riemannian volume. A closed geodesic means a nonconstant smooth periodic curve $\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to M$, for some $T>0$, satisfying $\nabla^g_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0$. Its length is \[ \ell_g(\gamma)=\int_0^T\sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] The specific question is whether the following assertion holds separately for each of the two manifolds: \[ \forall M\in\{S^3,S^2\times S^1\}\;\forall g\text{ a smooth Riemannian metric on }M,\qquad \operatorname{Vol}_g(M)=1 \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists\text{ a nonconstant closed }g\text{-geodesic }\gamma \text{ with }\ell_g(\gamma)\leq 10^{24}. \] The source also asks whether unit-volume metrics on these manifolds can have all closed geodesics "long." It does not give that qualitative word a separate numerical or asymptotic definition. In particular, the explicit bound question above must be distinguished from the stronger possible question of whether the lengths of all nonconstant closed geodesics can simultaneously exceed every prescribed threshold as the metric varies. No equivalence between these questions is asserted.

Short English statement

For each of $S^3$ and $S^2\times S^1$, must every Riemannian metric of total volume $1$ admit a nonconstant closed geodesic of length at most $10^{24}$? The source also asks qualitatively whether all closed geodesics can be long; the intended extent of "long" is unspecified.
