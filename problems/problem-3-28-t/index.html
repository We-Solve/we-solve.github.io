Problem 3.28 — (T.

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a surface $\Sigma$, let \[ \operatorname{Conf}_n(\Sigma)=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\Sigma^n:x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}, \qquad P_n(\Sigma)=\pi_1(\operatorname{Conf}_n(\Sigma),\mathbf x). \] This is the pure braid group on $n$ ordered strands, with multiplication by concatenation. Let $\mathcal A_n(\Sigma)$ denote the source's algebra of diagrams on the corresponding surface strands.

T. Kohno asks whether there exists an injective multiplicative homomorphism, defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, \[ \theta:P_n(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\mathcal A_n(\Sigma),\qquad \theta(\beta\gamma)=\theta(\beta)\theta(\gamma). \] With the usual unital interpretation, $\theta(1)=1$, all values lie in the unit group $\mathcal A_n(\Sigma)^\times$, and injectivity is $\ker\theta=\{1\}$. Defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ concerns rational coefficients in the target, not linearity of a map from a group.

The diagram generators, relations, degree-zero data and possible degree completion of $\mathcal A_n(\Sigma)$ are absent. Completion cannot be ignored when the intended values are formal series, but is not inserted silently into the named target. The surface class, allowed $n$, and basepoint/framing conventions also require context. The excerpt asks for injectivity and multiplicativity; universality, naturality under surface maps, and compatibility as $n$ changes are not additional stated requirements.

Short English statement

Does the pure braid group of the intended surface admit an injective multiplicative representation in its rational strand-diagram algebra? The algebra's relations and completion, and the surface class, need source definitions.
