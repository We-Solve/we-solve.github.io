Math-309

Listable and height-thin Diophantine subsets of the rationals

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Pasten's Question 8 asks: is an explicitly known recursively enumerable subset of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ non-Diophantine over ${\mathbb{Q}}$? What are the thinnest known Diophantine rational sets? Does a suitable Bombieri--Lang hypothesis force such sets to be finite or to have fast growth, potentially proving ${\mathbb{Z}}$ non-Diophantine? The source gives no growth parameter or precise "fast" threshold. A Diophantine set means an existential polynomial projection over ${\mathbb{Q}}$; recursively enumerable (listable) uses a computable encoding of rational numbers, with no decidability of membership required.

The aid reports that existence of a listable non-Diophantine subset of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ is equivalent to ${\mathbb{Z}}$ not being Diophantine in ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and remains open in its supplied assessment. It also reports no known general finite-or-fast height-growth dichotomy. The original statement of evidence against a Diophantine definition of ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is historical context, not a proof.

For its unconditional height calibration, write a reduced rational as $x=a/b$, $b>0$, and set \[ H(x)=\max(|a|,b),\qquad N_S(B)=\#\{x\in S:H(x)\le B\}. \] Consider \[ E:y^2+y=x^3-x,\qquad P=(0,0),\qquad S=\{x\in{\mathbb{Q}}:\exists y\in{\mathbb{Q}},\ y^2+y=x^3-x\}. \] This $S$ is Diophantine. The aid reports $E({\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Z}} P$, with $P$ of infinite order, and that \[ {\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\longrightarrow S,\qquad n\longmapsto x(nP) \] is bijective. The point at infinity is excluded by using affine $x$-coordinates, and the two signs of $n$ have the same $x$-coordinate. In the canonical-height convention $\log H(x(Q))=2\widehat h(Q)+O(1)$, it gives \[ N_S(B)=\sqrt{\frac{\log B}{2\widehat h(P)}}+O(1) \qquad(B\to\infty). \] Thus this infinite Diophantine set has counting function smaller than every positive power of $B$. The aid also refers to fixed-power sets as calibrations; such examples do not justify a universal positive-power lower growth threshold. The elliptic example is a concrete obstruction to that kind of interpretation of "fast", not a claim of an absolute thinnest set.

The conditional literature discussion has two separate hypotheses. Under Koenigsmann's stated strong Bombieri--Lang hypothesis, the aid says no infinite subset of ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is Diophantine over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. The exact strengthened hypothesis is not reproduced and must not be replaced by an unqualified standard Bombieri--Lang statement. Under Pasten's later approximation conjecture, it says explicit listable non-Diophantine sets follow; neither that conjecture nor those set definitions are given. Both conclusions remain conditional. They do not convert the unconditional open question into a known example.

Short English statement

The record asks for listable non-Diophantine rational sets and a meaningful notion of thin Diophantine sets. The aid gives an explicit elliptic-coordinate set with only square-root-logarithmic height growth, ruling out a universal positive-power growth interpretation. Its proposed non-Diophantine examples still depend on precisely specified conjectures that are not reproduced here.
