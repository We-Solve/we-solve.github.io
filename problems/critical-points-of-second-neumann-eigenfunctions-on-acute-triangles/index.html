Math-6796

Critical Points of Second Neumann Eigenfunctions on Acute Triangles

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $T\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be the open interior of a nondegenerate triangle whose three interior angles are strictly less than $\pi/2$, and let $V(T)$ be its set of three vertices. List the Neumann eigenvalues of $-\Delta$, counting multiplicity, as \[ 0=\lambda_1(T)<\lambda_2(T)\leq\lambda_3(T)\leq\cdots. \] Under the usual real-valued convention, a second Neumann eigenfunction is any nonzero $u\in H^1(T;{\mathbb{R}})$ satisfying \[ \int_T\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x =\lambda_2(T)\int_Tu\varphi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x \qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in H^1(T;{\mathbb{R}}). \] Thus the boundary condition is the homogeneous Neumann condition $\partial_\nu u=0$ along each open edge. No simplicity assumption on $\lambda_2(T)$ is imposed; the quantifier includes every nonzero element of its eigenspace.

Interpreting a nonvertex critical point as a point of the closed triangle other than a vertex at which the full gradient vanishes, set \[ C_T(u)=\{p\in\overline T\setminus V(T):\nabla u(p)=0\}. \] At an open-edge point, the gradient is evaluated using the smooth local extension supplied by reflection across that straight edge. Consequently, this convention counts both interior critical points and critical points on open edges.

The reconstructed assertion supplied by the registry is \[ \text{for every acute planar triangle }T\text{ and every second Neumann eigenfunction }u, \qquad \#C_T(u)\leq 1. \]

Unresolved source qualifications. The supplied statement does not explicitly specify the scalar field or whether open-edge points count as critical points. Short English statement

The supplied reconstructed claim says that every second Neumann eigenfunction on every acute planar triangle has at most one critical point away from the vertices. The displayed formulation uses real-valued eigenfunctions and counts points in the interior and on open edges. Those conventions and the reconstruction need review because the original extract omits the relevant preceding claim and includes an unrelated graph discussion.
