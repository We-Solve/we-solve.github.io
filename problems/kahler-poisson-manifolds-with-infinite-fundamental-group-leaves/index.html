Kähler Poisson Manifolds with Infinite-Fundamental-Group Leaves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A compact Kähler Poisson manifold is a pair $(X,\pi)$, where $X$ is a compact complex manifold admitting a Kähler form and $\pi\in H^0(X,\bigwedge^2T_X)$ is a holomorphic bivector satisfying $[\pi,\pi]=0$, with the bracket here denoting the Schouten bracket. It defines a Poisson bracket on local holomorphic functions by $\{f,g\}=\pi({\,\mathrm{d}} f,{\,\mathrm{d}} g)$. Write $\pi^{\sharp}:T_X^*\to T_X$ for the induced bundle map. A symplectic leaf $L$ is a connected maximal immersed complex submanifold whose tangent spaces are the images of $\pi^{\sharp}$; its induced holomorphic Poisson structure is nondegenerate. Compactness of a leaf refers to its intrinsic manifold topology.

Problem 6, as supplied. Describe the compact Kähler Poisson manifolds $(X,\pi)$ for which there exists a compact symplectic leaf $L\subseteq X$ such that \[ \lvert\pi_1(L,\ell)\rvert=\infty \] for a base point $\ell\in L$. Since $L$ is connected, infinitude is independent of the base point. The source does not specify an equivalence relation for this description or a precise form of the desired classification.

Supplied reduction. The report passage asserts that this problem reduces to the case where $L$ is a finite étale quotient of a torus. In the holomorphic setting, this means that there is a finite surjective étale holomorphic map $T\to L$, where $T={\mathbb{C}}^g/\Lambda$ is a compact complex torus and $\Lambda$ is a lattice of rank $2g$. The infinite-fundamental-group condition requires $g\geq 1$. This reduction is recorded as a source assertion; its proof and the precise manner in which it recovers the general case are not supplied.

Qualifications from the surrounding passage. The supplied Corollary 4 states that if $(X,\pi)$ is compact Kähler and \[ h^{2,0}(X):=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}H^0(X,\Omega_X^2)=0, \] then every compact symplectic leaf is a point. The hypothesis $h^{2,0}(X)=0$ belongs to that corollary and is not imposed in Problem 6. The preceding definition calls a holomorphic map $\phi:(X,\pi_X)\to(Y,\pi_Y)$ a Poisson morphism when $\phi^*\{f,g\}_Y=\{\phi^*f,\phi^*g\}_X$ for every pair of local holomorphic functions $f,g$ on $Y$.

The supplied Remark 5 says that the conclusion of an earlier theorem may fail if the complex Poisson manifold $X$ is noncompact, if $X$ is non-Kähler, or if the fundamental group of $L$ is infinite. It also reports smooth real Poisson examples in which both the ambient manifold and its leaf are simply connected but the ambient manifold does not decompose as a product. The earlier theorem and the intended product factors are absent from the record, so these contextual assertions do not determine an additional precise theorem or a product-decomposition requirement for this problem.

Short English statement

Describe compact Kähler manifolds with a holomorphic Poisson structure that has a compact symplectic leaf with infinite fundamental group. The supplied report asserts a reduction to leaves that are finite étale quotients of compact complex tori. The intended form of the description and the details of this reduction remain unspecified.
