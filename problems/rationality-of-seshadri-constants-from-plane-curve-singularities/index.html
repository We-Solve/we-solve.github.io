Rationality of Seshadri Constants from Plane-Curve Singularities

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2$ be a reduced plane curve, and let $Z=\operatorname{Sing}(C)$ be its set of singular points, regarded as a reduced set of points. Let $\pi:X=\operatorname{Bl}_Z({\mathbb{P}}^2)\to{\mathbb{P}}^2$ be the blowup at those points, and let $\widetilde C$ be the strict transform of $C$, namely the closure of $\pi^{-1}(C\setminus Z)$ in $X$. The notation $\widetilde C^{\,2}$ denotes its self-intersection on $X$.

The supplied record does not specify the ground field, its characteristic, the geometric-point convention, or the normalization of $\varepsilon(Z)$. Under the usual interpretation in which $Z$ consists of distinct geometric points and $\varepsilon(Z)$ is the unweighted multipoint Seshadri constant of $\mathcal O_{{\mathbb{P}}^2}(1)$, put $H=\pi^*\mathcal O_{{\mathbb{P}}^2}(1)$, and let $E_z$ be the exceptional curve over $z\in Z$. Then define \[ \varepsilon(Z)=\sup\left\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq0}:H-t\sum_{z\in Z}E_z\text{ is nef}\right\}. \] Here a real divisor class is nef if its intersection with every integral curve on $X$ is nonnegative. This makes explicit a conventional interpretation of the notation; the intended field and convention require source verification.

Question under this interpretation. For every reduced plane curve $C$ as above, does \[ \widetilde C^{\,2}<-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \varepsilon(Z)\in{\mathbb{Q}} \] hold? In addition, can $\varepsilon(Z)$ be computed for such a curve from its singular-point configuration? The source does not specify whether computation means an explicit formula, a terminating exact algorithm, or another form of determination.

The extracted statement concerns arbitrary reduced plane curves, whereas the supplied literature assessment describes a question associated with line arrangements. The intended scope of that assessment relative to this formulation is unresolved.

Short English statement

For a reduced plane curve, blow up all its singular points. If its strict transform has self-intersection less than $-1$, must the multipoint Seshadri constant of those points be rational, and how can it be computed? The ground field, Seshadri normalization, meaning of computation, and relationship to the supplied line-arrangement assessment require verification.
