Math-4969

Consistent Conical Bicombings in Metric Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. A geodesic bicombing on $X$ is a map \[ \sigma:X\times X\times[0,1]\longrightarrow X \] such that, for every $x,y\in X$ and $s,t\in[0,1]$, \[ \sigma(x,y,0)=x,\qquad \sigma(x,y,1)=y,\qquad d\left(\sigma(x,y,s),\sigma(x,y,t)\right)=|s-t|\,d(x,y). \] It is conical if, for every $x,y,x',y'\in X$ and $t\in[0,1]$, \[ d\left(\sigma(x,y,t),\sigma(x',y',t)\right) \leq (1-t)d(x,x')+t\,d(y,y'). \] It is consistent if, for every $x,y\in X$, $0\leq a\leq b\leq1$, and $u\in[0,1]$, \[ \sigma\left(\sigma(x,y,a),\sigma(x,y,b),u\right) =\sigma\left(x,y,(1-u)a+ub\right). \] These definitions use directed segments; reversibility, the additional identity $\sigma(y,x,t)=\sigma(x,y,1-t)$, is not imposed. The supplied record does not specify whether its terminology includes reversibility, so that convention requires source confirmation.

Question as recorded. Is it true that, for every metric space $(X,d)$, the existence of a conical geodesic bicombing on $X$ implies the existence of a consistent conical geodesic bicombing on $X$? The latter bicombing may differ from the initially given one.

Unresolved scope qualification. The recorded question imposes no completeness hypothesis, whereas the supplied literature assessment describes the unresolved general assertion for complete metric spaces. A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence converges in it. The record does not resolve whether completeness was intended as a hypothesis; the question above retains the literal quantification over all metric spaces.

The supplied dated curation labels the problem open, reports that the proper-space case is settled under unspecified weaker convexity assumptions, and reports no known counterexample to the complete-space assertion. Here proper means that every closed bounded subset is compact. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified mathematical claims; the unspecified weaker assumptions do not determine a precise auxiliary theorem.

Short English statement

If a metric space admits a conically controlled choice of geodesic between every pair of points, must it admit such a choice that agrees with its own subsegments? The record leaves completeness and the convention concerning reversibility unresolved. Its open-status assessment is supplied dated curation.
