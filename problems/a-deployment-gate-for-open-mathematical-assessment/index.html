Attempt: exact response equivalence in a restricted answer language

The original question about unconventional phrasing supplies no response semantics. I tested finite evaluation as a response validator for the explicitly restricted language of univariate polynomials over $\mathbb Q$, each of degree at most $d$. This excludes natural language and expressions with undeclared domains.
Proposition: Agreement at $d+1$ distinct rational inputs certifies equality of two such polynomials. Any fixed set of at most $d$ test inputs can accept an unequal polynomial pair. Cancelling rational expressions without tracking domains is a separate obstruction.
Proof: The difference of two accepted polynomials has degree at most $d$. A nonzero degree-$d$ polynomial has at most $d$ distinct roots: a root $a$ gives a factor $X-a$ by polynomial division, and repeated division at distinct roots decreases the degree each time. Thus $d+1$ agreements force zero difference. Conversely, for fixed test inputs $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ with $s\le d$, the polynomials zero and $\prod_{j=1}^s(X-a_j)$ agree at every test point but differ identically. Repeated test inputs only reduce the information. For $s=0$, the empty product is one.

Finally, $X/X$ and 1 have equal values at every nonzero input, but their natural real domains differ at zero. Reducing the first expression to 1 without preserving its excluded input therefore changes the represented partial function, even if numerator/denominator cancellation is algebraically correct. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

At $d=2$, the zero response and $X(X-1)$ pass checks at 0 and 1 but differ by two at 2. Exact arithmetic matters: no tolerance guarantee for floating-point comparisons is established. The concrete failed inference is that a finite test bank certifies unrestricted semantic equivalence. The polynomial degree bound repairs this particular inference, but the original adoption, readiness, language and implementation agenda remains unsolved; even its phrase "How people limit" is preserved without reinterpretation.
