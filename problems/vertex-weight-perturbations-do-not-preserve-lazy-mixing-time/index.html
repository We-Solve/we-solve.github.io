Math-1408

Vertex-weight perturbations do not preserve lazy mixing time

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

On a finite connected undirected graph $G$ with at least one edge, assign $W_v>0$ and define \[ P_W(u,v)=\frac{W_v}{\sum_{w\in N(u)}W_w}\quad(v\in N(u)). \] The source, attributed to Yuval Peres, asks whether changing weights by uniformly bounded factors changes mixing time only by a bounded factor. A precise convention uses $\overline P_W=(I+P_W)/2$ and worst-start total-variation mixing time $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(1/4)$. Laziness avoids periodicity of the raw walk. Bounded perturbation means $C^{-1}\le W'_v/W_v\le C$ for a fixed $C$ independent of graph size. The original remark states the analogous stability for spectral gap. The walk is reversible with stationary measure proportional to $W_u\sum_{v\in N(u)}W_v$, equivalently edge conductances $W_uW_v$.

The attached aid gives a negative mixing-time answer through a subdivision transfer. Start with any finite connected positive-conductance network $(G,c)$, put $c_u=\sum_{v\sim u}c_{uv}$ and let $Q_c=(I+P_c)/2$, $P_c(u,v)=c_{uv}/c_u$. Subdivide each edge $e=uv$ by one new vertex $m_e$ and set \[ W_u=1\quad(u\text{ original}),\qquad W_{m_e}=c_e. \] For the nonlazy lifted walk, its two-step skeleton on original vertices satisfies exactly \[ P_W^2(u,v)=Q_c(u,v). \] This identity concerns the nonlazy skeleton; the full lifted chain used for mixing is $\overline P_W$. The aid reports universal constants $c_0,C_0>0$ with \[ c_0\,t_{\mathrm{mix}}(Q_c;1/4)\le t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\overline P_W;1/4) \le C_0\,t_{\mathrm{mix}}(Q_c;1/4), \] using a large-set hitting-time comparison. The constants are independent of network size and conductance ratios in its stated theorem.

The aid then invokes Ding--Peres bounded-degree networks with edge perturbation ratios in $[1,2]$ and mixing-time ratio $\Omega(\log n/\log\log n)$. The fixed subdivided graph transfers those ratios to midpoint vertex weights in $[1,2]$, with original vertex weights unchanged, and retains an unbounded ratio of the same order. Its size is comparable to the original size for bounded degree. This refutes a universal bounded-factor conclusion even in the lazy total-variation setting, while not contradicting spectral-gap comparison. Full references and the hitting-time proof are absent from this summary.

Short English statement

Bounded vertex-weight changes need not preserve lazy mixing time up to a universal factor. The supplied aid transfers known edge-conductance sensitivity through subdivision, carefully distinguishing an exact nonlazy two-step identity from mixing of the lazy lifted chain.
