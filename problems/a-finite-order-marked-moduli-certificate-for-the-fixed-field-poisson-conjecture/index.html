A finite-order marked-moduli certificate for the fixed-field Poisson conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Original question (Kiran Kedlaya). Fix a prime power $q$. Describe, conjecturally, the distribution of $\#C({\mathbb{F}}_q)$ for genus-$g$ curves as $g\to\infty$. The source does not specify the curve class or sampling measure.

Attached unreviewed arithmetic-stack interpretation. For $g\ge2$, sample smooth projective geometrically connected curves over ${\mathbb{F}}_q$, giving each ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-isomorphism class weight $1/|\operatorname{Aut}_{{\mathbb{F}}_q}(C)|$, normalized to total probability one. Write $N_g=\#C({\mathbb{F}}_q)$ and \[\#_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal M_{g,r}({\mathbb{F}}_q)=\sum_{[C;x_1,\ldots,x_r]}\frac1{|\operatorname{Aut}_{{\mathbb{F}}_q}(C;x_1,\ldots,x_r)|},\] where the markings are ordered distinct rational points. The aid gives the factorial-moment identity \[\mu_r(g):=\frac{\#_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal M_{g,r}({\mathbb{F}}_q)}{\#_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal M_g({\mathbb{F}}_q)}={\mathbb{E}} (N_g)_r,\qquad (n)_r=n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1),\quad(n)_0=1.\] It uses the proposed Poisson mean $\lambda=q+1+1/(q-1)$, so the reconstructed fixed-field target is $\operatorname{Law}(N_g)\to\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$. This measure and mean are supplied by the aid, not explicit in the original one-line question.

The aid's finite-order certificate uses Bonferroni truncations. For integers $r,m\ge0$ put \[S_J(r;g)=\sum_{j=0}^J\frac{(-1)^j\mu_{r+j}(g)}{r!j!}.\] The reported bounds are \[S_{2m+1}(r;g)\le\Pr(N_g=r)\le S_{2m}(r;g),\qquad S_{2m}-S_{2m+1}=\frac{\mu_{r+2m+1}(g)}{r!(2m+1)!}.\] The same truncations with $\mu_j=\lambda^j$ bracket the Poisson mass $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^r/r!$. Thus finite moment errors and finite Taylor remainders yield pointwise bounds, without interchanging an unproved infinite inclusion-exclusion series with a limit.

To make the mentioned first-moment tail control explicit, any certified lower bounds $\ell_r\le\Pr(N_g=r)$ for $0\le r\le L$ give \[\Pr(N_g>L)\le\frac{\mu_1(g)-\sum_{r=0}^Lr\ell_r}{L+1}.\] For certified intervals $[\ell_r,u_r]\subseteq[0,1]$ and Poisson masses $\pi_r$, one obtains the finite-cutoff total-variation bound \[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12\sum_{r=0}^L\max\{|\ell_r-\pi_r|,|u_r-\pi_r|\}+\frac12\Pr(N_g>L)+\frac12\Pr(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)>L),\] using the displayed first-moment bound for the first tail. Moments through order $L+2m+1$ suffice for these Bonferroni intervals. The aid concludes that convergence $\mu_r(g)\to\lambda^r$ for every fixed $r$ implies total-variation convergence to this fixed Poisson law. It does not prove those marked-stack asymptotics for fixed $q$.

The aid also warns that known fixed-order asymptotics in regimes written $q(g)>g^K$ do not alone establish a moving-mean Poisson approximation as $q$ grows; the needed moment orders and error control are different. No exact theorem, exponent range or citation for that comparison is supplied.

Short English statement

Kedlaya asks for the large-genus point-count distribution over one fixed finite field. The aid chooses automorphism-weighted curves and a proposed Poisson mean, then gives finite-moment bounds that would certify convergence if the necessary marked-moduli asymptotics were known. It does not prove the fixed-field conjecture or replace it by a growing-field regime.
