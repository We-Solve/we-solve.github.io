Paramodular Forms and Isogeny Classes of Abelian Surfaces

Definitions and mathematical statement

An abelian surface over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ is an abelian variety of dimension $2$ defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. Two such surfaces are ${\mathbb{Q}}$-isogenous if there is a surjective homomorphism between them, defined over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, with finite kernel. For a positive integer $N$, let \[ \mathcal A_N=\left\{[A]_{{\mathbb{Q}}\text{-isog}}: A/{\mathbb{Q}}\text{ is an abelian surface of conductor }N\right\}, \] where the conductor is the usual arithmetic conductor of the abelian variety.

The supplied question asks whether the Brumer--Kramer paramodular correspondence gives a bijection between $\mathcal A_N$ and the appropriate paramodular forms of level $N$. If $\mathcal P_N$ denotes the intended collection of forms, the requested assertion has the schematic form \[ \Phi_N:\mathcal A_N\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\mathcal P_N, \] where $\Phi_N$ must be the intended Brumer--Kramer correspondence.

Unresolved formulation. The source does not define $\mathcal P_N$, specify an equivalence relation on its forms, or characterize $\Phi_N$. It supplies no weight, eigenform or newform conditions, coefficient-field conditions, normalization, or treatment of lifts. It also gives no restrictions on the abelian surfaces beyond their dimension, field of definition, and conductor. Thus $\mathcal A_N$ above records the literal source wording; it is not a verified identification of the domain of the precise Brumer--Kramer conjecture. No restriction on $N$ is stated, although one supplied reference concerns odd conductor. The displayed bijection records the intended mathematical target, but the missing specifications prevent a complete, unambiguous conjectural statement from being recovered from this record alone.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the Brumer--Kramer correspondence pairs the relevant ${\mathbb{Q}}$-isogeny classes of abelian surfaces of conductor $N$ bijectively with the relevant paramodular forms of level $N$. The supplied record leaves the required restrictions on surfaces, forms, and levels unspecified, so this is a qualified statement-only draft requiring mathematical review.
