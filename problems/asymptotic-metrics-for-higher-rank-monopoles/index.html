Asymptotic Metrics for Higher-Rank Monopoles

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A finite-energy monopole on $\mathbb R^3$ consists of a connection $A$ and Higgs field $\Phi$ for the gauge group, in the Bogomolny/monopole setting of the source. Framed moduli divide by gauge transformations tending to the identity at infinity. Magnetic charge is determined by the asymptotic Higgs field: one positive integer $k$ for $SU(2)$, or a vector of $N-1$ charges for maximally broken $SU(N)$. The moduli metric is the $L^2$ inner product on infinitesimal field variations perpendicular to gauge directions. Maximal symmetry breaking means the limiting Higgs value is regular, so its centralizer is a maximal torus. The source compactification adds boundaries describing clusters of lower-charge monopoles separating to infinity. Quasi-isometric metric comparison on an asymptotic region means two metrics bound one another by positive constants there, with the covering and cluster-coordinate conventions retained.
Source scope and qualifications.
The target includes arbitrary permitted magnetic-charge vectors and the compactification's cluster strata, rather than a single separated-charge example. The source calls this work in progress; local metric approximations alone do not yield the requested uniform quasi-isometric control across all relevant regions.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For $\operatorname{SU}(N)$-monopoles with maximal symmetry breaking, establish the quasi-isometric asymptotics of the monopole metric on the analogous compactification and determine whether they agree qualitatively with the $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ case.
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Asymptotic metrics for maximally broken SU(N) monopoles are known, but the proposed compactification and the claimed global quasi-isometric control for arbitrary charges remain unresolved; the record itself describes this as work in progress. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Control the higher-rank monopole metric near every compactification boundary and compare it with the SU(2) cluster asymptotics.

The exact relative-error condition for metric comparison.
Let $g_0$ be a positive metric on an asymptotic region and let $g=g_0+h$. Define the self-adjoint endomorphism $A=g_0^{-1/2}hg_0^{-1/2}$ in a local frame. If $\|A\|\leq\epsilon<1$ uniformly, then for every tangent vector $v$ \[ (1-\epsilon)g_0(v,v)\leq g(v,v)\leq(1+\epsilon)g_0(v,v). \] Indeed put $w=g_0^{1/2}v$ and bound $|w^TAw|\leq\epsilon|w|^2$. A relative estimate $\|A\|\leq C/R$ gives a uniform two-sided comparison for $R\geq2C$.
Small coordinate errors are not enough.
Take $g_0=\operatorname{diag}(R^{-4},1)$ and $h=\operatorname{diag}(R^{-2},0)$ for $R\to\infty$. Every entry of $h$ tends to zero, but $g(e_1,e_1)/g_0(e_1,e_1)=1+R^2\to\infty$. A collapsing phase direction can therefore amplify an apparently small asymptotic error. Controlling only the separated-position block would miss it.
Monopole gap.
This is a concrete criterion for the proposed quasi-isometric comparison, not an estimate for the actual higher-rank metric. One must show relative bounds simultaneously in position, phase and cluster directions, uniformly as several compactification faces meet and for each allowed magnetic-charge vector. Existing pointwise leading terms cannot be promoted by the coordinate-smallness argument just disproved. Establishing those uniform relative errors and a compatible compactification is the first unresolved step before comparing qualitatively with the $SU(2)$ ends.
