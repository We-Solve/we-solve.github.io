Math-3505

Classification of Simple Normal Surface Singularities

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Interpretive conventions. The supplied record does not specify the ground field, analytic category, deformation equivalence, or resolution convention. The following is a conditional formulation in the customary setting of complex analytic surface germs, using minimal good resolutions and analytic isomorphism. These conventions require confirmation against the cited sources.

Let $(X,x)$ be a singular normal complex analytic germ of dimension two. Call $(X,x)$ simple if, in a sufficiently small representative of a semiuniversal deformation, the germs at points of nearby fibres represent only finitely many analytic isomorphism classes. Call it rigid if every sufficiently small deformation is locally analytically trivial. These definitions concern deformations of the singularity, without imposing an additional condition such as preservation of the resolution graph.

A normal surface singularity is rational if, for a resolution $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$, one has $R^1\pi_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}=0$ near $x$. In this formulation, a rational double point is a rational surface singularity of multiplicity two, and a rational triple point is a rational surface singularity of multiplicity three.

For a minimal good resolution, let $\Gamma(X,x)$ be its weighted dual graph. Its vertices correspond to irreducible exceptional curves $E_v$, its edges record intersections, and each vertex has self-intersection weight $w_v=E_v^2$ and genus label. Say that a graph is obtained from another by decreasing vertex weights if their vertices, edges, and genus labels can be identified and their weights satisfy \[ w'_v=w_v-k_v,\qquad k_v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0} \quad\text{for every vertex }v. \] This permits all $k_v$ to vanish, so the original graphs are included; the supplied phrase "making some vertex weights more negative" does not explicitly settle this convention.

Proposed classification, conditional on these conventions. For every singular normal complex analytic surface germ $(X,x)$, \[ \begin{aligned} (X,x)\text{ is simple} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad& (X,x)\text{ is rational, and there exists a rational}\\ &\text{double point or rational triple point }(Y,y)\\ &\text{such that }\Gamma(X,x)\text{ is obtained from }\Gamma(Y,y)\\ &\text{by decreasing vertex weights as defined above.} \end{aligned} \] The supplied statement also asserts, as an intended consequence, that no singular normal surface germ is rigid: \[ \forall (X,x)\text{ in the stated category},\qquad (X,x)\text{ is not rigid}. \] Here the restriction to singular germs is essential: smooth germs are excluded. The supplied record does not provide the argument relating the classification to this additional assertion. Both assertions are presented as conjectural in the supplied dated assessment, not as established theorems.

Short English statement

The conjecture classifies simple normal surface singularities as precisely the rational ones whose resolution graphs come from rational double or triple points by decreasing self-intersection weights. It also asserts the nonexistence of rigid singular normal surface germs. The complex analytic setting, deformation definitions, resolution convention, and inclusion of unchanged graphs used here require source confirmation.
