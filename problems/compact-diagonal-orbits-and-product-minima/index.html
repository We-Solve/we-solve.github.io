Math-328

Compact diagonal orbits and product minima

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X_3=SL(3,{\mathbb{R}})/SL(3,{\mathbb{Z}})$ be the space of unimodular lattices and let $A$ be the positive diagonal subgroup of determinant one. Conjecture 30, attributed to G. Margulis, is \[ \text{for every compact }K\subset X_3, \quad\#\{A L:A L\text{ closed and }A L\subset K\}<\infty. \] Containment of the entire orbit is required, not merely intersection with $K$. Such closed orbits are compact. A full diagonal-group convention differs by finitely many components and must be kept consistent.

For $B=(b_{ij})\in GL(3,{\mathbb{R}})$ define \[ F_B(z)=\prod_{i=1}^3\left(\sum_{j=1}^3b_{ij}z_j\right),\quad \Delta(F_B)=\det B,\quad m(F_B)=\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^3\setminus\{0\}} \left|\frac{F_B(z)}{\det B}\right|. \] Nonsingularity is essential. Let $L_B=|\det B|^{-1/3}B{\mathbb{Z}}^3$, a covolume-one lattice, and put \[ \mu(L)=\inf_{v\in L\setminus\{0\}}|v_1v_2v_3|, \qquad\operatorname{sys}(L)=\min_{v\in L\setminus\{0\}}\|v\|_2. \] The aid's exact identities are $m(F_B)=\mu(L_B)$ and \[ \inf_{a\in A}\operatorname{sys}(aL)=\sqrt3\,\mu(L)^{1/3}. \] The second follows by balancing the three nonzero coordinate magnitudes; when a coordinate vanishes the infimum is zero. No attainment of this infimum is required.

For $\varepsilon>0$, the canonical Mahler set \[ K_\varepsilon=\{L:\operatorname{sys}(L)\geq\sqrt3\,\varepsilon^{1/3}\} \] is compact, and the threshold equivalence is \[ \mu(L)\geq\varepsilon\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A L\subset K_\varepsilon. \] Conversely any compact $K$ has a positive systole minimum, giving a positive $\mu$ threshold for every full orbit contained in it. Hence Conjecture 30 is exactly finiteness, for every $\varepsilon>0$, of compact $A$-orbits with $\mu\geq\varepsilon$. This is geometric cofinality, not yet a theorem about the number of scalar spectrum values for all forms.

The source introduces a numerical Markov-spectrum reformulation but stops at "Then". The aid audits the proposed all-form statement that $\{\mu(L):\mu(L)\geq\varepsilon\}$ is finite for every $\varepsilon>0$. To derive it from Conjecture 30, one needs every positive value represented by a compact orbit. The bounded-orbit closing assertion referred to as Conjecture 29 would suffice, since $\mu>0$ makes the orbit relatively compact; value coverage alone is a weaker sufficient substitute. In the reverse direction, numerical finiteness additionally needs finite fibers: \[ \text{for each }c>0,\quad \#\{\text{compact }A\text{-orbits }O:\mu(O)=c\}<\infty. \] Finitely many values cannot count orbit objects without this condition. Neither gate follows from Mahler compactness alone. Thus the workshop's reformulation is retained as a source claim, while the supplied reduction explicitly isolates the extra arithmetic inputs and proves no unconditional implication between the open conjectures.

Short English statement

Margulis asks whether a compact lattice-space set contains only finitely many full closed diagonal orbits. The supplied aid exactly converts compact containment into a positive product-minimum threshold. Turning that into finiteness of numerical spectrum values requires separate value-coverage and finite-fiber statements, which cannot be inferred from the geometric identity alone.
