Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.65

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. For $g\in G$, define its conjugacy class and centralizer by \[ g^G=\{x^{-1}gx:x\in G\},\qquad C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}. \] The size of this conjugacy class is $|g^G|=[G:C_G(g)]$. For a positive integer $m$, let $\pi(m)$ be the set of distinct prime divisors of $m$, with $\pi(1)=\varnothing$. Define \[ \rho(G)=\bigcup_{g\in G}\pi(|g^G|),\qquad \sigma(G)=\max_{g\in G}|\pi(|g^G|)|. \] Thus primes are counted without multiplicity, and the source's phrase "order of a conjugacy class" is understood as its cardinality. In particular, $\rho(G)=\varnothing$ and $\sigma(G)=0$ when $G$ is abelian, including when $G$ is trivial.

Problem (catalogue entry; permanent catalogue entry). Is it true that \[ \forall\,G\text{ finite},\qquad |\rho(G)|\leq 3\sigma(G)? \] The supplied source describes this as a well-known problem, proposed by A. Moretó in 1999.

Supplied background qualifications. The source attributes to C. Casolo and S. Dolfi (1996) a family of finite groups $(G_n)$ for which the ratios are defined and \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\rho(G_n)|}{\sigma(G_n)}=3. \] Accordingly, the source states that the coefficient $3$ cannot be reduced in a universal bound of the displayed form. Its comment of 2009 reports a quadratic bound due to A. Moretó (2005) and a linear bound due to C. Casolo and S. Dolfi (2007). No constants or precise formulations of those earlier bounds are supplied. These background assertions and the registry's open status are supplied source assessments, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

For every finite group, must the total number of distinct primes occurring in its conjugacy class sizes be at most three times the largest number of distinct primes occurring in any one class size? The supplied source reports examples approaching the factor $3$, and a 2009 comment reports earlier quadratic and linear bounds.
