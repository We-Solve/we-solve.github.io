Math-4754

Existence of Noncircular Degenerate Herman Rings

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}$, and let $f:\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\to\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$ be a nonconstant rational map of degree $d$. The supplied statement does not explicitly assume $d\geq 2$. An analytic Jordan curve is understood here as the image of a regular real-analytic embedding of the circle into $\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$; this regularity convention requires confirmation from the source.

Consider an analytic Jordan curve $\gamma\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$ for which there exists an integer $p\geq 1$ such that \[ f^p(\gamma)=\gamma, \qquad h=f^p|_\gamma:\gamma\to\gamma \] is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism with irrational Poincaré rotation number. More explicitly, after identifying the oriented curve with ${\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$, a lift $H:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ of $h$ satisfies $H(t+1)=H(t)+1$, and \[ \rho(h)=\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H^n(t)-t}{n}\right]\in({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\setminus({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}). \] Only irrationality is used, so reversing the chosen orientation does not affect the condition.

A Herman ring of $f$ means a periodic doubly connected Fatou component $U$ such that, for its period $q\geq 1$, the restriction $f^q|_U$ is conformally conjugate to an irrational rigid rotation of a round annulus $\{z:r<|z|<1\}$, where $0<r<1$. Require additionally that \[ \gamma\not\subseteq\overline U \quad\text{for every Herman ring }U\text{ of }f, \] where closure is taken in $\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$. This interprets the source's exclusion as noncontainment; it does not require disjointness from every such closure. Let $\mathcal D(f)$ denote the collection of curves satisfying these conditions, the candidates called degenerate Herman rings in the supplied problem. No separate definition of that term is supplied.

The existence question is whether there exist $f$ and $\gamma\in\mathcal D(f)$ such that $\gamma$ is not a circle. The source leaves the circle convention unspecified: in a chosen affine coordinate, an ordinary circle has the form $\{z:|z-a|=R\}$, with $a\in{\mathbb{C}}$ and $R>0$; a generalized circle on $\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$ may also be a straight line together with $\infty$. The intended convention must be fixed before this exclusion has a unique meaning.

The original statement also asks whether the number of degenerate Herman rings can be estimated in terms of $d=\deg f$; this question is absent from the cleaned statement. One possible uniform interpretation is the existence of a function $B:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$ such that \[ \#\mathcal D(f)\leq B(\deg f) \quad\text{for every admissible rational map }f. \] This is a provisional interpretation only: the source does not specify whether to count individual curves, periodic cycles of curves, or only noncircular examples, nor whether the intended degree range is $d\geq 2$. These choices remain unresolved.

Short English statement

Ask whether a rational map can have a noncircular analytic periodic Jordan curve on which an iterate is a homeomorphism with irrational rotation number, while the curve is contained in no Herman-ring closure. The original record also asks for an estimate of their number in terms of the map's degree. The circle convention, degree range, meaning of degenerate Herman ring, and counting convention require clarification. The supplied partial-resolution assessment concerns smooth examples and distinguishes the stronger analytic question.
