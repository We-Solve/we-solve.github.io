Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.91

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$ be the first-order language of groups. A group $G$ is stable if, for every $L_{\mathrm{grp}}$-formula $\phi(\bar x,\bar y)$, there exists an integer $n\geq 0$ such that, for every integer $m\geq 1$ and tuples \[ a_i\in G^{|\bar x|},\qquad b_i\in G^{|\bar y|}\qquad(0\leq i<m), \] if \[ G\models\phi(a_i,b_j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i<j \qquad\text{for all }0\leq i,j<m, \] then $m\leq n$. Thus $G$ is non-stable precisely when there is one such formula $\phi$ for which these configurations exist for every integer $m\geq 1$.

A group is hyperbolic if it has a finite generating set whose Cayley graph, with every edge of length one and its path metric, has $\delta$-thin geodesic triangles for some real $\delta\geq 0$: every side of every geodesic triangle lies in the closed $\delta$-neighborhood of the other two sides. A group is virtually free if it contains a free subgroup of finite index. The supplied record does not specify whether finite generation is additionally intended for virtually free groups in part (b); the definition just given imposes no rank restriction on the free subgroup, and this convention requires review against the original source. Write \[ \mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})=\{A\in M_2({\mathbb{Z}}):\det A=1\}, \] with matrix multiplication.

The supplied background attributes to Sela the theorem that every torsion-free hyperbolic group is stable; torsion-free means that the identity is the only element of finite order. This attribution is supplied context and has not been independently verified here.

For each of the following, determine whether the displayed assertion holds:

  1. (a) There exists a hyperbolic group that is non-stable.
  2. (b) There exists a virtually free group that is non-stable, subject to the unresolved finite-generation convention above.
  3. (c) The group $\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})$ is non-stable.

Short English statement

Stability requires a finite bound, depending on the formula, on the lengths of configurations in which the formula holds exactly when the first index is smaller than the second. The problem asks whether non-stable groups occur among hyperbolic groups and virtually free groups, and whether $\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})$ is non-stable. The finite-generation convention in the virtually free question remains unspecified.
