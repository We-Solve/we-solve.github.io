Math-5583

A fifth-root functional equation

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Set $\omega=\exp(2\pi i/5)$ and let \[ \mathcal S=\{f:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\text{ entire}:\ f(\omega z)f(\omega^{-1}z)=f(z)-1\text{ for every }z\in{\mathbb{C}}\}. \] Under the usual meaning of rotation of the independent variable, the literal uniqueness question is whether \[ \forall f,g\in\mathcal S\ \exists\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\ \forall z\in{\mathbb{C}}, \qquad g(z)=f(e^{i\theta}z). \] This is uniqueness of a rotation orbit, not an assertion of a specified growth order or normalization. If "unique" also includes existence, one must additionally require $\mathcal S\ne\varnothing$.

The supplied question states no nonconstancy condition, normalization, growth condition, or restriction on the rotation angle. Constants obey $c^2=c-1$, so the literal class includes two different constant solutions, which rotations cannot identify. Also, the equation is invariant under $f(z)\mapsto f(az)$ for every complex $a$, and nonzero dilation is a broader equivalence than rotation. These features require clarification of the intended normalized solution class or equivalence relation before the source's open-uniqueness interpretation can be adopted.

The research summary also cites the distinct equation \[ f(\lambda)+f(\omega\lambda)f(\omega^{-1}\lambda)=1. \] Its sign convention is not the displayed source equation; no identification between their solution classes is assumed here.

Short English statement

For the stated fifth-root functional equation, does every entire solution differ from every other only by rotating the argument? The literal question includes inequivalent constant solutions and gives no normalization to control dilation, so its intended open formulation needs clarification.
