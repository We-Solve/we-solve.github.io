Math-4243

Radin Forcing and Simultaneous Tree Properties

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A tree is a partially ordered set $(T,<_{T})$ such that the strict predecessors of each node are well ordered by $<_{T}$. The height of a node is the order type of its predecessors, and $T_{\xi}$ denotes the set of nodes of height $\xi$. For an infinite cardinal $\lambda$, a $\lambda$-Aronszajn tree is a tree of height $\lambda$ such that $|T_{\xi}|<\lambda$ for every $\xi<\lambda$, with no chain of order type $\lambda$. For an infinite cardinal $\mu$, a $\mu^{+}$-Aronszajn tree is special if there is a function $f:T\to\mu$ such that $f(s)\ne f(t)$ whenever $s<_{T}t$; equivalently, its nodes are the union of at most $\mu$ antichains.

For a limit ordinal $\kappa$, a set $C\subseteq\kappa$ is closed unbounded, or a club, if it is unbounded in $\kappa$ and contains every nonzero limit ordinal $\delta<\kappa$ satisfying $\sup(C\cap\delta)=\delta$.

The primary question is whether there is a Radin forcing $\mathbb P$, at an appropriate $\kappa$, which adds a club $C\subseteq\kappa$ such that in the forcing extension \[ \forall\mu\in C\;\left(\mu\text{ is a cardinal}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{there is no special }\mu^{+}\text{-Aronszajn tree}\right). \] Here $\mu^{+}$ denotes the successor cardinal computed in the extension, under the usual interpretation of a forcing assertion. The quantifier ranges over cardinal members of $C$, and the assertion must hold simultaneously for all such members. The source supplies neither the hypotheses on $\kappa$ nor the measure sequence or precise variant of Radin forcing. Thus this is a schematic existence question, not a fully specified forcing construction or relative-consistency assertion. The tree definitions above concern infinite cardinals; the source does not explicitly restrict the cardinal members of $C$ to be infinite.

The original statement also asks a second question, absent from the cleaned statement: is it consistent that \[ \forall\alpha<\omega_{1}\;\left(\alpha\text{ is a limit ordinal}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{there is no }\aleph_{\alpha+1}\text{-Aronszajn tree}\right)? \] The ordinals and cardinals in this assertion are computed in the proposed model. The source does not specify the base theory or consistency-strength assumptions, or whether its convention for limit ordinals includes $0$. These qualifications remain unresolved. This second assertion excludes all Aronszajn trees at the indicated cardinals; the first excludes only special Aronszajn trees. No equivalence or implication between the two questions is supplied.

Short English statement

The primary question asks whether Radin forcing can add a club whose cardinal members simultaneously have no special Aronszajn trees at their successors. The original record additionally asks for the consistency of having no Aronszajn trees at $\aleph_{\alpha+1}$ for every countable limit ordinal $\alpha$. The forcing hypotheses, consistency assumptions, and convention concerning $\alpha=0$ require clarification.
