Moments of L-functions — Moments of the Riemann zeta function — Problem 26

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\zeta(s)$ denote the meromorphic continuation of the Riemann zeta function, initially defined by $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. For $p>0$ and $T\geq 2$, define the critical-line moment \[ M_p(T)=\int_T^{2T}\left|\zeta\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\right|^p\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] To express the zero-density question, use the convention \[ N(\sigma,T)=\#\left\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\beta<1,\ \beta\geq\sigma,\ 0<\gamma\leq T\right\}, \qquad \tfrac12<\sigma<1, \] where zeros are counted with multiplicity. These counting endpoints and parameter ranges are conventions for this draft; the source does not specify them.

Research problem (Timothy Trudgian). Investigate quantitatively how upper bounds for $M_p(T)$ can be used to obtain upper bounds for $N(\sigma,T)$ as $T\to\infty$, with the dependence on the moment estimate and on $\sigma$ made explicit. The source states that Ingham's zero-density estimate uses $M_4(T)$. Determine whether and how other nontrivial moment estimates, specifically including Heath-Brown's estimate for $M_{12}(T)$, can be used in such zero-density results. Investigate analogous connections between moments and zero density for other $L$-functions.

Unresolved scope. The source poses an investigation rather than a single quantified conjecture. It supplies neither a target density bound nor the precise moment estimates to be used. It also does not specify a class of other $L$-functions, their moment integrals, or whether the intended density estimates concern individual functions or families. These choices must be stated in any precise theorem developed from the question; no universal implication from an arbitrary moment bound is asserted here.

Short English statement

Investigate how estimates for moments of the Riemann zeta function lead to estimates counting zeros near the line $\operatorname{Re}s=1$. In particular, examine whether moments other than the fourth, including Heath-Brown's twelfth-moment estimate, can be used, and whether the method extends to other $L$-functions. The source leaves the desired bounds and the class of $L$-functions unspecified.
