Math-1870

Compactness of Kőnig-property

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $H=(V,E)$ be a hypergraph, with edges regarded as subsets of $V$. A matching is a family $\mathcal D\subseteq E$ of pairwise disjoint edges. A vertex cover is a set $C\subseteq V$ satisfying $C\cap e\ne\varnothing$ for every $e\in E$.

The Kőnig property means that there exist a matching $\mathcal D$ and a choice $c(e)\in e$ for each $e\in\mathcal D$ such that \[ C=\{c(e):e\in\mathcal D\} \] is a vertex cover. Thus the cover consists exactly of one selected vertex from each matching edge, with no additional vertices; equality of infinite cardinalities alone is not the definition.

Compactness conjecture (R. Aharoni and N. Bowler, independently). Suppose every edge of $H$ is finite and, for every finite subfamily $E'\subseteq E$, the hypergraph $(V,E')$ has the Kőnig property. Then $H$ has the Kőnig property.

The finite-subfamily condition retains the entire vertex set $V$. Its witnessing matching and cover may depend on $E'$ and need not come with any prescribed compatibility. No bound on edge sizes, vertex degrees, or the cardinalities of $V$ and $E$ is imposed. Empty edges are automatically excluded by the hypothesis, since a singleton subfamily containing one could have no vertex cover. The source recalls finite bipartite graphs as the classical Kőnig-theorem case and reports no verified resolution of this infinite-hypergraph conjecture.

Short English statement

If every finite selection of finite hyperedges has a matching with a one-vertex-per-matched-edge cover, must the entire hypergraph have such a matching and cover? The conjecture allows arbitrary infinite hypergraphs and unbounded finite edge sizes.
