A noncompact index obstruction already at degree zero.
On $\mathbb C$, the holomorphic monomials $1,z,z^2,\ldots$ have equivariant weights $1,t,t^2,\ldots$ for the scaling action. The formal character is \[\sum_{n\ge0}t^n=\frac1{1-t},\qquad |t|<1. \] This is finite as a rational equivariant expression away from $t=1$, but has no finite nonequivariant limit. On $\mathbb C^d$ with independent weights the corresponding character is $\prod_{j=1}^d(1-t_j)^{-1}$. The trivial canonical bundle of affine space therefore does not by itself remove divergence of even the simplest holomorphic trace.
Why a regularization choice matters.
Multiplying by $1-t$ would assign the first expression the value one at $t=1$, while taking its finite Laurent part in the coordinate $s=1-t$ gives zero. Changing to $u=-\log t$ changes the finite part: $(1-e^{-u})^{-1}=u^{-1}+1/2+O(u)$, obtained by inverting the Taylor series of $e^{-u}$. Thus an unspecified finite-part prescription is coordinate dependent. No canonical ordinary index follows from the meromorphic character alone.
Gap.
An elliptic genus has additional graded tangent-bundle factors, so this character calculation is an obstruction test rather than its full definition. A sufficient noncompact construction must specify an equivariant/compact-support/analytic framework, prove finiteness or meromorphic localization in that framework, and explain any specialization.
