Consequently, a natural idea to circumvent the challenges induced by nonconvexity is to use such a decomposition f = g − h and solve the desired problem separately for g and h. Here the attempted simplification is to remove common convex bending from a univariate pair; the root supplied the slope-splitting route used in the analysis.

Exact cancellation of common bending in one dimension

Let $G,H$ be convex CPWL functions on $\mathbb R$ with finitely many breakpoints. At each position in the union of their breakpoint sets let $\alpha_t,\beta_t\ge0$ be their respective slope jumps, zero if absent. Define \[ C(x)=\sum_t\min(\alpha_t,\beta_t)(x-t)_+. \] Then $G-C$ and $H-C$ remain convex: their jumps are respectively $\max(\alpha_t-\beta_t,0)$ and $\max(\beta_t-\alpha_t,0)$, and their affine left tails are unchanged. Their difference is still $f=G-H$. This proof follows by integrating the stepwise derivatives, or by the hinge representation obtained by matching the affine left tail and every jump.

Writing $p=\#\{t:\alpha_t>\beta_t\}$ and $q=\#\{t:\alpha_t<\beta_t\}$, the resulting counts are $(p+1,q+1)$. These are globally minimal over every convex pair representing $f$: at a positive jump of $f$, any such pair must have a strictly positive jump in its first member; at a negative jump it must have one in the second, because convex jumps are nonnegative and their difference equals the jump of $f$. This gives independent lower bounds of $p+1$ and $q+1$, even if other positions are allowed. Thus removing common bending achieves both lower bounds in this restricted dimension.

An explicit obstruction to reading complexity from an arbitrary pair

For the affine function $f(x)=x$, the pair $(g,h)=(x,0)$ has one piece in each member. For any distinct $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ and positive coefficients $a_j$, adding $C(x)=\sum_{j=1}^ra_j(x-t_j)_+$ to both gives the same $f$ but $r+1$ pieces in each member. Therefore a raw convex-difference representation can be arbitrarily more complex than the minimum. Common-bend cancellation removes all these artificial pieces. Affine functions do not affect convexity, so the example is valid on the whole line.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact signed-jump tests check cancellation and the affine example with four common bends. In higher dimensions, a local prescription of bends may fail compatibility around faces; this one-dimensional cancellation proof does not establish a global convex function that removes them. Neither the original general-dimensional question nor an independent new problem for this duplicate is solved.

Global piece minima on the real line

Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be continuous piecewise affine with finitely many actual breakpoints $t_1<\cdots<t_m$, nonzero slope jumps $\Delta_j$, and affine formula $ax+b$ to the left of all breakpoints. Let $p$ count positive jumps and $q$ negative jumps, so $p+q=m$. Count pieces as maximal intervals on which a convex CPWL function is affine; an affine function has one piece.
Proposition: Among all representations $f=g-h$ by convex CPWL functions, the coordinatewise minimum piece counts are $(p+1,q+1)$, attainable simultaneously. In particular the minimum sum of piece counts is $m+2$.
Proof: Matching the initial affine formula and successive slopes gives \[ f(x)=ax+b+\sum_{j=1}^m\Delta_j(x-t_j)_+. \] Indeed the right side has the same slope on every intervening interval and agrees on the first interval; continuity then gives equality everywhere. Set \[ g(x)=ax+b+\sum_{\Delta_j>0}\Delta_j(x-t_j)_+, \qquad h(x)=\sum_{\Delta_j<0}(-\Delta_j)(x-t_j)_+. \] Each hinge has nondecreasing slope, so both functions are convex, and their strictly positive jumps occur at exactly $p$ and $q$ distinct positions. Thus they have $p+1$ and $q+1$ pieces.

For any other convex CPWL pair $G,H$, the one-sided derivatives exist and their jumps $\alpha_j,\beta_j$ at $t_j$ are nonnegative. Since $G-H=f$, $\alpha_j-\beta_j=\Delta_j$. A positive $\Delta_j$ forces a positive jump of $G$, and a negative one forces a positive jump of $H$. Hence $G$ has at least $p$ distinct breakpoints and $H$ at least $q$. Additional breakpoints outside this list cannot reduce either count. This proves the unrestricted one-dimensional lower bounds. ∎

For example $f(x)=x_+-2(x-1)_++(x-2)_+$ has minimal counts $(3,2)$. For rational sorted breakpoint/slope data the construction uses $O(m)$ arithmetic operations; sorting unsorted distinct positions costs $O(m\log m)$ comparisons. This is an arithmetic count, not a claim that bit lengths are constant.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact checks compare the hinge formulas interval by interval and count the jump signs, including the affine case $m=0$. In higher dimensions, independently repairing jumps across faces need not yield globally compatible convex functions. This proof supplies neither an enumeration bound for unknown complexes nor a global minimum algorithm in arbitrary dimension.
