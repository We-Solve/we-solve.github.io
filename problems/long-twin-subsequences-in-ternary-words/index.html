Math-6472

Long Twin Subsequences in Ternary Words

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be an alphabet of cardinality $k\geq 2$, and let $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\in A^n$. A subsequence of $w$ is a word $(w_{i_1},\ldots,w_{i_\ell})$ with $1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_\ell\leq n$; its positions need not be consecutive. Two subsequences are twins if they are identical as words and their sets of positions are disjoint. Thus a pair of twins of length $\ell$ consists of indices \[ 1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_\ell\leq n,\qquad 1\leq j_1<\cdots<j_\ell\leq n, \] with $\{i_1,\ldots,i_\ell\}\cap\{j_1,\ldots,j_\ell\}=\varnothing$ and $w_{i_r}=w_{j_r}$ for every $1\leq r\leq\ell$. Define $t(w)$ to be the maximum such $\ell$, allowing $\ell=0$, and define \[ t(n,k)=\min_{w\in A^n}t(w). \] The length counts the symbols in each twin separately, so $0\leq t(w)\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

Fix $|A|=3$, and let $W_3(n)$ be uniformly distributed on $A^n$. The supplied problem asks both of the following questions as $n\to\infty$:

  1. Is it true that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(t(W_3(n))\geq\left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)n\right)=1? \] This expresses the supplied asymptotically almost sure assertion $t(W_3(n))=n/2-o(n)$.
  2. Is it true that \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t(n,3)}{n}=\frac12? \] Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N\in{\mathbb{N}}$ such that, for every integer $n\geq N$ and every $w\in A^n$, \[ t(w)\geq\left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)n. \]
The extracted original uses the term "subwords" and contains corrupted notation. The subsequence interpretation and the uniform random model above follow the supplied clean statement; the original extraction alone does not fully specify the random model. These are questions, with the open status supplied by the record rather than independently verified here.

Short English statement

Must every sufficiently long ternary word contain two identical subsequences, using disjoint positions, each of length $n/2-o(n)$? Does the same conclusion hold with probability tending to one for a uniformly random ternary word? Length refers to each of the two subsequences. Both assertions are posed as questions requiring mathematical review.
