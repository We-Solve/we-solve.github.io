Symplectic Extremal Quasimorphisms on Free Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a free group, and write $[G,G]$ for its commutator subgroup, generated by the commutators $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. For $h\in[G,G]$, its commutator length $\operatorname{cl}_G(h)$ is the least number of commutators whose product is $h$, with $\operatorname{cl}_G(1)=0$. Its stable commutator length is \[ \operatorname{scl}_G(h)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cl}_G(h^n)}{n}. \] A quasimorphism is a function $\phi:G\to{\mathbb{R}}$ with finite defect \[ D(\phi)=\sup_{x,y\in G}|\phi(xy)-\phi(x)-\phi(y)|<\infty. \] It is homogeneous if $\phi(x^n)=n\phi(x)$ for all $x\in G$ and $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$. Under the usual positive-defect convention in stable commutator length, a homogeneous quasimorphism is extremal for $g\in[G,G]$ if \[ D(\phi)>0,\qquad \frac{\phi(g)}{2D(\phi)}=\operatorname{scl}_G(g). \] Source question. For every free group $G$ and every $g\in[G,G]$, does there exist an extremal quasimorphism for $g$ arising from symplectic geometry?

To express the intended restriction, let $\mathcal S_G$ stand for the class of quasimorphisms on $G$ meant by "arising from symplectic geometry." If homogeneous quasimorphisms with positive defect are intended, the question has the schematic form \[ \forall\,G\text{ free}\quad\forall\,g\in[G,G]\quad \exists\,\phi\in\mathcal S_G:\quad \phi\text{ is homogeneous},\quad D(\phi)>0,\quad \phi(g)=2D(\phi)\operatorname{scl}_G(g). \] The supplied record does not define $\mathcal S_G$, specify the permitted symplectic constructions, or explicitly specify the convention for extremality. Thus this display records a standard interpretation, not a fully determined reformulation. The source gives no rank restriction and does not exclude the identity. For free groups of rank zero or one, every homogeneous quasimorphism has zero defect, so the positive-defect interpretation requires a qualification absent from the source. Allowing zero defect in the equality alone would make the zero quasimorphism satisfy it for every $g$; that equality therefore cannot by itself resolve the intended boundary convention.

Short English statement

The question asks whether each element of the commutator subgroup of a free group admits a quasimorphism that realizes its stable commutator length and comes from symplectic geometry. The permitted geometric constructions and the treatment of zero defect and low-rank free groups are unspecified. The supplied literature assessment also discusses every extremal quasimorphism, a stronger quantifier than the existence question stated here.
