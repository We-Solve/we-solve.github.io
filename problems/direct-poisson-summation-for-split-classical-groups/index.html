Math-4986

Direct Poisson Summation for Split Classical Groups

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a split classical group, let $\widehat G$ denote its complex dual group, and let $\rho\colon\widehat G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)$ be its standard representation on a complex vector space $V$.

Requested statement. Prove directly the $(\sigma,\rho)$-Poisson summation formula for split classical groups $G$, with $\rho$ as above.

Unresolved formulation. The supplied record does not define $\sigma$, specify the base field or the precise family of classical groups, or state the Poisson summation identity. It also does not specify the test-function space, summation domain, transform, normalizations, convergence conditions, or any correction terms required by that identity. Thus the statement above preserves the source's named target, but an explicit identity with complete hypotheses and quantifiers cannot be recovered from the supplied record alone. In particular, no automorphic hypotheses on $\sigma$ are imposed here by inference. The requirement that the proof be direct is retained, although the record does not define which methods this excludes.

Supplied qualification. The dated curation states that the formula is proved when the relevant global functorial transfer to $\operatorname{GL}_n$ is known and cuspidal, and that $\operatorname{GL}_n$ has a direct theorem. It further states that this conditional mechanism does not establish the conjecture for all pairs $(G,\rho)$. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified assertions or additional hypotheses completing the missing formulation.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a direct proof of the named $(\sigma,\rho)$-Poisson summation formula for split classical groups, using the standard representation of the complex dual group. The record omits the identity and its essential hypotheses, including the meaning of $\sigma$, so this remains a statement-only draft requiring source clarification and human mathematical review. Its partially solved label is supplied dated curation.
