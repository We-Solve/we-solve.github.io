Two-dimensional models for every triangle-free right-angled Artin group

Two-dimensional models for every triangle-free right-angled Artin group

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: For every finite triangle-free graph Γ with at least one edge, its right-angled Artin group has cd_Z=gd=2, with an explicit finite square-complex classifying space.

Let Γ have one generator v for each vertex and a relation [v,w]=1 for each edge. Its square complex X_Γ has a single vertex, a circle for each generator, and one square torus cell for each edge. Triangle-freeness means there are no higher clique cells. We prove that X_Γ is aspherical, rather than inferring this from its dimension or homology.

We use the elementary graph-of-spaces fact: if vertex spaces and edge spaces are connected aspherical CW complexes, and the edge-to-vertex maps induce injections on fundamental groups, the resulting graph of mapping cylinders is aspherical. To see why, its universal cover is a tree of lifted vertex spaces connected by lifted edge cylinders. The incidence graph is the Bass–Serre tree, obtained from cosets of vertex and edge groups; reduced normal forms in an amalgam or HNN extension show that it has no nontrivial reduced circuit. Each lifted vertex or edge space is contractible. Over any finite subtree their union is contractible by removing leaf vertices and their contractible mapping cylinders, or by the homotopy pushout property of cofibrations. Every sphere in the whole CW universal cover is homotopic into a finite such union (cellular compactness), so all its homotopy groups vanish; the simply connected CW complex is contractible by Whitehead’s theorem. This proves the fact. The group normal-form ingredient here is the usual cancellation lemma: a word returning along the tree must have an adjacent inverse stable-edge pair whose intervening vertex word is in the corresponding edge subgroup; removing such a pair shortens the circuit. It is an algebraic reduced-word assertion, not an assumption that the proposed space is already aspherical.

Proceed by induction on the number of vertices. Choose a vertex v, let Γ' delete v and let L be its neighbor set. As Γ is triangle-free, L has no edges, and its right-angled Artin group is the free group F_L. The group G' of Γ' contains F_L injectively: the inclusion of the generator subpresentation has a left inverse sending all other generators to one. The new presentation is the HNN extension of G' with stable letter v commuting with F_L. Topologically, take X_Γ' and attach a cylinder R_L×[0,1], where R_L is the bouquet of the L-circles, with both ends attached by its natural inclusion into X_Γ'. Its central longitudinal edge is the v-circle, and its squares are exactly the [v,w] cells for w∈L. Thus it is precisely X_Γ. The vertex space is aspherical by induction; R_L is a graph and hence aspherical; both subgroup maps are injective. The graph-of-spaces argument gives asphericity at this step. Empty L is permitted and simply adds a free circle. The empty graph and one-vertex graph start the induction.

Therefore X_Γ is a two-dimensional K(G,1), giving gd G≤2 and cd_Z G≤2 from its augmented cellular free resolution. For the reverse inequality, choose an edge {v,w}. Sending every other generator to one gives a retraction G↠Z² with a section supplied by v,w. Pullback in group cohomology is therefore injective, and H²(Z²;Z)=Z survives in H²(G;Z). This forces cd_Z G≥2. Hence cd_Z G=gd G=2.

This includes graphs with arbitrary many vertices and cycles, for example the five-cycle. It is not limited to direct products of two free groups: a five-cycle is not a graph join. If Γ has no edges, G is free and both dimensions are one (or zero for the empty graph), so the at-least-one-edge hypothesis correctly isolates the dimension-two family. Graphs with triangles need higher clique cells and are not covered by the stated two-dimensional construction. Arbitrary groups of cohomological dimension two remain outside this proof.

Relation to earlier contributions. The exploratory prior proves Z² and products of two free groups using products of trees; the other prior announces an unverified counterexample. The present graph-of-spaces induction covers every finite triangle-free right-angled Artin group, including nonjoin graphs such as a five-cycle, and supplies the actual universal-cover argument rather than just a module resolution.

Result 1.

There is a group $G$, residually finite and finitely generated, for which \[ \operatorname{cd}G=2,\qquad\operatorname{gd}G=3. \] It takes a height kernel in a right-angled Artin group built from an acyclic flag triangulation of the presentation complex \[ \langle x,y\mid x^2=y^5,\ x^2=(xy^{-1})^3\rangle. \] Its nonexistence conclusion applies to every two-dimensional $K(G,1)$, including those with infinitely many cells.

Short English statement

If a group has integral cohomological dimension two, must it admit a genuinely two-dimensional classifying space?

Research attempt: an explicit dimension-two resolution and product-of-trees models

Status. We prove the conclusion for $\mathbb Z^2$ and for products of two nontrivial finitely generated free groups. We do not realize arbitrary projective resolutions geometrically. The primary Bestvina--Brady paper, inspected through [R1], studies finiteness properties of subgroups of right-angled Artin groups; it does not permit identifying all cohomological and geometric conditions without further work.

1. An exact algebraic calculation for the torus group

Let $R=\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]$, the group ring of $\mathbb Z^2$. Consider \[0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{d_2}R^2\xrightarrow{d_1}R \xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0, \quad d_2(c)=((1-y)c,(x-1)c),\quad d_1(a,b)=(x-1)a+(y-1)b.\tag{1}\] Here $\epsilon$ evaluates both variables at 1. Its kernel is $(x-1,y-1)$, so exactness at the last $R$ follows. If $d_1(a,b)=0$, reducing modulo $x-1$ gives $(y-1)\overline b=0$ in the integral domain $\mathbb Z[y^{\pm1}]$. Thus $b=(x-1)c$; cancellation in $R$ then gives $a=(1-y)c$. This proves exactness at $R^2$. The first map is injective because $x-1$ is not a zero divisor. Hence $1$ is a free resolution of length two.

Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,\mathbb Z)$ with the trivial action makes both differentials zero, since $x-1$ and $y-1$ act by zero. It follows that $H^2(\mathbb Z^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, proving the projective dimension cannot be smaller than two. The torus is independently a two-dimensional $K(\mathbb Z^2,1)$: its universal cover is $\mathbb R^2$, with the translation action. Thus both dimensions are exactly two, and the resolution is the cellular chain calculation of an actual space.

2. A larger family with genuine geometric models

Let $F_r,F_s$ be free groups with $r,s\geq1$, and take graphs $R_r,R_s$ consisting of bouquets of $r,s$ circles. Their universal covers are trees. The square complex $R_r\times R_s$ has fundamental group $F_r\times F_s$ and universal cover the product of those trees, which contracts coordinatewise. It is therefore a two-dimensional classifying space.

The elementary cellular chain complex of a bouquet has zero differential and first homology $\mathbb Z^r$. The product chain complex gives $H^2(R_r\times R_s;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{rs}\ne0$. Equivalently this is the torsion-free Kunneth calculation. Thus cohomological dimension is at least two and the geometric model bounds it above by two. Both dimensions are exactly two throughout this family.

3. The realization step that cannot be skipped

The hypothesis in the question supplies a length-two projective resolution of a module. A CW universal cover supplies a based free chain complex, with boundary maps arising from actual attaching maps, together with contractibility. These are additional requirements. Arbitrary ring matrices need not already be boundary maps of a chosen presentation complex, and vanishing ordinary homology of a proposed universal cover is not sufficient until simple connectivity and the required homotopical conditions are established.

The exactness proof of $1$ succeeds because it comes with a torus model; it is not a template for replacing all projectives by two-dimensional cells. Likewise a subgroup of a group acting on a contractible complex may act on a complex of a larger dimension than its cohomological dimension. Restricting the action does not reduce the dimension automatically.

4. Verification and residual question

The proof audit checks each kernel and image in $1$, including Laurent rather than polynomial variables, and the nonvanishing cohomology lower bound. No numerical test can verify arbitrary group resolutions. The product examples also show why a two-dimensional space can have many degree-two cohomology classes without requiring higher-dimensional cells. What remains is a general realization theorem that turns the given projective-dimension condition into a contractible two-dimensional universal cover, or a rigorously established group for which every classifying space requires dimension at least three.
