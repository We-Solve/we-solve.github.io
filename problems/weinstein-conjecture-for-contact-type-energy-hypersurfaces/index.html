Math-740

Weinstein Conjecture for Contact-Type Energy Hypersurfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M^{2n},\omega)$ be a smooth symplectic manifold and let $H$ be a smooth real-valued Hamiltonian defined near a nonempty compact regular energy hypersurface $S\subset H^{-1}(e)$, so ${\,\mathrm{d}} H\ne0$ along $S$. Here $S$ is understood as a hypersurface without boundary. Use the standard local meaning of contact type: there is a vector field $Y$ on a neighborhood of $S$ with $\mathcal L_Y\omega=\omega$ and $Y$ everywhere transverse to $S$. Then \[ \alpha=(\iota_Y\omega)|_S,\qquad {\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha=\omega|_{TS},\qquad \alpha\wedge({\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha)^{n-1}\ne0. \] Assume, exactly as in the source, \[ H^1(S;{\mathbb{R}})=0. \] The conjecture asks whether every such hypersurface carries a nonconstant periodic Hamiltonian trajectory. With the convention $\iota_{X_H}\omega=-{\,\mathrm{d}} H$, the required conclusion is \[ \exists T>0\ \exists x:{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow S: \qquad \dot x(t)=X_H(x(t)),\qquad x\text{ nonconstant}. \] Equivalently in the contact-type setting, the characteristic line field $\ker(\omega|_{TS})$ should have a closed orbit. Its parametrization can be expressed using the Reeb field of $\alpha$, defined by $\alpha(R_\alpha)=1$ and $\iota_{R_\alpha}{\,\mathrm{d}}\alpha=0$; the Hamiltonian and Reeb parametrizations need not have the same period.

The source's dated assessment explicitly intends an arbitrary symplectic ambient manifold. No assumption that $M={\mathbb{R}}^{2n}$, no global primitive of $\omega$, no displaceability condition, and no restriction to dimension three is inserted. The local contact-type convention and the usual regularity/no-boundary conventions should be checked against the report if a more restrictive meaning was intended.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every compact regular contact-type energy surface with vanishing first real cohomology has a nonconstant closed trajectory, in an arbitrary symplectic manifold. The supplied assessment distinguishes this general claim from the Euclidean case.
