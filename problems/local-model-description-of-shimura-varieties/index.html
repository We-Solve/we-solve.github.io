Write $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^3$ for a dominant integral weight of $\mathfrak{gl}_3$. With the usual upper-triangular convention, dominance means $\lambda_1\geq\lambda_2\geq\lambda_3$. Let $W\cong S_3$ be its Weyl group, with simple reflections $s_1,s_2$ and longest element $w_0$. The conventional dot action is $w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$, where one may use $\rho=(2,1,0)$. These conventions explain the notation; the excerpt does not specify the base field or triangular conventions. Let $M(w\cdot\lambda)$ denote the corresponding Verma module and $L(\lambda)$ its finite-dimensional simple highest-weight quotient. The excerpt invokes the following terminal portion of the Bernstein--Gelfand--Gelfand resolution: \[ M(s_1\cdot\lambda)\oplus M(s_2\cdot\lambda) \longrightarrow M(\lambda) \longrightarrow L(\lambda) \longrightarrow 0. \] It then reports an exact sequence of sheaves \[ F_\infty(M(s_1\cdot\lambda))\oplus F_\infty(M(s_2\cdot\lambda)) \longrightarrow F_\infty(M(\lambda)) \longrightarrow F_\infty(L(\lambda)) \longrightarrow 0. \tag{1} \] The functor $F_\infty$, its source and target categories, its exactness properties, and the maps are not defined in the supplied passage. Thus (1) is retained as a source assertion under omitted hypotheses.

The excerpt says that the sheaves in (1) are Cohen--Macaulay and studies their supports on an undefined space $E_\infty$, near a chosen form $f$ and classical forms $f'$ with the same Galois representation and different refinements. Here a refinement is described as an ordering of the eigenvalues of an operator $\varphi$; neither that operator nor the hypotheses on the forms and representations are supplied. For a coherent sheaf on a locally Noetherian scheme, the Cohen--Macaulay condition means that every nonzero stalk has depth equal to its dimension as a module. The ambient scheme and coherence hypotheses needed to apply this definition are omitted from the excerpt.

Using a local model attributed to an unidentified reference [3], the record displays the following expression, here reproduced literally in its matrix conditions: \[ X:=\left\{(gB,hB,N)\in \mathrm{GL}_3/B\times\mathrm{GL}_3/B\times\mathfrak{gl}_3 \;\bigm|\; g^{-1}Ng\in\mathfrak b,\quad h^{-1}Ng\in\mathfrak b \right\}. \] The notation suggests that $B$ is a Borel subgroup and $\mathfrak b$ its Lie algebra, but these choices and the base field are not specified. In particular, the second condition is printed as $h^{-1}Ng$, not as a conjugation by $h$; as written, it does not generally define a condition independent of representatives of the flags. It has therefore not been silently corrected.

The record describes $X$ as a union of pieces $X_w$, indexed by $w\in W$, obtained by taking closures of loci where the two flags have relative position $w$. It calls these pieces "connected components"; the precise component terminology and relative-position convention require verification. It further writes \[ Z=G/B\times G/B\subset X_{w_0} \] as the locus $N=0$, without defining $G$ separately or supplying the conventions needed for this inclusion.

According to the excerpt, $X$ models the image of $E_\infty$ in an undefined space $X_\infty$ at $f$, and the particular choice of $f$ corresponds to \[ x_0=(B,B,0)\in X_{w_0}. \] It asserts that $X_{w_0}$ is not Gorenstein at $x_0$, that the other components are smooth, and that $Z$ is smooth. The choice of $f$, the local-model comparison, and all these geometric assertions depend on omitted context and are not independently verified here.

Finally, the passage associates $F_\infty(M(\lambda))$ with $\mathcal O_{X_{w_0},x_0}$, and associates the other three sheaves in (1) with the local rings at $x_0$ of \[ X_{s_2s_1},\qquad X_{s_1s_2},\qquad Z. \] It phrases these associations as identifications of supports with local rings. The precise distinction between a support and a module over its local ring, and the individual correspondence for the remaining three sheaves, are not explicitly supplied. Short English statement

For $n=3$, the supplied fragment discusses a BGG sequence, its images under an undefined functor $F_\infty$, and proposed descriptions of sheaf supports using a Steinberg local model. It ends before its conclusion and contains unresolved notation and hypotheses.
