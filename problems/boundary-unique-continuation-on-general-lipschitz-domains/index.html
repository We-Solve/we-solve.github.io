Math-3366

Boundary Unique Continuation on General Lipschitz Domains

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be a Lipschitz domain: a connected open set whose boundary is locally representable, after a rigid change of coordinates, as the graph of a Lipschitz function, with $\Omega$ locally on one side of that graph. No smallness assumption is imposed on the Lipschitz constants. Let $B=B(x_0,r)$ be a Euclidean ball with $x_0\in\partial\Omega$ and $r>0$, and set $\Sigma=B\cap\partial\Omega$. Let $u\in C(\overline\Omega)$ be harmonic in $\Omega$, meaning that $\Delta u=0$ in the interior. Write $\mathcal H^{n-1}$ for $(n-1)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

The supplied question asks whether, for every such $\Omega,B,u$, \[ u|_{\Sigma}=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \mathcal H^{n-1}\left(\{x\in\Sigma:\nabla u(x)=0\}\right)>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\equiv0\ \text{in }\Omega. \] Here $u|_{\Sigma}=0$ is understood pointwise using continuity on $\overline\Omega$.

Unresolved formulation qualification. The supplied hypotheses do not define $\nabla u$ on $\Sigma$: interior harmonicity and continuity on the closure do not themselves provide a classical boundary gradient at every boundary point. The source record does not specify whether the displayed boundary gradient denotes a nontangential limit of the interior gradient, another trace, or a gradient supplied by additional boundary regularity. The implication above therefore retains the supplied notation as an unresolved part of the question; a precise boundary interpretation and any hypotheses needed for it require mathematical review. The record also does not specify the intended range of $n$ or whether $u$ is real-valued or complex-valued.

Short English statement

Does a harmonic function on an arbitrary Lipschitz domain vanish identically if it vanishes on a boundary patch and its boundary gradient vanishes on a subset of positive surface measure? The question removes every smallness assumption on the Lipschitz constant. The meaning of the boundary gradient remains unspecified in the supplied record.
