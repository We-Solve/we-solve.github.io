Math-236

Odd Brauer torsion on K3 surfaces: status and a Picard-rank filter

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a number field, $\bar k$ an algebraic closure, and $X/k$ a K3 surface, understood to be smooth, projective and geometrically integral, with trivial canonical bundle and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$. Put \[ \operatorname{Br}(X)=H^2_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X,\mathbb G_m)_{\mathrm{tors}},\quad \operatorname{Br}_0(X)=\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Br}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}(X)),\quad \operatorname{Br}_1(X)=\ker(\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\operatorname{Br}(X_{\bar k})). \] For an abelian group $B$, write $B[n]=\{b:nb=0\}$. For $\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(X)$, its Brauer--Manin pairing with an adelic point is $\sum_v\operatorname{inv}_v\alpha(P_v)$. An obstruction to the Hasse principle means that $X({\mathbb{A}}_k)\ne\varnothing$, but the points orthogonal to the specified class or subgroup form the empty set; it then follows that $X(k)=\varnothing$.

The 2014 question attributed by Várilly-Alvarado to Skorobogatov asks whether there exist a K3 surface $X/{\mathbb{Q}}$ and an odd-order class in $\operatorname{Br}(X)$ giving such an obstruction, and whether this can already occur in $\operatorname{Br}_1(X)$.

For the quartic specialization $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^3_{{\mathbb{Q}}}$, the source fixes an odd positive integer $u$ and $\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(X)[u]$. It states that, for every place $v$, there exists a zero-cycle $Z_v$ on $X_{{\mathbb{Q}}_v}$ of degree one orthogonal to $\alpha$. Here a zero-cycle is a finite integral linear combination of closed points, its degree is the corresponding sum of residue-field degrees, and orthogonality locally means that the invariant of the sum of the corestricted evaluations of $\alpha$ is zero. The source then invokes an unspecified conjecture of Colliot-Thélène to obtain a global degree-one zero-cycle and asks whether a rational point follows. The precise conjecture and the passage from orthogonality for a single class to the hypotheses of that conjecture are not supplied.

The explicit quartic question is whether every smooth quartic K3 surface $X/{\mathbb{Q}}$ satisfying \[ \left(\operatorname{Br}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_0(X)\right)[2]=0 \] satisfies $X({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow X({\mathbb{Q}})\ne\varnothing$. This retains the source's quotient $\operatorname{Br}(X)/\operatorname{Br}({\mathbb{Q}})$, interpreted using the image of the constants. Tschinkel also asks about weak approximation, meaning density of $X({\mathbb{Q}})$ in finite products $\prod_{v\in S}X({\mathbb{Q}}_v)$ for finite sets of places. Skorobogatov's response that this will probably fail is a prediction, not a theorem in the record. Hassett asks for the corresponding degree-six situation $X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^4$; the text does not restate the hypotheses or specify precisely which preceding implication is intended in that variant.

The supplied later research report describes affirmative algebraic and transcendental three-torsion examples on degree-two K3 surfaces, while retaining the quartic and degree-six questions as unresolved in the literature it checked. These reports do not identify degree-two examples with the quartic or degree-six cases. Its separately stated candidate lemma is: for any polarized K3 surface $(X,h)$ over a number field and any prime $p$, \[ (\operatorname{Br}_1(X)/\operatorname{Br}_0(X))[p]\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho(X_{\bar k})=\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\bar k})\ge p. \] The report additionally claims a simultaneous prime-to-embedding-degree construction of adelic degree-one zero-cycles orthogonal to the full Brauer group, with a conditional global zero-cycle conclusion for quartics and a remaining three-primary issue in degree six. Its full hypotheses and proof are absent; this is retained as a reported contribution requiring review, rather than an unconditional replacement for the original questions. A zero-cycle of degree one need not itself be a rational point.

Short English statement

Can odd-order Brauer classes, even algebraic ones, obstruct rational points on a K3 surface over ${\mathbb{Q}}$? The source also asks whether quartic K3 surfaces with no two-torsion in the Brauer quotient satisfy the Hasse principle, and raises weak approximation and degree-six variants. Later supplied reports claim three-torsion examples in degree two and a Picard-rank bound, but do not settle the stated quartic question or justify replacing degree-one zero-cycles by rational points.
