Barycentric Hyperplanes Through Convex Body Centroids

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an ambient dimension $n\geq 1$, use the standard convention that a convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Let $\lambda_n$ denote $n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure and define the barycenter of $K$ by \[ c_K=\frac{1}{\lambda_n(K)}\int_K x\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda_n(x). \] An affine hyperplane is a set \[ h(u,t)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\langle u,x\rangle=t\}, \qquad u\in{\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus\{0\},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] For a hyperplane $h$ containing $c_K$, equip $h$ with its induced $(n-1)$-dimensional Euclidean measure $\lambda_h$. Since $c_K$ is an interior point of $K$, its section has positive finite measure. Define \[ c_{K\cap h}=\frac{1}{\lambda_h(K\cap h)} \int_{K\cap h}x\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda_h(x). \] In dimension $n=1$, the induced zero-dimensional measure is counting measure. Call $h$ a barycentric hyperplane of $K$ through $c_K$ if \[ c_K\in h\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{K\cap h}=c_K. \] Hyperplanes are counted as distinct subsets of ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, without orientations or multiplicities.

Question as supplied. For every convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, must there exist pairwise distinct affine hyperplanes $h_1,\ldots,h_{n+1}$ such that \[ c_K\in h_i\quad\text{and}\quad c_{K\cap h_i}=c_K \qquad (i=1,\ldots,n+1)? \] Unresolved dimensional qualification. The supplied record gives no lower bound on $n$. The intended range of dimensions, particularly the treatment or exclusion of $n=1$, requires clarification; no restriction such as $n\geq2$ is silently added here.

The supplied dated curation labels this question open and states that the proposed $n+1$ assertion was reopened following a proof error, with lower bounds such as four cuts in dimensions at least three reported as known. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

Does every convex body in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ have at least $n+1$ distinct hyperplanes through its centroid whose sections also have that centroid? Centroids use uniform Euclidean volume in the appropriate dimension. The source leaves the intended range of $n$ unspecified. Its open-status label and account of a proof error are supplied dated curation requiring independent review.
