Math-3800

Strongly maximal matchings

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

A hypergraph is $H=(V,\mathcal E)$, with an arbitrary possibly infinite vertex set and a set of hyperedges. A matching $M\subseteq\mathcal E$ has pairwise disjoint edges. It is strongly maximal if, for every other matching $N$, \[|N\setminus M|\le |M\setminus N|,\] where the comparison is between cardinalities, including infinite ones. Bounded rank means that some fixed $k\in\mathbb N$ satisfies $|e|\le k$ for every $e\in\mathcal E$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Must every bounded-rank hypergraph have a strongly maximal matching? [S2] This is the historical matching part of Conjecture 2.1 in [S2]. Theorem 1.2 of [S3] gives a 3-uniform counterexample, so that question has a negative answer.

Short English statement

Does a uniform finite bound on hyperedge size force a matching that cannot be improved by any competing matching, even through infinitely many changes?
