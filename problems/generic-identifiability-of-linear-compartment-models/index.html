Math-3444

Generic Identifiability of Linear Compartment Models

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A linear compartment model describes transfers between finitely many compartments by a linear differential system. Once the compartments, transfers, leaks, measured outputs, applied inputs, parameter constraints, and experimental conventions have been specified, write its system schematically as \[ \frac{{\,\mathrm{d}} x}{{\,\mathrm{d}} t}=A_M(\theta)x+B_M(\theta)u, \qquad y=C_M(\theta)x, \] where $x$ is the vector of compartment states, $u$ is the input, $y$ is the measured output, and $\theta$ is the vector of unknown parameters. This notation does not prescribe which matrix entries are unknown or which initial conditions are known; those choices are not supplied by the record.

Eliminating the unobserved states produces input--output differential equations. After fixing their normalization and the coefficients retained, let \[ c_M:\Theta_M\longrightarrow K^{q_M} \] be the resulting coefficient map. Here $\Theta_M$ is the admissible parameter space, and the base field $K$, parameter constraints, and construction of $c_M$ must be specified for a concrete model.

To state the rank criterion precisely under the usual independent-parameter formulation, suppose that $\Theta_M$ is a nonempty open parameter domain with coordinates $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{p_M}$, over $K={\mathbb{R}}$ or $K={\mathbb{C}}$, and that $c_M$ is rational and defined on this domain. These are explicit conditional conventions, not additional hypotheses established by the supplied excerpt. Define \[ J_M(\theta)=\left(\frac{\partial(c_M)_i}{\partial\theta_j}(\theta)\right)_{\substack{1\leq i\leq q_M\\1\leq j\leq p_M}}. \] The Jacobian has generically full column rank if there is a proper algebraic exceptional set $Z_M\subset K^{p_M}$, with $\Theta_M\setminus Z_M\neq\varnothing$, such that \[ \operatorname{rank}J_M(\theta)=p_M \qquad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta_M\setminus Z_M. \] In particular, this condition requires $q_M\geq p_M$. Full column rank, rather than merely rank $\min\{p_M,q_M\}$, is the rank condition relevant to identifying the entire chosen parameter vector.

In terms of this coefficient map, generic local identifiability means that, outside a proper algebraic exceptional set, each parameter point $\theta$ has a neighborhood $U$ in the admissible parameter domain for which \[ \{\eta\in U:c_M(\eta)=c_M(\theta)\}=\{\theta\}. \] This expresses local determination of the chosen parameters and does not require global uniqueness among all admissible parameter values.

Requested classification problem. Determine which linear compartment models $M$, with all of the preceding modeling and coefficient-map choices fixed, satisfy \[ \operatorname{rank}J_M(\theta)=p_M \quad\text{generically on }\Theta_M. \] The supplied source identifies this rank condition with generic local identifiability using standard differential algebra techniques. Its applicability to a concrete model depends on the precise input--output construction and experimental assumptions. The record does not specify a family of models, a parameter domain, or these conventions, so it supplies a general classification question rather than a fully specified conjecture. The background discussion of parameters determined uniquely from data must not be interpreted as replacing local identifiability by global identifiability.

Short English statement

Classify the linear compartment models whose input--output coefficient map has a Jacobian of generic rank equal to the number of unknown parameters. The supplied source relates this condition to generic local identifiability. A concrete formulation still requires the model family, parameter domain, inputs, outputs, initial-condition conventions, and coefficient-map construction. Local identifiability does not assert global uniqueness.
