Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Question 48 (Question 4.11 of [Gromov2001])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $n$, let ${\mathbb{R}} P^n$ denote real projective space. Let $g_{\mathrm{can}}$ denote the canonical metric, interpreted as the quotient of the round metric of sectional curvature $1$ on $S^n$ by the antipodal identification. This fixes the length scale associated with the bound $\pi$.

The question is whether, for every smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on ${\mathbb{R}} P^n$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{Vol}({\mathbb{R}} P^n,g)=\operatorname{Vol}({\mathbb{R}} P^n,g_{\mathrm{can}}), \] there exist a real number $L$ and a smooth curve $\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}/L{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}} P^n$ such that \[ 0<L\leq\pi,\qquad \nabla^g_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0,\qquad g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=1. \] Thus $\gamma$ is a nonconstant closed geodesic of length $L$. No homotopy class, simplicity condition, or least-period condition is imposed by the supplied statement.

The source does not specify the dimension or any dimension restriction; the formulation above is parameterized by $n\geq1$, with its intended dimensional scope requiring confirmation. The curvature-$1$ normalization of the canonical metric and the exclusion of constant geodesics are standard interpretations needed to make the numerical bound and the question substantive, but are not explicitly stated in the supplied excerpt.

Short English statement

Must every Riemannian metric on real projective space with the same total volume as the canonical metric admit a nonconstant closed geodesic of length at most $\pi$? The dimension range and canonical normalization require confirmation from the source.
