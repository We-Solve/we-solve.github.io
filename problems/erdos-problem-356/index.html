Erdős problem #356

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT. Show that an absolute $c>0$ exists such that, for every sufficiently large $n$, some strictly increasing positive integers $a_1<\cdots<a_k\le n$ have at least $cn^2$ distinct consecutive sums.

2) CONTEXT AND PLAN. We reconstruct Beker's probabilistic proof, Theorems 1.3 and 2.1 of On a problem of Erdős and Graham about consecutive sums in strictly increasing sequences. Random bounded perturbations of $3i$ give small expected collision energy. The argument below proves the original assertion, without claiming an optimal constant or the additional consecutive-integer interval question in the editorial.

3) A BINOMIAL RESIDUE ESTIMATE. For $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,1/2)$ and any positive integer $d$, \[ \mathbb P(X\equiv0\pmod d)\le d^{-1}+2m^{-1/2}. \tag{1} \] Indeed the binomial mass function is increasing up to its center and decreasing afterward, and its maximum is at most $m^{-1/2}$. Outside the interval $(m/2-d,m/2+d)$, each sampled multiple of $d$ can be bounded by the average of the next $d$ masses towards the center. Those averaging blocks are disjoint. Their total contributes at most $1/d$; the central interval contains at most two multiples of $d$, contributing at most $2/\sqrt m$. The standard maximum estimate follows directly from the central binomial coefficient bound.

4) RANDOM SEQUENCE AND COLLISIONS. Take independent signs $\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}$ with equal probabilities, and put \[ a_i=3i+\epsilon_i\quad(1\le i\le N),\qquad P=\{0,a_1,a_1+a_2,\ldots,a_1+\cdots+a_N\}. \] Always $a_{i+1}-a_i\ge1$. Define \[ E(P)=\#\{(x,y,z,w)\in P^4:x-y=z-w\}. \] We show $\mathbb E E(P)=O(N^2)$.

Equal intervals contribute $O(N^2)$, and strictly nested unequal intervals cannot have equal sums because every $a_i>0$. A crossing pair $[i+1,j],[k+1,l]$ with $i<k<j<l$ has equal sums exactly when the disjoint pair $[i+1,k],[j+1,l]$ does. This cancellation is an injective correspondence of endpoint quadruples. It is therefore enough, up to an absolute factor, to bound equal-sum probabilities over disjoint interval pairs.

For disjoint intervals of lengths $k,l$ beginning at $i,j$, the difference of their random parts is a sum of $k+l$ independent signs. Its probability of cancelling the deterministic difference is consequently a binomial mass \[ \mathbb P(X=x),\qquad X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k+l,1/2), \] where the relevant linear equation is, with one immaterial choice of sign, \[ ki-lj=\frac{2x-k-l}{3}-\frac{k(k-1)}2+\frac{l(l-1)}2. \tag{2} \] We may upper bound the sum of these nonnegative masses by allowing all interval starting positions, even overlapping ones: this is a bound on a numerical sum, not an assertion that their random parts remain independent.

Let $q=\gcd(k,l)$ and $q'=q/\gcd(q,4)$. Multiplying $2$ by $6$ shows that $q\mid4x$, so $q'\mid x$. For a fixed $x$, the number of possible starting pairs is at most \[ \frac{qN}{l}+1, \] since $i$ lies in a single residue class modulo $l/q$. Separate the additional $1$ in this bound and apply $1$ only to the other term. The total for lengths $k,l$ is at most \[ 1+\frac Nl\left(4+\frac{2q}{\sqrt{k+l}}\right). \tag{3} \] By symmetry, sum over $1\le k\le l\le N$. The sums of $1$ and $N/l$ are $O(N^2)$. For the remaining term, the elementary gcd identity gives \[ \sum_{l\le N}\frac1{l^{3/2}}\sum_{k=1}^l\gcd(k,l) =\sum_{l\le N}\frac1{\sqrt l}\sum_{d\mid l}\frac{\phi(d)}d \le2\sqrt N\sum_{d\le N}\frac{\phi(d)}{d^2} \le2\sqrt N(1+\log N). \] Here $\sum_{k=1}^l\gcd(k,l)=\sum_{d\mid l}(l/d)\phi(d)$, and $\sum_{h\le L}h^{-1/2}\le2\sqrt L$. Multiplication by $N$ gives $O(N^{3/2}\log N)=O(N^2)$. This proves the expected-energy bound.

5) MANY DISTINCT SUMS AND THE ORIGINAL SCALE. There is a choice of signs with $E(P)\le C N^2$ for one absolute $C$. If $r(t)$ counts ordered differences from $P$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives \[ |P-P|\ge\frac{(\sum_t r(t))^2}{\sum_t r(t)^2} =\frac{(N+1)^4}{E(P)}\gg N^2. \] Positive differences of prefix sums are precisely consecutive sums, so their number is $(|P-P|-1)/2\gg N^2$. Finally choose $N=\lfloor(n-1)/3\rfloor$; then $a_N\le3N+1\le n$ and $N\asymp n$. This proves the desired lower bound $cn^2$.

6) VERIFICATION AND FINAL. The only independence assertion is for disjoint intervals; cancellation handles crossing pairs before probabilities are evaluated. The gcd restriction and the contribution of the rounding term in the number of starting positions are both retained. SOLVED. This is a full reconstruction of the known affirmative solution, not a novelty claim.
