Evidence sentence and a correlation obstruction to rate CLTs

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source is not a standalone Conjecture 41. It says numerical experiments [81,82] support that conjecture and begins a sentence predicting a random-wave central limit theorem. The supplied verification says the latter concerns convergence in Conjecture 38 and continues in AIM-OTHER-0141. Neither conjecture's full statement, random variable, probability law or normalization is supplied here. The line-break hyphen in "convergence" is editorial noise, not mathematical content.

The aid supplies a probabilistic diagnostic rather than an eigenfunction theorem. Let $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N)$ be jointly real Gaussian, centered with unit variances, and let $R_{k\ell}={\mathbb{E}}(Y_kY_\ell)$. For real deterministic weights $w_k$, its exact identity is \[ \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_kw_k(Y_k^2-1)\right) =2\sum_{k,\ell}w_kw_\ell R_{k\ell}^2. \] For equal weights $1/N$, define \[ N_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{N^2}{\sum_{k,\ell}R_{k\ell}^2}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1N\sum_k(Y_k^2-1)\right) =\frac2{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}. \] This is a variance-based correlation-cell diagnostic, not by itself a CLT criterion or a claim that every dependence structure is Gaussian.

The aid's explicit block construction uses one fixed infinite iid sequence $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots$ of standard real Gaussians. For $M$ blocks of a common positive integer size $b=b_M$, put $N=Mb$ and \[ Y_{(j-1)b+r}=Z_j\quad(1\leq j\leq M,\ 1\leq r\leq b). \] The empirical one-point distribution is exactly that of $Z_1,\ldots,Z_M$ and converges to the Gaussian law. The quadratic average is \[ Q_{M,b}=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N(Y_k^2-1) =\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M(Z_j^2-1), \] so it tends to zero almost surely and $\sqrt M\,Q_{M,b}$ converges in distribution to a centered normal of variance two. Here $R_{k\ell}$ is one within a block and zero between blocks, giving $N_{\mathrm{eff}}=M$.

The nominal $\sqrt N$ scaling instead has variance $2b$: for fixed $b$ its limit has that inflated variance, and for $b_M\to\infty$, \[ \Pr\{|\sqrt{Mb_M}\,Q_{M,b_M}|\leq C\}\longrightarrow0 \quad\text{for every fixed }C>0. \] Thus the nominal statistics are not tight, despite Gaussian one-point empirical laws and qualitative quadratic convergence. General weighted versions require the actual weights: in this model their effective coefficients are the sums of weights within each block, so no arbitrary-weight convergence is asserted from the unweighted calculation alone.

This demonstrates that one-point histograms and qualitative convergence do not determine fluctuation scale or variance without correlation information. It neither constructs actual Laplace eigenfunctions with these block correlations nor refutes the unstated random-wave or arithmetic CLT. The original numerical evidence and unfinished prediction remain attributed context.

Short English statement

The record is an evidence sentence and a truncated CLT prediction. The aid’s Gaussian block model shows why matching one-point statistics and qualitative averages is insufficient to identify a fluctuation scale. Its conclusion concerns a correlation model, not a proved or disproved eigenfunction conjecture.
