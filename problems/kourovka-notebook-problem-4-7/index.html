Kourovka Notebook Problem 4.7

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an integer $n\geq 2$. The supplied problem asks which ring epimorphisms $f:R\to Q$ induce an epimorphism $SL_n(R)\to SL_n(Q)$, and, in particular, which rings $R$ satisfy $SL_n(R)=E_n(R)$. The source does not specify the class of rings, the convention for ring homomorphisms, or the meaning of ring epimorphism. These qualifications remain unresolved.

For a precise formulation under the additional convention that rings are commutative with identity and homomorphisms preserve identity, define \[ SL_n(R)=\{A\in M_n(R):\det A=1_R\}. \] For $i\ne j$, let $e_{ij}$ be the matrix unit, and define the elementary subgroup by \[ E_n(R)=\left\langle I_n+r e_{ij}:r\in R,\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,\ i\ne j\right\rangle\leq SL_n(R). \] A homomorphism $f:R\to Q$ induces \[ f_n:SL_n(R)\longrightarrow SL_n(Q),\qquad (a_{ij})\longmapsto(f(a_{ij})). \] Under this convention, the first question is to characterize the ring epimorphisms $f$ for which \[ \forall B\in SL_n(Q)\ \exists A\in SL_n(R)\quad f_n(A)=B. \] Here an epimorphism of groups means a surjective group homomorphism. The admissible ring maps still depend on an unresolved source convention: a surjective ring homomorphism need not mean the same thing as a categorical ring epimorphism. In the category of commutative rings with identity and identity-preserving homomorphisms, the latter means that, for every ring $T$ and every pair of homomorphisms $g,h:Q\to T$, \[ g\circ f=h\circ f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g=h. \] The source does not determine which of these meanings is intended.

The particular question, under the same commutative-ring convention, is to characterize the rings $R$ such that every $A\in SL_n(R)$ is a finite product \[ A=\prod_{k=1}^{m}(I_n+r_k e_{i_kj_k}), \qquad m\geq0,\quad r_k\in R,\quad 1\leq i_k,j_k\leq n,\quad i_k\ne j_k, \] where the empty product is $I_n$. The integer $n$ remains fixed throughout; no simultaneous assertion for all $n$ is imposed. These definitions provide a conditional commutative-ring formulation, not a determination of the source's intended ring domain. If noncommutative rings are intended, the meaning of $SL_n$ must also be specified.

Short English statement

For a fixed $n\geq2$, characterize the ring epimorphisms for which every determinant-one matrix over the target lifts to a determinant-one matrix over the source. Also characterize the rings whose determinant-one matrices are all products of elementary matrices. The determinant formulation assumes commutative rings with identity; the source leaves the ring domain and the meaning of ring epimorphism unspecified.
