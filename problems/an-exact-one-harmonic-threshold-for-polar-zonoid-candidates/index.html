Math-265

An exact one-harmonic threshold for polar-zonoid candidates

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A full-dimensional origin-symmetric zonoid is a Hausdorff limit of finite Minkowski sums of centered line segments in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. For an origin-symmetric convex body $Z$, let $Z^\circ=\{y:x\cdot y\le1\text{ for all }x\in Z\}$, and let $B_2^n$ be the Euclidean unit ball. Use the Banach--Mazur convention \[ d_{\rm BM}(K,L)=\inf\{\lambda\ge1:\ K\subseteq TL\subseteq\lambda K\text{ for some invertible linear }T\}. \] R. Schneider's question asks whether, for every sequence $(Z_n)$ with both $Z_n\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$ and $Z_n^\circ$ zonoids, \[ d_{\rm BM}(Z_n,B_2^n)\longrightarrow1\quad(n\to\infty). \] Equivalently, does the supremum of these distances over the polar-zonoid class in dimension $n$ tend to 1? Full dimensionality and symmetry about the origin are the standard conventions needed for a bounded polar and this distance. The source's damaged symbols $Z_n$, $Z_n^\circ$, $B_2^n$ are interpreted accordingly.

The supplied aid reports the unrestricted asymptotic problem open in the literature it checked. Its special case fixes an integer $m\ge1$, takes $n\ge2m$, and defines the even spherical harmonic \[ Y_{m,n}(u)=(2m)^m\prod_{j=1}^{2m}u_j,\qquad u\in S^{n-1}. \] Its range is $[-1,1]$. For the candidate support function \[ h_\varepsilon(u)=1+\varepsilon Y_{m,n}(u), \] the aid asserts the exact criterion \[ h_\varepsilon\text{ is the support function of a zonoid} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\varepsilon|\le a_{m,n},\qquad a_{m,n}=\frac{(2m-3)!!}{\prod_{j=1}^m(n+2j-1)}. \] Here $(-1)!!=1$ for $m=1$; for $m\ge2$, the numerator is the product of positive odd integers through $2m-3$. This criterion concerns existence as a zonoid support function, not an assertion that every $\varepsilon$ outside the interval defines a convex body of a different class.

For each fixed pair $(m,n)$, the aid also asserts existence of $\delta_{m,n}>0$ such that the bodies with support function $h_\varepsilon$, for sufficiently small $|\varepsilon|<\delta_{m,n}$, have zonoid polars as well. One may decrease this neighborhood to have $\delta_{m,n}\le a_{m,n}$, so both body and polar are zonoids there. The summary attributes the conclusion to smooth polarity and continuity of the inverse cosine transform, but provides no explicit quantitative lower bound on $\delta_{m,n}$ or uniform neighborhood in $n$.

Every member of this fixed-harmonic family satisfying both zonoid conditions has the reported estimate \[ d_{\rm BM}(Z_{n,\varepsilon},B_2^n) \le\frac{1+a_{m,n}}{1-a_{m,n}} =1+O_m(n^{-m})\quad(n\to\infty,\ m\text{ fixed}). \] The denominator is positive for the stated range of $m,n$. The implicit constant may depend on $m$. This proves asymptotic closeness only for the displayed one-harmonic subfamilies; it does not supply a uniform estimate for all polar-zonoid bodies or for harmonic degree growing arbitrarily with dimension.

The registry labels the entry partially solved; attempt 1 is a partial result, with problem open at the run, internal rigor label proved and novelty confidence low. Those labels accompany a machine-generated aid that is not peer reviewed and requires independent verification before citation.

Short English statement

Schneider asks whether a zonoid whose polar is also a zonoid must become uniformly close to an ellipsoid in high dimension. The aid proves this only for a specified fixed-degree harmonic perturbation of a ball, with an exact zonoid threshold and an unquantified local interval for polarity. The full asymptotic question remains open according to the supplied checked-literature report.
