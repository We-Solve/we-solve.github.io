Math-14409

Ascending Chain Condition for Log Discrepancies

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A subset of an ordered set satisfies the descending chain condition (DCC) if it contains no infinite strictly decreasing sequence. Fix a dimension $n$, a DCC set $I\subseteq[0,1]$, and a DCC set $J\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq0}$.

In the usual birational formulation, a pair $(X,\Delta)$ consists of a normal variety $X$ and an effective real Weil divisor $\Delta$ such that $K_X+\Delta$ is real Cartier. The coefficient condition means that every nonzero coefficient of $\Delta$ belongs to $I$. For a prime divisor $E$ on a normal birational model $f:Y\to X$, its log discrepancy is \[ a(E;X,\Delta)=1+\operatorname{coeff}_E\left(K_Y-f^*(K_X+\Delta)\right), \] with compatible canonical divisors. The pair is log canonical if $a(E;X,\Delta)\geq0$ for every prime divisor over $X$.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied record uses "the log discrepancy" of a pair as a single number without defining it. A divisorial log discrepancy requires a choice of $E$; a minimal log discrepancy requires an infimum over specified divisors, usually with a specified center or locus. These are different formulations. The record also does not specify the base field or its characteristic. The definitions above explain standard terminology but do not resolve these omissions.

Short English statement

The recorded question asks whether, in fixed dimension and with boundary coefficients in a fixed DCC set, only finitely many permitted log discrepancy values can lie in any fixed DCC set $J$. The meaning of the numerical invariant, its required center or divisor data, and the underlying field remain unspecified. The supplied record attributes the conjecture to Shokurov and labels it open; this draft does not independently verify that attribution or status.
