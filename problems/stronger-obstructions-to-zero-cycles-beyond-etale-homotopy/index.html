Stronger Obstructions to Zero-Cycles Beyond Étale Homotopy

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a scheme $X$ of finite type over a field $k$, a zero-cycle is a finite formal sum \[ z=\sum_{x\in X_{\mathrm{cl}}}n_x[x],\qquad n_x\in{\mathbb{Z}}, \] where $X_{\mathrm{cl}}$ denotes the set of closed points of $X$. Its degree over $k$ is \[ \deg_k(z)=\sum_x n_x[k(x):k]. \] A zero-cycle is effective if all its coefficients are nonnegative. These definitions supply terminology only: the record does not specify the base field, the class of schemes or varieties, a required degree, or whether effectiveness is required. Without any additional requirement, the zero-cycle $0$ always exists, so an obstruction to the existence of unrestricted zero-cycles would have no nontrivial target.

Source question. Can one construct an obstruction to the intended zero-cycle existence problem that is strictly stronger than the obstruction arising from stable $\text{\'etale}$ homotopy?

The intended zero-cycle problem and the stable $\text{\'etale}$ homotopy obstruction are not defined in the supplied record.
