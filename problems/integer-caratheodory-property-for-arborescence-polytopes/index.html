The Integer Caratheodory Property requires integer points of dilates to admit integer-weighted decompositions using affinely independent integer polytope points, with coefficients totaling the dilation factor. The graphic matroid regards forests as independent; a partition matroid restricts how many elements can be chosen from each part.

A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed affine half-spaces; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Its faces are its intersections with supporting hyperplanes.

ICP for rooted arborescences on bidirected cactus graphs

ICP for rooted arborescences on bidirected cactus graphs

Problem: prb_681accc0953e4628afd6550934796843

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: For every connected undirected cactus, replace every edge by both directed arcs. Its outward arborescence polytope at any root has ICP for every integer dilation.

A cactus is a finite connected simple graph in which every edge lies on at most one simple cycle. Its blocks are bridges and cycles, meeting at articulation vertices. Fix any root r and replace every undirected edge by both arcs. Every undirected spanning tree has a unique orientation away from r. Conversely an outward arborescence cannot use both directions of one edge and its underlying edges are a spanning tree. In a cactus, a spanning tree retains all bridges and deletes exactly one edge from each cycle. Choices on distinct cycles are independent.

For a cycle label its vertices 0,1,...,l-1 cyclically, where 0 is the articulation vertex nearest r in the block tree (or r itself). Write e_i for edge {i,i+1 mod l}. If e_j is deleted, edges e_i with i<j are oriented clockwise and those with i>j counterclockwise; e_j has neither arc. Thus with p_j the probability of deleting e_j, the clockwise arc marginal on e_i equals
\[
c_i=\sum_{j>i}p_j.
\]
The affine inverse is p_0=1-c_0, p_j=c_{j-1}-c_j for 1<=j<=l-1, with c_{l-1}=0. In particular distinct deletion choices are affinely independent, and this simplex map has an integer affine inverse on its image. Bridge arcs are fixed. Consequently the full polytope is an affine image of the product of these cycle-choice simplices, with an integer affine inverse. This statement follows directly by independently sampling deletion choices, so no integrality theorem for general arborescence systems is needed.

If w is integral in kP, applying the inverse formulas at scale k yields nonnegative integers a_{C,j} summing to k in each cycle C: a_{C,0}=k-w(c_0) and a_{C,j}=w(c_{j-1})-w(c_j). Form all cumulative sums within all cycles; sort their distinct values together with 0,k as 0=t_0<...<t_h=k. On each interval (t_{s-1},t_s] choose the unique deletion option whose cumulative interval contains it, in every cycle. Let T_s be the resulting oriented spanning tree, with multiplicity n_s=t_s-t_{s-1}. Then n_s are positive integers, sum n_s=k, and all deletion marginals reconstruct; therefore w=sum n_s 1_{T_s}.

For affine independence, consider any internal breakpoint t_s. It is a cumulative sum in at least one cycle. The affine functional summing the corresponding initial deletion coordinates is one on T_1,...,T_s and zero on all later trees. The integer-affine inverse above makes this an affine functional of the arc coordinates themselves. In an affine relation sum alpha_s 1_{T_s}=0, sum alpha_s=0, applying each such functional forces all initial coefficient sums to vanish. Consecutive differences give every alpha_s=0. This verifies the stronger ICP requirement explicitly.

If there are no cycles, P is a singleton and the result holds with one coefficient k; a one-vertex graph is included. Cycles may have any length and be attached in any finite cactus pattern. Directed cycles are present, so this is not an acyclic-digraph theorem. Arbitrary directed graphs, missing reverse arcs, and graphs with overlapping cycles remain outside the scope.

Research attempt: Acyclic arborescence decomposition and a cyclic obstruction to naive peeling

An affine-independent integer decomposition of a product of simplices
Let $k\geq1$ be an integer. Consider nonnegative integer coordinates $w_{b,j}$ divided into finitely many nonempty blocks $b$, each satisfying $\sum_jw_{b,j}=k$. Form the cumulative sums $s_{b,j}=\sum_{i\leq j}w_{b,i}$ and sort all their distinct values together with zero and $k$: \[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_h=k. \] For each interval $(t_{\ell-1},t_\ell]$, choose in each block the coordinate whose cumulative interval contains it, and let $v_\ell$ be the corresponding zero-one vector with one chosen coordinate per block. This is unambiguous even with zero coordinates, because a nonempty interval lies inside exactly one positive-length cumulative interval per block. Assign multiplicity $n_\ell=t_\ell-t_{\ell-1}$. Lengths of the relevant intervals add to $w_{b,j}$, so \[ w=\sum_{\ell=1}^h n_\ell v_\ell,\qquad n_\ell\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad\sum_\ell n_\ell=k. \] To prove affine independence, suppose $\sum_\ell c_\ell v_\ell=0$ and $\sum_\ell c_\ell=0$. Each internal breakpoint $t_j$ is a cumulative sum in some block. The linear functional summing the corresponding initial coordinates of that block is one on $v_1,\ldots,v_j$ and zero on all later vectors. Applying it gives $\sum_{\ell\leq j}c_\ell=0$ for each $j<h$. Taking consecutive differences and using the total coefficient sum proves every $c_\ell=0$.
Application to acyclic directed arborescences
Let a directed acyclic graph have a first vertex $r$ in a topological order, no arcs entering $r$, and at least one incoming arc at every other vertex. Choose one incoming arc for each nonroot vertex. Following parent arcs backwards strictly decreases the order and must end at $r$, since every other vertex has a parent. Thus the chosen arcs connect every vertex to $r$ and form an outward rooted arborescence. Conversely an arborescence makes exactly these choices. The polytope is therefore a product of the incoming-arc simplices: the convex-hull equality follows by independently sampling each incoming choice from its specified probability vector. The construction above proves its Integer Caratheodory Property for every integer dilation. The graph with only $r$ has the singleton empty-vector polytope and has the property trivially.
Why independent incoming-arc subtraction fails with cycles
On vertices $r,a,b,c$, include the three spokes from $r$ and the directed cycle $a\to b\to c\to a$. Let $w$ put one on each arc and $k=2$. It belongs to $2P$: decompose it into the arborescence $\{r\to a,a\to b,b\to c\}$ and the arborescence $\{r\to b,r\to c,c\to a\}$. Let $x$ instead be the three-spoke arborescence. Every arborescence has at least one arc entering $\{a,b,c\}$ from $r$, so a residual in $(2-\lambda)P$ must satisfy \[ 3-3\lambda\geq2-\lambda, \] forcing $\lambda\leq1/2$ in the nonnegative-removal range. At $\lambda=1/2$, the normalized residual has spoke coordinates $1/3$ and cycle coordinates $2/3$. It is the average of the three arborescences obtained by taking one spoke and deleting the cycle arc entering its endpoint. Thus the maximum removable multiple of this chosen arborescence is exactly $1/2$, even though the smallest positive arc weight was one. Removing one full copy would leave the directed cycle, violating rooted reachability. This is a counterexample to arbitrary-coordinate-minimum subtraction, not to the existence of some better decomposition or to the original ICP question.

Verification and adversarial audit

The acyclic proof follows parent arcs in decreasing topological order, establishing rooted reachability rather than only indegree constraints. Cumulative breakpoints and multiplicities are integer; the prefix-coordinate functionals prove affine independence even when some original coordinates are zero. In the cyclic example, two explicit arborescences certify $w\in2P$, and three explicit arborescences certify the fractional maximum rather than relying only on a necessary cut inequality.
