Math-2179

3.6 (Agol) — Virtual semi-fibering

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold is a complete hyperbolic $3$-manifold of finite Riemannian volume. A manifold is virtually endowed with a specified property if some connected finite-sheeted cover has that property.

In the usual strict semi-bundle interpretation, a semi-fiber is a properly embedded separating surface $S$ in a compact manifold $N$ such that cutting along $S$ gives two interval bundles, \[ N=N_1\cup_S N_2, \] with $S$ the horizontal boundary of each interval bundle and with any vertical boundary contained in $\partial N$. In the closed orientable case, these are the familiar twisted interval bundles over nonorientable surfaces. For a cusped hyperbolic manifold one uses a compact core and the corresponding boundary convention. This is the interval-bundle interpretation suggested by the supplied summary, but the excerpt does not itself define semi-fibered.

Question (Agol). Does every finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold $M$ admit a connected finite-sheeted covering $\widehat M\to M$ for which a compact core $\widehat N$ contains a semi-fiber, with the interval-bundle decomposition described above? In the closed case $\widehat N=\widehat M$. The assertion concerns finite-volume manifolds, not only the closed case.

The intended convention must be confirmed: some terminology allows fiber surfaces as semi-fibers, whereas the supplied summary expressly distinguishes this problem from virtual fibering over the circle. With that distinction, merely finding a finite cover that is a surface bundle over $S^1$ does not supply the requested two-interval-bundle structure. The excerpt also leaves orientability assumptions and the precise boundary conventions for cusped manifolds unstated.

Short English statement

Does every finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold have a finite cover admitting the intended semi-fiber structure, namely a decomposition into two interval bundles along their horizontal boundaries? The source's exact convention for semi-fibering must be checked.
