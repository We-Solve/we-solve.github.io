Math-1276

Local R-equivalence and a geometric Brauer obstruction to eventual universal R-triviality

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a proper variety $X/k$, direct $R$-equivalence links two $k$-points by a rational curve parametrized over $k$; $R$-equivalence is generated by chains of such links. Write $X(k)/R$ for the resulting set. In the intended smooth proper geometrically integral characteristic-zero setting, rational connectedness is geometric; over ${\mathbb{C}}$ the source describes it by $X({\mathbb{C}})/R$ being a singleton.

Original question (Colliot-Thélène). What number-field results follow from techniques for rationally connected varieties? As background the source attributes to Kollár finiteness of $X(k)/R$ over local fields for rationally connected $X$, and to Kollár--Szabó triviality of $X(k_v)/R$ for almost all places of a number field. Exact smoothness/properness hypotheses and bibliography are not supplied in the short account.

Ellenberg's original remark asks whether, for a rationally connected variety over a number field, some extension $K\supset k$ makes $X(L)/R$ a singleton for every $L\supset K$. Raskind reports a negative answer using a conic bundle over ${\mathbb{P}}^2$, without specifying it. The quantifiers do not say whether $K$ or $L$ must be finite/algebraic extensions; universal triviality over arbitrary field extensions is stronger than a question restricted to number-field extensions.

Attached unreviewed Brauer obstruction. For every smooth proper geometrically integral $X$ over a characteristic-zero field with $\operatorname{Br}(X_{\overline k})\ne0$, the aid asserts: for every field extension $K/k$ there is a finite extension $E/K$, a point $P\in X(E)$, and the field $L=E(X_E)$ such that the generic point $\eta\in X(L)$ and the constant point $P_L$ are not $R$-equivalent. Here $\operatorname{Br}$ is the cohomological Brauer group, and the nonzero hypothesis is geometric, not merely a constant class from $\operatorname{Br}(k)$. The proposed witness arises from a descended geometric Brauer class and its evaluations. This prevents universal $R$-triviality after every proposed base extension in the all-field-extension sense. The witness $L$ is a function field and is not claimed finite over $K$, so it does not alone settle the finite-number-field-only interpretation.

Separately, for a smooth proper rationally connected variety over a number field, the aid combines the reported local results to obtain finiteness of \[\prod_vX(k_v)/R.\] If $X(k)/R$ were infinite, its localization map to this finite product would have an infinite fiber. Thus infinitely many global classes would be indistinguishable by their local $R$-classes at every completion. This is a conditional consequence, not a construction of an infinite global quotient or a proof of a local-to-global principle. All propositions and status claims are recorded as supplied research-aid assertions requiring review.

Short English statement

The source asks for arithmetic consequences of rational connectedness and an eventual universal $R$-triviality question. The aid gives a geometric Brauer obstruction using a function-field generic point, and observes that an infinite global $R$-quotient would have an infinite fiber in the finite product of local quotients. Arbitrary field extensions and finite number-field extensions remain distinct.
