Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 13.2

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq0$ be an integer. Use integral Poincaré duality: a PD$(n)$ group $G$ has cohomological dimension $n$, the trivial ${\mathbb{Z}} G$-module ${\mathbb{Z}}$ admits a finite resolution by finitely generated projective modules, and \[ H^i(G;{\mathbb{Z}} G)=0\quad(i\ne n),\qquad H^n(G;{\mathbb{Z}} G)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{as an abelian group}. \] The action on the top cohomology may be the orientation-character action; orientability is not imposed.

A group is solvable if its derived series $G^{(0)}=G$, $G^{(j+1)}=[G^{(j)},G^{(j)}]$ reaches the trivial group after finitely many steps. It is polycyclic if it has a finite subnormal series with cyclic factors. Its Hirsch length, when polycyclic, is the number of infinite cyclic factors in such a series.

Kevin Whyte's question asks whether \[ \forall n\geq0\ \forall G,\qquad \left(G\text{ solvable and PD}(n)\right) \Longrightarrow G\text{ polycyclic}. \] The supplied research summary reports an affirmative theorem of Bieri and the sharper classification: the solvable integral PD$(n)$ groups are precisely the torsion-free polycyclic groups of Hirsch length $n$. This is a reported literature resolution, not a proof supplied here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether integral Poincaré duality forces a solvable group to be polycyclic. The supplied research summary attributes an affirmative answer to Bieri, with dimension equal to the Hirsch length.
