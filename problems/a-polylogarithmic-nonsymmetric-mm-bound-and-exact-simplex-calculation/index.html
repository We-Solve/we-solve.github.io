Math-267

A polylogarithmic nonsymmetric MM* bound and exact simplex calculation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ be a convex body. For a body $L$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}L$, define \[ h_L(u)=\sup_{x\in L}\langle x,u\rangle,\qquad L^\circ=\{y:\langle x,y\rangle\leq1\ \forall x\in L\},\qquad M(L)=\int_{S^{n-1}}h_L(u)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma(u), \] where $\sigma$ is rotationally invariant probability measure. Put $\Psi(L)=M(L)M(L^\circ)$, and make the affine optimization explicit as \[ \Phi(K)=\inf_{\substack{A\in\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{R}})\\x\in\operatorname{int}K}} \Psi(A(K-x)). \] This normalization and interior-origin condition are essential. The source recalls that for origin-symmetric $K$, some invertible linear image has $\Psi(TK)\leq C\log n$, and asks for the nonsymmetric analogue with affine maps. The displayed $\log n$ baseline is meaningful for $n\geq2$; an all-dimension version uses $\log(n+1)$.

The aid reports a July 2026 preprint of Bizeul and Klartag proving that every convex body has an affine isotropic position $L$ in which \[ \Psi(L)\leq C\log^3(n+1), \] with an absolute constant. Isotropic position here means that the uniform random vector in $L$ has mean zero and covariance the identity, after a harmless scalar normalization. This is reported to supersede Rudelson's earlier $n^{1/3}$-times-polylogarithmic nonsymmetric bound; the exact logarithmic exponent remains open according to the aid. No exact earlier logarithmic factor is supplied.

The aid independently reports $\Psi(L)\geq1$, hence $\Phi(K)\geq1$, and an asymmetry-sensitive reduction. Define the Minkowski asymmetry by \[ s(K)=\inf_{x\in\operatorname{int}K}\inf\{s\geq1:-(K-x)\subseteq s(K-x)\},\qquad D=\tfrac12(K-K). \] With the standard affine/linear-position interpretation of $\Phi$, its reported inequality is \[ \Phi(K)\leq\frac{1+s(K)}2\,\Phi(D). \] For the symmetric body $D$, the comparison uses its centered linear optimization. The record does not explicitly define its symbols $\Phi,s$; these standard interpretations must be checked against the underlying attempt before using the formula as quoted notation.

For an explicit family, let $\Delta_n=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_{n+1}\}$, where $|v_i|=1$ and $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=-1/n$ for $i\ne j$. Its centroid is zero. Let $G_n$ be a standard Gaussian in ${\mathbb{R}}^n$ and let $g_1,\ldots,g_{n+1}$ be independent standard real Gaussians. The aid reports \[ \Delta_n^\circ=-n\Delta_n,\qquad \Psi(\Delta_n)= \frac{n+1}{({\mathbb{E}}|G_n|)^2} \left({\mathbb{E}}\max_{1\leq i\leq n+1}g_i\right)^2 \sim2\log n\quad(n\to\infty). \] This is the product in the displayed centered regular position. The supplied summary does not assert that this calculation alone proves affine optimality, so it is not silently identified with $\Phi(\Delta_n)$.

Short English statement

The question asks for the best affine-position bound on a nonsymmetric body's mean support function times that of its polar. The aid reports a July 2026 cubic-logarithmic bound, an asymmetry reduction and an exact Gaussian calculation for the regular simplex, while leaving the optimal exponent open.
