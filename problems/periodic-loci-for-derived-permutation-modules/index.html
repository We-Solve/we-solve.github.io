Periodic Loci for Derived Permutation Modules

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite or profinite group. For a prime number $p$, a finite $p$-group has order a power of $p$, and a pro-$p$ group is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. The source considers a finite $p$-subgroup or pro-$p$ subgroup $H\leq G$.

Write \[ \mathcal K=\operatorname{DPerm}(G;k)^\omega, \qquad X=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal K), \] following the source notation for the compact derived permutation modules and their tensor-triangular spectrum. Under the usual tensor-triangular interpretation, points of $X$ are prime thick tensor ideals of $\mathcal K$: a proper thick tensor ideal $\mathcal P$ is prime when $a\otimes b\in\mathcal P$ implies $a\in\mathcal P$ or $b\in\mathcal P$. Let \[ X_{\mathrm{cl}}=\{x\in X:\overline{\{x\}}=\{x\}\}. \] The source does not specify the coefficient object $k$, the precise construction of $\operatorname{DPerm}(G;k)$, or the definition of periodicity. Accordingly, write $\operatorname{Per}(X)$ for its intended periodic locus, with that definition still requiring recovery from the report.

Conjecture 7, as supplied. For every finite or profinite group $G$, the intended assertion is \[ \operatorname{Per}\!\left(\operatorname{Spec}\left(\operatorname{DPerm}(G;k)^\omega\right)\right) = \operatorname{Spec}\left(\operatorname{DPerm}(G;k)^\omega\right)\setminus X_{\mathrm{cl}}. \] The source also states the pointwise formulation, for the indicated subgroups $H\leq G$, \[ P(H,p)\text{ is periodic}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\text{ is periodic}. \] Here $P(H,p)$ and its parameter are not defined in the supplied passage. In particular, the occurrence of $p$ as a prime number in the subgroup hypothesis must be distinguished from whatever object is meant by "$p$ is periodic." No identification of these roles, or replacement by an unspecified prime ideal, is assumed. The range of the point parameter and the correspondence needed to justify the asserted equivalence with the global formulation remain unresolved. These displays preserve the requested target but do not constitute a fully specified standalone conjecture without the missing definitions and hypotheses.

Supplied qualifications and reported special cases. Remark 8 proposes following Example 2 and Remark 3, whose contents are not supplied. It reports that the line bundle denoted $\Sigma 1$ is not ample, with the stated exception $p\nmid\#G$, and that establishing ampleness of a suitable family is difficult. The meaning of this order condition for infinite profinite groups is not specified. The remark reports success for elementary abelian $p$-groups in [BG25], with associated global sections called permutation twisted cohomology. It attributes a generalization to $p$-groups to Miller [Mil25], without an ampleness result, but with injectivity of the comparison map to the Dirac scheme and control of sections after applying modular fixed points, described as $p$-local control. Theorem 9, attributed to [Gal25], reports that Conjecture 7 holds when $G$ is a finite $p$-group or pro-$p$ group; the passage also refers to [Gal25] for applications in motivic tensor-triangular geometry. These are supplied contextual claims, not independently verified results in this draft.

Short English statement

The proposed conjecture says that the periodic points in the tensor-triangular spectrum of compact derived permutation modules are exactly the nonclosed points. The supplied passage reports the finite $p$-group and pro-$p$ cases as established and labels the general problem partially solved. A precise standalone statement still requires the coefficient hypotheses, the definition of periodicity, and the meaning and parameter range of $P(H,p)$; the source's two uses of $p$ cannot safely be identified.
