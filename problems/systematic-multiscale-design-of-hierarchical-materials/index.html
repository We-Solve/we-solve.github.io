Systematic Multiscale Design of Hierarchical Materials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A hierarchical material has structural features at several characteristic length scales $\ell_1<\cdots<\ell_m$, rather than a single microstructure scale. Effective properties, such as stiffness, strength, and flaw tolerance, are macroscopic responses derived from the constitutive and geometric behavior at finer scales. In the report, flaw tolerance means that a pre-existing crack does not propagate before the surrounding material approaches its limiting strength.
Source scope and qualifications.
This is a four-part design research program. The source does not specify a universal class of admissible microstructures, a property map, an optimization norm, or an algorithmic complexity target. These must be chosen for any subsequent precise model; adding them here would invent a narrower problem.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
How can a hierarchical material be designed systematically to achieve a particular set of properties? How can different levels of structural hierarchy be bridged? What criteria should determine the characteristic length scales at all hierarchical levels? What is the theoretical basis for bottom-up materials design?
Saved literature context.
The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Hierarchical and flaw-tolerant material models provide design principles and examples, but the general systematic multiscale design, scale-selection criterion, and theoretical bottom-up basis asked here remain open research questions. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

Short English statement

Find a systematic theory for designing material structure across many scales to achieve specified mechanical properties.
