Entropy-Stable Paths for Approximate Riemann States

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question asks how entropy-conservative subpaths between approximate Riemann states should be chosen to obtain entropy-stable numerical schemes. However, its verification field says the original report presents this as an expository design question and answers it in the same abstract: choose subpaths along approximate Riemann solutions and derive entropy-stable viscosity. The record is marked statement_status unrecoverable. It therefore does not supply an independently recoverable open conjecture or the detailed construction and hypotheses of that answer.

The mathematical objects involved can be described conditionally. For a one-dimensional nonconservative system $u_t+A(u)u_x=0$ on a state domain $\mathcal U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^m$, an admissible path family joins $u_L$ to $u_R$ by a piecewise Lipschitz path $\Phi:[0,1]\to\mathcal U$. Given approximate intermediate Riemann states $u_0=u_L,u_1,\ldots,u_s=u_R$, the chosen subpaths concatenate paths from $u_{r-1}$ to $u_r$. They determine the nonconservative jump integral \[ \mathcal I(u_L,u_R)=\int_0^1 A(\Phi(t))\Phi'(t)\,dt. \] Thus specifying endpoints alone need not specify the weak jump relation. An entropy pair consists of a convex $\eta$ and flux $q$ satisfying $\nabla q=(\nabla\eta)^{\mathsf T}A$ wherever smooth.

For example, in a semidiscrete finite-volume fluctuation formulation, let $D^-+D^+=\mathcal I$, set $v=\nabla\eta$, and use the convention \[ \dot u_j=-\frac{D^-(u_j,u_{j+1})+D^+(u_{j-1},u_j)}{\Delta x}. \] An interface entropy-conservation criterion is \[ v_L^{\mathsf T}D^-+v_R^{\mathsf T}D^+=q_R-q_L; \] the corresponding entropy-stability inequality has $\geq$ in place of equality and yields nonincreasing total discrete entropy on a periodic grid. This convention illustrates what a subpath-and-viscosity construction must verify; it does not specify the missing approximate solver, admissibility conditions, or a time-discrete CFL theorem.

The dated supplied literature assessment, checked 21 August 2026, says that path-conservative theory supports entropy-stable schemes but that a canonical physical or mathematical path and robust general path selection remain model-dependent and open. That broader assessment must be distinguished from the report's already-answered expository question.

Short English statement

The report's design question is said to be answered there by using approximate-Riemann subpaths and suitable entropy-stable viscosity. Its precise construction is not recoverable from this record. The separate August 2026 assessment concerns the broader, model-dependent problem of selecting canonical paths for general nonconservative systems.
