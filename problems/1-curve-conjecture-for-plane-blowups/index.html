Math-1033

(−1)-Curve Conjecture for Plane Blowups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $r\geq 0$, let \[ \pi\colon X_r=\operatorname{Bl}_{p_1,\ldots,p_r}({\mathbb{P}}^2)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^2 \] be the blowup at $r$ points described in the supplied statement as general. For $r=0$, take $X_0={\mathbb{P}}^2$. Write $C^2=C\cdot C$ for the self-intersection number of a curve on $X_r$. A $(-1)$-curve means a smooth rational curve with self-intersection $-1$.

The supplied $(-1)$-curve conjecture asks whether, for every integer $r\geq0$, every such configuration of points, and every irreducible reduced curve $C\subset X_r$, \[ C^2<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C\text{ is smooth and rational and }C^2=-1. \] The ground field and its characteristic are not specified. Moreover, the statement says "general points," whereas the supplied background and literature assessment say "very general points." These qualifications cannot be silently identified: the intended condition on the configuration, the ground field, and the corresponding meaning of rationality require clarification before this becomes a fully specified conjecture.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether every irreducible reduced curve of negative self-intersection on a plane blown up at $r\geq0$ general points must be a smooth rational $(-1)$-curve. The supplied assessment instead uses very general points; that distinction and the unspecified ground field remain unresolved.
