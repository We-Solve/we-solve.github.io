Math-294

Rank-one normalization and status audit for the HRV mixed-Hodge formula

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a complex algebraic variety $Y$, use ordinary-cohomology mixed Hodge numbers \[ h^{p,q;k}(Y)=\dim\operatorname{Gr}_F^p \operatorname{Gr}_{p+q}^WH^k(Y;{\mathbb{C}}), \qquad H(Y;x,y,t)=\sum_{p,q,k}h^{p,q;k}(Y)x^py^qt^k. \] This is not a compactly supported or alternating-sign convention. Write $H_n(x,y,t)$ for this polynomial of the intended twisted Betti moduli space $M_B^d(\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}}))$. Its precise genus, twisting and coprimality hypotheses are not included in the extracted conjecture and require the preceding source definitions.

Conjecture 6.18, Hausel--Rodriguez--Villegas (2004). The source proposes \[ H_n(\sqrt q,\sqrt q,t)=\mathbb H_n(q,t), \] where $\mathbb H_n$ denotes the separately constructed plethystic expression, distinguished here from the trivariate geometric polynomial. The source extraction renders both symbols as $H_n$; the defining plethystic series is absent, so the right side is not treated as an arbitrary renaming of the left side. Specifying that series and its normalization is necessary for a complete all-rank statement.

Rank-two example as supplied. Example 6.19, attributed to Hausel--Thaddeus (2000), gives the following reconstructed reading of the line-broken fractions: \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{H_2(\sqrt q,\sqrt q,t)}{(qt+1)^{2g}} ={}&\frac{(q^2t^3+1)^{2g}}{(q^2t^2-1)(q^2t^4-1)} +\frac{q^{2g-2}t^{4g-4}(q^2t+1)^{2g}}{(q^2-1)(q^2t^2-1)}\\ &-\frac{q^{2g-2}t^{4g-4}(qt+1)^{2g}}{2(qt^2-1)(q-1)} -\frac{q^{2g-2}t^{4g-4}(qt-1)^{2g}}{2(q+1)(qt^2+1)}. \end{aligned} \] For $g=3$ its displayed polynomial is \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{H_2(\sqrt q,\sqrt q,t)}{(qt+1)^6} ={}&t^{12}q^{12}+t^{12}q^{10}+6t^{11}q^{10} +t^{12}q^8+t^{10}q^{10}\\ &+6t^{11}q^8+16t^{10}q^8+6t^9q^8+t^{10}q^6+t^8q^8 +26t^9q^6\\ &+16t^8q^6+6t^7q^6+t^8q^4+t^6q^6+6t^7q^4+16t^6q^4\\ &+6t^5q^4+t^4q^4+t^4q^2+6t^3q^2+t^2q^2+1. \end{aligned} \] The doubled plus sign in the extraction is interpreted as one plus sign. These are source examples with reconstructed-unverified status, not independently verified identities or a substitute for the missing all-rank definition.

Supplied rank-one result and scope audit. In rank one the closed Betti moduli space is $({\mathbb{C}}^*)^{2g}$, and the aid asserts \[ H_1(x,y,t)=(1+xyt)^{2g},\qquad \mathbb H_1(q,t)=(1+qt)^{2g},\qquad h^{k,k;k}=\binom{2g}{k} \] for $0\le k\le2g$, with all other mixed Hodge numbers zero. Its plethystic calculation is said to use the exact AIM normalization and a corrected one-box hook length; that construction is not supplied here.

The aid also distinguishes three literature claims: HRV (2008), Corollary 4.1.11, supplies Hodge--Tateness for the closed twisted family; Mellit (2020) supplies the Poincaré specialization, not the full weight-graded formula; Mellit (2025), Theorem 1.5, supplies a broader generic-semisimple Hodge--Tate and curious-Lefschetz result, not the missing closed formula. These are the aid's dated assessments, not an independent current survey. Under Hodge--Tateness, $h^{p,q;k}=0$ unless $p=q$, so the diagonal $H(\sqrt q,\sqrt q,t)$ retains every coefficient $h^{p,p;k}$. Without that independent condition diagonal specialization is not injective on general trivariate polynomials; knowing a diagonal alone is not a proof of the complete mixed Hodge structure. The Poincaré polynomial is the further specialization $H(1,1,t)$.

Short English statement

The 2004 conjecture identifies a diagonal mixed-Hodge polynomial with a specific plethystic expression. The supplied aid verifies the rank-one normalization and separates general Hodge--Tateness and the Poincaré specialization from the still-missing full formula. The actual plethystic definition and moduli-space hypotheses are absent from this extract.
