The row-size-two subclass and failure of a total-unimodularity shortcut

For a simple graph $G$, let $M$ be its edge-versus-vertex incidence matrix, with two ones in every row. Then the source's covering polyhedron is \[ P(G)=\{x\ge0:x_u+x_v\ge1\text{ for every edge }uv\}. \] We examine the restricted row-size-two case before attempting a general characterization of ideal matrices.
Proposition: Every vertex of $P(G)$ is integral if and only if $G$ is bipartite.
Proof: First suppose $G$ is bipartite. At a vertex, no coordinate can exceed 1: all incident inequalities would then be strict, allowing a sufficiently small positive and negative change of that coordinate while preserving feasibility. Let $F$ be the coordinates strictly between 0 and 1. A tight edge meeting $F$ has both endpoints in $F$. In any connected component of the graph of tight edges on $F$, assign opposite perturbations $+\epsilon,-\epsilon$ to the two bipartition classes, leaving all other coordinates fixed. Every tight edge remains tight, including isolated vertices of this tight-edge graph, which have no such condition. Finitely many nontight inequalities and positive coordinates remain feasible for both signs when $\epsilon>0$ is sufficiently small. If $F$ were nonempty this would express $x$ as the midpoint of two distinct feasible vectors, contradicting its being a vertex. Hence every coordinate is 0 or 1. Conversely, a nonbipartite graph has an odd cycle of length $2r+1$. Put $x=1/2$ on its vertices and $x=1$ elsewhere; this is feasible, even if the cycle has chords. Give cycle coordinates cost 1 and all other coordinates a positive cost $\delta$ chosen so that their total cost is less than $1/2$. The displayed fractional vector has cost less than $r+1$. Every integral feasible vector must have positive value on a vertex cover of the cycle. Summing its $2r+1$ edge inequalities gives twice the cycle sum at least $2r+1$, so that integer sum is at least $r+1$. A minimum of the positive-cost linear objective over $P(G)$ exists in a bounded sublevel set and has an optimal vertex, by the elementary extreme-point theorem for linear programming. That vertex therefore cannot be integral. ∎
The result is the familiar graph-cover characterization, not the sought classification of all $0$--$1$ matrices. In particular, total unimodularity is not necessary for ideality once redundant rows are permitted. Take the three identity rows and the three pair rows $(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$. Its covering polyhedron is exactly $\{x:x_i\ge1\}$, whose only vertex is integral. Yet the determinant of the submatrix formed by the pair rows has absolute value 2, so the whole matrix is not totally unimodular.

Verification and remaining gaps

The triangle has fractional cover value $3/2$ and integer value 2; the redundant-row example has its unique vertex at $(1,1,1)$ and a determinant of absolute value 2. These values were checked exactly. The attempted equivalence "ideal if and only if totally unimodular" fails on that example. The perturbation proof uses bipartite tight-edge components and does not extend directly to rows with three or more ones; the general ideal/minimally nonideal structure remains unresolved by this attempt.
