Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.18

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be an infinite set. For each integer $n\geq 0$, put \[ [\Omega]^n=\{X\subseteq\Omega:|X|=n\},\qquad V_n=\{f:[\Omega]^n\to{\mathbb{Q}}\}. \] Here $V_n$ contains all such functions, without a finite-support restriction. Define the graded ${\mathbb{Q}}$-algebra \[ A=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}V_n \] by extending bilinearly the multiplication \[ (fg)(X)=\sum_{Y\in[X]^n}f(Y)g(X\setminus Y),\qquad f\in V_n,\quad g\in V_m,\quad X\in[\Omega]^{n+m}. \] Thus an element of $A$ has only finitely many nonzero homogeneous components. For $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$, define \[ A^G=\{f\in A:\sigma\cdot f=f\text{ for every }\sigma\in G\},\qquad (\sigma\cdot f)(X)=f(\sigma^{-1}X) \] on homogeneous components. Assume that every orbit $G\omega$, for $\omega\in\Omega$, is infinite. No restriction on the number of orbits on finite subsets is supplied.

For each $n\geq0$, let $c_n\in V_n$ be the constant function with value $1$. To specify a nontrivial quotient, consider the positive-degree interpretation of the source phrase "the ideal generated by constant functions": \[ I_+=\big\langle c_n:n\geq1\big\rangle_{A^G}. \] Every constant function in $V_n$ is a rational multiple of $c_n$, and the multiplication gives $c_1^n=n!c_n$; hence $I_+=(c_1)$.

Under this explicitly qualified interpretation, the question is: for every infinite set $\Omega$ and every permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ whose point orbits are all infinite, is \[ A^G/I_+ \] an integral domain? Here an integral domain means a commutative ring with identity satisfying $1\neq0$ and having no zero divisors. Equivalently, is it true that \[ \forall f,g\in A^G,\qquad fg\in I_+\ \Longrightarrow\ f\in I_+\text{ or }g\in I_+? \] The ideal $I_+$ is proper because it has no nonzero degree-zero component.

The source does not explicitly restrict "constant functions" to positive degrees. With the conventional degree-zero summand used above, $c_0$ is the identity of $A$; including it among the generators instead gives the whole algebra and the zero quotient. Thus the positive-degree formulation is a stated interpretation requiring confirmation, rather than an unqualified transcription of the intended ideal. The source also does not explicitly give the lower limit in $\bigoplus V_n$; the use of $n\geq0$ here is the conventional unital interpretation.

As background, the supplied record attributes to M. Pouzet the result that $A^G$ itself is an integral domain under the stated orbit hypothesis. This attribution and the record's open-status label are supplied source information, not independent verification.

Short English statement

P. J. Cameron asks whether a certain quotient of the algebra of rational-valued functions on finite subsets, invariant under a permutation group with no finite point orbits, is an integral domain. Interpreting the generators as positive-degree constant functions makes the ideal equal to that generated by the constant degree-one function. The source must be checked to confirm this interpretation, since including the degree-zero constant would give the zero quotient.
