Conjecture 4.4 — (Nonpositive curvature).

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $g\geq2$, let $\mathcal M_g=\operatorname{Teich}_g/\operatorname{Mod}_g$ be the moduli orbifold of closed oriented genus-$g$ surfaces, in the usual convention. As the source's footnote specifies, an orbifold Riemannian metric is studied via a $\operatorname{Mod}_g$-invariant Riemannian metric on $\operatorname{Teich}_g$.

Conjecture 4.4 (nonpositive curvature). There is no complete finite-volume Riemannian metric $h$ on $\mathcal M_g$ for which sectional curvatures are nonpositive and uniformly bounded below. Explicitly, for every $g\geq2$, no such $h$ and finite constant $C\geq0$ satisfy \[ \operatorname{Vol}_h(\mathcal M_g)<\infty, \qquad -C\leq K_h(\Pi)\leq0 \] for every tangent $2$-plane $\Pi$, together with metric completeness. The lower bound is allowed to depend on the metric and genus but must hold uniformly throughout the space. The statement does not require a negative upper bound separated from zero.

The source suggests stronger possible versions: relax the finite-volume hypothesis, remove the uniform lower curvature bound, or extend the nonexistence statement to every finite cover and perhaps certain infinite covers. It also proposes an analogue for CAT(0) metrics using a generalized finite-volume notion from [BrF]. Those are suggestions, not included hypotheses or established extensions. The orbifold/cover formulation and the precise CAT(0) finite-volume convention must be specified.

The surrounding discussion points to relative curvature notions [MM1, MM2], weaker holomorphic or Ricci curvature and highly singular metrics [LSY], and Kobayashi or Kähler hyperbolicity [Mc2], and identifies cusp topology as an obstacle to standard metric models. The next section, "Scalar curvature and Q-rank," sets $d(S)=3g-3+n$ for a genus-$g$, $n$-punctured surface and describes it as Teichmüller complex dimension and the pants-decomposition curve count. Those descriptions require the usual stable-surface conventions, absent from that excerpt. It mentions work of Shmuel Weinberger and the author [FW2], but the theorem itself is not supplied and is not reconstructed here.

Short English statement

For every genus $g\geq2$, the conjecture rules out a complete finite-volume moduli-orbifold metric with all sectional curvatures between a finite uniform lower bound and zero. The optional stronger versions and later scalar-curvature discussion are kept separate.
