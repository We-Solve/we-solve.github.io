Open Problems and Questions about Geodesics — Conjecture 31 (Burago-Kleiner)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a closed surface of genus at least $2$, as in the supplied source, and let $S^1={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$. Let $g$ be a Riemannian metric on $M\times S^1$ with no conjugate points: along every geodesic, a Jacobi field that vanishes at two distinct parameter values is identically zero. Define \[ p:M\times{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow M\times S^1,\qquad p(x,t)=(x,t+{\mathbb{Z}}), \qquad \widetilde g=p^*g. \] This is the infinite cyclic cover obtained by unwrapping the circle factor; its covering subgroup is $\pi_1(M)\times\{0\}\subseteq\pi_1(M)\times{\mathbb{Z}}$.

The supplied Conjecture 31, attributed in context to Burago--Kleiner, asserts that for every such $M$ and $g$, the lifted metric splits as a Riemannian product. Interpreted as an isometric splitting, the assertion is that there exist a Riemannian metric $g_1$ on $M$ and a diffeomorphism $F:M\times{\mathbb{R}}\to M\times{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ F^*(g_1\oplus{\,\mathrm{d}} t^2)=\widetilde g. \] The source writes an equality of products without specifying whether the splitting must respect the original product coordinates or any prescribed deck action. The formulation above expresses an isometric splitting; these additional compatibility requirements remain unresolved. The source also does not explicitly specify orientability in its surface hypothesis.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether every Riemannian metric without conjugate points on the product of a closed surface of genus at least $2$ and a circle becomes isometric to a surface times a Euclidean line after unwrapping the circle. The supplied statement leaves the required compatibility of this splitting with the original coordinates unspecified.
