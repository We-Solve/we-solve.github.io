Maximum likelihood succeeds statistically but remains expensive

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, a secret $s\in\mathbb F_2^n$, independent uniform rows $a_i\in\mathbb F_2^n$, and independent errors $e_i$ with probability $\varepsilon$ of being 1. The observations are $b_i=\langle a_i,s\rangle+e_i$. The report already distinguishes polynomial sample requirements from the computational difficulty of finding the secret. We test exhaustive maximum likelihood and then a restricted noise-elimination route.
Proposition: For $m$ samples, the probability that some wrong secret has no more disagreements than $s$ is at most \[ (2^n-1)\rho^m,\qquad \rho=\tfrac12+\sqrt{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}<1. \] In particular $m\ge(n\log2+\log(1/\delta))/(-\log\rho)$ suffices for failure probability at most $\delta$, but enumerating the candidates costs $O(2^nmn)$ elementary operations.
Proof: For fixed $s'\ne s$, let $D_i$ be its disagreement indicator minus the true disagreement indicator. The nonzero linear form $\langle a_i,s'-s\rangle$ is uniform and independent of $e_i$. Hence $D_i$ takes values $0,1,-1$ with probabilities $1/2,(1-\varepsilon)/2,\varepsilon/2$. For $t>0$, \[ \mathbb E e^{-tD_i}=\tfrac12+\tfrac{1-\varepsilon}{2}e^{-t}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}e^t. \] Its minimum occurs at $t=\frac12\log((1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)$ and equals $\rho$. Independence and Markov's inequality give $\Pr(\sum_iD_i\le0)\le\rho^m$. A union bound over the $2^n-1$ wrong candidates proves the claim, including uniqueness when none ties or improves on $s$. ∎

A precise obstruction to a restricted elimination route

Suppose that, conditional on the sampled design, the only observations retained about a uniformly random secret bit are $r$ parity combinations, each measuring that bit plus the parity of exactly $w$ errors. Require that the error supports be pairwise disjoint and chosen using the design alone, independently of noisy labels. No other secret-dependent information is retained. Put $\beta=(1-2\varepsilon)^w>0$.
Proposition: Any decision rule using only this restricted transcript and having average error at most $1/3$ must satisfy $r\ge1/(9\beta^2)$.
Proof: The parity noise has bias $\mathbb E(-1)^{e_1+\cdots+e_w}=\prod_j\mathbb E(-1)^{e_j}=\beta$. The two conditional one-observation laws therefore have probabilities $(1+\beta)/2,(1-\beta)/2$ in opposite order. Their affinity, the sum of square roots of corresponding probability products, is $\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$. Independence gives product affinity $A=(1-\beta^2)^{r/2}$. For arbitrary finite laws $P,Q$, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $|P-Q|=|\sqrt P-\sqrt Q|(\sqrt P+\sqrt Q)$ gives \[ \operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{(2-2A)(2+2A)}=\sqrt{1-A^2}. \] Here $1-(1-\beta^2)^r\le r\beta^2$, by expanding the probability of at least one success in $r$ Bernoulli trials or by induction. Thus total variation is at most $\sqrt r\,\beta$. For a uniform hidden bit the best success probability is $(1+\operatorname{TV})/2$: at each observation value one chooses the larger of its two likelihoods, whose sum is $1+\operatorname{TV}$. Error at most $1/3$ requires total variation at least $1/3$, proving the bound. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

Parity biases were enumerated exactly for $\varepsilon=1/4$ and $w=1,\ldots,10$, and one seeded small maximum-likelihood instance was exhaustively checked. Dense parity combinations can need exponentially many disjoint repetitions in this restricted experiment. The conclusion does not cover overlapping combinations, label-dependent choices, other retained information, or general LPN algorithms. The exact missing step is a justified reduction of every efficient algorithm to this restricted transcript; no such reduction is available here. Neither the statistical bound nor this obstruction establishes polynomial-time learnability or average-case computational hardness.
