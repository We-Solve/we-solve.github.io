Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.44

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For elements of a group, put $[x,g]=x^{-1}g^{-1}xg$, and define the left-normed iterated Engel commutators by \[ [x,{}_1g]=[x,g],\qquad [x,{}_{k+1}g]=[[x,{}_kg],g]\quad(k\geq1). \] Following the supplied source, a profinite group has rank at most $r$ if every subgroup can be topologically generated by at most $r$ elements; it has finite rank if such an integer $r\geq0$ exists. For a subgroup that is not closed, topological generation may be interpreted in its induced topology: generators $h_1,\ldots,h_s\in H$, with $s\leq r$, satisfy $\overline{\langle h_1,\ldots,h_s\rangle}^{\,H}=H$. The source does not explicitly distinguish this reading from a definition quantified only over closed subgroups; this convention requires review.

Let $G$ be a profinite group satisfying \[ \forall g\in G\;\exists E_g\leq G\text{ closed of finite rank}\;\forall x\in G\;\exists n(x,g)\geq1\;\forall k\geq n(x,g), \qquad [x,{}_kg]\in E_g. \] The finite rank bound for $E_g$ may depend on $g$; the threshold may depend on both $x$ and $g$. No uniform bound is assumed.

Must there exist a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ of finite rank such that $G/N$ is locally nilpotent? Here local nilpotence means that every finitely generated abstract subgroup of $G/N$ is nilpotent, with no uniform bound on its nilpotency class.

The source does not explicitly require $N$ to be closed. Consequently, whether the intended conclusion includes closedness of $N$, and hence a profinite quotient with its quotient topology, remains unresolved. If nonclosed $N$ is allowed, its finite rank must also be interpreted using its induced topology, since the supplied definition is stated for profinite groups.

Short English statement

Suppose each element $g$ of a profinite group has a closed subgroup of finite rank containing all sufficiently long Engel commutators with $g$, with the required length allowed to depend on the starting element. Must the group have a normal subgroup of finite rank whose quotient is locally nilpotent? The source leaves the closedness of this normal subgroup and the precise subgroup convention in the rank definition unstated.
