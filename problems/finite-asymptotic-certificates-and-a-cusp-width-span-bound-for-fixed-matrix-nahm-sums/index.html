Finite asymptotic certificates and a cusp-width span bound for fixed-matrix Nahm sums

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source's symbols $A$ and $B$ are not defined in the isolated problem text. The supplied research aid interprets them through Nahm sums. In the standard convention, for rational symmetric positive-definite $A\in M_r({\mathbb{Q}})$, $B\in{\mathbb{Q}}^r$, and $C\in{\mathbb{Q}}$, put \[ F_{A,B,C}(q)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_0^r} \frac{q^{\frac12n^{\mathsf T}An+B^{\mathsf T}n+C}}{(q)_{n_1}\cdots(q)_{n_r}},\qquad (q)_m=\prod_{j=1}^m(1-q^j),\quad(q)_0=1, \] with $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$. A scalar weight-zero modular function for a finite-index group $\Gamma$ is invariant under $\Gamma$, holomorphic on the upper half-plane here, and meromorphic at its cusps; the reported span bound additionally requires trivial multiplier.

Problem 2.1. For fixed $A$, bound the number of possible $B$, and bound the dimension of the vector space determined by the $B$. The source allows ineffective as well as effective bounds. Under the aid's interpretation, "possible" means admitting a modular Nahm sum for some $C$, and the vector space is the complex span of the resulting functions. The precise original surrounding definition is not supplied.

Reported algebraic certificate. Scalar modularity forces necessary Vlasenko--Zwegers equations \[ P_p^A(B)=0,\qquad\deg_B P_p^A\leq2p, \] and uniquely determines $C$. Their explicit coefficients, index convention and the formula for $C$ are absent from the summary. Over a field $K$ containing their algebraic coefficients, consider a finite initial ideal $I_m$ generated by the first $m$ equations. If its complex obstruction locus is zero-dimensional, its finite quotient length \[ D_m=\dim_K K[B_1,\ldots,B_r]/I_m \] bounds the number of distinct modular $B$ by $D_m$, with multiplicities included in the length. The empty-locus case has bound zero. The aid invokes Noetherianity to say that if the full necessary-equation locus is finite, some finite truncation supplies such a certificate.

This is conditional on zero-dimensionality of the obstruction locus. It does not prove that locus finite for every $A$, that every solution is modular, or that mere finiteness of the actually modular $B$ forces the larger obstruction locus to be finite. Effective use also needs the equations in exact computable form.

Reported common-group span bound. Fix one common finite-index group $\Gamma$ on which all functions under consideration are scalar weight-zero modular functions with trivial multiplier. For each cusp $\mathfrak a$, let $h_{\mathfrak a}$ be its width. The aid cites a Garoufalidis--Zagier valuation bound denoted by $C_0(A)$; its definition is not included. In the convention where \[ \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak a}F\geq h_{\mathfrak a}C_0(A), \] the corresponding effective pole divisor has coefficients $\max\{0,\lfloor-h_{\mathfrak a}C_0(A)\rfloor\}$. Riemann--Roch then yields the reported bound \[ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{span}\{F_{A,B,C}\} \leq1+\sum_{\mathfrak a\ \mathrm{cusp\ of}\ \Gamma} \max\{0,\lfloor-h_{\mathfrak a}C_0(A)\rfloor\}. \] The exact valuation theorem and $C_0(A)$ must be verified before numerical use. The result depends on the common group and its cusp widths as well as $A$; it is not an $A$-only bound when modular groups and multipliers vary freely. The original fixed-$A$ problem remains broader than these certificate and common-group reductions.

Short English statement

For the report's Nahm-sum interpretation, modular parameters satisfy necessary polynomial equations. A zero-dimensional finite subsystem gives an effective bound on their number, but its existence is conditional. Separately, a common scalar modular group and a cusp valuation bound control the dimension of the span; allowing arbitrary groups is a broader problem.
