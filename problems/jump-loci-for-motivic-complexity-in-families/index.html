Jump Loci for Motivic Complexity in Families

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f\colon X\to S$ be a smooth morphism of quasi-projective varieties. The source does not specify their ground field, and does not assume that $f$ is proper or projective. For a point $s\in S$, write \[ X_s=X\times_S\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s), \qquad X_s^{\times n}=\underbrace{X_s\times_{\kappa(s)}\cdots\times_{\kappa(s)}X_s}_{n\text{ factors}} \quad(n\geq 1), \] where $\kappa(s)$ is the residue field. These are scheme-theoretic fibers; whether the intended comparison instead uses geometric fibers is unspecified. An algebraic cycle of codimension $r$ on a variety $Y$ is a finite integral linear combination of integral closed subvarieties of codimension $r$. This definition alone supplies neither an equivalence relation on cycles nor a measure of their abundance.

Source question. Describe the locus of points $s\in S$ for which the motive of $X_s$ is simpler than the motive of a very general fiber, in the stated sense that $X_s$ and its positive Cartesian powers contain more algebraic cycles.

Unresolved formulation. This is a research question whose exceptional locus is not precisely defined by the supplied record. A precise formulation requires a ground field and a choice of ordinary or geometric points; a category of motives appropriate to these possibly nonproper fibers; coefficients and an equivalence relation or realization for algebraic cycles; and a comparison that defines what it means to have more cycles. The source does not specify whether an increase in one power and one codimension suffices, or whether a stronger condition across powers is intended. It also does not specify a component of $S$, assumptions under which a very general fiber supplies a common comparison value, or the intended meaning of describing the locus. Over an appropriate uncountable algebraically closed field, a very general point usually means a point outside a countable union of proper closed subsets of a specified irreducible base; those hypotheses and the relevant exceptional subsets are not supplied here. Consequently, no numerical jump condition, algebraicity assertion, or fully quantified conjecture can be extracted without additional choices.

Short English statement

The question asks where fibers of a smooth family of quasi-projective varieties acquire additional algebraic cycles on themselves or their Cartesian powers, indicating a simpler motive than that of a very general fiber. The supplied record leaves the motivic framework, cycle comparison, and meaning of very general unspecified, so a precise exceptional locus requires further mathematical formulation.
