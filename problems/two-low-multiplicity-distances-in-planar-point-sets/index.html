Math-6939

Two Low-Multiplicity Distances in Planar Point Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ with $n>4$, and let $P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a set of $n$ distinct points, with Euclidean distance $\|p-q\|_2$. Define the set of distances determined by $P$ by \[ D(P)=\{\|p-q\|_2:p,q\in P,\ p\ne q\}. \] For each $d\in D(P)$, define its multiplicity by \[ m_P(d)=\#\left\{\{p,q\}:p,q\in P,\ p\ne q,\ \|p-q\|_2=d\right\}. \] Thus each unordered pair of distinct points is counted once, and every distance in $D(P)$ has positive multiplicity.

Question. Is it true that for every integer $n>4$ and every such set $P$, there exist $d_1,d_2\in D(P)$ such that \[ d_1\ne d_2,\qquad 1\le m_P(d_1)\le n,\qquad 1\le m_P(d_2)\le n? \] The question imposes no convexity or general-position assumption on $P$. The supplied background mentions results for convex or "not-too-convex" sets, but does not define the latter class or state the precise hypotheses of those results; those qualifications cannot be formalized further from this record.

Short English statement

Must every set of $n>4$ distinct points in the Euclidean plane determine two different positive distances, each realized by at most $n$ unordered pairs of points? The supplied record labels the general question open; this status is not independently verified here.
