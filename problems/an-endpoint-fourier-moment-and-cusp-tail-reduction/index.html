Math-1592

An endpoint Fourier-moment and cusp-tail reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the recovered cusp normalization, $X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H$ has finite hyperbolic area and \[ \Gamma\cap\{z\mapsto z+a:a\in{\mathbb{R}}\}=\{z\mapsto z+a:a\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}. \] Write $u_y(t)$, periodic in $t$ with period $1$, for the corresponding closed horocycle of length $y^{-1}$ in $T^1X$, and $\lambda$ for probability Liouville measure. Conjecture 21 of J. Marklof and A. Strömbergsson asks, for a fixed badly approximable $\alpha$ and fixed $0<c_1<c_2$, whether \[ \sup_{c_1M^{-2}\leq y\leq c_2M^{-2}} \left|\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M f(u_y(m\alpha))-\int f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda\right|\longrightarrow0. \] The continuous function $f$ must satisfy the cusp-growth condition referred to as [136], whose exact exponent range and definition are not supplied. Bad approximation means $\|k\alpha\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\geq c_\alpha/|k|$ for every nonzero integer $k$.

The aid gives a conditional Fourier reduction. Set $F_y(t)=f(u_y(t))$, let $\widehat F_y(k)$ be its period-one Fourier coefficient, and assume \[ K(y)=\sum_{k\ne0}|k|\,|\widehat F_y(k)|<\infty. \] Then the sampling error relative to the continuous horocycle mean satisfies \[ \left|\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M F_y(m\alpha)-\int_0^1F_y(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t\right| \leq\frac{K(y)}{2c_\alpha M}. \] Hence uniform $K(y)=o(M)$ across the full critical band, together with continuous-horocycle equidistribution for the test function, proves the displayed conclusion. A bound $K(y)=O(y^{-\beta})$ gives sampling error $O(M^{2\beta-1})$, sufficient for $\beta<1/2$. At $\beta=1/2$ this only yields $O(1)$ and is inconclusive, not a counterexample. The aid does not establish the required Fourier bound for every test function in the conjecture.

To pass from compact support to an unbounded cusp-growth class, a separate uniform-integrability condition is required. Let $H\geq1$ be a proper invariant cusp-height function and suppose the class under consideration has $|f|\leq C H^\gamma$, with $H^\gamma\in L^1(\lambda)$. Define the sampled probabilities $\eta_{M,y}=M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M\delta_{u_y(m\alpha)}$. The aid's truncation criterion requires, in addition to uniform compact-core equidistribution, \[ \lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{M\to\infty} \sup_{c_1M^{-2}\leq y\leq c_2M^{-2}} \int H^\gamma\mathbf1_{\{H>R\}}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\eta_{M,y}=0. \] Under these hypotheses cutoff approximation gives the desired convergence for that growth class. The exact class denoted $B_\gamma$ and its admissible exponents in [136] still need recovery. Compact-support convergence alone does not automatically control positive-growth cusp tails. The extracted ending "It was observed in [136] that" is incomplete, and its missing qualification is not invented.

Short English statement

At the critical height scale, the supplied aid reduces sampling to a uniform Fourier-moment estimate and handles cusp growth by a separate sampled-tail condition. Neither unproved gate is treated as a full solution of the conjecture.
