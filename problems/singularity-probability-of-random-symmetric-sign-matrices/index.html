Singularity Probability of Random Symmetric Sign Matrices

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: The symmetric unbiased sign-matrix singularity probability is claimed to be $(1/2+o(1))^n$; With fixed sign bias $p\ne1/2$, the claimed symmetric-matrix singularity rate is $(p^2+(1-p)^2+o(1))^n$, attained by equal rows. Together they give a claimed affirmative answer in the matching scope.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Let $A_n$ be real symmetric, with independent uniform $\{-1,1\}$ entries on and above its diagonal. The claimed singularity asymptotic is \[ \mathbb P(\det A_n=0)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)^n=2^{-n+o(n)}. \] The diagonal entries are randomized as well as the off-diagonal entries.
Result 2.
Fix $p\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}$. In a symmetric matrix $A_n^{(p)}$, let the upper-triangular entries, including the diagonal, be independent, equal to $1$ with probability $p$ and $-1$ otherwise. The source claims \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P(\det A_n^{(p)}=0)^{1/n}=p^2+(1-p)^2. \] This is a fixed-bias assertion; the unbiased case is supplied by a separate manuscript.

Outline of the argument

Lattice covering separates robust kernel vectors from structured subspaces. Discriminant pairings and principal-minor algebra restrict admissible new columns; typical rank estimates and small-ball bounds then exclude other singular mechanisms at a larger exponential rate.

Kernel vectors determine possible exposed columns, with sparse-tail cases separated from the conditioned bit model. Lattice tools and prime-field corank bounds control the structured alternatives; the biased column estimate matches the repeated-row lower rate.
