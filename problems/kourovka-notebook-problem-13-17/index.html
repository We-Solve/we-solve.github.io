Math-2884

Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.17

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero, let $G$ be a group, and let $V\ne\{0\}$ be a right module over the group algebra $K[G]$. No finite-dimensionality assumption is imposed. For $g\in G$, the expression $v(g-1)^n$ denotes the action of the indicated group-algebra element on $v$.

The representation is a nil-representation if \[ \forall v\in V\;\forall g\in G\;\exists n=n(v,g)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1} \quad v(g-1)^n=0. \] It is irreducible if its only $K$-linear subspaces invariant under every element of $G$ are $\{0\}$ and $V$. It is trivial if $vg=v$ for every $v\in V$ and $g\in G$.

Question (S. Vovsi). For every field $K$ of characteristic zero, every group $G$, and every nonzero irreducible nil-representation $V$ over $K$, must the representation be trivial? Equivalently, must \[ vg=v\qquad\text{for all }v\in V\text{ and }g\in G? \] The supplied source additionally states that the analogous assertion is false in prime characteristic; it supplies no counterexample or further qualification for that statement.

Short English statement

If each group element acts locally unipotently on a nonzero irreducible representation over a field of characteristic zero, must every group element act as the identity? The exponent may depend on both the vector and the group element, and the representation need not be finite-dimensional.
