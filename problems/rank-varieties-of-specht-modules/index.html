Rank Varieties of Specht Modules

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The record contains a complexity question and related rank-variety questions. The conventional setting below makes the complexity question precise, but the field and rank-variety conventions are reconstructed mathematical context rather than explicit hypotheses supplied by the record.

Let $p$ be a prime, let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and let $\mu$ be a partition of an integer $m\geq 1$. Write $\mathfrak S_m$ for the symmetric group and $S^\mu$ for its Specht module over $k$: the submodule of the permutation module on $\mu$-tabloids generated by the polytabloids \[ e_t=\sum_{\sigma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\{\sigma t\}, \] where $t$ ranges over tableaux of shape $\mu$, $C_t$ is the subgroup permuting entries within each column, and $\{t\}$ denotes the tabloid obtained by forgetting the order within each row.

Let $\operatorname{core}_p(\mu)$ denote the partition obtained by successively removing rim hooks of length $p$. The $p$-weight is \[ w_p(\mu)=\frac{m-\lvert\operatorname{core}_p(\mu)\rvert}{p}. \] Call $\mu$ a $p\times p$ partition if every distinct part is divisible by $p$ and the multiplicity of every distinct part is divisible by $p$. Equivalently, its Young diagram can be tiled by aligned $p\times p$ squares.

For a finite-dimensional $k\mathfrak S_m$-module $M$, its complexity $c_{\mathfrak S_m}(M)$ is zero if $M$ is projective; otherwise it is the least integer $c\geq 1$ for which the terms $P_j$ of a minimal projective resolution satisfy $\dim_k P_j=O(j^{c-1})$ as $j\to\infty$.

The converse question in the supplied original statement has the following conventional formulation: for every prime $p$, every algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and every partition $\mu$ that is not $p\times p$, must \[ c_{\mathfrak S_m}(S^\mu)=w_p(\mu) \] hold? The supplied dated assessment describes the $p\times p$ direction as settled and the converse as unproved in general; this is source-reported status, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.

For context on the related questions, fix an elementary abelian subgroup $E=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_r\rangle\cong(C_p)^r$ of $\mathfrak S_m$, with $r\geq1$. One conventional rank variety of a $kE$-module $M$ is \[ V_E^{\mathrm{rank}}(M)=\{0\}\cup\left\{\alpha\in k^r\setminus\{0\}:M\text{ is not free over }k[t]/(t^p),\quad t\cdot v=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i(g_i-1)v\right\}. \] Its projectivization consists of the lines represented by its nonzero points, inside $\mathbb P^{r-1}_k$.

For the relevant partitions whose parts are all divisible by $p$, the record asks for a characterization of their Specht modules through rank varieties, asks whether an integer denoted $n$ in a rank-variety formula is always $1$, and asks whether a stated upper bound for the degree of the projectivized rank variety can be improved or made sharp. These questions cannot be formulated completely from the supplied record: it omits the relevant subgroup $E$, any further restrictions on the partitions, the rank-variety formula defining $n$, and the stated degree bound. The conventional definition above does not identify those missing data. The background also mentions a proposed universal bound on the complexity drop, but supplies neither that bound nor its hypotheses; no numerical inequality is reconstructed here.

Short English statement

In the conventional modular representation setting, the main question asks whether every partition that is not $p\times p$ has a Specht module whose complexity equals its $p$-weight. Related questions concern rank varieties for partitions with parts divisible by $p$, an unspecified integer $n$, and a sharp degree bound. Their defining formula and hypotheses are missing. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation requiring independent review.
