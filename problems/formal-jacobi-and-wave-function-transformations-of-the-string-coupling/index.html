A formal transformation criterion.
Treat $F(\tau,\lambda)=\sum_{g\ge0}f_g(\tau)\lambda^{2g-2}$ as a formal Laurent series with only the possible order-two pole. Let $\gamma\tau=(a\tau+b)/(c\tau+d)$, and suppose, as an explicit hypothesis, that each coefficient transforms by \[f_g(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{k+2g-2}f_g(\tau). \] Substituting coefficient by coefficient proves the formal identity \[F(\gamma\tau,\lambda/(c\tau+d))=(c\tau+d)^kF(\tau,\lambda). \] Conversely, equality of these formal Laurent series forces exactly the individual coefficient laws. Thus this scaling transformation neither adds nor removes modular information: it packages the specified weights consistently. Also $F(\tau,-\lambda)=F(\tau,\lambda)$ is automatic from the even powers, with no modular hypothesis.
Why this is not a Jacobi law.
A genuine Jacobi transformation can contain an exponential quadratic factor in $\lambda$ and elliptic translations $\lambda\mapsto\lambda+m\tau+n$. Such translations are not automatically defined on an arbitrary infinite formal series about zero: a fixed low-order coefficient after a nonzero shift may receive infinitely many contributions. Nor does the weight hypothesis allow the quadratic multiplier unless corresponding mixing among the $f_g$ is present. Convergence or a specified formal completion is needed.
Gap.

The algebra above is a concrete conditional test, not evidence that an unspecified genus expansion satisfies such a law.
