Arithmetic Finiteness of Gushel–Mukai Threefolds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field, let $S$ be a finite, possibly empty, set of finite places of $K$, and let \[ \mathcal O_K[S^{-1}]=\{a\in K:v(a)\geq 0\text{ for every finite place }v\notin S\},\qquad B=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_K[S^{-1}]). \] Under the usual geometric-fiber interpretation, a smooth Fano threefold over $B$ is a smooth projective morphism $f:X\to B$ whose geometric fibers are connected three-dimensional varieties and whose relative anticanonical line bundle $\omega_{X/B}^{-1}$ is relatively ample. For a geometric fiber $X_{\bar b}$, write \[ \rho(X_{\bar b})=\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\left(\operatorname{NS}(X_{\bar b})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\right), \] where $\operatorname{NS}$ denotes the group of line bundles modulo algebraic equivalence. In the formulation below, Picard rank one means $\rho(X_{\bar b})=1$ for every geometric point $\bar b\to B$. The supplied record does not explicitly specify whether Picard rank is geometric or arithmetic, or whether it is imposed on every fiber or only on the generic fiber; this convention therefore requires confirmation.

Two such families $f:X\to B$ and $f':X'\to B$ are $B$-isomorphic if there is an isomorphism $\phi:X\to X'$ satisfying $f'\circ\phi=f$. No marking or chosen polarization is included in this equivalence relation. Let $\mathcal F_1(B)$ be the set of these $B$-isomorphism classes.

General finiteness question. Is it true that, for every number field $K$ and every finite set $S$ of finite places of $K$, \[ \#\mathcal F_1\left(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_K[S^{-1}])\right)<\infty? \]

Distinguished Gushel--Mukai case. Does the same finiteness assertion hold for the subclass consisting of smooth Gushel--Mukai Fano threefolds over $B$? To identify the characteristic-zero geometric objects, a smooth Gushel--Mukai threefold over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic zero is a smooth three-dimensional intersection \[ C\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_5)\cap\mathbf P(W)\cap Q \subseteq\mathbf P(k\oplus\textstyle\bigwedge^2 V_5), \] where $\dim_k V_5=5$, $C\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_5)$ is the projective cone over the Plücker-embedded Grassmannian, $W\subseteq k\oplus\bigwedge^2 V_5$ has dimension eight, and $Q$ is a quadric hypersurface in $\mathbf P(W)$; the intersection avoids the cone vertex. This description identifies the geometric type but does not settle the intended definition over $B$. The record does not specify whether Gushel--Mukai type is required only on the geometric generic fiber or on all geometric fibers, nor does it supply an integral definition at residue characteristic two. Consequently, the restricted finiteness question retains this unresolved qualification. No restriction on residue characteristics is stated in the source.

The original statement singles out the Gushel--Mukai case, whereas the cleaned statement also asks the general Picard-rank-one question. Finiteness for the general class would imply finiteness for its Gushel--Mukai subclass; a converse is not asserted here.

Short English statement

For every number field and every finite set of allowed finite places, ask whether only finitely many smooth Fano threefold families of Picard rank one exist over the corresponding ring of $S$-integers, up to isomorphism over that ring. The source particularly asks this for Gushel--Mukai threefolds. The Picard-rank convention and the integral meaning of Gushel--Mukai type require clarification. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
