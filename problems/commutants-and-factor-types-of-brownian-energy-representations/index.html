Commutants and Factor Types of Brownian Energy Representations

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record refers to left and right Brownian energy representations but does not define them, their underlying groups, or their Hilbert spaces. The following is therefore a formal template for the intended question, conditional on supplying those data; it does not specify a particular Brownian model.

Suppose the intended representations are realized on a common complex Hilbert space $H$, and let $\mathcal U_L,\mathcal U_R\subseteq B(H)$ denote their respective operator images. Here $B(H)$ is the algebra of bounded linear operators on $H$. For $S\subseteq B(H)$, define its commutant by \[ S'=\{T\in B(H):TA=AT\text{ for every }A\in S\}, \qquad S''=(S')'. \] Define the generated von Neumann algebras by \[ M_L=W^*(\mathcal U_L),\qquad M_R=W^*(\mathcal U_R), \] where $W^*(S)$ is the smallest unital von Neumann subalgebra of $B(H)$ containing $S$.

The intended question is whether \[ M_L'=M_R\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_R'=M_L. \] If these equalities hold, the record further asks for the type of the resulting factor. For a von Neumann algebra $M\subseteq B(H)$, its center is $Z(M)=M\cap M'$, and $M$ is a factor when $Z(M)={\mathbb{C}} I_H$. Mutual commutant equality alone does not imply this condition. Indeed, under the displayed equalities, \[ Z(M_L)=M_L\cap M_R=Z(M_R). \] Thus the factor-type question requires a supplied or established trivial-center hypothesis; without one, factoriality must also be determined before a factor type can be assigned.

The representation domains, Brownian measure or process, energy-space construction, parameter ranges, and any boundary or normalization conditions are absent from the record. Consequently, the template above cannot identify a unique mathematical problem without further source information.

Short English statement

For the intended left and right Brownian energy representations, ask whether their generated von Neumann algebras are each other's commutants and, if they are factors, determine their type. The record omits the representation data needed to make this question specific, and mutual commutant equality by itself does not imply factoriality.
