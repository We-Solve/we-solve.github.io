Math-4219

Arithmetic Integer Parts of Surreal and Real Closed Fields

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An ordered field $K$ is real closed if every positive element has a square root in $K$ and every polynomial in $K[X]$ of odd degree has a root in $K$. An integer part of $K$ is a subring $I\subseteq K$, containing the same identity element, whose induced order is discrete with least positive element $1$, and such that \[ \forall x\in K\;\exists a\in I\quad a\leq x<a+1. \] Write $I_{\geq 0}=\{a\in I:a\geq 0\}$. In the arithmetic language $L_{\mathrm{ar}}=\{0,1,+,\cdot,<\}$, true arithmetic is the complete theory \[ \operatorname{Th}_{L_{\mathrm{ar}}}({\mathbb{N}},0,1,+,\cdot,<). \] The phrase "an integer part that is a model of true arithmetic" is interpreted here in the usual arithmetic sense that its nonnegative cone, with the inherited operations and order, satisfies this theory. This convention requires confirmation against the intended source formulation.

The supplied record groups three questions:

  1. Investigate the behavior of fast-growing functions, including the Ackermann function, in real closed fields. The record does not specify a version of the Ackermann function, a domain for such functions, a method of extending them beyond ${\mathbb{N}}$, or a property to prove. This part therefore remains a research direction rather than a determinate mathematical assertion.
  2. Let $\mathbf{No}$ denote the ordered field of surreal numbers. Does there exist an integer part $I$ of $\mathbf{No}$ such that \[ (I_{\geq 0},0,1,+,\cdot,<) \models \operatorname{Th}_{L_{\mathrm{ar}}}({\mathbb{N}},0,1,+,\cdot,<)? \] Here the integer-part condition is the condition above with $K=\mathbf{No}$. Since $\mathbf{No}$ is a proper class, the permitted class framework and the meaning of satisfaction for the resulting class structure must be specified before this can be treated as an ordinary formal existence statement. The supplied record does not specify that framework.
  3. Does every real closed field $K$ have a normal integer part? The universal quantifier ranges over all real closed fields, with no additional hypothesis supplied. The adjective "normal" is not defined in the record. Consequently, this question cannot be made into a fully specified predicate on integer parts from the supplied evidence; its intended additional condition must be recovered from the source.
These are statement-only formulations with unresolved qualifications, not mathematical resolutions.

Short English statement

The problem asks how fast-growing arithmetic functions relate to real closed fields, whether the surreal numbers have an integer part whose nonnegative cone satisfies true arithmetic, and whether every real closed field has a normal integer part. The fast-growth target, the meaning of "normal", and the class framework for the surreal question remain unspecified.
