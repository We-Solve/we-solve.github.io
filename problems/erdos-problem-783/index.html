Statement

Fix a constant $C>0$ and let $n$ be large. We choose a set $A\subseteq\{2,3,\dots,n\}$ subject to

  1. $\gcd(a,b)=1$ for all distinct $a,b\in A$ (pairwise coprime);
  2. $\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A} \frac1a \le C$.
Define the "uncovered" set \[ U(A) := \big\{ m\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}: \forall a\in A,\ a\nmid m\big\}. \] Equivalently, $U(A)$ are those integers $\le n$ not divisible by any element of $A$.

Goal. Choose $A$ (depending on $n$ and $C$) to minimize $|U(A)|$.

Candidate suggested in the problem. Let $q_1>q_2>\cdots$ be the primes in decreasing order starting at the largest prime $\le n$. Let $k$ be maximal such that $\sum_{i=1}^k 1/q_i \le C$, and take $A=\{q_1,\dots,q_k\}$. The question asks whether this choice minimizes $|U(A)|$.

Erdős Problem #783: sieving with pairwise coprime moduli

Statement and scope

Fix $C>0$. Among pairwise coprime sets $A\subseteq\{2,\ldots,N\}$ with $\mu(A):=\sum_{a\in A}1/a\leq C$, which minimize \[ U_N(A):=\#\{m\leq N:a\nmid m\text{ for every }a\in A\}? \] The current editorial records Tao's asymptotic resolution. We reconstruct the reduction to prime moduli, including uniform error control, and obtain \[ \min_A U_N(A)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o_C(1)\right)N. \tag{1} \] The Dickman function satisfies $\rho(u)=1$ on $[0,1]$ and $u\rho'(u)=-\rho(u-1)$ for $u>1$. A suitable terminal interval of primes attains $1$. We also verify a finite counterexample to exact terminal-prime optimality. Attempt status: unresolved for an exact description of the finite minimizing sets; the asymptotic value and an asymptotically optimal choice are established relative to the inputs stated next.

External inputs

We use three classical analytic facts explicitly.
  1. Hildebrand's prime-sieve lower bound, in the uniform form quoted as Theorem 1.2 of Tao's note: for sets $P$ of primes with $\mu(P)\leq C$, \[ N^{-1}U_N(P)\geq\rho(e^{\mu(P)})-o_C(1), \] uniformly in $P=P(N)$.
  2. The Dickman inequality $\rho(u)\rho(v)\geq\rho(uv)$ for $u,v\geq1$, stated and proved in Tao's Lemma 3.1. One formulation of its analytic basis is log-concavity of $\rho$ on $[1,\infty)$.
  3. The prime-counting bound $\pi(x)\ll x/\log x$, Mertens' estimate $\sum_{p\leq x}1/p=\log\log x+B+o(1)$, and the fixed-parameter smooth-number asymptotic $\Psi(N,N^{1/u})/N\to\rho(u)$.
The prime-sieve theorem and Dickman facts are not reproved below. The passage from primes to arbitrary pairwise coprime moduli is.

Two uniform elementary estimates

Write $\sigma_N(A)=U_N(A)/N$. The union bound gives \[ |\sigma_N(A)-\sigma_N(A')|\leq\mu(A\triangle A'). \tag{2} \] Every composite $a\leq x$ has a prime factor at most $\sqrt x$, and pairwise coprimality makes these chosen factors distinct. Thus \[ \#\{a\in A:a\leq x,\ a\text{ composite}\}\leq\pi(\sqrt x)\leq\sqrt x. \] By summing over dyadic intervals, \[ \sum_{\substack{a\in A,\ a>z\\a\ {\rm composite}}}\frac1a \leq\sum_{j\geq0}\frac{\sqrt{2^{j+1}z}}{2^jz} \ll z^{-1/2}. \tag{3} \] We may therefore discard all sufficiently large composite moduli at uniformly small cost. Also \[ |A\cap[2,x]|\leq\pi(x)+\sqrt x\ll x/\log x\quad(x\geq3). \tag{4} \]

Truncated inclusion--exclusion, including its rounding error

For an integer $r\geq1$, define \[ T_{N,r}(A)=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j \sum_{\substack{a_1<\cdots<a_j\in A\\a_1\cdots a_j\leq N}} \frac1{a_1\cdots a_j}, \] where the empty product contributes 1. For fixed $C,r$, uniformly over $\mu(A)\leq C$, \[ \sigma_N(A)=T_{N,r}(A) +O\left(\frac{C^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}\right) +O_{C,r}\left(\frac1{\log N}\right). \tag{5} \]

To prove this, the two successive Bonferroni sums sandwich the exact indicator of avoiding all divisors. Pairwise coprimality means that being divisible by $a_1,\ldots,a_j$ is equivalent to divisibility by their product. Averaging over $[1,N]$ gives the corresponding terms $N^{-1}\lfloor N/(a_1\cdots a_j)\rfloor$. The difference between consecutive truncations is at most the sum of reciprocal products of $r+1$ distinct moduli, at most $C^{r+1}/(r+1)!$.

It remains to bound the accumulated floor errors for $j\leq r$. Fix the first $j-1$ moduli and their product $D$; for $j=1$ take $D=1$. If a final modulus $a_j$ exists, then $X=N/D\geq N^{1/j}$, because all the earlier moduli are at most $a_j\leq X$. By $4$, the number of possible final moduli is \[ \ll \frac{X}{\log X}\leq \frac{jN}{D\log N}. \] Summing over the earlier moduli bounds the number of degree-$j$ floor errors by \[ \ll \frac{jN}{\log N}\frac{C^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}. \] Divide by $N$ and sum over $1\leq j\leq r$ to obtain $5$. This avoids an unjustified error proportional to $2^{|A|}/N$.

Separating small moduli from large primes

Suppose $A=A_1\sqcup A_2$, where $A_1\subseteq[2,z_-]$ and $A_2$ consists of primes greater than $z_+$, with $2<z_-<z_+$ and $z_-^r\leq N$. Then \[ T_{N,r}(A)=T_{N,r}(A_1)T_{N,r}(A_2) +O\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}\frac{C^j}{j!}\right) +O\left(e^C\frac{r\log z_-}{\log z_+}\right). \tag{6} \] Here and below constants may depend on the fixed bound $C$.

Indeed, terms in the product of truncations with total degree greater than $r$ give the first error. At total degree at most $r$, the only extra terms have total product greater than $N$, while the product of the small moduli and the product of the large primes separately are at most $N$. Such a term has a largest prime $p>z_+$. Fix its other factors; write their small-factor product as $D_s\leq z_-^r$ and their large-factor product as $D_\ell$. Then \[ \frac{N}{D_sD_\ell}<p\leq\frac N{D_\ell}. \] Intersecting with $p>z_+$ confines $p$ to an interval above $z_+$ whose endpoint ratio is at most $z_-^r$. The prime-counting bound, summed over dyadic subintervals, gives \[ \sum_{\text{such }p}\frac1p \ll \frac{r\log z_-}{\log z_+}. \] Summing reciprocal products of the other factors costs at most $\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}C^j/j!\leq e^C$. This proves $6$.

For the small set, the condition $z_-^r\leq N$ makes the product cutoff redundant. Expanding the finite Euler product therefore gives \[ T_{N,r}(A_1)=\prod_{a\in A_1}(1-1/a) +O\left(\sum_{j>r}\frac{C^j}{j!}\right). \tag{7} \]

Parameter choices and the uniform lower bound

Fix an error tolerance $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $r$ so large, depending only on $C,\varepsilon$, that the factorial tails above and $C/r+e^C/r$ are at most $O_C(\varepsilon)$. Choose $z_0>2$ so large that the right side of $3$ is at most $\varepsilon$. Discard those composite moduli, and define the fixed sequence \[ z_j=z_0^{\,r^{2j}}\qquad(0\leq j\leq r). \] One of the $r$ intervals $(z_{j-1},z_j]$ contains reciprocal mass at most $C/r$. Discard its moduli using $2$, and put $z_-=z_{j-1}$, $z_+=z_j$. What remains is $A_1\cup A_2$ as in $6$, with $\log z_+ =r^2\log z_-$. Every threshold here is fixed before $N\to\infty$; for sufficiently large $N$, all satisfy $z_-^r\leq N$.

Equations $2$, $5$--$7$, with $5$ also applied to $A_2$, yield \[ \sigma_N(A) =\left(\prod_{a\in A_1}(1-1/a)\right)\sigma_N(A_2) +O_C(\varepsilon)+o_{C,\varepsilon}(1). \tag{8} \] The prime-sieve input applies uniformly to $A_2$, so the right side is at least the product in $8$ times $\rho(e^{\mu(A_2)})$, up to the same error.

For $a\geq2$, $e^{1/a}<2$, and the Dickman equation on $[1,2]$ gives $\rho(e^{1/a})=1-1/a$. Repeated use of the Dickman inequality gives \[ \left(\prod_{a\in A_1}(1-1/a)\right)\rho(e^{\mu(A_2)}) \geq\rho(e^{\mu(A_1)+\mu(A_2)}) \geq\rho(e^C), \] because $\rho$ is decreasing. Thus $\sigma_N(A)\geq\rho(e^C)-O_C(\varepsilon)-o_{C,\varepsilon}(1)$, uniformly in the original $A$. Taking $N$ large and then $\varepsilon$ small proves the lower bound in $1$, and justifies taking a minimum over $A=A(N)$.

An exactly admissible prime tail

For large $N$, let $q_N$ be the largest prime $q\leq N$ for which $\sum_{q\leq p\leq N}1/p>C$, and take \[ P_N=\{p\text{ prime}:q_N<p\leq N\}. \] Equivalently, add primes in descending order and stop just before the budget would be exceeded. Then \[ \mu(P_N)\leq C<\mu(P_N)+1/q_N. \] The threshold $q_N\to\infty$, since a bounded threshold would leave a diverging reciprocal prime sum. Hence $\mu(P_N)=C+o(1)$. Mertens' estimate gives \[ \log\log N-\log\log q_N=C+o(1),\qquad q_N=N^{e^{-C}+o(1)}. \] An integer avoids $P_N$ exactly when all its prime factors are at most $q_N$. Thus $U_N(P_N)=\Psi(N,q_N)$, and the fixed-parameter smooth-number theorem gives \[ U_N(P_N)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \] For the slightly moving parameter $\log N/\log q_N\to e^C$, this follows by sandwiching between two fixed nearby parameters and using continuity of $\rho$. This proves the matching upper bound with the budget satisfied exactly.

A finite obstruction and the remaining question

At $N=30,C=1$, the terminal prime tail is \[ A_0=\{5,7,11,13,17,19,23,29\}. \] Its reciprocal sum is $754911236/1078282205<1$, but adjoining 3 would give $3343015913/3234846615>1$. It leaves exactly $\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,16,18,24,27\}$ uncovered. The alternative \[ A_1=\{3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23\} \] has reciprocal sum $111429982/111546435<1$ and leaves only $\{1,2,4,8,16,29\}$ uncovered. Thus the prime-tail rule is not an exact optimizer for every finite $N,C$. This is a finite exception, not a refutation of $1$ or of any eventual classification with fixed $C$. No exact classification of all finite minimizers is supplied.

5.3 Uniform lower bound.

Equivalently: for every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $N_0(C,\varepsilon)$ such that for all $N\ge N_0(C,\varepsilon)$ and all admissible $A$, \[ |U_A(N)|\ge (\rho(e^C)-\varepsilon)N. \] Taking the minimum over admissible $A$ gives \[ F_N(C)\ge \left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \]

5.4 Exact-budget prime-tail upper bound.

Let $q_N$ be the minimal prime for which \[ \sum_{q_N\le p\le N}\frac1p>C, \] and define \[ A_N:=\{p\ \mbox{prime}: q_N<p\le N\}. \] Then $A_N\subseteq\{2,\dots,N\}$, its elements are pairwise coprime, and by minimality of $q_N$, \[ \mu(A_N)=\sum_{q_N<p\le N}\frac1p\le C<\mu(A_N)+\frac1{q_N}. \] Thus $A_N$ is exactly admissible.

Since $\sum_{p\le N}1/p\to\infty$, we necessarily have $q_N\to\infty$, and therefore \[ 0\le C-\mu(A_N)<\frac1{q_N}=o(1). \] So \[ \mu(A_N)=C+o(1). \]

By Mertens' theorem for the prime harmonic series, \[ \sum_{q_N<p\le N}\frac1p = \log\log N-\log\log q_N+o(1), \] hence \[ \log\log N-\log\log q_N=C+o(1). \] Exponentiating, \[ \log q_N=e^{-C+o(1)}\log N, \] or equivalently \[ q_N=N^{e^{-C}+o(1)}. \] Define \[ u_N:=\frac{\log N}{\log q_N}. \] Then \[ u_N=e^C+o(1). \]

Now an integer $n\le N$ is not divisible by any prime in $A_N$ if and only if every prime factor of $n$ is at most $q_N$. Therefore \[ |U_{A_N}(N)|=\Psi(N,q_N), \] where \[ \Psi(x,y):=\#\{n\le x:\ P^+(n)\le y\} \] is the smooth-number counting function.

By the classical Dickman--de Bruijn asymptotic for smooth numbers, whenever \[ \frac{\log x}{\log y}\to u\in[1,\infty), \] one has \[ \Psi(x,y)=x\rho(u)+o(x). \] Applying this with $x=N$, $y=q_N$, and $u=u_N=e^C+o(1)$, and using continuity of $\rho$, we conclude that \[ |U_{A_N}(N)|=\Psi(N,q_N)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \] Since $A_N$ is admissible, it follows that \[ F_N(C)\le \left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \]

5.5 Conclusion.

Combining the lower and upper bounds gives \[ F_N(C)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \]

This completely proves the corrected asymptotic minimum-value theorem.

5.6 What is solved and what is not.

The argument above proves the minimum value \[ F_N(C)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N. \] It does not determine the exact extremizing set $A$ for each finite $N$, nor does it prove uniqueness or even asymptotic uniqueness of minimizers. Thus the original optimizer-classification problem remains open, but the minimal corrected asymptotic statement is now fully settled.

6. ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

I checked the new work against the most likely failure modes.

  1. Uniformity in $A$. This was the main logical gap in Round 2. It is now explicitly addressed: Tao's repaired note states that the $o(1)$ is taken with $N\to\infty$, holding $C$ fixed but allowing $A$ to vary. Therefore the lower bound may indeed be passed to the minimum $F_N(C)$.

  2. Exact budget versus $C+o(1)$. The new prime-tail competitor $A_N$ satisfies $\mu(A_N)\le C$ by construction. No trimming or Lipschitz estimate is needed.

  3. Does $q_N\to\infty$? Yes. If $q_N$ stayed bounded, then $\sum_{q_N<p\le N}1/p$ would diverge like $\log\log N$, contradicting $\mu(A_N)\le C$.

  4. Applicability of the smooth-number asymptotic. We only use the fixed-$u$ regime $u_N\to e^C$, which is exactly the classical Dickman regime.

  5. What exactly has been disproved? The false statement is the literal finite-$N$ optimizer prescription "take the consecutive largest primes." The proven corrected statement concerns only the asymptotic minimum value. No stronger optimizer-classification claim is asserted.

7. FINAL

FULL SOLUTION -- COUNTEREXAMPLE / DISPROOF.

The literal exact finite-$N$ prescription "take the consecutive largest primes" is false, by the explicit Round-2 counterexample at $(N,C)=(30,1)$.

The minimal corrected statement is the asymptotic minimum-value theorem \[ F_N(C)=\left(\rho(e^C)+o(1)\right)N, \] and this is now proved completely:

  1. the lower bound follows from Tao's repaired Theorem 1.1, using the crucial uniformity in $A$;
  2. the matching upper bound is attained by an exactly admissible prime tail $A_N=\{p: q_N<p\le N\}$, whose uncovered set is counted by $\Psi(N,q_N)$.

So path (C) is complete: the exact optimizer prescription is disproved, the exact optimizer problem remains open, and the asymptotic minimum-value problem has a full proof.
