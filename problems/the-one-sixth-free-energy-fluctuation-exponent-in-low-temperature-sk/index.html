The one-sixth free-energy fluctuation exponent in low-temperature SK

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. At each fixed zero-field Gaussian SK inverse temperature $\beta>1$, both standard deviation and typical centered fluctuations are claimed to have exponent $1/6$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For fixed $\beta>1$, let $g_{ij}$ be independent standard Gaussians and define \[ F_n=\log\left(2^{-n}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n} \exp\left(\frac\beta{\sqrt n}\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j\right)\right). \] The claim is \[ \operatorname{sd}(F_n)=n^{1/6+o(1)},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(F_n)=n^{1/3+o(1)}. \] More precisely, for every $\eta>0$ and sufficiently large $n$, $n^{1/6-\eta}\le\operatorname{sd}(F_n)\le n^{1/6+\eta}$, and \[ \mathbb P\{n^{1/6-\eta}\le|F_n-\mathbb EF_n|\le n^{1/6+\eta}\}\to1. \] Probability is over the zero-field Gaussian disorder.

Outline of the argument

Gaussian covariances are revealed along finite hierarchical paths, with pinned inverse-Hessian estimates controlling moments. Optimized local comparisons and signed replica attachments cancel the susceptibility correction; the resulting small-mass overlap profile gives a positive Laplace window at scale $n^{-1/6}$ and the matching fluctuation exponent.
