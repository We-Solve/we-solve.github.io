Kourovka Notebook Problem 14.41

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, define its lower central series by \[ \gamma_1(G)=G,\qquad \gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_i(G),G]\quad(i\geq 1), \] where $[H,G]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $h^{-1}g^{-1}hg$ with $h\in H$ and $g\in G$. Following the supplied definition, call $G$ para-free if there exists a free group $F$ such that \[ \gamma_i(G)/\gamma_{i+1}(G)\cong \gamma_i(F)/\gamma_{i+1}(F)\quad\text{for every }i\geq 1, \qquad \bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma_i(G)=\{1\}. \] No finite generation or restriction on the rank of $F$ is imposed. The displayed isomorphisms are isomorphisms of groups; the supplied definition does not require compatibility between them or isomorphisms of the corresponding nilpotent quotients.

The question of A. N. Zubkov is whether every such group $G$ is representable by matrices over a commutative associative ring with identity. Interpreting matrix representability in its usual faithful, finite-dimensional sense, the question is whether, for every para-free group $G$, there exist a positive integer $n$, a commutative associative ring $R$ with identity, and an injective group homomorphism \[ \rho:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(R), \] where $\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ denotes the group of invertible $n\times n$ matrices over $R$. Both $n$ and $R$ may depend on $G$. The source does not explicitly specify faithfulness or finite matrix size; these are the interpretive qualifications in this formulation.

Short English statement

Does every para-free group, as defined by its lower central factors and trivial intersection of its lower central series, admit a faithful representation by finite square matrices over some commutative associative ring with identity? The supplied record labels this question open; that status has not been independently verified.
