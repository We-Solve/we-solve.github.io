Math-12794

Sparse relative hypergraph regularity and its necessary hypotheses

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

T. Tao's source problem asks for a hypergraph regularity lemma for subsets of large relative density in pseudorandom sparse hypergraphs, motivated by a new proof of arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions in the primes and possible broader applications. The source gives a graph analogue: for $|A|=|B|=N$, a suitably "sparsely $c(\epsilon,\delta)$-quasirandom" host $G_0\subseteq A\times B$ and $G\subseteq G_0$ with $|G|>\delta|G_0|$, there are equitable partitions into $s,t<C(\epsilon,\delta)$ classes with at least $(1-\epsilon)st$ relatively $\epsilon$-regular pairs. The exact host and relative-regularity definitions are not supplied. The presenter suggests a correctly organized induction could be cleaner than existing hypergraph arguments; this is a methodological remark, not a theorem.

The attached aid reports that the arithmetic application is known under explicit linear-forms hypotheses, while the unrestricted host question remains underspecified. Its independent weak regularization can be stated on edge space as follows. Let $\mu$ be the normalized host probability measure, $0\le f\le1$ a relative edge-density function, $0<\epsilon<1$, and $\mathcal T$ a specified family of test sets. Call a positive-mass cell $C$ weakly regular if for every $T\in\mathcal T$ with $\mu(T\cap C)\ge\epsilon\mu(C)$, \[ \left|{\mathbb{E}}_\mu(f\mid T\cap C)-{\mathbb{E}}_\mu(f\mid C)\right|\le\epsilon. \] A normalized conditional-energy increment gives a partition of edge space into at most $2^{\lceil\epsilon^{-4}\rceil}$ cells whose irregular cells have total host mass at most $\epsilon$. This explicitly states a compatible weak test-set convention; the summary omits its exact norm definition. Such cells need not arise from equitable vertex partitions or a coherent hypergraph-complex hierarchy, and this assertion alone is not a sparse counting lemma.

The aid's counting obstruction is the $3$-partite host on ${\mathbb{F}}_q^3$ with normalized weight \[ \nu(x,y,z)=q\mathbf1_{x+y+z=0}. \] It has mean one and conditional expectation one on every proper coordinate subset. For a nontrivial additive character $\chi$, however, ${\mathbb{E}}\nu(x,y,z)\chi(x)\chi(y)\chi(z)=1$, while the dense product average is zero. For the nondegenerate two-edge cherry, \[ {\mathbb{E}}_{x,y,z,z'}\nu(x,y,z)\nu(x,y,z')\mathbf1_{z\ne z'}=0, \] whereas replacing $\nu$ by the dense weight $1$ gives $1-q^{-1}$. Perfect proper-coordinate marginals therefore do not by themselves supply the stronger counting control needed for the original sparse-transference program. The example does not refute results with adequate linear-forms hypotheses.

Short English statement

Seek sparse relative hypergraph regularity with enough pseudorandomness for arithmetic applications. The supplied aid separates a weak host-mass partition theorem from counting, and gives a sparse host whose perfect lower-coordinate marginals still fail a two-edge count.
