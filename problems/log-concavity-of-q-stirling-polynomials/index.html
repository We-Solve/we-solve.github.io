Log-Concavity of q-Stirling Polynomials

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a polynomial $P(q)=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_iq^i$ with real coefficients, extend its coefficient sequence by zeros beyond its degree. Following the supplied definition, call $P$ log-concave if \[ a_i^2\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}\qquad\text{for every integer }i\geq 1. \] Here log-concavity concerns the coefficients as the exponent of $q$ varies.

The primary question, attributed in the extract to Conjecture 3 and to reference [3, Conjecture 7.4], is the following. Writing the source's $q$-Stirling polynomials as \[ S_q[n,k]=\sum_{i\geq0}a_{n,k,i}q^i, \] is it true that, for every admissible pair $(n,k)$, \[ a_{n,k,i}^{\,2}\geq a_{n,k,i-1}a_{n,k,i+1} \qquad\text{for every integer }i\geq1? \] Unresolved specification. The supplied extract does not define this family $S_q[n,k]$, give its recurrence or initial conditions, or explicitly state its index domain. Thus the displayed question preserves the source's target but does not uniquely determine the polynomials or the quantification over $(n,k)$. No convention for these missing data is imposed here.

The extract reports computer checks for all $n,k\leq50$, without specifying the lower bounds or valid index pairs in that sentence. It also reports that, for $k\geq2$, asymptotic normality of the coefficients as $n\to\infty$ has been confirmed, citing [3, Theorem 7.7]. The probability distribution, normalization, and treatment of $k$ in that limit are not supplied, so this report is retained without a reconstructed theorem statement. The extract records attempts using Lorentzian polynomials [7], says that the theory of atlases [6] had not been tried, and associates a separate question about $q$-log-concavity with ideas in [10]. These remarks do not specify an additional precise conjecture.

The extract also gives a related type $B$ extension from set partitions to signed set partitions of type $B_n$: \[ S_q^B[n,k]=S_q^B[n-1,k-1]+[2k+1]_qS_q^B[n-1,k], \qquad S_q^B[0,k]= \begin{cases} 1,&k=0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \] The conventional interpretation of the bracket is $[m]_q=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}q^j$ for positive integers $m$; the extract does not explicitly define it. The recurrence's index domain and boundary values for negative second indices are also unspecified.

For $P(q)=\sum_{i\geq0}a_iq^i$, define its even and odd coefficient polynomials by \[ P_{\mathrm{even}}(q)=\sum_{j\geq0}a_{2j}q^j, \qquad P_{\mathrm{odd}}(q)=\sum_{j\geq0}a_{2j+1}q^j. \] Call $P$ parity log-concave when both of these polynomials are log-concave. The extract states that the type $B$ polynomials are not log-concave in general and gives the related Conjecture 4, attributed to [3, Conjecture 7.5]: every $S_q^B[n,k]$ is parity log-concave for its admissible indices. This auxiliary conjecture retains the unresolved domain and boundary qualifications above.

Short English statement

The main question asks whether the coefficients of every source-defined $q$-Stirling polynomial $S_q[n,k]$ form a log-concave sequence. The supplied record omits the definition and index domain of that family, so a fully specified conjecture requires further source clarification. The extract also records a type $B$ parity log-concavity conjecture, computational evidence, and a reported asymptotic normality theorem.
