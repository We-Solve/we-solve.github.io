Kirby Problem 1.32

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$ be a smooth link, and let $y$ denote the positive coordinate in ${\mathbb{R}}_+=(0,\infty)$. The source proposes a gauge-theoretic construction with boundary at $y=0$ and a special link singularity along $L\times\{0\}$. It poses three connected but distinct tasks.
  • (a) On the four-dimensional space ${\mathbb{R}}^3\times{\mathbb{R}}_+$, construct and analyze a moduli theory for solutions of the Kapustin--Witten (KW) equations with Nahm pole boundary conditions, modified along the link. Define appropriate graded solution counts that recover the coefficients of the Jones polynomial of $L$.
  • (b) On ${\mathbb{R}}_t\times{\mathbb{R}}^3\times{\mathbb{R}}_+$, construct the corresponding Haydys--Witten (HW) theory. The source's proposed complex has KW solutions as generators and a differential given by counting HW solutions with appropriate limits as $t\to\pm\infty$ and the link/Nahm pole boundary conditions. The task includes defining the counts and gradings, proving that the differential squares to zero, proving independence from auxiliary choices, and identifying its homology with the chosen version of Khovanov homology of $L$.
  • (c) Replace ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ by another $3$-manifold $Y$ and use KW or HW equations to define link invariants for $L\subset Y$. The source suggests KW counts might recover classes in the Kauffman bracket skein module or relate to physicists' $\widetilde Z$ invariants, while HW counts might give Khovanov-type homology. Comparisons with Khovanov lasagna skein modules are part of the proposed programme, not an established identification.
Khovanov homology is a combinatorially defined link homology whose graded Euler characteristic recovers the Jones polynomial with corresponding conventions. Its cobordism behavior motivates the gauge-theoretic interpretation. In the source, KW and HW refer to the equation systems introduced in [KW07], [Wit12], and [Hay15], and the relevant singular boundary conditions are referred to [Wit12], [MW14], and [MW20]. The complete PDE systems, structure group and bundle, perturbations, behavior at spatial and $y$ infinity, compactification, orientations, gradings, coefficient ring, and reduced/unreduced and Jones normalization choices are not given in this record. These choices are necessary for a single rigorous counting theorem; none is silently selected. Formal solution counts are requirements to construct, not assumed finite or already invariant.

The literature notes credit Witten and Gaiotto--Witten with proposing/sketching the Jones and Khovanov interpretations. They list analytic progress in [MW14], [MW20], [Tau13], [LT20], [He19], [HM19a], [Tau18], [Tau19]. They emphasize that some compactness results, including the cited [Tau18, Theorem B], require positive Ricci curvature of the $3$-manifold, suggesting this as an initial setting for (c), not a theorem for every $Y$. The notes list concrete combinatorial theories for links in $S^3$, interval bundles over surfaces, ${\mathbb{R}}\mathrm P^3$, and connected sums of $S^1\times S^2$, with references [APS04], [Man07], [Gab13], [Roz10], [Wil21]. Their scope is reported as source context rather than an independently updated exhaustive classification. The $\widetilde Z$ direction refers to source Problem 3.68 and [GPV17], [GPPV20]; [MWW22] supplies the lasagna comparison context.

The supplied triage checked 2026-08-17 labels the three-part programme open (OPEN-TRIAGE) and says no complete analytic construction matching all requested properties was verified. The registry is also open. The intended task is rigorous gauge-theoretic recovery, not merely naming a proposed physics interpretation.

Short English statement

Make the proposed Kapustin--Witten counts recover the Jones polynomial, and the Haydys--Witten complex recover Khovanov homology, with link-sensitive Nahm pole boundaries. Then extend the construction to links in other $3$-manifolds. The source reports substantial analytic groundwork but no complete general counting theory, and leaves several defining conventions unspecified.
