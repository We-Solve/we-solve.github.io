Math-4268

Covering Balls by Translated Slabs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ satisfy $d>2$. Equip ${\mathbb{R}}^d$ with its Euclidean inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and norm $\|\cdot\|_2$, and define \[ S^{d-1}=\{n\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\|n\|_2=1\},\qquad \overline B_d=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\|x\|_2\leq1\}. \] For $n\in S^{d-1}$, $\varepsilon>0$, and $a\in{\mathbb{R}}$, define the closed slab of width $\varepsilon$, normal direction $n$, and offset $a$ by \[ P(n,\varepsilon,a)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:a\leq\langle x,n\rangle\leq a+\varepsilon\}. \] Changing $a$ allows an arbitrary translate of the slab while preserving its normal direction and width.

The finite covering question described in the supplied record is whether, for every integer $d>2$, there exists a finite constant $C(d)>0$ such that, for every positive integer $m$, every choice of directions $n_1,\ldots,n_m\in S^{d-1}$, and every choice of widths $\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_m>0$, \[ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_i>C(d) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in{\mathbb{R}}^m\quad \overline B_d\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}P(n_i,\varepsilon_i,a_i). \] Thus $C(d)$ must depend only on the dimension; the offsets may depend on the entire given family. The displayed slab inequalities and the strict inequality on the total width are retained exactly.

Formulation qualifications. The source statements do not specify an index set, but the supplied literature assessment explicitly calls the target a finite slab-translate covering question; the formulation above follows that qualification. The source says "unit ball" without specifying whether its boundary is included. The display above explicitly uses the closed unit ball. If the intended convention is the open unit ball, replace $\overline B_d$ by $B_d^{\circ}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\|x\|_2<1\}$; the intended boundary convention requires confirmation.

Short English statement

For each integer dimension $d>2$, is there a threshold depending only on $d$ such that every finite family of slabs with prescribed normal directions and positive widths whose sum exceeds that threshold can be translated to cover the unit ball? The supplied curation labels this question open. The source does not specify whether the ball includes its boundary; the displayed formulation uses the closed ball.
