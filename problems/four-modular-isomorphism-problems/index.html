Four Modular Isomorphism Problems

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p$. For a finite group $G$, its group algebra is \[ kG=\left\{\sum_{g\in G}a_g g:a_g\in k\right\}, \] with coefficientwise addition and multiplication induced by the group operation and extended $k$-bilinearly. All group-algebra isomorphisms below mean isomorphisms of unital $k$-algebras; group isomorphisms are indicated separately. The prime subfield of $k$ is identified with ${\mathbb{F}}_p$.

For a class $\mathcal C$ of finite $p$-groups, define the recovery assertion \[ \begin{split} \mathcal M_p(\mathcal C):\quad &\text{for every field }k\text{ of characteristic }p,\\ &\text{every }G\in\mathcal C\text{ and every finite }p\text{-group }H,\\ &kG\cong_{k\text{-alg}}kH\ \Longrightarrow\ G\cong_{\mathrm{grp}}H. \end{split} \] This makes explicit a usual finite $p$-group formulation of the Modular Isomorphism Problem. The supplied excerpt does not give its preceding definition. Consequently, the field quantifier, the restriction to finite $p$-groups, and whether $H$ must also belong to the specified subclass require confirmation from the complete source context. The first two questions below are stated under this explicit, provisional interpretation.

Write $[G,G]$ for the subgroup generated by the commutators $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and $Z(G)$ for the center. A group has nilpotency class exactly $2$ when \[ 1\ne [G,G]\subseteq Z(G). \] Let $\mathcal P_p$ be the class of finite $p$-groups and let $\mathcal C_{p,2}\subseteq\mathcal P_p$ consist of those of nilpotency class exactly $2$.

  1. Determine whether $\mathcal M_p(\mathcal P_p)$ holds for every odd prime $p$. This expresses the odd-order question in the finite $p$-group setting. The literal phrase "groups of odd order" could instead include finite groups whose orders have several prime divisors; the supplied record does not specify that broader formulation.
  2. Determine whether $\mathcal M_p(\mathcal C_{p,2})$ holds for every prime $p$. The source says class $2$; whether it intends exactly $2$ or at most $2$ is not explicit.
  3. Determine whether there exist a prime $p$, finite $p$-groups $G,H$, and a field $k$ of characteristic $p$ such that \[ {\mathbb{F}}_pG\not\cong_{{\mathbb{F}}_p\text{-alg}}{\mathbb{F}}_pH, \qquad kG\cong_{k\text{-alg}}kH. \] There is no supplied restriction that $k$ be finite or algebraic over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$.
  4. Determine which group invariants are determined by the group algebra. More precisely, for a fixed field $k$ of characteristic $p$, an isomorphism-invariant function $I$ on finite $p$-groups is determined by $kG$ if \[ \forall G,H\in\mathcal P_p,\qquad kG\cong_{k\text{-alg}}kH\ \Longrightarrow\ I(G)=I(H). \] A group property is covered by taking $I$ to be its indicator function. The request is to identify such invariants and properties, including their dependence on $k$. The source supplies neither a prescribed family of invariants nor a precise completeness criterion for this classification request; its intended group domain also needs confirmation.

The original excerpt contains the self-referential sentence that the isomorphism type of $kG$ cannot be recovered from $kG$. Taken literally, that sentence does not express a coherent obstruction to recovery. The supplied corrected statement instead asks which properties of $G$ are determined by $kG$; item 4 follows that corrected statement while retaining the textual discrepancy for review.

Short English statement

The record asks whether modular group algebras recover odd-order groups and groups of nilpotency class $2$, whether extending the coefficient field can make two previously nonisomorphic group algebras isomorphic, and which group invariants the algebra determines. The finite $p$-group scope and quantifiers of the first two questions are provisional because their defining context is absent. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
