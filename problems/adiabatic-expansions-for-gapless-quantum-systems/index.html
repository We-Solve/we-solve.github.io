Testing imaginary-time moment integrability.
Consider a nonnegative spectral measure $d\nu(E)=E^\alpha\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(E)\,dE$, with $\alpha>-1$, and its correlation $C(t)=\int_0^1 e^{-tE}E^\alpha dE$, $t\geq0$. This is realized on $L^2((0,1),E^\alpha dE)$ by multiplication by $E$ and the vector $1$. The spectrum touches zero. For an integer $j\geq0$, nonnegativity permits exchanging integrals, and repeated integration by parts in the inner integral gives \[ \int_0^\infty t^j C(t)\,dt =j!\int_0^1 E^{\alpha-j-1}\,dE =\frac{j!}{\alpha-j}\quad\hbox{if }\alpha>j. \] For $\alpha\leq j$ it diverges; equality is a logarithmic divergence. Thus the zeroth time moment can exist while the first fails, for example $0<\alpha\leq1$.
Attempted extension and obstruction.
Any source expansion coefficient that requires this absolute $j$-th moment cannot be justified from decay sufficient only for lower moments. The example supplies a gapless correlation with an exactly computable order cutoff. It is not a no-go theorem for adiabatic evolution: cancellations, a different observable or a resummation may avoid that moment requirement.
