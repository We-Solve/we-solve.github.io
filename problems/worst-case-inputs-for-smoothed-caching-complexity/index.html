A specified perturbation can reverse a ranking

The source leaves both the small-perturbation distribution and the online caching algorithm unspecified. We choose an explicit limited experiment rather than assert a characterization under every smoothing law. The cache has capacity two and starts empty. The online algorithm is least-recently-used (LRU); the comparison is the offline minimum number of misses, also starting empty. Given a six-request sequence, with probability $1-p$ leave it unchanged and with probability $p$ swap one uniformly selected adjacent pair, with $0\le p\le1$. An adjacent pair of equal requests may be chosen, in which case the sequence is unchanged. The performance criterion is the expected ratio of misses, not the ratio of expected misses.
Proposition: For $A=(0,1,2,0,2,1)$ and $B=(0,2,0,1,1,2)$, both having two occurrences of each page, the respective expected ratios are \[ R_A(p)=\frac54+\frac{p}{15},\qquad R_B(p)=\frac43-\frac{p}{12}. \] Thus $A$ is better at $p=0$ but worse whenever $p>5/9$.
Proof: Here are the complete miss counts. Column 0 is the original sequence; column $j$ swaps positions $j$ and $j+1$, for $1\le j\le5$. \[ \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr} &0&1&2&3&4&5\\\hline \mathrm{LRU}(A)&5&4&6&4&5&6\\ \mathrm{OPT}(A)&4&4&4&3&4&4\\ \mathrm{LRU}(B)&4&4&3&5&4&4\\ \mathrm{OPT}(B)&3&3&3&4&3&3 \end{array} \] The LRU row follows by moving a requested resident page to the most-recent position, or evicting the least-recent page on a miss in a full cache. To verify every optimum count, let $F(s,C)$ be the minimum remaining misses on suffix $s$ with cache $C$. For its first request $a$, the exact recursion is \[ F((),C)=0,\quad F((a,s'),C)=\begin{cases} F(s',C),&a\in C,\\ 1+F(s',C\cup\{a\}),&a\notin C,\ |C|<2,\\ 1+\min_{v\in C}F(s',(C\setminus\{v\})\cup\{a\}),&a\notin C,\ |C|=2. \end{cases} \] This is valid by considering the first miss and every permitted victim, followed by an optimal suffix decision; induction on suffix length proves it. Applying it to the displayed twelve length-six inputs gives the table. Averaging the five ratios in columns 1 through 5 gives $79/60$ for $A$ and $5/4$ for $B$. Mixing with column 0 gives the stated formulas. Their difference is $-1/12+3p/20$, which changes sign at $5/9$. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

The exact recursion was executed with rational averaging, and all 90 distinct sequences with multiset $\{0,0,1,1,2,2\}$ were searched for such a reversal. The displayed pair is one verified outcome. This refutes a proposed shortcut of ranking these inputs by their unperturbed ratio alone, even after the smoothing law is fixed. Equal page histograms also fail to determine the smoothed ratio. It is not a characterization for arbitrary request lengths, cache sizes, online algorithms or perturbations, and it does not establish the source's asymptotic smoothed competitive guarantees. The chosen law is an explicit auxiliary model, not a reconstruction of an unspecified intended law.
