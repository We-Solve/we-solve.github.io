Closure-area limit sets and geometric phase on the shape sphere

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For the planar three-body problem, a shape is a configuration modulo translation, rotation and positive scaling. Its normalized shape space is a sphere, with collisions marked and collinear configurations on an equator. A syzygy is a collinear configuration; its symbol records which body is in the middle. For a trajectory, its $\omega$-limit set consists of accumulation points as forward time tends to its endpoint at infinity; its $\alpha$-limit set is defined backward in time. A limit cycle is a closed dynamical orbit. A shape curve is the image of a motion in the shape sphere; its closure has area measured with the source’s sphere normalization, whose total area is $\pi$.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the three-body shape space: determine whether a solution can have an $\alpha$- or $\omega$-limit set that is a limit cycle. Prove or refute that for every $0<A_0\le\pi$ there is a solution whose shape-curve closure has area $A_0$. Determine whether two closed shape curves in the same homotopy class can be joined through closed shape curves of solutions; the source conjectures no, and asks for the connected components.
Scope and source qualification.
The last question concerns realizable dynamical shape curves, not arbitrary loops for which homotopy already provides a deformation. The source leaves the topology and deformation class to be specified. The damaged area notation A0 is repaired. Smooth image versus closure area.
A smooth curve on a two-dimensional sphere has area zero on every compact time interval: in a coordinate chart its Lipschitz image can be covered by $O(1/\delta)$ squares of side $O(\delta)$, giving total area $O(\delta)$. A countable union over bounded intervals still has zero area. This does not imply that its closure has zero area, which is the actual source target.
An explicit kinematic dense-band example.
On a sphere of radius $1/2$, whose total area is $\pi$, use coordinates $\theta\pmod{2\pi}$ and normalized height $z\in[-1,1]$. The curve $\theta(t)=t\pmod{2\pi}$, $z(t)=a\sin(\sqrt2t)$, $0<a<1$, is smooth and dense in the band $|z|\le a$. To verify density, times $t=\theta_0+2\pi n+o(1)$ reduce the second phase to an irrational circle rotation; its orbit is dense, as follows by the pigeonhole approximation of a nonzero arbitrarily small rotation step and its multiples. The area element is $\tfrac14d\theta\,dz$, so the closure has area $\pi a$. These curves are kinematic examples, not three-body shape trajectories.
Gap.
Thus no purely dimension-of-a-curve argument refutes the requested positive closure areas. Conversely the ability to draw a dense curve gives no Newtonian realization. One must construct a physical invariant set or orbit whose projected closure has the specified area, while controlling collisions and conserved quantities. Shape-space limit cycles and connected components of closed realizable shape curves are also unproved; homotopy through arbitrary curves does not supply a deformation through solutions. The first missing step is a dynamical realization with a specified topology and reconstruction condition.
