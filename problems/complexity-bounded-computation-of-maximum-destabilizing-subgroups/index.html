Complexity-Bounded Computation of Maximum Destabilizing Subgroups

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Let $G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb C)$ be reductive and closed under conjugate transpose, with a compatible rational representation $\pi$ on a Hermitian space $V$ and $v\ne0$. Set $K=G\cap U(n)$ and $M=G\cap\{x=x^\dagger>0\}\cong K\backslash G$, with metric $\langle U,W\rangle_x=\operatorname{tr}(x^{-1}Ux^{-1}W)$. The Kempf-Ness objective is $f_v(x)=\log\langle\pi(x)v,v\rangle$. Instability means $0\in\overline{\pi(G)v}$. For a unit tangent $H$ at $I$, define $f_v^\infty(H)=\lim_{t\to\infty}(f_v(e^{tH})-f_v(I))/t$. Maximum destabilization minimizes this slope; the associated real one-parameter flow is $e^{tH}$, destabilizing when $\pi(e^{tH})v\to0$. Gradient flow uses $\dot x=-\nabla f_v(x)$ in this metric, with its direction limit taken at infinity.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Construct an algorithm that computes the maximum destabilizing one-parameter subgroup represented by the limiting direction of the relevant gradient flow, and prove explicit complexity bounds for the algorithm. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The primary convergence theorem identifies the limit but does not give the requested algorithmic bound. Representation encoding, arithmetic versus bit cost, and exact versus approximate output must be specified; this is the source's real one-parameter-flow setting.

Short English statement

Compute the maximum destabilizing direction associated with the cited gradient flow and prove quantitative complexity bounds.
