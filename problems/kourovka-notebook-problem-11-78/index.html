Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.78

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an algebraic group $G$ defined over a field $k$, write $G(k)$ for its abstract group of $k$-rational points. A field $k$ is called algebraic if every element of $k$ is algebraic over its prime subfield. An algebraic group is directly undecomposable, in the usual algebraic-group sense, if it is not isomorphic to a direct product of two nontrivial algebraic groups. A trivial centre means that the centre consists only of the identity. The source does not specify the category in which direct decomposability and the centre are to be interpreted; this qualification remains unresolved.

The source calls an abstract group isomorphism between groups of points semialgebraic if it is a composition of an "isomorphism of translation of the field of definition" and a rational morphism. The natural interpretation of the field-translation step is transport by a field isomorphism $\sigma:k\to\ell$. Writing ${}^{\sigma}G$ for the algebraic group obtained by transporting the defining data of $G$ along $\sigma$, this interpretation gives a factorization \[ G(k)\xrightarrow{\sigma_*}({}^{\sigma}G)(\ell)\xrightarrow{f_*}H(\ell), \qquad \varphi=f_*\circ\sigma_*, \] where $f$ is the rational morphism intended by the source and $f_*$ must be defined on all points needed for this factorization. The source does not explain the quoted field-translation phrase or specify whether its rational morphism is a rational map, an everywhere-defined morphism, or an isomorphism of algebraic groups. This factorization is therefore an interpretation of the supplied definition requiring review, rather than a resolution of those distinctions.

  1. (a) Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, and let $G$ and $H$ be directly undecomposable algebraic groups over $k$, both with trivial centres. Does the existence of an abstract group isomorphism $G(k)\cong H(k)$ imply the existence of a semialgebraic isomorphism between these groups of points? Equivalently, with the preceding qualifications, \[ \operatorname{Iso}_{\mathrm{grp}}(G(k),H(k))\ne\varnothing \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists\psi\in\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathrm{grp}}(G(k),H(k)) \text{ such that }\psi\text{ is semialgebraic}. \] The assertion concerns the existence of a semialgebraic isomorphism; it does not require every given isomorphism to be semialgebraic.
  2. (b) Is every abstract group isomorphism between the groups of points of directly undecomposable algebraic groups with trivial centres, defined over algebraic fields, semialgebraic? If separate fields of definition are allowed, the quantified question is whether, for every pair of algebraic fields $k,\ell$, every such $G/k$ and $H/\ell$, and every \[ \varphi\in\operatorname{Iso}_{\mathrm{grp}}(G(k),H(\ell)), \] $\varphi$ is semialgebraic. If a common field of definition is intended, the question instead restricts this formulation to $k=\ell$. The supplied wording does not explicitly distinguish these two scopes. Algebraic fields in this part are not assumed to be algebraically closed.

Short English statement

Part (a) asks whether abstractly isomorphic point groups of directly undecomposable, centreless algebraic groups over an algebraically closed field always admit some semialgebraic isomorphism. Part (b) asks whether every such abstract isomorphism over algebraic fields is semialgebraic. The precise meaning of the field-translation step, the rational morphism, and the field relationship in part (b) requires clarification.
