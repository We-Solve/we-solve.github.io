Open Problems on Central Simple Algebras — Problem 14

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a finitely generated field extension $K/F$, call $K/F$ rational if $K\cong_F F(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ for some integer $r\geq 0$, where the $t_i$ are algebraically independent over $F$. Call it stably rational if there exist integers $r,s\geq 0$ and algebraically independent indeterminates such that \[ K(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\cong_F F(v_1,\ldots,v_s). \] Call it retract rational if there exist a finitely generated integral $F$-subalgebra $R\subseteq K$ with $\operatorname{Frac}(R)=K$, an integer $s\geq 0$, a nonzero polynomial $f\in F[t_1,\ldots,t_s]$, and unital $F$-algebra homomorphisms \[ R\xrightarrow{\alpha}F[t_1,\ldots,t_s][1/f]\xrightarrow{\beta}R, \qquad \beta\circ\alpha=\operatorname{id}_R. \] Two extensions $K/F$ and $L/F$ are stably $F$-isomorphic if $K(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\cong_F L(v_1,\ldots,v_s)$ for some finite sets of algebraically independent indeterminates.

The supplied source asks whether each of the extensions \[ Z(F,n)/F,\qquad Z_m(F,n)/F,\qquad Z_H(F,n)/F \] is rational, stably rational, or retract rational, with the three properties considered separately. However, the supplied record does not define these fields, specify the hypotheses on $F$, or give the admissible values and meaning of the parameters $n,m,H$. Thus this is a conditional statement of the intended rationality questions: the target extensions and their parameter ranges must be recovered from the cited source before a fully specified quantified problem can be stated. The definitions above do not supply those missing constructions or assert that the displayed extensions satisfy their prerequisites.

As background, the source reports a result attributed to Katsylo and Schofield: for admissible indices satisfying $n=ab$ and $\gcd(a,b)=1$, the field $Z(F,n)$ is stably isomorphic to the compositum $Z(F,a)Z(F,b)$. The supplied record does not specify the embeddings or ambient field used for this compositum, nor the full hypotheses of that result. It also reports that similar statements are possible for $Z_m(F,n)$ and $Z_H(F,n)$, without stating them precisely. The source qualifies the resulting reduction to prime-power indices as applying often, but not always.
