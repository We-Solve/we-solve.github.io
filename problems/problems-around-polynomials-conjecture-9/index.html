Problems Around Polynomials — Conjecture 9

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Let $n\geq1$ and $f(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jz^j$ with all $a_j>0$, and put $a_{-1}=a_{n+1}=0$. For an increasing index list $0=k_1<\cdots<k_m=n$, define its parity-change count by \[v=\left|\{j:1\leq j<m,\ k_j\not\equiv k_{j+1}\pmod2\}\right|.\] Real roots are counted with multiplicity.

Put $\widetilde c_j=(j+1)a_j^2-ja_{j-1}a_{j+1}$, and let $k_1,\ldots,k_m$ be exactly the indices for which $\widetilde c_j>0$, in increasing order. The endpoint indices belong to the list since $\widetilde c_0>0$ and $\widetilde c_n>0$. Write $v_9(f)$ for its parity-change count.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Is the number of real zeros of $f$ at most $v_9(f)$ for every such $f$? [S2]

Short English statement

Does the parity-change count among indices with positive shifted coefficient differences bound the number of real roots?
