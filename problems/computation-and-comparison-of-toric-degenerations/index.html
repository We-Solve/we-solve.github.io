Computation and Comparison of Toric Degenerations

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a projective variety over a field $k$. The supplied statement does not specify the base field or the permitted class of varieties beyond projectivity.

For terminology only, under the normal split convention, a toric variety over $k$ is a normal integral variety $Y$ containing a dense open torus $T\cong(\mathbb G_{m,k})^r$, whose multiplication action on itself extends to an action on $Y$. One common model of a toric degeneration of $X$ is a flat projective morphism \[ \pi\colon\mathcal X\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_k \] whose fiber over $0$ is a toric variety and for which there is a nonempty open subset $U\subseteq\mathbb A^1_k\setminus\{0\}$ such that every geometric fiber over $U$ is isomorphic to the corresponding base change of $X$. This is an illustrative convention: the supplied record does not determine the parameter space, normality or splitness requirements, or the precise meaning of the general fiber being $X$.

Research problem. For a projective variety $X$, develop general methods to compute toric degenerations of $X$, and characterize the relationships among distinct toric degenerations of the same $X$.

The source presents two methodological questions rather than a quantified conjecture. It does not specify an effective presentation of $X$, the required output of a computation, an equivalence relation on degenerations, or which relationships are to be characterized. Consequently, the requested methods and comparison problem require further specification before they become a single precise algorithmic or classification statement. The original excerpt refers more broadly to an algebraic variety; projectivity is retained from the supplied reconstructed statement.

Short English statement

The problem asks how to construct toric degenerations of a projective variety and how to compare different degenerations of that variety. The base field, admissible families, toric conventions, and intended comparison relation remain unspecified. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
