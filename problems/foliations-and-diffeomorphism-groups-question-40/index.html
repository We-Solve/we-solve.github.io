Foliations and Diffeomorphism Groups — Question 40

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^1={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$, and write $G=\mathrm{Diff}(S^1)$ for the diffeomorphism group intended in the source. The source does not specify the differentiability class or whether only orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms are allowed; these conventions remain unresolved. Let $G^\delta$ denote this group with the discrete topology, and let $BG^\delta$ be its classifying space.

For an explicit integral interpretation, use the bar chain complex $C_*(G;{\mathbb{Z}})$ with trivial coefficients. Its degree-two generators are written $[u\mid v]$, and \[ \partial [u\mid v]=[v]-[uv]+[u]. \] Thus, for $f,g\in G$ satisfying $fg=gf$, the chain \[ z(f,g)=[f\mid g]-[g\mid f] \] is a cycle. It is a boundary precisely when there exists $b\in C_3(G;{\mathbb{Z}})$ such that \[ \partial b=z(f,g), \] or equivalently when \[ [z(f,g)]=0\quad\text{in }H_2(BG^\delta;{\mathbb{Z}}). \] Question (source Question 40). For commuting circle diffeomorphisms $f$ and $g$, to what extent can one establish that $z(f,g)$ is a boundary? Determine conditions on the commuting pair under which this vanishing holds.

The integral bar-complex formulation above makes one conventional interpretation explicit; the source does not specify homology coefficients. Its wording also leaves open whether the intended goal is universal vanishing, a characterization of the pairs giving boundaries, or sufficient conditions. These qualifications must be resolved before assigning a more specific conjecture.

Short English statement

For two commuting circle diffeomorphisms, the ordered pairs $(f,g)$ and $(g,f)$ determine a difference that is a two-cycle in the classifying space of the discrete diffeomorphism group. The question asks when this cycle bounds. The regularity, orientation convention, coefficients, and intended scope of the answer are not specified in the supplied source.
