Math-1264

Two Tate lines and the missing adjoint correspondence

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

K. Murty's AIM question starts with an automorphic form $\pi$ over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, a real quadratic field $F$, and its base change $\Pi$. It describes cohomological summands $H^1(\pi)\subset H^1(M)$ for a modular curve and $H^2(\Pi)\subset H^2(S_F)$ for a Hilbert modular surface, with \[ H^2(\Pi)|_{G_F}\cong H^1(\pi)\otimes H^1(\pi). \] For two such fields $F_1,F_2$, put $L=F_1F_2$. The resulting summand of $H^4(S_{F_1}\times S_{F_2})$ restricts to $V^{\otimes4}$, $V=H^1(\pi)$, as a $G_L=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/L)$-module. The question asks for the expected codimension-two algebraic cycles defined over $L$. Precise cohomology, coefficient, compactification, automorphic weight, and character conventions are not specified; actual Tate classes belong to $H^4(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell(2))^{G_L}$, not the untwisted full tensor space.

The attached report imposes an explicit big-monodromy hypothesis: over a characteristic-zero coefficient field, $V$ is two-dimensional and the Zariski closure of the $G_L$-image contains $SL(V)$. Put $D=\det V$. It asserts \[ \dim\left(V^{\otimes4}\otimes D^{-2}\right)^{G_L}=2. \] With alternating-pair tensors $t_{ij\mid kl}$ in the indicated tensor positions and determinant normalization, its stated basis is \[ p=t_{12\mid34},\qquad c=t_{13\mid24}+t_{14\mid23}. \] The first line is the external product of the two determinant/divisor lines. The second is the unique non-product Casimir line in $\operatorname{Ad}^0(V)\otimes\operatorname{Ad}^0(V)$, where $\operatorname{Ad}^0(V)$ is the trace-free endomorphism representation, and corresponds via its invariant pairing to the identity on $\operatorname{Ad}^0(V)$. Tensor sign and normalization conventions are not fully supplied.

After restoring the actual codimension-two Tate twist, the report gives the global characters of these two lines as \[ \epsilon^2\chi_1\chi_2\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon^2, \] respectively. Here $\chi_i$ is the quadratic character associated to $F_i/{\mathbb{Q}}$; the precise convention for $\epsilon$ must be fixed through the determinant of the automorphic representation. In compatible notation it is the finite character left by $D(1)$. Since $\chi_i|_{G_L}=1$, the actual classes over $L$ require the corresponding restriction of $\epsilon^2$ to be trivial. Two determinant-normalized invariant lines do not by themselves ensure two untwisted or incorrectly twisted Tate classes. The source does not state the needed central-character restriction.

The report reduces the non-product geometric target to constructing an algebraic adjoint identity correspondence between the two surface summands. It explicitly does not construct that codimension-two cycle for distinct $F_1,F_2$. Thus the two-line computation is a representation-theoretic reduction, not a proof of algebraicity of the desired non-product class.

Short English statement

Find codimension-two cycles linking Hilbert modular surfaces from two quadratic base changes. Under big monodromy the report isolates two determinant-normalized lines and their descent characters, leaving the non-product adjoint correspondence unconstructed.
