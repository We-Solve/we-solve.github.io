Complexity of Counting versus Recovering Planted Communities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not determine a complete mathematical problem. Its principal question concerns the computational complexity of inferring community statistics compared with recovering communities, but it does not specify the random graph model, parameter regimes, success criteria, or recovery criterion. The following notation describes that question conditionally, without supplying the missing hypotheses.

Let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. If the latent communities form a partition $\Pi_n=\{C_1,\ldots,C_{k_n}\}$ of $[n]$ into nonempty blocks, define the number of communities and their unordered size multiset by \[ K(\Pi_n)=k_n,\qquad M(\Pi_n)=\{\!\{|C_1|,\ldots,|C_{k_n}|\}\!\}. \] Given an observed random graph $X_n$ generated using such a latent structure, the question is whether estimating $K(\Pi_n)$, $M(\Pi_n)$, or other specified community statistics can be achieved by a polynomial-time algorithm in a regime where recovering the communities is believed to be computationally hard, as $n\to\infty$. A precise formulation requires a specified family of joint laws for $(X_n,\Pi_n)$, the permitted parameter sequences, the information available to an algorithm, an estimation accuracy and success probability, a recovery criterion, and a computational hardness assumption or restricted computational model. None of these choices is determined by the supplied record; even the partition representation above is only a conditional interpretation.

The supplied abstract reports a negative answer for certain hypothesis tests between models with different planted community structures, within the low-degree polynomial framework. This reported result must not be interpreted as a lower bound for every polynomial-time algorithm or every community model. The particular planted distributions, allowed polynomial degrees, parameter ranges, and comparison with recovery are absent. Consequently, the reported result does not supply a precise theorem or a precise remaining open problem here.

The record also contains a separate graph-alignment discussion. For each unordered pair $\{i,j\}\subseteq[n]$, it samples an edge-indicator pair $(A_{ij},B_{ij})$ with Bernoulli marginals of mean $p=\lambda/n$, independently across distinct unordered vertex pairs, and with a stated correlation parameter $s$. These indicators define simple undirected graphs $G$ and $H$. Necessarily $0\leq\lambda/n\leq1$. For a random permutation $\pi$ of $[n]$, let $G'=\pi(H)$, so that \[ E(G')=\left\{\{\pi(i),\pi(j)\}:\{i,j\}\in E(H)\right\}. \] The alignment task is to infer the hidden relabelling from $(G,G')$. The source discusses information-theoretically impossible, polynomial-time achievable, and putatively exponential-time regimes as $n\to\infty$, depending on $\lambda$ and $s$, and mentions Otter's constant, associated with the growth of the number of unlabelled trees, as a correlation threshold. It supplies no threshold formula, convention for that constant, recovery accuracy, permutation distribution or independence assumption, or precise definition and admissible range of $s$. Thus this discussion cannot be promoted to a precise threshold assertion. No connection between this alignment model and the community-counting question is supplied.

The registry marks the statement as unrecoverable and supplies a partially_solved status. These are source labels, not independently verified mathematical conclusions.

Short English statement

The intended question asks whether community counts or sizes can be inferred efficiently when finding the communities is computationally hard. The source reports limitations for certain low-degree polynomial tests, but omits the models and success criteria needed for a precise statement. It also includes a separate, insufficiently specified graph-alignment discussion. This draft requires human mathematical and source review.
