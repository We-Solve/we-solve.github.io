Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Zilber–Pink conjecture that if $X$ is a mixed Shimura variety or semiabelian variety defined over $\mathbb{C}$, and $V \subseteq X$ is a subvariety, then $V$ contains only finitely many maximal atypical subvarieties.

Zilber–Pink conjecture

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 016; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that non-CM elliptic-square points on Hodge-generic curves in $\mathcal A_2$ are finite, and combining the three endomorphism branches gives the full curve-level Zilber--Pink assertion there; In manuscript 2 (family 016; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that a Hodge-generic algebraic curve in $\mathcal A_2$ contains only finitely many points whose full rational endomorphism algebra is quaternion division, with no boundary condition; In manuscript 3 (family 016; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that subvarieties of abelian varieties over algebraic numbers have only finitely many maximal atypical intersections, measured inside their smallest containing torsion coset; In manuscript 4 (family 016; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that a Hodge-generic curve in $\mathcal A_2$ over algebraic numbers meets the locus of surfaces isogenous to an elliptic product with a CM factor only finitely often.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $\mathcal A_2$ be the moduli space of principally polarized abelian surfaces over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, and let $C$ be any nonempty reduced irreducible closed Hodge-generic algebraic curve therein. The complete asserted conclusion is \[ \#\left(C(\overline{\mathbb Q})\cap \bigcup_{\substack{Z\subset\mathcal A_2\ \mathrm{special}\\\dim Z\le1}} Z(\overline{\mathbb Q})\right)<\infty. \] In particular the points whose surface is isogenous to an elliptic square are finite. Two additional assertions give finiteness for surfaces isogenous to $E_1\times E_2$ with a CM factor, and for surfaces having a full rational endomorphism algebra that is quaternionic, a division algebra and indefinite. There are no bounds on CM fields, isogeny degrees, endomorphism orders or polarizations, and no height, boundary or integrality restrictions.
Manuscript 2.
Let $C$ be a closed algebraic curve, reduced, irreducible and Hodge generic, in the Siegel threefold $\mathcal A_2$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, the asserted finiteness is \[ \#\{s\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q}): \operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(A_s)\otimes\mathbb Q \text{ is an indefinite quaternion division algebra over }\mathbb Q\}<\infty. \] The full endomorphism algebra, not merely a contained quaternion order, is specified; the quaternion algebra and order may vary.
Manuscript 3.
For a number field $K$, abelian variety $A/K$, and irreducible $X\subseteq A_{\bar K}$, let $S$ be the smallest special subvariety containing $X$; special subvarieties are torsion translates of abelian subvarieties. An irreducible component $Y$ of $X\cap Z$, $Z\subseteq S$ special, is atypical when \[ \dim Y>\dim X+\dim Z-\dim S. \] The claim is finiteness of maximal atypical subvarieties of $X$, equivalently existence of finitely many proper atypical $Y_j\subsetneq X$ containing every atypical subvariety. No dimension bound on $A$ or $X$ is imposed.
Manuscript 4.
Take a closed curve $C$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, with its reduced structure and assumed irreducible, in $\mathcal A_2$, the moduli of principally polarized abelian surfaces. Assume Hodge genericity: $C$ lies in no proper special subvariety. The claimed finite set is \[ \{s\in C(\overline{\mathbb Q}):A_s\text{ is isogenous to }E_1\times E_2, \text{ with at least one }E_i\text{ having CM}\}. \] Both the CM fields and the isogeny degrees may vary; the theorem does not restrict to a fixed product locus.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 016; 2026-09-24), two auxiliary markings control bad-center proximity in a five-dimensional orthogonal variation. Common-parameter interpolation gives arithmetic height bounds, and orbit counting handles elliptic squares; classification of special curves then joins this branch to the CM-product and quaternionic branches. For manuscript 2 (family 016; 2026-09-24), a rank-five variation detects the endomorphism locus. Two-argument interpolation and an ordinary-point zero estimate produce a height estimate unaffected by how many places exhibit supersingular closeness; Galois-orbit counting then gives finiteness.

For manuscript 3 (family 016; 2026-09-24), a counterexample sequence is decomposed into distinct height scales. Uniform tangent positivity and weighted small sections give a height inequality despite growing homomorphism coefficients and point degrees; passing to suitable quotients rules out the remaining scale configurations. For manuscript 4 (family 016; 2026-09-24), canonical product isogenies and degeneration comparisons bound heights along the curve. Period estimates produce a Galois-orbit lower bound, which conflicts with the definable counting bound unless only finitely many such points occur.
