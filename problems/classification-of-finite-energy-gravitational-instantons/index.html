Math-3379

Classification of Finite-Energy Gravitational Instantons

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a complete four-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Write $\operatorname{Rm}_g$ for its Riemann curvature tensor and $\mathrm{dvol}_g$ for its Riemannian volume measure. The finite curvature energy condition is \[ \operatorname{Rm}_g\in L^2(M,g) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \int_M |\operatorname{Rm}_g|_g^2\,\mathrm{dvol}_g<\infty. \] Consider the class \[ \mathcal C=\left\{(M,g):\begin{array}{l} \dim M=4,\quad g\text{ is complete},\\ (M,g)\text{ is a gravitational instanton in the source's sense},\\ \displaystyle\int_M|\operatorname{Rm}_g|_g^2\,\mathrm{dvol}_g<\infty,\\ (M,g)\text{ is neither ALE nor ALF} \end{array}\right\}. \] Here ALE and ALF denote asymptotically locally Euclidean and asymptotically locally flat, respectively. The supplied record does not specify the geometric models, decay estimates, or end conditions defining these predicates. It also does not specify the convention for gravitational instantons, including whether hyperkähler structure is required or a broader Ricci-flat class is intended. Consequently, $\mathcal C$ is a conditional description of the intended class, pending these conventions.

Classification problem. Classify all members of $\mathcal C$, with the equivalence relation for the classification to be specified. Determine whether members can have slow curvature decay. More specifically, the original formulation asks whether the geometries described as newly described in the source exhaust the members with slow curvature decay. Determine also whether such metrics can occur as singularity dilations.

Unresolved qualifications. The supplied record neither identifies those newly described geometries nor defines slow curvature decay. It does not specify the ambient geometric problem, singularities, rescaling procedure, or convergence notion meant by singularity dilations. The referent of such metrics in the last question could be all members of $\mathcal C$, its slow-decay members, or the newly described examples. These missing specifications prevent a fully quantified classification, exhaustiveness, or singularity-limit statement; no choice among them is made here.

Short English statement

Classify complete four-dimensional gravitational instantons of finite total squared curvature that are neither ALE nor ALF. The source also asks about slow curvature decay, whether its unnamed new geometries exhaust that behavior, and realization as singularity dilations. Definitions, the candidate geometries, and the intended singularity setting require clarification before these questions become precise.
