A phase-balanced start on a solvable bipartite family

The source asks about well-chosen random initialization for Glauber dynamics on low-temperature lattice models. We test the phase-selection idea on $K_{m,m}$, with integer $m\ge1$ and fixed hard-core activity $\lambda\ge1$. This family is not the source's fixed-dimensional torus: its degree grows with $m$. Put $a=(1+\lambda)^m$. Every nonempty independent set lies entirely in one side, so its partition function is $Z=2a-1$. Choose a side with probability $1/2$, then include each vertex on that side independently with probability $\lambda/(1+\lambda)$. Call the resulting distribution $\mu$, and write $\pi(I)=\lambda^{|I|}/Z$ for stationarity.
Proposition: The initialization satisfies \[ \|\mu-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\frac{a-1}{a(2a-1)}\le a^{-1}. \] In contrast, single-site heat-bath Glauber dynamics started from $\pi$ conditioned on a nonempty independent set on the left remains at total-variation distance at least $1/2-t\Phi$ after $t$ updates, where \[ \Phi=\frac{\lambda}{2(1+\lambda)(a-1)}. \]
Proof: The initialization assigns mass $1/a$ to the empty set and mass $\lambda^{|I|}/(2a)$ to each nonempty independent set. Since $2a-1<2a$, the latter probabilities are all below their stationary probabilities. Thus total variation is exactly the excess at the empty set, $1/a-1/(2a-1)$, giving the formula. For the chain, choose one of $2m$ vertices uniformly. If its neighbors are absent, set it occupied with probability $\lambda/(1+\lambda)$; otherwise set it absent. Two configurations differing by the addition of one permitted vertex have stationary weights in ratio $\lambda$ and transition probabilities in the same forward/backward ratio, proving detailed balance; self transitions need no condition. Hence $\pi$ is stationary. Let $A$ be the nonempty left-side independent sets. Its mass is $(a-1)/Z<1/2$. A transition from $A$ to its complement can occur only from one of the $m$ singleton sets, by selecting that occupied vertex and removing it. Each singleton has mass $\lambda/Z$, and this transition has probability $1/[2m(1+\lambda)]$. The stationary flow out of $A$ is therefore $Q=\lambda/[2(1+\lambda)Z]$, and $Q/\pi(A)=\Phi$. The initial law $\nu_0=\pi(\cdot\mid A)$ is pointwise at most $\pi/\pi(A)$. If this domination holds at time $j$, applying the transition matrix and stationarity shows it holds at time $j+1$. Thus the probability of an $A$-to-complement transition at any fixed step is at most $Q/\pi(A)=\Phi$. Starting inside $A$, a union bound gives $\nu_t(A)\ge1-t\Phi$. By the event characterization of total variation, $\|\nu_t-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge\nu_t(A)-\pi(A)\ge1/2-t\Phi$. ∎
The one-phase start can itself be generated by independent Bernoulli choices conditioned on nonemptiness; since $a\ge2$, rejection has expected at most two trials. The lower bound remains at least $1/4$ for integer $t\le1/(4\Phi)$, exponential in $m$ at fixed activity. Meanwhile the balanced initialization already has exponentially small error before any updates; applying a Markov kernel cannot increase total variation, by the triangle inequality on its nonnegative transition entries.

Verification and remaining gaps

For $m=1,\ldots,6$ and $\lambda=1,2$, all independent sets were enumerated and the stationary weights, balanced-start error, detailed balance and phase flow checked exactly. This proves that phase weights matter on this restricted family. It does not show that a balanced start works on a torus, where phases have internal correlations and their weights must be controlled. Nor does it establish a general counting reduction from such a start. The exact formula $Z=2a-1$ already makes counting easy here, and no low-temperature lattice algorithm is claimed.
