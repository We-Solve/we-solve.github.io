Math-4183

Sublinear Generator Growth of Higher-Rank Lattices

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a higher-rank simple Lie group, as in the supplied source. Fix a Haar measure on $G$. A lattice $M\leq G$ is a discrete subgroup for which the quotient $G/M$ has finite invariant volume; write its covolume as $\operatorname{vol}(G/M)$. For a finitely generated group $M$, let \[ d(M)=\min\{\lvert S\rvert:S\subseteq M,\ \langle S\rangle=M\} \] be its minimal number of generators. The displayed question presupposes that these generator numbers are finite.

The supplied question asks whether, for every such $G$ and every sequence of lattices $(M_i)_{i\geq1}$ in $G$ with unbounded covolumes, one has \[ \lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(M_i)-1}{\operatorname{vol}(G/M_i)}=0. \] No torsion-free hypothesis is imposed in this question.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. Literally, unbounded covolumes means \[ \sup_{i\geq1}\operatorname{vol}(G/M_i)=\infty. \] This is weaker than $\operatorname{vol}(G/M_i)\to\infty$. The supplied record does not clarify whether the latter condition was intended; the displayed question retains the supplied wording. The record also does not specify the rank convention or the connectedness and centre conventions for a simple Lie group. Under the real-rank convention, higher rank means $\operatorname{rank}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G)\geq2$; this interpretation requires confirmation against the source.

Short English statement

The question asks whether the minimal number of generators of higher-rank lattices, minus one, divided by covolume tends to zero along every sequence described in the source. The distinction between unbounded covolumes and covolumes tending to infinity remains unresolved. Supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and reports a torsion-free result; it does not impose torsion-freeness on the original question.
