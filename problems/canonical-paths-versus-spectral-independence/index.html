Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

The canonical-path method assigns a path through Markov-chain transitions to every pair of states and bounds congestion relative to stationary edge capacities, thereby controlling mixing. For a distribution on fixed-size subsets, an influence matrix has entries $\Psi(i,j)=\Pr(j\in S\mid i\in S)-\Pr(j\in S)$ on feasible coordinates. Spectral independence bounds the relevant largest eigenvalue uniformly under admissible pinnings or conditionings. Sampling perfect matchings uses a distribution over edge subsets that form perfect matchings.

Attempted connection and normalization

The primary question, printed page2977, asks for a relationship between canonical paths and spectral independence useful for sampling, especially nonbipartite perfect matchings. We try the direct path-congestion-to-Poincare-to-covariance route. It gives a complete conditional bound below, but requires good path bounds after every pinning. An explicit example shows why an unconditional polynomial path bound does not supply that premise. The results are elementary transfer mechanisms, not a claimed new solution of the source application.

A fully proved conditional transfer

Let $\pi$ be a probability law of positive mass on each state of a finite family of $k$-element subsets of a ground set, with $k\ge1$. Discard coordinates with zero inclusion probability and put $p_i=\Pr(i\in S)>0$. Write $I_i=\boldsymbol{1}_{i\in S}$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)$, and \[ \Psi(i,j)=\Pr(j\in S\mid i\in S)-p_j. \] Then $D\Psi=\operatorname{Cov}(I)$, so $\Psi$ is similar to $D^{-1/2}\operatorname{Cov}(I)D^{-1/2}$, a symmetric PSD matrix. This fixes exactly the source's inclusion-probability normalization.

If there is only one supported state, all indicators are constant, the covariance and influence matrices are zero, and no congestion parameter is needed. In the remaining case there are at least two states. Let $P$ be the down-up chain: remove a uniform element of $S$ and resample a containing $k$-set conditionally on the remaining set. It is reversible, because for neighbouring $S,T$ sharing a $(k-1)$-set $R$, its edge flow is $\pi(S)\pi(T)/(k\sum_{U\supset R}\pi(U))$, symmetric in $S,T$. Suppose the chain is connected. For each ordered pair of states $s,t$ choose a path $\gamma_{st}$ through positive-probability transitions, with the empty path for $s=t$. Put \[ \rho=\max_{e=(u,v):P(u,v)>0,\ u\ne v} \frac{\sum_{s,t:e\in\gamma_{st}}\pi(s)\pi(t)|\gamma_{st}|} {\pi(u)P(u,v)}. \] Choose paths without repeated directed edges. Telescoping along a path and Cauchy--Schwarz give \[ \operatorname{Var}_\pi f =\tfrac12\sum_{s,t}\pi(s)\pi(t)(f(s)-f(t))^2 \le\rho\,\mathcal E_P(f,f), \] where $\mathcal E_P=\tfrac12\sum_{u,v}\pi(u)P(u,v) (f(u)-f(v))^2$. Indeed the coefficient of each directed edge's squared difference in the telescoped sum is its displayed load, at most $\rho\pi(u)P(u,v)$.

For $f(S)=\sum_i a_iI_i(S)$, a down-up update removes some $i$ and adds some $j$, possibly the same. Its squared change is at most $2a_i^2+2a_j^2$. At stationarity the removed element has law $p_i/k$; the added element has the same law by the symmetry of the joint deletion/resampling construction. Hence \[ \mathcal E_P(f,f)\le\frac2k\sum_i p_i a_i^2. \] Set $a_i=v_i/\sqrt{p_i}$ and use the covariance identity to get \[ \lambda_{\max}(\Psi)\le2\rho/k. \] Apply the same proof after any positive-probability pinning, removing its fixed coordinates. If every remaining $k'$-set conditional chain has path parameter at most $Ck'$, then every conditional influence norm is at most $2C$. The cases with no remaining random coordinates have zero influence and need no path argument. This is an exact sufficient transfer; its strong conditional hypothesis is part of the theorem.

A rare pinning can destroy connectivity

We now show why one unconditional path bound is insufficient. For $k\ge2$, let a state be $(Z,X_1,\ldots,X_k)$ with binary coordinates. Put $\delta=2^{-2k}$ and give each state with $Z=0$ mass $w=(1-\delta)2^{-k}$. With $Z=1$ allow only $X=0^k$ and $X=1^k$, each of mass $v=\delta/2$. Then $v\le w$. Represent each binary assignment as the $(k+1)$-element set choosing one outcome from each of $k+1$ coordinate pairs. Its down-up walk is exactly the random-scan single-coordinate heat-bath chain.

For unconditional canonical paths, first move a $Z=1$ start to its $Z=0$ copy, then flip disagreeing $X$ coordinates in increasing order in the $Z=0$ layer, then move to a $Z=1$ endpoint if needed. Delete vacuous moves; equal endpoints use the empty path. The length is at most $k+2$. Every directed cube edge in the $Z=0$ layer has flow $w/(2(k+1))$. Mapping each endpoint to its cube copy gives projected mass at most $w+v\le2w$ at each cube vertex. For a fixed oriented edge in the coordinate-order paths, there are $2^{k-1}$ ordered cube endpoint pairs using it: earlier starting bits and later target bits are free, while all other bits are fixed by the edge. Thus the weighted pair mass is at most $4w^2 2^{k-1}\le2w$. Its length-weighted congestion ratio is at most $4(k+1)(k+2)$.

A bridge between a rare state and its cube copy has flow $wv/((k+1)(w+v))\ge v/(2(k+1))$. In either orientation only paths whose corresponding endpoint is that rare state use it, with pair mass at most $v$. Its ratio is therefore at most $2(k+1)(k+2)$. We have proved $\rho\le4(k+1)(k+2)$ for the unconditional chain, and hence a polynomial Poincare bound by the earlier complete proof.

But pinning $Z=1$ leaves only two equally likely assignments, $0^k$ and $1^k$. The single-coordinate chain is disconnected for $k\ge2$. On the $2k$ outcome coordinates the influence matrix, ordered by the two groups, is \[ \Psi=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}J_k&-J_k\\-J_k&J_k\end{pmatrix}. \] It sends $(\boldsymbol{1},-\boldsymbol{1})$ to $k$ times that vector and vanishes on its orthogonal complement, so its largest eigenvalue is $k$. Thus a polynomial unconditional canonical-path proof does not give a constant uniform spectral independence bound after pinnings.

Audit and missing application

The audit checked source normalization, reversibility, both removed/added marginals, directed-edge factors of two, and the rare-state bridge capacities. The example does not disprove a more refined relationship that controls all conditional chains; it identifies exactly why unconditional mixing alone is too weak.
