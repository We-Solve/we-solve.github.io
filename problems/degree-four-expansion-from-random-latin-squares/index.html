Degree-Four Expansion from Random Latin Squares

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks about first coboundary expansion in a random Latin-square complex. It does not explicitly define the associated complex, coefficient system, or normalization of the expansion constant. The following gives a conventional precise interpretation; these conventions require confirmation against the supplied references.

For an integer $n\geq 1$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A Latin square of order $n$ is a map $L:[n]\times[n]\to[n]$ such that each row and each column is a permutation of $[n]$. Let $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$, $B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}$, and $C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_n\}$ be disjoint vertex sets. In the customary tripartite construction, $X(L)$ has vertex set $A\cup B\cup C$, all edges joining different parts, and triangles \[ \{a_i,b_j,c_{L(i,j)}\},\qquad i,j\in[n]. \] For a positive integer $d$, choose $L_1,\ldots,L_d$ independently and uniformly from all Latin squares of order $n$, and set \[ Y_{n,d}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{d}X(L_r). \] This union is a simplicial complex: repeated triangles are counted once. Thus $d$ counts sampled Latin squares and does not assert that every edge belongs to exactly $d$ distinct triangles.

One conventional expansion constant uses ${\mathbb{F}}_2={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}$ coefficients and unnormalized support cardinalities. For a finite two-dimensional simplicial complex $Y$, let $Y(k)$ denote its set of $k$-simplices and put $C^k(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)=\{f:Y(k)\to{\mathbb{F}}_2\}$. Define \[ (\delta_0g)(\{u,v\})=g(u)+g(v),\qquad (\delta_1f)(\{u,v,w\})=f(\{u,v\})+f(\{u,w\})+f(\{v,w\}), \] with sums in ${\mathbb{F}}_2$, and let $B^1(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)=\operatorname{im}\delta_0$. For a cochain $f$, let $|\operatorname{supp}f|$ be the number of simplices on which it is nonzero. Define \[ \|[f]\|=\min_{b\in B^1(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)}|\operatorname{supp}(f+b)|, \qquad h_1^{\mathrm{count}}(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)= \min_{f\in C^1(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)\setminus B^1(Y;{\mathbb{F}}_2)} \frac{|\operatorname{supp}(\delta_1f)|}{\|[f]\|}. \] Under this interpretation, the question is whether \[ \exists\,\varepsilon>0\quad \lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb P\!\left(h_1^{\mathrm{count}}(Y_{n,4};{\mathbb{F}}_2)>\varepsilon\right)=1, \] where $n$ tends to infinity through positive integers and $\varepsilon$ is independent of $n$.

The source writes this target using $h_1(Y)$. Identifying that notation with the displayed counting convention, and identifying its associated complexes with the tripartite construction above, remain explicit interpretive qualifications rather than facts established by the supplied record.

Short English statement

Does the union of the complexes from four independent uniform Latin squares have first coboundary expansion bounded below by a fixed positive constant with probability tending to one as the order grows? The displayed precise interpretation uses tripartite complexes and ${\mathbb{F}}_2$ cochains with support-counting norms; the source's exact conventions require confirmation.
