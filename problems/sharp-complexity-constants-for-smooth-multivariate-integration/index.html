Math-7221

Sharp Complexity Constants for Smooth Multivariate Integration

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D_d\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be a bounded domain, where $d\geq 1$ is an integer, and let $k$ denote the smoothness order appearing in the source. Under the usual nonnegative-integer interpretation of $k$, $C^k(D_d)$ denotes functions with continuous partial derivatives of orders at most $k$ on $D_d$. The supplied record does not specify the admissible range of $k$, the scalar field, whether derivatives must extend continuously to the boundary, or any norm or bounded subset of this function space.

The symbol $\operatorname{INT}$ denotes the intended integration functional on $D_d$. Its measure, normalization, and integrability assumptions are not specified. Retain the source notation \[ E_n(D_d):=e_n(C^k(D_d),\operatorname{INT}),\qquad n\geq 1, \] for its integration error quantity. The record does not define $e_n$, including the permitted information, algorithm class, cost convention, or error criterion; consequently this notation cannot yet be interpreted as a uniquely defined numerical sequence.

Source questions, conditional on specifying these conventions. For fixed $d$ and $k$, determine whether \[ L_{d,k}(D_d):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n(D_d)\,n^{k/d} \] depends on the bounded domain $D_d$. Equivalently, after specifying the admissible family of domains and using the same integration and error conventions throughout, is \[ L_{d,k}(D_d)=L_{d,k}(D'_d) \] for every pair of admissible domains $D_d,D'_d\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$? For each admissible $D_d$, is the limsup an actual limit, that is, does \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}E_n(D_d)\,n^{k/d} \] exist and equal $L_{d,k}(D_d)$? The source does not specify whether finiteness of this limit is required.

The original statement also asks more broadly how integration complexity depends on $d$ and $D_d$. Domain regularity, volume normalization, function-class bounds, and the computational model must be recovered before either the asymptotic questions or this broader request has a fully precise mathematical formulation.

Short English statement

For smooth multivariate integration, the source asks whether the asymptotic error constant after scaling by $n^{k/d}$ depends on the integration domain, and whether the scaled errors converge. The function-class bounds, integration convention, admissible domains, and meaning of the error quantity remain unspecified, so this is a conditional statement requiring mathematical review.
