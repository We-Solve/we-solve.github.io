Math-4872

Vertices, Sources, and Filtrations of Symmetric-Group Modules

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record collects several questions, rather than one fully specified conjecture. The following statements retain its separate targets and identify the missing qualifications.
  1. Let $F$ be a field, $G$ a finite group, and $H\leq G$. For each integer $i\geq 1$, define \[ T_i=\underbrace{FG\otimes_{FH}\cdots\otimes_{FH}FG}_{i\text{ factors}}, \] with its $(FH,FH)$-bimodule structure given by multiplication on the outer factors. Let $\mathcal I_i$ be the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable $(FH,FH)$-bimodules occurring as direct summands of $T_i$; multiplicities are discarded. Determine \[ s_F(G,H)=\min\{i\geq 1:\mathcal I_i=\mathcal I_{i+1}\}. \] The minimum denotes a value only when the displayed set is nonempty. The record also requests the alternating-group case, but does not specify the subgroup inclusion intended in that case.

  2. Write $S_n$ for the symmetric group on $n$ letters and $A_n$ for its alternating subgroup. In characteristic $p>0$, a vertex of a finite-dimensional indecomposable $FK$-module $M$, for a finite group $K$, is a $p$-subgroup $P\leq K$, minimal under inclusion, for which $M$ is a direct summand of $\operatorname{Ind}_P^K U$ for some $FP$-module $U$. A source at a vertex $P$ is an indecomposable direct summand $U$ of $\operatorname{Res}_P^K M$ such that $M$ is a direct summand of $\operatorname{Ind}_P^K U$.

    Determine the vertices and sources of the irreducible $FS_n$-modules denoted by $D^\lambda$. In the usual split modular convention, $\lambda$ is a $p$-regular partition of $n$, meaning that no positive part occurs $p$ or more times. The record does not explicitly specify the field hypotheses, characteristic range, or partition-label convention for this question; this convention therefore describes a conditional interpretation.

    In characteristic $2$, decide whether every nontrivial source of such an irreducible module has even $F$-dimension. If "nontrivial" means not isomorphic to the one-dimensional trivial $FP$-module $F$, the proposed assertion is \[ U\not\cong F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dim_F U\equiv 0\pmod 2 \] for every source $U$ at every vertex $P$ of every module under consideration. The intended meaning of "nontrivial" is not explicitly defined in the record.

  3. With the customary convention, $\operatorname{Lie}_F(n)$ is the multilinear component of the free Lie algebra over $F$ on $x_1,\ldots,x_n$: it is spanned by Lie monomials in which every generator occurs exactly once. The group $S_n$ acts by permuting the generators. For an $FS_n$-module $M$, its dual $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}_F(M,F)$ carries the action $(g\varphi)(m)=\varphi(g^{-1}m)$. A permutation module is an $FS_n$-module isomorphic to $F[X]$ for a finite $S_n$-set $X$.

    The explicit Lie-module question in the supplied statement is whether \[ \operatorname{Lie}_F(n)\otimes_F\operatorname{Lie}_F(n)^* \] is a permutation module under the diagonal $S_n$-action. The record does not specify restrictions on $n$ or $\operatorname{char}F$. Its original statement additionally requests Lie-module vertex, source, and indecomposability results, without identifying the exact modules, summands, hypotheses, or assertions for those additional questions. Those targets remain unresolved at the formulation level.

  4. In characteristic $2$, decide whether every nonsimple indecomposable Specht module for $S_n$ remains indecomposable upon restriction to $A_n$. Writing $S_F^\lambda$ for the usual Specht module associated to a partition $\lambda\vdash n$, the proposed assertion is \[ S_F^\lambda\text{ indecomposable and not simple} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Res}_{A_n}^{S_n}S_F^\lambda\text{ indecomposable}. \] The characteristic is specified, but no further hypotheses on $F$ or restrictions on $n$ and $\lambda$ are supplied.

  5. Determine the graded multiplicities of irreducible modules denoted by $D^\lambda$ in the coinvariant algebras of symmetric groups and of $\operatorname{GL}_n(q)$, or in their socles.

    To describe the data needed for a precise version, for a specified finite group $K$ acting linearly on a specified finite-dimensional $F$-space $V$, one usual definition is \[ C_F(K,V)=\operatorname{Sym}_F(V^*)\big/\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}_F(V^*)^K_{>0}\right\rangle, \] where the denominator is the ideal generated by homogeneous invariants of positive degree. For a finite-dimensional graded $FK$-module $C=\bigoplus_{d\geq0}C_d$ and a simple module $D$, its graded composition multiplicity is encoded by \[ \sum_{d\geq0}[C_d:D]t^d. \] Here $[C_d:D]$ is the Jordan--Hölder multiplicity. The socle $\operatorname{soc}_{FK}C$ is the sum of all simple submodules, and its graded multiplicities can be expressed using its degree components in the same way.

    These definitions give a possible framework, not a determination of the intended coinvariant algebras. The record does not specify the representation $V$, the coefficient field and its relation to the prime power $q$, the labels for simple $\operatorname{GL}_n(q)$-modules, or whether "multiplicities" means composition multiplicities or another notion. It also leaves unclear whether both the whole algebra and its socle are requested, or whether these are alternative targets.

Short English statement

This record bundles questions about tensor-chain stabilization, vertices and sources of symmetric-group modules, Lie modules, restriction of Specht modules to alternating groups, and graded multiplicities in coinvariant algebras. Several field assumptions and intended targets are missing. The supplied open status and verification claims are curation metadata; this statement-only draft requires human mathematical review.
