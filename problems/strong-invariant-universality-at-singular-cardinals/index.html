Strong Invariant Universality at Singular Cardinals

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\kappa$ denote the cardinal parameter in the supplied record. A quasi-order on a set $X$ is a reflexive, transitive relation $R\subseteq X\times X$. For quasi-orders $R$ on $X$ and $S$ on $Y$, a reduction is a map $f:X\to Y$ satisfying \[ x\,R\,x'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)\,S\,f(x') \qquad(x,x'\in X). \] Borel reducibility additionally requires specified Borel structures on $X,Y$ and a Borel map $f$. This definition alone does not define the stronger property called strong invariant universality in the source.

For a specified signature, logic, and sentence $\varphi$, write $\operatorname{Mod}_{\kappa}(\varphi)$ for the structures of cardinality $\kappa$ satisfying $\varphi$. A descriptive-set-theoretic treatment additionally requires a coding and topology. The supplied record does not specify these data, the sentence $\varphi$, the definition of generalized tree, the intended embeddings, or the precise meanings of $\kappa$-analytic and strongly invariantly universal. Consequently, the following preserves the source questions but remains a conditional formulation requiring those definitions and hypotheses from the cited report.

  1. For the intended class of generalized trees of cardinality $\kappa$, is the hypothesis printed as \[ \kappa^{\kappa}=\kappa \] necessary in the referenced theorem asserting that embeddability is strongly invariantly universal for $\kappa$-analytic quasi-orders?

    There is an unresolved defect in this printed hypothesis: for every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, \[ \kappa^{\kappa}\geq 2^{\kappa}>\kappa. \] Thus the displayed equality cannot hold in the intended infinite-cardinal setting, and the referenced theorem and its intended cardinal-arithmetic hypothesis must be identified before a meaningful necessity question can be stated. No replacement exponent is inferred here.

  2. For singular $\kappa$, that is, an infinite cardinal with $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)<\kappa$, determine the descriptive-set-theoretic complexity of embeddability on the intended space $\operatorname{Mod}_{\kappa}(\varphi)$. Can this embeddability relation consistently be strongly invariantly universal for the intended $\kappa$-analytic quasi-orders? The sentence, coding, topology, embedding convention, meaning of strong invariant universality, and background theory for the consistency assertion remain unspecified.

  3. In the corresponding universality theorems for combinatorial trees and generalized trees, can the tree classes be replaced by linear orders or groups while retaining the respective universality conclusions, including strong invariant universality where asserted? The particular theorems, cardinal assumptions, and structure-preserving maps for these replacements must be supplied to make this question precise.

These are statement-only questions attributed to the supplied record; the missing specifications and incompatible printed hypothesis prevent a fully precise standalone formulation.

Short English statement

The record asks whether a cardinal-arithmetic assumption is necessary for strong invariant universality of tree embeddability, what happens at singular cardinals, and whether linear orders or groups can replace trees. The printed assumption $\kappa^{\kappa}=\kappa$ is impossible for infinite $\kappa$, and several essential definitions and theorem hypotheses are absent. The intended questions therefore require source clarification.
