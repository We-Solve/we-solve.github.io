FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #26; reconstruction, 2026-09-23. For an infinite set $A$ of positive integers and $k\geq1$, put \[ M_k=\{n\geq1:\text{some }a\in A\text{ satisfies }a+k\mid n\}. \] Must some $M_k$ have natural density one? We construct an $A$ for which every $M_k$ has upper density at most $1/2$. We also give a counterexample whose reciprocal sum diverges, so the obstruction need not be summability.

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

The current page labels the problem disproved and reports formal verification. It credits Ruzsa with a Chinese-remainder construction and discusses the reciprocal-sum obstruction and a divergent-sum modification. The proofs below reconstruct these elementary mechanisms; they are not presented as new results. No formal verification is claimed for this write-up.

WORK

1. A direct counting counterexample

Take $A=\{2^j:j\geq2\}$. For every positive integer $X$, \[ |M_k\cap[1,X]| \leq\sum_{\substack{j\geq2\\2^j+k\leq X}} \left\lfloor\frac{X}{2^j+k}\right\rfloor \leq X\sum_{j\geq2}\frac1{2^j+k} \leq X\sum_{j\geq2}2^{-j}=\frac X2. \] The first inequality is a union bound: overcounting an integer with several such divisors is harmless. Terms with modulus larger than $X$ contribute zero. Thus there is no interchange of a limiting density with an infinite union. Dividing by $X$ and taking an upper limit proves $\overline d(M_k)\leq1/2$ simultaneously for every $k\geq1$. This already answers the problem negatively. More generally, starting the powers at $2^J$ gives the uniform bound $2^{1-J}$.

2. A divergent reciprocal sum is possible as well

Let $p_1,p_2,\ldots$ be the primes. At stage $j$ the Chinese remainder theorem gives a residue class $r_j$ modulo $P_j=p_1\cdots p_j$ such that \[ r_j\equiv-i\pmod{p_i}\qquad(1\leq i\leq j). \] Choose a finite block $F_j$ of positive integers in this residue class, all larger than every integer chosen earlier, with \[ \sum_{a\in F_j}\frac1a\geq1. \] Such a block exists because the reciprocal sum over any positive arithmetic progression diverges: for sufficiently large $t$, $r_j+tP_j\leq2P_jt$, so its reciprocal dominates $1/(2P_jt)$. One can make the choice deterministic by taking successive members until the displayed sum first reaches one. Put $A=\bigcup_{j\geq1}F_j$. The blocks are disjoint, so $A$ is infinite and $\sum_{a\in A}1/a=\infty$.

Fix $k\geq1$. Every element from a block of index $j\geq k$ satisfies $a+k\equiv0\pmod{p_k}$. Consequently its positive multiples lie among the multiples of $p_k$. Let $E_k=\bigcup_{j<k}F_j$, a finite set, and set \[ L_k=p_k\prod_{a\in E_k}(a+k). \] Every integer $n\equiv1\pmod{L_k}$ is indivisible by $p_k$ and by each $a+k$ with $a\in E_k$. The latter moduli exceed one. Such $n$ therefore has no divisor from $A+k$. This missing arithmetic progression has density $1/L_k>0$, whence \[ \overline d(M_k)\leq1-1/L_k<1. \] The dependence of $L_k$ on $k$ is immaterial: the original assertion requires density one for at least one fixed shift. $\square$

VERIFICATION AND LIMITS

The first construction proves the claim using inequalities for every finite $X$, which is stronger than merely disproving existence of a density-one limit. The second construction checks the quantifiers separately for each shift and permits finitely many exceptional blocks. There is no requirement that the missing progressions be the same for different shifts. The Chinese remainder theorem only involves finitely many pairwise coprime primes at any stage.

FINAL STATUS

SOLVED (known negative answer reconstructed). Both constructions above are complete. The first gives a uniform quantitative density bound; the second proves that even divergence of the reciprocal sum does not force a successful shift. No new literature result or Lean verification is claimed.
