Connected Monodromy Groups of Kodaira Fibrations

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question does not define connected monodromy. The following makes explicit the usual interpretation in terms of algebraic monodromy on rational first cohomology; this interpretation requires confirmation against the original report.

A Kodaira fibration is understood here to be a smooth surjective holomorphic map $f\colon S\to B$ with connected fibres, where $S$ is a smooth connected projective complex surface, $B$ is a smooth connected projective complex curve of genus $b\geq 2$, and the fibres are smooth curves of genus $g\geq 2$ whose complex structures are not all isomorphic. For a base point $t\in B$, put $F_t=f^{-1}(t)$ and $V=H^1(F_t,{\mathbb{Q}})$. The cup product gives a nondegenerate alternating form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ on $V$. Parallel transport defines a monodromy representation \[ \rho_f\colon\pi_1(B,t)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(V,\langle\ ,\ \rangle). \] Under the stated interpretation, define the connected monodromy group by \[ G_f=\left(\overline{\rho_f(\pi_1(B,t))}^{\,\mathrm{Zar},{\mathbb{Q}}}\right)^\circ, \] where the closure is the algebraic Zariski closure over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, and the superscript $\circ$ denotes its identity component.

Classification question. Which connected algebraic groups occur as $G_f$ for Kodaira fibrations $f\colon S\to B$, allowing the base and fibre genera to vary? More explicitly, for each $g\geq 2$, determine which connected algebraic ${\mathbb{Q}}$-subgroups of $\operatorname{Sp}_{2g,{\mathbb{Q}}}$ arise in this way, up to conjugacy by a rational symplectic change of basis, as $b\geq 2$ and $f$ vary.

The subgroup formulation specifies an ambient representation and an equivalence relation beyond those stated in the source record. The record does not settle whether the intended classification is instead up to abstract algebraic-group isomorphism, after extension of scalars, or under another convention for connected monodromy. It supplies no restriction to a particular fibre genus. Its literature assessment refers to a specified genus-three construction without supplying that construction or its hypotheses; those qualifications cannot be incorporated as a precise subsidiary statement.

Short English statement

Classify the connected monodromy groups arising from Kodaira fibrations. Under the explicitly stated cohomological interpretation, these are identity components of rational Zariski closures of monodromy on the first cohomology of a fibre. The intended field and classification equivalence require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved, but this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
