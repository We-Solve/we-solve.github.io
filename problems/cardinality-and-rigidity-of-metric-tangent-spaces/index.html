Math-13954

Cardinality and Rigidity of Metric Tangent Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A metric space $(X,d)$ is geodesic if, for every $p,q\in X$, there is an isometric map $\gamma:[0,d(p,q)]\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=p$ and $\gamma(d(p,q))=q$. Fix a basepoint $x_0\in X$. A pointed isometry is a bijective isometry that sends the distinguished basepoint to the distinguished basepoint.

The supplied question concerns tangents obtained by blowing up the metric near $x_0$, but it does not specify a notion of convergence. One customary interpretation, stated here conditionally, uses pointed Gromov--Hausdorff convergence of proper metric spaces. A metric space is proper if every closed bounded ball is compact. If $X$ is additionally assumed proper, define \[ \operatorname{Tan}_{\mathrm{pGH}}(X,x_0) = \left\{ [(Y,d_Y,y_0)]_{\mathrm{pt}}: \begin{array}{l} (Y,d_Y)\text{ is a proper metric space, and there exists}\\ (r_j)_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\text{ with }r_j>0,\ r_j\to0,\text{ such that}\\ (X,r_j^{-1}d,x_0)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{pGH}}(Y,d_Y,y_0) \end{array} \right\}, \] where $[\cdot]_{\mathrm{pt}}$ denotes the pointed-isometry class. Under this interpretation, uniqueness means \[ \left|\operatorname{Tan}_{\mathrm{pGH}}(X,x_0)\right|=1, \] so it includes existence of a tangent. No existence or subsequential compactness assumption is supplied in the record.

Source question. For a geodesic metric space $(X,d)$ and $x_0\in X$, how large can the collection $\operatorname{Tan}(X,x_0)$ of pointed-isometry classes of tangents be, and what structural consequences follow when this collection has exactly one element?

Unresolved qualifications. The proper-space pointed Gromov--Hausdorff definition above is a possible interpretation, not a hypothesis or definition established by the supplied source record. The original question assumes only geodesicity. It does not specify convergence for nonproper spaces, a cardinal bound or classification to establish, or the structural conclusions intended by uniqueness. Consequently, the supplied question does not determine a single precise conjecture without further source clarification.

Short English statement

For a geodesic metric space with a chosen basepoint, ask how many distinct tangents can arise and what the existence of exactly one tangent implies. A conditional pointed Gromov--Hausdorff formulation is given, but the source leaves the convergence convention, additional hypotheses, and intended rigidity conclusions unspecified.
