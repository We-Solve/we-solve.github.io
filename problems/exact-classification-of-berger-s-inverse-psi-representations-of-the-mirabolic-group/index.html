Exact classification of Berger's inverse-psi representations of the mirabolic group

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Definitions and mathematical statement

The extraction joins Berger's main question with separate discussions by Ribet and Buzzard. They are retained separately, rather than extending a solved label to all of them.

Berger's inverse-$\psi$ question. Let $G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}$ be the absolute Galois group and $V$ an irreducible finite-dimensional continuous representation. The extraction prints ${\mathbb{F}}_p$, while the aid identifies the recovered coefficient field as $k=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_p$. The classification below explicitly uses that algebraically closed field; descent to a literal finite coefficient field is not silently included. Let $D(V)$ be the associated $\acute{\mathrm e}$tale $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module, with the usual cyclotomic $\Gamma$ and left inverse $\psi$ of $\varphi$. Write \[ \Omega(V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont},k} \left(\left(\varprojlim_\psi D(V)\right)^b,k\right),\qquad P=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}: a\in{\mathbb{Q}}_p^*,\ b\in{\mathbb{Q}}_p\right\}. \] The bounded inverse limit consists of compatible sequences $\psi(v_{j+1})=v_j$ lying in a common lattice in $D(V)$, with the standard bounded-limit topology and Berger $P$-action. Exact lattice/topology and action conventions are not supplied in the source. The source says $\Omega(V)$ is smooth irreducible admissible and asks which such $P$-representations arise for arbitrary $\dim V$.

The aid reports the exact answer in its recovered conventions: $V\mapsto\Omega(V)$ is a bijection on isomorphism classes between irreducible finite-dimensional continuous $k$-representations of $G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}$ and infinite-dimensional smooth irreducible admissible $k$-representations of $P$. Thus the infinite-dimensional qualification is essential; finite-dimensional smooth irreducible $P$-representations are not in this image. The reconstructed free irreducible $(\psi,\Gamma)$-module has rank equal to the dimension of the unique Galois parameter. This is a reported classification of irreducibles, not a stated equivalence of entire abelian categories.

The aid attributes the input to the Berger--Vienney Borel classification. For the upper Borel $B\subset\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb{Q}}_p)$, scalar center $Z$, there is a topological direct product \[ B=Z\times P,\qquad \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix} =(dI_2)\begin{pmatrix}a/d&b/d\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \] A fixed smooth central character extends a $P$-representation to $B$. The aid transfers the classification through this product and explains that the resulting Weil parameter extends to a continuous Galois parameter over $\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_p$: its relevant unramified scalars have finite order. This coefficient-field fact should not be generalized to arbitrary characteristic-$p$ fields with infinite-order scalars. Faithfulness supplies the reported uniqueness. Full theorem references and hypotheses are not provided here.

Ribet's separate extension question. Does the $P$-action extend naturally to a larger group? The source reports yes for one- and two-dimensional $V$, without defining the larger group or naturality requirement. The trivial direct-product extension to the Borel after choosing a central character does not settle a substantive extension to a larger group such as the intended rank-two ambient group. The arbitrary-dimensional natural-extension question is not answered by the reported image classification.

Buzzard's separate generalization question. Is there a useful generalization of $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-modules with $\Gamma$ a higher-dimensional $p$-adic Lie group, classifying $p$-adic Galois representations, not merely mod-$p$ representations? The source records Kisin recalling Fontaine's norm-field procedure for mod-$p$ representations in a broad $\Gamma$ setting. Towers, norm-field hypotheses, coefficient rings, operators and the precise category equivalence are omitted, so this recalled statement is not promoted to a theorem for every arbitrary Lie group or every tower. No general $p$-adic solution is supplied by the aid. The following "Breuil" heading contains no further question in the record.

Short English statement

The aid reports an exact irreducible classification for Berger's mirabolic construction over $\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_p$, with infinite-dimensional smooth admissible representations as its image. The coefficient field, bounded-limit conventions and Borel central character matter. The appended natural-extension and higher-dimensional $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module questions remain separate and are not resolved by that classification.
