Finite reduction and the first requested values.
Use the conventional weight $2/|\operatorname{Aut}_{SL_2}(Q)|$ for a positive-definite integral form $Q=(a,b,c)$ of discriminant $-m$. The substitution $x\mapsto x+ky$ lets one reduce the middle coefficient relative to the leading one after exchanging variables as needed. More explicitly, choose $k$ to put $-a<b\le a$; if $c<a$, exchange the two variables with determinant-one map $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$, which strictly decreases the positive leading coefficient. Iteration terminates with $|b|\le a\le c$. Then \[m=4ac-b^2\ge3a^2,\qquad 1\le a\le\sqrt{m/3}. \] Thus only finitely many reduced candidates occur for each positive $m$.
Explicit low-index enumeration.
Discriminants satisfy $-m\equiv0,1\pmod4$, so $h_9=h_{10}=0$. For $m=11$, the bound gives $a=1$, and the only candidates are $(1,\pm1,3)$, equivalent by an integral translation; there is one class with weight one. For $m=12$, the candidates are $(1,0,3)$ and $(2,\pm2,2)$, with the latter pair equivalent. Their coefficient gcds differ, so the two classes are inequivalent. The first has only $\pm I$ as determinant-one automorphisms: its shortest primitive vectors are $\pm(1,0)$, forcing the remaining basis vector. The second is twice the hexagonal form, whose six shortest vectors give exactly six orientation-preserving symmetries. Hence \[h_{11}=1,\qquad h_{12}=1+\tfrac13=\tfrac43. \]
