Physically Admissible Weak Solutions of Incompressible Euler

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns the incompressible Euler equations and the meaning of physical admissibility for their weak solutions. The record does not specify a spatial domain, dimension, time interval, initial or boundary data, regularity class, or admissibility criterion. Consequently, it does not determine a unique mathematical problem.

For reference only, a parameterized interior distributional formulation is as follows. Let $d$ be a positive integer, let $\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^d$ be open, and let $I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ be an open interval. Write $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)$ for velocity and $p$ for pressure. The equations are \[ \partial_tu+\operatorname{div}_x(u\otimes u)+\nabla_xp=0, \qquad \operatorname{div}_xu=0 \quad\text{in }\Omega\times I, \] where $(u\otimes u)_{ij}=u_i u_j$. One possible interior weak-solution class consists of pairs \[ u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\times I;{\mathbb{R}}^d), \qquad p\in\mathcal D'(\Omega\times I), \] such that, for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega\times I;{\mathbb{R}}^d)$, \[ \int_I\!\int_\Omega \left(\sum_{i=1}^d u_i\partial_t\varphi_i +\sum_{i,j=1}^d u_i u_j\partial_{x_j}\varphi_i\right)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t +\langle p,\operatorname{div}_x\varphi\rangle=0, \] and, for every $\psi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega\times I)$, \[ \int_I\!\int_\Omega u\cdot\nabla_x\psi\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t=0. \] Here $\mathcal D'$ denotes distributions and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ denotes their action on test functions. Compactly supported interior tests impose no initial or boundary conditions. This reference definition is not asserted to be the solution class intended by the source.

Source question. What definition of a weak solution of the incompressible Euler equations, together with what additional admissibility requirements, captures solutions that are physically reasonable?

The phrase "physically reasonable" is not defined in the record. No particular energy inequality, regularity threshold, approximation procedure, or other selection criterion is specified. Thus the intended admissible class and a precise property to establish about that class remain unresolved; the question cannot faithfully be converted into a single quantified conjecture from the supplied information.

Short English statement

The source asks how to define physically reasonable weak solutions of the incompressible Euler equations. An interior distributional formulation is provided as a reference, but the intended domain, data, solution class, and physical admissibility criterion are unspecified. The source therefore records a formulation question requiring further mathematical clarification.
