Degree-two-and-three Markov basis for the Birkhoff ranking model

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For an integer matrix $A$ and statistic vector $b$, the nonnegative integer fiber is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb N^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves in $\ker A$ connecting every such fiber by moves that remain nonnegative. Its binomial generators form the toric ideal $I_A=\langle x^u-x^v:Au=Av\rangle$. In the ranking model, coordinates count permutations and $A$ has the vectorized permutation matrices as columns. The fixed summary counts how often each candidate appears at each rank.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Problem 1. Explicitly describe a Markov basis for tables with fixed first-order summary. That is, describe a set of moves which will connect any two surveys that have the same first order summary. In a purely mathematical language: describe a generating set for the toric ideal given by the p2 × p! matrix whose columns are the p! p × p permutation matrices.

Contingency tables with quadratic statistics. [S2]
