Dynamic Induction for Strictly Positive Definitions

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns dynamic induction for inductively defined predicates. To display its known special case, write $D$ for the underlying domain, $<$ for the relation used in the definition, and $B(x)$ for the base predicate. These are placeholders: the supplied record specifies neither the domain nor hypotheses on the relation or base predicate. The displayed defining clause is \[ I(x)\leftrightarrow B(x)\lor\exists y\,(y<x\land I(y)). \] Under the usual least-fixed-point interpretation of an inductive definition, its associated operator is \[ \Phi(X)=\{x\in D:B(x)\lor\exists y\in D\,(y<x\land y\in X)\}, \qquad I=\mu\Phi, \] where $\mu\Phi$ denotes the least fixed point under inclusion. This explains a conventional interpretation of "inductive"; the displayed equivalence alone does not specify a least fixed point, and the supplied record does not explicitly give its fixed-point semantics.

For a general defining formula $A(X,x)$, strict positivity conventionally requires that occurrences of the predicate variable $X$ appear only positively and never in an implication antecedent, with negation understood as implication to falsity. The precise permitted syntax and ambient logic must be specified to make this condition formal. In a setting where such a formula defines a monotone operator $\Phi_A$ and its least fixed point exists, write $I_A=\mu\Phi_A$.

Question (schematic). Does dynamic induction, reported in the supplied source to extend from the natural numbers to inductive definitions of the displayed special form, extend to every strictly positive inductive definition $I_A=\mu\Phi_A$ in the intended framework?

The supplied literature assessment describes the intended target as a dynamic-induction extraction theorem. However, the record supplies neither the dynamic-induction rule nor the extraction theorem, its hypotheses, or its required conclusion. Consequently, this is a statement of the supplied extension question, not a fully specified proof-theoretic assertion. No well-foundedness assumption on $<$, choice of ambient logic, or extension to coinductive definitions is added.

Short English statement

The question asks whether dynamic induction extends from the stated special kind of inductive definition to every strictly positive inductive definition. A precise theorem requires the original dynamic-induction principle, its extraction guarantee, and the intended logical framework, which the supplied record does not provide. The supplied status is partially_solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
