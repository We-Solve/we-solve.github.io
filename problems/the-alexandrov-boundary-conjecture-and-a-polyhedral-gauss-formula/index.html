Math-356

The Alexandrov boundary conjecture and a polyhedral Gauss formula

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a real lower bound $\kappa$, write $\operatorname{CBB}(\kappa)$ for a complete finite-dimensional Alexandrov length space with curvature bounded below by $\kappa$ in triangle comparison. The intended interpretation of the source question is: if a convex hypersurface $Y$ is the boundary of an appropriate full-dimensional convex domain in an Alexandrov space $X\in\operatorname{CBB}(\kappa)$, does $Y$, with its intrinsic length metric, also have curvature at least $\kappa$? Here intrinsic distance is the infimum of lengths of curves constrained to $Y$, considered on each relevant connected component. "Convex hypersurface" does not mean that the hypersurface itself contains every ambient minimizing segment.

The aid identifies the general issue with the Alexandrov boundary conjecture: the intrinsic boundary of an Alexandrov space with lower curvature bound should retain that bound, under the appropriate completeness and boundary conventions. This is a lower-bound assertion for every fixed $\kappa$, not a tensorial sectional-curvature identity or a pointwise comparison of undefined singular sectional curvatures. The aid reports the general conjecture still open.

The supplied sharp special case is the boundary of a compact full-dimensional convex Euclidean polyhedron $P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$. Its intrinsic polyhedral surface $Y=\partial P$ is $\operatorname{CBB}(0)$. If $\theta_v$ is the sum of the incident face angles at a vertex, its intrinsic curvature measure is \[ \mu_Y=\sum_{v}(2\pi-\theta_v)\,\delta_v. \] Convexity gives $\theta_v\leq2\pi$. Face interiors and edge interiors are intrinsically flat; the singular Gauss contribution is concentrated at vertices, not at extrinsic dihedral bends. More generally, for a complete locally finite Euclidean polyhedral surface without boundary and with manifold neighborhoods, the aid states that local $\operatorname{CBB}(0)$ is equivalent to $\theta_v\leq2\pi$ at every vertex. If polyhedral surfaces with boundary or accumulating singularities are allowed, additional local hypotheses must be supplied.

For comparison, in the smooth setting an orthonormal tangent pair $u,v$ satisfies the Gauss equation \[ K_Y(u,v)=K_X(u,v)+ \mathrm{II}(u,u)\mathrm{II}(v,v)-\mathrm{II}(u,v)^2. \] A semidefinite second fundamental form gives a nonnegative last contribution. Without convexity the conclusion fails: the surface $z=xy$ in Euclidean $3$-space has Gaussian curvature $-1$ at the origin. The metric convention is equally essential: the round sphere with ambient Euclidean chord distance is not a length space, since antipodal points have chord distance $2$ while every surface path has length at least $\pi$ on the unit sphere. That chord example is a failure of the required intrinsic-length setup, not a counterexample to the boundary conjecture. The polyhedral and smooth cases do not establish the full singular-space assertion.

Short English statement

Does a convex Alexandrov hypersurface retain the ambient lower curvature bound when given its intrinsic metric? The supplied aid treats this as the boundary conjecture and gives the Euclidean polyhedral surface case, where vertex angle defects are exactly the curvature measure. It also explains why convexity and intrinsic length, rather than chord distance, are essential.
