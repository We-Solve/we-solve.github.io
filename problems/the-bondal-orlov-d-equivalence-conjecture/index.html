The Bondal–Orlov D-Equivalence Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth projective complex variety $X$, write \[ D^b(X)=D^b\left(\operatorname{Coh}(X)\right) \] for the bounded derived category of coherent algebraic sheaves on $X$. Here derived equivalence is interpreted in its usual sense as a ${\mathbb{C}}$-linear exact equivalence of triangulated categories. Varieties $X$ and $X'$ are birational if they have isomorphic dense Zariski open subsets.

The supplied statement assumes that $X$ and $X'$ are Calabi--Yau varieties, but does not define this term. In particular, it does not specify the precise canonical-bundle condition or any additional cohomological vanishing or simple-connectedness requirements. This hypothesis is retained without selecting a convention; the precise scope of the quantifiers requires clarification.

Question (attributed in the supplied record to Bondal--Orlov). For every pair $X,X'$ of smooth projective complex Calabi--Yau varieties, does birationality imply an equivalence \[ X\dashrightarrow X'\ \text{birational} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D^b\left(\operatorname{Coh}(X)\right) \simeq D^b\left(\operatorname{Coh}(X')\right)? \] No dimension restriction is stated in the supplied question.

Qualifications retained from the source record. The original excerpt states that a more general version involving bounded derived categories of twisted sheaves is proved. It supplies neither the applicable hypotheses nor the twisting data or a precise theorem reference, so this assertion does not specify an additional mathematical statement here. The excerpt also discusses the cohomology of $A_g$, saying that full cohomology had only been known for $g\leq 3$ and that the existence of odd-degree classes had been a longstanding open problem. The supplied dated assessment instead reports odd cohomology in genera $5$ and $7$, while saying that full cohomology in higher genus is unknown. These contextual claims are retained as supplied literature descriptions; their coefficient systems, exact scope, and relation to the derived-equivalence question are unspecified.

Short English statement

The question asks whether any two birational smooth projective complex Calabi--Yau varieties have equivalent bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves. The meaning of Calabi--Yau and the scope of the surrounding twisted-sheaf assertion require clarification. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved; this draft does not independently verify that assessment.
