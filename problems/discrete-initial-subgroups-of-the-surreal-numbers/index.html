Math-7007

Discrete Initial Subgroups of the Surreal Numbers

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathbf{No}$ denote the ordered field of surreal numbers, with its usual addition, order, and simplicity relation. Write $y\prec_s x$ when the sign expansion of $y$ is a proper initial segment of the sign expansion of $x$. A subclass $S\subseteq\mathbf{No}$ is initial if \[ x\in S\ \text{and}\ y\prec_s x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\in S. \] An additive subgroup $G\subseteq\mathbf{No}$, with the inherited order, is called discrete here if it has a least positive element: \[ \exists u\in G\;\left(u>0\ \land\ \forall g\in G\,(g>0\Rightarrow u\le g)\right). \] This convention excludes the trivial subgroup; including it separately does not affect the question, since it is itself initial and contained in $\mathbf{Oz}$.

The omnific integers $\mathbf{Oz}$ are the surreal numbers whose Conway normal form has only nonnegative exponents and an integer coefficient at exponent zero. Explicitly, if \[ x=\sum_{\alpha<\lambda}r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha}, \] where $\lambda$ is an ordinal, the exponents $a_\alpha\in\mathbf{No}$ strictly decrease, and $r_\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}$, then $x\in\mathbf{Oz}$ precisely when every $a_\alpha\ge0$ and any coefficient with $a_\alpha=0$ is an integer. The empty sum represents zero.

The supplied problem asks: for every discrete initial additive subgroup $G\subseteq\mathbf{No}$, does there exist an initial additive subgroup $H\subseteq\mathbf{Oz}$ isomorphic to $G$? Here initiality of $H$ is understood using the ambient surreal simplicity relation.

There are two precise readings of the unspecified word "isomorphic." The abstract group reading requires a bijection $\phi:G\to H$ satisfying \[ \phi(g_1+g_2)=\phi(g_1)+\phi(g_2) \qquad(g_1,g_2\in G). \] The ordered group reading additionally requires \[ g_1<g_2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\phi(g_1)<\phi(g_2) \qquad(g_1,g_2\in G). \] The supplied record does not explicitly distinguish these readings. It also does not specify whether the quantified subgroups may be proper classes or must be sets. These scope and isomorphism qualifications require source review before a single definitive formalization is selected.

Short English statement

Does every discrete additive subgroup of the surreal numbers that is closed under simpler predecessors have an isomorphic initial copy inside the omnific integers? The source leaves preservation of order and the allowance of proper class subgroups unspecified.
