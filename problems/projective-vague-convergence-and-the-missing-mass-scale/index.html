Math-335

Projective vague convergence and the missing mass scale

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^2$, where $\Gamma$ is a noncocompact arithmetic lattice, and let $V$ be normalized hyperbolic area, $V(X)=1$. The surrounding quantum-chaos setup, recovered from companion records, uses $L^2(V)$-normalized eigenfunctions $\varphi_i$ with Laplace eigenvalues tending to infinity, jointly diagonalizing the relevant invariant differential and Hecke operators, and probabilities \[ {\,\mathrm{d}}\mu_i=|\varphi_i|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V. \] Problem 40 of E. Lindenstrauss asks for the projective convergence \[ \frac{\int_X f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu_i}{\int_X g\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu_i} \longrightarrow \frac{\int_X f\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V}{\int_X g\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V} \qquad(f,g\in C_c(X)). \] The damaged displayed extraction is a ratio, not a product. The necessary convention is $V(g)\ne0$ and $\mu_i(g)\ne0$ for all sufficiently large $i$; an unrestricted statement for every $g$, including zero, is undefined. Here and below $\rho(f)=\int f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\rho$. The source's final "The analog of" is incomplete.

The supplied aid gives a zero-safe formulation valid for probabilities on a locally compact second-countable space. Fix $h\in C_c(X)$ with $h\ge0$, $V(h)>0$, and $\mu_i(h)>0$ eventually. All admissible ratios above are equivalent to \[ \frac{\mu_i}{\mu_i(h)}\longrightarrow\frac{V}{V(h)} \quad\text{vaguely, that is, against every }f\in C_c(X). \] This anchor-normalized convergence forces every ordinary vague cluster measure to be $cV$ for some $0\le c\le1$. Conversely, the condition that every vague cluster point lies in this ray gives the ratios if $\liminf_i\mu_i(h)>0$, excluding the zero-cluster obstruction. Anchor-normalized convergence together with either $\mu_i(h)\to V(h)$ or tightness of $(\mu_i)$ gives full probability convergence $\mu_i\to V$.

The reported quantitative ratio propagation is explicit. Put \[ A_i=\frac{\mu_i(f)}{\mu_i(h)},\quad B_i=\frac{\mu_i(g)}{\mu_i(h)},\quad a=\frac{V(f)}{V(h)},\quad b=\frac{V(g)}{V(h)}\ne0. \] If $|A_i-a|\le\varepsilon_f$ and $|B_i-b|\le\varepsilon_g\le|b|/2$, then, also for signed or complex tests, \[ \left|\frac{A_i}{B_i}-\frac ab\right| \le\frac{2\varepsilon_f}{|b|} +\frac{2|a|\varepsilon_g}{|b|^2}. \] The aid emphasizes that ratios do not recover the missing mass scale. For points $x_i$ leaving every compact set and fixed $0<c<1$, probabilities $cV+(1-c)\delta_{x_i}$ have the required ratios but vague limit $cV$. Similarly, $\varepsilon_i\rho+(1-\varepsilon_i)\delta_{x_i}$ with $\varepsilon_i\downarrow0$ and a fixed probability $\rho$ not proportional to $V$ have only the zero vague cluster, while their local ratios can reflect $\rho$ rather than $V$. The assertion that all nonzero cluster points lie on the $V$-ray would then be vacuous. These measure-theoretic equivalences and examples do not themselves prove arithmetic QUE or no escape for the eigenfunction sequence.

Short English statement

The intended problem asks whether compact-test ratios for arithmetic quantum measures approach the corresponding volume ratios. One positive reference test makes this precise, but ratios determine only a common scale; recovering total mass still requires an anchor-mass limit or no escape.
