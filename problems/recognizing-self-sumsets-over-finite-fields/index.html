Math-5220

Recognizing self-sumsets over finite fields

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A. Granville asks how to decide, in reasonable time, whether a given finite-field set is a sumset of another set. The aid interprets this as self-sumset recognition: given $S\subseteq F$, decide whether $S=A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}$ for some $A\subseteq F$. Repeated summands are allowed. A two-independent-set problem $A+B$, or a restricted sumset forbidding $a=b$, would be different.

Use an explicit list of target elements with polynomial-time field addition/comparison. For complexity claims in fixed characteristic $p$, let the additive dimension $d$ of $F\cong{\mathbb{F}}_p^d$ grow. The aid reports primary-literature NP-completeness over the integers and, for every fixed prime $p$, over these growing vector spaces. This is a worst-case input-complexity statement, not a proof that no efficient algorithm exists on every structured input. It also does not by itself state the complexity of prime fields with $d=1$ and varying $p$. No full reference for the NP-completeness theorem is supplied.

The support-sensitive exact algorithm follows two reported normalizations. In odd characteristic, $2a\in S$ for every root element, so \[ A\subseteq\tfrac12S=\{s/2:s\in S\}. \] Enumerating these candidate subsets and checking their self-sums is exact. In characteristic two, a nonempty root can be translated so $0\in A$ without changing $A+A$; then $A\subseteq S$. Thus reject a nonempty target not containing zero, and otherwise enumerate subsets of $S$ containing zero. For $s=|S|$, this gives at most $2^s$ candidates in odd characteristic and $2^{s-1}$ normalized candidates in characteristic two, each checked using polynomially many support operations. This is exponential in target support size, not a polynomial worst-case algorithm. The empty target is realized by the empty root.

The aid completely classifies nonempty targets of size at most four:

  • In characteristic two, exactly $\{0\}$, sets $\{0,u\}$ with $u\neq0$, and two-dimensional ${\mathbb{F}}_2$-linear subspaces occur. Their sizes are respectively one, two and four; size three is impossible.
  • In odd characteristic, exactly singletons and nondegenerate three-term arithmetic progressions $\{b-d,b,b+d\}$ with $d\neq0$ occur. Their sizes are one and three; sizes two and four are impossible.
For odd-characteristic progressions a two-point root is $\{(b-d)/2,(b+d)/2\}$. In characteristic two the displayed subspaces are self-sumsets themselves. These small-support classifications and the enumeration procedure coexist with the reported hardness when support and dimension are unbounded.

Short English statement

The aid reads the question as deciding whether a set is $A+A$. It reports worst-case NP-completeness for growing additive dimension, but gives exact enumeration depending only exponentially on target size. It also lists every possible self-sumset of size at most four, with different answers in characteristic two and odd characteristic.
