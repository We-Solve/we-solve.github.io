Weak Duality Proof Complexity and Strongly Polynomial Linear Programming

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks two related questions about weak linear-programming duality and strongly polynomial circuits for linear programming. It does not specify enough encoding or computational conventions to determine unique formal statements of either question.

For orientation, a conventional real formulation of weak duality is the following. Let $m,n\geq 1$, $A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}$, $b\in{\mathbb{R}}^m$, $c\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$, $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$, and $y\in{\mathbb{R}}^m$. All vector inequalities are coordinatewise. Then \[ \left(x\geq 0\ \land\ Ax\leq b\ \land\ y\geq 0\ \land\ A^{\mathsf T}y\geq c\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ c^{\mathsf T}x\leq b^{\mathsf T}y. \] Here $x$ and $y$ are feasible solutions of the corresponding primal and dual programs. This is an explicitly illustrative convention: the source record does not specify the coefficient domain, primal--dual convention, or encoding intended by its phrase "variable-matrix form." In particular, treating the entries of $A$ as variables introduces products of matrix entries and solution coordinates, whose representation in a proof system must be specified.

  1. Does the intended variable-matrix weak-duality family $\mathrm{WD}$ have no polynomial-size proof family in the Lovász--Schrijver system, abbreviated LS? To express the requested lower bound precisely after the missing conventions are fixed, let $\mathrm{WD}_N$ denote an encoded instance family indexed by its specified size parameter $N\geq 1$, and let $s_{\mathrm{LS}}(\mathrm{WD}_N)$ be its minimum proof size, with value $+\infty$ if no proof exists. The proposed absence of a polynomial bound would mean \[ \neg\exists C>0\ \exists k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\ \forall N\geq 1:\quad s_{\mathrm{LS}}(\mathrm{WD}_N)\leq C N^k. \] This is a conditional formalization template. The supplied record does not define $\mathrm{WD}_N$, $N$, the LS variant, the proof-size measure, or whether proofs of implications or refutations of encoded counterexamples are intended.
  2. Do strongly polynomial circuits for linear programming exist? The intended circuit family must solve the specified class of linear-programming instances under a specified input and output representation. The phrase "strongly polynomial" suggests a resource bound polynomial in the numbers of variables and constraints, independent of the numerical magnitudes of the data. However, the record does not specify the allowed gates, uniformity requirements, treatment of numerical representation, required output, or handling of infeasible and unbounded instances. Consequently, it does not determine a precise circuit-existence assertion.

The supplied background associates the first question with the unresolved existence of strongly polynomial algorithms for general linear programming. It supplies no precise reduction or equivalence, and it does not identify circuits with algorithms. Short English statement

The problem asks whether weak duality with a variable constraint matrix requires more than polynomial-size Lovász--Schrijver proofs, and whether strongly polynomial circuits can solve linear programming. Precise statements require missing choices about encodings, proof systems, circuit models, and outputs. The supplied open status requires independent review.
