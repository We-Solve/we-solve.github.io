Definitions and mathematical statement

For notation, let $k$ denote a base field and let ${\mathbb{P}}^n_k$ denote projective $n$-space over $k$. A projective hypersurface is the zero locus $V(F)\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^n_k$ of a nonzero homogeneous polynomial $F\in k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ of positive degree. These conventions introduce notation only: the supplied record does not specify the base field, its characteristic, the ambient dimensions, or the degrees under consideration.

Research question (catalogue entry). What general geometric or algebraic mechanism accounts for examples of unexpected projective varieties, including unexpected projective hypersurfaces?

The question asks for a general explanation of the origin of unexpected varieties and hypersurfaces in projective space. The supplied record leaves both "unexpected" and the required scope of an explanation undefined, so a precise theorem-like target remains unresolved.
