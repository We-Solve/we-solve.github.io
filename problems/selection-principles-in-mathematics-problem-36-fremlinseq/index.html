Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a topological space $X$, let $C_p(X)$ denote the space of continuous functions $X\to{\mathbb{R}}$, equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence, and let $\mathbf{0}$ denote its zero function. Using the usual convergence-set convention, let $\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}$ consist of the infinite sets $A\subseteq C_p(X)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$ such that every neighborhood of $\mathbf{0}$ contains all but finitely many members of $A$. Equivalently, for each finite $F\subseteq X$ and each $\varepsilon>0$, all but finitely many $f\in A$ satisfy $|f(x)|<\varepsilon$ for every $x\in F$.

The property $\mathsf{S}_1(\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}},\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}})$ means that for every sequence $(A_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ of members of $\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}$, there are choices $f_n\in A_n$ such that $\{f_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}$. These definitions explain the notation under a standard convention; the supplied record does not explicitly give the definitions or the underlying category of spaces.

For $f,g\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}$, write $f\leq^*g$ if $f(n)\leq g(n)$ for all but finitely many $n$. The bounding number is \[ \mathfrak b=\min\left\{|B|:B\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}},\ (\forall g\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{N}}})(\exists f\in B)\ f\not\leq^*g\right\}. \]

The supplied Problem 36 asks: assuming \[ \kappa<\mathfrak b \quad\text{and}\quad (\forall\alpha<\lambda)\; C_p(X_\alpha)\text{ satisfies }\mathsf{S}_1(\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}},\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}), \] must \[ C_p\!\left(\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}X_\alpha\right) \text{ satisfy }\mathsf{S}_1(\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}},\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}})? \] The hypothesis is indexed by $\lambda$, whereas the union is indexed by $\kappa$. The supplied record neither defines $\lambda$ nor relates it to $\kappa$; this discrepancy is retained and prevents a unique precise interpretation. It also does not specify the class of spaces $X_\alpha$ or the topology on their union. If they are subspaces of a common topological space, a subspace topology on the union would supply such a topology, but this ambient-space hypothesis is not supplied. No identification of $\lambda$ with $\kappa$, or choice of ambient category, is asserted here.

Short English statement

The question asks whether taking a union of fewer than $\mathfrak b$ spaces preserves the stated selection property of their pointwise continuous-function spaces. The source uses different index bounds in the hypothesis and conclusion and leaves the topology of the union unspecified, so this intended preservation question requires clarification. The registry labels it open; that label is supplied information, not an independently verified assessment.
