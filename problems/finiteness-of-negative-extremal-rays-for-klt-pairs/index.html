Math-6671

Finiteness of Negative Extremal Rays for KLT Pairs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Put $D=K_X+\Delta$. A Kawamata log terminal (klt) pair consists, in the usual effective-boundary convention, of a normal variety $X$ and an effective boundary divisor $\Delta$, with $D$ ${\mathbb{R}}$-Cartier, whose discrepancies are all strictly greater than $-1$. The source assumes $\kappa(X,\Delta)\geq 0$, understood as the nonnegativity of the Kodaira dimension of $D$. For a ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier divisor, this is the usual Iitaka dimension defined using integral Cartier multiples. The source does not specify the boundary coefficient class or a convention for Kodaira dimension beyond that setting.

In the absolute projective setting, let $N_1(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}$ be the real vector space of one-cycles modulo numerical equivalence, and let $\overline{\mathrm{NE}}(X)$ be the closure of the cone generated by classes of integral curves. A ray $R\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{NE}}(X)$ is extremal if $\alpha+\beta\in R$, with $\alpha,\beta\in\overline{\mathrm{NE}}(X)$, implies $\alpha,\beta\in R$. It is $D$-negative if $D\cdot\gamma<0$ for every nonzero $\gamma\in R$.

Source conjecture, with unresolved ambient hypotheses. For every klt pair $(X,\Delta)$ satisfying $\kappa(X,\Delta)\geq0$ to which the intended cone theorem applies, do there exist $m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}$ and integral curves $C_1,\ldots,C_m\subseteq X$ such that \[ \left\{R\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{NE}}(X):R\text{ is a }D\text{-negative extremal ray}\right\} =\left\{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq0}[C_i]:1\leq i\leq m\right\}? \] Here the set on the right is empty when $m=0$. Under the curve-generation conclusion of the intended cone theorem, this asks whether there are only finitely many $D$-negative extremal rays.

The projective notation above describes the absolute interpretation; the source does not explicitly specify projectivity, a ground field, characteristic, dimension, or whether a relative cone theorem is intended. These are unresolved hypotheses, so this is a conditional reconstruction of the stated target. The supplied background also mentions a restricted smooth threefold problem without stating its full hypotheses; it does not provide a precise replacement for the general conjecture.

Short English statement

The source asks whether a klt pair of nonnegative log Kodaira dimension has only finitely many negative extremal rays, each represented by one of finitely many curves. Its ambient hypotheses require clarification. Supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and reports failure for general klt pairs while mentioning restricted threefold cases; those claims are not independently verified here.
