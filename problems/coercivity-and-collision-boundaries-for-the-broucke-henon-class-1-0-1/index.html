Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. For a planar collision-free periodic loop, identify the plane with $\mathbb C$ and define $k_{ij}=\deg(t\mapsto(q_i(t)-q_j(t))/|q_i(t)-q_j(t)|)\in\mathbb Z$. These pairwise winding numbers describe homology information; they do not determine the full braid homotopy class. The Broucke--Hénon orbit is the named planar periodic family shown in the source. Its class $(1,0,1)$ is the ordered triple of pairwise windings used there. Small and big masses concern mass ratios, not scaling all masses together.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Prove existence of the Broucke--Hénon orbit for the planar three-body problem. Determine whether such orbits exist for the source’s small-plus-big mass regimes. Determine whether the Broucke--Hénon solution minimizes action in class $(1,0,1)$.
Scope and source qualification.
All three questions remain separate. The source gives numerical and symmetry observations, including failure of coercivity from D2 symmetry alone; these are not an existence or global-minimization proof. Coercivity in the specified winding class.
Take the pair ordering $(k_{12},k_{13},k_{23})=(1,0,1)$, explicitly matching the named pairs for this calculation. Set $K=\int_0^P\tfrac12\sum_im_i|\dot q_i|^2dt$. For either nonzero-winding pair, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives \[\|\dot q_i-\dot q_j\|_2^2\le2(1/m_i+1/m_j)K. \] A nonzero-degree loop $r=q_i-q_j$ must have its mean within its oscillation radius; otherwise it lies in a disk avoiding zero and has degree zero. Since its oscillation about the mean is at most $\sqrt P\|\dot r\|_2$, \[\sup_t|q_i-q_j|\le2\sqrt{2P(1/m_i+1/m_j)K}. \] Apply this to pairs 12 and 23, then use $q_1-q_3=(q_1-q_2)+(q_2-q_3)$. The center-of-mass relation bounds all positions. Thus an action-bounded sequence cannot escape spatially for fixed positive masses.
The mass-ratio and collision limitations.
The constants diverge as a mass tends to zero. They therefore give no uniform compactness across the source's small-plus-big regimes. Moreover finite action permits $r\sim|t-t_0|^{2/3}$ binary collision behavior; both $|\dot r|^2$ and $1/|r|$ are locally integrable. A weak limit can lose its winding class at such a collision even though spatial escape is excluded.
Gap.
A direct minimization proof still needs a collision-exclusion argument and identification of the resulting orbit with the Broucke--Henon geometry. Neither follows from coercivity, and even existence would not settle whether the named orbit globally minimizes the class for every relevant mass ratio. The source's D2-symmetry warning is respected: the estimate here uses the actual winding constraints, not symmetry alone. All three source questions remain unresolved beyond this elementary bound.
