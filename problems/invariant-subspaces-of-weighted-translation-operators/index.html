Math-3339

Invariant Subspaces of Weighted Translation Operators

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\omega:{\mathbb{Z}}\to(0,\infty)$ satisfy, for some finite $c\geq1$, \[ c^{-1}\leq\frac{\omega(n+1)}{\omega(n)}\leq c\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}). \] Use the weighted Hilbert-space convention \[ \ell^2_\omega({\mathbb{Z}})=\left\{a:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}:\|a\|_\omega^2=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a(n)|^2\omega(n)^2<\infty\right\}. \] Define the bilateral translation $(Ta)(n)=a(n-1)$. The ratio bounds make $T$ bounded and invertible, with $\|T\|,\|T^{-1}\|\leq c$; translation invariance of the ambient space does not mean that translations are isometries.

On the usual all-integer-translations reading, the question is whether for every such $\omega$ there exists a closed complex linear subspace $Y$ such that \[ \{0\}\subsetneq Y\subsetneq\ell^2_\omega({\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad T^kY\subseteq Y\quad\text{for every }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Equivalently, $TY=Y$. Closedness is essential to the usual invariant-subspace question. Invariance only under $T$, with no condition on $T^{-1}$, is a weaker and distinct formulation; the report's precise use of translation-invariant should be confirmed.

Some conventions put $\omega(n)$ rather than $\omega(n)^2$ in the norm. The definition above makes the chosen convention explicit; replacing the weight by its square root translates between the two, preserving the class of positive bounded-ratio weights, though not the name of each individual weight. No symmetry, subexponential growth, or extra regularity is imposed.

The supplied assessment checked 2026-08-21 reports that the universal existence question remains open, with positive results for important subclasses of admissible weights. The subclasses and exact hypotheses are not reproduced.

Short English statement

Does every positive bounded-ratio weight on ${\mathbb{Z}}$ give a weighted square-summable sequence space with a nonzero proper closed subspace invariant under all integer translations? The norm and two-sided invariance conventions are stated explicitly.
