Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 8.22

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a bounded smoothly bounded domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, a standard inner-function convention is a bounded holomorphic function $f:\Omega\to\mathbb D$ whose non-tangential boundary values satisfy $|f^*(\zeta)|=1$ for surface-almost every $\zeta\in\partial\Omega$. The intended function must be nonconstant; otherwise unimodular constants give a trivial answer under definitions that allow them. This convention requires a suitable boundary and boundary-value measure, not specified in the source.

Under a $C^2$ defining-function convention, strict pseudoconvexity means that locally $\Omega=\{\rho<0\}$, $d\rho\ne0$ on the boundary, and \[ \sum_{j,k=1}^n\frac{\partial^2\rho}{\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k}(\zeta) v_j\overline{v_k}>0 \] for every nonzero complex tangent vector $v$ at each boundary point $\zeta$. The source does not state boundedness or boundary regularity, so this supplies a standard version rather than silently narrowing "any strictly pseudoconvex domain."

Question (R. Zeinstra). For every strictly pseudoconvex domain $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^n$, $n\geq2$, in the intended domain and boundary-value class, does there exist a nonconstant inner function? In the smooth bounded version this asks for \[ \exists f\in\mathcal O(\Omega),\quad |f|<1\text{ on }\Omega,\quad f\text{ nonconstant},\quad |f^*|=1\text{ surface-almost everywhere on }\partial\Omega. \] The universal quantifier is over all domains of the intended type, not merely balls or one favorable subclass.

The source reports existence for the ball, attributed to Alexandrov and independently to Løw and to Hakim--Sibony. The research summary likewise marks the ball solved (mentioning $n\geq1$ for that result), reports positive results for further domains under additional conditions, and leaves the fully general strictly pseudoconvex case open. Its mixed "SOLVED-IN-LITERATURE" and partial-progress wording is interpreted only at those stated scopes; it supplies neither a precise general-domain theorem nor hypotheses for the further positive cases. No scope is enlarged here.

Short English statement

Does every strictly pseudoconvex domain in ${\mathbb{C}}^n$, $n\geq2$, admit a nonconstant holomorphic inner function? The record reports affirmative results for the ball and some other classes while leaving the fully general question open. Its boundary regularity and boundary-value conventions are unspecified.
