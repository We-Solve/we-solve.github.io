Question 2 — What are the equivalence classes of braids modulo the moves • ab↔ba, and • b↔σnι(b)?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $B_n$ be the Artin braid group on $n$ strands with generators $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}$, and let $\iota:B_n\to B_{n+1}$ be the standard inclusion adding a trivial strand. On the disjoint union of these groups, take the equivalence relation generated by \[ ab\longleftrightarrow ba\quad(a,b\in B_n), \qquad b\longleftrightarrow\sigma_n\iota(b)\quad(b\in B_n). \] The usual braid relations are already imposed inside each $B_n$. The second relation includes positive destabilization as its inverse. The negative stabilization/destabilization $b\leftrightarrow\sigma_n^{-1}\iota(b)$ is omitted.

Question 2 asks to classify the resulting equivalence classes. The source reports [OS03] identifying this problem with transverse-link classification up to transverse isotopy in the intended contact-geometric setting. A transverse link is everywhere transverse to the contact planes, and transverse isotopy stays within that class; the ambient contact manifold, positivity and closure conventions are not stated here and must be recovered.

Let $e:B_n\to{\mathbb{Z}}$ be exponent sum, with $e(\sigma_i)=1$. The source defines the Bennequin number \[ \beta(b)=e(b)-n \] and states its invariance under the two retained moves. It notes that this separates some ordinary-Markov-equivalent braids. It further reports independent examples of Birman--Menasco [BM] and Etnyre--Honda [EH] that are ordinary-Markov-equivalent and have equal Bennequin number but are inequivalent under the retained moves. The example words and detailed hypotheses are not supplied. Besides full classification, the source asks for a new invariant distinguishing those example pairs, motivated by the complexity of their existing proofs; it does not assert that the Bennequin number is complete.

Short English statement

Classify braids under cyclic/conjugacy moves and positive Markov stabilization only, the source's transverse-link equivalence. The Bennequin number is invariant but the cited pairs show it does not classify everything; find a further invariant for those pairs as well.
