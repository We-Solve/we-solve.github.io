Boundaries of Groups and Kleinian Groups — Problem 4

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A group $G$ is word hyperbolic if it is finitely generated and a Cayley graph for a finite generating set has uniformly thin geodesic triangles. It is torsion free if its identity is its only finite-order element. For $R={\mathbb{Z}}$ or ${\mathbb{Q}}$, define the cohomological dimension \[ \operatorname{cd}_R(G)=\operatorname{pd}_{RG}R, \] the projective dimension of the trivial $RG$-module $R$.

Mike Davis's question is whether there exists a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ for which \[ \frac{\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)}{\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G)}<\frac23. \] The quotient is read with a finite positive denominator, excluding the trivial-group $0/0$ case. Equivalently in this finite-dimensional setting, the desired strict inequality is \[ 3\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)<2\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(G). \] No weakening to a non-strict inequality, removal of torsion freeness, or replacement of the coefficient rings is intended.

Short English statement

Can the rational cohomological dimension of a torsion-free hyperbolic group be less than two-thirds of its integral cohomological dimension? The supplied summary reports no accessible resolution and qualitatively connects the threshold to boundary and topological-dimension phenomena, without stating a precise theorem. Its OPEN-TRIAGE label is a supplied assessment. The 2005 source lists the problem, and provenance marks current status for review.
