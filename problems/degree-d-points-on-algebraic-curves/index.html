Math-4977

Degree-$d$ Points on Algebraic Curves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $K$ be a number field and let $X$ be a smooth projective curve over $K$, as specified in the supplied statement. For a closed point $P\in X$, let $\kappa(P)$ denote its residue field and define its degree over $K$ by \[ \deg_K(P)=[\kappa(P):K]. \] For an integer $d$, put \[ S_d(X/K)=\{P\in X:\ P\text{ is a closed point and }[\kappa(P):K]=d\}. \] Under this interpretation, $S_d(X/K)=\varnothing$ for $d\leq 0$.

Problem. For each number field $K$, smooth projective curve $X/K$, and positive integer $d$, determine whether $S_d(X/K)$ is finite or infinite; that is, characterize the inputs $(K,X,d)$ for which infinitely many closed points have degree exactly $d$.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not explicitly define a degree-$d$ point; the formulation above uses closed points of exact residue-field degree, rather than effective divisors of degree $d$ or points of degree at most $d$. The original wording uses "nice curve," whereas the supplied cleaned statement specifies smoothness and projectivity without explicitly requiring geometric integrality or connectedness. No such additional hypothesis or genus restriction is imposed here. The source asks for a finiteness classification but supplies no proposed criterion or precise required form of that classification.

Short English statement

For a smooth projective curve over a number field and a positive integer $d$, determine when there are finitely or infinitely many closed points whose residue fields have degree exactly $d$. The meaning of "degree-$d$ point" and the hypotheses implicit in the original phrase "nice curve" require confirmation.
