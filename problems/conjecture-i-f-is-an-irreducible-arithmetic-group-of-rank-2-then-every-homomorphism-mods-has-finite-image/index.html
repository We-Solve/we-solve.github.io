Math-478

Conjecture I — f Γ is an irreducible arithmetic group of rank ≥ 2, then every homomorphism Γ→ ModS has finite image.

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Gamma$ be an irreducible arithmetic group of rank at least $2$, in the source's higher-rank lattice setting, and let $\operatorname{Mod}_S$ be the mapping class group of the intended surface $S$. Surface type, marking/boundary conventions, and the precise ambient rank convention are not specified. The rank must be checked in that setting rather than silently interpreted as ${\mathbb{Q}}$-rank; the discussion includes cocompact lattices in $SU(p,q)$ among its motivating cases.

Conjecture I, reported in the source as completely proved. Every homomorphism $\rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Mod}_S$ has finite image: \[ \forall\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,\operatorname{Mod}_S), \qquad |\rho(\Gamma)|<\infty. \] No injectivity or other representation hypothesis is imposed. The initial extracted "f" is read as the missing beginning of "If."

The source says elementary combinations of arithmetic-group and mapping-class-group facts prove many cases, but notes cocompact $SU(p,q)$ lattices as a case where that approach appeared to fail, crediting G. Prasad for the example. It then expressly says the full conjecture has been proved. It credits V. A. Kaimanovich and H. Masur [KaM] with showing that nonelementary mapping-class subgroups are not isomorphic to irreducible arithmetic groups of rank at least $2$. Here nonelementary is described as containing two pseudo-Anosov elements with disjoint fixed-point sets in the Thurston boundary. The proof uses random walks and H. Furstenberg's arithmetic-group results; combined with Margulis's finiteness theorem and Ivanov's techniques, the source says it proves the conjecture. It also credits B. Farb and H. Masur [FM] with a different proof.

The source further credits S-K. Yeung [Y] with finite image for homomorphisms from lattices in $\operatorname{Sp}(m,1)$ or $F_4^{-20}$, the latter associated with the Cayley plane. The admissible range of $m$ and exact hypotheses are absent and must be checked; no unrestricted rank-one extension is inferred. It notes that lattices in remaining simple rank-one Lie groups often have nontrivial homomorphisms to ${\mathbb{Z}}$, and hence can yield infinite-image mapping-class homomorphisms in the intended surface setting. "Often" is not replaced by "every.

Short English statement

Every homomorphism from an irreducible higher-rank arithmetic group to a mapping class group should have finite image. The source explicitly reports this conjecture completely proved and gives several proof attributions; an editorial summary incorrectly labels it still open.
