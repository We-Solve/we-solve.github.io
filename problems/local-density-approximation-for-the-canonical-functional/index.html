Testing projection from fluctuating particle number.
A proposed route to the canonical functional is to build a grand-canonical local-density trial state and project to the desired total number. Even for two disjoint one-mode cells this changes the local density. Let independent occupations $X_1,X_2\in\{0,1\}$ have probabilities $p_1=1/4$, $p_2=3/4$. Their expected total is one, but \[ \mathbb P(X_1=1,X_2=0)=1/16,\quad \mathbb P(X_1=0,X_2=1)=9/16. \] Conditioning on $X_1+X_2=1$ gives local means $1/10$ and $9/10$, instead of $1/4$ and $3/4$. These probabilities define legitimate diagonal fermionic density matrices, so the obstruction is present without coherences or interactions.
A repair in the finite example.
Correlating the original cells instead, use the canonical mixture \[ \Gamma=\tfrac14|10\rangle\langle10|+\tfrac34|01\rangle\langle01|. \] It has exactly one particle and the prescribed local means. For a diagonal one-body energy $h_1X_1+h_2X_2$, its expectation equals the independent state's energy. For an interaction $JX_1X_2$, however, the expectations are respectively zero and $3J/16$. Thus exact density repair is possible here but changes correlations and interaction energy in a controlled, explicitly nonzero way.
Remaining canonical estimate.
The continuum constrained search must preserve the entire density, not just its integral. The first missing step is a number-correlation or density-repair construction with kinetic and interaction costs negligible in the source local-density scaling. This two-cell calculation supplies neither that uniform estimate nor an asymptotic local-density theorem, and no grand-canonical result is substituted for the canonical target.
