Kourovka Notebook Problem 7.3

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$, let $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ be odd with $n\geq 665$, and let $F_1,\ldots,F_k$ be nontrivial groups containing no involutions. An involution is an element of order exactly $2$. Thus, for each $i$, the group $F_i$ has an element other than its identity, and $x^2=1$ implies $x=1$ for every $x\in F_i$.

Write \[ P=\operatorname{Per}_n(F_1,\ldots,F_k) \] for the periodic product of exponent parameter $n$ intended in S. I. Adian's problem. This notation names the source construction; the supplied record does not define that construction. In particular, no exponent assumption on the individual groups $F_i$ is stated, and no such assumption is added here. A complete definition of this periodic product must be supplied before this draft is a self-contained formulation.

For a group $G$ and a subset $X\subseteq G$, let $\langle X\rangle$ denote the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $X$. The requested assertion, subject to the construction qualification above, is that for every choice of $k,n,F_1,\ldots,F_k$ satisfying the stated hypotheses, \[ \forall X\subseteq P,\qquad |X|<k\ \Longrightarrow\ \langle X\rangle\neq P. \] Here $\langle\varnothing\rangle=\{1\}$, so the assertion for $k=1$ says that $P$ is nontrivial. The assertion is a lower bound on the number of generators; it does not assert that $P$ itself is generated by $k$ elements.

The source additionally states that this assertion, together with the cited work of Adian from 1978, would imply \[ \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \exists S\quad \left\{ \begin{aligned} &S\text{ is a nontrivial simple group},\\ &\exists Y\subseteq S:\ |Y|=k\text{ and }\langle Y\rangle=S,\\ &\forall X\subseteq S:\ |X|<k\Longrightarrow\langle X\rangle\neq S. \end{aligned} \right. \] A nontrivial group is simple if its only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. This implication is reported as a source qualification; its supporting hypotheses and argument are not supplied in the record.

Short English statement

Adian asks whether every periodic product with odd exponent parameter $n\geq665$ of $k$ nontrivial groups without involutions requires at least $k$ generators. The source says this would yield simple groups requiring exactly $k$ generators for every positive integer $k$. The intended periodic product needs a complete definition from the source literature.
