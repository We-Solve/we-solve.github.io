Nonarithmetic Lattices in Higher-Dimensional Complex Hyperbolic Space

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n$ be an integer with $n\geq 3$, and put $J=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)$. Define \[ G_n=\operatorname{SU}(n,1)=\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}({\mathbb{C}}):A^*JA=J\}, \] where $A^*$ denotes the conjugate transpose. Complex hyperbolic space $\mathbb H_{{\mathbb{C}}}^n$ is the space of negative complex lines for this Hermitian form, equipped with its complex hyperbolic metric. Its holomorphic isometry group is \[ \operatorname{PU}(n,1)\cong G_n/Z(G_n),\qquad Z(G_n)=\{\zeta I:\zeta^{n+1}=1\}. \] The full isometry group also contains antiholomorphic isometries. Thus the source's displayed identification of $\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H_{{\mathbb{C}}}^n)$ with $\operatorname{SU}(n,1)$ requires correction: the holomorphic group is a finite central quotient of $G_n$, and the full isometry group is larger.

A subgroup $\Gamma\leq G_n$ is a lattice if it is discrete and $\Gamma\backslash G_n$ has finite Haar volume. No compactness of this quotient is required. Two subgroups of the same group are commensurable if their intersection has finite index in each.

Use the following conventional arithmeticity definition. A lattice $\Gamma\leq G_n$ is arithmetic if there exist a connected semisimple algebraic group $\mathbf H$ over ${\mathbb{Q}}$, a faithful rational matrix representation $\mathbf H\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_m$, a subgroup $\Lambda\leq\mathbf H({\mathbb{Q}})$ commensurable with $\mathbf H({\mathbb{Q}})\cap\operatorname{GL}_m({\mathbb{Z}})$, and a surjective Lie group homomorphism \[ \phi:\mathbf H({\mathbb{R}})^\circ\longrightarrow G_n \] with compact kernel, such that $\Gamma$ is commensurable with $\phi(\Lambda\cap\mathbf H({\mathbb{R}})^\circ)$. Here the superscript $\circ$ denotes the identity component. A lattice is nonarithmetic if it is not arithmetic. Passing between $G_n$ and its finite central quotient preserves the lattice existence question and arithmeticity.

Question, interpreted dimension by dimension. For each integer $n\geq3$, determine whether there exists a nonarithmetic lattice in $G_n$; equivalently, determine the set \[ \left\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:n\geq3\text{ and there exists a nonarithmetic lattice }\Gamma\leq G_n\right\}. \] The source does not explicitly distinguish a question for every dimension from a question asking for an example in some dimension $n\geq3$. The dimension-by-dimension interpretation above is supported by its supplied literature assessment and remains a qualification requiring review.

The supplied curation labels the problem partially solved: it reports examples in $\operatorname{SU}(2,1)$ and $\operatorname{SU}(3,1)$, and reports existence in dimensions $n\geq4$ as open. Thus its stated remaining target is the same existence question for each integer $n\geq4$. These are supplied dated assessments, not independently verified mathematical or current-status claims.

Short English statement

For each complex dimension $n\geq3$, ask whether complex hyperbolic space admits a nonarithmetic lattice, meaning a discrete group of finite covolume that does not arise from the arithmetic construction specified above. The supplied assessment reports dimension $3$ as resolved and dimensions $n\geq4$ as open. The source's isometry-group notation and intended quantifier require the qualifications recorded here.
