The all-weights strategy for Littlewood's conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

This record is a transition in the discussion of Littlewood's Conjecture 28, not a standalone conjecture. It says that the conjecture holds outside each $C_s$ and suggests trying to prove $\bigcap_{0\leq s\leq1}C_s=\varnothing$; the next conjecture and preceding definition are absent. The aid reconstructs the literal weighted definition as follows, with $\|x\|$ distance to ${\mathbb{Z}}$: \[ C_s=\left\{(\alpha,\beta):\inf_{q\geq1} \max\{q^s\|q\alpha\|,q^{1-s}\|q\beta\|\}>0\right\},\qquad0\leq s\leq1, \] where $q$ is integer. Define $\mathrm{Bad}=\{\xi:\inf_{q\geq1}q\|q\xi\|>0\}$.

The aid asserts that $(\alpha,\beta)\notin C_s$ implies Littlewood: \[ \liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|=0. \] For an unbounded sequence making the weighted maximum tend to zero, its square bounds this product. If only bounded denominators witness infimum zero, both coordinates are rational and multiples of a common denominator give the required unbounded sequence. Thus the finite-infimum formulation is not silently confused with a limit at infinity.

The endpoint identities under this literal formula are \[ C_0={\mathbb{R}}\times\mathrm{Bad},\qquad C_1=\mathrm{Bad}\times{\mathbb{R}}. \] The aid's quantitative multiples lemma says that for $0<\delta=q\|q\beta\|\leq1$, some $Q=kq$ satisfies \[ \max\{\|Q\alpha\|,Q\|Q\beta\|\}\leq2\delta^{1/3}. \] A suitable $k$ can be chosen by a one-dimensional pigeonhole approximation with size on the order of $\delta^{-1/3}$. If $\delta=0$, one instead uses multiples with vanishing second error and arbitrarily small first error; no finite exact-zero conclusion is inferred for irrational $\alpha$. Swapping coordinates gives the other endpoint.

For a finite integer scale $Q\geq1$, put \[ c_s(Q)=\min_{1\leq q\leq Q}\max\{q^s\|q\alpha\|,q^{1-s}\|q\beta\|\}. \] The stated weight-window estimate is \[ Q^{-|s-t|}c_s(Q)\leq c_t(Q)\leq Q^{|s-t|}c_s(Q). \] Thus finite-scale continuity in the weight is quantitative, but its constants deteriorate with $Q$. The aid notes that results on countable weighted intersections do not automatically determine the uncountable intersection, and a point lying in all rational-weight sets need not be promoted to all weights without the needed uniform bounds. Emptiness of the all-weights intersection would suffice for Littlewood by the first implication; it is not proved here or asserted equivalent to the conjecture.

Short English statement

The supplied aid makes the weighted-set transition precise, checks both zero-weight endpoints and the unbounded-denominator issue, and identifies the uniformity missing from a countable-to-all-weights passage. It does not prove the proposed empty-intersection strategy.
