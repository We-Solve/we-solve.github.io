Invariant Analytic Jordan Curves of Rational Maps

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}$ be the Riemann sphere. An analytic Jordan curve $\Gamma\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}$ is understood here to be the image of a real-analytic embedding of the circle into the sphere, with nonvanishing derivative in local coordinates. A rational self-map of the sphere is given by a rational function, interpreted at poles and at infinity.

For a specified class $\mathcal R$ of rational self-maps, define \[ \mathcal I(\mathcal R)=\left\{\Gamma\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}:\ \Gamma\text{ is an analytic Jordan curve and }\exists R\in\mathcal R\text{ with }R(\Gamma)=\Gamma\right\}. \] Under the convention that invariant means setwise invariant, the classification question in the supplied record is: characterize $\mathcal I(\mathcal R)$. Its additional question asks whether such invariant analytic Jordan curves can occur outside the examples described in the record as arising from rotation domains or Lattès functions.

Unresolved qualifications. The record does not specify the admissible degrees of $R$, so it does not determine $\mathcal R$. In particular, allowing the identity map makes every analytic Jordan curve invariant; a restriction such as $\deg R\geq2$ would be an additional hypothesis requiring confirmation. The record also does not specify whether invariance means $R(\Gamma)=\Gamma$, merely $R(\Gamma)\subseteq\Gamma$, or invariance under an iterate. The displayed formulation explicitly uses the first convention. Finally, the phrase "arising from rotation domains or Lattès functions" does not define a class of curves or pairs $(R,\Gamma)$, or the allowed constructions and equivalences. Consequently, the exceptional-example question cannot be given a definite quantified formulation from this record alone. The definition of analytic Jordan curve above is an explicit regularity convention that also requires confirmation against the intended source usage.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks which analytic simple closed curves on the Riemann sphere can be preserved by rational maps, and whether examples occur beyond those associated with rotation domains or Lattès functions. A precise final formulation requires the allowed map degrees, the meaning of invariance, and the meaning of those two families of examples. The supplied open label is a dated curation assessment, not an independently verified mathematical conclusion.
