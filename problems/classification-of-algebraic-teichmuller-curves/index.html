Math-3628

Classification of Algebraic Teichmüller Curves

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks which types of algebraic curves occur as Teichmüller curves. In the usual unpointed genus-$g\geq2$ setting, a nonzero holomorphic quadratic differential on a marked Riemann surface determines a Teichmüller disk $\mathbb H\to\mathcal T_g$, isometric for the relevant Teichmüller metric. A Teichmüller curve is an algebraic curve arising from its projection to the moduli space $\mathcal M_g$, with the appropriate normalization/orbifold convention. Translation-surface examples use squares of abelian differentials; general quadratic differentials need not be squares.

For a flat surface, the associated Veech group is the derivative group of its affine automorphisms, viewed in the appropriate $\operatorname{PSL}_2({\mathbb{R}})$ convention. The lattice condition, finite hyperbolic covolume, provides the usual algebraic Teichmüller-curve criterion. The resulting normalized curve is described by an orbifold quotient $\mathbb H/\Gamma$ in that framework. This recognition criterion for a specified flat surface does not classify the abstract algebraic curves that can occur.

A precise classification request must distinguish: abstract curves versus their maps/images in moduli space, orbifold curves versus coarse curves, punctured curves versus smooth projective compactifications, and actual normalizations versus arbitrary finite covers. It must also fix whether genus/marked points and strata are prescribed or allowed to vary. The source does not choose these meanings of "types", and no restriction to translation surfaces, a fixed genus or a named family is silently imposed.

The research target is to describe the possible algebraic-curve types in the intended convention, with existence and exclusion criteria or a full classification as appropriate. The supplied literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports Veech-group criteria and many families but says no complete general classification answers the broad question. This is a dated supplied assessment, not an independent current literature determination.

Short English statement

Which algebraic curves can be Teichmüller curves? Lattice Veech groups recognize many examples, but the question still needs a convention about abstract curves, orbifolds, compactifications, covers and ambient genus. The supplied assessment reports no complete general classification.
