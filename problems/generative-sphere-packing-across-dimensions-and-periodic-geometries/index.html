Research attempt: Packing certificates, projection ambiguity and periodic checks

For centers $c_1,\ldots,c_n\in[0,1]^d$, $n\geq2$, balls of radius $r\geq0$ are contained in the cube and have disjoint interiors exactly when \[ r\leq c_{i\ell}\leq1-r\quad\text{for all }i,\ell, \qquad \|c_i-c_j\|^2\geq4r^2\quad(i\ne j). \] This is a finite exact certificate for rational centers and radius, using rational arithmetic. If each center is perturbed by Euclidean norm at most $\eta$, pair distances decrease by at most $2\eta$ and each wall clearance by at most $\eta$, by the triangle inequality and the coordinate bound. Thus a packing of radius $r$ remains certified after perturbation at radius $\max(0,r-\eta)$ provided the new centers stay in the cube when $r<\eta$; for the useful regime $\eta\leq r$, cube containment follows from the clearance bound. No floating-point near-contact claim is needed.

Projection onto packing constraints is nonconvex and can be ambiguous even in one dimension. Take two labeled centers in $[0,1]$ with prescribed radius $r=1/5$. The feasible set requires wall clearance $1/5$ and separation at least $2/5$. The point $(1/2,1/2)$ has two equally near feasible points, $(3/10,7/10)$ and $(7/10,3/10)$. To prove optimality, on the component $x_2-x_1\geq2/5$ the squared distance to $(1/2,1/2)$ is minimized at mean $1/2$ and gap $2/5$; the opposite component gives its reflection. Perturbing the proposed centers to $(1/2-\delta,1/2+\delta)$ selects the first component for positive small $\delta$ and the second for negative $\delta$. Thus every nearest-point selection has a discontinuity at this tie. A fixed generator repeatedly proposing the midpoint and a fixed tie rule can return the same radius-$1/5$ feasible packing forever, despite the feasible radius $1/4$ at centers $(1/4,3/4)$. Feasibility projection alone guarantees no optimization or generalization.

Indeed, for $n$ equal intervals of radius $r$ in the unit interval, ordering centers gives \[ 1\geq r+2r(n-1)+r=2nr. \] Equality is attained by centers $(2i-1)/(2n)$. Any extrapolation keeping a positive radius while increasing $n$ indefinitely must therefore fail even in dimension one.

Finite verification of a specified periodic candidate.
Let an invertible lattice matrix $A\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ and finitely many centers $c_i$ in one cell be specified. A periodic packing of radius $r>0$ requires \[ \|c_i-c_j+Az\|\geq2r \quad\text{for all }i,j,z\in\mathbb Z^d, \quad(i,j,z)\ne(i,i,0). \] If a violation occurs, then \[ \|z\|\leq\|A^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}(2r+\|c_i-c_j\|). \] Choose a certified larger integer bound using $\|A^{-1}\|_F$ and the largest center difference. Enumerating all integer vectors in that bounded cube therefore suffices for exact checking, with rational squared norms when the data are rational. Self-translates $i=j,z\ne0$ must be included: a single center with lattice $\operatorname{diag}(1/10,10)$ already overlaps its nearest copy at radius $1/10$, although no distinct-center pair exists in its cell. The enumeration is finite but can be very expensive for ill-conditioned lattices; it is a verifier, not a generative algorithm.
