Math-1716

From one Thurston co-sphere to pointed global geometry

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The extraction-damaged source asks whether a Thurston unit sphere at a point of Teichmüller space determines global stretch lines or geodesics. The supplied interpretation retains the full linearly embedded cotangent dual co-sphere, rather than only its abstract topology or face incidence. For an oriented finite-type surface $S$ with negative Euler characteristic, write $\mathcal T(S)$ for marked hyperbolic Teichmüller space and $\ell_\lambda(X)$ for measured-lamination length. The asymmetric tangent norm and distance are \[ \|v\|_{\mathrm{Th},X}=\sup_{\lambda\ne0}{\,\mathrm{d}}\log\ell_\lambda|_X(v),\qquad d_{\mathrm{Th}}(X,Y)=\log\sup_{\lambda\ne0}\frac{\ell_\lambda(Y)}{\ell_\lambda(X)}. \] Let $K_X\subset T_X^*\mathcal T(S)$ be the closed dual unit ball, characterized by its support function $\sup_{p\in K_X}p(v)=\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th},X}$, and let $\partial K_X$ be its co-sphere. Asymmetry is retained; it is not replaced by a symmetric norm.

The aid's rigidity reduction assumes a real linear isomorphism \[ A:T_Y^*\mathcal T(S)\longrightarrow T_X^*\mathcal T(S),\qquad A(\partial K_Y)=\partial K_X. \] Convexity determines the balls from their boundaries, and polarity gives $\|A^*v\|_{\mathrm{Th},Y}=\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th},X}$ for $A^*:T_X\mathcal T(S)\to T_Y\mathcal T(S)$. In the surface range of the infinitesimal rigidity theorem invoked by the aid, this tangent isometry is induced by an extended mapping class $f$ taking $X$ to $Y$. It follows that the pointed global Thurston metric is determined up to that action. The source does not supply the theorem's full surface range or low-complexity exceptions, so those are required hypotheses rather than silently universal assertions.

The resulting global isometry transports whole families of paths, not a uniquely selected geodesic. For the standard stretch path based at $X$ along a specified complete geodesic lamination $\Lambda$, the reported equivariance is \[ f(\operatorname{Stretch}_t(X,\Lambda)) =\operatorname{Stretch}_t(fX,f\Lambda). \] It also preserves oriented geodesic envelopes, expressed by \[ \operatorname{Env}(X,Z)=\{W:d_{\mathrm{Th}}(X,W)+d_{\mathrm{Th}}(W,Z)=d_{\mathrm{Th}}(X,Z)\}. \] Thus full embedded co-sphere data provide a sufficient rigidity-level formulation of the source question, without an explicit reconstruction algorithm.

For closed $S$ of genus at least two, the aid further reports that extreme points of the polar tangent unit body recognize maximal chain-recurrent stretch directions. It also reports basepoint independence of abstract tangent-face combinatorics, showing that face incidence alone cannot retain the affine-linear data needed for this conclusion. Neither precise extreme-point correspondence nor the face-combinatorics theorem is stated or cited fully in the record. The source's remark about infinitesimal lamination-length behavior giving global coordinates is retained as background motivation, not used to identify abstract face data with the full embedded convex body.

Short English statement

The supplied precise answer keeps the entire cotangent co-sphere inside its linear space. Polarity turns a linear equivalence of such co-spheres into a tangent Thurston isometry, and the applicable rigidity theorem then gives a global mapping-class isometry transporting stretch paths and geodesic envelopes. Abstract face combinatorics is weaker and is reported insufficient. Surface-range and normalization details still need the missing references.
