Statement

Call a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $(a_n)_{n\ge 1}$ a Type-2 irrationality sequence if for every integer sequence $(b_n)_{n\ge 1}$ with \[ \frac{b_n}{a_n}\to 1\qquad (n\to\infty), \] the series $\sum_{n\ge 1} 1/b_n$ is irrational (assuming the sum converges, which it does if $b_n\to\infty$ sufficiently fast).

Questions:

  1. Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ a Type-2 irrationality sequence?
  2. Must every Type-2 irrationality sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?

Restatement from Math-5538-A2 r1, with the source manuscript’s qualifications.

Erdős Problem #263

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

I pursue path (C): identify a fatal statement-level obstruction in the modern online formulation, give the minimal corrected statement, and then push the corrected problem substantially further at the critical growth scale.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

Round 1 used two facts about the corrected increasing formulation:

  1. if \[ a_{n+1}\geq c\,a_n^{2+\varepsilon} \] eventually, then $(a_n)$ is Type-2;
  2. if $(a_n)$ is strictly increasing, $\sum 1/a_n$ converges, and \[ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 0, \] then $(a_n)$ is not Type-2.

These left the critical regime \[ a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2 \] completely open in Round 1.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

There are two genuinely new ingredients.

  1. The current problem-page wording omits the original hypothesis that the defining sequence is strictly increasing. Erdős' 1988 source explicitly writes \[ a_1<a_2<\cdots \] when introducing this modified notion.
  2. Koizumi's 2025 Theorem 4 proves that for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, \[ \lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor \] is a Type-2 irrationality sequence. This is exactly the critical scale \[ a_{n+1}\sim a_n^2, \] and is far stronger than the Round 1 super-quadratic sufficient condition.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

There are two exact gaps to close.

  1. Statement correction. Compare the modern online formulation with Erdős' original wording and isolate the missing hypothesis.
  2. Critical-scale advance. Use Koizumi's theorem to show that the critical regime $a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2$ is generically positive, even though the special point $a_n=2^{2^n}$ remains open.

This overcomes the Round 1 obstacle because Round 1 only had results below and above the quadratic boundary; Round 2 adds a theorem on that boundary.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Step 1: the modern problem-page statement is missing a hypothesis.

The current problem page states, in effect:

"Let $a_n$ be a sequence of positive integers such that for every positive-integer sequence $b_n$ with $b_n/a_n\to 1$, the sum $\sum 1/b_n$ is irrational. Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ such a sequence? Must such a sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?"

But Erdős' original 1988 source, when introducing this modified notion, explicitly says (on p. 105) that one should call \[ a_1<a_2<\cdots \] an irrationality sequence of this kind if for every $b_n/a_n\to 1$ the reciprocal sum is irrational.

So the minimal corrected statement is:

Corrected formulation. Assume \[ a_1<a_2<\cdots \] are positive integers and for every positive-integer sequence $b_n$ with $b_n/a_n\to 1$ the sum $\sum 1/b_n$ is irrational. Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ such a sequence? Must such a sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?

Why this correction matters. A Feb. 2026 forum post reports a formally verified Lean counterexample to the non-increasing formulation of the second question. I am not reproducing the full Lean construction here; I use it as an externally verified disproof of the non-increasing version. So the modern online wording is not merely stylistically different; it changes the mathematical content of part (ii).

Step 2: a new theorem at the critical scale.

I now use Koizumi's Theorem 4.

Theorem (Koizumi, 2025). Let \[ I:=\{\alpha>1:\ \lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor\ \text{is a Type-2 irrationality sequence}\}. \] Then \[ (1,\infty)\setminus I \] is countable.

This immediately yields the following Round 2 corollary.

Corollary. For all but countably many $\alpha>1$, every integer sequence $(b_n)$ satisfying \[ b_n\sim \alpha^{2^n} \] has irrational reciprocal sum. In particular, for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, the sequence \[ a_n:=\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor \] is Type-2.

Proof. By definition of $I$, this is exactly Koizumi's theorem. More concretely, if \[ a_n=\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor \] and \[ \frac{b_n}{a_n}\to 1, \] then also \[ b_n\sim \alpha^{2^n} \] because $a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}$. Koizumi's theorem says that for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, every such asymptotic sequence has irrational reciprocal sum. Hence $a_n$ is Type-2.

Step 3: relevance to the corrected Erdős problem.

Since \[ \lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor \sim \alpha^{2^n}, \] we have \[ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 1. \] Thus Koizumi's theorem gives Type-2 irrationality sequences exactly on the critical boundary \[ a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2, \] which Round 1 could not touch.

If one insists on strict increase from the very beginning, note that for every fixed $\alpha>1$ the sequence $\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is eventually strictly increasing, because the ratio of consecutive underlying real terms is \[ \frac{\alpha^{2^{n+1}}}{\alpha^{2^n}}=\alpha^{2^n}\to\infty. \] Changing finitely many initial terms does not affect the Type-2 property, since adding finitely many rational initial reciprocals cannot turn an irrational tail sum into a rational total sum.

So Round 2 proves:

New corrected-formulation theorem. For all but countably many $\alpha>1$, there exists a strictly increasing Type-2 irrationality sequence $(a_n)$ with \[ a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}. \]

In particular, the critical regime is generically positive. What remains open is the specific arithmetic point \[ \alpha=2, \] i.e. the original question about $a_n=2^{2^n}$.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Does the correction affect part (i)? No. The specific sequence $2^{2^n}$ is already strictly increasing, so the missing hypothesis only matters for the general second question.
  • Does Koizumi's theorem really match the Type-2 definition? Yes. The theorem is stated precisely for the set of $\alpha$ such that $\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is Type-2.
  • Strict increase versus eventual strict increase. Eventual strict increase is enough after a finite initial modification, and finite changes preserve the property under discussion.
  • What is and is not solved? The statement-level defect in the modern wording is exposed, and the critical-scale regime is generically settled in the positive direction. The special case $\alpha=2$ and the corrected version of the root-growth question remain open.

7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)

Round 2 achieves two real advances:

  1. it identifies and corrects a fatal missing hypothesis in the modern statement of part (ii);
  2. it proves, via Koizumi's theorem, that the critical boundary \[ a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2 \] is generically positive: for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, one has a Type-2 sequence with \[ a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}. \]

The corrected Erdős question remains open for the special sequence $a_n=2^{2^n}$ and for the corrected root-growth question.

OBJECTIVE AND REVIEW OF ALL VERSIONS

Erdős #263; independent attempt, 2026-09-23. Is $2^{2^n}$ a Type-2 irrationality sequence, and must every increasing such sequence grow faster than every exponential? I read 263.tex and 263_v2.tex; they repeat the generic critical-scale result. The official entry now already includes the increasing hypothesis. I investigate why a generic statement cannot settle a particular base by constructing an exceptional critical base explicitly through a recurrence.

WORK: AN EXCEPTIONAL CRITICAL BASE

Let $s_1=2$ and $s_{n+1}=s_n^2-s_n+1$. Induction using $s_{n+1}-1=s_n(s_n-1)$ gives \[ \sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{s_i}=1-\frac1{s_{n+1}-1}, \qquad \sum_{i\ge1}\frac1{s_i}=1. \] Write $\delta_n=\log(1-1/s_n+1/s_n^2)=O(1/s_n)$. The recurrence gives \[ 2^{-n}\log s_n\longrightarrow\log\alpha =\tfrac12\log2+\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j-1}\delta_j. \] This base exceeds one: since $s_{n+1}\ge s_n^2/2$ and $s_2=3$, induction gives $s_n\ge2(3/2)^{2^{n-2}}$ for $n\ge2$. Finally, \[ \log s_n-2^n\log\alpha =-\sum_{j\ge n}2^{n-j-1}\delta_j=O(1/s_n)\to0. \] Hence $s_n\sim\alpha^{2^n}$. Taking $b_n=s_n$ shows that $\lfloor\alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is not Type-2, despite lying exactly on the quadratic-growth boundary. This is the standard Sylvester-sequence obstruction, here verified directly.

ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

The exceptional base is defined by a convergent limit, not identified with two. The proof uses asymptotic equivalence, stronger than equality of logarithmic growth rates; the tail estimate above supplies that missing precision. A countable exceptional set can contain any particular named base, including two, so Koizumi's generic theorem supplies no conclusion there. The increasing-sequence root-growth question is also not settled. No theorem beyond the known generic result is claimed.
