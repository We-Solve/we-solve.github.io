Definitions and mathematical statement

In a standard measured formulation, a bounded-degree graphing is a symmetric Borel graph $E\subseteq X\times X$ without loops on a standard probability space $(X,\mu)$, generated by measure-preserving partial isomorphisms, with vertex degrees at most $D<\infty$. For a measurable edge set use the edge-counting measure \[ \nu(F)=\frac12\int_X\#\{y:(x,y)\in F\}\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(x). \] For measurable $A\subseteq X$, let $\partial_E A$ denote the edges with one endpoint in $A$ and one outside it. A measured-expansion convention requires some $h>0$ such that $\nu(\partial_E A)\geq h\mu(A)$ whenever $0<\mu(A)\leq1/2$. A strong-ergodicity convention requires that $\nu(\partial_E A_j)\to0$ imply $\mu(A_j)(1-\mu(A_j))\to0$ for every sequence of measurable sets $A_j$.

Fix an integer $C\geq1$. For a measurable coloring $c:X\to\{1,\ldots,C\}$ define \[ \operatorname{err}(c)=\nu\left(\{(x,y)\in E:c(x)=c(y)\}\right). \] Under these conventions, the question asks whether, for every graphing satisfying either the expansion hypothesis or the strong-ergodicity hypothesis, \[ \left[\forall\epsilon>0\ \exists\text{ measurable }c_\epsilon:X\to\{1,\ldots,C\},\ \operatorname{err}(c_\epsilon)<\epsilon\right] \] \[ \Longrightarrow \left[\exists\text{ measurable }c:X\to\{1,\ldots,C\},\ \operatorname{err}(c)=0\right]. \] The number $C$ is fixed throughout; it cannot grow as the error decreases.

The source does not give precise conventions for expansion, strong ergodicity, small error, or "properly colored." The display specifies a natural measurable, almost-everywhere edge formulation consistent with the supplied assessment. Requiring a Borel coloring at every vertex, or a proper coloring on all edges including a null exceptional set, is a distinct target and is not silently identified with zero error. Bounded degree makes the small-edge-error and small-measure-of-incident-bad-vertices versions comparable, but their exact source convention remains to be confirmed.

Short English statement

For an expanding or strongly ergodic graphing, does the existence of measurable $C$-colorings with errors tending to zero force an exact measurable $C$-coloring?
