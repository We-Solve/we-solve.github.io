Math-1712

The solved two-step flag puzzle conjecture and projection-lift support tests

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For integers $0\le a\le b\le n$, let \[ \operatorname{Fl}(a,b;n)=\{(A,B):A\subseteq B\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^n,\ \dim A=a,\ \dim B=b\}. \] Its integral cohomology Schubert basis is indexed by words $u$ containing $a$ zeros, $b-a$ ones and $n-b$ twos, using a fixed Schubert convention. Define the structure constants by \[ \sigma_u\sigma_v=\sum_w c_{u,v}^{w}\sigma_w. \] The supplied aid interprets the AIM request for Buch's weakened version of Knutson's conjecture as the two-step puzzle rule: $c_{u,v}^{w}$ is the number of two-step puzzles with the boundary data corresponding to $u,v,w$, under the rule's prescribed pieces and orientations. This is a statement about all two-step flag varieties, rather than the false unrestricted full-flag conjecture. The record does not list the puzzle pieces or specify boundary reversal/duality conventions; consequently it does not by itself define a self-contained counting function. An exact puzzle formulation must import those conventions from the intended theorem, not silently choose them.

The research aid reports the two-step rule proved by Buch, Kresch, Purbhoo and Tamvakis, and an equivariant version proved by Buch. These are reported literature results. The ordinary and equivariant counts should not be conflated: the latter require their equivariant weights.

A further reported support check uses the forgetful maps $\pi_a:\operatorname{Fl}(a,b;n)\to\operatorname{Gr}(a,n)$ and $\pi_b:\operatorname{Fl}(a,b;n)\to\operatorname{Gr}(b,n)$. Index a Grassmannian basis by $02$-words with respectively $a$ or $b$ zeros. In the standard stable-order lift convention:

  • $\ell_a$ keeps every zero and replaces the first $b-a$ twos, scanning left to right, by ones.
  • $\ell_b$ keeps every two and replaces the zeros after the first $a$ zeros, scanning left to right, by ones.
With compatible Schubert conventions, $\pi_j^*\sigma_\alpha=\sigma_{\ell_j(\alpha)}$ for $j=a,b$. If $c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(\operatorname{Gr}(j,n))$ are ordinary Grassmannian Littlewood--Richardson coefficients, then \[ c_{\ell_j(\alpha),\ell_j(\beta)}^{w}= \begin{cases} c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(\operatorname{Gr}(j,n)),&w=\ell_j(\gamma),\\ 0,&w\notin\operatorname{im}\ell_j. \end{cases} \] This check applies when both inputs come from the same forgetful projection. It is not a rule for arbitrary pairs of two-step classes.

The source also motivates the two-step case by a quantum-to-classical reduction for Grassmannians. It prints a Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis--Yong citation in Duke Mathematical Journal 122 (2004), 125--143. The research aid explicitly says this citation is incorrect for that reduction and identifies the intended reduction as the 2003 JAMS kernel-and-span theorem of Buch, Kresch and Tamvakis. Both the original citation and the reported correction are preserved below; neither is silently substituted for a fully verified bibliography.

Short English statement

Express two-step flag Schubert constants by the intended puzzle rule, with the source's full-flag warning retained. The supplied aid reports that rule solved and gives exact Grassmannian-lift support checks, while also flagging the source's quantum-to-classical citation as incorrect.
