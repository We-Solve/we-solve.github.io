Math-5230

A weighted product of chordal distances

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $r\ge1$, let $z_1,\ldots,z_r$ lie on $\mathbb T=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|=1\}$, and let $p_j\ge0$ satisfy $\sum_jp_j=r$. Omit zero-weight factors, so no $0^0$ convention is needed, and put \[ M=\max_{z\in\mathbb T}\prod_{j:p_j>0}|z-z_j|^{p_j}. \] The conjecture, brought by V. Lev and attributed to Konyagin and the presenter, is $M\ge2$. The original remark says the constant is optimal and records the equal-weight and equally spaced special cases and a polynomial reformulation.

Combine repeated positive-weight locations into distinct active points $\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_m$ with aggregate weights $w_i>0$, $\sum_iw_i=r$. Necessarily $1\le m\le r$. The attached aid attributes to Dubinin's sharp hedgehog-capacity inequality, through a published weighted conformal-map correspondence, the stronger result \[ M\ge2^{r/m}\ge2. \] It identifies equality in the first bound with a regular $m$-gon of active locations and equal aggregate weights $r/m$. Consequently equality $M=2$ in the original normalization requires $m=r$, the original points a regular $r$-gon and all weights $1$. This includes the one-point case; for two entries equality occurs exactly for antipodal points of weights $1,1$. Repeated locations or zero weights reduce $m$ and give a strictly larger lower bound when $m<r$.

For the polynomial version let $P$ be monic of degree $d\ge1$ with all zeros on $\mathbb T$ and exactly $m$ distinct zeros. The aid reports \[ \|P\|_{\mathbb T}:=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\ge2^{d/m}. \] The unit-circle-root hypothesis is essential; this is not asserted for arbitrary monic polynomials.

If the active support lies in a closed arc of angular length $0\le L\le2\pi$, the additional reported bound is \[ M\ge\max\left\{2^{r/m},\,[2\cos(L/4)]^r\right\}. \] The second term comes from the point opposite the arc midpoint. The aid supplies a full-solution literature claim for the original inequality, while exact bibliographic statements of the capacity result and correspondence remain absent.

Short English statement

Maximize a weighted product of distances from prescribed circle points. The supplied aid reports a sharp bound in terms of distinct active support, covering zero weights and repetitions, and gives polynomial and arc-concentration consequences.
