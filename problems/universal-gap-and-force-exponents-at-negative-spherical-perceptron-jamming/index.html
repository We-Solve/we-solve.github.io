Universal gap and force exponents at negative spherical-perceptron jamming

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. At spherical-perceptron margin $-1$, the proposed threshold and microscopic gap and force laws have exponents $\gamma=(2+\theta)^{-1}$, approximately $0.412693$ and $0.423107$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Let $M=\lfloor\alpha N\rfloor$ and let $A$ have independent entries of law \[ \nu_\varepsilon=\tfrac12N(0,1-\varepsilon)+\tfrac12N(0,1+\varepsilon). \] For $\|x\|^2=N$, set $g_\mu=A_\mu x/\sqrt N$, $h_\mu=1+g_\mu$, and $H_N(x)=\frac12\sum_{\mu=1}^M(-h_\mu)_+^2$. Use spherical Gibbs probability proportional to $e^{-\beta H_N}$. SAT means that $H_N$ vanishes somewhere on the sphere. For $0<\delta<u$ and $s>0$, define \[ G_{N,\beta,\alpha}(\delta,u)=\mathbb E\left\langle M^{-1}\sum_\mu\mathbf1_{\{\delta<h_\mu\le u\}}\right\rangle. \] On $C(x)=\{\mu:h_\mu<0\}$ put $\bar r=|C|^{-1}\sum_{\mu\in C}(-h_\mu)$ and $f_\mu=-h_\mu/\bar r$. Set \[ F_{N,\beta,\alpha}(s)=\mathbb E\left\langle |C|^{-1}\sum_{\mu\in C}\mathbf1_{\{f_\mu\le s\}}\right\rangle, \] with value zero when $C$ is empty. There exist $\alpha_c>0$ and $0<\varepsilon_0<1$ such that, for each fixed $0\le\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, SAT probability tends to one for $\alpha<\alpha_c$ and zero for $\alpha>\alpha_c$. The ordered limits \[ G_J(u)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{\alpha\downarrow\alpha_c}\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}G_{N,\beta,\alpha}(\delta,u),\quad F_J(s)=\lim_{\alpha\downarrow\alpha_c}\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,\beta,\alpha}(s) \] exist and are independent of $\varepsilon$. The second is a probability distribution function on $(0,\infty)$, of mean one and with no atom at zero. As $u,s\downarrow0$, \[ G_J(u)=u^{1-\gamma+o(1)},\quad F_J(s)=s^{1+\theta+o(1)},\quad \gamma=(2+\theta)^{-1},\quad 0.4126930<\gamma<0.4126934,\quad 0.4231063<\theta<0.4231088. \]

Outline of the argument

A pressure formula and feasibility repair identify the threshold before the zero-temperature critical layer is analyzed. A convex Brownian martingale problem and contraction characterize all rescaled layer limits; an error-controlled finite numerical certificate brackets the exponents, and coordinate comparison extends the result to the specified Gaussian mixture.
