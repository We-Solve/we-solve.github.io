Definitions and notation.
An integrable evolution admits a special algebraic or spectral description allowing exact solution; a perturbation changes its equation by a small parameter-dependent term. For nonlinear Schrödinger equations, scattering coordinates may be used to compare perturbed and unperturbed dynamics. Long-time persistence, soliton behavior and asymptotic stability are different mathematical targets.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Develop perturbation theory for infinite-dimensional integrable systems. For the NLS example retain initial data $u_0\in H^{1,1}=\{f\in L^2(\mathbb R):f',xf\in L^2(\mathbb R)\}$ and a perturbing function $V(r)\sim r^p$ as $r\to0$, with sufficiently large $p>2$ and small positive $\varepsilon$. Beyond the source's defocusing context $iu_t+u_{xx}-2|u|^2u-\varepsilon V(|u|)u=0$, analyze the focusing sign, particularly near a $k$-soliton with $k\ge2$, and obtain detailed long-time asymptotics. Investigate persistence of Toda shock and rarefaction phenomena when exponential forces are perturbed, and sine-Gordon perturbations motivated by the Fermi--Pasta--Ulam problem. Investigate related spatially periodic Hamiltonian perturbations and surviving finite-gap tori, where whole-line dispersive decay cannot be assumed.

A finite-time perturbation bound for the NLS component.
Restrict to smooth solutions on $[0,T]$ of the focusing cubic equation and its real quintic perturbation, \[iu_t+u_{xx}+2|u|^2u=0,\qquad iv_t+v_{xx}+2|v|^2v-\varepsilon|v|^4v=0, \] with common initial data. The quintic corresponds to the source's $V(r)=r^4$, which is one admissible higher-power test, not the full perturbation class. Assume $\|u(t)\|_\infty,\|v(t)\|_\infty\le M$ and $\|v(t)\|_2\le K$. For $w=v-u$, the skew-adjoint linear Schrödinger part drops out of the derivative of $\|w\|_2$. The elementary pointwise inequality $||v|^2v-|u|^2u|\le3M^2|v-u|$ gives \[\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_2\le6M^2\|w\|_2+\varepsilon M^4K. \] The inequality at zero norm follows by regularizing the norm with a positive constant and taking its limit. Integrating yields $\|w(t)\|_2\le\varepsilon M^4K(e^{6M^2t}-1)/(6M^2)$ for $M>0$; if $M=0$, both solutions vanish.
Why it does not supply scattering asymptotics.
Even assuming the bounds remain uniform, this estimate grows exponentially with time and becomes useless on sufficiently long scales. A multi-soliton background has neutral position and phase directions, so persistent small forcing can accumulate into modulation even when a naive norm difference grows. The estimate neither removes those directions nor gives asymptotic soliton parameters.
Full scope comparison.
The initial-data space $H^{1,1}$ and the source's broader $V(r)\sim r^p$ assumptions are not replaced by a claimed global theorem; the extra smoothness and a priori bounds are explicit. Focusing $k\ge2$ soliton asymptotics, perturbed Toda shocks/rarefactions, sine-Gordon/Fermi--Pasta--Ulam questions and periodic finite-gap tori all remain. The first missing step for this route is a coercive modulation estimate with time-integrable forcing, rather than the displayed finite-time Gronwall factor.
