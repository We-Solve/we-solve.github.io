Kourovka Notebook Problem 1.27

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Work in the first-order language of groups $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}=\{\cdot,{}^{-1},1\}$, with equality and without parameters. A free group on a set $S$ is a group $F(S)$, together with a map $\iota:S\to F(S)$, such that every map from $S$ to a group $H$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism $F(S)\to H$. Its rank is $|S|$.

A universal sentence is a sentence of the form $\forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\,\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, where $n\geq 0$ and $\varphi$ is a quantifier-free Boolean combination of equations between group terms. For a group $G$, define \[ \operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(G)=\{\sigma:\sigma\text{ is a universal }\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}\text{-sentence and }G\models\sigma\}. \] For a nonempty class $\mathcal F$ of groups, define its common universal theory by \[ \operatorname{Th}_{\forall}(\mathcal F)=\{\sigma:\sigma\text{ is a universal }\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}\text{-sentence and }(\forall G\in\mathcal F)\ G\models\sigma\}. \]

Source request (M. I. Kargapolov, 1965). Describe the universal theory of free groups.

Unresolved scope. The supplied statement does not specify ranks, whether only nonabelian free groups are intended, or whether the target is the theory of an individual free group or the common theory of a class of free groups. Thus the notation above defines the possible targets without selecting one. In particular, inclusion of ranks $0$ and $1$, and any restriction to finite rank, remain unspecified. Short English statement

Kargapolov asks for a description of the universally quantified group statements true in free groups. The source leaves the intended ranks, the individual-versus-common interpretation, and the required form of description unspecified.
