Math-286

Higher-dimensional asymptotic geodesics and the homology obstruction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Schmidt's Problem 8.6.1 says "Generalize this statement to higher dimensions." The preceding Gaidukov statement is absent from the supplied record, so its exact hypotheses and asymptotic conclusion cannot be reconstructed uniquely. The text says Gaidukov's proof is intrinsically two-dimensional and suggests Mather's minimizing measures, citing [Mather1991] and [Con-Itu1999], with the support of a minimal measure replacing a single closed geodesic.

The paired aid reports a strong lifted conclusion for closed strictly negatively curved manifolds in every dimension: asymptotic geodesics converge exponentially after Busemann phase matching. The exact original Gaidukov formulation is still missing. For general metrics it identifies an obstruction to replacing free homotopy data by classical Mather rotation data. For the kinetic Lagrangian \[ L(x,v)=\tfrac12\|v\|_g^2, \] rotation vectors of invariant probability measures lie in $H_1(M;\mathbb R)$. At rotation zero, the minimum average kinetic action is zero and the union of supports of minimizing measures is the zero section of $TM$. A nontrivial null-homologous free homotopy class consequently loses its conjugacy-class information. Attraction merely to the projection of that zero section is vacuous, while attraction in phase space by a unit-speed geodesic to the zero section is impossible.

The aid therefore proposes retaining a conjugacy-class-sensitive minimizing-axis set $\mathcal A_{[\gamma]}$, and asking whether its stable basin projects onto every base point. Schematically the proposed question is \[ \pi\left(W^s(\mathcal A_{[\gamma]})\right)=M. \] The precise axis-minimization notion, treatment of deck transformations, and whether stable attraction means convergence to a set or to a phase-matched individual axis require definition. This is a proposed reduction, not a completed general higher-dimensional theorem.

The record also concatenates Section 8.7, "Systolic and diastolic inequalities for surfaces," communicated by Guth, Rotman and Sabourau. For a compact Riemannian surface it uses $\operatorname{sys}(M,g)$ for the shortest nontrivial closed geodesic. Since it applies this to $S^2$, "nontrivial" here must distinguish nonconstant geodesics rather than require a noncontractible loop; the latter systole would not give its sphere quantity.

For Morse functions $f:M\to\mathbb R$, the two source minimax expressions are \[ L_a(M,g)=\inf_f\max_{t\in\mathbb R} \mathcal F_a(f^{-1}(t)),\qquad L_b(M,g)=\inf_f\max_{t\in\mathbb R} \mathcal F_b(f^{-1}(t)), \] where $\mathcal F_a$ is total level-set length and $\mathcal F_b$ is the length of its longest component. The source reports realization by unions of closed geodesics and calls $L_a$ one version of the diastole, while warning that different notions exist [Bal-Sab2010]. Critical-level conventions and the exact realization theorem are not supplied; replacing a maximum by a supremum may be necessary without an attainment statement.

The source attributes to [Croke1988], with improvements [Nab-Rot2002], [Sabourau2004] and [Rotman2006], the sphere inequality \[ \operatorname{sys}(S^2,g)\leq\sqrt{32}\, \sqrt{\operatorname{Area}(S^2,g)}. \] It reports a conjectured smaller constant, printed in the extraction as "$\sqrt2\sqrt3$", and says two congruent equilateral triangles glued along their boundaries obstruct any smaller value. That radical string may represent $\sqrt{2\sqrt3}$ or $\sqrt2\,\sqrt3$; the nesting is not resolved by this source text and is not silently chosen here. The doubled-triangle example is nonsmooth and motivates metrics with conical singularities.

For that enlarged class the source considers \[ \sigma(g)=\frac{\operatorname{Area}(S^2,g)} {\operatorname{sys}(S^2,g)^2}. \] It says $\sigma$ is Lipschitz for an appropriate metric-space distance, asserts a positive minimum attributed to [Croke1988], conjectures that the doubled equilateral triangle attains it, and calls the round sphere a critical point in connection with the large family of Zoll metrics having the same value [Balacheff2006]. The metric distance, admissible singularities, attainment/compactness and critical-point notion are not supplied. A positive systolic lower ratio by itself gives a positive infimum, not a proof of attainment. These adjacent surface questions and contextual claims are distinct from Schmidt's higher-dimensional asymptotic-geodesic problem.

Short English statement

The main problem asks for an unspecified Gaidukov generalization; the aid explains why ordinary homological Mather measures lose essential free-homotopy information. It proposes a conjugacy-sensitive stable-basin formulation. A concatenated surface-systolic section is retained in full scope, including its minimax definitions, references, doubled-triangle conjecture and unresolved extracted constant.
