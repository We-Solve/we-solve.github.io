Math-2775

Hamiltonian Decomposition of Hypergraphs

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For integers $k\geq2$ and $n\geq k+1$, the complete $k$-uniform hypergraph $K_n^{(k)}$ has vertex set $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and all $k$-element subsets as edges. A tight Hamilton cycle is given by a cyclic ordering $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of all vertices, with edges \[ \{v_i,v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{i+k-1}\},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n, \] where indices are taken modulo $n$. In this range it has $n$ distinct edges and each vertex has degree $k$. A tight Hamilton decomposition is a partition of the entire edge set of $K_n^{(k)}$ into such cycles.

The supplied sentence asks whether complete uniform hypergraphs admit such decompositions, but omits the parameters and admissibility hypotheses. Necessarily the number of cycles would be \[ \frac1n\binom nk, \] and the degree at a vertex would be a multiple of $k$. Thus the necessary divisibility condition is \[ n\mid\binom nk \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k\mid\binom{n-1}{k-1}. \] The supplied triage describes the intended asymptotic conjecture as sufficiency of this condition for sufficiently large admissible parameters. A standard fixed-uniformity version has quantifier pattern \[ \forall k\geq3\ \exists n_0(k)\ \forall n\geq\max\{n_0(k),k+1\}: \quad k\mid\binom{n-1}{k-1} \Longrightarrow K_n^{(k)}\text{ has a tight Hamilton decomposition}. \] This makes the inferred large-$n$, fixed-$k$ reading explicit; the short source sentence itself does not specify that quantifier pattern or its small-parameter exceptions. Its unrestricted universal reading is not adopted, since the supplied triage says it fails divisibility.

A Hamilton Berge cycle is a different object: it uses a cyclic listing of all vertices and distinct edges, each assigned edge containing the corresponding consecutive vertex pair, rather than requiring edges to be precisely consecutive $k$-vertex windows. The supplied triage dated 2026-08-17 reports a Berge-cycle decomposition theorem of Kühn--Osthus for admissible $n$ when $k=3$, and for $n\geq30$ when $k\geq4$; it does not identify this with a tight-cycle theorem. It also reports tight decompositions for restricted complete multipartite families, not for all complete uniform hypergraphs. The background's unqualified graph-decomposition analogy likewise omits admissibility such as the degree parity condition. The registry status is open, while the dated triage labels the row partially solved and the intended general tight-cycle conjecture unresolved.

Short English statement

Can every sufficiently large complete $k$-uniform hypergraph satisfying the necessary divisibility condition be partitioned into spanning tight cycles? The original sentence omits that condition and its parameter quantifiers. Berge-cycle decompositions and restricted multipartite results do not settle the tight-cycle question.
