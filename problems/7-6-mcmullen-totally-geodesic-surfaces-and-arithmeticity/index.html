Math-469

7.6 (McMullen) — Totally geodesic surfaces and arithmeticity

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold. In the complete quotient interpretation write $M=\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$, where $\Gamma$ is a lattice in the appropriate hyperbolic isometry group. Call $M$ arithmetic when $\Gamma$ is commensurable with an arithmetic lattice, up to conjugacy and the usual orientation convention.

Question (McMullen). If $M$ contains infinitely many immersed totally geodesic surfaces, must $M$ be arithmetic? An intended geometric formulation requires infinitely many genuinely distinct surface images, not just infinitely many parametrizations or finite covers of one immersed surface. With an admissible class of surfaces fixed, it asks whether \[ \#\{\text{distinct immersed totally geodesic surface images in }M\}=\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\text{ is arithmetic}. \] The term "surface" must include the intended completeness and area conditions: arbitrary local geodesic patches would not express the arithmeticity problem. The source excerpt does not state whether the surfaces are required to be closed or may be noncompact of finite area, nor how distinctness is counted. Its short formulation is therefore preserved with those necessary conventions unresolved.

The supplied registry labels the problem solved. Its summary credits Margulis and Mohammadi with an arithmeticity theorem for finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifolds containing infinitely many immersed, closed totally geodesic surfaces, and reports a later broader generalization by Bader--Fisher--Miller--Stover. These are supplied literature claims, not independently verified theorems here; in particular the summary's added word "closed" must be compared with the broader source wording.

Short English statement

Does an infinite collection of distinct immersed totally geodesic surfaces force a finite-volume hyperbolic $3$-manifold to be arithmetic? The registry reports a solution, but the source's surface completeness, finite-area, closedness, and distinctness conventions need to be matched to the cited result.
