Math-1651

Uniform spectral tails and compact-resolvent obstruction for the free heat semigroup

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,\tau)$ be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state, generated by a finite self-adjoint tuple $x_1,\ldots,x_m$. In the standard bounded-generator interpretation, the free difference quotients satisfy $\partial_jx_i=\delta_{ij}1\otimes1$. Finite free Fisher information means that conjugate variables $\xi_j\in L^2(M,\tau)$ exist with \[ \tau(\xi_jP)=(\tau\otimes\tau)(\partial_jP),\qquad \Phi^*(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=\sum_j\|\xi_j\|_2^2<\infty. \] Use the closed free-difference-quotient quadratic form, with its required domains, to interpret \[ \Delta=\sum_j\partial_j^*\partial_j\geq0, \qquad\phi_t=e^{-t\Delta}. \] The source treats $\phi_t$ as a completely positive heat deformation; the form/Markov construction must be checked in the chosen setting.

Source question. Can this semigroup converge uniformly to the identity on the operator-norm unit ball of $M$ as $t\downarrow0$? The aid identifies the intended output norm as $L^2$, hence the target is \[ H(t)=\sup_{\|a\|_\infty\leq1}\|\phi_t(a)-a\|_2\longrightarrow0. \] This is stronger than pointwise $L^2$ continuity and differs from operator-norm convergence. The source adds a proposed non-weak-rigidity consequence for a nonhyperfinite generated algebra; that consequence requires the appropriate rigidity theorem, not merely a change of norm convention.

Reported general spectral criterion. For any positive self-adjoint $\Delta$ on $L^2(M)$, let \[ Q(R)=\sup_{\|a\|_\infty\leq1} \|\mathbf1_{(R,\infty)}(\Delta)a\|_2, \qquad V(t)=\sup_{\|a\|_\infty\leq1} \|(I+t\Delta)^{-1}a-a\|_2. \] The aid reports equivalence of $H(t)\to0$, $V(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$, and $Q(R)\to0$ as $R\to\infty$. Quantitative sufficient comparisons, using $\|a\|_2\leq1$, are \[ H(t)\leq tR+Q(R),\qquad Q(R)\leq\frac{H(t)}{1-e^{-tR}}, \] \[ V(t)\leq tR+Q(R),\qquad Q(R)\leq\frac{1+tR}{tR}V(t) \quad(t,R>0). \] Thus it is uniform high-spectral-tail control of the whole operator unit ball that is at issue; unboundedness of $\Delta$ alone does not answer the source question.

Reported compact-resolvent obstruction. If $M$ is diffuse and $\Delta$ has compact resolvent, the aid reports \[ H(t)\geq1\qquad(t>0). \] A diffuse finite algebra contains a Haar unitary $u$, meaning $\tau(u^k)=0$ for every nonzero integer $k$. Its positive powers are an orthonormal $L^2$ sequence, with $\|u^k\|_\infty=\|u^k\|_2=1$. Compactness of the positive-time heat operator supplies the reported witnesses \[ \|e^{-t\Delta}u^k\|_2\to0, \qquad\|e^{-t\Delta}u^k-u^k\|_2\to1. \] This is a sufficient obstruction under compact resolvent; no compactness is inferred for every finite-Fisher tuple.

For a finite standard freely independent semicircular family, the aid reports that the difference-quotient generator is the free number operator, with eigenvalue word length and finite-dimensional eigenspaces. Its resolvent is compact, and the generated algebra is diffuse, so the semigroup is nonuniform in the stated sense, including the one-variable case.

The aid also cites Peterson's $L^2$-rigidity framework and Dabrowski--Ioana criteria for non-$L^2$-rigidity/non-weak-rigidity in broad finite-Fisher regimes, but supplies neither full hypotheses nor citations. In the usual terminology weak rigidity concerns a diffuse subalgebra with a relative rigidity/property-$T$ condition, while hyperfiniteness concerns finite-dimensional approximation; the exact theorem connecting these notions is not reconstructed. The fully general finite-Fisher formulation is explicitly not claimed solved.

Short English statement

The question concerns uniform $L^2$ approximation on the operator-norm unit ball as time tends to zero. The aid characterizes this by uniform spectral-tail decay, and compact resolvent in a diffuse algebra gives explicit nonuniformity witnesses. Semicircular families satisfy that special criterion; the general finite-Fisher and rigidity claims retain their missing hypotheses.
