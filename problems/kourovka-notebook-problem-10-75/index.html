Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.75

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group, let $p$ be a prime, and let $a\in G$ have order $p$. Write \[ a^g=g^{-1}ag,\qquad H_g=\langle a,a^g\rangle,\qquad \mathcal H=\{H_g:g\in G\}. \] For a subgroup $F\leq G$, its normalizer is \[ N_G(F)=\{g\in G:g^{-1}Fg=F\}. \] A group is soluble if its derived series reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps.

The source asks: if the normalizer of every finite subgroup of $G$ containing $a$ has finite periodic part, every $H_g$ is finite, and almost all of these subgroups are soluble, must $G$ possess a periodic part when $p>2$?

The supplied record does not define "periodic part" or specify the indexing convention for "almost all." To make the possible interpretation explicit, put \[ T(K)=\{x\in K:\text{the order of }x\text{ is finite}\} \] for any group $K$. Under the interpretation that possessing a periodic part means that $T(K)$ is a subgroup, having finite periodic part means that this subgroup is finite, and "almost all" means all but finitely many distinct members of $\mathcal H$, the requested assertion becomes the following conditional statement:

For every group $G$, every odd prime $p$, and every element $a\in G$ of order $p$, the assumptions \[ \begin{gathered} \text{for every finite }F\leq G\text{ with }a\in F, \\ T(N_G(F))\leq N_G(F)\quad\text{and}\quad |T(N_G(F))|<\infty, \\[2pt] |\langle a,a^g\rangle|<\infty\quad\text{for every }g\in G, \\[2pt] \left|\{H\in\mathcal H:H\text{ is not soluble}\}\right|<\infty \end{gathered} \] imply $T(G)\leq G$. This explicit formulation is conditional on the stated interpretations and requires comparison with the source terminology. If instead "almost all" counts elements $g\in G$, its corresponding assumption would be \[ \left|\{g\in G:H_g\text{ is not soluble}\}\right|<\infty; \] the supplied record does not resolve this distinction.

The source additionally reports that Shunkov's cited 1986 works establish an affirmative answer when $a$ is a point, and exhibit a group satisfying the stated hypotheses with a point $a$ of order $2$ but without a periodic part. The definition of a point is referred to the cited 1983 paper and is not supplied here; no definition or additional hypothesis concerning points is reconstructed in this draft. These are supplied historical reports, not independently verified results.

Short English statement

Shunkov asks whether a group containing an element of odd prime order must have a periodic part when the normalizers of all finite subgroups containing that element have finite periodic part, all subgroups generated by the element and a conjugate are finite, and almost all of those subgroups are soluble. The meanings of "periodic part" and "almost all" require source confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open and reports an additional positive result for points and a counterexample at prime $2$.
