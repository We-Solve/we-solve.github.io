Open Problems in Topological Algebra — Problem 6 (Protasov)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a group equipped with a topology. It is a semitopological group if multiplication is separately continuous: for every $a\in G$, both maps $x\mapsto ax$ and $x\mapsto xa$ are continuous. It is a quasitopological group if, in addition, inversion $x\mapsto x^{-1}$ is continuous. Joint continuity of multiplication is not required in either definition.

Use the usual two-sided interpretation of bounded: for every neighborhood $U$ of the identity there exist finite subsets $F_L,F_R\subseteq G$ such that \[ G=F_LU=UF_R, \qquad F_LU=\{fu:f\in F_L,\ u\in U\},\quad UF_R=\{uf:u\in U,\ f\in F_R\}. \] The supplied excerpt does not explicitly define boundedness, so this interpretation requires confirmation against the source. The cellularity of $G$ is \[ c(G)=\sup\left\{|\mathcal U|:\mathcal U\text{ is a pairwise disjoint family of nonempty open subsets of }G\right\}. \] Here countable cellularity means $c(G)\leq\aleph_0$, equivalently that every such family is at most countable.

The question attributed to Protasov asks whether every bounded semitopological group has countable cellularity: \[ \forall G\quad \left(G\text{ is a bounded semitopological group}\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ c(G)\leq\aleph_0. \] The parenthetical wording also asks the same question for bounded quasitopological groups. These are recorded as two versions of the question, with the quasitopological version imposing continuous inversion. The supplied record does not specify separation axioms; the intended separation convention must therefore be checked before either version is regarded as a fully settled formulation.

For context, the supplied source reports that $\sigma$-compact topological groups have countable cellularity, citing [Tk], and that compact topological groups support a strictly positive Borel probability measure $\mu$, meaning $\mu(U)>0$ for every nonempty open subset $U$. It further reports a result of T. Banakh and O. Ravsky, citing [BR] and also [BR2], that every bounded paratopological group has countable cellularity and, moreover, every cardinal of uncountable cofinality is a precaliber of that group. A paratopological group has jointly continuous multiplication, without requiring continuous inversion. A cardinal $\kappa$ is a precaliber of a space if every indexed family of $\kappa$ nonempty open sets has a subfamily indexed by a set of cardinality $\kappa$ whose every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection. In contrast, the source reports, citing [Pr2], that for every infinite cardinal $\tau$ there exists a left bounded left topological group with cellularity $\tau$. These contextual assertions are supplied source reports, not independently verified results in this draft.

Short English statement

The question attributed to Protasov asks whether every bounded group with separately continuous multiplication has at most countably many pairwise disjoint nonempty open sets. It also asks the version in which inversion is continuous. The intended separation axioms and the source's exact convention for boundedness require confirmation. The registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
