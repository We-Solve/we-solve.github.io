Kourovka Notebook Problem 12.48

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Omega$ be a set with at least two elements, and let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$. The action is sharply doubly transitive if, for all $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\Omega$ with $\alpha\neq\beta$ and $\gamma\neq\delta$, there exists exactly one $g\in G$ such that $g\alpha=\gamma$ and $g\beta=\delta$. Fix $\omega\in\Omega$ and write \[ H=G_\omega=\{g\in G:g\omega=\omega\}. \] A group is locally finite if each of its finitely generated subgroups is finite. A subgroup $N\leq G$ acts regularly on $\Omega$ if, for every $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$, there exists exactly one $n\in N$ such that $n\alpha=\beta$.

For every sharply doubly transitive permutation group as above, ask the following two questions separately:

  1. (a) If $H$ is locally finite, must there exist a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ acting regularly on $\Omega$?
  2. (b) If there exists an abelian subgroup $A\leq H$ with $[H:A]<\infty$, must there exist a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ acting regularly on $\Omega$?
No finiteness assumption on $G$ or $\Omega$ is imposed. Since point stabilizers are conjugate, either hypothesis may equivalently be imposed on any point stabilizer.

Supplied qualification (Comments of 2022). The source reports an affirmative answer to part (b) in permutation characteristic $0$, attributed to F. O. Wagner's 2022 preprint. In the standard convention for sharply doubly transitive groups, permutation characteristic $0$ means that involutions (elements of order two) have a fixed point and the product of any two distinct involutions has infinite order. The reported affirmative answer is restricted to this characteristic and is supplied source information, not an independently verified result here.

Short English statement

Must a sharply doubly transitive permutation group have a regular normal subgroup whenever a point stabilizer is locally finite, or whenever a point stabilizer contains an abelian subgroup of finite index? These are separate questions. The supplied 2022 comment reports an affirmative answer to the second question in permutation characteristic $0$.
