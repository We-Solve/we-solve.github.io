Existence of small threshold weights does not compress them efficiently

The source itself notes the existence of small threshold representations and asks for an efficient reduction from very long given coefficients. We spell out that existence argument to locate its computational gap. Consider a linear threshold function on $\{0,1\}^n$, $n\ge1$, with ties labeled positive. A representation is a vector $w$ and threshold $t$ deciding whether $w\cdot x-t\ge0$.
Proposition: Every such function has an integer representation whose $n+1$ coefficients each have absolute value at most $(n+1)^{(n+1)/2}$. This is $O(n\log(n+1))$ bits per coefficient, or $O(n^2\log(n+1))$ total bits.
Proof: If there are negative inputs, decrease the threshold by less than their minimum positive distance from the threshold. Every positive input then has positive margin and every negative input stays negative. If there are no negative inputs, choose the constant-positive representation. Scaling gives signed margins at least one. For labels $y_x\in\{-1,1\}$, the polyhedron in $d=n+1$ variables $z=(w,t)$ defined by \[ y_x(x,-1)\cdot z\ge1\qquad(x\in\{0,1\}^n) \] is therefore nonempty. Its constraint normals span $\mathbb R^d$, since multiplying by nonzero signs preserves the span of $(x,-1)$, which contains a basis from $x=0,e_1,\ldots,e_n$. It has a point with $d$ independent tight constraints. To see this directly, start at a feasible point. If tight normals do not span the space, move along a nonzero vector orthogonal to all of them. Some remaining normal has nonzero inner product with this direction. Choose a sign in which at least one slack decreases; the first tight constraint is reached after a finite positive step, preserving feasibility. Its normal is independent of the old tight normals, since it is not orthogonal to the direction. Repeat at most $d$ times. At the resulting point, solve the independent tight equations $Az=\mathbf1$. Entries of $A$ lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Cramer's rule gives $z_j=\det A_j/\det A$, where $A_j$ replaces column $j$ by ones. Each determinant has absolute value at most $d^{d/2}$: the determinant is the product of lengths of successive orthogonal components of its rows, each at most the row norm $\sqrt d$. Multiply $z$ by $|\det A|$, a positive integer. All coordinates become integers of the stated size and all margins remain positive. Thus the labels are preserved. ∎
The construction potentially uses all $2^n$ labeled inputs to identify tight constraints. Enumerating them or their $d$-tuples is not polynomial-time compression from the succinct original weights. The existence proof therefore stops before the requested algorithm. The source's per-coefficient bit convention must also not be replaced by a total-bit claim. Naive coarse rounding is insufficient: weights $(M,M+1)$ and threshold $M+1$, with $M\ge1$, label $(1,0)$ negative and $(0,1)$ positive. Replacing both weights by the same rounded value makes these two inputs indistinguishable under every threshold.
