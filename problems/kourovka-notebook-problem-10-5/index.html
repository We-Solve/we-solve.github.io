Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.5

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, let $F(X)$ be the free group on the countably infinite set $X=\{x_1,x_2,\ldots\}$, and form the free product $G*F(X)$. A word $w\in G*F(X)$ is a mixed identity of $G$ if every homomorphism $\varphi:G*F(X)\to G$ whose restriction to $G$ is the identity satisfies $\varphi(w)=1_G$. Thus variables may be replaced independently by arbitrary elements of $G$, while coefficients from $G$ retain their specified values. Write \[ \operatorname{MI}(G)=\bigcap_{\substack{\varphi:G*F(X)\to G\\ \varphi|_G=\mathrm{id}_G}}\ker\varphi. \] Use the following equational interpretation of a finite basis: a finite set $B\subseteq\operatorname{MI}(G)$ is a basis if \[ \operatorname{MI}(G)=\left\langle\!\left\langle \alpha(b)\;\middle|\; b\in B,\ \alpha\in\operatorname{End}(G*F(X)),\ \alpha|_G=\mathrm{id}_G \right\rangle\!\right\rangle_{G*F(X)}, \] where the double brackets denote normal closure. This allows substitution of words with coefficients for variables, together with the usual consequences of group equations.

Problem (V. S. Anashin, 1986). Construct a finite group $G$ for which $\operatorname{MI}(G)$ has no finite basis. In addition, determine whether the group referred to in R. Bryant, Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 14, no. 2 (1982), 119--123, is such an example.

Short English statement

Find a finite group whose equations with coefficients from that group cannot all be deduced from finitely many such equations, using the interpretation specified above. Also determine whether the group constructed in the cited 1982 paper by Bryant provides an example. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this status has not been independently verified.
