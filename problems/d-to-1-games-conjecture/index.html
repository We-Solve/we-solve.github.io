Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every integer $d\geq2$ and every $s>0$, does there exist an alphabet size $k$ such that it is NP-hard to distinguish satisfiable $d$-to-$1$ Label-Cover instances from instances of value at most $s$?

OpenAI's claim

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For every fixed rational $\delta\in(0,1)$, the claim gives $q(\delta)\ge2$ and a deterministic polynomial reduction from $3$SAT to explicit unweighted $2$-to-$1$ projection games with left alphabet $[2q]$, right alphabet $[q]$, and exactly two preimages per right label, satisfying \[ \varphi\text{ satisfiable}\Rightarrow\operatorname{val}(G)=1, \qquad\varphi\text{ unsatisfiable}\Rightarrow\operatorname{val}(G)\le\delta. \] The nonempty edge-occurrence list and every projection table are output explicitly. An accompanying graph consequence gives absolute $C,k_0$ such that for each fixed $k\ge k_0$, distinguishing a $k$-colorable graph from one for which every $k$-coloring properly colors at most $1-1/k+C\log k/k^2$ of its edges is NP-hard. The Boolean Not-Two predicate accepts zero, one or three true literals; the further claimed hardness gap is satisfiable versus value at most $5/8+\varepsilon$ for every fixed $0<\varepsilon<3/8$. Thus NP has three nonadaptive query PCPs with perfect completeness and soundness $5/8+\varepsilon$, using $O(\log n)$ random bits.
