Math-1646

Cauchy smoothing for free-coordinate tuples

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Source question and reconstruction. In one variable, perturbing a free stochastic process $A(t)$ by a free Cauchy variable is said to replace its drift by a conditional expectation and make it smooth. Is there a several-variable analogue? The extraction corrupts the epsilon glyph and omits a clear drift time differential. The aid's conservative notation is \[ C_\epsilon=\epsilon C,\qquad k_t^\epsilon=E[k_t\mid W^*(A(t)+C_\epsilon)],\qquad\epsilon>0. \] Here $E$ is a trace-preserving conditional expectation, not the scalar trace itself. The intended stochastic model is of the usual form $\mathrm dA=\mathrm dS+k_t\,\mathrm dt$, with drift allowed to depend on the past; its adaptedness, integrability, driving-noise conventions and target multivariable smoothness are not fully specified. The following aid result is at fixed time under a restrictive free-coordinate hypothesis.

Reported one-coordinate posterior. Work in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state $\tau$. Let $A_i$ be bounded self-adjoint and $C_i$ a standard Cauchy-distributed self-adjoint affiliated variable, free from $A_i$. Unbounded affiliated variables are needed because the Cauchy law is not a bounded or square-integrable variable. Put $B_i=A_i+\epsilon C_i$, $\mu_i=\operatorname{law}(A_i)$, and \[ P_\epsilon(u)=\frac{\epsilon}{\pi(u^2+\epsilon^2)},\qquad (T_i f)(y)= \frac{\int f(x)P_\epsilon(y-x)\,\mathrm d\mu_i(x)} {\int P_\epsilon(y-x)\,\mathrm d\mu_i(x)}. \] The denominator is strictly positive. The aid reports \[ E_{W^*(B_i)}[f(A_i)]=(T_i f)(B_i). \] For the required square-integrable inputs this is an $L^2$ contraction, preserving the trace. The real function $T_if$ is locally real analytic; the aid reports quantitative derivative bounds, but supplies no explicit constants or norm-radius formula. Smoothness of these scalar posterior factors is not an automatically defined global noncommutative smoothness theorem.

Reported free-product multivariable special case. Assume the pair algebras $\mathcal M_i=W^*(A_i,C_i)$ are mutually free, and let \[ \mathcal B=W^*(B_1,\ldots,B_m). \] For a reduced word $f_1(A_{i_1})\cdots f_\ell(A_{i_\ell})$ with bounded centered factors $\tau(f_j(A_{i_j}))=0$ and adjacent indices distinct, the aid reports \[ E_{\mathcal B}\left[\prod_{j=1}^\ell f_j(A_{i_j})\right] =\prod_{j=1}^\ell(T_{i_j}f_j)(B_{i_j}). \] The output factors remain centered. Freeness gives the exact word norm formula and contraction \[ \left\|E_{\mathcal B}\left[\prod_j f_j(A_{i_j})\right]\right\|_2 =\prod_j\|(T_{i_j}f_j)(B_{i_j})\|_2 \leq\prod_j\|f_j(A_{i_j})\|_2 =\left\|\prod_jf_j(A_{i_j})\right\|_2. \] Finite reduced-word sums extend by the stated $L^2$ continuity. The bounded-input framework makes products well defined; extension to arbitrary unbounded products requires additional domain/integrability hypotheses. This is a reduced-free-product conditional expectation, not a componentwise multiplicative map on all noncommutative polynomials. General dependent coordinate tuples are not covered.

Reported multiplicativity obstruction. For a von Neumann subalgebra $\mathcal B$ and bounded self-adjoint $X$, the identity \[ E_{\mathcal B}(X^2)=E_{\mathcal B}(X)^2 \] forces $X=E_{\mathcal B}(X)\in\mathcal B$, by faithfulness of the trace and vanishing $L^2$ conditional variance. Thus a proposed universally multiplicative componentwise smoother is trivial on such hidden coordinates. This does not obstruct the centered alternating-word formula, which does not assert multiplicativity within a single coordinate algebra. The source's general several-variable smoothing question and path-dependent drift regularity remain beyond this supplied special case.

Short English statement

The aid gives a multivariable Cauchy posterior only when the coordinate pairs are freely independent. Conditional expectation acts factorwise on centered alternating words and contracts their $L^2$ norms. It cannot be promoted to a multiplicative map on all polynomials unless the hidden variable is already observed.
