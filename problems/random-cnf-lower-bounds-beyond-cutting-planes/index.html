Research attempt: Simulation direction and the failure of width padding

The direction needed to transfer a lower bound.
Write $L_P(F)$ for shortest refutation length in a system $P$. If every $P$ refutation of length $s$ can be transformed into a Cutting Planes refutation of length at most $a(s+|F|)^d$, then \[ L_{\mathrm{CP}}(F)\le a(L_P(F)+|F|)^d. \] A sufficiently large lower bound on the left therefore gives a lower bound on $L_P(F)$. Conversely, a simulation of Cutting Planes by $P$ only proves an upper bound on $L_P$ from $L_{\mathrm{CP}}$ and has no such lower-bound consequence. This is a direction-of-inequality issue, not an appeal to relative power by name.
A concrete simulation calculation.
Resolution clauses can be written as $L_C\ge1$, where $L_C$ is the sum of their $0/1$ literals. Suppose a resolution step has parents $A\lor x$ and $B\lor\neg x$, with $A,B$ not containing the pivot. Addition of the parent inequalities gives \[ L_A+L_B\ge1. \] Assume the resolvent $C=A\lor B$ is not tautological. Every literal of $C$ occurs once or twice in this sum. Add the valid nonnegativity inequality for each literal occurring once, obtaining $2L_C\ge1$, and round after dividing by two to obtain $L_C\ge1$. Literal nonnegativity is exactly $x\ge0$ or $1-x\ge0$. A tautological resolvent follows directly from these bounds and $x+(1-x)=1$. Thus a resolution step has a Cutting Planes simulation with $O(n)$ additional lines and small coefficients. This verifies one legitimate transfer direction for a weaker system. It does not extend a Cutting Planes lower bound to systems that merely simulate Cutting Planes.
Naive width padding changes satisfiability.
Given a clause $C$, append a fresh positive variable $z_C$. The padded formula $\bigwedge_C(C\lor z_C)$ is always satisfiable by setting all the new variables to one, even if the original formula was inconsistent. Appending fresh literals once therefore cannot convert a fixed-width refutation problem into a wider one.

A satisfiability-preserving replacement does exist. With $d$ fresh variables, replace $C$ by all $2^d$ clauses \[ C\lor\ell_1\lor\cdots\lor\ell_d, \qquad \ell_i\in\{z_i,\neg z_i\}. \] If $C$ is true every extension holds. If $C$ is false, any assignment to the new variables falsifies the extension whose literals are all false; thus the conjunction is equivalent to $C$. Opposite-literal resolution, or the Cutting Planes addition-and-rounding calculation, recovers $C$ in $2^d-1$ resolution steps or twice as many Cutting Planes steps. But the clauses in the block have a common old clause and common fresh variable set and contain every sign pattern. They are strongly dependent, unlike independently sampled random clauses. The replacement also multiplies the number of clauses by $2^d$. Hence it provides neither the original random distribution nor a cost-free transfer of a random logarithmic-width lower bound to constant width.

The converse temptation also fails: a lower bound on random wide formulas says nothing about this highly structured padded family without a separate distributional reduction. These are exact obstructions to the attempted route, rather than assertions that no other proof-system lower bound can be obtained.
