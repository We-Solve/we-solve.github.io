Math-6480

Discrete Interaction-Matrix Inequalities in Three Dimensions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each positive integer $p$, let $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_p)\in({\mathbb{R}}^3)^p$ satisfy $a_i\ne a_j$ whenever $i\ne j$. Write $a_i=(a_i^1,a_i^2,a_i^3)$ and define the real symmetric matrix $A(a)=(\alpha_{ij}(a))_{i,j=1}^p$ by \[ \alpha_{ii}=0,\qquad \alpha_{ij}=\frac1{|a_i-a_j|}\quad(i\ne j). \] All vector norms are Euclidean. The matrix derivative $\partial A/\partial a_i^k$ differentiates the entries with respect to coordinate $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ of point $a_i$, holding the other coordinates fixed. For $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_p)\in{\mathbb{R}}^p$, this gives \[ u^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial A}{\partial a_i^k}u =-2u_i\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{u_j(a_i^k-a_j^k)}{|a_i-a_j|^3}. \]

The conjecture is the following uniform inequality: \[ \forall p\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\ \exists c(p)>0\ \forall a\in({\mathbb{R}}^3)^p \text{ with pairwise distinct entries}\ \forall u\in{\mathbb{R}}^p, \] \[ \max_{\substack{1\le i\le p\\1\le k\le3}} \left|u^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial A(a)}{\partial a_i^k}u\right| +|A(a)u|^2 \ \ge\ c(p)\sum_{\substack{1\le i,j\le p\\i\ne j}} \frac{u_i^2}{|a_i-a_j|^2}. \] The finite maximum is the source's $\sup_{i,k}$. The right-hand sum is over ordered pairs, exactly as written in the source. The constant may depend on $p$ but must be independent of both the configuration and $u$; no lower bound on separation or bound on diameter is added. The substantive cases have $p\ge2$; allowing $p=1$ gives the vacuous zero inequality.

The supplied literature assessment also mentions a proposed continuous limit, but no continuous-limit formulation is present in the source record. It is therefore recorded as missing context, not reconstructed as another conjecture.

Short English statement

The question asks for a positive constant depending only on the number of points that controls the weighted squared coordinates of every real vector by the interaction matrix and its coordinate derivatives, uniformly over all distinct configurations in three dimensions.
