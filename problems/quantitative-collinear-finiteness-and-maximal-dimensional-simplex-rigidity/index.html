Quantitative collinear finiteness and maximal-dimensional simplex rigidity

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. A central configuration has $\nabla_{q_i}U=-\lambda m_iq_i$ in center-of-mass coordinates for a common scalar $\lambda$. After fixing $I=1$, it is a critical point of $U$ on the collision-free inertia ellipsoid. Congruent configurations and scales are factored out for a finiteness count. A relative equilibrium is a rigidly rotating solution with time-independent shape.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the Newtonian $n$-body problem with $n\ge4$ and every choice of positive masses $m_i>0$, prove that the number of central configurations is finite, or give a counterexample, counting configurations modulo the standard translation, scale and rotational equivalences.
Scope and source qualification.
The source asks for all positive masses, not only generic masses or collinear configurations. The spatial configuration class must be stated; finiteness of normalized labeled configurations without rotation quotient would not be the intended statement. The catalogue title/status was paired with a machine-generated research attempt; that attempt is not peer-reviewed evidence of settlement. The statement covers the original source questions rather than only the title’s subcase.

Short English statement

Determine whether every positive-mass n-body system with n≥4 has finitely many central configurations up to the usual symmetries.

Collinear finiteness from strict convexity.
Fix positive labeled masses and one ordering $x_1<\cdots<x_n$ on a line. Consider \[W(x)=\sum_{i<j}\frac{m_im_j}{x_j-x_i}+\frac12\sum_im_ix_i^2. \] The chamber is convex. Along a nonzero vector $v$, its Hessian quadratic form is \[D^2W[v,v]=\sum_{i<j}\frac{2m_im_j(v_j-v_i)^2}{(x_j-x_i)^3}+\sum_im_iv_i^2>0. \] Thus W is strictly convex. It tends to infinity when a collision boundary is approached with bounded coordinates, or when the coordinate norm tends to infinity, so it attains a unique minimum in the chamber. At that point $\partial_iU=-m_ix_i$. Summing derivatives gives $\sum m_ix_i=0$, so it is a centered collinear central configuration.
Counting up to scale and reversal.
Every collinear central configuration has a positive multiplier $\lambda$, because $\lambda I=U>0$. Scaling coordinates by $\lambda^{1/3}$ changes the multiplier to one, putting it at the unique minimum for its ordering. Thus each labeled ordering gives exactly one shape. There are $n!$ orderings; identifying reversal by a spatial rotation gives $n!/2$ labeled collinear classes for $n\ge2$. Additional identifications of equal-mass labels can only reduce that count and are a separate convention.
Full target remaining.
Strict convexity relies on fixed one-dimensional order and the convexity of reciprocal positive gaps. Noncollinear chambers do not have that structure, and the potential Hessian has negative tangential directions. This reconstructs the known collinear subcase for all positive masses, not finiteness of all normalized central configurations for $n\ge4$. The first missing step is excluding continuous families or accumulation of noncollinear critical shapes after rotational reduction, including exceptional mass choices.
