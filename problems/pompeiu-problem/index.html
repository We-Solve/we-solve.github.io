Math-386

Pompeiu problem

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the conventional Euclidean formulation, fix a dimension $n\geq2$ and a bounded nonempty domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$, where domain means open and connected. A congruent copy is $R\Omega+a$, with $R\in O(n)$ and $a\in\mathbb R^n$. Consider continuous complex-valued functions on $\mathbb R^n$ and Lebesgue integration.

Pompeiu problem, in the source's failure-of-uniqueness form. Characterize the domains $\Omega$ for which there exists $f\in C(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C)$, not identically zero, such that \[ \int_{R\Omega+a}f(x)\,dx=0 \qquad\text{for every }R\in O(n)\text{ and }a\in\mathbb R^n. \] A domain is conventionally said to have the Pompeiu property when this vanishing condition instead forces $f=0$. Thus the displayed target asks for domains failing that property, not for those having it. The source sentence itself does not define the ambient dimension, function class, boundedness, or boundary regularity; the displayed version is an explicit standard setting, not a claim that all these hypotheses were supplied.

The research summary reports partial progress for balls and special domains and mentions boundary-regularity necessary conditions, without stating those results. Its phrase balls as Pompeiu sets does not specify whether it means the property or the failure class just defined. That terminology cannot be used to reverse the source's existential target. The supplied status is partial progress/open, rather than a verified complete characterization.

Short English statement

Classify domains admitting a nonzero function whose integral vanishes over every rigidly moved copy. This is the failure of the usual Pompeiu uniqueness property; the source leaves the function and domain classes unspecified.
