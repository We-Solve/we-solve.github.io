Math-342

Generic Mather measures, periodic support, and rational locking flats

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $M$ be a compact manifold and $\mathbb T={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$. In the usual Tonelli interpretation, $L:TM\times\mathbb T\to{\mathbb{R}}$ is time-periodic, fiberwise strictly convex and superlinear, with the required complete Euler--Lagrange flow. For $c\in H^1(M;{\mathbb{R}})$, choose a closed form $\eta_c$ representing it and let $\mathcal M_c$ be the invariant probability measures minimizing $\int(L-\eta_c(v))\,\mathrm d\mu$. Its minimum is $-\alpha(c)$. The Mather set is the union of supports of these minimizing measures. Ergodic minimizing measures are the extreme invariant components. Write $b_1(M)=\dim H^1(M;{\mathbb{R}})$.

Problem 21. The source attributes generic uniqueness of the minimizing measure, for generic $L$ and generic $c$, to Mañé. Must this unique measure generically be supported on a periodic orbit? It separately records Jeff Xia's conjecture: for generic $L$, the number of ergodic minimizing measures is finite for every $c\in H^1(M;{\mathbb{R}})$. Genericity requires a topology and perturbation category; generic $c$, generic pairs and a residual set of $L$ with residual sets of classes are not automatically the same formulation.

Reported literature scope. The aid attributes to Bernard and Contreras (2008) the stronger Mañé-generic bound \[ \#\{\text{ergodic }c\text{-minimizing measures}\}\leq1+b_1(M) \quad\text{for every }c. \] Mañé genericity here concerns residual potential perturbations of a fixed Tonelli Lagrangian; the precise differentiability/topology and full theorem hypotheses must be checked from the missing primary reference. The aid reports the general periodic-support question unresolved in the full time-periodic arbitrary-dimensional category. This is a supplied literature assessment, not an independent updated search.

Reported rational-locking obstruction. For a fixed time-periodic $L$, a periodic orbit of period an integer $q>0$ has rotation vector $h\in H_1(M;{\mathbb{Q}})$, viewed in real homology. Its measure being $c$-minimizing implies $h\in\partial\alpha(c)$. Define the closed rational subgradient fibers \[ F_h=\{c:h\in\partial\alpha(c)\},\qquad h\in H_1(M;{\mathbb{Q}}). \] If periodic minimizing measures exist for a residual set of $c$ in a nonempty open region $U\subseteq H^1(M;{\mathbb{R}})$, the aid reports that \[ \bigcup_{h\in H_1(M;{\mathbb{Q}})}\operatorname{int}_U(F_h\cap U) \quad\text{is dense in }U. \] If $U$ is convex and $\alpha$ is strictly convex there, each $F_h\cap U$ contains at most one point. Hence periodic minimizing classes in $U$ are at most countable, and are meagre when $b_1(M)>0$. The positive-dimensional qualification is essential: a singleton zero-dimensional cohomology space is not meagre in itself. This is a necessary obstruction for a fixed $L$, not a proof that the Lagrangian-generic periodicity question is false.

Reported flat-torus calibration. For the time-periodic extension of $L(x,v)=|v|^2/2$ on $M=\mathbb T^n$, $n\geq1$, the exact model has $\alpha(c)=|c|^2/2$, and minimizing measures are supported on $v=c$. When \[ k\cdot c+m\ne0\quad\text{for every }(k,m)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^n\times{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{(0,0)\}, \] the translation with frequency $(c,1)$ on the extended $(x,t)$-torus is uniquely ergodic; the unique minimizing measure is Haar measure on that torus at $v=c$, and is nonperiodic. Such classes form a residual full-Lebesgue-measure set. This explicitly distinguishes uniqueness from periodic support for a fixed integrable example; it is not a generic perturbation theorem. All supplementary results require independent verification.

Short English statement

Keep generic uniqueness, generic periodic support and Xia's all-cohomology finiteness conjecture separate. The aid reports Bernard--Contreras finiteness and a rational-subgradient obstruction to periodic support, with a flat-torus example of unique nonperiodic minimizers. None of these fixed-system observations settles the full generic periodicity question.
