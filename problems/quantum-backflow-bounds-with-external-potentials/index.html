Quantum Backflow Bounds with External Potentials

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In one-dimensional quantum mechanics, the probability current of a wave function is $j_\psi(x)=\frac{\hbar}{m}\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\psi(x)}\partial_x\psi(x))$. A smeared flux integrates this current against a spatial test function or the source's associated quadratic form. Positive incoming states are scattering states whose asymptotic incoming momentum is positive. Backflow is negative current despite that positive incoming momentum. A lower bound compares the flux quadratic form with the state norm uniformly over this positive-incoming subspace. The primary smearing convention is a nonnegative Schwartz function $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ and $J_f=\int f(x)J(x)\,dx$ as a quadratic form. The scattering Hamiltonian is $H=P^2/2+V(X)$ in units $\hbar=m=1$.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For a quantum particle scattering from an external potential, is the smeared probability-flux operator on positive incoming states bounded below, analogously to the free-particle quantum-backflow inequality?
Source scope and qualification.
The potential class, smearing function and scattering-wave-operator assumptions are essential and must follow the report. Positivity of incoming momentum does not mean the interacting state has positive momentum support at every time.

Short English statement

Does quantum backflow remain bounded when a particle scatters through an external potential?
