Accumulation Points of Convex Billiard Length Spectra

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question concerns a real-analytic convex billiard domain $\Omega$, its perimeter $P=|\partial\Omega|$, and its length spectrum. The record does not explicitly specify the ambient space or the convention for the length spectrum. The following gives a precise conditional formulation under the usual planar Euclidean interpretation; these conventions require confirmation against the source.

Assume that $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$ is a bounded open convex domain with regular real-analytic boundary. A periodic billiard trajectory is a closed polygonal path with finitely many nonzero edges, vertices on $\partial\Omega$, and edge interiors in $\Omega$, satisfying specular reflection at each vertex. Explicitly, if $v_-$ and $v_+$ are the incoming and outgoing unit velocity vectors and $n$ is the outward unit normal, then \[ v_+=v_- -2\langle v_-,n\rangle n. \] Under the convention that repeated traversals are included and that lengths are recorded as a set, define \[ \mathcal L(\Omega)=\{\ell(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a periodic billiard trajectory in }\Omega\}, \] where $\ell(\gamma)$ is the total Euclidean length of the polygonal path. Define its set of finite accumulation points by \[ \operatorname{Acc}\mathcal L(\Omega) =\left\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\quad \mathcal L(\Omega)\cap\left((a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon)\setminus\{a\}\right)\ne\varnothing\right\}. \] Thus accumulation concerns distinct length values, rather than repeated occurrences of the same value.

Question under these conventions. For every such $\Omega$, does \[ \operatorname{Acc}\mathcal L(\Omega) \subseteq \{mP:m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ m\ge 1\} \] hold?

The wording "the only accumulation points" could additionally intend the stronger assertion \[ \operatorname{Acc}\mathcal L(\Omega) =\{mP:m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ m\ge 1\}. \] The supplied record does not distinguish these readings. It also does not explicitly require strict convexity or everywhere positive curvature, specify whether only primitive trajectories are counted, or address degenerate or grazing trajectories. These qualifications remain unresolved; no stronger convexity hypothesis is imposed here.

Short English statement

Under an explicitly provisional planar billiard convention, ask whether every finite accumulation point of the set of periodic trajectory lengths is a positive integer multiple of the boundary perimeter. The source leaves some geometric and spectral conventions unspecified, and its wording may intend equality with all positive perimeter multiples.
