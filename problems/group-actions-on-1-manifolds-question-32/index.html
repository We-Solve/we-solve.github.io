Math-538

Group Actions on 1-Manifolds — Question 32

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S^1={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$, with its standard smooth structure and orientation. Let \[ G=\operatorname{Diff}^{2}_{+}(S^1) \] be the group, under composition, of all orientation-preserving bijections $f:S^1\to S^1$ such that both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are of class $C^2$. Equivalently, each element has a $C^2$ lift $F:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}$ satisfying $F(x+1)=F(x)+1$ and $F'(x)>0$ for every $x\in{\mathbb{R}}$.

An abstract group is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. The supplied question is: is $G$ simple? Equivalently, does \[ \forall N\leq G,\qquad N\trianglelefteq G\ \Longrightarrow\ \left(N=\{\operatorname{id}_{S^1}\}\ \text{or}\ N=G\right) \] hold? Here simplicity is interpreted in the usual abstract group sense, with no closedness requirement on $N$. The supplied record does not explicitly distinguish abstract simplicity from topological simplicity.

Short English statement

Does the group of all orientation-preserving $C^2$ diffeomorphisms of the circle have no proper nontrivial normal subgroup? Simplicity is understood here in the abstract group sense.
