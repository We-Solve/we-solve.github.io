Gaussian Extremizers for Strichartz Inequalities

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $d\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$ and set $p_d=2+4/d$. Use the normalization of the free Schrödinger equation \[ i\partial_tu+\Delta_xu=0,\qquad u(0,x)=f(x),\qquad (t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^d, \] where $\Delta_x=\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_{x_j}^2$, $f\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d;{\mathbb{C}})$, and $u(t)=e^{it\Delta}f$. The source does not specify the equation's normalization; this convention fixes the meaning of the solution and its sharp constant. All norms below use Lebesgue measure, and the spacetime norm extends over all time and space: \[ \|u\|_{L^{p_d}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^d)} =\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^d}|u(t,x)|^{p_d}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t\right)^{1/p_d}. \] Define the sharp Strichartz constant by \[ C_d=\sup_{0\ne f\in L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d;{\mathbb{C}})} \frac{\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{p_d}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^d)}}{\|f\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d)}}. \] An extremizer is a nonzero initial datum attaining this supremum, equivalently attaining equality in the inequality with constant $C_d$.

Question. Do Gaussian initial data attain the sharp constant $C_d$ for every positive integer $d$? Under the usual isotropic interpretation, a precise formulation is: with $g_d(x)=e^{-|x|^2}$, does \[ \|e^{it\Delta}g_d\|_{L^{2+4/d}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^d)} =C_d\|g_d\|_{L^2({\mathbb{R}}^d)} \qquad\text{hold for every }d\geq1? \] The source does not define its Gaussian class or specify whether its wording means existence of Gaussian extremizers or extremality of every member of a Gaussian class. The displayed formulation records the standard isotropic candidate; an interpretation encompassing arbitrary anisotropic or complex quadratic Gaussian profiles requires clarification. The question concerns attainment, and does not request a classification of all extremizers.

The supplied record labels the problem partially solved. Its dated assessment discusses hyperboloid endpoint constants and extremizers, including purported results for all non-endpoints in dimensions one and two, and says that the all-dimensional Gaussian-extremizer question remains open. The connection between those hyperboloid claims and this free Schrödinger inequality is not explained in the supplied evidence. These are supplied curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or literature conclusions.

Short English statement

For each positive integer dimension, consider the sharp spacetime Strichartz inequality for the free Schrödinger evolution of square-integrable initial data. The question asks whether Gaussian initial data attain its best constant in every dimension. The precise Gaussian class is unspecified; the displayed draft uses the standard isotropic Gaussian candidate. The supplied partial-solution label and hyperboloid literature discussion require independent review.
