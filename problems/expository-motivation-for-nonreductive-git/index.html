Expository Motivation for Nonreductive GIT

Definitions and mathematical statement

Geometric invariant theory (GIT) studies quotients associated with algebraic group actions. In a GIT presentation with a group $G$ acting on a space $X$ and a chosen linearization $L$, write $X^{\mathrm{ss}}(L)$ for the semistable locus and $X^{\mathrm{us}}(L)=X\setminus X^{\mathrm{ss}}(L)$ for the unstable locus. This notation only explains the terminology: the supplied record specifies no base field, acting group, parameter space, or linearization.

The retained source question is:

Why is non-reductive geometric invariant theory needed to construct moduli spaces of unstable curves and related unstable objects, even when the initial presentation involves a reductive group action?
The record does not identify the curves, the related objects, their stability conditions, the intended moduli construction, or the meaning of necessity in this question. Consequently, it supplies no precise mathematical proposition or quantifiers to formalize. In particular, no universal assertion that non-reductive GIT is necessary is made here.
