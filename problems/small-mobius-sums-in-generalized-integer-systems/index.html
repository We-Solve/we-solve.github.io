Math-7081

Small Möbius Sums in Generalized Integer Systems

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard discrete Beurling interpretation: let $\mathcal P=(p_j)_{j\geq1}$ be a sequence of labelled generalized primes satisfying $1<p_1\leq p_2\leq\cdots$ and $p_j\to\infty$. A generalized integer is a formal product $n=\prod_j p_j^{a_j}$, where $a_j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}$ and only finitely many $a_j$ are nonzero. Its numerical value is $v(n)=\prod_j p_j^{a_j}$. Let $\mathcal N$ denote these formal products, including the empty product of value $1$; distinct formal products are counted separately even when their numerical values coincide. Define \[ N(x)=\#\{n\in\mathcal N:v(n)\leq x\},\qquad x\geq1, \] and define the generalized Möbius function by \[ \mu_{\mathcal P}(n)= \begin{cases} (-1)^{\sum_j a_j},&a_j\in\{0,1\}\text{ for every }j,\\ 0,&a_j\geq2\text{ for some }j. \end{cases} \] Thus $\mu_{\mathcal P}(1)=1$, and \[ M_{\mathcal P}(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\in\mathcal N\\v(n)\leq x}}\mu_{\mathcal P}(n). \] Assume \[ N(x)=x^{1+o(1)}\qquad(x\to\infty), \] meaning that $\log N(x)/\log x\to1$.

Problem. Among Beurling generalized integer systems satisfying this counting hypothesis, determine how small the asymptotic growth of $M_{\mathcal P}(x)$ can be.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The source does not specify a comparison scale for "how small", such as an optimal power exponent, an eventual bound for $\lvert M_{\mathcal P}(x)\rvert$, or an oscillation criterion. Consequently, it supplies an extremal research question rather than a fully specified conjectured inequality. The discrete, multiplicity-counted convention above makes the summation precise, but the supplied record does not explicitly state that convention. Its literature assessment mentions an exponent below one half and a proposed nonperiodic zero-set improvement without defining the associated bound, zero set, or improvement; these remarks do not determine an additional precise conjecture.

Short English statement

For a Beurling system whose generalized integers have counting function $N(x)=x^{1+o(1)}$, ask how slowly its generalized Möbius partial sums can grow. The source leaves the exact measure of smallness unspecified. This statement uses the standard discrete interpretation with multiplicities and requires human mathematical review.
