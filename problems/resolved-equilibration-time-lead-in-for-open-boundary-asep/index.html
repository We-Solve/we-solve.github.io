Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not determine a well-posed open mathematical problem. Its recoverable question is: how long does the stochastic system under discussion take to reach equilibrium? The title identifies the context as the asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) with open boundaries, but the supplied statement specifies neither the process nor its state space, transition rates, initial conditions, boundary parameters, stationary distribution, convergence criterion, or asymptotic size parameter. Consequently, no precise equilibration-time or mixing-time assertion can be extracted without additional source material.

The accompanying text labels a result as "Theorem 1 ([1, Theorem 1.1])" and gives the hypothesis \[ \beta<\alpha. \] The meanings and admissible domains of $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\rho$, and the definition of the random height $h(t,0)$, are absent. As $t\to\infty$, the damaged display appears to describe three distributional limits involving centering by $\mu t$ or $\mu_\rho t$, scaling by $\sigma t^{1/3}$ or $\sigma_\rho t^{1/2}$, and limiting distribution functions $F_{\mathrm{GSE}}$, $F_{\mathrm{GOE}}$, and $\Phi$, respectively. Here the source identifies the first two as Tracy--Widom GSE and GOE distribution functions, and $\Phi$ as the standard normal distribution function, \[ \Phi(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{-u^2/2}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} u, \qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}}. \] The source calls $\mu,\sigma,\mu_\rho,\sigma_\rho$ explicit constants, but supplies no formulas or parameter dependence. The probability expressions and their signs are corrupted. The regime thresholds are rendered as "12" and "21"; interpreting these as $1/2$ would require confirmation. The intended quantification over $s$ and the Tracy--Widom normalization conventions are also unspecified. Thus these fragments are retained as provenance, not asserted as a reconstructed theorem.

The supplied verification field classifies the statement as unrecoverable and reports that the equilibration-time question is a lead-in immediately followed by a claim that the reported joint work obtains the sharp cutoff profile. It therefore does not identify a separate open problem. This conflicts with the supplied open-status label and broad KPZ mixing-time assessment. No new conjecture, cutoff formula, or general KPZ universality claim is asserted here.

Short English statement

The record asks how long an open-boundary ASEP system takes to equilibrate, but omits the model and the meaning of equilibration. Its adjoining limit theorem is corrupted and cannot be stated reliably. Supplied verification says the question introduces work obtaining a sharp cutoff profile, whereas other supplied fields label it open. Human source review is required before a precise mathematical target can be selected.
