Kourovka Notebook Problem 17.90

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a group $G$, define its lower central series by \[ \gamma_1(G)=G,\qquad \gamma_{n+1}(G)=[G,\gamma_n(G)]\quad(n\geq 1), \] where $[A,B]$ is the subgroup generated by the commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$. The group $G$ is residually nilpotent if \[ \bigcap_{n\geq 1}\gamma_n(G)=\{1\}. \] A group $G$ is called parafree if it is residually nilpotent and there exists a free group $F$ such that \[ G/\gamma_n(G)\cong F/\gamma_n(F)\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq 1. \] Here the source's phrase "the same lower central quotients" is interpreted in the standard sense of these quotient groups, with one fixed free group $F$.

Question (Kourovka Notebook, Problem 17.90). For every finitely generated parafree group $G$, must \[ H_2(G;{\mathbb{Z}})=0 \] hold? Here $H_2(G;{\mathbb{Z}})$ denotes second group homology with trivial integral coefficients. The source writes $H_2(G)$ without specifying coefficients; the integral interpretation follows the usual convention and requires confirmation in review.

Short English statement

Must every finitely generated parafree group have trivial second integral homology? A parafree group is residually nilpotent and has the same lower central quotient groups as a free group. Integral coefficients are understood by convention because the source does not specify them.
