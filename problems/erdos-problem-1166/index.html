Statement

Almost surely, only $O((\log n)^2)$ different sites have ever been favourites by time $n$. We prove the maximum-local-time upper bound directly, and give the deterministic record-level argument converting a bound on simultaneous favourites to a bound on all historical favourites.

Statement and input

Almost surely, only $O((\log n)^2)$ different sites have ever been favourites by time $n$. We prove the maximum-local-time upper bound directly, and give the deterministic record-level argument converting a bound on simultaneous favourites to a bound on all historical favourites.

The one deep input is Hao--Li--Okada--Zheng, Favorite Sites for Simple Random Walk in Two and More Dimensions, Theorem 1.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00995: for planar simple random walk, almost surely $\limsup_n|F(n)|=3$, hence $|F(n)|\le3$ eventually. Their theorem concerns dimension two. Tóth's earlier three-favourite theorem concerned dimension one, so we do not attribute the planar input to that earlier paper.

An elementary lower bound on the return-time tail

Let $S_n$ be simple symmetric nearest-neighbour walk on $\mathbb Z^2$, let $u_j=\mathbb P(S_j=0)$, and let $q_j$ be the probability of no positive return to zero by time $j$. Rotating coordinates to $(X+Y,X-Y)$ turns the two coordinate processes into independent one-dimensional simple walks. Consequently \[ u_{2j}=\left(\frac{\binom{2j}{j}}{4^j}\right)^2\le\frac1{j+1}, \qquad u_{2j+1}=0. \tag{1} \] The binomial inequality follows by induction: the squared ratio of consecutive normalized central binomial coefficients is $(2j+1)^2/(4(j+1)^2)\le(j+1)/(j+2)$. In particular $u_j\le2/(j+1)$ and \[ G_N:=\sum_{j=0}^N u_j\le2(1+\log(N+1)). \] Decompose a walk according to its last visit to zero before time $4m$. The Markov property gives the exact renewal identity \[ 1=\sum_{j=0}^{4m}u_jq_{4m-j}. \] For $j\le3m$, monotonicity gives $q_{4m-j}\le q_m$. The other $m$ terms have total at most $2m/(3m+1)<2/3$, by $1$. Therefore \[ q_m\ge\frac1{3G_{4m}} \ge\frac1{6(1+\log(4m+1))} \ge\frac1{24\log m}\qquad(m\ge3). \tag{2} \] This quantitative tail bound is obtained without invoking a local-time limit theorem.

A summable bound for excessive local time

Write $T_N=\max_x f_N(x)$. Once the walk first reaches a site, its successive positive return gaps there are independent with the return-time law, by the strong Markov property. If it visits that site at least $k$ times before time $N$, then the next $k-1$ return gaps are all at most $N$. Its conditional probability is at most $(1-q_N)^{k-1}$.

Every site visited by time $N$ is first visited at one of $N+1$ times. Applying the same conditional bound at each such time and taking a union bound gives \[ \mathbb P(T_N\ge k)\le(N+1)(1-q_N)^{k-1} \le(N+1)\exp\left(-\frac{k-1}{24\log N}\right). \tag{3} \] Choose $k=\lceil120(\log N)^2\rceil+1$. The right side is at most $2N^{-4}$ for $N\ge3$, so its sum over all integer $N$ is finite. Borel--Cantelli proves \[ T_N=O((\log N)^2)\qquad\text{almost surely, eventually}.\tag{4} \]

Favourites can accumulate only within a record-level plateau

This part is deterministic for any walk. The integer $T_n$ is nondecreasing and increases by at most one in a step. During a time interval on which $T_n=m$ is fixed, the set $F(n)$ can only grow: a visit to a site already at level $m$ would raise the maximum to $m+1$ and end the interval, while another site can enter $F(n)$ by reaching level $m$. No existing favourite disappears before the interval ends.

Work on an outcome where the planar three-favourite theorem holds after a finite random time $n_0$. For each value of the maximum encountered between $n_0$ and $N$, all the sites that are favourites during that part of its plateau belong to its last favourite set before $N$ or before the next increase. That set has size at most three. There are at most $T_N$ such levels. All earlier favourite sites belong to a finite random set $C_0$. Hence \[ \left|\bigcup_{k\le N}F(k)\right|\le |C_0|+3T_N. \tag{5} \] This reasoning includes a plateau straddling $n_0$ and the final plateau truncated at $N$; neither requires the next record time to have occurred. Combining $4$ and $5$ proves the claimed almost-sure logarithmic-square bound.

Assessment

The return estimate, maximum-local-time bound, and record-level counting are proved. The only imported probabilistic result is eventual at-most-three simultaneous favourites for planar walk. The conclusion is pathwise eventual, not merely an expectation or high-probability bound.
