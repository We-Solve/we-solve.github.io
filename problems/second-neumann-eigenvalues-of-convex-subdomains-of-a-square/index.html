Math-6793

Second Neumann Eigenvalues of Convex Subdomains of a Square

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Interpret the unit square as the open domain $D=(0,1)^2\subset{\mathbb{R}}^2$. A convex subdomain is understood in the usual sense as a nonempty open convex set $\Omega\subseteq D$. For each such domain $U$, define its Neumann eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, by \[ 0=\lambda_0(U)<\lambda_1(U)\leq\lambda_2(U)\leq\cdots. \] Here $\lambda$ is a Neumann eigenvalue if there exists a nonzero real-valued function $u\in H^1(U)$ such that \[ \int_U \nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x =\lambda\int_U uv\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x \qquad\text{for every }v\in H^1(U). \] This is the weak formulation of $-\Delta u=\lambda u$ in $U$, with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition $\partial_\nu u=0$ on $\partial U$; it does not require a classical normal derivative at corners.

The supplied source asks for the inequality \[ \forall\,\Omega\subseteq D\text{ nonempty, open, and convex}, \qquad \mu_2(\Omega)\geq\mu_2(D). \] The title identifies these as Neumann eigenvalues, but the supplied record does not define their indexing. Consequently, the intended precise assertion remains unresolved between the following conventions:

  • If the zero eigenvalue is indexed by $1$, then $\mu_2(U)=\lambda_1(U)$, and the assertion is \[ \forall\,\Omega\subseteq D\text{ nonempty, open, and convex}, \qquad \lambda_1(\Omega)\geq\lambda_1(D). \]
  • If the zero eigenvalue is indexed by $0$, then $\mu_2(U)=\lambda_2(U)$, and the assertion is \[ \forall\,\Omega\subseteq D\text{ nonempty, open, and convex}, \qquad \lambda_2(\Omega)\geq\lambda_2(D). \]
The preceding spectral convention must be recovered from the source before selecting one formulation. The supplied record labels the problem open and the reconstructed statement corrected_verified; these are supplied curation labels, not independent verification by this draft.

Short English statement

The proposed inequality says that every convex subdomain of the unit square has a specified Neumann eigenvalue at least as large as the corresponding eigenvalue of the square. The supplied excerpt leaves unresolved whether $\mu_2$ means the first positive eigenvalue or the second positive eigenvalue, counted with multiplicity.
