Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.53

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $q\geq 3$ be an integer. A prime power means an integer $p^e$, where $p$ is prime and $e\geq 1$ is an integer. Let $H=(V,E)$ be a connected simple undirected graph of diameter $3$, and let $d$ denote graph distance. For $y\in V$, write $H(y)=\{z\in V:\{y,z\}\in E\}$. The graph is distance-regular with intersection array \[ \{b_0,b_1,b_2;c_1,c_2,c_3\} \] if, for every ordered pair $(x,y)$ with $d(x,y)=i$, the quantities \[ \left|\{z\in H(y):d(x,z)=i+1\}\right|=b_i\quad(0\leq i\leq 2) \] and \[ \left|\{z\in H(y):d(x,z)=i-1\}\right|=c_i\quad(1\leq i\leq 3) \] depend only on $i$. Such a graph is finite because it has finite valency and finite diameter.

Existence question. Does there exist an integer $q\geq 3$ which is not a prime power and a distance-regular graph $H$ with intersection array \[ \{q(q-1),(q+1)(q-2),q+1;1,1,q(q-2)\}? \] The integer domain and the restriction $q\geq 3$ follow from the standard diameter-three interpretation: $b_2=q+1$ is an integer, and $b_1,b_2,c_3$ must be positive. In particular, $q=2$ does not give an intersection array of a diameter-three graph.

Supplied context and qualifications. The source describes a nonisotropic unitary graph $\Gamma$, for prime-power $q$, with this intersection array, and states that $G=\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ acts transitively on its vertices and on its unordered edges. It also states that $\Gamma$ is distance-transitive for $q=3$, meaning that its automorphism group is transitive on ordered vertex pairs at each fixed distance. These are supplied background assertions, not independently verified here. The source does not define the nonisotropic unitary construction or specify its parameter exceptions; its prime-power assertion therefore requires qualification at $q=2$ under the diameter-three convention. The final existence question asks only for distance-regularity with the displayed array; it does not explicitly require the sought graph to be unitary, vertex-transitive, edge-transitive, or distance-transitive.

Short English statement

Does some integer $q\geq 3$ that is not a prime power admit a distance-regular graph of diameter $3$ with the displayed intersection array? The supplied prime-power unitary examples provide context; the final question imposes no explicit automorphism-transitivity requirement. The registry labels the problem open, without independent verification here.
