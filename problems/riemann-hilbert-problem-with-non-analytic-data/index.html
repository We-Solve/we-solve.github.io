Riemann-Hilbert Problem with non-analytic data

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
A matrix Riemann--Hilbert problem asks for a matrix function analytic off an oriented contour, with prescribed jumps $M_+(z)=M_-(z)J_n(z)$ and normalization. The oscillatory data contain $e^{in\phi(z)}r(z)$, with $n\to\infty$. Nonlinear steepest descent deforms the contour and analyzes local and global model problems. The source asks to relax analyticity of the phase, both in equilibrium-measure analysis and contour deformation.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
In many situations one is concerned with the asymptotic behavior of Riemann-Hilbert problems with exponentially varying data of the form $e^{in\phi(z)} r(z)$, $n \to \infty$. The Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method for such problems requires $\phi(z)$ to be analytic. The analyticity is used in two ways: to control the equilibrium measure associated with the problem, and then to deform the contour for the RHP. By contrast, the method only requires minimal smoothness for $r(z)$ (see eg. Deift-Zhou in the context of Problem 12 below). It is of considerable theoretical and practical interest to extend the nonlinear steepest descent method to situations where $\phi$ is no longer analytic, and has, for example, only a finite number of derivatives. For very interesting work on the analyticity problem, we refer the reader to a recent paper of Miller and McLaughlin. There is also interesting, older work due to Varzugin.
Scope and source qualification.
This method-development problem leaves the minimal differentiability and error estimates to be determined. The source mentions Miller--McLaughlin and Varzugin work as partial context, not as a general solution.

Short English statement

Extend nonlinear steepest descent for oscillatory Riemann--Hilbert problems to phases with only finitely many derivatives.

Finite-smoothness extension of a phase.
Let a real phase $\phi$ be $C^{k+1}$ on a compact interval, where $k\ge1$, and suppose $\phi'(x)\ge c>0$. For $z=x+iy$ define the truncated extension \[\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(iy)^j}{j!}\phi^{(j)}(x),\qquad \bar\partial=\tfrac12(\partial_x+i\partial_y). \] Differentiating term by term cancels all adjacent terms and leaves \[\bar\partial\Phi=\frac{(iy)^k}{2k!}\phi^{(k+1)}(x). \] For sufficiently small $y\ge0$, uniformly on a smaller compact interval, $\operatorname{Im}\Phi\ge cy/2$: the linear imaginary term is $y\phi'$, and all remaining terms are bounded by a constant times $y^2$ (or vanish for $k=1$). Hence \[|\bar\partial e^{in\Phi}|\le C n y^k e^{-ncy/2},\qquad \int_0^{y_0}|\bar\partial e^{in\Phi}|dy\le C' n^{-k}. \] The last estimate follows by substituting $s=ny$; the integral of $s^ke^{-cs/2}$ is finite.
Why the local estimate is insufficient.
This gives a concrete replacement for exact analyticity on a nonstationary patch. It does not bound the inverse of a matrix singular-integral problem. In particular the Cauchy kernel $(z-w)^{-1}$ is singular, so a small area $L^1$ norm of a source does not by itself imply a small $L^\infty$ Cauchy transform. A source concentrated in a shrinking disk already warns against that inference. Stationary points, where $\phi'=0$, also remove the uniform exponential factor used above.
First gap.
A full method must construct suitable extensions near stationary points and equilibrium-measure endpoints, then prove an operator-norm estimate for the resulting matrix $\bar\partial$ problem and its interaction with local models. Neither the equilibrium-measure regularity nor those uniform inverse bounds are obtained here. The calculation is an elementary almost-analytic extension estimate, not a general finite-regularity steepest-descent theorem.
