Atomic Decomposition of Rank-Two Bundle Moduli

Definitions and mathematical statement

Write $X=SU_C(2,\Lambda)$ for the rank-two bundle moduli space with fixed determinant $\Lambda$ named in the supplied record. The record does not specify the hypotheses on $C$, the degree of $\Lambda$, the ground field, or the stability convention defining this space. These must be recovered from the cited report before a fully specified conjecture can be stated.

A semiorthogonal decomposition of a triangulated category $\mathcal D$ is an ordered collection of full triangulated subcategories $\mathcal A_1,\ldots,\mathcal A_m$ that generate $\mathcal D$ and satisfy \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(A_j,A_i[k])=0 \quad\text{for every }j>i,\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}, \ A_j\in\mathcal A_j,\ A_i\in\mathcal A_i. \] The source refers specifically to the semiorthogonal decomposition of $X$ in its Theorem 1, but neither that theorem nor its ambient category and components are supplied. Interpreting the ambient category as $D^b(\operatorname{Coh}(X))$ would require confirmation from that theorem.

For the geometric hypotheses and quantum-cohomology parameters intended by the source, let \[ H=H^*(X,{\mathbb{C}}),\qquad T(\alpha)=c_1\star\alpha, \] where $\star$ denotes the intended quantum product and $c_1$ the class appearing in the source. Its usual interpretation as $c_1(T_X)$, as well as the quantum product and any specialization needed to obtain a complex-linear endomorphism of $H$, requires confirmation. If these data give an endomorphism of finite-dimensional $H$, its generalized eigenspaces are \[ H_\mu=\ker\left((T-\mu\operatorname{id}_H)^N\right) \quad(N\text{ sufficiently large}),\qquad H=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)}H_\mu. \] The original statement calls this decomposition orthogonal, without specifying the pairing.

Source Conjecture 5, with unresolved specifications. The particular semiorthogonal decomposition of $SU_C(2,\Lambda)$ given in Theorem 1 of the cited report is atomic: it is compatible with the decomposition of $H^*(SU_C(2,\Lambda),{\mathbb{C}})$ into generalized eigenspaces of quantum multiplication by $c_1$, in precisely the sense of the Sanda--Shamoto conjecture cited there as reference [13]. The supplied record does not reproduce that compatibility condition. Thus this is a reference-dependent statement of the intended conjecture, and the missing theorem and compatibility definition prevent a complete intrinsic formulation.

The original extraction also contains the fragment that the eigenspace corresponding to $8\lambda$ is isomorphic to \[ H^*\left(\operatorname{Sym}^{g-1-|\lambda|}(C),{\mathbb{C}}\right). \] For a nonnegative integer $n$, $\operatorname{Sym}^n(C)=C^n/\mathfrak S_n$. The fragment does not specify the range or meaning of $\lambda$, define $g$, identify which operator has eigenvalue $8\lambda$, or specify the structure preserved by the isomorphism. Its placement after the conjecture appears disrupted. It is retained as unresolved source context, not asserted as an independently specified theorem or an additional conjectural conclusion.

Short English statement

The source conjectures that its specific semiorthogonal decomposition of the rank-two fixed-determinant bundle moduli space matches the generalized quantum-cohomology eigenspace decomposition in the Sanda--Shamoto sense. The geometric hypotheses, proposed categorical components, quantum parameters, and precise matching condition are missing from the supplied extract, so the statement remains reference-dependent and requires mathematical review.
