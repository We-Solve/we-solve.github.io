Kourovka Notebook Problem 6.31

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ range over finitely generated residually finite groups. Here residually finite means that for every $g\in G\setminus\{1\}$ there is a homomorphism from $G$ to a finite group whose value at $g$ is nonidentity. Define \[ d(G)=\min\{\lvert S\rvert:S\subseteq G,\ \langle S\rangle=G\}. \] The profinite completion of $G$ is \[ \widehat G=\varprojlim_{\substack{N\trianglelefteq G\\[G:N]<\infty}}G/N, \] with its inverse-limit topology, and define \[ \delta(G)=\min\{\lvert T\rvert:T\subseteq\widehat G,\ \overline{\langle T\rangle}=\widehat G\}. \] Both minima are finite because $G$ is finitely generated.

Part (b), the remaining question in the supplied record. Is the following assertion true? \[ \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge2}\ \exists B_k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge0}\ \forall G\quad \left(G\text{ is finitely generated and residually finite and }\delta(G)=k\right) \Longrightarrow d(G)\le B_k. \] Thus the bound may depend on the fixed value $k$, but must be independent of the group $G$.

Archived part (a), retained for context. The original question asks whether every finitely generated residually finite group satisfies $\delta(G)=d(G)$. The supplied archive reports a negative answer, attributed to G. A. Noskov, and the supplied statement reports examples with $d(G)>\delta(G)$. This reported resolution concerns part (a); it does not by itself answer the boundedness question in part (b). The supplied registry labels the overall record partially_solved; this is supplied curation, not an independently verified literature assessment.

Short English statement

For each fixed integer $k\ge2$, is there a uniform bound on the number of abstract generators needed by finitely generated residually finite groups whose profinite completions require exactly $k$ topological generators? The supplied archive reports that equality of the two generator numbers, asked in part (a), can fail.
