Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a research programme rather than a single conjecture with specified hypotheses. The following conventional notation explains its terminology without resolving the missing choices.

In a standard local potential presentation of the Witten--Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde (WDVV) equations, let $n\geq 1$, let $\eta=(\eta_{ij})$ be a constant, invertible, symmetric matrix, and write $(\eta^{ij})=\eta^{-1}$. For a potential $F$ in local coordinates $t^1,\ldots,t^n$, with the required third derivatives, set \[ F_{ijk}=\frac{\partial^3 F}{\partial t^i\partial t^j\partial t^k}. \] The equations are \[ \sum_{a,b=1}^{n}F_{ija}\eta^{ab}F_{bk\ell} = \sum_{a,b=1}^{n}F_{ika}\eta^{ab}F_{bj\ell}, \qquad 1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n. \] Equivalently, these equations express associativity of the multiplication with structure constants \[ c_{ij}^{\ k}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\eta^{ka}F_{ija}. \] This is contextual notation: the record does not specify the ground field, regularity class, coordinate domain, metric, unit normalization, or additional conditions relating these equations to its intended Frobenius manifolds.

For an endomorphism $A$ of an $n$-dimensional vector space, the usual meaning of $\mathrm{gl}$-regularity is \[ \deg m_A=n, \] where $m_A$ is the minimal polynomial; equivalently, the minimal and characteristic polynomials coincide. For a tangent bundle endomorphism $L$, this condition can be imposed on $L_p$ at each point $p$ of a specified domain. The record does not specify that domain or whether regularity is required everywhere or only on a generic locus. It also does not define the pair $(L,e)$, its compatibility conditions, or its construction from a Frobenius manifold; these cannot be recovered from the statement alone.

Research questions (Bolsinov, Konyaev, Matveev, 2024, Problem 3).

  1. Explain why the WDVV equations are interesting and identify their applications.
  2. Determine whether the part of WDVV theory relevant to those applications can be reformulated by replacing a solution of the equations with a pair $(L,e)$, at least in the $\mathrm{gl}$-regular case.
  3. Determine which pairs $(L,e)$ occur in known examples of Frobenius manifolds.
The intended applications, the meaning of replacement or equivalence, the admissible pairs, and the family of known examples remain unspecified. Thus the supplied evidence does not determine a precise correspondence theorem or classification problem. Short English statement

The problem asks what makes the WDVV equations useful, whether their application-relevant content can be expressed through pairs $(L,e)$, at least in the $\mathrm{gl}$-regular case, and which such pairs arise from known Frobenius manifolds. The supplied record leaves the pairs, correspondence, applications, and examples undefined, so a precise theorem statement requires further source context.
