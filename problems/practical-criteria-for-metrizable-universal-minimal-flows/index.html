Math-4240

Practical Criteria for Metrizable Universal Minimal Flows

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a topological group. A $G$-flow is a nonempty compact Hausdorff space $X$ equipped with a jointly continuous action $G\times X\to X$. It is minimal if every orbit is dense. A universal minimal flow $M(G)$ is a minimal $G$-flow such that every minimal $G$-flow $X$ admits a continuous surjective $G$-equivariant map $M(G)\to X$. Metrizability means that the topology of $M(G)$ is induced by a metric.

A subgroup $H\leq G$, with its subspace topology, is extremely amenable if every $H$-flow has a point fixed by every element of $H$. It is co-precompact in $G$ if, for every identity neighbourhood $U\subseteq G$, there is a finite set $F\subseteq G$ such that $G=UFH$.

General criterion problem. Find a verifiable condition $\mathcal C$ on $G$, together with a proof of \[ \mathcal C(G)\ \Longrightarrow\ M(G)\text{ is metrizable}, \] that is easier to check than the existence of a closed, co-precompact, extremely amenable subgroup $H\leq G$. The source gives no formal meaning to "easier to check." It also gives no explicit restriction of the ambient class of topological groups, such as a Polish-group hypothesis, and does not specify separation axioms for $G$. These omissions require review: the comparison must not be interpreted as asserting that the subgroup condition alone implies metrizability for every topological group.

Particular question in the supplied expanded statement. Let $L$ be a finite relational language, meaning that it has finitely many relation symbols, each of finite arity. Let $\mathcal A$ be a countable $L$-structure that is homogeneous: every isomorphism between finite induced substructures of $\mathcal A$ extends to an automorphism of $\mathcal A$. Equip $G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A)$ with the topology of pointwise convergence on the underlying discrete set. Thus a neighbourhood basis at the identity consists of the subgroups \[ G_{(B)}=\{g\in G: g(b)=b\text{ for every }b\in B\}, \qquad B\subseteq A\text{ finite}, \] where $A$ is the underlying set of $\mathcal A$. Is $M(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A))$ metrizable for every such $\mathcal A$? The pointwise-convergence topology and the stated meaning of homogeneity are conventional interpretations of terminology left implicit in the supplied statement. The record does not specify whether "countable" means at most countable or countably infinite.

Short English statement

Find a practical sufficient condition ensuring that a topological group's universal minimal flow is metrizable, with a precise account of its applicable hypotheses. The expanded source statement also asks whether this always holds for automorphism groups of countable homogeneous structures in finite relational languages, using the conventional pointwise-convergence topology. The ambient hypotheses and the meaning of an easier criterion require clarification.
