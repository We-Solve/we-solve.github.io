Goldman-Type Theorem for Sharply Transitive Permutation Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A factor is a von Neumann algebra with center consisting of scalar multiples of its identity, and a subfactor inclusion is a unital inclusion of factors. Let $\Omega$ be a finite set. For an integer $k$ with $1\leq k\leq |\Omega|$, a permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ is sharply $k$-transitive if, for any two ordered tuples $(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ and $(y_1,\ldots,y_k)$, each having pairwise distinct entries in $\Omega$, there is exactly one $g\in G$ such that $g x_i=y_i$ for every $i$. Fix $\omega\in\Omega$ and put \[ H=G_\omega=\{g\in G:g\omega=\omega\}. \] An action $\alpha:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(R)$ on a factor $R$ is outer if $\alpha_g$ is not an inner automorphism for every $g\neq e$. Its restriction to $H$ gives the crossed-product inclusion \[ R\rtimes_{\alpha|_H}H\subset R\rtimes_\alpha G. \] In the usual finite-index subfactor setting, the principal graph is the rooted bipartite graph recording the multiplicities arising from the inclusion's iterated bimodule decompositions. The supplied record denotes the principal graph of a reference group--subgroup crossed-product inclusion by $\Gamma_{G>H}$. It does not specify the reference factor, reference action, factor type, or precise principal-graph conventions; these data must be fixed in the intended setting before the assertion is fully determined.

The proposed assertion is the following. For a sharply $k$-transitive finite permutation group $G$ and its point stabilizer $H$, every subfactor inclusion $Q\subset P$ whose principal graph is isomorphic to $\Gamma_{G>H}$ is isomorphic, as an inclusion, to a crossed-product inclusion for this same pair $(G,H)$. Explicitly, there should exist a factor $R$, an outer action $\alpha:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(R)$, and a von Neumann algebra isomorphism \[ \Phi:P\longrightarrow R\rtimes_\alpha G \quad\text{such that}\quad \Phi(Q)=R\rtimes_{\alpha|_H}H. \] The source does not specify the intended range of $k$, beyond using sharply $k$-transitive groups, or the ambient class of subfactors quantified over. No factor type, irreducibility condition, or additional analytic hypothesis is imposed here to fill that gap. The hypothesis names a principal graph only; the supplied statement does not additionally require a dual principal graph or an identification of standard invariants.

The supplied excerpt asserts that the conjecture holds for $k=2,3,4$. This is retained as a source claim, not as an independently verified theorem. Its background discussion also asserts that sharply $3$-transitive permutation groups form exactly two families, denoted $L(q)$ and $M(q)$, which are transitive extensions of groups denoted $H(q)$ and $S(q)$, respectively. It identifies $L(q)=\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)$, describes its fractional-linear action on the projective line, and describes $M(q)$ as having the same underlying set as $\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)$, with an action whose branches depend on whether the determinant is a square or a nonsquare. The admissible parameters, the automorphism denoted $\sigma$, and parts of the displayed formulas are missing or corrupted. The claimed example printed as $S(32)$ and $Z23\rtimes Q_8$ is likewise not unambiguously recoverable. These background assertions therefore do not supply additional precise hypotheses or constructions for the conjecture.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether the principal graph associated with a sharply $k$-transitive finite group and a point stabilizer forces every subfactor with that graph to arise from an outer action of the same group and subgroup. The supplied source claims the cases $k=2,3,4$ and labels the general problem open. The intended range of $k$, analytic setting, and some background notation require clarification.
