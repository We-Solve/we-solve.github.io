Multiscale Principal Flows on Riemannian Manifolds

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a Riemannian manifold, with Riemannian distance $d_g$. The supplied record does not specify the data model, geometric hypotheses on $M$, or a definition of a principal flow. Consequently, the following is a formulation of the supplied research questions with their unresolved specifications made explicit, rather than a fully quantified conjecture.

For terminology only, if the data are represented by a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on $M$, the usual squared-distance Fréchet potential is \[ F_\mu(p)=\int_M d_g(p,x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x), \] where this integral is finite. A local Fréchet mean is a point $p$ for which there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $p$ such that $F_\mu(p)\leq F_\mu(q)$ for every $q\in U$. This conventional definition supplies terminology; the record does not specify whether the intended potential is this function, an empirical version, or a scale-dependent localized potential.

Write $h$ for a proposed scale parameter and $\gamma_h:I_h\to M$ for a proposed principal-flow curve, with its defining rule and parameter domain still to be specified. A parametrized curve has a self-intersection if $\gamma_h(s)=\gamma_h(t)$ for some distinct $s,t\in I_h$; whether repeated traversal or closure is to count as an unwanted intersection is not specified. For a specified real-valued potential $F$, its sublevel sets are $\{p\in M:F(p)\leq a\}$, indexed by $a\in{\mathbb{R}}$. Persistence diagrams associated with this filtration require choices of homology degree, coefficients, and hypotheses ensuring the intended diagram is defined. No such choices are supplied.

  1. Determine whether a scale parameter can describe a principal flow that "dies out." The meaning of dying out, the variable with respect to which it occurs, and the relevant endpoint or limiting regime must be specified.
  2. Determine whether additional regularization is needed to prevent self-intersection or winding of principal flows. The underlying construction, admissible regularizers, and meaning of unwanted winding must be specified before a necessity or sufficiency assertion can be stated.
  3. Determine whether persistence diagrams of the intended Fréchet potential can detect local means at different scales. The relationship between the persistence filtration parameter and the geometric scale, and the meaning of detection, remain unspecified.
  4. Determine whether one scale parameter can govern both the choice of starting points and the estimation of local covariance. The starting-point rule, localization procedure, and tangent-space covariance construction remain unspecified.
  5. Develop higher-dimensional analogues of principal flows, including canonical principal submanifolds and related generalizations. The dimension, admissible class of submanifolds, defining criterion, and meaning of canonical must be supplied.

The source gives no universal or existential quantifiers over manifolds, data sets or measures, scales, or regularization classes. These omissions prevent replacing the research questions by a single precise theorem or conjecture without adding hypotheses.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a multiscale theory of principal flows on Riemannian manifolds: how flows terminate, how to control intersections and winding, how persistence might identify local Fréchet means, whether one scale can govern initialization and local covariance, and how to obtain canonical higher-dimensional principal submanifolds. The source leaves the constructions and success criteria unspecified, so this draft preserves the questions and identifies the definitions needed for a precise conjecture.
