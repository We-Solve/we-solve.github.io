Converse Bessel-Pair Criterion for Grushin Operators

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(V,W)$ denote the pair of weights appearing in the supplied Grushin-operator problem. For an intended Grushin setting $G$, write $\mathsf{H}_{G}(V,W)$ for validity of the associated Hardy inequality, $\mathsf{HR}_{G}(V,W)$ for validity of the associated Hardy--Rellich inequality, and $\mathsf{B}_{G}(V,W)$ for the associated Bessel-pair condition. These are placeholders for source-specific conditions, not definitions of those conditions: the supplied record gives neither the inequalities nor the differential equation and positivity requirement defining the Bessel pair.

The requested converse question is whether, for every admissible pair of weights in the intended setting, the relevant implication holds: \[ \mathsf{H}_{G}(V,W)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{B}_{G}(V,W), \qquad\text{or, for the intended Hardy--Rellich version,}\qquad \mathsf{HR}_{G}(V,W)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{B}_{G}(V,W). \] Each intended converse is to be proved or disproved under the hypotheses of its corresponding source inequality. The record does not determine whether these are separate questions or which precise variants are intended. In particular, it does not specify the Grushin vector fields, dimensions, operator parameters, domain, measure, admissibility and regularity of the weights, test-function class, boundary or support conditions, constants, or Bessel-pair equation. Consequently, the displayed implications record the logical target only; a fully specified mathematical statement requires those missing source hypotheses.

Short English statement

Does validity of the relevant Hardy or Hardy--Rellich inequality for Grushin vector fields force its weight pair $(V,W)$ to satisfy the corresponding Bessel-pair condition? The supplied excerpt leaves the exact inequalities and hypotheses unspecified, so the converse remains a statement template requiring human mathematical review.
