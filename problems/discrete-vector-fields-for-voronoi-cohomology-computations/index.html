Literature and context check

Problem 5 in OWR 52/2016, p. 2971, was read: it proposes applying discrete vector fields to Voronoi cohomology computations, without specifying a matching or a guaranteed reduction. The calculation below rederives the algebraic cancellation that such a method must preserve and exhibits two concrete failure modes. It is not an implementation or a new theorem about all arithmetic Voronoi complexes.

Research attempt: Exact chain cancellation and the torsion boundary

One cancellation, including its correction terms.
Let $C_*$ be a finite chain complex of finite free modules over a commutative ring. Suppose one differential, in chosen bases, has a unit entry $u$ in the top-left position: \[ d_k=\begin{pmatrix}u&a\\b&D\end{pmatrix}. \] Write the source basis as $e_1,e_2,\ldots$ and the target basis as $f_1,f_2,\ldots$. Replace \[ f_1\text{ by }f'_1=u^{-1}d_ke_1 =f_1+\sum_{i>1}u^{-1}b_i f_i, \qquad e_j\text{ by }e'_j=e_j-u^{-1}a_j e_1\quad(j>1). \] These are invertible basis changes, and the differential becomes the direct block matrix with blocks $u$ and \[ D-bu^{-1}a. \] There is no remaining entry in the first row or column outside $u$. The equation $d_{k-1}d_k=0$ gives $d_{k-1}f'_1=0$. The equation $d_kd_{k+1}=0$ gives zero $e_1$ coordinate in $d_{k+1}$, since its first image coordinate is $u$ times that coordinate and $u$ is a unit. Thus the two generators $e_1,f'_1$ split off as a contractible subcomplex, and the remaining differential blocks still square to zero.

The contracting homotopy on this pair is $h(f'_1)=u^{-1}e_1$, $h(e_1)=0$, so $dh+hd$ is its identity. Deleting the pair therefore preserves homology exactly. Repeating any sequence of presently valid unit cancellations remains correct by composition, even if sparsity gets worse. An acyclic discrete matching is a means of organizing such cancellations; no claim that an arbitrary proposed matching can be cancelled in arbitrary order is needed here.

A coefficient obstruction.
Over $\mathbb Z$, the complex $\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z$ has $H_0=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Deleting its two generators because the entry is nonzero would instead leave zero homology. Over $\mathbb Q$ the entry is a unit and cancellation is valid. Thus integral computations need unit pivots, not merely nonzero incidences; stabilizer and orientation effects in a quotient complex must be accounted for before applying the rule.
A correspondence obstruction.
Suppose a chain map $T$ is supplied together with the complex. If $i$ is the inclusion of the surviving complex in the changed bases and $p$ its projection, the transferred map is $pTi$. Both $i$ and $p$ are chain maps by the splitting, and the removed summand is contractible, so this formula induces the original map on homology. In contrast, coordinate deletion in the original bases can fail. Take \[ d:\mathbb Q\longrightarrow\mathbb Q^2,\quad d(1)=(1,1),\qquad T_1=2,\quad T_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}. \] Here $T_0d=dT_1$, while on the surviving $H_0$ the correct projection and inclusion are $p(x,y)=y-x$ and $i(z)=(0,z)$. Thus $pT_0i=1$, whereas retaining only the original bottom-right entry would give $2$. Even a correct reduction of Betti numbers need not carry Hecke eigenvalues correctly without these maps.
The attempted efficiency inference stops.
A single cancellation updates a potentially dense outer product $bu^{-1}a$. For example, with $u=1$, every entry of $a,b$ equal to one and $D=0$, it creates a full nonzero lower block $-ba$ from a sparse block matrix. This matrix is a legitimate two-term chain complex, since there are no adjacent nonzero differentials. Fewer generators alone therefore does not prove lower arithmetic or storage cost. A useful Voronoi matching must control fill-in, allowable coefficients, and transferred operators.
