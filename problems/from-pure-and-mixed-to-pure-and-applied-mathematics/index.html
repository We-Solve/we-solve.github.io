From Pure and Mixed to Pure and Applied Mathematics

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Nature and terminology of the question. The supplied record presents a historical and conceptual research question about the classifications "pure mathematics," "mixed mathematics," and "applied mathematics." These expressions designate historical categories whose meanings and uses are themselves under investigation; the record supplies no fixed definitions of them. Here "opposition" refers to a conceptual distinction between categories, rather than a formally defined mathematical relation.

Research question. Determine the conceptual framework that made the pure/mixed or pure/applied oppositions plausible, and explain how one opposition came to replace the other.

Unresolved scope. The record does not specify the historical period, geographical or institutional setting, relevant community of practitioners, documentary corpus, or criteria for establishing plausibility or replacement. Its title suggests a transition from pure/mixed to pure/applied, but the statement itself says only that one opposition replaced the other. The extent, direction, chronology, and possible coexistence of these classifications therefore require source clarification. The replacement is a premise of the supplied question, not an independently established historical fact here. No mathematical objects, domains, hypotheses, boundary conditions, or quantifiers are supplied from which a theorem-style statement could be formed without adding content. This draft consequently preserves the interpretive research question.

Short English statement

Investigate the historical ideas that made distinctions between pure and mixed mathematics, or pure and applied mathematics, plausible, and explain the reported transition between these classifications. Their meanings and the scope of that transition remain to be clarified. The supplied "open" label concerns an interpretive historical question.
