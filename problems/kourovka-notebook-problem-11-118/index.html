Kourovka Notebook Problem 11.118

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

All groups considered are finite. A formation is a nonempty class $\mathfrak F$ of groups, closed under isomorphisms and homomorphic images, with the following subdirect-product closure property: if $N_1,\ldots,N_r\trianglelefteq G$, $r\geq 1$, $\bigcap_{i=1}^r N_i=1$, and $G/N_i\in\mathfrak F$ for every $i$, then $G\in\mathfrak F$. It is hereditary if every subgroup of each member also belongs to $\mathfrak F$. It is soluble if every member is soluble, where solubility means that the derived series terminates at the identity subgroup.

A chief factor of $G$ is a quotient $A/B$, where $B<A$ are normal subgroups of $G$ and no normal subgroup of $G$ lies strictly between them. Write \[ C_G(A/B)=\{g\in G: gag^{-1}a^{-1}\in B\text{ for every }a\in A\}. \] Using the standard chief-factor convention, a formation $\mathfrak F$ is local if there is a function $f$, assigning to each prime $p$ a formation or the empty class, such that, for every finite group $G$, \[ G\in\mathfrak F \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G/C_G(A/B)\in f(p) \quad\text{for every chief factor }A/B\text{ of }G \text{ and every prime }p\mid |A/B|. \] For the trivial group the condition on chief factors is vacuous.

An $\mathfrak F$-covering subgroup of $G$ means a subgroup $U\leq G$ such that $U\in\mathfrak F$ and \[ \forall H\ (U\leq H\leq G)\quad \forall N\trianglelefteq H,\qquad H/N\in\mathfrak F\ \Longrightarrow\ H=UN. \] Here $UN=\{un:u\in U,\ n\in N\}$.

Classification problem (L. A. Shemetkov, 1990). Determine all hereditary soluble local formations $\mathfrak F$ satisfying \[ \forall\text{ finite groups }G\quad \exists U\leq G\quad \left[ U\in\mathfrak F\ \land\ \forall H\ (U\leq H\leq G)\ \forall N\trianglelefteq H\, \left(H/N\in\mathfrak F\Longrightarrow H=UN\right) \right]. \] The quantifier over $G$ includes all finite groups, including nonsoluble groups; solubility is a requirement on the members of $\mathfrak F$. The supplied record does not explicitly define the technical terms; the standard conventions specified above require confirmation against the cited source during mathematical review.

Short English statement

Classify the hereditary soluble local formations $\mathfrak F$ for which every finite group has an $\mathfrak F$-covering subgroup. Such a subgroup belongs to $\mathfrak F$ and maps onto every quotient belonging to $\mathfrak F$ of every intermediate subgroup containing it.
