Math-6798

Balmer Spectra for Constant Mackey Functors

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime, including $p=2$, and let $G=C_p$ be the cyclic group of order $p$. Let $R$ be a commutative ring and write $\underline R$ for its constant $G$-Mackey functor: its value at every orbit is $R$, restrictions and conjugations are identities, and the transfer associated with $H\leq K\leq G$ is multiplication by $[K:H]$. Write $H\underline R$ for the associated genuine $G$-equivariant Eilenberg--Mac Lane ring spectrum, denoted $HR$ in the source.

Under the usual interpretation of the source notation, let \[ \mathcal T_R=\operatorname{Perf}_{C_p}(H\underline R) \] be the tensor triangulated category of compact genuine $C_p$-equivariant $H\underline R$-modules, with tensor product over $H\underline R$. The supplied excerpt does not explicitly define its convention for $\operatorname{Perf}_{C_p}$; this interpretation requires confirmation against the report.

For an essentially small tensor triangulated category $\mathcal T$, its Balmer spectrum $\operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T)$ consists of its prime thick tensor ideals. A thick tensor ideal $\mathfrak p$ is a full triangulated subcategory closed under direct summands and under tensoring with every object of $\mathcal T$. It is prime if it is proper and \[ X\otimes Y\in\mathfrak p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\in\mathfrak p\ \text{or}\ Y\in\mathfrak p. \] The Balmer topology has the supports \[ \operatorname{supp}(X)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T):X\notin\mathfrak p\} \] as a closed subbasis.

Problem (source Question 8). For every prime $p$ and every commutative ring $R$, describe the topological space \[ \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}_{C_p}(H\underline R)\right). \] The source supplies the case in which $p$ is invertible in $R$ as \[ p\in R^\times\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T_R)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(R). \] Thus its remaining question concerns $p\notin R^\times$. Here $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is the prime ideal spectrum with its Zariski topology.

Supplied structural context. For a subgroup $H\leq G$, write $\Phi^H$ for geometric fixed points and $W_G(H)=N_G(H)/H$ for the Weyl group. The source attributes to Barthel--Castellana--Heard--Naumann-Pol, under its reference label [1], a description for finite groups of the form \[ \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}_{G}(H\underline R)\right) \simeq \coprod_{(H)\leq G} \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(\Phi^H H\underline R)\right)/W_G(H) \qquad\text{as sets}, \] where $(H)$ ranges over conjugacy classes of subgroups, and each quotient is an orbit set. For $G=C_p$, with $e$ the trivial subgroup, the supplied excerpt reduces this to \[ \operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T_R) \simeq \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(\Phi^{C_p}H\underline R)\right) \amalg \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(\Phi^eH\underline R)\right)/C_p. \] It further states that $H\underline R$ is a global spectrum and that the indicated $C_p$-action is trivial, giving a set bijection \[ \operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T_R) \simeq \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(\Phi^{C_p}H\underline R)\right) \amalg \operatorname{Spc}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(\Phi^eH\underline R)\right). \] These are supplied set descriptions, not assertions that the Balmer topology is the disjoint union topology. The requested description must determine the topology, including the specialization relations between the two displayed parts. The precise hypotheses and construction underlying the source's global-spectrum and trivial-action assertions require review.

Short English statement

For each prime $p$ and commutative ring $R$, describe the prime tensor ideals of perfect genuine $C_p$-equivariant modules over the Eilenberg--Mac Lane spectrum of the constant Mackey functor $\underline R$, together with their Balmer topology. The source supplies the answer when $p$ is invertible and a geometric fixed point description of the underlying set. Its remaining target is the topology and how the parts fit together when $p$ is not invertible. The supplied open status and structural assertions require human mathematical and source review.
