Math-1442

Noncontractible equilateral trefoil components after rotation reduction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive length vector $\ell=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n)$, use the standard labelled, oriented spatial-polygon convention \[ M_\ell=\{(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\in(S^2)^n:\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_i u_i=0\}/\operatorname{SO}(3). \] The edge vectors are $\ell_i u_i$; translations have been removed by placing the first vertex at the origin. Let $\Sigma\subset M_\ell$ be the locus whose polygonal map of the circle is not embedded, including self-intersections and overlapping edges. Write \[ M_\ell\setminus\Sigma=\bigsqcup_i U_i \] for its connected components. Farber's source questions are whether the $U_i$ are contractible and, more generally, what can be said about their topology. The quotient convention is the aid's standard Kapovich--Millson--Klyachko interpretation; neither reflections nor cyclic relabellings are additionally identified.

The supplied partial result gives a negative answer to universal contractibility under that convention. For the equilateral hexagon $\ell=(1,1,1,1,1,1)$, it reports four reduced embedded trefoil components and asserts, for each, \[ {\mathbb{Z}}\hookrightarrow\pi_1(U_i). \] In particular these components are not contractible. The aid attributes the corresponding infinite-cyclic subgroup in the unreduced fixed-length trefoil components to Calvo, without a full reference.

The aid's quotient-transfer statement is explicit. Let $E$ be a component of rooted, labelled, oriented embedded fixed-length spatial polygons before translation and rotation reduction, and let $E_0$ be obtained by pinning its first vertex. Translation contributes a contractible ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ factor. Rotation acts freely on $E_0$, since an embedded closed polygon is not collinear, giving the principal bundle \[ \operatorname{SO}(3)\longrightarrow E_0\longrightarrow U=E_0/\operatorname{SO}(3). \] Its homotopy exact sequence shows \[ \ker\left(\pi_1(E_0)\to\pi_1(U)\right) =\operatorname{im}\left(\pi_1(\operatorname{SO}(3))\to\pi_1(E_0)\right), \qquad \pi_1(\operatorname{SO}(3))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}. \] Thus the kernel has order at most two. Every infinite cyclic subgroup of $\pi_1(E_0)$ meets it trivially and maps injectively to $\pi_1(U)$. Applied to the reported Calvo subgroups, this retains noncontractibility after the rotation quotient. It does not classify all components or describe their full homotopy types for arbitrary length vectors.

Short English statement

Farber asks whether each component of the embedded spatial-polygon space is contractible. Under the stated rigid-motion quotient, the aid reports four equilateral-hexagon trefoil components whose fundamental groups contain ${\mathbb{Z}}$. A principal-bundle argument shows that removing rotations cannot kill those infinite cyclic subgroups.
