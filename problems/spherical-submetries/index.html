Spherical submetries

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional Euclidean real vector space, let ${\mathbb{R}}[V]$ be its polynomial algebra, and put $r^2(v)=\langle v,v\rangle$. In orthonormal coordinates, the Laplacian is $\Delta=\sum_i\partial_i^2$. Under the standard terminology relevant to the cited result, a Laplacian algebra is a unital real subalgebra $A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}[V]$ such that \[ r^2\in A,\qquad \Delta A\subseteq A. \] For $x,y\in V$, define $x\sim_A y$ when $p(x)=p(y)$ for every $p\in A$.

Maximality here means maximality with respect to the induced fibers: every polynomial constant on all $\sim_A$ classes already belongs to $A$. Thus the assertion is \[ A=\{q\in{\mathbb{R}}[V]: \forall x,y\in V,\ x\sim_Ay\Longrightarrow q(x)=q(y)\} \] for every Laplacian algebra $A$. Equivalently, no strictly larger polynomial subalgebra has exactly the same fibers. This is not the unrelated notion of a maximal proper subalgebra of ${\mathbb{R}}[V]$.

The short source question supplies no definitions of Laplacian algebra or maximal. The above standard fiber-based definitions should be matched to the cited theorem during review. No homogeneity, connected-fiber, or group-invariant-algebra hypothesis is added.

Short English statement

Must a polynomial algebra containing the squared norm and closed under the Laplacian contain every polynomial constant on its common fibers? The source's intended maximality is fiber-based.

The supplied research summary reports an affirmative solution by R. Mendes and M. Radeschi and quotes Theorem A: every Laplacian algebra $A\subset{\mathbb{R}}[V]$ is maximal. It identifies this as resolving the earlier question. The registry solved label is retained, while provenance for the 2018 question still marks current status for review. No independent article check is claimed.
