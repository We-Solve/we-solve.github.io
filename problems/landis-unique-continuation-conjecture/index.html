Landis Unique-Continuation Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $N\geq 1$ be an integer and let $|x|$ denote the Euclidean norm on ${\mathbb{R}}^N$. For an essentially bounded potential $V$, write \[ H=-\Delta+V,\qquad \Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_j^2},\qquad \|V\|_{L^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^N)}=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^N}|V(x)|. \] The reconstructed source question asks whether, without a positivity assumption on $H$, every solution of \[ Hu=0\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^N,\qquad \|V\|_{L^\infty({\mathbb{R}}^N)}\leq 1, \] that decays at least as fast as $e^{-k|x|}$ for some $k>1$ must satisfy $u\equiv0$.

Under the usual uniform big-$O$ interpretation of the decay phrase, its hypothesis is \[ \exists k>1\;\exists C>0\;\exists R>0\;\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^N: \quad |x|\geq R\ \Longrightarrow\ |u(x)|\leq C e^{-k|x|}. \] This interpretation is explicit here but is not specified by the supplied wording. For a solution defined only almost everywhere, the corresponding interpretation uses an almost-everywhere inequality outside the ball. The domain is all of ${\mathbb{R}}^N$; no finite boundary or boundary data are supplied.

Unresolved formulation qualifications. The reconstructed statement does not specify whether $V$ and $u$ are real-valued or complex-valued, or the regularity and solution concept for $u$. The supplied literature assessment refers to the real-valued Landis conjecture, suggesting a real-valued intended setting, but this does not unambiguously settle both scalar domains. Thus the displayed implication is a source-faithful question requiring these choices before it becomes a fully specified conjecture; it is not an assertion for every possible scalar domain or solution class.

Qualified background from the supplied excerpt. The excerpt separately describes a partial affirmative result under $H\geq0$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^N$, where this notation is defined there by the existence of a positive supersolution of $H\phi=0$. Its listed hypotheses are \[ N\geq1,\qquad V\leq1,\qquad V\in L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^N),\qquad q>N/2, \] with a solution apparently belonging to $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^N)$. Here $L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ denote local Lebesgue and local first-order Sobolev spaces, respectively. The excerpt also appears to list the growth bounds \[ |u(x)|= \begin{cases} O(1),&N=1,\\ O\!\left(|x|^{(2-N)/2}\right),&N\geq2, \end{cases} \qquad |x|\to\infty. \] These lines are damaged and incomplete in the supplied extraction. They do not provide the complete sharp decay criterion and must not be presented as sufficient hypotheses for a vanishing theorem. In particular, the background condition $V\leq1$ is a one-sided bound and is distinct from the reconstructed question's condition $\|V\|_\infty\leq1$. The source describes its positivity-dependent criterion as weaker than Landis's proposed decay condition; no missing criterion is reconstructed here.

Short English statement

The supplied Landis question asks whether a solution on all of ${\mathbb{R}}^N$, with $\|V\|_\infty\leq1$, must vanish if it decays uniformly like $O(e^{-k|x|})$ for some $k>1$, without assuming positivity of $-\Delta+V$. The scalar domains, solution class, and exact decay convention require clarification. The supplied positivity-dependent partial result has an incomplete extracted statement.
