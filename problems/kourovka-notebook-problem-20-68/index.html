Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.68

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a finite set, let $F(S)$ be the free group on $S$, and write $S^{\pm1}$ for the alphabet of generators and their formal inverses. A one-relator group is a group admitting a presentation \[ G=\langle S\mid r\rangle=F(S)/\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle, \qquad r\in F(S), \] where $\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle$ denotes the normal closure of $r$. The group $G$ is hyperbolic if, for some finite generating set, its Cayley graph with edges of length $1$ has $\delta$-thin geodesic triangles for some $\delta\geq0$: every side of every geodesic triangle lies in the closed $\delta$-neighborhood of the union of the other two sides.

For a word $u\in(S^{\pm1})^*$, let $\overline{u}$ denote its value in $G$. Given $n\geq1$ and words $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, define their generated submonoid by \[ M_G(x_1,\ldots,x_n) =\left\{\overline{x_{i_1}}\cdots\overline{x_{i_k}}: k\geq0,\ i_j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}, \] where the product for $k=0$ is the identity element. The submonoid membership problem for the fixed group $G$ asks, on input $n\geq1$ and words $x_1,\ldots,x_n,w\in(S^{\pm1})^*$, whether $\overline{w}\in M_G(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. It is undecidable if no algorithm terminates on every such input and always answers correctly.

Question (C.-F. Nyberg-Brodda, 2022; Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.68). Does there exist a hyperbolic one-relator group $G$ whose submonoid membership problem is undecidable?

The source describes words over a generating alphabet $A$ without explicitly specifying inverse letters. The formulation above uses the usual group-word convention of allowing generators and their formal inverses. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied source information, not an independently verified assessment.

Short English statement

Does some hyperbolic group with a presentation having one defining relator admit no algorithm that always decides whether a given group word is a finite product of a given finite list of group words? The empty product is allowed. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
