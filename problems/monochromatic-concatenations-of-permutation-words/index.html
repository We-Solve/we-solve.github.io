Monochromatic Concatenations of Permutation Words

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For each positive integer $n$, let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and identify $S_n$ with the set of words of length $n$ in which every symbol of $[n]$ occurs exactly once. A word over $[n]$ is a finite sequence of symbols from $[n]$; concatenation $xy$ places the sequence $y$ after the sequence $x$. Let $[n]^*$ denote all such words, including the empty word, and let $[n]^+$ denote the nonempty words. A $k$-colouring is a function $c:S_n\to\{1,\ldots,k\}$, with no requirement that every colour occur.

The supplied statement leaves unresolved whether $x,y,z$ must be nonempty. To record this qualification explicitly, choose either $\mathcal W_n=[n]^*$ for every $n$, or $\mathcal W_n=[n]^+$ for every $n$. For that choice, the proposed assertion is \[ \begin{gathered} \forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall c:S_n\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\ \exists x,y,z\in\mathcal W_n:\\ xyz,xzy,yxz,yzx,zxy,zyx\in S_n,\\ c(xyz)=c(xzy)=c(yxz)=c(yzx)=c(zxy)=c(zyx). \end{gathered} \] Membership of the concatenations in $S_n$ requires that the letters occurring in $x,y,z$, taken together, contain each element of $[n]$ exactly once. The source does not separately require the six displayed words to be distinct. Allowing empty component words permits coincidences among them. The nonempty-word version is therefore a possible intended formulation, but its nonemptiness hypothesis is not explicit in the supplied record and requires confirmation. No choice between these formulations is asserted here.

Short English statement

For every positive number of colours, is there a permutation size such that every colouring contains three blocks whose six possible concatenation orders are permutations of that size and all receive the same colour? The source does not specify whether the blocks must be nonempty; this qualification remains unresolved.
