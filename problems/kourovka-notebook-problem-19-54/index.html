Kourovka Notebook Problem 19.54

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. A subgroup $A<B$ is maximal in $B$ if there is no subgroup $L$ with $A<L<B$. For an integer $m\geq 1$, a subgroup $H\leq G$ is called $m$-maximal in $G$ if there exists a chain \[ H=H_0<H_1<\cdots<H_m=G \] in which $H_i$ is maximal in $H_{i+1}$ for every $0\leq i<m$. This definition requires the existence of a chain of length $m$; a subgroup may satisfy it for more than one value of $m$.

A chief factor of $G$ is a quotient $N/K$, where $K,N\trianglelefteq G$, $K<N$, and there is no normal subgroup $L\trianglelefteq G$ satisfying $K<L<N$.

Determine the chief factors of finite groups $G$ satisfying \[ \forall H\leq G,\qquad \left(H\text{ is }2\text{-maximal in }G\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ m\geq3,\ H\text{ is not }m\text{-maximal in }G\right). \] In particular, characterize the groups $N/K$ that can occur as chief factors of such a $G$. If $G$ has no $2$-maximal subgroups, the displayed condition holds vacuously.

The source additionally states that for every integer $m\geq3$, there exists a finite group $G_m$ with a subgroup that is both $2$-maximal and $m$-maximal in $G_m$. This is a supplied contextual assertion, not a conclusion proved here. The source poses a classification question and supplies no proposed characterization.

Short English statement

Determine the chief factors of a finite group in which every subgroup lying two maximal inclusions below the whole group admits no chain of maximal inclusions to the whole group of any length at least three. The source attributes this question to V. S. Monakhov.
