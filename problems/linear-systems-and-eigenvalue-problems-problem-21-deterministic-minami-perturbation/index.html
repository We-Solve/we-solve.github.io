Math-5965

Linear Systems and Eigenvalue Problems — Problem 21 (Deterministic Minami Perturbation)

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}$ be Hermitian and tridiagonal, meaning $A=A^*$ and $A_{ij}=0$ whenever $|i-j|>1$. For a Hermitian matrix $B$ of size $n\ge2$, list its eigenvalues with multiplicity as $\lambda_1(B)\le\cdots\le\lambda_n(B)$, and define \[ \operatorname{gap}(B)=\min_{1\le i<n}\left(\lambda_{i+1}(B)-\lambda_i(B)\right). \] Write $\operatorname{nnz}(A)=\#\{(i,j):A_{ij}\ne0\}$. A diagonal perturbation preserving Hermitian symmetry has the form $E=\operatorname{diag}(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ with $e_i\in{\mathbb{R}}$.

The supplied record attributes to Minami [minami1996local] a random perturbation result: when the diagonal entries of $E$ are independent and absolutely continuously distributed, it states a bound of the form \[ \operatorname{gap}(A+\delta E)\ge C\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^c, \] with constants depending on the entry distributions. The record does not specify the probability of this event, the admissible distributions, or any additional hypotheses of that result; thus this attribution is not a complete probabilistic theorem statement.

Requested deterministic construction. Given $A$ and a perturbation parameter $\delta$, construct a diagonal matrix $E$ deterministically so that the displayed gap bound holds for positive constants $C,c$ with the permitted distribution dependence. The positive regime $\delta>0$ is implicit in the displayed scale and running times, but the source does not specify its admissible range. It also does not specify a bound on $E$, a normalization of $A$, or how the entry distributions constrain a deterministic output. These are unresolved parts of the intended construction problem.

The source asks for efficiency relative to the parent algorithm using the perturbation. Its stated target for diagonalization is \[ O\!\left(n^3\log^c(n/\delta)\right), \] and its more ambitious targets for Krylov methods are \[ O\!\left(\operatorname{nnz}(A)\log^c(n/\delta)\right) \quad\text{or}\quad O\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{nnz}(A)\log^c(n)}{\delta^c}\right). \] These expressions are retained as supplied: the computational model, precision requirements, parameter range, and relationship between the occurrences of $c$ are unspecified. The source describes efficiency as being faster than the parent algorithm, but gives no more precise comparison criterion. No convention for $\operatorname{gap}$ when $n=1$ is supplied.

Short English statement

For a Hermitian tridiagonal matrix, find an efficient deterministic diagonal perturbation producing a polynomial lower bound on the minimum eigenvalue spacing. The source motivates this task by a random perturbation result attributed to Minami and gives running-time targets for diagonalization and Krylov methods. Perturbation-size restrictions, probability qualifications, and computational assumptions remain unresolved.
