Dimensional and spectral resolution map for planar Euler--Korteweg phase boundaries

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The extracted question asks whether a planar profile has eigenvalues on the imaginary axis, whether it is spectrally stable, and whether linear information yields full nonlinear stability. The adjacent "R. Pego: Stability of Toda Lattice Solitons" heading is extraction contamination, not a specification of the equation. The supplied verification assigns the preceding problem to S. Benzoni-Gavage's "Shocks with Capillarity" discussion and reconstructs the Euler--Korteweg system \[ \rho_t+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\qquad u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla P(\rho) =\nabla\left(K(\rho)\Delta\rho+\tfrac12K'(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^2\right), \] where $\rho>0$ and $u\in{\mathbb{R}}^3$. In particular the inner operator is $\Delta\rho$, not a vector $\nabla\rho$. The source calls $P$ pressure; in this velocity formulation its convention must be distinguished from a physical pressure $p$, for which the usual relation is $P'(\rho)=p'(\rho)/\rho$. The supplied verification stops before completing that qualification, and coefficient hypotheses are not fully given.

For a planar traveling profile $\bar U(\xi)$, $\xi=x_1-st$, let $L(k)$ be its comoving linearized operator after Fourier transformation in the transverse variables, with transverse frequency $k\in{\mathbb{R}}^2$, on a specified normal-variable space and domain. The questions concern imaginary-axis point spectrum, possible spectrum in $\{\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\}$, and nonlinear stability in a stated perturbation class. Neither the extract nor the summary supplies the full operator domain, profile assumptions, essential-spectrum convention, or nonlinear stability norm.

One precise forced eigenvalue is reported: if translation invariance is differentiable at the profile and \[ 0\ne\bar U'\in D(L(0))\cap L^2({\mathbb{R}}), \] then $L(0)\bar U'=0$, so $0$ is a point eigenvalue of the zero-transverse-frequency fiber. This does not classify other imaginary eigenvalues or nonzero-frequency Evans roots. It also does not make the transversely constant translation mode an $L^2({\mathbb{R}}^3)$ eigenfunction.

The aid reports a 2005 constrained-Hamiltonian orbital-stability result for one-dimensional heteroclinic phase boundaries, where $\bar U(-\infty)\ne\bar U(+\infty)$. Its full hypotheses and norm are missing. It explicitly separates this from the remaining nonzero-transverse-frequency and multidimensional nonlinear questions. Reported later transverse-instability theorems for homoclinic solitary waves, where the two endstates coincide, do not automatically apply to this heteroclinic problem. The aid additionally advertises a Fredholm zero-chain diagnostic only on that separate solitary-wave branch, without supplying its full operator setup or assumptions here. None of these partial statements proves that spectral information alone implies multidimensional nonlinear stability.

Short English statement

The corrupted extract concerns planar capillary phase boundaries, not the following Toda-lattice heading. Translation forces a zero eigenvalue in the zero-frequency fiber under domain and integrability assumptions. The reported one-dimensional heteroclinic stability theorem leaves transverse spectrum and multidimensional nonlinear stability unresolved.
