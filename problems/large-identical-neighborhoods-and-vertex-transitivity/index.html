Math-2058

Large identical neighborhoods and vertex transitivity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In a radius interpretation, let $G$ be a finite connected simple graph on $n$ vertices, with graph distance and diameter $D$. Write $B_G(v,k)$ for the induced radius-$k$ ball with distinguished root $v$. Suppose every pair $u,v$ has a rooted isomorphism \[ (B_G(u,k),u)\cong(B_G(v,k),v). \] The first question asks whether this forces vertex transitivity when $k>n/2$, and separately when $k$ is within a fixed constant of $D$. Vertex transitivity means that for every $u,v$ some automorphism of the whole graph sends $u$ to $v$.

The source says balls of size $k$, not explicitly radius $k$; cardinality and radius are different. It also does not state connectedness or the exact second-threshold quantifier. For a radius parameter, $k\geq D$ makes every rooted ball the whole graph, so the substantive near-diameter range is below $D$. Whether the claim concerns each fixed positive constant $C$, a particular $C$, or existence of one in a bound such as $D-C\leq k<D$ is missing. No such choice is silently made.

The second, separate question samples a vertex-transitive graph $G_n$ uniformly on an odd number $n$ of vertices and asks whether its common degree $d(G_n)$ is concentrated near $(n-1)/2$. A possible relative-concentration formulation is \[ \forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad \Pr\left(\left|d(G_n)-\frac{n-1}{2}\right|>\varepsilon n\right)\longrightarrow0 \quad(n\to\infty\text{ through odd integers}). \] This is an indicated interpretation only. Uniform sampling of labelled graphs on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ differs from uniform sampling of isomorphism classes, and conditioning on connectedness changes the model. The source fixes none of these or the concentration scale. An $o(n)$ statement, bounded fluctuations and a specified limiting fluctuation law are distinct targets.

Short English statement

Do identical large rooted neighborhoods force global vertex transitivity? Separately, does a uniformly sampled odd-order vertex-transitive graph have degree close to half its order? Clarify ball size, threshold quantifiers and the random-graph sampling model.
