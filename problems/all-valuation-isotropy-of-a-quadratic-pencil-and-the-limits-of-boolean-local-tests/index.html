Math-1244

All-valuation isotropy of a quadratic pencil and the limits of Boolean local tests

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Over $F={\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, consider the homogeneous diagonal quadratic equation \[ q_t(x)=\sum_{j=1}^s A_j(t)x_j^2=0,\qquad A_j(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}(t), \] where solubility means a nonzero vector over the relevant field. The source asks for a circle method over ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, and for a Hasse principle using appropriate local conditions, for example those from discrete valuation rings. Wooley notes difficulty identifying major arcs; the valuation class and analytic setup are not specified.

For linear coefficients $A_j=c_j+td_j$, put $q_0=\sum c_jx_j^2$, $q_1=\sum d_jx_j^2$, and \[ X=\{q_0=q_1=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{s-1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}. \] The cited Amer--Brumer result is a solubility equivalence between isotropy of $q_0+tq_1$ over ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$ and a rational point on $X$; it is not coefficientwise equality for arbitrary rational-function solutions. This is a quartic del Pezzo surface specifically when $s=5$ and the intersection is smooth, not for every number of variables. The source asks whether the "obvious" local conditions over ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$ capture the Brauer--Manin obstruction for $X$, and Harari suggests corresponding questions over ${\mathbb{Q}}_p(t)$, referring to Harari--Szamuely.

The attached aid treats a smooth complete intersection $X=\{q_0=q_1=0\}$ over a number field $k$. For each place $v$ of $k$, it reports \[ X(k_v)\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_0+Tq_1\text{ isotropic over }k_v(T), \] and the analogous equivalence over $k$ and $k(T)$. If $X(k_v)\ne\varnothing$ for every $v$, it additionally reports isotropy over the completion $k(T)_w$ for every rank-one discrete valuation $w$ of $k(T)$, not only the coefficient-field tests. It says closed discriminant points are included through the corank-one first residue form; the full rank/smoothness proof hypotheses are not supplied.

Consequently any Hasse counterexample of this type gives a globally anisotropic pencil passing both all coefficient-field and all rank-one-DVR isotropy tests. If its failure is Brauer--Manin, the tests cannot encode that obstruction merely as Boolean isotropy assertions. For clarity, the Brauer--Manin condition uses adelic points $(P_v)$ and simultaneous reciprocity equations \[ \sum_v\operatorname{inv}_v\alpha(P_v)=0\quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(X), \] which depend on the chosen points across places. The record supplies no explicit Hasse counterexample or full replacement local-global principle, and does not construct the requested general circle method.

Short English statement

Develop local-global and circle-method techniques over ${\mathbb{Q}}(t)$, especially for quadratic pencils, and compare their local tests with Brauer--Manin on intersections of two quadrics. The attached aid reports that adelically soluble base intersections pass all rank-one valuation isotropy tests, so such tests alone can miss global failures.
