Math-1288

Fourier norms of indicator functions and a two-interval splitting benchmark

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix $d\geq1$ and a finite real exponent $2<q<\infty$. For a Lebesgue measurable set $E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$ of measure $1$, use \[ \widehat{\mathbf1_E}(\xi)=\int_E e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x, \qquad M_{d,q}=\sup_{|E|=1}\|\widehat{\mathbf1_E}\|_{L^q({\mathbb{R}}^d)}. \] Christ's Question 2 asks to describe all measurable extremizers $E$ satisfying $\|\widehat{\mathbf1_E}\|_q=M_{d,q}$. Sets must be identified up to null sets; translations and volume-preserving invertible affine changes give the natural symmetries. An ellipsoid here is an invertible affine image of a ball, with volume $1$. In one dimension this is an interval of length $1$. The question is not a classification of arbitrary complex-valued functions under a norm constraint.

The source's Theorem 0.1, attributed to Christ [3], reports existence of an extremizing set for every $q>2$; ellipsoid rigidity in each dimension for sufficiently large exponents sufficiently close to even integers; the corresponding rigidity in dimension one near even integers; and rigidity in dimension two for $q$ sufficiently close to $4$. The source writes the even exponents as $2{\mathbb{N}}$ without giving thresholds, neighborhood sizes, or an explicit lower endpoint for that notation. Consequently no uniform neighborhood size in $d$ or in the even exponent, and no extension to $q=2$, is asserted here. Outside the supplied partial ranges, the global classification is described as open by the attached aid.

The aid supplies a one-dimensional test family \[ E_g=[0,\tfrac12]\cup[\tfrac12+g,1+g],\qquad g\geq0. \] Writing $t_+=\max(t,0)$ and $\delta(g)=\inf_{I:\,|I|=1}|E_g\mathbin{\triangle}I|$, it claims \[ \delta(g)=\min(2g,1),\qquad \|\widehat{\mathbf1_{E_g}}\|_4^4 =\frac12+\frac43(\tfrac12-g)_+^3, \] \[ \|\widehat{\mathbf1_{[0,1]}}\|_4^4- \|\widehat{\mathbf1_{E_g}}\|_4^4 =\frac{\delta(g)}2-\frac{\delta(g)^2}2+\frac{\delta(g)^3}6. \] For each fixed finite $q>2$, it also reports \[ \lim_{g\to\infty} \frac{\|\widehat{\mathbf1_{E_g}}\|_q^q}{\|\widehat{\mathbf1_{[0,1]}}\|_q^q} =c_q=\frac{2\Gamma((q+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((q+2)/2)}<1, \] and \[ c_q=1-(\log2-\tfrac12)(q-2)+O((q-2)^2) \quad(q\downarrow2). \] These supplied special-family claims compare equal half-intervals with a single interval. They do not classify all unit-measure sets, prove ellipsoid extremality for every $q$, or give a global quantitative stability theorem.

Short English statement

For each dimension and finite $q>2$, classify unit-measure sets maximizing the $L^q$ norm of their indicator's Fourier transform. The source reports ellipsoid rigidity in specified neighborhoods of even exponents. The attached two-interval formulas are partial benchmarks rather than a global classification.
