Erdős Problem 904: a clique of large degree sum

Statement and conventions.
Let $n\geq r\geq2$ and $m\geq t_r(n)$. For every $n$-vertex graph with $m$ edges there is an $r$-clique whose degree sum is at least $2rm/n$. The natural condition $n\geq r$ is necessary to speak of an $r$-clique. We reconstruct the greedy proof of Bollobás and Nikiforov.
Greedy choices and their degree bound.
Choose $v_1$ of maximum degree. After choosing a clique $v_1,\ldots,v_i$, choose $v_{i+1}$ of maximum degree in its common neighbourhood, stopping if that set is empty. Write $d_i=d(v_i)$; thus $d_1\geq d_2\geq\cdots$. For any available prefix $v_1,\ldots,v_q$, partition the vertices into the successive losses from the common neighbourhoods, and a final common neighbourhood: \[ V_i=\bigcap_{j<i}N(v_j)\setminus N(v_i)\ (i<q), \qquad V_q=\bigcap_{j<q}N(v_j). \] The empty intersection in $V_1$ is the whole vertex set. Every vertex of $V_i$ has degree at most $d_i$, and $|V_i|\leq n-d_i$ for $i<q$. Consequently \[ 2m\leq nd_q+\sum_{i<q}(d_i-d_q)(n-d_i). \tag{1} \]
The greedy clique has enough vertices.
Suppose a first index $q$ satisfies $\sum_{i\leq q}d_i\leq(q-1)n$. Put $k_i=n-d_i$ for $i<q$, $K=\sum_{i<q}k_i$, and $k_q=n-K$. Minimality gives $K<n$, so all $k_i$ are positive integers and their sum is $n$. Also $d_q\leq K$. Substituting in $1$ gives \[ 2m\leq\sum_{i<q}(n-k_i)k_i+d_qk_q \leq n^2-\sum_{i=1}^q k_i^2 =2\sum_{i<j}k_ik_j\leq2t_q(n). \tag{2} \] The last inequality follows by balancing the integer part sizes: moving one vertex from a part at least two larger than another increases the number of cross pairs.

If the greedy process stops after $k<r$ choices, its common neighbourhood is empty. The union bound on the complements gives $\sum_{i\leq k}d_i\leq(k-1)n$, so an index $q\leq k$ as above exists. But $t_q(n)<t_r(n)$ for $q<r\leq n$: splitting any part of size at least two increases the number of cross pairs. This contradicts $2$. Thus at least $r$ choices exist.

Moreover, if $S_r=\sum_{i\leq r}d_i<(r-1)n$, choose the first such non-strict crossing $q\leq r$. Again $q<r$ is impossible; if $q=r$, then $d_q<K$, and the positive factor $k_q$ makes $2$ strict, also contradicting $m\geq t_r(n)$. We have proved $S_r\geq(r-1)n$.

The average-degree conclusion.
Apply $1$ with $q=r$, expand, and then use $d_r\leq S_r/r$ and Cauchy--Schwarz: \[ \begin{split} 2m &\leq nS_r-\sum_{i=1}^r d_i^2 +d_r(S_r-(r-1)n)\\ &\leq nS_r-\frac{S_r^2}{r} +\frac{S_r}{r}(S_r-(r-1)n) =\frac{nS_r}{r}. \end{split} \] The substitution for $d_r$ has the correct direction because its coefficient was proved nonnegative. Hence $S_r\geq2rm/n$, as required.
Attribution and assessment.
The proof follows the greedy algorithm and estimates in B. Bollobás and V. Nikiforov, The sum of degrees in cliques, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410218, Theorems 1 and 2, with the threshold argument written directly using integer part sizes. The statement is recorded at https://www.erdosproblems.com/904, checked 24 September 2026. No resolution theorem is assumed without proof above, and no new discovery or local Lean verification is claimed.
