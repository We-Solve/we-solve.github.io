Counting Integer Matrices with Fixed Characteristic Polynomial

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record concerns the following conjecture, with two conventions requiring confirmation: it does not explicitly define matrix height or state the full range of matrix dimensions. Under the usual entrywise-height interpretation, for a fixed positive integer $n$ and a real number $H\geq 1$, put \[ \mathcal M_n({\mathbb{Z}};H)=\{A=(a_{ij})\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}({\mathbb{Z}}):\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|a_{ij}|\leq H\}. \] For a monic polynomial $f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]$ of degree $n$, define \[ R_n(H;f)=\#\{A\in\mathcal M_n({\mathbb{Z}};H):\det(XI_n-A)=f(X)\}, \] where $I_n$ is the identity matrix. Restricting attention to monic integral polynomials of degree $n$ retains every possible characteristic polynomial of an integer $n\times n$ matrix.

Conjecture 1 in the supplied excerpt. For each fixed dimension $n$ in the intended range, \[ R_n(H;f)\leq H^{n(n-1)/2+o(1)}\qquad(H\to\infty), \] uniformly over $f$. Explicitly, uniformity means that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist constants $C_{n,\varepsilon}>0$ and $H_{n,\varepsilon}\geq1$, independent of $f$, such that \[ \forall H\geq H_{n,\varepsilon}\quad \forall f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]\text{ monic of degree }n,\qquad R_n(H;f)\leq C_{n,\varepsilon}H^{n(n-1)/2+\varepsilon}. \] In particular, $f$ may vary with $H$; no uniformity in $n$ is asserted. The entrywise-height convention and the intended lower bound on $n$ remain subject to source confirmation.

Supplied contextual claims, not independently verified here. The excerpt states that the conjecture holds for $n=2$, and gives the unconditional uniform estimate \[ R_n(H;f)\leq H^{n^2-n+o(1)}. \] It also introduces the conditional hypothesis that, for some $\gamma_n\geq0$, uniformly over $f$, \[ R_n(H;f)\leq H^{n^2-n-\gamma_n+o(1)}. \] The excerpt identifies $\gamma_n=n(n-1)/2$ with the conjectured exponent, states that $\gamma_n=0$ is available unconditionally, and reports an explicit positive sequence with $\gamma_3=\gamma_4=1$.

For integers $d,t$, the excerpt further considers \[ S_n(H;d,t)=\#\{A\in\mathcal M_n({\mathbb{Z}};H):\det A=d,\ \operatorname{tr}A=t\}. \] Writing $f(X)=X^n+c_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0$, it observes that \[ R_n(H;f)\leq S_n\left(H;(-1)^nc_0,-c_{n-1}\right). \] Its Theorem 3 is stated for $n\geq4$, uniformly over $d,t$, and reports \[ S_4(H;d,t)\ll H^{11+o(1)},\qquad S_n(H;d,t)\ll H^{n^2-n-\gamma_n}\quad(n\geq5). \] The explicit formula for $\gamma_n$ accompanying this theorem is corrupted in the supplied extraction and cannot be reconstructed reliably. These contextual bounds are recorded as source claims rather than additional targets or independently established facts.

Short English statement

For each fixed matrix size, count integer matrices whose entries have absolute value at most $H$ and whose characteristic polynomial equals a prescribed polynomial. The conjecture asks whether this count is at most $H^{n(n-1)/2+o(1)}$, with the error uniform over the polynomial. The height convention and intended range of matrix sizes require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open and reports the case $n=2$ as proved; these are supplied assessments.
