Math-6571

Additive Eigenvalue Bounds for Domain Boundaries

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $m\geq 1$ be an integer and let $\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}$ be a nonempty smooth bounded domain with boundary $\Sigma=\partial\Omega$. Equip $\Sigma$ with the metric induced by the Euclidean metric. Write $\operatorname{Vol}_m(\Sigma)$ for its hypersurface volume and $\operatorname{Vol}_{m+1}(\Omega)$ for the volume of the domain.

The supplied record does not define $I(\Omega)$ or $\lambda_k(\Sigma)$. The conventional interpretation to be checked against the report is \[ I(\Omega)=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_m(\Sigma)}{\operatorname{Vol}_{m+1}(\Omega)^{m/(m+1)}}, \] with $\lambda_k(\Sigma)$ an eigenvalue of the nonnegative Laplace--Beltrami operator on the closed hypersurface $\Sigma$, ordered nondecreasingly and counted with multiplicity. This is an intrinsic spectral problem on $\Sigma$; it requires no boundary condition on $\Sigma$. The record does not specify whether the first eigenvalue is indexed by $0$ or $1$, whether zero eigenvalues are included, or whether connectedness of $\Sigma$ is assumed. These conventions remain unresolved.

Subject to confirmation of these definitions and conventions, the proposed assertion is that for every integer $m\geq1$ there exist finite constants $A_m,B_m\geq0$, depending only on $m$, such that for every such domain $\Omega$ and every positive integer eigenvalue index $k$, \[ \lambda_k(\Sigma)\operatorname{Vol}_m(\Sigma)^{2/m} \leq A_m I(\Omega)^{1+2/m}+B_m k^{2/m}. \] The positive-dimensional range and the positive integer range for $k$ make explicit the intended interpretation of the displayed exponents and spectral index; their precise source conventions require confirmation. The question concerns this additive estimate uniformly over domains and indices.

Short English statement

Does every positive dimension admit constants depending only on that dimension which bound the scale-invariant boundary eigenvalues by an additive combination of an isoperimetric term and $k^{2/m}$? The meanings of the isoperimetric ratio, the spectral indexing, and the connectedness assumptions require confirmation from the source. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
