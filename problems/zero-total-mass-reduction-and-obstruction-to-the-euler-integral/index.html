Definitions and notation.
In the circular restricted three-body problem, two primaries follow prescribed circular motion and a test particle has negligible effect on them. In rotating coordinates the Jacobi constant combines kinetic energy and the effective gravitational/centrifugal potential. The Hill region is the set where the zero-velocity inequality permits motion. Its necks occur near collinear equilibria. A first integral is constant along trajectories; functional independence and Poisson commutation are needed for the usual Hamiltonian notion of complete integrability. The signed-mass condition $m_1+m_2=0$ is exceptional and cannot hold for two positive primaries. It must be interpreted in the generalized model intended by the source, not ordinary positive-mass circular dynamics.

Testing the zero-total-mass kinematics.
In the signed two-primary Newtonian equations, let $r=q_1-q_2$. Then \[\ddot q_1=-m_2r/|r|^3,\quad \ddot q_2=m_1r/|r|^3,\quad \ddot r=-(m_1+m_2)r/|r|^3. \] If $m_1=m\ne0$ and $m_2=-m$, the relative acceleration vanishes. A prescribed nonzero constant separation $r_0$ is compatible with both primaries sharing the constant acceleration $a=mr_0/|r_0|^3$. It is not compatible with an ordinary nonzero-frequency circular relative motion, whose relative acceleration would be $-\omega^2r_0$.
The coaccelerating restricted equation.
Write the primary trajectories as $Q(t)+a_i$, with $Q''=a$, and the test-particle position as $Q(t)+x(t)$. Its equation is \[\ddot x=-\nabla U(x),\quad U(x)=-\frac{m}{|x-a_1|}+\frac{m}{|x-a_2|}+a\cdot x. \] The signs follow by differentiating the two Coulomb terms and subtracting the common frame acceleration. Hence $\tfrac12|\dot x|^2+U(x)$ is conserved on collision-free trajectories. The inertial-frame correction is a linear potential; simply replacing the signed pair by fixed centers and omitting it changes the model.
