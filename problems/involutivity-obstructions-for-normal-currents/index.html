Involutivity Obstructions for Normal Currents

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal L^3$ denote Lebesgue measure on ${\mathbb{R}}^3$. A $2$-current $T$ is normal if both $T$ and its boundary $\partial T$ have finite mass, where $\partial T(\eta)=T({\,\mathrm{d}}\eta)$ for every smooth compactly supported $1$-form $\eta$. Write $N_2({\mathbb{R}}^3)$ for this class.

Retain exactly the vector fields supplied in the record: \[ X(x,y,z)=(1,0,-x/2),\qquad Y(x,y,z)=(0,1,y/2),\qquad \xi=X\wedge Y. \] The source does not specify whether $\mu$ is a nonnegative or signed scalar Radon measure. Under either interpretation, interpret its notation $T=\xi\wedge\mu$ as the current represented by \[ T(\omega)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^3}\langle\omega(p),\xi(p)\rangle\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu(p) \] for smooth compactly supported $2$-forms $\omega$. The hypothesis $T\in N_2({\mathbb{R}}^3)$ includes the required global finite-mass conditions. Absolute continuity $\mu\ll\mathcal L^3$ means that $\mu(A)=0$ for every Borel set $A$ with $\mathcal L^3(A)=0$.

The literal measure question in the supplied record is whether, for every such $\mu$, \[ \xi\wedge\mu\in N_2({\mathbb{R}}^3) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu\ll\mathcal L^3. \] Unresolved formulation conflict. The record calls $X,Y$ horizontal fields of the Heisenberg group $\mathbb H^1$ and calls their span non-involutive. However, the displayed fields satisfy $[X,Y]=0$. Since they are everywhere linearly independent, their span is a smooth involutive rank-two distribution. Thus the literal displayed question and the described Heisenberg non-involutive target are incompatible. The implication above records the supplied formulas; it does not repair or identify the intended non-involutive example.

The record also asks for a classification of normal $k$-currents that fail a Frobenius-type involutivity conclusion asserted for integral currents, and asks what survives below $C^1$ regularity or when involutivity is defined without Lie brackets. These are retained as research questions only: the supplied record does not state the precise integral-current conclusion, the proposed classification class, the ambient dimensions and hypotheses, the weaker regularity class, or the replacement definition of involutivity. Consequently, these extensions do not yet determine precise quantified mathematical statements.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether normality of a current represented by a prescribed plane field forces its representing measure to be absolutely continuous. Its displayed fields commute, conflicting with the intended non-involutive Heisenberg example. The broader classification and weaker-regularity questions lack necessary specifications. The supplied partially solved label is dated curation, not an independently verified status.
