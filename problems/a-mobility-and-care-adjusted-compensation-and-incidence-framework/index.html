A restricted resource-allocation route

The source combines mobility, compensation, family support and healthcare access. A mathematical allocation model cannot resolve those institutional questions. To test one limited way of allocating a fixed support budget, let $c_1,\ldots,c_n\ge0$ be externally supplied commensurable burdens and let $0\le B\le\sum_i c_i$. A transfer $f_i\ge0$ uses budget $\sum_i f_i\le B$ and leaves residual burden $(c_i-f_i)_+$, where $u_+=\max(u,0)$.
Proposition: For $0<B<\sum_i c_i$, there is a unique $t\in(0,\max_i c_i)$ with $\sum_i(c_i-t)_+=B$. Transfers $f_i=(c_i-t)_+$ minimize the largest residual burden, with optimal value $t$. For $B=0$ the optimum is $\max_i c_i$, and for $B=\sum_i c_i$ it is zero.
Proof: The function $F(t)=\sum_i(c_i-t)_+$ is continuous, with $F(0)=\sum_i c_i$ and $F(\max c_i)=0$. It is strictly decreasing on $[0,\max c_i]$ if the maximum is positive: for any two distinct points below the maximum, at least one term strictly decreases, while none increases. The intermediate value theorem gives the unique interior root for the stated interior budgets. The proposed transfers sum to $B$ and leave each residual equal to $\min(c_i,t)$, so their maximum is $t$. If some allocation had maximum residual at most $r<t$, it would need $f_i\ge(c_i-r)_+$ for every $i$, hence total at least $F(r)>F(t)=B$, a contradiction. The endpoint budgets give the asserted optima by nonnegativity and the allocations $f=0$ or $f=c$. If all burdens are zero, both endpoint conclusions coincide. ∎
For $c=(8,4,2)$ and $B=6$, the equation gives $t=3$ and $f=(5,1,0)$; residuals are $(3,3,2)$. Equal transfers $(2,2,2)$ instead leave maximum 6. This illustrates the model's objective, not an assertion that minimizing the maximum of these invented numbers is the community's chosen fairness criterion.

Verification and remaining gaps

The example and all integer allocations with total at most 6 were enumerated; the smallest maximum residual was 3. The proof covers real transfers as well.
