Finite Factorization Bases for Breaking Maps

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $X$ be a smooth uniruled projective variety, as in the supplied source. The base field and any characteristic restrictions are unspecified. The source also does not define a breaking map, specify the allowed varieties serving as its domain, or state whether the definition depends on additional data on $X$. Consequently, the following is a conditional formulation of the requested finiteness assertion, pending those definitions.

Write $\mathcal B(X)$ for the intended class of breaking maps to $X$. For maps $f:Y\to X$ and $f_i:Y_i\to X$, rational factorization through $f_i$ means the existence of a rational map $g:Y\dashrightarrow Y_i$ for which the composite is defined on a dense open subset of $Y$ and \[ f=f_i\circ g \] there. Any requirements that $g$ be dominant or that these maps be defined over a particular field remain unspecified by the record.

Question. For every smooth uniruled projective variety $X$ in the intended setting, does there exist an integer $r\geq 0$ and maps \[ f_i:Y_i\to X\quad (1\leq i\leq r),\qquad f_i\in\mathcal B(X), \] such that \[ \forall\,(f:Y\to X)\in\mathcal B(X),\quad \exists\,i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\quad \exists\,g:Y\dashrightarrow Y_i \quad\text{with}\quad f=f_i\circ g \] as rational maps? The finite family may depend on $X$. Without the missing definition of $\mathcal B(X)$ and the intended geometric setting, this schema does not determine a fully specified mathematical conjecture.

Short English statement

The question asks whether each smooth uniruled projective variety admits finitely many breaking maps through which every breaking map to it factors rationally. The supplied record leaves the meaning of breaking map and the base field unspecified, so the statement requires clarification before mathematical review.
