Moments of L-functions — Moments of the Riemann zeta function — Problem 39

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $k$ and a positive integer $m$, let the generalized divisor function be \[ d_k(m)=\#\{(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}^{\,k}:a_1\cdots a_k=m\}. \] Let $r\ge3$ be an integer, let $k_1,\ldots,k_r$ be positive integers, and let $h_1,\ldots,h_r$ be pairwise distinct positive integers. For real $x\ge1$, define \[ S_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{h}}(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,;\ 1\le n\le x}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}d_{k_j}(n+h_j). \] The problem is to formulate an explicit conjectural asymptotic formula for $S_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{h}}(x)$ as $x\to\infty$. In particular, seek an explicit leading expression $A_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{h}}(x)$ such that one conjectures \[ \frac{S_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{h}}(x)}{A_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{h}}(x)}\longrightarrow1. \] Here the parameters are held fixed when taking the limit; the supplied source does not specify uniformity in them, the desired error term, or the extent of any lower-order expansion. The source writes $(k_1,\ldots,k_r)\in{\mathbb{N}}^r$; the formulation above uses the positive-integer convention. If zero is intended to belong to ${\mathbb{N}}$, the source does not specify the meaning of $d_0$.

The source motivates this problem by analogy with conjectural Hardy--Littlewood asymptotics for \[ \sum_{\substack{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,;\ 1\le n\le x}}\Lambda(n+h_1)\cdots\Lambda(n+h_r), \] where $\Lambda(m)=\log p$ if $m=p^a$ for a prime $p$ and an integer $a\ge1$, and $\Lambda(m)=0$ otherwise. This analogy does not supply a formula for the divisor correlation. The source also states that Ng--Thom and Tao used different probabilistic arguments to derive conjectures for $r=2$, but supplies no specific references for those works.

Short English statement

For every fixed choice of at least three pairwise distinct positive shifts and positive divisor-function orders, conjecture an explicit asymptotic formula for the sum of the corresponding shifted divisor-function products. The source compares this task with Hardy--Littlewood prime correlations and mentions probabilistic conjectures for two shifts. This is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
