Math-1926

Termination of juggler sequences

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Define $J:{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}$ by \[ J(a)=\begin{cases} \lfloor\sqrt a\rfloor,&a\text{ even},\\ \lfloor a^{3/2}\rfloor,&a\text{ odd}. \end{cases} \] For each positive integer $a_0$, its juggler sequence is $a_n=J^n(a_0)$, $n\ge0$. The termination conjecture is \[ \forall a_0\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}\ \exists N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\ge0},\qquad J^N(a_0)=1. \] Since $J(1)=1$, this is equivalent to eventual constant value $1$. The initial value $a_0=1$ is included, with $N=0$; no upper bound on $N$ or on intermediate values is asserted.

The supplied research summary reports the conjecture open and empirical verification to unspecified large starting-value bounds, mentioning OEIS without an entry or exact numerical threshold. Its phrase uniform boundedness for all starting values is not a precise subsidiary conjecture: a single bound on all values of all sequences would already fail at $n=0$, whereas boundedness of each individual orbit is a different assertion. Neither interpretation is substituted for termination.

Short English statement

Does every positive integer eventually reach $1$ when repeatedly replaced by the floor of its square root if even, or of its $3/2$ power if odd? The supplied source labels this open.
