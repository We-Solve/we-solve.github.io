Math-264

Exact half-volume graph theorem for symmetric planar rectangles

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

David Jerison's source asks whether a least-area interface dividing a symmetric convex body into halves, or any fixed proportion, is a graph, although it need not be a hyperplane. A precise relative-isoperimetric interpretation takes a centrally symmetric convex body $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^n$, $0<\theta<1$, and a finite-perimeter set $E\subset K$ minimizing \[ P(E;\operatorname{int}K)=|D\mathbf1_E|(\operatorname{int}K),\qquad |E|=\theta|K|. \] Only interior interface area is counted, not the part on $\partial K$. The graph question concerns the interior measure-theoretic interface, after some rotation and with an appropriate representative. The original does not fix regularity, treatment of singular points or the exact graph convention.

The attached aid settles a planar half-volume family. Let $R=(-a,a)\times(-b,b)$ with $a\ge b>0$. For every finite-perimeter $E\subset R$ with $|E|=2ab$, \[ P(E;R)\ge2b. \] If $a>b$, equality holds exactly, up to planar null sets, for the left or right half-rectangle $R\cap\{x<0\}$ and $R\cap\{x>0\}$. If $a=b$, the top and bottom halves are the only additional equality cases. Their reduced interior interfaces are the corresponding central shortest-length segments and global affine graphs over intervals after rotation. This does not establish the arbitrary-proportion or general-convex-body assertion.

The aid describes a BV-compatible even reflection into the $4a$ by $4b$ flat torus, multiplying area and relative perimeter by four without seam jumps, followed by a classification of half-area equality bands and imposition of the fold symmetries. Its full torus equality proof is not supplied here.

The original remark discusses the Simons cone with displayed equation \[ x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_4^2=x_5^2+x_6^2+x_7^2+x_8^2. \] It reports minimization with a prescribed boundary on the ball, nongraph behavior, and smaller area for a bisecting plane. The fixed-boundary comparison differs from unconstrained relative half-volume minimization, so it is not a counterexample to the question. The remark further claims a growing-dimension approach to the plane area and asks whether a Gaussian limit could conflict with Borell's finite-dimensional uniqueness of half-spaces. The cone family, normalization and limiting notion are not specified in this record; that proposed limit and its inference are retained as an unverified source discussion, not an established contradiction or failure of Borell's theorem.

Short English statement

Ask whether relative area-minimizing partitions of symmetric convex bodies have graphical interfaces. The supplied aid proves the exact half-area rectangle case, while preserving the different fixed-boundary and Gaussian-limit qualifications in the original Simons-cone remark.
