Math-1231

Smoothable closure and stable reduction for stable surfaces

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A stable surface is conventionally a projective surface with semi-log-canonical singularities and ample ${\mathbb{Q}}$-Cartier canonical divisor. Fix the relevant bounded moduli data, including the source's $K^2$ and $\chi(\mathcal O)$; the precise index/Hilbert data are not supplied. A smoothable surface occurs as a special fiber of an appropriate family with smooth or rational-double-point general fibers, but the source asks for a more precise infinitesimal notion rather than choosing one here.

The recovered 2004 Problem 1.2 has two parts. First, give a satisfactory boundary scheme structure on $\overline{M^{\mathrm{sm}}_{K^2,\chi}}$, the compactification of the smoothable locus. The source says discarding components parametrizing only worse-than-rational-double-point surfaces loses infinitesimal information. Second, retaining all stable-surface components, ask whether every punctured-disk family $\mathcal X\to\Delta'$ satisfying Kollár's condition extends, after possible base change, to a stable family over $\Delta$ satisfying the same condition. Kollár's condition concerns compatible relative reflexive canonical powers and base change; its exact flatness/index formulation is not included. In the usual notation $\omega^{[m]}=(\omega^{\otimes m})^{**}$ for positive $m$.

The supplied report describes a characteristic-zero KSBA answer through the scheme-theoretic closure of a rational-Gorenstein open substack $U$ in the full stack $\mathcal M$, after fixing precise bounded data. It attributes stable reduction after finite base change to Hacon--Xu, and uniqueness up to unique isomorphism under a finite separated diagonal hypothesis. The exact interior, relation to the original smoothability notion, and passage from stack closure to a coarse boundary scheme require specification.

Its candidate formal lemma is: for a noetherian algebraic stack $\mathcal M$ and open immersion $j:U\hookrightarrow\mathcal M$, let $\mathcal Z$ be cut out by \[ \ker(\mathcal O_{\mathcal M}\longrightarrow j_*\mathcal O_U). \] It claims $\mathcal Z$ is the smallest closed substack containing $U$ schematically densely, inherits properness over a base if $\mathcal M$ is proper over that base, and every map from an integral discrete valuation ring to $\mathcal M$ whose generic point maps to $U$ factors uniquely through $\mathcal Z$. This would place a full-stack stable extension with such generic fiber in the scheme-theoretic smoothable closure. It does not make an arbitrary noetherian stack proper or identify the closure with a reduced union of components.

For positive/mixed characteristic, the report mentions fixed-volume stacks over ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/30]$ and sufficiently-large-characteristic stable reduction for fixed data, while explicitly leaving uniform properness over ${\mathbb{Z}}[1/30]$ conditional on locally stable reduction and calling small characteristics delicate. These later assessments and the formal lemma remain unreviewed reports, distinct from the original source-era lack-of-proof statements.

Short English statement

Give an infinitesimally meaningful smoothable boundary and stable reduction for all components while preserving Kollár's condition. The report proposes characteristic-zero KSBA closure/reduction results and a general DVR factorization lemma. It keeps the positive/mixed-characteristic properness claims conditional.
