Symmetries of Open-Boundary Asymmetric Exclusion Processes

Definitions and mathematical statement

An asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) is a continuous-time particle process with at most one particle at each lattice site: particles attempt directionally biased jumps to neighbouring sites, and jumps to occupied sites are suppressed. Open boundaries allow particle exchange with external reservoirs. The supplied record does not specify the lattice, its size, the jump rates, or the boundary exchange rules and rates.

For a finite configuration space $\Omega$, write $c(\eta,\xi)\geq 0$ for the transition rate from $\eta$ to $\xi$, for distinct configurations. Using the convention that the intensity matrix acts on functions $f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}$, its action is \[ (Qf)(\eta)=\sum_{\substack{\xi\in\Omega\\\xi\ne\eta}}c(\eta,\xi)\left(f(\xi)-f(\eta)\right). \] This gives a finite-state framework only; the supplied record does not explicitly restrict the intended process to finite volume. In a linear-operator interpretation, a symmetry operator $S$ commutes with the intensity matrix, $SQ=QS$. A non-Abelian family of such symmetries contains operators $S,T$ satisfying $ST\ne TS$. The source does not identify the intended symmetry operators or algebra, so this interpretation does not establish which symmetries the report means.

The supplied research question is: What role do the non-Abelian symmetries of the intensity matrix play for ASEP with open boundaries?

The retained report passage reads: "An intriguing open problem is the role of such symmetries for the ASEP with open boundaries." The antecedent of "such symmetries" is absent from the supplied excerpt; its identification as non-Abelian symmetries of the intensity matrix comes from the supplied curated statement. Neither a particular consequence to establish nor parameter assumptions and quantifiers are supplied. Consequently, this is an unresolved research question requiring further source context, rather than a uniquely specified conjecture or theorem statement.

Short English statement

The question asks how non-Abelian symmetries of the transition-rate matrix affect ASEP when particles can enter or leave at the boundaries. The intended symmetries, boundary parameters, and mathematical consequences are unspecified.
