The supplied normalization uses the pushforward density of Lebesgue measure on a cutoff $D$ under $Q$: \[ Q_*(1_D\,{\,\mathrm{d}} x)=r_{Q,D}(t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t, \qquad\kappa(Q,D)=\int_{\mathbb{R}} r_{Q,D}(t)^3\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t. \] At regular values, the standard coarea interpretation is \[ r_{Q,D}(t)=\int_{D\cap Q^{-1}(t)}\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}\mathcal H^{d-1}(x)}{\|\nabla Q(x)\|}. \] The aid reports that the intended continuum and inhomogeneous-Poisson triple model forces normalization $\kappa(Q,D)T^2$. The exact deterministic lattice-count definition and scaling conventions are absent, so this is retained as the model normalization, not a proof for the original quadratic-form count. It also reports an angular formula for the Euclidean unit-ball cutoff, without including that formula or its dimension/singularity conventions in this record.

A precise version of the supplied Poisson model is obtained from a nonzero bounded compactly supported density $r\geq0$, with $0<\kappa=\int r^3<\infty$, by giving a real-line Poisson process intensity $r_T(s)=r(s/T^2)$. For bounded intervals $I=(a,b)$, $J=(c,d)$, let $Z_T(I,J)$ count ordered triples of pairwise distinct process points $(s_0,s_1,s_2)$ with $s_1-s_0\in I$, $s_2-s_0\in J$. The reported continuum mean and overlap-variance conclusions are \[ {\mathbb{E}} Z_T\sim\kappa T^2|I||J|,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_T)=O(T^2), \] \[ \frac{Z_T}{\kappa T^2}\longrightarrow|I||J|\quad\text{in }L^2. \] Bounded compact support and this scaling are explicit hypotheses; no rate or conclusion for all unbounded or singular coarea densities is inferred. Nor is a Poisson assumption imposed on actual quadratic-form values.

The aid's one-point obstruction is the randomly shifted integer lattice ${\mathbb{Z}}+U$, with $U$ uniform on $[0,1)$. Its expected counting measure is exactly Lebesgue measure, but every difference of its points is an integer. Its two-gap count for \[ I=J=(1/4,3/4) \] is therefore zero, despite $|I||J|=1/4$. This separates one-point intensity from the proposed two-gap law. It is a stochastic point-process countermodel, not an actual generic-quadratic-form counterexample.
