Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.118

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A profinite group is a topological group isomorphic to an inverse limit of finite discrete groups. A group is periodic if every element has finite order; no uniform bound on these orders is assumed. For a profinite group $G$, let \[ Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\} \] be its center, which is closed. Thus the hypothesis that $G/Z(G)$ is periodic means \[ \forall g\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\quad g^n\in Z(G). \] Define the abstract derived subgroup by \[ D(G)=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G\rangle, \] and let $\overline{D(G)}$ denote its closure in $G$.

The supplied question, proposed by P. Shumyatsky, asks whether $[G,G]$ is necessarily periodic whenever $G$ is profinite and $G/Z(G)$ is periodic. Under the abstract derived subgroup interpretation $[G,G]=D(G)$, the requested assertion is \[ \forall\,G\text{ profinite},\qquad \left(\forall g\in G\;\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\;g^n\in Z(G)\right) \Longrightarrow \left(\forall d\in D(G)\;\exists m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\;d^m=1\right). \] The supplied record does not specify whether $[G,G]$ means the abstract derived subgroup or the closed derived subgroup. Under the latter convention, the conclusion instead quantifies over every $d\in\overline{D(G)}$. This convention requires clarification from the source; the two formulations are not identified here.

Short English statement

For a profinite group whose quotient by its center consists entirely of elements of finite order, must every element of its derived subgroup also have finite order? The supplied notation leaves unresolved whether the derived subgroup includes its topological closure.
