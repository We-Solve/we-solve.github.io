Statement

Stem-cell niche control

Domain. Stem-cell niche dynamics.   Retained status/source context. Open homeostatic feedback programme with established crypt-specific models. The supplied collection is background; neutral clonal competition gives one restricted starting point.

Definitions and assumptions.
For an intestinal crypt let $S$ be stem-cell number, $D$ differentiating-cell number and $n$ a measured niche signal. A birth--death model has stem division rate $\beta(n)$, self-renewal probability $p(n)$, loss rate $\mu_S$ and differentiated output clearance $\mu_D$. Niche dynamics depend on occupancy and tissue demand. Rates are per day, division outcomes are observed by lineage tracking, and immigration or reserve-cell recruitment is explicitly included or excluded.
Problem statement.
Identify feedback that keeps stem abundance bounded while meeting output demand $Q$: \[ \dot S=[\beta(n)(2p(n)-1)-\mu_S]S,\quad \dot D=2\beta(n)(1-p(n))S-\mu_DD, \quad\mu_DD_*\geq Q. \] Find a stable full equilibrium $(S_*,D_*,n_*)$ and predict recovery and stochastic extinction risk after niche or stem-cell ablation. Balanced average growth alone is not sufficient homeostasis.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
Define the full daughter-number distribution, not only a mean self-renewal probability, and supply the niche-state dynamics. A nonlinear birth--death model does not generally yield a closed deterministic equation for the mean without an approximation and error regime. In a finite population without immigration, a stable positive mean-field equilibrium can coexist with eventual extinction; distinguish finite-horizon maintenance, quasi-stationarity and true invariant persistence.

Alternative formulations.
(i) At fixed crypt capacity, use neutral competition to predict clone-size distributions and replacement times. (ii) Allow capacity, feedback signal and demand to change after injury, testing coupled abundance regulation. The first concerns which stem clones occupy a niche; it does not explain why total niche size or tissue output is maintained.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Snippert and colleagues established a neutral-competition description in a restricted intestinal system. Models with symmetric or asymmetric individual divisions can share similar mean output, so lineage distributions matter. A finite stem pool can go extinct despite a stable deterministic mean unless replenishment is modelled. The unresolved target is causal feedback and its transfer from steady turnover to regeneration or dysregulated growth.

Attempted answer

Proposition: In a finite-capacity stem-cell birth--death chain with zero absorbing, positive death rate at every positive count, and no immigration, extinction occurs almost surely even when a mean-field equation has a stable positive equilibrium. No nonzero stationary population law exists.
Proof: From every positive state there is a finite sequence of deaths reaching zero with positive probability. Finiteness supplies a common finite-time extinction probability bounded below away from zero. Iterating the Markov property makes survival probability decay geometrically; stationary mass outside zero must vanish. ∎

What remains unresolved

Finite-horizon reliability or quasi-stationarity is a different criterion, and reserve-cell recruitment removes the absorbing-state assumption. Niche feedback, output demand and measured extinction times remain unresolved.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed.
