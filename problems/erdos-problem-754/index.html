Erdős Problem #754

Statement

Statement and result.
For an $n$-point set $A\subset\mathbb R^4$, each point chooses its most frequent positive distance to the other points. Let $f(n)$ be the largest possible minimum of these multiplicities. Then \[ f(n)\leq \frac n2+O(1). \] We reconstruct Swanepoel's reduction to the classical unit-distance bound, state that external input explicitly, and verify the matching leading-term construction.
The external geometric input.
Let $u_4(s)$ be the maximum number of unordered unit-distance pairs among $s$ points in $\mathbb R^4$. We use the known bound \[ u_4(s)\leq \frac{s^2}{4}+Cs\qquad(s\geq1), \tag{1} \] for an absolute constant $C$. Theorem 2 of Swanepoel's paper records this result of Erdős. A bound initially stated for sufficiently large $s$ gives $1$ for all $s$ by increasing $C$. Its geometric proof is an external dependency here.
From unit distances to arbitrary favourite distances.
Assign an arbitrary positive radius $r(x)$ to every $x\in A$, and draw an arc $x\to y$ when $|x-y|=r(x)$. Form an undirected graph from the pairs having arcs in both directions. Its connected components have vertex sets $A_1,\ldots,A_k$, of sizes $n_1,\ldots,n_k$.

Along any double edge, the two radii agree. Consequently $r$ is constant throughout each $A_i$, including the vacuous case of a singleton. Every arc within $A_i$ is therefore accompanied by its reverse; after scaling, its underlying edge is a unit-distance pair. Thus there are at most $2u_4(n_i)$ internal arcs. Between different components a pair supports at most one arc, since otherwise it would be a double edge joining the components. The total number of arcs is consequently at most \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_i2u_4(n_i)+\sum_{i<j}n_i n_j &\leq \frac12\sum_i n_i^2+2C\sum_i n_i +\frac12\left(n^2-\sum_i n_i^2\right)\\ &=\frac{n^2}{2}+2Cn. \end{aligned}\tag{2} \] Now choose at each point a radius attaining its maximum multiplicity. If every point has multiplicity at least $f$, there are at least $nf$ arcs. Dividing $2$ by $n$ proves $f\leq n/2+2C$, uniformly in $A$. The counts in this argument are directed in $2$ and unordered in $1$; the factor of two is essential.

The lower-bound construction.
In two mutually orthogonal coordinate planes take circles of radius $1/\sqrt2$ centred at the origin. Put $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct points on one circle and $\lceil n/2\rceil$ on the other. For points $x,y$ on different circles, \[ |x-y|^2=|x|^2+|y|^2=1. \] Every point therefore has at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ unit-distance neighbours. In particular $f(n)=n/2+O(1)$.

There is a useful refinement when $n=8t$. On each circle place $t$ disjoint rotated inscribed squares. Every point has its $4t$ neighbours on the other circle and two neighbours in its own square at distance one. This gives \[ f(8t)\geq4t+2. \] Distinct rotations modulo a quarter-turn ensure that all vertices are distinct. No assertion about an exact formula for every residue class of $n$ is needed.

Attribution and assessment.
The component argument is reconstructed from K. J. Swanepoel, Favourite distances in high dimensions, Theorem A and its proof, https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.4817. The Lenz construction and the prior work of Avis, Erdős, and Pach are recorded there and at https://www.erdosproblems.com/754, checked 24 September 2026. This is an exposition of known results, with $1$ explicitly assumed. No novelty or local Lean verification is claimed.
