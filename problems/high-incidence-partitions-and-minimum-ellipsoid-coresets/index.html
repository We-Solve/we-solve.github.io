High Incidence Partitions and Minimum Ellipsoid Coresets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Two questions recorded under one permanent ID.

(1) Incidence in convex-cell partitions. The supplied record asks whether, for every incidence threshold $c$, there exists a locally finite partition $\mathcal P$ of ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ into convex cells such that \[ \#\{v\in V(\mathcal P):v\text{ is incident with }C\}\ge c \quad\text{for every }C\in\mathcal P, \] \[ \#\{C\in\mathcal P:v\text{ is incident with }C\}\ge c \quad\text{for every }v\in V(\mathcal P). \] Here $V(\mathcal P)$ denotes the intended set of vertices, and local finiteness means that every compact subset of ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ meets only finitely many cells. For closed cells, the natural interpretation of incidence is $v\in C$. The source record does not specify whether cells must be polyhedral, bounded, full-dimensional, or face-to-face, or whether an incident vertex must be a vertex of the particular cell. These choices affect the meaning of $V(\mathcal P)$ and must be resolved before this becomes a precise existence question. The domain of $c$ is also unspecified; positive integer thresholds give the usual incidence interpretation, but this convention is not explicitly supplied.

(2) Subsets and minimum enclosing ellipsoids. For a finite set $S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d$, the record denotes its minimum enclosing ellipsoid by $\operatorname{MEE}(S)$ and calls $\operatorname{DBM}$ the Banach--Mazur distance. It proposes \[ f(d,\varepsilon)= \sup_{\substack{S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^d\\S\text{ finite}}} \min\left\{|X|:X\subseteq S,\quad \operatorname{DBM}\left(\operatorname{MEE}(X),\operatorname{MEE}(S)\right) \le\varepsilon\right\}, \] and asks whether \[ f(d,\varepsilon)=O\!\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^2}\right). \] This displays the supplied target literally; several ingredients remain unresolved. For a finite set $T$ with $\operatorname{aff}(T)={\mathbb{R}}^d$, the usual minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid minimizes volume among ellipsoids \[ E(a,A)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:(x-a)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}(x-a)\le1\}, \qquad a\in{\mathbb{R}}^d,\quad A=A^{\mathsf T}\succ0, \] that contain $T$. For lower-dimensional or empty sets, this full-dimensional definition does not supply a minimum enclosing ellipsoid; a relative-affine-hull or degenerate-ellipsoid convention and corresponding admissibility conditions on $S$ and $X$ are missing.

There is also a substantive distance ambiguity. Under the usual multiplicative affine Banach--Mazur convention, distances are at least $1$, and any two nondegenerate ellipsoids have distance $1$, since they are affinely equivalent. Thus the displayed comparison cannot express a nontrivial approximation of one such ellipsoid by another under that convention. A logarithmic convention changes the baseline but still assigns distance zero to affinely equivalent ellipsoids. The record does not specify a comparison retaining their relative position and shape, a different normalization, or another intended definition. No replacement of $\varepsilon$ by $1+\varepsilon$, or replacement of the distance, is made here. The admissible range of $\varepsilon$, the convention for a minimum over an empty admissible family, and the parameter range and uniformity intended by the $O$-bound also require clarification.

Short English statement

The record asks two separate questions: whether convex-cell partitions of ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ can have arbitrarily large incidence counts at every cell and vertex, and whether subsets of size $O(d/\varepsilon^2)$ suffice for the stated comparison of minimum enclosing ellipsoids. The partition conventions and ellipsoid comparison are insufficiently specified; ordinary affine Banach--Mazur distance makes all nondegenerate ellipsoids equivalent. Both questions therefore require clarification and human mathematical review.
