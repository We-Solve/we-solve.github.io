Selection Principles in Mathematics — Problem 9 ([MilNonGamma])

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathfrak c=|{\mathbb{R}}|=2^{\aleph_0}$. For a set $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$, use its subspace topology. A countable Borel $\omega$-cover of $X$ is a countable family $\mathcal U$ of Borel subsets of $X$ such that $X\notin\mathcal U$ and every finite subset of $X$ is contained in some member of $\mathcal U$. A countable Borel $\gamma$-cover of $X$ is a countably infinite family $\mathcal U$ of Borel subsets of $X$, with $X\notin\mathcal U$, such that each point of $X$ belongs to all but finitely many members of $\mathcal U$. Write $\mathrm B_\Omega(X)$ and $\mathrm B_\Gamma(X)$ for these two classes of covers.

The selection property $\mathsf S_1(\mathrm B_\Omega,\mathrm B_\Gamma)$ means that for every sequence $(\mathcal U_n)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}$ with $\mathcal U_n\in\mathrm B_\Omega(X)$, there are choices $U_n\in\mathcal U_n$ such that \[ \{U_n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\in\mathrm B_\Gamma(X). \] In particular, the selected family must be infinite; the displayed condition concerns the family of distinct selected sets.

For this rendering, Martin's Axiom $\mathrm{MA}$ denotes the assertion that for every partially ordered set satisfying the countable chain condition and every family of fewer than $\mathfrak c$ dense subsets, there is a filter meeting every member of that family. The countable chain condition means that every antichain is countable. With stronger conditions ordered below weaker ones, a filter is a nonempty upward closed, downward directed subset, and a set $D$ is dense if every condition has a stronger condition in $D$.

Question. Does Martin's Axiom imply, over $\mathrm{ZFC}$, that there exists $X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}$ such that \[ |X|=\mathfrak c \qquad\text{and}\qquad X\models\mathsf S_1(\mathrm B_\Omega,\mathrm B_\Gamma)? \] Here the last expression abbreviates the selection property defined above, rather than satisfaction in a first-order structure.

Short English statement

Under the stated conventional definitions, does Martin's Axiom guarantee a set of reals of size continuum from whose every sequence of countable Borel $\omega$-covers one can select one member per cover so that the selected family is a $\gamma$-cover?
