Math-6563

Maximum Families of Pairwise Colliding Permutations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $S_n$ be the set of bijections $[n]\to[n]$. Distinct permutations $\sigma,\tau\in S_n$ collide when \[ \exists i\in[n],\qquad |\sigma(i)-\tau(i)|=1. \] Thus the two permutations take the same input to consecutive integer values; no adjacency of input positions or cyclic adjacency of the values $1,n$ is intended by this definition. Define \[ \rho(n)=\max\{\,|\mathcal F|:\mathcal F\subseteq S_n, \ \forall\sigma\ne\tau\in\mathcal F\ \exists i\in[n] \text{ with }|\sigma(i)-\tau(i)|=1\,\}. \] The witness $i$ may depend on the pair. Since $S_n$ is finite and singleton families are allowed, $1\leq\rho(n)\leq n!$, so the maximum and its base-two logarithm are defined.

The question is whether the exponential upper growth rate is exactly one: \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2\rho(n)}{n}=1. \] Equivalently, the claimed value requires both an eventual upper estimate $\rho(n)\leq2^{(1+\varepsilon)n}$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, and arbitrarily large $n$ with $\rho(n)\geq2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. The lower estimate is only required along arbitrarily large indices for this limsup statement. The question does not request an exact formula for $\rho(n)$, equality with a finite-$n$ upper bound, or existence of the ordinary limit. In particular, it does not assert $\rho(n)=2^n$.

The literature triage checked 2026-08-21 reports a known upper bound and equality verified only for small $n$, with general equality conjectural. It does not print that finite-$n$ bound, the tested values of $n$, or its exact relation to the weaker exponential-rate question. These omissions are recorded rather than replacing the displayed limsup by a guessed exact extremal conjecture. The supplied open label and reported official-source normalization remain dated metadata, not independently verified status.

Short English statement

A family is pairwise colliding if every two different permutations differ by exactly one at some common input. Does its maximum size have base-two exponential limsup growth rate one? The asymptotic question is kept distinct from any stronger exact formula suggested by the literature summary.
