Math-1682

Locally analytic vectors determine the Hodge filtration through topology and extension data

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime, let $L/{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be a finite coefficient extension, and let $V$ be a continuous two-dimensional irreducible potentially crystalline $L$-representation of $G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_p/{\mathbb{Q}}_p)$. Assume it is of supercuspidal type, meaning that its associated Weil--Deligne representation is irreducible. Let $B(V)$ denote the associated admissible Banach representation of $\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{Q}}_p)$ in the $p$-adic local Langlands correspondence. The 2006 question describes its irreducibility as conjectural; that wording is historical source qualification.

Write $B(V)^{\mathrm{an}}$ for its locally analytic vectors, with their canonical locally convex topology, $D_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)$ for the associated overconvergent $(\varphi,\Gamma)$-module over the Robba ring, and $D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(V)$ for its potentially crystalline module with descent data. More explicitly, for a finite extension $K/{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ making $V$ crystalline, the latter is represented by $D_{\mathrm{cris},K}(V)$ and its descent data, with Hodge filtration on the corresponding de Rham realization. The source asks whether $B(V)^{\mathrm{an}}$ determines that filtration, up to the appropriate isomorphism of filtered modules.

Emerton's follow-up asks more generally to relate $B(V)^{\mathrm{an}}$ to $D_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)$, and to relate its dual $(B(V)^{\mathrm{an}})'$ to de Rham cohomology of coverings of the $p$-adic upper half-plane. Here the geometric interpretation in the supplied aid uses the continuous strong dual, rather than an unspecified algebraic dual. The Drinfeld upper half-plane is $\Omega={\mathbb{P}}^1_{{\mathbb{Q}}_p}\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^1({\mathbb{Q}}_p)$ in its rigid analytic sense; the intended coverings belong to its Drinfeld tower.

The aid reports that Colmez relates the locally analytic representation directly to $D_{\mathrm{rig}}(V)$ and that Colmez--Dospinescu identify its universal unitary completion, with this canonical topology, with $B(V)$. Recovery of $V$ from $B(V)$ then recovers its Hodge filtration. It further attributes to Dospinescu--Le Bras a geometric realization in normalized supercuspidal Hodge--Tate weights $(0,1)$: the strong dual is an inverse-image subspace in the de Rham complex selected by the Hodge line. The exact tower level, isotypic component, comparison map, twists, and normalization of that formula are not supplied, so no unrestricted cohomology isomorphism is asserted.

The aid's extension-data assertion has the following precise scope. Fix an absolutely irreducible Weil--Deligne module $M$ with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{WD}}(M)=L$, fix normalized Hodge--Tate weights $(0,1)$, and consider admissible Hodge lines $\ell\subset M_{\mathrm{dR}}$ for which the associated potentially crystalline representations $V_\ell$ exist. Put $E_\ell=B(V_\ell)^{\mathrm{an}}$. It reports exact sequences in the relevant topological locally analytic category \[ 0\longrightarrow\pi\longrightarrow E_\ell\longrightarrow A_M\longrightarrow0, \] with the same smooth subrepresentation $\pi$ and quotient $A_M$ for all $\ell$, but \[ E_\ell\simeq E_{\ell'}\ \text{topologically and }\operatorname{GL}_2({\mathbb{Q}}_p)\text{-equivariantly} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ell=\ell'. \] Thus the two-step associated graded object $\pi\oplus A_M$ cannot recover the varying Hodge line; the full topological extension does. The record does not define $A_M$ or the category's detailed exactness conventions, which remain review requirements.

Short English statement

For an irreducible potentially crystalline representation of supercuspidal type, ask whether its locally analytic $p$-adic Langlands representation determines the Hodge filtration, and how it relates to the Robba-ring module and Drinfeld-tower de Rham geometry. The supplied aid emphasizes canonical topology and extension data, with its geometric statement restricted to weights $(0,1)$.
