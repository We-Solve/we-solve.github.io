Math-3595

Multiplicative p-Groups of Braces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime. A finite $p$-group is a group of order $p^n$ for some integer $n\geq 0$. Under the conventional interpretation of a brace as a left brace with abelian additive group, a brace is a set $B$ with operations $+$ and $\circ$ such that $(B,+)$ is an abelian group, $(B,\circ)$ is a group, and \[ a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c \qquad(a,b,c\in B). \] Its multiplicative group is $(B,\circ)$. A skew left brace satisfies the same identity but need not have abelian additive group; thus the distinction between braces and skew braces is material here.

For a finite $p$-group $G$, define the realization property \[ \mathcal R(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there exists a finite left brace }(B,+,\circ) \text{ with }(B,\circ)\cong G. \] Equivalently, one asks whether an abelian group operation $+$ can be placed on the underlying set of $G$ so that its given multiplication and $+$ satisfy the displayed brace identity.

Statement under this interpretation. Determine which finite $p$-groups satisfy $\mathcal R(G)$, as $p$ ranges over the primes. Give a characterization, or necessary or sufficient group-theoretic conditions, for this realization property. In particular, determine a smallest finite $p$-group failing $\mathcal R$, with the following qualification about the intended minimum. If size means group order and $p$ is fixed, the numerical target is \[ m_p=\min\left\{|G|:G\text{ is a finite }p\text{-group and }\neg\mathcal R(G)\right\}, \] whenever this set is nonempty, together with an example attaining the minimum. If the minimum is intended across all primes, the corresponding target is \[ m=\min\left\{|G|:G\text{ is a finite }p\text{-group for some prime }p, \ \neg\mathcal R(G)\right\}, \] again conditional on nonemptiness. The supplied record does not specify which minimum is intended, and these alternatives must not be silently identified.

Short English statement

Determine which finite $p$-groups are multiplicative groups of braces, and seek a characterization, necessary or sufficient conditions, and a smallest nonrealizable example. The draft explicitly uses left braces with abelian additive group. The intended brace convention, the referent of "counterexample," and whether the minimum is for a fixed prime or across all primes require review. The accompanying skew-brace factorization question is preserved as unresolved context.
