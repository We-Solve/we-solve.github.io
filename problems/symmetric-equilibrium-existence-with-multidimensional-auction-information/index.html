Econ-85

Symmetric equilibrium existence with multidimensional auction information

LLM status Open unverified

D44 — Auctions Economics catalogue openaigpt 6 astra progpt 6 astra ultra 15%50%60% cataloguednot recordedOpen

Statement

Original source: Continuous-Strategy Nonexistence in a Two-Bidder Auction, 8 October 2026. Formulation: The historical continuous-strategy existence question, a model-specific classification, and the broader question remaining after removing strategy continuity.

Definitions and assumptions

Consider $n\ge2$ symmetric bidders and $1\le k<n$ identical units. A common quality $q\in[0,1]$ has a strictly positive continuous density $f$, and bidder $i$ has independently drawn taste $t_i\in[0,\bar t]$ with strictly positive continuous density $w$, independent of $q$. Each bidder privately observes $(t_i,s_i)$, where $s_i\in\{1,\ldots,S\}$ has conditional law \[ \Pr(s_i=s\mid q,t_i)=\alpha_s(q)\beta_s(t_i). \] Assume the resulting conditional probabilities sum to one, the $\alpha_s$ are continuously differentiable and bounded away from zero, $\beta_s$ are nonnegative measurable, and signal likelihood ratios $\alpha_r(q)/\alpha_s(q)$ are strictly increasing in $q$ whenever $r>s$. Types and signals are conditionally independent across bidders in the manner specified by these primitives. Values are $u(q,t)$ for a $C^1$ function strictly increasing in both arguments. These are the source note's finite-auction/basic information restrictions, with all conditional distributions interpreted as proper probability laws.

Each bidder submits a nonnegative bid. The highest $k$ bids win and pay the $(k+1)$st-highest bid per object; ties are broken symmetrically. For a symmetric pure bid profile $b=(b_s)_{s=1}^S$, let $\Pi_{\mathcal A}(t,s;x,b_{-i})$ be a bidder's conditional expected payoff from bid $x\ge0$ when all opponents use $b$. A symmetric pure Bayesian Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ \Pi_{\mathcal A}(t,s;b_s(t),b_{-i}) \ge \Pi_{\mathcal A}(t,s;x,b_{-i}) \quad\text{for all }x\ge0, \] for almost every $(t,s)$ in the type distribution. Write $\mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm{A2}}$ for those equilibria whose $b_s$ are continuous, strictly taste-increasing and differentiable except at finitely many points; $\mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm c}$ for those whose bids are merely continuous and strictly taste-increasing; and $\mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm{mon}}$ for measurable, weakly taste-increasing pure equilibria, allowing jumps and pooling.

Formal research question

  1. Historical continuous-strategy existence question (answered negatively in the supplied note). For every admissible auction primitive tuple $\mathcal A$, must \[ \mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm{A2}}\ne\varnothing? \] If not, specify primitives obeying the basic information assumptions for which no equilibrium in that regularity class exists.
  2. Exact continuous-existence classification in a binary-signal family (classified in the supplied note). Fix $n=2$, $k=1$, $q\sim U[0,1]$, $t_i\sim U[0,T]$, $u(q,t)=q+t$, and conditionally independent signals with \[ \Pr(s=1\mid q)=\frac34-\frac q2,\qquad \Pr(s=2\mid q)=\frac14+\frac q2. \] For each $T>0$, characterize whether $\mathcal E_T^{\mathrm c}$ is empty. In particular, the supplied note reports the exact parameter classification \[ \mathcal E_T^{\mathrm c}\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\in\left(0,\frac{2}{13}\right] \ \cup\ \left[\frac16+\frac{11}{144}\log\!\frac{13}{11},\,\infty\right). \] This is a source-stated result to be distinguished from the open general question; it is not asserted here as a newly unsolved conjecture. Separately classify existence in $\mathcal E_T^{\mathrm{mon}}$ when continuity is dropped.
  3. General symmetric pure existence without continuity (the remaining question in the source note). Does every primitive tuple $\mathcal A$ as above satisfy \[ \boxed{\quad\mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm{mon}} \ne\varnothing\ ?\quad} \] Here jumps, flat regions, and weak rather than strict monotonicity are permitted, but the auction rule, information assumptions, and ordinary pure Bayesian Nash incentives are unchanged.
  4. Primitive and approximation variants. Characterize additional restrictions on $(f,w,u,\alpha,\beta,n,k)$ that are necessary or sufficient for $\mathcal E_{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm c}\ne\varnothing$, and distinguish this from the existence, for each fixed finite $\mathcal A$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, of a symmetric $\varepsilon$-Bayesian-Nash profile in a specified continuous or monotone bid class. Do not replace this fixed-auction quantifier by an asymptotic conclusion as $n\to\infty$.

Interpretation and scope

The two-bidder family in (b) is a statement about the strategy regularity class, not about the absence of all symmetric pure Bayesian equilibria: the supplied note constructs discontinuous monotone equilibria in its continuous-nonexistence region. Therefore failure of Assumption 2 is not a counterexample to (c). The equilibrium inequalities are global best-response conditions; local bidding equations alone do not define an equilibrium. Any counterexample must specify signal probabilities, all priors, the utility function, the payment and tie rules, and the allowed bid class.

Source audit and status

The source note states and argues a complete threshold classification for its explicit two-signal family, including nonexistence of continuous strictly increasing equilibrium at $T=1/6$, and states that a discontinuous pure equilibrium exists there. The historical claim of universal continuous-strategy existence is thus treated as negatively resolved within that note. The broader general monotone pure-existence question in (c) is explicitly left open in the note; this catalogue has not independently verified its 2026 literature status. The 2000 Pesendorfer--Swinkels paper gives a conditional framework and asymptotic approximate-equilibrium results, not the asserted general finite-auction existence theorem.

Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions

Finite games. For a finite set $A$, $\Delta(A)$ is its probability simplex. Players choose independent mixed strategies in Nash equilibrium; correlation is allowed only when explicitly part of the solution concept. In a game with payoff functions $u_i$ and strategy profile $x$, an additive $\varepsilon$ Nash equilibrium satisfies \[ u_i(x)\geq u_i(x_i',x_{-i})-\varepsilon \quad\text{for every player $i$ and every admissible deviation $x_i'$}. \] For numerical approximation, payoffs are normalized as locally specified. An $\varepsilon$ payoff residual is distinct from distance at most $\varepsilon$ to the set of exact equilibria. Point convergence, convergence of empirical play, and convergence in distance to an equilibrium set are also distinct.

Computational representations. Unless stated otherwise, a complexity question takes rational data with binary encoding; polynomial time is measured in total bit length. A fixed-accuracy polynomial algorithm is not necessarily polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$, and neither is necessarily polynomial in $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Succinct games and value, demand, or sampling oracles must declare their interfaces. Exact real solutions require an explicit representation; an unspecified list of arbitrary real numbers is not a finite computational output. A claimed algorithmic barrier is meaningful only for its specified model and reductions.

Dynamic games. Histories, information, observation, and allowable randomization are part of the model. A stationary strategy depends only on the current state; a positional strategy in a deterministic graph game chooses one action at each controlled vertex. Nash equilibrium at an initial state and equilibrium after every history are different requirements. An expectation of a pathwise $\liminf$ payoff, a $\liminf$ of expected averages, a limiting expected average, and a uniform finite-horizon equilibrium are different criteria. None is silently substituted for another.

Allocation and mechanisms. A complete allocation assigns every item exactly once unless otherwise stated. Zero-valued goods, negative values, capacity constraints, feasibility, lotteries, and copies of goods affect fairness definitions and are specified locally. Dominant-strategy incentive compatibility, Bayesian incentive compatibility, universal truthfulness, and truthfulness in expectation impose different inequalities. Whenever an objective ratio has zero denominator, its convention is stated or those instances are excluded.

Infinite-dimensional models. Expectations, integrals, stochastic equations, and optimization objectives require their local regularity, integrability, filtrations, and admissible domains. A backward--forward mean-field system is a boundary-value problem; it is not an initial-value semiflow without an additional construction. Long-time, large-population, and vanishing-noise limits require an order of limits and a convergence topology. If a minimum need not be attained, the objective is its infimum or supremum and the approximation requirement is stated separately.
