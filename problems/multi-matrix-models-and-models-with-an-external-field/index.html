Multi-matrix models and models with an external field

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
A multi-matrix model is a probability law on several interacting random matrices; an external-source model couples a random matrix to a fixed matrix. Matrix-valued Riemann--Hilbert problems encode the relevant multiple orthogonal polynomials. The matrix size of the Riemann--Hilbert problem need not equal the size of the sampled random matrices.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
There has been considerable progress (Kuijlaars,...) in understanding basic statistics such as the correlation functions for the 2-matrix random matrix model, and also matrix models with a source. The key element in these developments has been the successful extension by Kuijlaars et al of the Riemann-Hilbert/steepest descent method to $3\times3$ Riemann-Hilbert problems. So far only the simplest situations have been considered. In order to consider the generic situation, one must, in particular, extend the Riemann-Hilbert/steepest descent method to $n\times n$ Riemann-Hilbert problems. This is a challenging problem which would have important implications, not only for random matrix models, but also for problems in other areas, such as Padé-Hermite approximations and irrationality questions for distinguished real numbers, and multi-orthogonal polynomials.

Short English statement

Extend Riemann--Hilbert steepest descent from special low-dimensional cases to general multi-matrix and external-source models.

A matrix-size-independent small-jump step.
Work on the counterclockwise unit circle and let $J=I+W$, where $W$ is an $r\times r$ matrix with $\|W\|_{L^\infty(\mathrm{op})}=\delta<1$. Use the Hilbert--Schmidt matrix norm in $L^2$. The interior/exterior Cauchy boundary projections are Fourier projections and have norm one; in the convention $C_+-C_-=I$, the operator $C_-$ has norm one as well. Thus $T_Wf=C_-(fW)$ has norm at most $\delta$. The Neumann series gives \[\mu=(I-T_W)^{-1}I,\qquad \|\mu-I\|_2\le\frac{\|W\|_2}{1-\delta}. \] Define $M(z)=I+C(\mu W)(z)$. Its negative boundary value is $\mu$, and its jump is $\mu W$; consequently $M_+=M_-J$, with $M(\infty)=I$. These equalities follow directly from the boundary projection identity. No smallness factor growing with $r$ appears in the operator norm, although $\|W\|_2$ itself may depend on $r$.
Why this does not solve the generic problem.
The final error problem in steepest descent can therefore be treated in arbitrary fixed matrix size once a uniformly small jump is actually produced. The difficult step is constructing the global and local parametrices and selecting spectral sheets for generic multi-matrix data. An original jump containing large exponentials is not covered by the hypothesis $\delta<1$, and a growing matrix size requires separate bounds on $\|W\|_2$. The explicit calculation locates the missing work before the small-norm inversion, rather than attributing the entire obstruction to matrix size. It yields no generic correlation-function asymptotic or theorem about the other applications named in the source.
