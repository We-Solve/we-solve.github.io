Math-4772

Character-Degree Criteria for Nilpotent Blocks

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime and $G$ a finite group. Work over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p$. A $p$-block $B$ of $G$ is specified by a primitive central idempotent $e_B$ of $kG$. Write $\operatorname{Irr}(B)$ for the ordinary irreducible complex characters belonging to $B$ under the usual $p$-block partition of $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$.

Let $D$ be a defect group of $B$, and write $|G|_p=p^a$ and $|D|=p^d$, where $n_p$ denotes the largest power of $p$ dividing a positive integer $n$. For $\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)$, its height $h_B(\chi)$ is the nonnegative integer determined by \[ \chi(1)_p=p^{a-d+h_B(\chi)}. \] Define \[ \operatorname{Irr}_0(B)=\{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B):h_B(\chi)=0\}. \] Here $\chi(1)$ is the full ordinary character degree.

For a $p$-subgroup $Q\leq G$, the Brauer map \[ \operatorname{Br}_Q:(kG)^Q\longrightarrow kC_G(Q) \] retains precisely the coefficients of group elements in $C_G(Q)$; the superscript denotes invariants under conjugation by $Q$. A $B$-Brauer pair is a pair $(Q,b_Q)$, where $b_Q$ is a block of $kC_G(Q)$ with block idempotent $e_{b_Q}$ satisfying \[ \operatorname{Br}_Q(e_B)e_{b_Q}\ne 0. \] Set \[ N_G(Q,b_Q)=\{g\in N_G(Q):{}^g b_Q=b_Q\}. \] The block $B$ is called nilpotent if, for every $B$-Brauer pair $(Q,b_Q)$, the quotient $N_G(Q,b_Q)/C_G(Q)$ is a $p$-group, allowing the trivial group.

The supplied question asks whether, for every prime $p$, every finite group $G$, and every $p$-block $B$ of $G$, \[ B\text{ is nilpotent} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall\chi,\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_0(B),\qquad \chi(1)=\psi(1). \] No restriction to positive defect or to a particular family of finite groups is stated in the supplied formulation.

The record labels this question partially solved and reports results for broad families, including quasi-simple groups, while describing the unrestricted formulation as unresolved. These are supplied dated curation claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft. The precise hypotheses and scope of the cited partial results are not provided.

Short English statement

For every finite group and every prime $p$, is a $p$-block nilpotent exactly when all its height-zero ordinary irreducible characters have the same full degree? The supplied record describes partial results; its literature assessment requires independent verification.
