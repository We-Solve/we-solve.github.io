Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 7.3

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Does there exist a surface in $\mathbb R^3$, complete for its induced metric, with strictly negative Gaussian curvature, negative Euler characteristic, and bounded height between two parallel planes?

Under the standard embedded-surface reading, seek a connected boundaryless $C^2$ surface $\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3$ such that \[ K_\Sigma(p)<0\quad(p\in\Sigma),\qquad\chi(\Sigma)<0, \] and there are a unit vector $u\in\mathbb R^3$ and finite real numbers $a<b$ with \[ a\le\langle p,u\rangle\le b\qquad(p\in\Sigma). \] The slab has positive finite width $b-a$ but is unbounded in directions parallel to its planes. Completeness is intrinsic and does not add properness of the inclusion. Negativity is pointwise: no bound $K\le-c<0$, constant-curvature assumption, or finite-total-curvature condition is inserted.

The excerpt does not expressly fix embedded versus immersed surfaces, connectedness, boundary or smoothness conventions. For an immersed reading the same requirements would concern the complete induced metric on the source and slab containment of the image, a different existence question. It also does not specify finite topological type or the Euler-characteristic convention for infinite topology; a negative finite Euler characteristic must not be silently replaced by merely nontrivial or hyperbolic-type topology.

The supplied summary describes the slab question as open and mentions Efimov-type curvature restrictions and bounded-height issues without exact hypotheses or references. Such results are not treated as resolving the weaker pointwise-negative-curvature problem. The historical problem label and later "no direct solution located" report are retained without independent current verification.

Short English statement

Can a complete surface with strictly negative Gaussian curvature and negative Euler characteristic fit inside a finite-width slab in $\mathbb R^3$? The source does not prescribe a uniform negative curvature bound, and the embedding and topology conventions need review.
