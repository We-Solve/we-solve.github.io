A critical imbalance parameter for the B_s interpolation

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $\|x\|=\operatorname{dist}(x,{\mathbb{Z}})$ and $0<s<1$, Question 37, communicated by Yann Bugeaud, asks for the Hausdorff dimension of \[ B_s=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^2: \inf_{n\geq1}n\min(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)^s \max(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)^{2-s}>0\right\}. \] The infimum is over all positive integers, not just a subsequence or an asymptotic rate. The source identifies $B_0$ with the simultaneously badly approximable vectors, of dimension $2$, and $B_1$ with the possible Littlewood-exception set, of dimension $0$; the latter does not assert nonemptiness.

The aid gives a pointwise reduction, not the requested intermediate dimensions. For a fixed pair put $m_n=\min(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)$, $M_n=\max(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)$, and \[ A_n=nM_n^2,\qquad P_n=n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|,\qquad F_s(n)=A_n^{1-s}P_n^s. \] This is exactly the defining expression. For $s<t$ it decreases, so $B_t\subset B_s$. Its infima $C_s=\inf_nF_s(n)$ obey \[ C_{\theta s+(1-\theta)t}\geq C_s^\theta C_t^{1-\theta} \quad(0<\theta<1), \] with the usual zero-value convention. Thus the pointwise infimum is log-concave in the parameter, while $\dim_HB_s$ is nonincreasing; neither statement evaluates that dimension.

For a pair in $B_0$, both coordinates are irrational: a rational coordinate combined with one-dimensional approximation of the other would force $\inf_n nM_n^2=0$. Hence $m_n>0$. Define \[ r_n=\log(M_n/m_n)\geq0,\qquad a_n=\log A_n, \] so $\log F_s(n)=a_n-sr_n$ and $a_n$ is bounded below. The aid's critical imbalance parameter is \[ \tau=\lim_{K\to\infty}\inf\{a_n/r_n:r_n\geq K\}, \] using $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. For the tested parameters, $s<\tau$ implies membership in $B_s$, while $s>\tau$ implies nonmembership. At a finite critical value the exact extra condition is \[ (\alpha,\beta)\in B_\tau \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \inf_n(a_n-\tau r_n)>-\infty. \] A ratio threshold alone does not decide this additive endpoint. Bounded-imbalance terms are controlled by the $B_0$ lower bound; the tail detects failure along arbitrarily imbalanced terms.

If $(\alpha,\beta)\in B_s$ with $0<s<1$ and $F_s(n)\geq c_s>0$, then \[ P_n\geq c_s^{1/s}A_n^{-(1-s)/s}. \] Therefore any subsequence with $P_n\to0$ must have $A_n\to\infty$ and imbalance tending to infinity. This is a restriction on possible Littlewood-small subsequences, not exclusion of them. The supplied assessment leaves the intermediate dimension question open; no dimension distribution of $\tau$ is provided.

The extensive following text is separate spillover into quantum chaos, not part of Question 37. It introduces Laplace eigenfunctions, their probability densities $|\varphi_i|^2dV$, density-one quantum ergodicity for ergodic geodesic flow, and historical numerical scarring/bouncing-ball observations before a missing Rudnick--Sarnak conjecture. Its simultaneous zero-eigenvalue and Dirichlet-boundary conventions need separation, and finite-volume noncompact manifolds require continuous-spectrum/tightness qualifications. Its historical statement about the absence of rigorous scarring examples is not asserted as a current literature conclusion. None of these spectral matters changes the definition of $B_s$.

Short English statement

Compute the Hausdorff dimensions of the multiplicative approximation sets $B_s$ between badly approximable vectors and Littlewood exceptions. The supplied aid proves nesting, pointwise log-concavity and a critical imbalance test, including a separate additive endpoint condition. It does not compute the intermediate dimensions; the appended quantum-chaos material is unrelated context.
