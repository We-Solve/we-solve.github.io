One-Dimensional Fermi Gas with Attractive Interaction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
Let $\psi_s(x)$ be fermionic fields with spin index $s$, and let the one-particle density matrix be $\rho_{ss'}(x,y)=\langle\psi_s^\dagger(x)\psi_{s'}(y)\rangle$. For a translation-invariant state it depends on $x-y$. The source considers a weakly interacting one-dimensional gas, first in an interval with periodic boundary conditions, then in the thermodynamic limit and the zero-temperature limit. Mass, chemical potential, pair potential and coupling determine the model. The target concerns spinful attractive interactions, including how decay exponents depend on the coupling.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Determine the large-distance behavior of the one-particle reduced density matrix for a one-dimensional Fermi gas with spin and attractive interaction.
Scope and source qualification.
The catalogue target is the one-dimensional spinful gas. The source additionally discusses strip and two-row lattice variants as related first steps, without identifying them with this canonical record. Smooth finite-range potentials are mentioned as a context for known perturbative results, not imposed as a replacement for the original target.

Short English statement

Determine the long-distance one-particle correlations of a weakly attractive one-dimensional spinful Fermi gas.

The zero-coupling benchmark.
For a translation-invariant free gas with two spin states at zero temperature, suppose each spin occupies momenta $[-k_F,k_F]$, $k_F>0$. In the Fourier convention $\psi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int e^{ikx}a(k)\,dk$, the spin-diagonal one-particle kernel is \[\rho_{ss'}(0,r)=\delta_{ss'}\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-k_F}^{k_F}e^{ikr}\,dk =\delta_{ss'}\frac{\sin(k_Fr)}{\pi r},\] with the continuous value $k_F/\pi$ at $r=0$. This follows immediately from the occupied-mode projection; off-diagonal spin components vanish for the chosen unpolarized state. It fixes both the oscillation and the $r^{-1}$ envelope at the noninteracting endpoint.
Why finite-order perturbation is insufficient.
A candidate coupling-dependent algebraic envelope might have the form $r^{-1-\alpha(g)}$, with attractive coupling $g<0$ and $\alpha(g)=ag^2+O(g^3)$. Its formal expansion begins \[r^{-1-\alpha(g)}=r^{-1}\left(1-ag^2\log r+\cdots\right). \] For fixed nonzero $g$, the purported second-order correction ceases to be small when $\log r$ is comparable to $|g|^{-2}$. This is an exact warning about the model expression: expanding $e^{-\alpha(g)\log r}$ requires $|\alpha(g)\log r|\ll1$. Likewise an arbitrarily small positive inverse correlation length $m(g)$ in $r^{-1}e^{-m(g)r}$ changes the fixed-$g$ large-distance law, however closely it approximates the free expression on bounded intervals. These are competing ansatz tests, not a claim that either is the actual attractive-gas law.
Gap and limits.
The first missing step is a uniform-in-distance control of interacting correlations after the thermodynamic and zero-temperature limits, including possible spin-sector scales. No such bound is derived here. The spinful attractive target cannot be replaced by a free gas or a spinless power law, and the source's strip variants remain related questions rather than substitutes. The benchmark and nonuniform-expansion calculation establish no new attractive-coupling exponent.
