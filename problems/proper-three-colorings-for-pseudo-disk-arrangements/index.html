Proper Three-Colorings for Pseudo-Disk Arrangements

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an explicit conventional interpretation, work in the Euclidean plane ${\mathbb{R}}^2$. A closed Jordan region is the union of a simple closed curve and its bounded complementary component. A pseudo-disk family $\mathcal D$ is a family of such regions satisfying \[ \lvert\partial D\cap\partial D'\rvert\leq 2 \qquad\text{for all distinct }D,D'\in\mathcal D. \] This convention is provisional: the supplied record does not define pseudo-disks or specify boundary and degeneracy conventions. In this model, boundary points belong to the regions, and distinct boundaries cannot share an arc.

For a finite point set $P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$, a three-coloring is a function $c:P\to\{1,2,3\}$; using all three colors is not required. A set $P\cap D$ is nonmonochromatic if it contains points receiving different colors.

The conjecture in the supplied original statement is that, for every pseudo-disk family $\mathcal D$ and every finite point set $P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2$, there exists a three-coloring $c$, allowed to depend on both $P$ and $\mathcal D$, such that \[ \forall D\in\mathcal D,\qquad \lvert P\cap D\rvert\geq 4 \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists p,q\in P\cap D\quad c(p)\neq c(q). \] Thus the threshold counts points of $P$ in an individual member of the family. Members containing at most three points impose no condition.

The source does not explicitly restrict the family to be finite. For finite $P$, the finite-family and arbitrary-family versions of the displayed coloring assertion are equivalent, because only finitely many distinct subsets $P\cap D$ occur.

The supplied rewritten statement has a quantifier ambiguity: it could require one coloring of $P$ to work simultaneously for every pseudo-disk family. The displayed formulation follows the original statement, which fixes the arrangement before choosing the coloring. The precise geometric conventions still require source verification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is not an independently verified mathematical or literature assessment.

Short English statement

Given a pseudo-disk family in the plane and finitely many points, can the points be colored with at most three colors so that every region containing at least four of the points contains two different colors? The coloring may depend on the family. Boundary conventions and the rewritten statement's quantifier order require review.
