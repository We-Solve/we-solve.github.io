Math-1366

Multiplicity and an exact weighted-cut identity for three-term progressions

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

R. Graham asks: for a partition-regular homogeneous linear system $S$, determine the minimum number of nontrivial monochromatic solutions in an $r$-coloring of $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, as a function of $n,r$. The source defines partition regularity by monochromatic nontrivial solvability in every finite coloring of ${\mathbb{Z}}$. To state a counting function one must additionally fix the variables, what "nontrivial" excludes, and whether solutions are ordered or identified by symmetries. Given a specified admissible solution set $\mathcal U_S(n)$, the target is \[ M_S(n,r)=\min_{c:[n]\to\{1,\ldots,r\}} \#\{u\in\mathcal U_S(n):\text{all coordinates of }u\text{ have one color}\}. \] No universal asymptotic formula for all such systems is supplied.

For the source's Schur example $x+y=z$, use solutions with $x\leq y$, counted once up to exchanging the summands. Its quoted two-color minimum is $n^2(1+o(1))/22$, compared with random two-color expectation $n^2/16+O(n)$, attributed to Schoen and Robertson--Zeilberger. Ordered counting would change the leading constants by a factor of two; it is not mixed with this convention.

For the highlighted equation $x+y=2z$, count each nonconstant progression $(a,a+d,a+2d)$ once, with $d\geq1$ and $a+2d\leq n$. Let $\mathcal A_n$ be this set and $T_n=|\mathcal A_n|$. Put $V(n)=\min_c M_2(c)$, where $M_r(c)$ counts monochromatic members of $\mathcal A_n$. Independent uniform random two-coloring gives expectation $T_n/4=n^2/16+O(n)$. The aid reports that random two-colorings are not optimal and quotes the published bounds it located: \[ \left(\frac{1675}{32768}+o(1)\right)n^2 \leq V(n)\leq \left(\frac{117}{2192}+o(1)\right)n^2, \] with the exact asymptotic constant still open in its assessment.

Its exact finite-$n$ reduction defines, for $1\leq i<j\leq n$, \[ w_n(i,j)=\mathbf1_{\{2j-i\leq n\}}+ \mathbf1_{\{2i-j\geq1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{2\mid(j-i)\}},\qquad C_r(c)=\sum_{i<j}w_n(i,j)\mathbf1_{\{c(i)\ne c(j)\}}. \] Let $R_r(c)$ count progressions whose three colors are pairwise distinct. The supplied identity is \[ M_r(c)=T_n-\tfrac12 C_r(c)+\tfrac12R_r(c). \] For two colors $R_2=0$, so minimizing monochromatic progressions is exactly maximizing the weighted cut $C_2$. For $r\geq3$ the rainbow correction remains and cannot be discarded. This reduction does not determine the open exact constant or the answer for arbitrary systems $S$.

Short English statement

Minimize monochromatic solutions of partition-regular systems under a specified counting convention. For three-term progressions the supplied aid says random two-colorings are not optimal and gives an exact weighted Max-Cut formulation for two colors, with a rainbow correction for more colors.
