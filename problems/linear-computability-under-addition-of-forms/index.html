Linear Computability Under Addition of Forms

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question is: "Is the sum of two linearly computable forms always linearly computable?"

A form ordinarily means a homogeneous polynomial. However, the supplied record does not define linearly computable, specify the coefficient field or characteristic, or state the degrees and variable spaces of the two forms. Consequently, it does not determine a complete mathematical formulation.

The closure question can be recorded schematically as follows. Let $\mathcal H$ denote the intended class of forms for which addition is defined, and let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal H$ denote those forms satisfying the intended, unspecified definition of linear computability. Is it true that \[ \forall F,G\in\mathcal L,\qquad F+G\in\mathcal L? \] Here $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal L$ are placeholders for missing source definitions, not independently specified mathematical objects. This display encodes only the wording of the question.

A precise statement requires the definition of linear computability and its ambient hypotheses. In particular, the source must clarify whether the summands have the same degree, whether they use a common variable space or disjoint sets of variables, and whether the zero form is included when cancellation occurs. No choice among these interpretations is made here.

The supplied curation labels the problem partially solved. Its assessment says that the record itself gives a counterexample to the claim that all forms are linearly computable, that additivity and closure are established only in special cases, and that the stated closure question remains open. The supplied material includes neither that counterexample nor the definitions and hypotheses of those special cases. These are supplied literature claims, not mathematical facts independently verified in this draft.

Short English statement

The question asks whether adding two linearly computable forms always produces another linearly computable form. The supplied record leaves the key property and the permitted summands undefined, so only a schematic closure statement can be given. The supplied partially solved status and claim that the closure question remains open require independent review.
