Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.70

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ be a finite transitive permutation group on a nonempty finite set $\Omega$. The action is primitive if its only $G$-invariant partitions are the partition into singletons and the partition with sole part $\Omega$. Fix $\alpha\in\Omega$ and put $H=G_\alpha=\{g\in G:g\alpha=\alpha\}$. The rank of $G$ is \[ r(G)=|H\backslash\Omega|, \] where $H\backslash\Omega$ denotes the set of $H$-orbits on $\Omega$.

For each $H$-orbit $\Delta\subseteq\Omega$, let $H^\Delta\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Delta)$ be the permutation group induced by $H$ on $\Delta$. This transitive action is a transitive constituent of the point stabilizer. For $\beta\in\Delta$, define \[ r(H^\Delta)=|H_\beta\backslash\Delta|, \qquad H_\beta=\{h\in H:h\beta=\beta\}. \] This rank is independent of the choice of $\beta\in\Delta$. The subrank of $G$ is \[ s(G)=\max_{\Delta\in H\backslash\Omega}r(H^\Delta). \] Both $r(G)$ and $s(G)$ are independent of the chosen point $\alpha$. Singleton constituents are included and have rank $1$.

Conjecture of P. Cameron, as posed in Kourovka Notebook Problem 9.70. There exists a function \[ f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1} \] such that every finite primitive permutation group $G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$, with $m=s(G)$, satisfies \[ r(G)\leq f(m)\qquad\text{or}\qquad |G_\alpha|\leq m. \] Thus the bound on rank must depend only on the subrank, uniformly over all such groups and degrees. Equivalently, for each positive integer $m$, the ranks of finite primitive permutation groups with subrank $m$ and point stabilizer order greater than $m$ are uniformly bounded.

The supplied source does not specify whether one-point actions are admitted as primitive. The partition definition above admits them; their rank, subrank, and point stabilizer order are all $1$, so their inclusion does not change the conjecture.

Short English statement

Cameron's conjecture asks whether the subrank uniformly bounds the rank of a finite primitive permutation group whenever its point stabilizer has order greater than the subrank. Subrank measures the largest rank among the actions of a point stabilizer on its individual orbits. The supplied registry labels this problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
