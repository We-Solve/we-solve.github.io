Chromatic Numbers of Exchangeable Graphon Random Graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For terminology, let $(\Omega,\mathcal A,\mu)$ be a probability space. A graphon is a symmetric measurable function $W:\Omega^2\to[0,1]$. For an integer $n\geq1$, the associated random simple graph $G(n,W)$ has vertex set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$: sample independent points $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ with law $\mu$, and, conditional on these points, include each edge $\{i,j\}$, $i<j$, independently with probability $W(X_i,X_j)$. Its distribution is invariant under permutations of the vertex labels. These are standard definitions supplied for context; the excerpt does not specify its underlying probability space or additional hypotheses on $W$.

For a finite nonempty simple graph $G$, its chromatic number is \[ \chi(G)=\min\left\{k\geq1:\text{there exists }c:V(G)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\text{ with }c(u)\neq c(v)\text{ for every }\{u,v\}\in E(G)\right\}. \] A block graphon is constant almost everywhere on each product $A_i\times A_j$ of cells of a finite measurable partition of $\Omega$. For graphons represented on the same probability space, approximation in $L^\infty$ means \[ \|W_m-W\|_{L^\infty(\mu\otimes\mu)} =\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{(x,y)\in\Omega^2}|W_m(x,y)-W(x,y)|\longrightarrow0. \]
