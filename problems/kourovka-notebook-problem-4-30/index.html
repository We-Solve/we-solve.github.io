Kourovka Notebook Problem 4.30

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a topological group $H$, define its group of topological automorphisms by \[ \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{top}}(H) =\{\alpha:H\to H\mid \alpha\text{ is a group isomorphism and both }\alpha\text{ and }\alpha^{-1}\text{ are continuous}\}, \] with composition as the group operation. The word "full" means that all such automorphisms are included.

Let $\mathcal T$ denote the class of topological groups permitted as realizing groups. For each of the target classes \[ \mathcal C=\{\text{all groups}\},\qquad \mathcal C=\{\text{finite groups}\},\qquad \mathcal C=\{\text{abelian groups}\}, \] characterize the groups $G\in\mathcal C$ for which \[ \exists H\in\mathcal T\quad G\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{top}}(H). \] Here $\cong$ denotes abstract group isomorphism. Thus the requested description is a necessary and sufficient characterization of the groups arising as full automorphism groups of topological groups, with finite and abelian target groups singled out in the source.

The formulation remains qualified: the supplied record does not specify whether the realizing topological groups must be Hausdorff or satisfy another separation condition, so the intended class $\mathcal T$ is unresolved. It also does not specify a topology on the automorphism group or on the target group. The display therefore gives the abstract-group interpretation; a classification up to topological group isomorphism would require additional data. The parenthetical phrase "finite groups, abelian groups" is retained as specifying target subclasses, although its precise intended relationship to the unrestricted classification is not explained in the record.

Short English statement

Describe which groups occur as the full groups of continuous automorphisms with continuous inverses of topological groups, including the finite and abelian target cases. The permitted topological groups and whether realization is meant abstractly or topologically require clarification.
