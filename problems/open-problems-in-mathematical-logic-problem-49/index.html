Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\omega=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, and equip $X=2^{\omega\times\omega}$ with the product topology, where $2=\{0,1\}$ is discrete. An element of $X$ codes a binary relation on $\omega$ by its characteristic function. The source does not explicitly specify its graph conventions. Under the usual convention of simple undirected graphs, put \[ \mathcal G=\{G\in X:(\forall m\in\omega)\ G(m,m)=0\text{ and }(\forall m,n\in\omega)\ G(m,n)=G(n,m)\}. \] For $G\in\mathcal G$, its complement $G^c$ has the same vertex set and is defined by \[ G^c(m,n)= \begin{cases} 1-G(m,n),&m\ne n,\\ 0,&m=n. \end{cases} \] Graphs $G,H\in\mathcal G$ are isomorphic, written $G\cong H$, if there is a bijection $p:\omega\to\omega$ such that $G(m,n)=H(p(m),p(n))$ for every $m,n\in\omega$. Thus define \[ S=\{G\in\mathcal G:G\cong G^c\} =\left\{G\in\mathcal G:\exists p:\omega\to\omega\text{ bijective }\ \forall m\ne n,\ G(m,n)=1-G(p(m),p(n))\right\}. \] The question attributed by the supplied source to Matt Foreman in 2021 is: Is $S$ a Borel subset of $X$? Here Borel means belonging to the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the product-open subsets of $X$. Since $\mathcal G$ is closed in $X$, asking for Borelness in $\mathcal G$ gives the equivalent question under these conventions. The simple undirected graph convention and the corresponding off-diagonal complement are explicit interpretations requiring confirmation against the source; the supplied wording does not resolve them.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks whether the countable graphs that are isomorphic to their own complements form a Borel set in the space of graph codes. It attributes the question to Matt Foreman in 2021. The displayed formulation uses an explicitly stated simple undirected graph convention, which requires source confirmation.
