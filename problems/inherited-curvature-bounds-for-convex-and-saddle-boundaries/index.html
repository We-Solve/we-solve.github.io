Inherited Curvature Bounds for Convex and Saddle Boundaries

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks two intrinsic curvature-inheritance questions in Alexandrov geometry. A lower bound $\operatorname{CBB}(\kappa)$ is understood through the usual lower triangle comparisons with the constant-curvature model; an upper bound is understood through the corresponding upper comparisons, locally $\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)$ where appropriate. Positive-curvature comparison size restrictions and local versus global conventions must be retained from the source.

First, let $X$ have curvature bounded below by $\kappa$, and let $K\subset X$ be a convex subset in the intended sense. Does its boundary inherit the same lower bound when equipped with the intrinsic length metric \[ d_{\partial K}(x,y)=\inf\{\operatorname{Length}_X(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a curve in }\partial K\text{ from }x\text{ to }y\}? \] The target is the intrinsic boundary geometry, not the restriction of the ambient distance, which can be smaller. The source does not specify closedness, full dimension, nonempty interior, compactness, connectedness, regularity, completion, or whether boundary means ambient topological boundary or the intrinsic Alexandrov boundary of the convex region. If components have no joining boundary curve, this formula is not a finite metric between them; a componentwise or connected-boundary formulation needs to be fixed.

Second, let the ambient space have curvature bounded above by $\kappa$, and let $\Sigma$ be a saddle surface in the source's intended sense. Does the intrinsic metric on $\Sigma$ satisfy the same upper bound $\kappa$? The saddle condition, surface class, immersion or embedding requirements, singularities, and boundary conditions are not defined in the extract. A smooth differential-geometric saddle condition is not silently substituted for an Alexandrov one. A local upper-comparison assertion must also be distinguished from a global $\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)$ conclusion, which can require additional global hypotheses.

The supplied literature assessment checked 2026-08-21 reports the general lower-bound boundary problem open and mentions semiconvex, ruled, minimizing, and other special cases. It does not reproduce their hypotheses or give a separate exact status theorem for the saddle-surface question. Both questions remain part of the source record; no special-case surface theorem is promoted to either unrestricted claim.

Short English statement

Do convex boundaries retain an ambient Alexandrov lower curvature bound, and do saddle surfaces retain an ambient upper bound, using intrinsic length geometry? Boundary, saddle, completeness, and local-versus-global definitions are essential missing hypotheses.
