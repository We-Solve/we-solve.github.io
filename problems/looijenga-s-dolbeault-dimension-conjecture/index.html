Looijenga's Dolbeault Dimension Conjecture

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $g\geq 2$, let $\mathcal M_g$ denote the moduli object of smooth, connected, compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g$, with no marked points. The supplied record does not specify whether this means the coarse complex analytic moduli space or the complex analytic moduli orbifold.

For a complex analytic space or orbifold $X$, one possible precise convention for Dolbeault cohomological dimension is \[ \operatorname{cohdim}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X) :=\sup\left\{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}: H^q(X,\mathcal F)\neq0 \text{ for some coherent analytic }\mathcal O_X\text{-module }\mathcal F\right\}, \] where $H^q$ denotes analytic sheaf cohomology. This convention is provisional: the supplied record does not define the invariant or identify its coefficient category, and its agreement with the cited formulation requires source verification.

Conjecture attributed to Looijenga in the supplied record. For every integer $g\geq2$, \[ \operatorname{cohdim}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(\mathcal M_g)=g-2. \] Under the preceding coherent-analytic-sheaf convention and a fixed interpretation of $\mathcal M_g$, this means that, for every integer $g\geq2$, \[ H^q(\mathcal M_g,\mathcal F)=0 \quad\text{for every coherent analytic sheaf }\mathcal F \text{ and every integer }q>g-2, \] and there exists a coherent analytic sheaf $\mathcal F_g$ on $\mathcal M_g$ such that \[ H^{g-2}(\mathcal M_g,\mathcal F_g)\neq0. \] The boundary case $g=2$ is included. These expanded quantifiers describe the stated provisional convention; the supplied evidence does not establish that this is the exact intended definition. The conjecture is retained as a source-attributed question, not asserted as a theorem.

Short English statement

The supplied conjecture asks whether the Dolbeault cohomological dimension of the moduli space of compact genus-$g$ Riemann surfaces is exactly $g-2$ for every integer $g\geq2$. The precise moduli interpretation and definition of Dolbeault cohomological dimension require verification. The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved.
