Finite-dimensional embeddings of doubling Hilbert subsets

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Suppose a metric space $X$ is doubling, meaning that every ball can be covered by a fixed finite number of balls of half its radius. If $X$ admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into a Hilbert space, must it admit a bi-Lipschitz embedding into $\mathbb R^m$ for some finite $m$?

Finite-dimensional embeddings of doubling Hilbert subsets

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 098; 2026-09-25), the asserted result is that in each infinite-dimensional real Banach space one can choose a compact subset with a universal doubling constant for which every bi-Lipschitz map into a finite-dimensional normed target is impossible.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
A single subset $S$ of real $\ell_2$, with the distance inherited from its Hilbert norm, is claimed to satisfy \[ \operatorname{dbl}(S)\le76800,\qquad \forall k\ge1\ \forall D<\infty:\ S\text{ has no bi-Lipschitz embedding into }\mathbb R^k\text{ of distortion }D. \] Here doubling means that every metric ball is covered by at most that many balls of half its radius. The obstruction concerns one fixed doubling space, not a sequence with increasing doubling constants.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 098; 2026-09-25), sheet-like pieces are arranged so that coverings remain uniformly doubling. Near-extremal derivatives of a hypothetical finite-dimensional embedding have small quadratic variation; crossings between separated sheets then force more packed images than the target can accommodate.
