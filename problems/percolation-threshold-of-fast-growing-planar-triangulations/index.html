Math-2063

Percolation threshold of fast-growing planar triangulations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be an infinite connected locally finite planar triangulation, and write $B_G(v,r)$ for its graph-distance ball. One natural interpretation of uniform growth faster than quadratic is \[ \lim_{r\to\infty}\inf_{v\in V(G)}\frac{|B_G(v,r)|}{r^2}=+\infty. \] A lower bound $|B_G(v,r)|\geq c r^{2+\varepsilon}$ uniformly in $v,r$, for fixed $c,\varepsilon>0$, would be stronger; the source does not specify which is intended.

Under independent site percolation, each vertex is open with probability $p$, independently, and \[ p_c(G)=\inf\{p:\Pr_p(o\text{ belongs to an infinite open cluster})>0\} \] for a fixed vertex $o$. The source asks whether every triangulation satisfying its uniform superquadratic-growth hypothesis has $p_c(G)<1$, and asks the stronger equality $p_c(G)=1/2$.

The percolation type is not explicit in the record; the site convention is the natural one to check for the $1/2$ comparison, whereas bond percolation would be a different assertion. The definition of triangulation, degree bounds, and any one-endedness or subexponential-growth restrictions are also unstated. In particular, superquadratic growth as written does not exclude exponential growth. Both questions are retained with their exact inequality/equality signs, without changing the equality to an upper bound or adding geometric restrictions.

Short English statement

Does uniform superquadratic growth of a planar triangulation force a percolation threshold below one, or even exactly one-half? Specify site versus bond percolation and the precise growth/graph assumptions.
