Math-2249

Question I — s there a constant NS, depending only on S, such that the following holds?

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be the surface in the source and $\operatorname{Mod}_S$ its mapping class group. In the usual multitwist convention, choose finitely many pairwise disjoint, nonisotopic essential curves $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$ and signs $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$, and put $t=t_{\alpha_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots t_{\alpha_n}^{\epsilon_n}$. For $f\in\operatorname{Mod}_S$, assume the family \[ A=\{f^m(\alpha_i):1\leq i\leq n,\ m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\} \] fills $S$: every essential nonperipheral simple closed curve $\gamma$ has positive geometric intersection number with some member of $A$. The source says "nontrivial circle" and does not explicitly state these standard curve conventions.

Question I asks whether there is a positive integer $N_S$, depending only on $S$, such that for every such $f,t$ the exceptional set \[ E(f,t)=\{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}:t^j\circ f\text{ is not pseudo-Anosov}\} \] is contained in a block of $N_S$ consecutive integers. Precisely, there should exist $a=a(f,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}$ with \[ E(f,t)\subset\{a,a+1,\ldots,a+N_S-1\}. \] This includes finiteness and a bound on the span of exceptions. The source separately asks the weaker question whether $\#E(f,t)\leq N_S$, without requiring the exceptional indices to be consecutive or close together.

The source attributes the single-Dehn-twist case with $N_S=7$ to A. Fathi [Fath]. It says the original motivation, proving omitted Conjecture 4 in certain cases, is now obsolete (with a qualification about complete obsolescence). It also cites an application [I2] giving mapping-class-group rank one in an unspecified sense and [I7], Section 9.4. These contextual statements do not supply a proof for multitwists.

Short English statement

For a multitwist whose curves have filling orbit under $f$, are all non-pseudo-Anosov members of $t^j f$ confined to a uniformly bounded block of exponents? A weaker question bounds only the number of exceptions.
