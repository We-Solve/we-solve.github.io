Math-4217

Siegel's conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Define Bernoulli numbers by the formal power series $t/(e^t-1)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_mt^m/m!$. An odd prime $p$ is regular if $p$ divides none of the numerators, in lowest terms, of $B_2,B_4,\ldots,B_{p-3}$. For $p=3$ this list is empty. Include $2$ as regular by convention. Put $R(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ is a regular prime}\}$ and $\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ is prime}\}$. Relative natural density among primes means the limit of $R(x)/\pi(x)$ as $x\to\infty$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Are there infinitely many regular primes? Does their relative natural density exist and equal $e^{-1/2}$, that is, does $\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)/\pi(x)=e^{-1/2}$? [S2]

Short English statement

Are there infinitely many regular primes, and do they constitute the predicted fraction of all primes?
