Monochromatic Subspaces in Binary Vector Spaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let ${\mathbb{F}}_2$ be the field with two elements. For each positive integer $n$, a two-coloring is any map $c:{\mathbb{F}}_2^n\setminus\{0\}\to\{1,2\}$; both colors need not occur. A nonzero linear subspace $V\leq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n$ is monochromatic away from zero if there exists $i\in\{1,2\}$ such that $c(v)=i$ for every $v\in V\setminus\{0\}$. Define \[ M(c)=\max\left\{|V|-1: \{0\}\ne V\leq{\mathbb{F}}_2^n,\ V\setminus\{0\}\text{ is monochromatic under }c\right\}, \qquad f(n)=\min_{c:{\mathbb{F}}_2^n\setminus\{0\}\to\{1,2\}}M(c). \] These extrema exist: there are finitely many colorings and subspaces, and every one-dimensional subspace has a monochromatic singleton of nonzero vectors. A subspace of dimension $d$ contributes $2^d-1$ nonzero vectors. Thus $f(n)$ counts nonzero vectors, rather than dimension. This definition also agrees with the supplied largest guaranteed exact cardinality: each $f(n)$ has the form $2^d-1$, and any larger monochromatic subspace contains a subspace of dimension $d$.

The question is whether \[ \exists C>0\ \exists n_0\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1}\ \forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq1},\qquad n\geq n_0\Longrightarrow f(n)\leq Cn. \] Equivalently, does there exist such a constant $C$ so that, for every sufficiently large positive integer $n$, some coloring of ${\mathbb{F}}_2^n\setminus\{0\}$ has at most $Cn$ nonzero vectors in every monochromatic linear subspace? The zero vector is excluded from coloring and from the cardinality being bounded. The supplied record does not specify an $n=0$ convention; none is needed for this asymptotic question.

Short English statement

For every positive integer $n$, let $f(n)$ be the largest number of nonzero vectors guaranteed in a monochromatic linear subspace under every two-coloring of ${\mathbb{F}}_2^n\setminus\{0\}$. Is $f(n)$ bounded above by a constant times $n$ for all sufficiently large $n$? The count concerns vectors, not dimension.
