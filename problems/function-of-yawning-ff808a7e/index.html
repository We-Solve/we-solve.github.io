Statement

Function of yawning

Domain. Yawning; mechanism and function discrimination.   Retained status/source context. Open comparison of mechanisms and context-dependent functions. Correlation with a proposed physiological variable does not by itself establish adaptive function.

Definitions and assumptions.
For a declared species and setting let $N_t$ count independently coded yawns, with counting-process intensity $\lambda_{m,\theta}(X_{t-})$ per minute under candidate mechanism $m$. Covariates $X_t$ include ambient and measured body temperature, sleep pressure, arousal and social exposure; time-varying confounding and event-coding error are specified. A physiological outcome $Y$ has a fixed metric and horizon. Restrict intensity and outcome models to finite smooth families. Spontaneous and contagious yawns have distinct labels; a displayed yawn is not assumed to reveal its selected evolutionary function.
Problem statement.
Determine which model jointly predicts event occurrence and the relevant physiological contrast: \[\lambda_{m,\theta}(x),\qquad \Delta_m(a,a')=\mathbb{E}[Y^{a}-Y^{a'}],\] where $a,a'$ are explicitly defined, ethically observed or controlled conditions. A causal interpretation requires consistency and exchangeability or random assignment. Compare predictions on held-out contexts and state which hypothesized pathways remain observationally equivalent.
Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.
The counting intensity is predictable from the history immediately before the event, for example $\lambda(X_{t-})$; post-yawn temperature or arousal cannot be inserted as pretreatment predictors. Include self-history where events are dependent. A contrast between interaction conditions can affect physiology through paths other than yawning, so identify which intervention and exclusion assumptions isolate the proposed mechanism. Present physiological utility separately from an evolutionary selected-function claim.
Alternative formulations.
(i) A thermoregulation model links yawning and temperature-related outcomes. (ii) An arousal/state-transition model predicts yawns around vigilance changes. (iii) A social-contagion model predicts response to conspecific signals, without assuming shared physiological benefit. More than one mechanism or function may operate; the alternatives need not be mutually exclusive.
Obstructions and partial progress.
Thermal-window studies offer concrete predictions and data, but seasonal or ambient associations are weaker than complete causal identification of cooling. Temperature, sleep and social context can covary, while yawn self-report can differ from actual event coding. Frequent occurrence under a condition does not demonstrate a benefit or explain evolutionary retention. The remaining task is joint mechanism/outcome prediction with explicit causal assumptions and enough context variation to distinguish competing explanations or recognize coexistence.

Objects, quantifiers and non-vacuity.
An instance declares measurable state, input, output and parameter spaces; a fixed candidate family; admissible initial laws, interventions and environments; a finite horizon or specified asymptotic regime; observation and sampling laws; and performance metrics, positive normalization scales and positive tolerances. These are chosen before fitting or testing, not adjusted after seeing failures. Shared parameters must genuinely be shared across the declared validation domain. Finite dimension alone does not restrict arbitrary real-number encodings: specify a finite library, bounded description length, or regularity/complexity bounds. Exclude constant or no-signal solutions through a fixed informative-output or baseline-improvement requirement where appropriate. A universally quantified result cannot be established by choosing one trivial favorable instance.
Probability laws and data.
Every expectation or probability refers to the specified stochastic law, including initial uncertainty, process noise, observation noise and sampling as appropriate. A deterministic equation is not a probability model without a declared random input or initial law. "Observed" targets in population-level formulas mean population observables $g_j^*$, not noisy estimates asserted to be exact. Let $\widetilde g_j$ be independent validation estimates and suppose \[ \mathbb{P}\{\lvert\widetilde g_j-g_j^*\rvert\le r_j\text{ for every }j\}\ge1-\alpha. \] Then an uncertainty-aware sufficient certification rule for positive target tolerances $\varepsilon_j$ is \[ \max_j\frac{|\widehat g_j-\widetilde g_j|+r_j}{\varepsilon_j}\le1. \] On that simultaneous confidence event it implies every population error is at most its tolerance, by the triangle inequality. Vector/norm versions use corresponding valid simultaneous bounds. Training uncertainty must also be included when the claim averages over repeated training data. Evaluation on finitely many conditions does not establish a continuous-domain supremum without covering and regularity bounds.
Dynamics and well-posedness.
For ODEs and SDEs give coefficient domains, initial laws and existence/uniqueness conditions in the intended solution sense, or keep the entire admitted solution set. For stochastic jump models specify nonnegative off-diagonal rates, diagonal normalization, rate orientation and non-explosion. For PDEs specify spatial domains, boundary/initial conditions, conservation or source terms, and a weak or classical solution class. Moving-domain concentration equations include dilution/transport terms. A stationary law is used only for a time-homogeneous augmented model under existence assumptions; a stationary approximation to a driven system must be justified. Absorbing extinction states are retained. Survival-conditioned, quasi-stationary and unconditional laws are distinct and must not be substituted for one another.
Identification and causal meaning.
Two parameter values are observationally equivalent when they induce the same full distribution under all allowed observation/intervention designs, not merely the same likelihood value for one sample. A target is identified only if constant on the equivalence class. Gauge symmetries, label switching and reference conventions are quotiented or fixed. Finite-data compatible sets use a declared confidence, likelihood or discrepancy rule; an empty set signals model rejection. Bayesian credible sets are not automatically confidence sets. A prior or regularizer does not create data identification. Bounds intended to resolve a question must be informative at a predeclared width or decision threshold.

A causal contrast uses well-defined intervention versions and a stated causal model. Random assignment, or explicit consistency, overlap and exchangeability assumptions, is required for causal interpretation from observations. Interference and time-varying confounding are represented when present. Fixing a mediator by intervention differs from conditioning on its observed value. Marginal potential-outcome laws do not determine individual cross-world effects or an arbitrary joint coupling. Ethically admissible designs, rather than unconstrained interventions, define the experimental menu.

Attempted answer

Preliminary step: the following worked reduction has not been shown to instantiate all assumptions of the original target. Its applicability is an outstanding proof obligation.
Proposition: As an operational subproblem, fix a candidate model before collecting $n$ independent biological-unit validation outcomes, and let its prespecified loss lie in $[0,1]$. With probability at least $1-\delta$, its population mean loss differs from the observed mean by at most $\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2n)}$.
Proof: Apply Lemma Lemma (Independent bounded sums) to the independent bounded losses. This bound requires a fixed candidate and evaluation protocol. For a finite prespecified family of $M$ candidates, a union bound replaces $\log(2/\delta)$ by $\log(2M/\delta)$ to provide simultaneous coverage. ∎

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
