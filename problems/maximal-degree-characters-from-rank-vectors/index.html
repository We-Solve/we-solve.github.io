Math-1679

Maximal-degree characters from rank vectors

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Problem 1.9 asks to describe maximal-degree irreducible characters of pattern groups, as supercharacters, using dimension vectors and polynomial equations. Its remark says to check a paper by Dixmier, without identifying it. The source does not define the intended dimension vectors or whether a particular supercharacter theory is required. The supplied aid uses the standard algebra-group theory and proves a claimed special case for pattern posets with no four-element chain.

Let $P$ be a finite poset on labelled indices, let $J_P$ be the ${\mathbb{F}}_q$-algebra spanned by matrix units $E_{ik}$ for $i<_Pk$, and set $G_P=1+J_P$. Matrix multiplication is $E_{ij}E_{jk}=E_{ik}$ when indices match. Fix a nontrivial additive character $\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_q\to{\mathbb{C}}^*$. For $\lambda\in J_P^*$, the standard Diaconis--Isaacs supercharacter is \[ \chi_\lambda(g)=\frac{|G_P\lambda|}{|G_P\lambda G_P|} \sum_{\nu\in G_P\lambda G_P}\psi(\nu(g-1)). \] Different $\lambda$'s in the same two-sided orbit label the same supercharacter; rank loci below are parameter loci, not a claim of unique character labels.

Suppose $P$ has height at most three, meaning no chain of four distinct elements, so $J_P^3=0$. For each index $j$, let $a_j$ and $b_j$ be the numbers of predecessors and successors of $j$, and form the $a_j\times b_j$ matrix \[ A_j(\lambda)=\left(\lambda(E_{ik})\right)_{i<_Pj<_Pk}. \] The aid reports that every standard supercharacter is irreducible, all ordinary irreducible characters are obtained, and \[ \chi_\lambda(1)=q^{\sum_j\operatorname{rank}A_j(\lambda)}. \] Define the exact finite-field optimization \[ R_q(P)=\max_{\lambda\in J_P^*} \sum_j\operatorname{rank}A_j(\lambda),\qquad R(P)=\sum_j\min(a_j,b_j). \] The maximal ordinary character degree in this special case is $q^{R_q(P)}$. Its parameter locus is the union of the rank-vector strata with total rank $R_q(P)$. Exact rank $r_j$ means all $(r_j+1)$-minors vanish and at least one $r_j$-minor is nonzero, with the usual empty-minor convention for rank zero. Thus these are determinantal equations and inequations. A nonzero minor condition can be encoded by an auxiliary variable $u$ and equation $uD-1=0$, on the corresponding minor chart. Shared entries between different $A_j$'s must be retained; their feasible ranks are not independent choices.

The aid reports the uniform sufficient field-size criterion \[ q>R(P)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_q(P)=R(P), \] so the maximum degree is $q^{R(P)}$ in that range. It does not claim this field-size threshold is necessary or sharp, or that the same formula always holds over smaller fields. The exact optimization remains valid for every prime power.

For arbitrary finite-dimensional nilpotent algebra $J$, the aid gives a separate polynomial-rank test for irreducibility of a standard supercharacter. In a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_s$, let \[ B_\lambda=(\lambda(e_ue_v))_{u,v=1}^s. \] The left and right orbit-direction spaces have dimensions $\operatorname{rank}B_\lambda$; their intersection vanishes exactly when \[ \operatorname{rank}[B_\lambda\ B_\lambda^T] =2\operatorname{rank}B_\lambda. \] The aid identifies this with irreducibility of $\chi_\lambda$; its degree is $q^{\operatorname{rank}B_\lambda}$. This is a test for irreducible standard supercharacters. Outside the height-three setting it does not imply all irreducibles are standard supercharacters or solve the maximal ordinary-degree problem. Nor does it specify the broader dimension-vector interpretation requested by the original source.

Short English statement

The aid answers a height-at-most-three special case using matrices of functional values along length-two paths. Their rank sum gives every irreducible degree, and maximal-degree parameters are determinantal strata. A field-size condition realizes all individual rank maxima simultaneously. For general algebra groups only a standard-supercharacter irreducibility test is supplied, not the full maximal-degree classification.
