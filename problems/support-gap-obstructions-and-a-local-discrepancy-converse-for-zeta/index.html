Math-1316

Support-gap obstructions and a local-discrepancy converse for zeta

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p_n$ be the $n$-th prime. The corrupted source hypothesis is recovered in the supplied research summary as \[ (\mathrm H)\qquad p_{n+1}-p_n\ll_\varepsilon p_n^\varepsilon \quad\text{for every fixed }\varepsilon>0. \] Precisely, for each $\varepsilon>0$ there are $C_\varepsilon<\infty$ and $n_\varepsilon$ such that the displayed bound holds for every $n\geq n_\varepsilon$. No constant uniform in $\varepsilon$, bounded-gap conclusion, or logarithmic gap bound is implicit.

Y. Motohashi asks what analytic information, if any, about $\zeta(s)$ in $0<\Re s<1$ can be deduced from $(\mathrm H)$, reversing the usual passage from zeta information to prime-gap upper bounds. The source does not name a particular desired zero-free region or growth estimate, so it is a broad converse question, not an assertion that $(\mathrm H)$ implies the Riemann Hypothesis.

The attached aid stresses that small gaps give one-sided nonemptiness of intervals, not signed prime-counting discrepancy. Its first observation is that the contribution from a fixed zero $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta<1$, to an explicit-formula interval difference is of size $O_\rho(yx^{\beta-1})=o(y)$ for $y$ on a sublinear power scale. This addresses a fixed zero term, not a uniform sum over all zeros or growing heights.

Its second claim is a model obstruction. For every prescribed $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ with $0<\beta<1$ and $\gamma\ne0$, it reports a bounded sequence $a_m>0$, $m\geq2$, such that \[ D_\rho(s)=\sum_{m\geq2}a_m m^{-s} \] has a meromorphic continuation with residues $+1$ at $1$ and $-1$ at $\rho,\overline\rho$. It reports an associated meromorphic $Z$ satisfying $-Z'/Z=D_\rho$, with a pole at $1$ and simple zeros at the prescribed conjugate pair. This model has dense positive coefficient support but is not the Riemann zeta function and is not supplied with its prime Euler product or other arithmetic structure. Therefore it limits arguments using only support and positivity; it does not disprove a logical implication from actual prime gaps to actual zeta information.

The aid contrasts $(\mathrm H)$ with a genuinely stronger hypothesis involving $\psi(x)=\sum_{m\leq x}\Lambda(m)$, where $\Lambda(p^j)=\log p$ and is zero otherwise. In a nontrivial fixed parameter range $0<\delta<\eta<1$, assume uniformly for large real $x$ that \[ \psi(x+x^\eta)-\psi(x)=x^\eta+O(x^{\eta-\delta}). \] It reports that telescoping gives $\psi(x)=x+O(x^{1-\delta})$, and consequently $\zeta(s)$ has no zeros in $\Re s>1-\delta$. This conditional discrepancy implication must not be attributed to the weaker gap hypothesis. The exact broad parameter scope and full proofs of the aid's three claims are not reproduced. They are supplied unreviewed partial obstructions and a stronger-hypothesis converse, not a solution of Motohashi's original question.

Short English statement

Ask what zeta information follows if every prime gap is bounded by $p_n^\varepsilon$ for each fixed positive $\varepsilon$. The supplied aid separates interval nonemptiness from signed prime-counting estimates, gives limited model obstructions, and derives a zero-free region only from an explicitly stronger discrepancy assumption.
