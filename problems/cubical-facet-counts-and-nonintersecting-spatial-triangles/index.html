Cubical Facet Counts and Nonintersecting Spatial Triangles

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A convex $d$-polytope is a convex polytope of affine dimension $d$. For $d\geq 2$, call it cubical if each facet is combinatorially equivalent to the $(d-1)$-dimensional cube. Here a facet is a face of dimension $d-1$, and combinatorial equivalence means isomorphism of face lattices. Write $f_i(P)$ for the number of $i$-dimensional faces of $P$.

For each integer $d\geq 2$, define the set of admissible vertex counts by \[ A_d=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}: \text{there exists a cubical convex }d\text{-polytope }P\text{ with }f_0(P)=n\}. \] For $n\in A_d$, set \[ C(d,n)=\max\{f_{d-1}(P): P\text{ is a cubical convex }d\text{-polytope},\ f_0(P)=n\}. \] The first problem is to determine the asymptotic growth of $C(d,n)$. In particular, for every fixed integer $d\geq 2$, is \[ C(d,n)=o(n^2)\qquad(n\to\infty,\ n\in A_d)? \] Explicitly, the latter asks whether for every such $d$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is an integer $N$ such that \[ n\in A_d,\ n\geq N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(d,n)\leq\varepsilon n^2. \] The source does not specify a dimension range or define $C(d,n)$ when no such polytope exists. The formulation above uses the usual convex-polytope interpretation, treats $d\geq2$, and takes asymptotics through admissible vertex counts. These conventions require review against the source report; no value is assigned for inadmissible $n$. No joint asymptotic regime for varying $d$ and $n$ is specified in the supplied record.

For the second problem, interpret a triangle as the closed convex hull of three noncollinear points in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$. If $T$ is such a triangle, let $V(T)$ denote its three vertices. For an integer $n\geq1$, define \[ \begin{split} t(n)=\max\bigl\{|\mathcal T|:\;&S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3,\ |S|=n,\quad \mathcal T\subseteq\{\operatorname{conv}\{a,b,c\}:a,b,c\in S\text{ are noncollinear}\},\\ &\text{for all distinct }T,U\in\mathcal T,\quad T\cap U=\varnothing\ \text{or}\ T\cap U=\{v\}\text{ for some }v\in V(T)\cap V(U)\bigr\}. \end{split} \] Thus the intersection condition applies to the entire filled triangles. A shared edge, an intersection along triangle interiors, or contact at a point that is not a vertex of both triangles is forbidden. Points of $S$ need not all be used, and no general-position assumption on $S$ is imposed.

Determine the asymptotic growth of $t(n)$ as $n\to\infty$. The supplied background additionally identifies the question \[ t(n)=o(n^2)? \] although this particular subquadratic question is not explicitly posed in the supplied original or clean statement. The closed, nondegenerate interpretation of triangles is conventional but is not expressly defined in the record and requires source review.

Short English statement

Determine how the largest possible number of facets of a cubical convex polytope grows with its vertex count, especially whether it is subquadratic in every fixed dimension. Separately, determine the largest number of filled triangles on an $n$-point set in ${\mathbb{R}}^3$ when distinct triangles may intersect only at a shared vertex. Dimension conventions, admissible vertex counts, and the interpretation of triangles require review.
