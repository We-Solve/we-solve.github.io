Math-837

Log Canonical Thresholds and Modular $F$-Thresholds

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The question compares a threshold at the origin in characteristic zero with the threshold of reductions of the same polynomial. An explicit standard arithmetic setting is a nonzero polynomial $f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[1/N][x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $f(0)=0$, viewed over ${\mathbb{C}}$. For primes $p\nmid N$ where its reduction is nonzero, let $f_p\in{\mathbb{F}}_p[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, let $R_p={\mathbb{F}}_p[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_{\mathfrak m}$, and let $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)R_p$.

Write $\operatorname{lc}_0(f)$ for the log canonical threshold at $0$. In the complex analytic convention it is \[ \operatorname{lc}_0(f)=\sup\{c\geq0:|f|^{-2c}\text{ is integrable on some neighborhood of }0\}. \] For $q=p^e$, the Frobenius power is $\mathfrak m^{[q]}=(x_1^q,\ldots,x_n^q)R_p$. Set \[ \nu_{f_p}^{\mathfrak m}(q)=\max\{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}: f_p^r\notin\mathfrak m^{[q]}\},\qquad c^{\mathfrak m}(f_p)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{f_p}^{\mathfrak m}(p^e)}{p^e}. \] This is the usual maximal-ideal $F$-threshold in this regular local setting.

The requested assertion is that, for each fixed polynomial in the intended arithmetic class, \[ \#\{p\text{ of good reduction}:\operatorname{lc}_0(f)=c^{\mathfrak m}(f_p)\}=\infty. \] The polynomial is fixed before the infinitely many primes are chosen. Convergence of the characteristic-$p$ thresholds to the characteristic-zero threshold does not itself give equality for infinitely many primes.

The supplied extract does not specify the coefficient ring, an integral model, nonzero and origin-vanishing assumptions, or the ideal $\mathfrak m$. The displayed definitions make the customary origin/maximal-ideal interpretation explicit; they do not assert that these missing hypotheses were verified in the supplied record. Polynomials over more general characteristic-zero fields require an identified model and a precise choice of reduction places.

Short English statement

For each fixed polynomial, ask whether its log canonical threshold at the origin agrees exactly with the maximal-ideal $F$-threshold of its reduction for infinitely many primes. Exact equality, rather than convergence, is the target; the arithmetic model and local conventions are missing from the extract.
