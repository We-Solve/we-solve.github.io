Newton–Okounkov Geometry for Positive Curve Classes

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual projective setting, let $Z_1(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}$ denote the real vector space of finite real linear combinations of integral curves on $X$. Two such cycles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are numerically equivalent if \[ D\cdot\alpha=D\cdot\beta\qquad\text{for every Cartier divisor }D\text{ on }X. \] The space of numerical curve classes is \[ N_1(X)=Z_1(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}/\!\equiv_{\mathrm{num}}. \] A convex body means a nonempty compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional real vector space. These definitions explain the customary notation; the source does not specify the class of varieties $X$, a ground field, a dimension, or regularity assumptions.

Source question. Can the positivity theory for curve classes in $N_1(X)$ be expressed through Newton--Okounkov-type convex bodies?
