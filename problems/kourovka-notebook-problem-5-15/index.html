Kourovka Notebook Problem 5.15

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S$ be a finite set, let $F(S)$ be the free group on $S$, and let $R\subseteq F(S)$ be finite. The presentation $\langle S\mid R\rangle$ defines the finitely presented group $G=F(S)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$, where $\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$ is the normal closure of $R$. The group $G$ is residually finite if, for every $g\in G\setminus\{1_G\}$, there exist a finite group $H$ and a homomorphism $\phi:G\to H$ such that $\phi(g)\ne 1_H$.

Fix a standard effective encoding of finite words over $S\cup S^{-1}$ by natural numbers, with primitive recursive syntactic operations and validity test. Define the word-problem characteristic function by \[ \chi_{G,S}(n)= \begin{cases} 1,&\text{if }n\text{ encodes a word representing }1_G,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise, including invalid codes.} \end{cases} \] A recursive solution of the word problem means that $\chi_{G,S}$ is a total recursive function. A primitive recursive solution means that $\chi_{G,S}$ is primitive recursive.

Does there exist a finite presentation $\langle S\mid R\rangle$ whose group $G$ is residually finite and for which $\chi_{G,S}$ is recursive but not primitive recursive? Equivalently, the requested decision function must be computable by an algorithm that halts on every input, while \[ \forall f:{\mathbb{N}}\to\{0,1\}\;\left(f\text{ primitive recursive}\Longrightarrow \exists n\in{\mathbb{N}}\; f(n)\ne\chi_{G,S}(n)\right). \] The supplied statement does not specify a word encoding or formally define a primitive recursive solution. The formulation above uses the decision-function interpretation with standard primitive recursive coding; this interpretive qualification requires review against the original problem.

Short English statement

Does some finitely presented residually finite group have a word problem that an algorithm can decide on every input, but that no primitive recursive decision function can decide? The supplied record labels this problem open; that label is not an independently verified assessment.
