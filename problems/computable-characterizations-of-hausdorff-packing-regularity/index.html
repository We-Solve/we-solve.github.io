Computable Characterizations of Hausdorff Packing Regularity

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $2^\omega=\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}$ be Cantor space, where ${\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, and let $A\subseteq 2^\omega$. The usual metric interpretation is the ultrametric $d(x,x)=0$ and, for $x\ne y$, \[ d(x,y)=2^{-\min\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:x(n)\ne y(n)\}}. \] This metric convention is made explicit for interpretation; the supplied record does not specify a metric, and this convention requires source confirmation. Write $\dim_H(A)$ and $\dim_P(A)$ for the classical Hausdorff and packing dimensions of $A$, respectively. In the terminology of this problem, $A$ is regular precisely when \[ \dim_H(A)=\dim_P(A). \] A subset of Cantor space is analytic if it is empty or is the image of Baire space ${\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}$ under a continuous map into $2^\omega$.

The supplied record poses the following research questions.

  1. For arbitrary $A\subseteq 2^\omega$, can computability theory provide a partial characterization of the equality $\dim_H(A)=\dim_P(A)$? The record does not specify the computability property sought, allowable oracles, or the meaning of a partial characterization.
  2. For analytic $A\subseteq 2^\omega$ satisfying $\dim_H(A)=\dim_P(A)$, can point-to-set principles provide an alternative proof of the referenced closed-subset result?
  3. Can the analyticity hypothesis in that closed-subset result be removed?
The closed-subset result is not stated in the supplied record. In particular, the required properties of the closed subset, the dimension relation it must satisfy, its quantifiers, and any additional hypotheses are missing. Thus the second and third questions cannot be converted into a complete quantified mathematical assertion from the supplied evidence. No particular closed-subset conclusion is asserted here.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether computability theory can help characterize subsets of Cantor space whose Hausdorff and packing dimensions agree, and whether point-to-set principles can reprove and extend a closed-subset theorem for regular analytic sets. The theorem itself and the intended computability criterion are unspecified, so those parts remain unresolved formulation issues.
