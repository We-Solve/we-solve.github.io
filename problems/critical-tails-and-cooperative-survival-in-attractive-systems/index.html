Critical Tails and Cooperative Survival in Attractive Systems

Definitions and mathematical statement

Scope and unresolved specifications. The supplied record combines two questions: exponential infection-time tails for bootstrap percolation above criticality, and the behaviour of attractive probabilistic cellular automata (PCA) in a regime described as cooperative survival with individual death. It does not give a complete class of bootstrap rules, an initial distribution, a definition of the relevant critical parameter, or mathematical definitions of cooperative survival and individual death. Consequently, the following is a qualified statement of the requested targets, not a fully specified universal conjecture.

Bootstrap percolation target. Once a bootstrap percolation rule, an initial law $\mathbb P_p$, and its intended critical parameter $p_c$ have been specified, let $A_t$ be the infected set at discrete time $t$, and define the infection time of a specified site $x$ by \[ \tau_x=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0}:x\in A_t\},\qquad \inf\varnothing=\infty. \] The requested exponential-tail conclusion has the form \[ \text{for every admissible }p>p_c,\quad \exists C,c>0\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\qquad \mathbb P_p(\tau_x>t)\leq C e^{-ct}. \] Here the constants may depend on the specified model, parameter, and site; no uniformity is asserted by the supplied record. The question asks whether such decay holds for bootstrap percolation "in full generality," but the intended class of models is unspecified. The record also does not say whether the tail is unconditional or conditioned on eventual infection. These alternatives must be resolved before the display can be taken as the precise intended conjecture.

Attractive PCA framework. The excerpt uses a lattice dimension $d$, a finite neighbourhood radius $R$, and a configuration space $\Omega_R$ whose definition is missing. A natural, explicitly provisional reconstruction is \[ B_R=[-R,R]^d\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^d,\qquad \Omega_R=\{0,1\}^{B_R}, \] with coordinatewise order: $\xi\leq\zeta$ means $\xi(y)\leq\zeta(y)$ for every $y\in B_R$. A subset $U\subseteq\Omega_R$ is an up-set if $\xi\in U$ and $\xi\leq\zeta$ imply $\zeta\in U$. The identification of $\Omega_R$, and the admissible choices of $d$ and $R$, require confirmation from the source.

Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on these up-sets, and let $(U_{x,t})$ be independent, identically distributed random up-sets with law $\nu$, indexed by $x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^d$ and positive integer times $t$. Starting from $\eta(0)\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^d}$, the displayed update rule is reconstructed as \[ \eta_x(t)= \begin{cases} 1,& (\eta_{x+y}(t-1))_{y\in B_R}\in U_{x,t},\\ 0,& \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \] Thus $U_{x,t}=\varnothing$ forces death, whereas $U_{x,t}=\Omega_R$ forces birth. The up-set property makes the update monotone under the coordinatewise order when the same random up-sets are used.

For context, the excerpt reports a result labelled Theorem 2 for the PCA with update-set law \[ \nu_p=p\nu+(1-p)\delta_{\varnothing},\qquad p\in[0,1], \] where $\delta_{\varnothing}$ is the point mass at the empty up-set. With $\mathbb P_p^1$ denoting the law written in the excerpt, its reported assertion is that there exists $p_c\in[0,1]$ such that \[ \begin{aligned} p>p_c&\ \Longrightarrow\ \lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb P_p^1(\eta_0(t)=1)>0,\\ p<p_c&\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists C,c>0\ \forall t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq0},\quad \mathbb P_p^1(\eta_0(t)=1)\leq Ce^{-ct}. \end{aligned} \] The parameter comparisons here are within $[0,1]$. The superscript $1$ is not defined in the supplied excerpt; an all-one initial configuration is a possible intended convention, requiring confirmation. No assertion at $p=p_c$ is supplied. This is a contextual result reported by the record, not an independently verified theorem in this draft. The excerpt points to an unidentified reference [6] for greater generality and an unidentified reference [3] for the proof method.

The second requested task is to describe the behaviour of attractive PCA in the intermediate regime of cooperative survival but individual death. The supplied record specifies neither the initial configurations and survival events defining these terms nor a particular conclusion to establish. It therefore supports an open research question with this description, but not a unique quantified mathematical statement. Its relationship to the critical parameter in the reported Theorem 2 is also unspecified.

Short English statement

the catalogue statement asks whether infection times in supercritical bootstrap percolation have exponentially decaying tails in full generality, and how attractive PCA behave when cooperation permits survival although individuals die. The supplied record leaves essential model choices and survival definitions unspecified, so both targets require clarification before a complete precise conjecture can be stated. Its open-status assessment is supplied dated curation, not independently verified here.
