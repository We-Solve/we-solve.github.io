Math-1696

Exchangeability in the mean-zero exclusion process

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix $d\ge1$ and a probability kernel $p:\mathbb Z^d\to[0,1]$ with finite first absolute moment and zero mean: \[ \sum_z p(z)=1,\qquad \sum_z\|z\|p(z)<\infty, \qquad \sum_z z p(z)=0. \] Assume that the random walk with jumps distributed as $p$ is irreducible on $\mathbb Z^d$. In the exclusion process, each ordered pair $(x,x+z)$ has a rate-$p(z)$ clock. A ring moves a particle from $x$ to $x+z$ exactly when the former site is occupied and the latter is empty. At most one particle occupies a site.

A probability measure $\mu$ on $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z^d}$ is stationary if it is preserved by this evolution. It is exchangeable if its law is unchanged by every permutation of lattice sites which fixes all but finitely many sites. Must every stationary measure be exchangeable?

Equivalently, must there exist a probability measure $\nu$ on $[0,1]$ such that \[ \mu=\int_0^1\bigotimes_{x\in\mathbb Z^d}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\rho)\,\nu(d\rho)? \] Thus the proposed classification allows a random global density but requires conditional independence and identical occupation probabilities once that density is chosen. No spatial invariance hypothesis is placed on $\mu$: it is part of the proposed conclusion. The zero-mean condition permits asymmetric jump laws and does not require $p(z)=p(-z)$. Irreducibility prevents independent lattice classes from carrying different invariant densities, and the absolute moment condition makes the displayed mean a well-defined vector.

Short English statement

Are all stationary laws of every irreducible mean-zero exclusion process mixtures of independent occupations with a common density?
