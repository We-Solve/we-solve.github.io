Math-846

Periodic Simple Modules and Periodic Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record does not specify the base field or the precise convention for periodicity. The following is a provisional formulation using ordinary syzygy periodicity; these conventions and any additional source hypotheses require confirmation.

Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a finite-dimensional associative unital $k$-algebra. Write $\operatorname{mod}A$ for the category of finite-dimensional right $A$-modules, choosing right modules as a notation convention. For a module $M$, let $\Omega_A(M)$ be the kernel of a projective cover, and define higher syzygies by iteration. Call a nonzero module $M$ periodic if \[ \exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\qquad \Omega_A^n(M)\cong M \] as $A$-modules. The period may depend on $M$.

Put $A^{\mathrm e}=A\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{op}}$, where $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the opposite algebra. Regard the regular bimodule $A$ as a left $A^{\mathrm e}$-module through $(a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}})x=axb$. Call $A$ a periodic algebra if \[ \exists p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\qquad \Omega_{A^{\mathrm e}}^p(A)\cong A \] as bimodules, with syzygies taken in a minimal projective bimodule resolution.

  1. Determine whether, for the intended class of finite-dimensional algebras, the implication \[ \left(\forall\text{ simple }S\in\operatorname{mod}A\;\exists n_S\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\ \Omega_A^{n_S}(S)\cong S\right) \ \Longrightarrow\ \left(\exists p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\ \Omega_{A^{\mathrm e}}^p(A)\cong A\right) \] holds. No common period for the simple modules is assumed in the supplied statement.
  2. Classify periodic finite-dimensional algebras up to Morita equivalence, and separately up to derived equivalence. Under the usual fixed-field interpretation, Morita equivalence means an equivalence of module categories as $k$-linear categories, and derived equivalence means a $k$-linear triangulated equivalence \[ D^b(\operatorname{mod}A)\simeq D^b(\operatorname{mod}B), \] where $D^b$ denotes the bounded derived category.

The record does not determine whether $k$ must be algebraically closed or satisfy other restrictions, whether further hypotheses on $A$ are intended, or whether periodicity is instead meant in a stable category. This distinction affects boundary cases: under the displayed ordinary-syzygy definition a nonzero projective module is not periodic, whereas it represents the zero object in the stable category. Consequently, the treatment of projective simple modules and semisimple algebras requires confirmation. The fixed-field and $k$-linear conventions for the classification questions also require confirmation.

Short English statement

Ask whether periodicity of every simple module forces a finite-dimensional algebra to be periodic as a bimodule over itself, and classify periodic algebras up to Morita equivalence and, separately, derived equivalence. The base-field hypotheses and periodicity conventions are unresolved in the supplied record, so the displayed formulation is provisional.
