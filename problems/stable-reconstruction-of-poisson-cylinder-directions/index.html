Stable Reconstruction of Poisson Cylinder Directions

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A stationary Poisson cylinder process is a Poisson process of cylinders in a Euclidean space whose law is invariant under translations. The supplied record does not specify the ambient dimension, the dimension of the cylinder axes, or the distribution of cylinder cross-sections.

For standard spherical cosine-transform notation, let $d\geq 2$, let $S^{d-1}=\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^d:\|u\|=1\}$, and let $\mu$ be a finite Borel measure on $S^{d-1}$. Its spherical cosine transform is \[ (\mathcal C\mu)(u)=\int_{S^{d-1}}|\langle u,v\rangle|\,\mu({\,\mathrm{d}} v),\qquad u\in S^{d-1}. \] If the cylinder directions are unoriented lines, their directional distribution can be represented by an even probability measure $\mu$, meaning $\mu(A)=\mu(-A)$ for every Borel set $A\subseteq S^{d-1}$. This representation is conditional on the intended axis model; that model is not specified in the record. The notation above does not impose a dimension or a range of dimensions on the source problem.

Source question. Given finitely sampled intersection intensities $g$ of a stationary Poisson cylinder process, can its directional distribution be estimated stably through a continuous inversion of the spherical cosine transform?

In the conditional spherical model above, finite directional samples would have the form \[ \left(u_i,g(u_i)\right)_{i=1}^{m},\qquad m\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad u_i\in S^{d-1}. \] The requested task is to reconstruct an estimate of the directional distribution from such finite observations using cosine-transform inversion. The source does not give the equation relating $g$ to $\mathcal C\mu$, including any process-intensity or cross-section factors. It also does not specify sampling assumptions, an observation-error model, admissible directional distributions, topologies for continuity, or a stability or convergence criterion. Consequently, this is an unresolved formulation of an estimation problem, not a fully specified existence assertion for a continuous inverse or estimator. In particular, whether "continuous inversion" concerns complete transform data or a reconstruction procedure acting on finite samples remains unresolved.

Short English statement

The problem asks whether finitely many intersection-intensity observations of a stationary Poisson cylinder process allow stable estimation of its directional distribution through spherical cosine-transform inversion. The geometric model, observation equation, sampling assumptions, and meaning of stability require clarification. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
