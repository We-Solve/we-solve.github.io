What symmetry averaging preserves, and what polar reduction loses

The source asks for interpolation respecting rank-one convexity and frame indifference. A function $W$ on real matrices is rank-one convex if its restriction to every affine line with rank-one direction is convex. We test two possible routes: average an already valid interpolant over a finite symmetry group, or interpolate directly in polar coordinates.
Proposition: Let a finite group $\Gamma$ act linearly by maps $T(F)=QFR$, with $Q,R$ invertible. If $W$ is rank-one convex, then $\overline W(F)=|\Gamma|^{-1}\sum_{T\in\Gamma}W(T(F))$ is rank-one convex and invariant under $\Gamma$. If a sample set is closed under $\Gamma$, its specified values are constant on each orbit, and $W$ interpolates them, then so does $\overline W$.
Proof: If $H$ has rank one, $QHR$ also has rank one because multiplication by invertible matrices preserves rank. For any $F,H$ with this property, each summand $t\mapsto W(T(F)+tT(H))$ is convex; their average is convex. For $T_0\in\Gamma$, composition $T\mapsto T\circ T_0$ permutes the group, proving $\overline W(T_0(F))=\overline W(F)$. At a sample point $F$, every $T(F)$ is another sample with the same specified value. Each summand therefore equals that value, proving interpolation. ∎
This route presupposes existence and construction of $W$; averaging does not produce one from inconsistent or otherwise difficult data. Finite-group invariance also does not by itself imply invariance under all rotations. For the second route consider the rank-one affine line \[ F(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad U(t)=(F(t)^TF(t))^{1/2}. \] Its polar stretch values satisfy \[ U(0)=I,\qquad U(\pm1)=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2&\pm1\\\pm1&3\end{pmatrix}. \] Squaring the last matrices gives $F(\pm1)^TF(\pm1)$, and they are positive definite because their leading entry and determinant are positive. Hence they are the positive square roots. But their midpoint is $\operatorname{diag}(2,3)/\sqrt5\ne I$. The polar map therefore does not carry this rank-one line to the corresponding straight line in stretch space.

Verification and remaining gaps

The two matrix squares, determinants and midpoint inequality were checked exactly up to the displayed symbolic square root. The attempted inference that ordinary straight-line interpolation of stretch matrices automatically checks rank-one convexity fails at this geometric step. This does not prove that all methods using polar coordinates fail; they could track the resulting curved paths or impose stronger sufficient conditions. No existence theorem or algorithm for a general frame-indifferent rank-one-convex interpolant is obtained, and the report's stated approximation results are not claimed as new.
