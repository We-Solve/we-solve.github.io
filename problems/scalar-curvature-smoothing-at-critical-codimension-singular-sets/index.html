Scalar-Curvature Smoothing at Critical-Codimension Singular Sets

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\geq 3$ be an integer and let $\delta$ denote the Euclidean metric on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$. An $L^\infty$ Riemannian metric uniformly bilipschitz to $\delta$ is a measurable symmetric tensor $g$ for which there is a constant $C\geq 1$ such that \[ C^{-1}|v|^2\leq g_x(v,v)\leq C|v|^2 \qquad\text{for almost every }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n\text{ and every }v\in{\mathbb{R}}^n. \] The source considers such metrics admitting a representative smooth away from a singular set $S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$.

For $r>0$, write \[ S_r=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n:\operatorname{dist}_{\delta}(x,S)<r\}. \] A usual upper $(n-2)$-dimensional Minkowski-content quantity is \[ \limsup_{r\downarrow0}\frac{\mathcal L^n(S_r)}{r^2}, \] where $\mathcal L^n$ denotes Euclidean Lebesgue measure. The source does not specify whether its Minkowski content uses this global upper-content convention, an actual limit, or a local convention. Accordingly, let $\mathcal M^{n-2}(S)$ denote the content intended by the source, pending verification of that definition.

Let $\mathcal P_1(g,S)$ stand for the exact conclusion of the source's "Theorem 1," described in the cleaned record as nonnegative-scalar-curvature smoothing. This is a placeholder: the theorem's conclusion, approximation topology, curvature requirements, and any additional hypotheses are not included in the supplied record.

With those unresolved meanings retained explicitly, the questions are whether there exist $S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$ and an $L^\infty$ metric $g$, uniformly bilipschitz to $\delta$ and smooth away from $S$, satisfying respectively \[ \mathcal M^{n-2}(S)<\infty \quad\text{and}\quad \neg\mathcal P_1(g,S), \] and \[ \mathcal M^{n-2}(S)=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \neg\mathcal P_1(g,S). \] The source also asks whether the conclusion of Theorem 1 still holds in the zero-content case. Any counterexample must satisfy the other hypotheses of that theorem; those hypotheses cannot be reconstructed from the supplied text. In particular, the cited work's title refers to nonnegative scalar curvature outside the singular set, but the displayed source question does not explicitly impose $R_g\geq0$ on ${\mathbb{R}}^n\setminus S$. Whether that hypothesis is inherited must be checked. These are therefore conditional formulations of the source questions, not a complete independently specified smoothing problem.

Short English statement

For dimensions $n\geq3$, the source asks whether a bounded metric on ${\mathbb{R}}^n$, uniformly comparable to the Euclidean metric and smooth outside a singular set of finite $(n-2)$-dimensional Minkowski content, can violate a cited scalar-curvature smoothing theorem. It asks the same question for zero content. The missing theorem and content convention must be recovered before a fully precise statement is possible.
