A local descent test.
Let a moduli chart be a product $U\times V$, with complex coordinates $z$ on $U$ and $w_1,\ldots,w_r$ on connected $V$, and let the proposed reduction be projection to $U$. In a chosen holomorphic trivialization, a holomorphic section $F(z,w)$ descends to a function of $z$ exactly when $\partial_{w_j}F=0$ for every $j$. Necessity follows by differentiation. For sufficiency, vanishing derivatives makes $F$ locally constant along each fiber, hence constant on the connected fiber; its value at a fixed $w_0$ gives a holomorphic descended function.
Restriction is weaker.
For $F(z,w)=f(z)+w g(z)$, restriction to $w=0$ is $f$, regardless of $g$, while descent fails whenever $g\ne0$. Thus checking only the restricted Fourier series or value on a lower-dimensional locus cannot prove reduction. The first normal derivative detects this example. Vanishing finitely many normal derivatives is also insufficient: $f(z)+w^{m+1}g(z)$ passes every test through order $m$.
Automorphic compatibility and gap.
A modular section transforms with a multiplier or line-bundle automorphy factor. Even fiberwise constancy in one trivialization is not globally meaningful unless that factor descends along the proposed map. Establishing group compatibility, transition functions and behavior at singular fibers is the first missing global step. No actual Calabi--Yau moduli space or modular object is supplied by this local criterion. It distinguishes restriction from descent and gives explicit failed finite-jet tests, but does not classify the reductions requested in the exploratory source programme.
