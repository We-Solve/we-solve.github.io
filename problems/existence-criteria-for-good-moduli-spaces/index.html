Math-4613

Existence Criteria for Good Moduli Spaces

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal X$ be an Artin stack over an unspecified base. In the usual definition, a good moduli space is a morphism $\phi:\mathcal X\to X$ to an algebraic space such that $\phi$ is cohomologically affine and the natural map $\mathcal O_X\to\phi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal X}$ is an isomorphism. Here cohomologically affine means quasi-compact with exact pushforward on quasi-coherent modules.

The source poses three directions for good moduli spaces:

  1. Is there a Hilbert--Mumford-type criterion in this setting? The numerical data, degeneration tests, and precise property to be characterized are not specified.
  2. Do suitable topological criteria on an Artin stack, such as an intended weak valuative criterion, imply existence of a good moduli space? The task is to formulate effective sufficient conditions; the extraction supplies no particular weak valuative condition or additional stack hypotheses.
  3. Can good moduli spaces be constructed locally? The intended topology or system of local charts, compatibility of local constructions, and gluing statement are not specified.
These are exploratory existence and construction questions. They do not assert that every Artin stack has a good moduli space or that an unnamed valuative condition alone is sufficient.

Short English statement

Seek a Hilbert--Mumford-type criterion, topological or weak valuative existence criteria, and local construction methods for good moduli spaces of Artin stacks. The source does not fix the hypotheses or the exact criteria to be tested.
