Bijectivity of Antipodes in Noetherian Hopf Algebras

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $A$ be a Hopf algebra over the field $k$ intended by the source. Thus $A$ has multiplication $m$, unit $\eta$, coproduct $\Delta$, counit $\epsilon$, and antipode $S:A\to A$ satisfying, in particular, \[ m(S\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta =\eta\epsilon =m(\operatorname{id}\otimes S)\Delta. \] Bijectivity of $S$ is not included in the definition of Hopf algebra used for this question.

Assume $A$ is noetherian as an algebra. Under the usual two-sided convention, every ascending chain of left ideals and every ascending chain of right ideals stabilizes. The question is whether \[ \forall A\text{ noetherian Hopf algebra}:\qquad S:A\longrightarrow A\text{ is bijective}. \] Bijective means both injective and surjective as a map, equivalently as a $k$-linear map; it does not mean that the antipode is an algebra homomorphism rather than an antihomomorphism.

The source does not specify the base field, its characteristic, or whether noetherian is intended on one or both sides. The draft states the standard two-sided reading while retaining that convention issue. No finite-dimensionality, affine finite generation, semiprimeness, polynomial-identity assumption, or homological regularity is added. The supplied assessment mentions results under additional hypotheses, but those hypotheses are contextual and do not narrow the universal question.

Short English statement

Must the antipode of every noetherian Hopf algebra be both injective and surjective? Bijectivity is part of the proposed conclusion, with the noetherian side convention and base field still needing source confirmation.
