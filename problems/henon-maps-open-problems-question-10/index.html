Henon Maps: Open Problems — Question 10

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $f:{\mathbb{R}}^2\to{\mathbb{R}}^2$ be a real Hénon map. The supplied record does not specify the precise class of maps intended by this term, including the allowed polynomial degrees, normal forms, or compositions; this qualification requires confirmation from the source. No mild dissipativity hypothesis is imposed in the question.

Define the set of periodic points by \[ \operatorname{Per}(f)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2: f^n(x)=x\text{ for some integer }n\geq 1\}, \qquad K_f=\overline{\operatorname{Per}(f)}, \] where closure is taken in the Euclidean topology of ${\mathbb{R}}^2$. The union of all periodic orbits equals $\operatorname{Per}(f)$. An invariant Borel probability measure is a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ satisfying \[ \mu(f^{-1}(A))=\mu(A)\qquad\text{for every Borel set }A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^2. \] Its support is \[ \operatorname{supp}\mu=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^2:\mu(U)>0\text{ for every open neighborhood }U\text{ of }x\}. \]

The question is whether, for every real Hénon map $f$ in the intended source class and every invariant Borel probability measure $\mu$, \[ \operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq K_f. \] Equivalently, must every such measure satisfy $\mu(K_f)=1$? Here "support all the invariant probability measures" is interpreted as this support containment, without a compactness assumption on the measures' supports.

As background, the supplied record reports this periodic-point density property for mildly dissipative real Hénon maps, citing [CP], and for complex Hénon maps, citing [MR4142445]. These are supplied contextual assertions, not independently verified here. Mild dissipativity is not defined in the record and is not an assumption of the question.

Short English statement

For every real Hénon map in the source's intended class, must the support of every invariant Borel probability measure lie in the closure of the periodic points? The exact class of real Hénon maps requires confirmation. The registry labels this question open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
