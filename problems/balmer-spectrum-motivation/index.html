Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record is motivational and is marked statement_status: unrecoverable. Its verification note says that the broad spectrum question motivates three questions preserved in a following record. Thus the material below records the available mathematical framework and questions, with unresolved hypotheses made explicit; it does not recover a uniquely specified standalone conjecture.

The supplied literature assessment suggests the setting of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra $A$ over a field $k$. This is a contextual interpretation: the quoted passage does not specify the class of algebras, the field, its characteristic, or any additional tensor hypotheses. In this setting, let $\operatorname{mod}_{\mathrm{fd}}A$ denote the category of finite-dimensional left $A$-modules, and define the stable category by \[ \operatorname{stab}A =\operatorname{mod}_{\mathrm{fd}}A/[\operatorname{proj}A], \] where morphisms are taken modulo those factoring through projective modules. Finite-dimensional Hopf algebras are self-injective, so projective and injective modules coincide and this stable category is triangulated. The Hopf structure supplies the tensor product over $k$, with unit the trivial module $k$. Set \[ H^*(A,k)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}^n_A(k,k), \] with its cohomological grading and Yoneda product.

For an essentially small symmetric tensor triangulated category $\mathcal T$, the usual Balmer spectrum consists of its proper prime thick tensor ideals: \[ \operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T) =\{\mathcal P\subsetneq\mathcal T:\mathcal P\text{ is a thick tensor ideal and } X\otimes Y\in\mathcal P\Rightarrow X\in\mathcal P\text{ or }Y\in\mathcal P\}. \] Here thick means triangulated and closed under direct summands, and a tensor ideal is closed under tensoring with arbitrary objects. Its topology has closed subsets generated by the object supports \[ \operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal T}(X) =\{\mathcal P\in\operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal T):X\notin\mathcal P\}. \] A general finite-dimensional Hopf algebra need not supply a symmetric tensor category. Consequently, this definition cannot be imposed on the entire contextual class without an additional hypothesis or a specified alternative spectrum. The source writes $\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{stab}A)$; its intended spectrum convention remains unresolved.

The source displays a comparison map \[ \Psi:\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{stab}A) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}H^*(A,k). \] Under the usual graded-commutative interpretation, the underlying points of $\operatorname{Proj}H^*(A,k)$ are homogeneous prime ideals not containing the positive-degree ideal, with the usual Zariski topology. The passage does not specify the construction of $\Psi$, its complete hypotheses, or the convention under which this target is a scheme.

Source question. For the algebras $A$ intended by the report, how can one compute $\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{stab}A)$? The passage develops this motivation into the following questions:

  1. When is $\operatorname{Proj}H^*(A,k)$ a scheme of finite type? The source adds the parenthetical question: when is $H^*(A,k)$ Noetherian?
  2. When is the displayed comparison map $\Psi$ a homeomorphism?
  3. Can one calculate $\operatorname{Proj}H^*(A,k)$?
These are questions about identifying hypotheses and carrying out computations, rather than a supplied universal assertion. The finite-type base is not stated; finite type of $\operatorname{Proj}H^*(A,k)$ and Noetherianity of the graded ring are retained as separate questions, without asserting their equivalence.

The passage asserts that $\Psi$ is surjective when $H^*(A,k)$ is Noetherian. This is preserved as a source assertion with unspecified ambient hypotheses, not as an independently verified theorem for every finite-dimensional Hopf algebra. The passage also invokes results for finite groups and finite group schemes over a field, and says that the second question was touched upon in N. Andruskiewitsch's talk.

Short English statement

The record asks how to compute the tensor triangular spectrum of a stable representation category, using cohomology and a proposed comparison map. It motivates questions about cohomological finiteness, when that map is a homeomorphism, and explicit computation of the cohomological projective spectrum. The record is marked unrecoverable: the intended algebra class, spectrum convention, and comparison-map hypotheses require human review.
