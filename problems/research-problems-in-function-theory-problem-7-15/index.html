Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.15

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $D$ be a domain in the Riemann sphere with boundary of positive logarithmic capacity, and let $\omega_D^z$ denote harmonic measure viewed from $z\in D$, under an appropriate boundary measurability convention. A finite boundary point $p$ is angular if some $\varepsilon>0$ has the following property: every component $V$ of $D\cap\{|w-p|<\varepsilon\}$ with $p\in\partial V$ is contained in an angle of opening strictly less than $\pi$ and vertex $p$. The angle may depend on the component. The source defines angularity at infinity similarly without giving its coordinate convention. Write $A(D)$ for these points.

The source reports $\omega_D^z(A(D))=0$ for every $z\in D$ and asks for a direct proof, avoiding its cited unpublished Brownian-path theorem. It also says a nonempty $A(D)$ forces positive boundary capacity and that $A(D)$ can have positive linear measure. Its example takes a Cantor set $C\subset\{|z|=1\}$ and deletes the radial segments $\{rz:z\in C,\ 1/2\le r<1\}$ from the open unit disk, with the claimed conclusion $A(D)=C$. These are attributed contextual statements, not proved here.

For $1/2<\alpha<1$, the proposed replacement region is printed as \[ Q_\alpha=\{x+iy:0<x<|y|^\alpha\}. \] The intended $B_\alpha(D)$ replaces the angles in the angular-point definition by translates of $Q_\alpha$. A literal finite-point version would require some $\varepsilon>0$ such that every corresponding component $V$ is contained in $p+Q_\alpha$. The source does not specify rotations, dependence on components, normalization of the profile, or the version at infinity. The principal question is to determine which $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$ have the universal property \[ \omega_D^z(B_\alpha(D))=0 \quad\text{for every domain }D\text{ in the stated capacity class and every }z\in D. \] This schema is conditional on recovering the exact intended replacement region and boundary-set convention.

The source says the analogous assertion is false at $\alpha=1/2$, using a disk. The displayed $Q_\alpha$ has two separated components and no point on $y=0$, so its literal geometry and this disk comparison require source review; no inequality reversal or alternative cusp is silently substituted. The quoted Brownian formula has a separate transcription defect: its displayed quantity is \[ \left|\frac{\omega(t)-\omega(t_0)}{\omega(t)-\omega(t_0)}\right|, \] which, whenever defined, equals $1$ and cannot fill an open unit-circle arc of length $\pi$ as claimed. The intended directional expression is absent, and this formula cannot be used as a theorem.

A harder requested extension is to characterize the monotone profiles $f$ for which replacing the angles by translates of $\{x+iy:0<x<f(|y|)\}$ always yields a harmonic-measure-zero boundary set. Positivity, monotonicity direction, regularity, local normalization, and allowable coordinate changes are not stated. Analogous questions for Riemann surfaces and $n$-dimensional space are mentioned without further specifications. The supplied triage reports the item open as of 2018 with no definitive later resolution; no independent verification is supplied.

Short English statement

Determine which proposed cusp profiles force the corresponding boundary points to have harmonic measure zero, and seek a direct proof of the reported angular-point result. The printed Brownian expression is malformed, and the printed cusp's geometry needs reconciliation with its claimed disk endpoint example. Those defects prevent a unique corrected profile question from being inferred.
