Scalar Curvature Question [?8]: Find a useful local geometric definition of a scalar-curvature lower bound $\operatorname{Sc}\geq\sigma$ that

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a smooth Riemannian manifold $(M^n,g)$, scalar curvature is $\operatorname{Sc}_g=\operatorname{tr}_g\operatorname{Ric}_g$, normalized so that the unit round $n$-sphere has scalar curvature $n(n-1)$. The usual lower bound means \[ \forall x\in M,\qquad \operatorname{Sc}_g(x)\ge\sigma \] for a fixed real $\sigma$.

Gromov's question asks for a local geometric notion extending this condition to singular spaces and still implying useful global scalar-curvature theorems. To formulate the requested kind of extension, one would choose an admissible singular category $\mathcal C$ and predicates $\mathsf S_\sigma(U)$ on neighborhoods, agreeing with the displayed bound in the smooth category and depending on local geometric data. Local spectral information and localized minimal-hypersurface constructions are suggested directions. The source asks for more than a definition based solely on small-ball volumes; agreement with a smooth volume expansion by itself does not supply the required singular theory or global conclusions.

This is a definition-and-theorem programme, not one specified inequality. The record does not prescribe the singular category, metric versus metric-measure data, spectral operator or boundary conditions, hypersurface admissibility and dimension restrictions, or the global theorems to be recovered. Those choices cannot be replaced by an invented universally sufficient axiom list.

The supplied research summary reports partial attempts described as volumic/macroscopic methods and localized minimal hypersurfaces, with restricted low-dimensional or spin applications. It mentions Lohkamp's negative-scalar-curvature $C^0$-density result and recent Chodosh--Li/Gromov work, but supplies no exact hypotheses or references. A negative-curvature density statement is kept distinct from a definition or stability theorem for a positive lower bound. The summary explicitly does not claim a universally valid local definition recovering all desired global results.

Short English statement

Develop a local geometric meaning of $\operatorname{Sc}\ge\sigma$ that agrees with the smooth condition, extends to specified singular spaces, and supports specified global theorems. Spectral or localized minimal-hypersurface methods are proposed, with no single finished formulation supplied.
