Math-6995

Diophantine Definability of Rational Subfields

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p$ be a prime, let $t$ be transcendental over ${\mathbb{F}}_p$, and put $K={\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$. For $g\in K$, let ${\mathbb{F}}_p(g)$ denote the smallest subfield of $K$ containing ${\mathbb{F}}_p$ and $g$. This definition includes constant $g$, for which ${\mathbb{F}}_p(g)={\mathbb{F}}_p$. Define \[ S=\{(f,g)\in K^2:f\in{\mathbb{F}}_p(g)\}. \] A subset $D\subseteq K^n$ is Diophantine in the parameter-free ring language if there exist integers $m\geq0$, $r\geq1$, and polynomials \[ P_1,\ldots,P_r\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X_1,\ldots,X_n,Y_1,\ldots,Y_m] \] such that, for every $\mathbf x\in K^n$, \[ \mathbf x\in D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\mathbf y\in K^m\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{r}P_i(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=0. \] Integer coefficients are interpreted through the canonical map ${\mathbb{Z}}\to K$; in particular, no parameter naming $t$ is allowed. Diophantine definability with parameters in $K$ is defined in the same way, allowing the finitely many defining polynomials to have coefficients in $K$.

Question. Is $S\subseteq K^2$ Diophantine in the parameter-free ring language? Explicitly, do there exist $m\geq0$, $r\geq1$, and $P_1,\ldots,P_r\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X,Z,Y_1,\ldots,Y_m]$ such that \[ \forall f,g\in K,\qquad f\in{\mathbb{F}}_p(g)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\mathbf y\in K^m\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{r}P_i(f,g,\mathbf y)=0? \] The source also asks, in particular, whether the subfield ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t^2)\subseteq K$ is Diophantine with parameters in $K$. That is, do there exist $a\geq0$, $b\geq1$, and $Q_1,\ldots,Q_b\in K[X,Y_1,\ldots,Y_a]$ such that \[ \forall f\in K,\qquad f\in{\mathbb{F}}_p(t^2)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\mathbf y\in K^a\ \bigwedge_{j=1}^{b}Q_j(f,\mathbf y)=0? \] A positive answer for $S$ implies a positive answer to this latter question by specializing $g=t^2$, which is then an allowed parameter. The supplied record gives no restriction on the prime $p$, and does not specify whether the question concerns a particular fixed prime, every prime separately, or a single formula uniform in $p$. The displayed questions describe definability in each fixed field ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$; the intended quantification over primes remains unresolved.

Short English statement

In the rational function field ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$, can membership of $f$ in the subfield generated by $g$ be expressed by existential polynomial equations without parameters? The source also asks whether membership in ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t^2)$ can be expressed by existential polynomial equations allowing parameters from ${\mathbb{F}}_p(t)$. The intended scope over primes is unspecified.
