Math-15686

Optimal Inverse-Growth Conditions for Bicyclic Operator Cyclicity

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For orientation, in the usual bounded-operator setting, let $X$ be a Banach space and let $T\colon X\to X$ be a bounded linear operator with bounded inverse. The operator $T$ is called bicyclic if there exists $x\in X$ such that \[ \overline{\operatorname{span}\{T^k x:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}=X. \] It is called cyclic if there exists $y\in X$ such that \[ \overline{\operatorname{span}\{T^k y:k=0,1,2,\ldots\}}=X. \] Here closures are in the norm topology, spans use the scalar field of $X$, and operator norms are induced by that norm. These are conventional definitions supplied to explain the terminology; the source record does not specify the ambient space, its scalar field, or the complete hypotheses of the referenced cyclicity theorem.

For positive integers $n$, the two inverse-growth conditions in the record are \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\lVert T^{-n}\rVert}{\sqrt n}=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \log\lVert T^{-n}\rVert=O(\sqrt n). \] The second condition means that there exist $C>0$ and a positive integer $N$ such that \[ \left|\log\lVert T^{-n}\rVert\right|\le C\sqrt n \qquad\text{for every integer }n\ge N. \]

Conjecture as supplied, with unresolved scope. The record refers to a theorem asserting that a bicyclic operator satisfying the first growth condition is cyclic, under the remaining hypotheses of that theorem. It conjectures that the first condition cannot be replaced by the second condition while retaining those remaining hypotheses.

More formally, let $\mathcal H(X,T)$ stand for the full remaining hypotheses of the referenced theorem, which are not supplied. The intended counterexample assertion is \[ \exists (X,T)\;\left[ \mathcal H(X,T)\ \land\ T\text{ is bicyclic}\ \land\ \log\lVert T^{-n}\rVert=O(\sqrt n) \ \land\ T\text{ is not cyclic} \right]. \] The quantifier ranges over the spaces and operators permitted by the referenced theorem. Because those domains and $\mathcal H$ are unspecified, this is a conditional formulation of the intended target, not a complete standalone conjecture for all Banach-space operators. The supplied claim of optimality concerns this particular replacement of the growth hypothesis.

Short English statement

The conjecture asks whether the square-root little-$o$ bound on inverse powers in a bicyclic-to-cyclic theorem can fail to imply cyclicity when weakened to a square-root big-$O$ bound. A counterexample must satisfy all other hypotheses of that theorem. Those hypotheses and the intended class of spaces are missing from the supplied record, so the precise scope remains unresolved.
