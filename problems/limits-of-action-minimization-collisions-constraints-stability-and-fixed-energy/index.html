Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. Coercivity means action bounds control the chosen loop-space norm after removing allowed symmetries. Hyperbolic and elliptic directions of a periodic orbit refer to spectral stability of its linearized return map; the exact convention must address neutral symmetry directions. Fixed-energy variational principles differ from fixed-period action minimization.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Understand the natural limits of action minimization: determine its relationship to symmetry constraints; whether topological constraints can be imposed without forcing collisions; what results hold for arbitrary masses; the hyperbolic and elliptic dimensions of minimizers; and the usefulness of minimization at fixed energy.

Action minimality and stability are different.
For the one-dimensional mechanical Lagrangian $L=\tfrac12\dot x^2+\tfrac12\omega^2x^2$, $\omega>0$, the zero periodic path is the strict global minimizer among period-P loops: its action is zero and every nonzero loop has positive action. Yet its Euler--Lagrange equation is $\ddot x=\omega^2x$, whose nonzero solutions grow or decay exponentially; the equilibrium is hyperbolic. Conversely $L=\tfrac12\dot x^2-\tfrac12\omega^2x^2$ has a linearly stable equilibrium but negative second variation in constant loop directions. These examples show that no generic equivalence between variational minimality and elliptic linear stability is available. They are not Newtonian n-body counterexamples to any specialized theorem.
A fixed-period versus fixed-energy calculation.
For a fixed collision-free geometric loop $Q(s)$, $0\le s\le1$, traversed in time $P$, put $K_0=\tfrac12\int\sum m_i|Q_i'|^2ds$ and $U_0=\int U(Q)ds$. Then \[\mathcal A(P)=K_0/P+PU_0,\qquad \mathcal A'(P)=-K_0/P^2+U_0. \] Thus free variation of the traversal time in the ordinary action imposes zero average energy. To target a prescribed energy $E_*$, the modified functional $\mathcal A(P)+E_*P$ instead has time stationarity $K_0/P^2-U_0=E_*$. Fixed-period and fixed-energy minimization are therefore different problems even before collision issues.
