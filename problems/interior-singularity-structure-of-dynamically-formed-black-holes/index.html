Separating metric continuity from curvature regularity.
A local warped-product diagnostic makes the distinction precise. For $v>0$, let \[ g=-2\,du\,dv+r(v)^2d\Omega^2,\qquad r(v)=r_0+v^\alpha, \quad r_0>0,\quad0<\alpha<1. \] The metric extends continuously and nondegenerately to $v=0$, and the symmetry-sphere area tends to $4\pi r_0^2>0$. Its angular Christoffel component is $\Gamma^\theta_{v\theta}=r'/r$. Substituting this into the curvature formula yields \[ R_{v\theta v\theta}=-rr''=-r\alpha(\alpha-1)v^{\alpha-2}, \] which is unbounded in this coordinate frame that remains regular for the continuous extension. The derivative $r'$ also diverges.
What it demonstrates.
A boundary at positive area radius may be continuous at the metric level and singular at curvature level; it need not be the center, where area radius vanishes. Hence a proof of one type of extendibility cannot by itself classify the global terminal boundary or identify its endpoints with the center.
