Math-6885

Babai Polylogarithmic Diameter Conjecture for Finite Simple Groups

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite simple group, meaning that its only normal subgroups are $\{1_G\}$ and $G$. A subset $A\subseteq G$ generates $G$ if $\langle A\rangle=G$. For a positive integer $\ell$, define the exact product set \[ A^\ell=\{a_1\cdots a_\ell:a_1,\ldots,a_\ell\in A\}. \] The supplied assertion is that there exists an absolute real constant $C>0$, independent of $G$ and $A$, such that \[ \forall\,G\text{ finite simple}\quad \forall\,A\subseteq G\text{ with }\langle A\rangle=G\quad \exists\,\ell\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}: \quad \ell\leq(\log|G|)^C\quad\text{and}\quad A^\ell=G. \] Unresolved formulation qualifications. The source does not assume $1_G\in A$ or $A=A^{-1}$, does not exclude cyclic simple groups, and does not specify the logarithm base. With the exact-product definition above, a singleton generating set in a cyclic group has singleton powers, so the displayed assertion cannot hold over the stated domain. This is a defect in the supplied formulation requiring clarification, rather than an accepted formulation of the intended conjecture.

For comparison of conventions only, put $A^0=\{1_G\}$. Products of at most $L$ elements of $A$ form $\bigcup_{j=0}^{L}A^j$, whereas the usual undirected word ball uses $\bigcup_{j=0}^{L}(A\cup A^{-1})^j$. These differ from the source's exact product set. The record does not establish which convention or additional hypotheses were intended; none is substituted here. The missing logarithm convention also affects the literal bound for small groups because no multiplicative constant or small-group exception is stated.

Short English statement

The supplied target asks for one absolute exponent giving a polylogarithmic product-length bound for every generating subset of every finite simple group. Its exact-length wording and inclusion of cyclic simple groups create an obstruction. The intended group domain, word-length convention, and logarithm normalization require clarification before this can serve as a precise statement of Babai's conjecture.
