Conjecture 2.23 — {K∼ Cd O}/∼ Cd+1 is torsion free for each d.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\mathcal K$ denote the source's knot class, let $O$ be the unknot, and let $\sim_{C_d}$ be its $C_d$-equivalence relation. Under the usual interpretation these are knots in $S^3$ and equivalences generated by the specified degree-$d$ clasper moves, but the record provides no move definition or knot-category conventions. For each admissible index $d$, form \[ G_d=\{K\in\mathcal K:K\sim_{C_d}O\}/\sim_{C_{d+1}}. \] Conjecture 2.23 states that $G_d$ is torsion free for every $d$. With the source's intended group operation of connected sum and identity $[O]$, this means \[ \forall [K]\in G_d\ \forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\quad m[K]=[O]\ \Longrightarrow\ [K]=[O]. \] Equivalently, under that group interpretation, for every $K\sim_{C_d}O$ and every positive integer $m$, \[ K^{\# m}\sim_{C_{d+1}}O\ \Longrightarrow\ K\sim_{C_{d+1}}O, \] where $K^{\#m}$ is the connected sum of $m$ copies of $K$. The definition and nesting of the $C_d$-relations, the induced group structure, and the index range must be supplied from the source; "for each $d$" is retained without inventing a $d=0$ convention.

Short English statement

At each level of the $C_d$-equivalence filtration, the group of knots equivalent to the unknot at that level, modulo the next equivalence, is conjectured to have no nonzero torsion. Thus a finite connected sum becoming trivial at the next level should force the original class to be trivial there as well.
