Unique Recovery of Continuous Motions

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied problem asks for a classification of functions whose tomographic data permit unique recovery of a continuous motion. The admissible functions, the forward measurement operator, the allowed motions, and the equivalence under which recovery is unique are not specified in the supplied record. Consequently, a fully determined mathematical statement cannot be recovered from this record alone.

A conditional formulation can make the missing specifications explicit. Let $\mathcal A$ denote an as-yet unspecified class of admissible functions, let $\mathcal M$ denote an as-yet unspecified class of continuous motions, and let $\mathcal D(f,m)$ denote the complete tomographic data generated by $f\in\mathcal A$ undergoing $m\in\mathcal M$. Let $\sim$ denote the equivalence of motions allowed by the intended notion of recovery. If the function $f$ is known and uniqueness is required for every admissible motion, the classification target would be \[ \mathcal U=\left\{f\in\mathcal A:\ \forall m_1,m_2\in\mathcal M,\quad \mathcal D(f,m_1)=\mathcal D(f,m_2)\ \Longrightarrow\ m_1\sim m_2\right\}. \] The task under those specifications would be to characterize $\mathcal U$ by necessary and sufficient conditions on $f$. This is a schematic formulation only: the source does not determine these classes and operators, whether $f$ is known, whether uniqueness is required for every motion, or whether $\sim$ means exact equality or equality modulo specified ambiguities. If $f$ is also unknown, the comparison must instead allow competing functions, and the displayed condition does not express that joint inverse problem.

The accompanying extracted theorem fragment assumes a rotation \[ R\in C^1([0,T];\mathrm{SO}(3)),\qquad \mathrm{SO}(3)=\{Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times3}:Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I_3,\ \det Q=1\}, \] and an associated angular velocity $\omega:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^3$. Its claimed relation is stated for every $r\in(-k_0,k_0)$. However, the measurement formula and differential identity are corrupted, and their remaining symbols are undefined. These fragmentary hypotheses do not establish the motion class or measurement model of the classification problem. In particular, the source's use of continuous motion must not silently be replaced by continuously differentiable rotation.

Short English statement

Classify the functions that allow a continuous motion to be determined uniquely from tomographic measurements. The supplied record leaves the measurement model, function class, motion class, and meaning of uniqueness unresolved, so the displayed mathematical formulation is conditional and requires human review.
