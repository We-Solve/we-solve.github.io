Research attempt

Literature, source context and scope

The report asks about efficient weight computation even when an approximation architecture is known, and describes a hierarchical approach under a stability hypothesis. It does not specify one loss or a uniform complexity model for all architectures. This attempt studies exact squared-loss training of one scalar ReLU and then locates the failure in extending gradient-based reasoning even to that model.

A complete global algorithm for a single scalar weight

For rational data $(x_i,y_i)_{i=1}^N$, $N\ge1$, consider the fixed architecture $f_w(x)=\max(0,wx)$ with one real weight and no bias, and loss $L(w)=\sum_i(f_w(x_i)-y_i)^2$. Set \[ z_i^+=\max(x_i,0),\quad z_i^-=\min(x_i,0),\quad A_\pm=\sum_i(z_i^\pm)^2,\quad B_\pm=\sum_i y_i z_i^\pm,\quad C=\sum_i y_i^2. \]
Proposition: A global optimum is found by evaluating just two candidates: \[ w_+=\begin{cases}\max(0,B_+/A_+),&A_+>0,\\0,&A_+=0,\end{cases} \quad w_-=\begin{cases}\min(0,B_-/A_-),&A_->0,\\0,&A_-=0.\end{cases} \] Choose the one of smaller loss. This uses $O(N)$ rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit complexity in the rational input length.
Proof: On $w\ge0$, the prediction is $wz_i^+$, and on $w\le0$ it is $wz_i^-$. Thus the loss on each closed half-line is $A_\pm w^2-2B_\pm w+C$. If $A_\pm>0$, completing the square gives its unconstrained minimizer $B_\pm/A_\pm$, clipped to the appropriate half-line as displayed. If $A_\pm=0$, every $z_i^\pm=0$, hence also $B_\pm=0$ and the loss is constantly $C$ there. The two half-lines cover every real weight, so comparison gives a global optimum. Computing the sums is linear in $N$. With rational numerators and denominators in binary, naive common-denominator additions and multiplications give intermediate bit lengths bounded by a polynomial in the total input length, and ordinary integer arithmetic then gives polynomial bit cost. ∎

A failed stationary-point route

For the single data point $(x,y)=(1,1)$, every $w<0$ has loss one and derivative zero, while $w=1$ has loss zero. Exact gradient descent started at any negative weight remains there, independently of its step sizes, since each computed gradient is zero. This is a suboptimal open plateau, not a nondifferentiability issue at zero. Thus a certificate of stationarity is insufficient even where the global optimum is cheaply computable.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact rational checks compare the two-candidate solution against each half-line quadratic for several datasets, and verify the dead-ReLU plateau. For more weights the activation regions depend jointly on parameters; enumerating them may become expensive and multilayer loss is no longer quadratic on each region. No general efficient optimal-weight algorithm or complexity lower bound for the source's broad problem follows from this one-weight calculation.
