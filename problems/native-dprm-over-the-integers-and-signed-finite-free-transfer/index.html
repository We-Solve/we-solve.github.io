Math-1574

Native DPRM over the integers and signed finite-free transfer

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Davis's Question 10 has two methodological requests: find a native proof of DPRM over ${\mathbb{Z}}$ without referring to ${\mathbb{N}}$, and prove DPRM abstractly for a class of rings without reference to ${\mathbb{N}}$. The source does not define "native" or the class of rings. For a computably presented ring $R$, use DPRM here to mean that every recursively enumerable finite-arity relation on $R$ has a positive-existential ring definition, equivalently an existential finite system of polynomial equations, in its stated language. Noncommutative rings use ring equations with their multiplication order respected; no unqualified single-polynomial encoding is assumed.

The aid reports the following signed scalar-transfer theorem. Let $A$ be a unital ${\mathbb{Z}}$-algebra, finite free as a ${\mathbb{Z}}$-module, with a named additive basis \[ e_0=1,e_1,\ldots,e_{d-1}. \] Its multiplication is given by fixed integral structure constants, and use the computable signed-coordinate presentation $A\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^d$. Suppose the scalar subring ${\mathbb{Z}}\cdot1$ is positive-existentially definable without extra parameters in this expanded language. Then the aid states \[ \operatorname{DPRM}(A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{DPRM}({\mathbb{Z}}). \] "Parameter free" here allows the named basis constants already in the language. The expansion of $A$-equations into signed integral coordinates handles all ring operations, and the scalar predicate restricts the integer witnesses in the reverse interpretation. The transfer does not use a nonnegative-cone definition or DPRM over ${\mathbb{N}}$.

If the center is exactly $Z(A)={\mathbb{Z}}\cdot1$, the scalar predicate is the finite system \[ \bigwedge_{i<d}xe_i=e_ix. \] Thus, in this precise central finite-free setting with named basis, the aid concludes DPRM from the known integer theorem. No assumption that every finite-free commutative extension has center ${\mathbb{Z}}$ is made. The transfer is conditional on DPRM$({\mathbb{Z}})$ and does not itself prove that integer theorem natively.

The supplied literature discussion says Pasten's 2022 framework still obtains DPRM$({\mathbb{Z}})$ through positive-existential bi-interpretation with ${\mathbb{N}}$, and that his characterization obstructs a stronger reading requiring structural separation from ${\mathbb{N}}$. A structural interpretation characterization does not by itself dictate the exposition of every proof. The exact characterization and its hypotheses are not reproduced, so no blanket impossibility of a new ${\mathbb{Z}}$-internal proof is asserted. The original undefined methodological goal is not marked settled by the transfer theorem.

Short English statement

The source asks for a proof method avoiding appeal to the natural numbers. The aid gives a signed-coordinate DPRM equivalence for finite-free integer algebras with a definable scalar copy, including its central special case. This transfers the known integer theorem and does not supply the requested native proof of it.
