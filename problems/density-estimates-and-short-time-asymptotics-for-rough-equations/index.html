Density Estimates and Short-Time Asymptotics for Rough Equations

Definitions and mathematical statement

A real standard fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter $H\in(0,1)$ is a centered Gaussian process $(B_t^H)_{t\geq0}$ with covariance \[ {\mathbb{E}}[B_s^H B_t^H]=\frac12\left(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right). \] This definition fixes terminology only: the source does not specify the admissible Hurst parameters, the dimension or covariance structure of the driving process, or the interpretation of the stochastic equation.

For an equation in the intended class, write $X_t^x\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$ for its solution started at $x$, whenever such a solution is defined. If its time-$t$ law admits a Lebesgue density, denote that density by $p_t(x,y)$, so that \[ \mathbb P(X_t^x\in A)=\int_A p_t(x,y)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} y \] for every Borel set $A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^n$. This notation refers to the law from the specified initial condition and does not assume a Markov transition semigroup.

A uniform density estimate means a bound valid throughout a specified set of time, initial-point and terminal-point parameters, with bounding constants independent of those parameters on that set. A short-time asymptotic expansion describes the behavior of the density as $t\downarrow0$, with an explicitly specified normalization, expansion scale, remainder and parameter regime. The source supplies none of these specifications, and it does not define the intended meaning of sharpness.

Research question supplied by the record. How do sharp uniform density estimates for stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion relate to existing short-time asymptotic expansions for elliptic and hypoelliptic stochastic differential equations?

More precisely, the requested comparison concerns the information that the estimates and expansions provide about the same densities in regimes where their hypotheses overlap. The source does not state a particular implication, equivalence, comparison formula or remainder estimate to be proved. It also does not specify the coefficient assumptions, the ellipticity or hypoellipticity conditions, or whether the expansions being compared use the same driving process.
