Math-4232

Flat Bands on Nonpositively Curved Surfaces

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $(S,g)$ be a Riemannian surface of genus at least $2$, with Gaussian curvature $\kappa_g\leq 0$, and write \[ T^1S=\{v\in TS:g(v,v)=1\}. \] For $v\in T^1S$, let $\gamma_v:I_v\to S$ be its maximal unit-speed geodesic, with $\dot\gamma_v(0)=v$. Define its rank as the dimension of the space of parallel Jacobi fields along $\gamma_v$. On a surface, rank $2$ is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero parallel normal Jacobi field, and hence to $\kappa_g(\gamma_v(t))=0$ for every $t\in I_v$. Thus the set in the supplied problem is \[ K=\{v\in T^1S:\operatorname{rank}(\gamma_v)=2\} =\{v\in T^1S:\kappa_g(\gamma_v(t))=0\text{ for all }t\in I_v\}. \] If the metric is geodesically complete, then $I_v={\mathbb{R}}$ for every $v$.

Requested conclusions. Must $K$ have measure zero, and must it consist of finitely many flat bands of closed geodesics? The natural measure interpretation is the Liouville measure $\mu_L$ on $T^1S$, locally given by Riemannian area on $S$ times angular measure on the unit tangent circle. Under this explicitly provisional interpretation, the first question is \[ \mu_L(K)=0\;? \] A flat band ordinarily refers to a Euclidean product cylinder $({\mathbb{R}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}})\times J$, with metric ${\,\mathrm{d}} s^2+{\,\mathrm{d}} r^2$, mapped isometrically into $S$, whose curves with constant $r$ are closed geodesics. Its corresponding subset of $T^1S$ consists of the unit tangent vectors to those curves in both directions. With a specified convention for such bands, the second question asks whether \[ K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}B_i \qquad\text{for some finite }m\geq 0, \] where each $B_i$ is the corresponding set of tangent vectors for one band.

Unresolved qualifications. The source does not specify the measure, compactness, completeness, orientability, boundary conditions, or metric regularity beyond the Riemannian curvature terminology. It also does not specify whether bands are embedded or immersed, which transverse intervals $J$ are allowed, or whether isolated closed flat geodesics count as zero-width bands. These choices are not imposed here. In particular, the finite-union formulation remains dependent on the intended band convention; the supplied record does not determine a fully qualified conjecture.

Short English statement

For a nonpositively curved surface of genus at least $2$, consider all unit tangent vectors whose geodesics remain entirely in zero Gaussian curvature. The problem asks whether this set has measure zero and is made up of finitely many flat bands of closed geodesics. The intended surface hypotheses, measure, and treatment of isolated closed flat geodesics require clarification.
