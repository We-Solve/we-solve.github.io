Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for bounds on Skewes's numbers of other highly biased prime races, conjecturally or provably. It mentions related investigations in number fields by Fiorilli and Jouve [FJ] and Bailleul [Bai].

For an intended race $\mathcal R$, write $\mathbf p(\mathcal R)$ for its defining parameters and $S(\mathcal R)$ for its Skewes number, once these objects have been specified. The requested task is:

Obtain explicit bounds for $S(\mathcal R)$ in terms of $\mathbf p(\mathcal R)$, stating all hypotheses for each conjectural or proved bound.
This is only a formulation schema. The supplied excerpt does not specify the races, their counting functions, their parameter ranges, the meaning of "highly biased," or the convention defining a Skewes number. In particular, it does not determine the direction of the inequality whose first reversal is sought, whether equality counts as a reversal, the starting threshold, or the treatment of races with no reversal. These choices cannot be supplied silently, so the excerpt does not determine a single precise bounding problem.

The record also contains a separately attributed passage from Alexandre Bailleul, Université Paris--Saclay, concerning Chebyshev's bias over function fields. To explain the distinction in that passage, for a selected finite family of real zero arguments $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m$, linear independence over ${\mathbb{Q}}$ means \[ \left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\theta_j=0,\quad q_1,\ldots,q_m\in{\mathbb{Q}}\right) \Longrightarrow q_1=\cdots=q_m=0. \] Linear independence together with $\pi$ means \[ \left(q_0\pi+\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\theta_j=0,\quad q_0,\ldots,q_m\in{\mathbb{Q}}\right) \Longrightarrow q_0=\cdots=q_m=0. \] For an infinite selected family, each condition is imposed on every finite subfamily. The source does not specify the relevant $L$-functions, which zero arguments are selected, their representatives, or how multiplicities are handled.

The supplied passage reports that, following Cha [Cha], function-field treatments typically assume independence together with $\pi$, in addition to independence of the zero arguments themselves. It reports no known rational linear relations among the arguments themselves, while reporting known instances of an argument in $\pi{\mathbb{Q}}$. It says these instances usually arise from special geometric conditions, such as supersingularity of elliptic curves or Jacobians that are not absolutely simple. These are supplied source qualifications, not independently verified assertions. No explicit question accompanies this passage, and its connection to the Skewes-number bounding request is unresolved.

Short English statement

The main request is to bound the first reversal thresholds called Skewes's numbers for highly biased prime races using the parameters defining those races. The excerpt leaves the races and threshold conventions unspecified. An additional function-field passage discusses independence of zero arguments, including independence together with $\pi$, but supplies no explicit question.
