Math-2268

Problem 2.6 — Schleimer has proved in [42] that each fixed 3-manifold M has a bound on the distances of its Heegaard splittings.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a Heegaard splitting $M=V_1\cup_\Sigma V_2$ in the standard closed oriented setting, let $\mathcal D(V_i)$ be the set of essential curves on $\Sigma$ bounding disks in $V_i$. For genus at least $2$, its Hempel distance is \[ d(\Sigma)=\min\{d_{\mathcal C(\Sigma)}(\alpha,\beta): \alpha\in\mathcal D(V_1),\ \beta\in\mathcal D(V_2)\}, \] where the curve graph has essential-curve vertices and disjointness edges. Low-genus and boundary conventions require separate specification.

The source reports Schleimer [42] proving that each fixed $3$-manifold $M$ has a finite uniform bound $B(M)$ on distances of all its Heegaard splittings. The task is to study this bound and develop an algorithm for computing it. With a finite triangulation as input, a natural precise algorithmic goal is to output an integer $B(M)$ such that \[ \forall\Sigma\text{ a Heegaard splitting of }M,\qquad d(\Sigma)\leq B(M). \] The source does not say whether it requires any computable bound, the particular theorem's bound, or the optimal bound $\sup_\Sigma d(\Sigma)$. It imposes no fixed Heegaard genus in the printed statement.

The following background defines the handlebody subgroup $H_g$ as surface mapping classes extending over a genus-$g$ handlebody. It credits Wajnryb [51] with a finite presentation, reports simplification only in genus two at that time, and describes the induced subgroup of $\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})$ on surface homology as transparent while the group's structure is little understood. The extracted $H_1(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})$ notation is not defined; usual surface homology is ${\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}$. A further handlebody problem is announced but not printed, so none is reconstructed.

Short English statement

Make the reported uniform Heegaard-distance bound for a fixed $3$-manifold algorithmically computable, clarifying whether an arbitrary upper bound or the optimal bound is required.
