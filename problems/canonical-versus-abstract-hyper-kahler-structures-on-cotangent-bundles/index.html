Canonical versus abstract hyper-Kähler structures on cotangent bundles

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
A Kähler manifold has a complex structure $I$ and a compatible Riemannian metric whose associated two-form is closed. A hyper-Kähler structure consists of a Riemannian metric and three parallel compatible complex structures $I,J,K$ satisfying $I^2=J^2=K^2=IJK=-1$. The holomorphic cotangent bundle $T^*M$ also has a canonical holomorphic symplectic form. Compatibility with that canonical structure, completeness and existence merely near the zero section are additional, distinct conditions.

A global flat positive example.
Let $M=\mathbb C^m/\Lambda$, where $\Lambda$ is any full lattice of translations, equipped with the quotient flat Kahler metric. Its holomorphic cotangent bundle is $(\mathbb C^m_z\times\mathbb C^m_w)/\Lambda$, where $\lambda$ acts by $(z,w)\mapsto(z+\lambda,w)$. On the cover take the Euclidean metric and real-linear maps \[I(z,w)=(iz,iw),\qquad J(z,w)=(-\overline w,\overline z),\qquad K=IJ. \] Their derivatives are constant, $I^2=J^2=-I_{\mathrm{id}}$, and $IJ=-JI$. They are orthogonal and parallel, so their metric two-forms are closed. All these tensors are invariant under translations and descend to the cotangent bundle, giving a complete hyper-Kahler metric. Completeness follows because the covering Euclidean metric is complete and the translations act by isometries.
Compatibility check.
In these coordinates the canonical holomorphic symplectic form is $\sum_j dz_j\wedge dw_j$, up to the conventional overall sign for the tautological form. The Euclidean hyper-Kahler structure produces this form up to a constant phase/sign choice of $J,K$, so this particular construction is compatible. It uses the affine transition functions critically: for general base coordinate changes, the conjugate-linear exchange defining $J$ does not descend by the same argument.
