Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record poses a research programme rather than a single quantified conjecture. It does not specify an ambient space, a class of admissible nonconvex families, or particular numerical bounds. The following definitions provide conventional language for its requested conclusions; they do not supply the missing hypotheses.

Let $X$ be a set and $\mathcal F\subseteq 2^X$ a family of subsets. The intersection-generated closure associated with $\mathcal F$ is \[ \operatorname{cl}_{\mathcal F}(A)=\bigcap\{F\in\mathcal F:A\subseteq F\},\qquad A\subseteq X, \] where an intersection over no sets is $X$. More generally, a closure operator $\operatorname{cl}:2^X\to2^X$ satisfies, for all $A,B\subseteq X$, \[ A\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(A),\qquad A\subseteq B\Longrightarrow\operatorname{cl}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(B),\qquad \operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{cl}(A))=\operatorname{cl}(A). \] The source does not specify whether the intended family is $\mathcal F$, its intersection closure, or the closed sets of some separately given operator. Nor does it specify the ambient convexity relative to which sets are nonconvex.

A finite Helly bound $h\geq1$ for $\mathcal F$ means that every finite subfamily $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal F$ satisfies \[ \left[\bigcap\mathcal B\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A\text{ with }1\leq|\mathcal B|\leq h\right] \Longrightarrow\bigcap\mathcal A\ne\varnothing. \] For a closure operator, a Radon conclusion for a finite set $P\subseteq X$ is the existence of a partition $P=P_1\sqcup P_2$ into nonempty parts with \[ \operatorname{cl}(P_1)\cap\operatorname{cl}(P_2)\ne\varnothing. \] For an integer $r\geq2$, a Tverberg conclusion is a partition $P=P_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_r$ into nonempty parts such that \[ \bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{cl}(P_i)\ne\varnothing. \] Corresponding quantitative questions seek cardinality thresholds guaranteeing these conclusions for every admissible finite point set. Whether points may repeat, and whether conclusions must instead hold for sequences or multisets, is unspecified.

A conventional fractional Helly conclusion has an integer parameter $k\geq1$: for each $\alpha\in(0,1]$, there is $\beta\in(0,1]$ such that, for every finite $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal F$ with $n=|\mathcal A|\geq k$, \[ \left|\left\{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A:|\mathcal B|=k,\ \bigcap\mathcal B\ne\varnothing\right\}\right| \geq\alpha\binom nk \Longrightarrow \exists x\in X:\ \left|\{A\in\mathcal A:x\in A\}\right|\geq\beta n. \] A conventional weak $\varepsilon$-net conclusion, for $0<\varepsilon\leq1$ and a finite nonempty $P\subseteq X$, seeks a finite $N\subseteq X$ meeting every $F\in\mathcal F$ with $|F\cap P|\geq\varepsilon|P|$, together with a bound on $|N|$ independent of $|P|$. Here the net points need not belong to $P$. The source does not select this finite uniform formulation over possible measure-based variants or specify the threshold convention.

For integers $p\geq q\geq2$, a finite family $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal F$ with $|\mathcal A|\geq p$ has the $(p,q)$-property if every $p$-member subfamily contains $q$ members with nonempty common intersection. A corresponding piercing conclusion seeks a finite $T\subseteq X$ meeting every member of $\mathcal A$, with a uniform bound on $|T|$.

Source question. Determine hypotheses on nonconvex set systems or their associated closure operators under which Helly, Radon, Tverberg, fractional Helly, weak $\varepsilon$-net, and $(p,q)$-type conclusions hold, and obtain quantitative bounds with explicit parameter dependence. The admissible systems, hypotheses, dependencies, and intended variants remain unspecified. Consequently the supplied question cannot be sharpened to one definite theorem or conjecture without additional information. The supplied background mentions Betti-number and topological-complexity hypotheses, but gives no precise assumptions or bounds to formalize.

Short English statement

This is a broad research question about extending intersection, partition, and piercing theorems to nonconvex families and closure systems. The definitions describe conventional possible targets, but the source leaves the ambient setting, hypotheses, variants, and quantitative bounds unspecified.
