Rigorous Asymptotics for Hamiltonian Cycles in Random Maps

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A map is a finite connected graph cellularly embedded in a closed surface, so that each complementary face is a topological disc. A map is trivalent if every vertex has degree three, and vertex bicolored if its vertices have two colors with adjacent vertices of different colors. A Hamiltonian cycle traverses every vertex exactly once before returning to its starting vertex. Conventions concerning multiple edges, cycle orientation, and counting multiplicities must be specified for an enumeration problem.

The supplied figure caption describes a vertex-bicolored trivalent map of genus zero, with \[ F-E+V=3-3+2=2=2-2g,\qquad g=0. \] It also describes a redrawing displaying a given Hamiltonian cycle and a rooted edge indicated by scissors. These descriptions provide context but do not specify the full class of maps or the rooting convention used in the requested asymptotics.

Requested statement, with unresolved parameters. Establish by rigorous probabilistic or combinatorial methods the predicted asymptotic behavior associated with Hamiltonian cycles in the intended class of random maps. The supplied record refers to "this prediction" without supplying its formula. In particular, it does not specify the counted quantity or random variable, the probability measure, the size parameter and its admissible values, the genus restrictions, the exponential growth constant, the critical exponent, or the strength of the asymptotic assertion. Consequently, a precise quantified asymptotic conjecture cannot be recovered from this record alone. The figure's genus-zero example does not establish a genus-zero restriction for the problem.

Short English statement

The problem asks for a rigorous probabilistic or combinatorial proof of a physics prediction concerning Hamiltonian cycles in random maps. The prediction and the random-map model are missing, so the supplied material does not determine a precise asymptotic statement.
