Classification of Ancient Noncollapsed Mean Curvature Flows

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

An ancient mean-curvature flow of hypersurfaces in Euclidean space is a family $(M_t^n)_{t\in(-\infty,T)}$ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}$, defined for all sufficiently negative times, whose smooth local parametrizations satisfy \[ (\partial_t X)^\perp=-H\nu, \] where $\nu$ is a chosen unit normal and $H$ is the sum of the principal curvatures with the corresponding sign convention. The source's reference to flows in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ is understood as referring to three-dimensional hypersurfaces.

Under the usual mean-convex interpretation, an $\alpha$-noncollapsed flow has $H>0$ and a constant $\alpha>0$, independent of position and time, such that at every $p\in M_t$ there are tangent interior and exterior balls, disjoint from $M_t$ in their interiors and lying on the respective sides, each of radius at least \[ \frac{\alpha}{H(p,t)}. \] Here the interior is the mean-convex side of a region bounded by the hypersurface. This is a conventional interpretation of "noncollapsed"; the supplied excerpt does not specify the precise definition, the global region hypotheses, or whether degenerate cases with $H=0$ are admitted.

For $n\geq2$, write the principal curvatures as $\lambda_1\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_n$. Strict two-convexity means $\lambda_1+\lambda_2>0$; uniform two-convexity means that some fixed $\beta>0$ satisfies $\lambda_1+\lambda_2\geq\beta H$ everywhere in spacetime. The excerpt does not distinguish these conventions.

Classification problem. Classify ancient noncollapsed mean-curvature flows of hypersurfaces in higher-dimensional Euclidean spaces without imposing a two-convexity hypothesis. The absence of this hypothesis does not exclude flows that happen to be two-convex. The requested classification concerns ancient flows generally, without requiring self-similarity or the translating-soliton property.

Unresolved scope. The supplied record does not specify the exact range of dimensions, completeness, embeddedness, properness, connectedness, compactness, admissible singularities, or the equivalence relation for classification. Consequently, it does not determine a single fully specified classification theorem. The conventional definitions above explain the terminology and require confirmation against the complete mathematical source before they can fix the intended class.

The original excerpt describes classification in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ under a self-similarity assumption as a first step. Separately, the supplied dated curation reports a later classification of ancient noncollapsed flows in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$ and labels the general higher-dimensional problem open. These are source-specific historical and curatorial claims, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.

Short English statement

Describe all ancient noncollapsed mean-curvature flows in higher dimensions without assuming two-convexity or self-similarity. The precise dimension range and global geometric hypotheses remain unspecified. The supplied curation labels the general problem open and reports later progress in ${\mathbb{R}}^4$; these assessments require independent verification.
