For normalization, let $M$ have finite hyperbolic volume, and let $\phi_j$ be the eigenfunctions in the intended arithmetic setting with $\int_M|\phi_j|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V=1$, Laplace eigenvalues $\lambda_j\to\infty$. Put \[ \mu_j=|\phi_j|^2\,{\,\mathrm{d}} V,\qquad m={\,\mathrm{d}} V/\operatorname{Vol}(M),\qquad L_j(a)=\int a\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu_j-\int a\,{\,\mathrm{d}} m. \] Both measures have mass one. If the eigenfunctions are instead normalized against $m$, the defining probability must be adjusted consistently. The aid reports Watson's fixed-test estimate under the relevant generalized Lindelof-type bounds implied by GRH, \[ L_j(a)=O_{a,\varepsilon}(\lambda_j^{-1/4+\varepsilon}). \] For spectral parameter $r_j$ with $\lambda_j=1/4+r_j^2$, this corresponds to a power $r_j^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ after renaming $\varepsilon$. The source's "optimal" is a supplied historical description, not a lower-bound theorem proved here. The following "Unconditionally" is not a claim of an unconditional optimal individual rate.

A fixed finite span does inherit a precise bound: if $|L_j(a_i)|\leq C_iR_j$ for finitely many fixed tests $a_1,\ldots,a_N$, then \[ \left|L_j\!\left(\sum_i c_ia_i\right)\right| \leq R_j\sum_i|c_i|C_i. \] This does not give a uniform bound on an entire infinite-dimensional smooth norm ball.

The aid's separate smooth-to-Hölder lemma assumes a compact smooth manifold and a uniform estimate \[ |L_j(a)|\leq A R_j\|a\|_{C^s}\qquad\text{for every smooth }a, \] with $A$ independent of the test and $j$, and discrepancies of probability measures. For $0<\beta\leq1$ with $\beta<s$, in the standard Hölder/smoothing convention, it yields \[ |L_j(f)|\leq C(AR_j)^{\beta/s}\|f\|_{C^\beta}. \] The probability bound $|L_j(g)|\leq2\|g\|_\infty$ is part of this interpolation. The stronger uniform smooth hypothesis cannot be replaced merely by separate fixed-test big-O constants; compactness and the relevant smoothing norms are also required before applying the lemma to a QUE setting.

For a fixed test, an average variance \[ V_N(a)=\frac1N\sum_{j\leq N}|L_j(a)|^2 \] only supplies the corresponding average consequences, for example \[ \frac1N\#\{j\leq N:|L_j(a)|>\eta\}\leq V_N(a)/\eta^2, \qquad\max_{j\leq N}|L_j(a)|\geq\sqrt{V_N(a)}. \] A positive variance asymptotic constrains such averages and maxima, but is not an individual upper rate for every eigenfunction. The exact variance normalization and asymptotic referred to by the report are not supplied. The normalization, finite-span estimate, compact interpolation lemma, and variance audit are conditional logical transfers, not a new proof of the missing Conjecture 39 or an unconditional quantitative QUE theorem.
