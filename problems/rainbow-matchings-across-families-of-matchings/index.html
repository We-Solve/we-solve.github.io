Math-1067

Rainbow Matchings Across Families of Matchings

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$, and let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph. A matching is a set of edges whose sets of incident vertices are pairwise disjoint; its size is its number of edges. Let $(M_i)_{i=1}^{n}$ be an indexed family of matchings in $G$, with $|M_i|=n$ for every $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The matchings are not required to be disjoint from one another. A rainbow matching for this family is a matching $R\subseteq E$ together with an injective map $c:R\to\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that $e\in M_{c(e)}$ for every $e\in R$. Thus an edge belonging to several matchings is assigned just one index in a rainbow matching.

Question. Does every such family in every graph contain a rainbow matching of size $n-1$? Equivalently, must there exist $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $|I|=n-1$ and edges $e_i\in M_i$ for $i\in I$ whose sets of incident vertices are pairwise disjoint? For $n=1$, the required rainbow matching is empty.

Unresolved qualifications. The source imposes no bipartiteness hypothesis or explicit finiteness restriction on $G$. It does not explicitly specify graph conventions concerning directed edges, loops, or parallel edges; the quantification over graphs retains this source limitation and requires review. The family is indexed to make the rainbow condition precise; the source does not explicitly discuss repeated identical matchings.

Short English statement

Given $n$ matchings containing $n$ edges each in a graph, can one choose $n-1$ pairwise vertex-disjoint edges and assign them to distinct matchings that contain them? The supplied curation identifies this as the Aharoni--Berger rainbow-matching conjecture and labels it open; that attribution and status have not been independently verified.
