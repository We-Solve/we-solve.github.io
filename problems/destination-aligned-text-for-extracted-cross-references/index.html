Research attempt: Reference text can disagree with a correct destination

Suppose a figure $q$ is Figure 3 in the full publication and Figure 1 in an extraction. An external link correctly targeting the full publication's $q$ can still have text "Figure 1". The link identity is right, while its number describes a different rendition. Thus correct destination resolution does not by itself determine correct visible reference text.

Model a reference as $(v,q,\ell)$, where $v$ is the destination rendition, $q$ its semantic target, and $\ell$ the visible label. A number-style consistency condition is $\ell=N_v(q)$, using that rendition's display map. A title-style condition can instead be $\ell=T_v(q)$. These are separate policies, not interchangeable constraints. For a fixed policy and a complete map, constructing the label by lookup proves consistency immediately; a subsequent renumbering requires updating the map and every derived label together.

Titles do not generally give unique identity. Let two theorems in the same rendition both be titled "Existence". Title-style labels can be equal while their targets differ. This is not necessarily a broken link, but the text alone cannot distinguish the targets. If the requirement is uniqueness of visible references, a discriminator such as the display number or chapter context must be included. The impossibility follows from the two distinct objects sharing one title: no decoder taking only that title can reconstruct both identities.

These calculations separate three checks: target identity, rendition-relative label correctness, and uniqueness of the displayed text. A reference can pass either of the first two and fail the third, or have a valid destination with a stale label. No source-specified aesthetic rule selects one universal label policy.
