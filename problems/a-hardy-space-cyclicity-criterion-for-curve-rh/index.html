Math-1260

A Hardy-space cyclicity criterion for curve RH

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Brian Conrey's question asks for a function-field analogue of the Nyman--Beurling criterion that could lead to a different proof of RH. The source literally says "Nymon-Barling"; Nyman--Beurling is the attached aid's explicit reconstruction.

For a smooth projective geometrically connected curve $C/{\mathbb{F}}_q$ of genus $g$, let $A_n$ count effective divisors of degree $n$, put $A_{-1}=A_{-2}=0$, and write \[ Z_C(u)=\sum_{n\geq0}A_nu^n=\frac{P_C(u)}{(1-u)(1-qu)}, \qquad P_C(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_i u). \] Curve RH is $|\alpha_i|=\sqrt q$ for all $i$. The aid pole-kills and critically normalizes the divisor sequence: \[ \pi_n=q^{-n/2}\left(A_n-(q+1)A_{n-1}+qA_{n-2}\right), \qquad Q_C(z)=\sum_n\pi_nz^n=P_C(z/\sqrt q). \] This is a polynomial with constant coefficient one. Its zeros $\beta_i=\sqrt q/\alpha_i$ satisfy reciprocity through $Q_C(z)=z^{2g}Q_C(1/z)$.

Let $H^2$ be the Hardy space of analytic series $\sum c_nz^n$ on the unit disk with norm squared $\sum|c_n|^2$. The aid reports the exact shift-cyclicity criterion \[ \mathrm{RH}(C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \overline{\operatorname{span}\{z^jQ_C:j\geq0\}}=H^2. \] More precisely, if $B$ is the finite Blaschke product for the zeros of $Q_C$ strictly inside the disk, with multiplicities, that closed span is $BH^2$, and \[ \operatorname{dist}(1,BH^2)^2 =1-|B(0)|^2 =1-\prod_{|\beta_i|<1}|\beta_i|^2. \] Boundary zeros do not enter the Blaschke product. Reciprocity makes absence of interior zeros equivalent to all zeros being on the unit circle.

For $V_N=\operatorname{span}\{Q_C,zQ_C,\ldots,z^NQ_C\}$, let $G_N$ be the Gram matrix of this ordered list in $H^2$, and put $D_N=\operatorname{dist}(1,V_N)$. The aid reports \[ D_N^2=1-(G_N^{-1})_{00}, \qquad \sqrt{1-D_N^2}\leq|\beta_i|\leq(1-D_N^2)^{-1/2} \quad\text{for every zero }\beta_i. \] The same symmetric annulus applies to normalized reciprocal roots $\alpha_i/\sqrt q$. The coefficients form a finite convolution Gram matrix, so these are finite optimal-approximant calculations.

This supplies an exact criterion and quantitative reduction. It does not independently establish cyclicity or a new proof of Weil RH, which is the additional goal in the original question. The record gives no separate argument proving the required density without known RH.

Short English statement

Construct a Nyman--Beurling-type criterion for function-field RH and seek an independent proof through it. The attached aid reports a pole-killed divisor-sequence Hardy-space criterion and finite Gram-matrix annuli, explicitly distinguishing this reduction from a new proof of RH.
