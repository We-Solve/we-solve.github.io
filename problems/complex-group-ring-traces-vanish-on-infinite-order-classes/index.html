Complex group-ring traces vanish on infinite-order classes

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a proof in the selected manuscript. The Bass trace restriction is claimed for complex group rings of arbitrary discrete groups, implying triviality of scalar idempotents for torsion-free groups over characteristic-zero domains.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For an arbitrary discrete group $G$, let $\operatorname{HS}_G$ be the Hattori--Stallings trace, whose value at a conjugacy class sums the coefficients on that class. For every $x\in K_0(\mathbb C[G])$, \[ |g|=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{HS}_G(x)([g])=0. \] The torsion-free corollary states that, for every $q\ge1$ and idempotent $e\in M_q(\mathbb C[G])$, its identity-coefficient trace equals the rank of its augmentation matrix: \[ \tau_{G,q}(e)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}(\epsilon_{G,q}(e)). \] For such $G$, any commutative unital domain $R$ of characteristic zero satisfies \[ e\in R[G],\ e^2=e\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e\in\{0,1\}. \] The characteristic-zero qualification does not cover positive-characteristic group rings.

Outline of the argument

An idempotent is encoded by finite cyclic path configurations whose weighted deletion relations generate cycles. Exterior differential forms and a cyclic Pfaffian identity detect forbidden conjugacy support; trace-rank arguments then turn the complex trace conclusion into the stated idempotent consequence.
