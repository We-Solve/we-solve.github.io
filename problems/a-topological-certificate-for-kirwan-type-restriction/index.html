Math-1478

A topological certificate for Kirwan-type restriction

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

E. Lerman's Question 5.1 asks for a hyperkähler analogue of the topological mechanisms behind Kirwan surjectivity, allowing maps more general than geometric moment maps. The source invokes [LT97] for a small-resolution calculation using a map that is not a moment map, and [GGK02] for abstract moment maps on $G$-manifolds without assuming a symplectic form. An abstract moment map $\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*$ is described as equivariant and subject to subgroup/fixed-point conditions that are not reproduced in the record. Thus no complete definition or single fully specified general conjecture is supplied.

The excerpt proposes componentwise nondegeneracy: component functions are Morse--Bott and their critical sets correspond to fixed-point sets. It writes $M^G$ for these sets, without stating which components or subgroup conventions are intended. For a component $\mu^\xi=\langle\mu,\xi\rangle$, full-group fixed points and fixed points of the subgroup generated by $\xi$ need not agree; the zero component also needs exclusion in a nontrivial action. These qualifications cannot be silently supplied as the exact [GGK02] definition. The rotating ${\mathbb{R}}^2$ with a constant map is the source's example showing why the abstract axioms alone do not ensure the desired nondegeneracy condition.

The supplied aid gives the following conditional topological certificate. Let $G$ be a compact Lie group, $\Bbbk$ a field, and $\mu$ an equivariant map with zero set $Z$. Assume a finite $G$-equivariant filtration \[ Z=X_0\subset X_1\subset\cdots\subset X_N=M \] whose successive stages are Thom attachments over $G$-spaces $C_j$, with negative bundles $\nu_j$ of rank $\lambda_j$. Require the Thom/excision identification for each pair, with the restriction to the zero section identified with multiplication by the equivariant Euler class. Assume $\nu_j$ is equivariantly $\Bbbk$-oriented and \[ e_G(\nu_j):H_G^{q-\lambda_j}(C_j;\Bbbk)\longrightarrow H_G^q(C_j;\Bbbk) \] is injective for every $q$. Here $H_G^*(X;\Bbbk)=H^*(EG\times_GX;\Bbbk)$. The reported stagewise conclusion is \[ 0\longrightarrow H_G^{q-\lambda_j}(C_j;\Bbbk) \longrightarrow H_G^q(X_j;\Bbbk) \longrightarrow H_G^q(X_{j-1};\Bbbk)\longrightarrow0, \] and hence surjectivity of $H_G^*(M;\Bbbk)\to H_G^*(Z;\Bbbk)$.

For a hyperkähler moment map $\boldsymbol\mu=(\mu_I,\mu_J,\mu_K):M\to\mathfrak g^*\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^3$, choose an invariant norm and consider $F=\|\boldsymbol\mu\|^2$, with $Z=\boldsymbol\mu^{-1}(0)$. The aid states the certificate as a conditional Kirwan-type criterion when the required finite filtration comes from $F$. Its two layers are distinct: construct the filtration with its Thom properties, then check orientations and Euler-class injectivity over the chosen field. It does not assert these hypotheses for every hyperkähler or abstract moment map. Identifying Borel cohomology of $Z$ with ordinary cohomology of a geometric quotient requires additional action hypotheses not given here.

The aid's obstruction to a simpler criterion is a shifted-height map on the rotating sphere. An explicit representative is $S^2\subset{\mathbb{R}}^3$, with $S^1$ rotating about the vertical axis, height $h(x,y,z)=z$, and $\mu=h-1$. Every nonzero scalar component of this one-dimensional map is Morse with critical set equal to the two poles. Nevertheless $F=(h-1)^2$ has an isolated critical minimum at the north pole with vanishing Hessian and is not Morse--Bott. Thus componentwise nondegeneracy does not supply the missing norm-square or filtration hypothesis. This illustrates the reported obstruction, not a counterexample to surjectivity under the certificate's full assumptions.

Short English statement

The source asks which topological features of moment maps can yield hyperkähler Kirwan-type surjectivity. The aid separates a finite Thom-filtration hypothesis from coefficient-sensitive orientation and Euler-class checks. A shifted height function shows why nondegeneracy of individual components cannot replace a hypothesis on the norm-square.
