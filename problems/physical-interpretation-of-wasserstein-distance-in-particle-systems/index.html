Math-15651

Physical Interpretation of Wasserstein Distance in Particle Systems

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a standard mathematical framework in which to read the question, let $(X,d)$ be a complete separable metric space and fix $x_0\in X$. Write \[ \mathcal P_2(X)=\left\{\mu:\mu\text{ is a Borel probability measure on }X,\quad \int_X d(x,x_0)^2\,\mathrm d\mu(x)<\infty\right\}. \] For $\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_2(X)$, let $\Pi(\mu,\nu)$ denote the Borel probability measures on $X\times X$ with first marginal $\mu$ and second marginal $\nu$. The $2$-Wasserstein distance is \[ W_2(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^2\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/2}. \] If $N\geq1$ particles have positions $X_1(t),\ldots,X_N(t)\in X$ at time $t$, their empirical probability measure is \[ \mu_t^N=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{X_i(t)}, \] where $\delta_x$ is the unit point mass at $x$. For finite particle positions this measure belongs to $\mathcal P_2(X)$. These definitions provide a conventional framework; the supplied source does not prescribe the state space or metric. In the usual Euclidean realization of its example, the particle positions are independent Brownian motions, but the dimension, initial conditions, and boundary conditions are unspecified.

Source question. What physical meaning can be assigned to the $2$-Wasserstein distance for particle systems, in particular for empirical measures of many independent Brownian motions?

The supplied record poses an interpretive research question, rather than a quantified conjecture. It does not identify the two measures to be compared, a physical observable or operational meaning to be recovered, or a limiting regime for the number of particles or the observation times. Thus no unique theorem-level target or criterion for a satisfactory physical interpretation can be extracted without additional specifications.

Short English statement

The question asks how to understand the $2$-Wasserstein distance physically when probability measures describe particle configurations, especially configurations of many independent Brownian particles. The source leaves the comparison measures and the intended physical interpretation unspecified, so this draft preserves the question as an interpretive research problem.
