Math-4191

Dubner's conjecture

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

A prime $p$ is a twin member if at least one of $p-2$ and $p+2$ is prime. The two chosen summands need not come from the same twin pair and need not be distinct.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For every even integer $N>4208$, do there exist primes $p,q$ with $N=p+q$ such that each of $p$ and $q$ is a twin member? [S2]

Short English statement

Can every even integer above four thousand two hundred eight be expressed as a sum of two primes, each belonging to some twin-prime pair?
