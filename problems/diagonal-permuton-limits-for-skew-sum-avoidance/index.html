Math-3894

Diagonal Permuton Limits for Skew-Sum Avoidance

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For an integer $m\geq 1$, write $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and let $S_m$ be the set of permutations of $[m]$. Fix $\alpha\in S_m$. Its skew sum with the one-entry permutation is $\tau=\alpha\ominus 1\in S_{m+1}$, defined by \[ \tau(i)=\alpha(i)+1\quad(1\leq i\leq m),\qquad \tau(m+1)=1. \] A permutation $\pi\in S_n$ contains a pattern $\sigma\in S_k$ if there are indices $1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n$ such that \[ \pi(i_a)<\pi(i_b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma(a)<\sigma(b) \qquad(1\leq a,b\leq k). \] It avoids $\sigma$ if no such indices exist. Set \[ \operatorname{Av}_n(\sigma)=\{\pi\in S_n:\pi\text{ avoids }\sigma\}, \qquad \operatorname{Av}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{Av}_n(\sigma). \] For $\tau=\alpha\ominus1$, these finite avoidance sets are nonempty: the increasing permutation avoids $\tau$.

A permuton is a Borel probability measure on $[0,1]^2$ whose two coordinate marginals are Lebesgue measure on $[0,1]$. The diagonal permuton $\mu_{\mathrm{diag}}$ is the distribution of $(U,U)$, where $U$ is uniform on $[0,1]$; equivalently, \[ \int f\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{diag}}=\int_0^1 f(t,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t \quad\text{for every }f\in C([0,1]^2). \] For $\pi\in S_n$, define its empirical plot measure by \[ \nu_\pi=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{(i/n,\pi(i)/n)}. \] Its finite marginals are discrete uniform measures; its weak limits as $n\to\infty$ have uniform marginals and hence are permutons.

Conjecture attributed in the supplied record to David Bevan. For every integer $m\geq1$ and every fixed $\alpha\in S_m$, let $\Pi_n$ be uniformly distributed on $\operatorname{Av}_n(\alpha\ominus1)$. Then \[ \nu_{\Pi_n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{weakly\ in\ probability}}\mu_{\mathrm{diag}}. \] Explicitly, for every $f\in C([0,1]^2)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb P\!\left( \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f\!\left(\frac{i}{n},\frac{\Pi_n(i)}{n}\right) -\int_0^1 f(t,t)\,{\,\mathrm{d}} t\right|>\varepsilon \right)=0. \] The uniform sampling law and convergence in probability make explicit the interpretation suggested by the source's uniform-random rare-region discussion. The conjecture sentence itself does not explicitly specify either convention, so this interpretation requires human confirmation.

Supplied rare-region background and qualifications. For the relevant pattern $\tau=\alpha\ominus1$, write $\mathbb P_n^\tau$ for the uniform probability measure on $\operatorname{Av}_n(\tau)$. A point $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$ is $\tau$-rare if, for every sequence $(i_n,j_n)\in[n]^2$ with \[ (i_n/n,j_n/n)\longrightarrow(x,y), \] there exists $r\in(0,1)$, possibly depending on that sequence, such that \[ \mathbb P_n^\tau\{\pi(i_n)=j_n\}=o(r^n) \qquad(n\to\infty). \] Let $R_\tau\subseteq[0,1]^2$ be the set of these points. The excerpt introduces this definition for a general pattern $\tau\in S_k$; its probability law presupposes that the relevant avoidance sets are nonempty. That condition holds for the family in the conjecture.

The excerpt attributes to Atapour and Madras, reference [1, Theorem 8.1], and to Madras and Yıldırım, reference [2, Theorem 1], the assertion that, whenever $\tau=\alpha\ominus1$ with $\alpha$ nonempty, $(1,0)$ has an open neighborhood contained in $R_\tau$. At this boundary point, the neighborhood is naturally read relative to $[0,1]^2$.

The excerpt also attributes to Madras and Yıldırım, reference [2, Theorem 17], a convex-hull assertion printed as \[ (x,y)\in[0,1]\setminus R_\tau \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{conv}\{(x,y),(0,0),(0,1)\} \subseteq[0,1]\setminus R_\tau. \] This is retained only as a report of the supplied wording. Its ambient set is dimensionally inconsistent with the definition of $R_\tau$, and both the intended ambient square and the listed vertices require checking against the original theorem. No corrected version is assumed here, and this background assertion is not a hypothesis of the conjecture.

Short English statement

For each fixed nonempty permutation $\alpha$, choose a uniformly random permutation of size $n$ avoiding $\alpha\ominus1$. The conjecture says that its rescaled plot converges in probability to uniform mass on the increasing diagonal. This states the natural uniform-random interpretation of the supplied conjecture; its convergence convention and a malformed auxiliary rare-region theorem require review.
