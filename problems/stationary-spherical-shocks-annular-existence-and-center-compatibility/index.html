Stationary spherical shocks: annular existence and center compatibility

Definitions and mathematical statement

The source asks whether the preceding barotropic flow model has stationary spherical viscous or inviscid shocks; how its mass and force sources $s,f$ should be chosen; whether either source may vanish identically; how curvature affects stability and nearby perturbed spherical fronts; and whether stability can change at a critical radius $r_*$, with larger radii more stable. It notes that curvature does not enter Majda's short-time analysis. The extracted text omits the preceding equations. The supplied verification field identifies dimensions $d=2,3$, $m=d-1$, radial density $\rho(r,t)$, and velocity $u(r,t)x/r$, but does not actually reproduce the full displayed PDE, viscous stress, or force convention.

The aid's explicit stationary inviscid reduction uses the normalization \[ (\rho u)'+\frac mr\rho u=s(r),\qquad (\rho u^2+p(\rho))'+\frac mr\rho u^2=F(r), \] where $F$ denotes the radial momentum source in this normalization. Its relation to the source's $f$ requires the omitted equation. Assume $\rho>0$, $p\in C^2$, and $c(\rho)^2=p'(\rho)>0$. With $q=\rho u$, source-free mass balance on an annulus gives \[ s=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q(r)=Jr^{-m}. \] If also $F=0$, the radial momentum equation becomes \[ (c(\rho)^2-u^2)\rho'=\frac{m\rho u^2}{r}. \] This equation is locally regular away from the center and from sonic states.

The local realization assertion is: fix a nonsonic stationary planar Lax shock pair $(\rho_-,u_-),(\rho_+,u_+)$ for this pressure law, with common nonzero flux and momentum flux \[ \rho_-u_-=\rho_+u_+=j,\qquad \rho_-u_-^2+p(\rho_-)=\rho_+u_+^2+p(\rho_+), \qquad u_\pm^2\ne c(\rho_\pm)^2. \] For every prescribed $R>0$, set $J=R^mj$. Solving the radial ODE on each side with these traces at $r=R$ produces a local stationary source-free spherical shock, retaining the strict Lax condition near the interface. This is local annular existence. It does not fix one global flux while varying $R$, impose remote boundary conditions, or establish viscous profiles or stability at all radii.

On a full ball a regular center instead imposes $\lim_{r\downarrow0}r^m q(r)=0$. Integration yields the exact compatibility condition \[ q(r)=r^{-m}\int_0^r\tau^m s(\tau)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\tau, \qquad \int_0^R\tau^m s(\tau)\,{\,\mathrm{d}}\tau=R^mj \] for a front with mass-flux trace $j$ at $R$. In particular $s\equiv0$ excludes a nonzero-flux stationary front with a regular center. This distinguishes a full ball from an annulus and does not assert nonexistence of all possible zero-flux configurations.

The aid reports post-workshop constructions of source-free stationary spherical Euler shocks and Navier--Stokes profiles on bounded annuli, settling that existence issue in that geometry. No precise pressure-law, boundary, viscosity hypotheses, or citations are supplied for those constructions. It explicitly does not settle the spherical stability or critical-radius questions. A stability transition must be posed for a specified family with fixed physical and boundary data, perturbation class, and norm; the local covariance $J=R^mj$ is not itself evidence for or against that transition.

Short English statement

The aid separates annular stationary-shock existence from the regular-center problem and from stability. A fixed nonsonic planar shock pair extends locally to any positive spherical radius when the radial flux is adjusted. A regular center instead forces an exact source integral, excluding nonzero flux when the mass source vanishes.
