Parabolic stiffness, the solved moduli example, and finite root/factor diagnostics

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $H$ act continuously on a space $X$, with a specified subgroup $P\leq H$ and standard Borel/Radon measure conventions. Call the action $P$-stiff when every finite positive $P$-invariant Borel measure is $H$-invariant. The source asks for geometric conditions ensuring stiffness and suggests the $SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ action on quadratic-differential moduli spaces. It does not define $H,P$ or the full category of geometric actions in this isolated record.

For the supplied motivating case, take $H=SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ and $P=AN$, the connected upper-triangular subgroup with positive diagonal. The aid attributes to Eskin--Mirzakhani that every ergodic $P$-invariant probability on a unit-area Abelian-differential stratum is $SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$-invariant and affine in period coordinates. It reports the corresponding quadratic-stratum invariance via the orientation double cover, with area normalization and deck-involution data treated compatibly. Ergodic decomposition then gives $H$-invariance for arbitrary finite nonergodic measures in those stated strata. The exact theorem references and transfer details are not supplied; this is not an unqualified assertion about every unspecified geometric action or moduli compactification.

The aid's finite-root diagnostic can be stated for a connected split semisimple real Lie group $H$, a fixed simple-root system $\Delta$, and a standard parabolic $P$ whose Levi simple roots are $\Theta\subset\Delta$. If a finite measure $\mu$ is $P$-invariant and additionally invariant under each negative simple-root subgroup $U_{-\alpha}$ for $\alpha\in\Delta\setminus\Theta$, then it is $H$-invariant, since these groups together generate $H$. The split/connected hypothesis makes this formulation explicit; other real forms require the corresponding generation theorem. This is a sufficient measure-symmetry test, not a claim that $P$-invariance automatically supplies those extra symmetries.

For $SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$ the aid gives the sharper test: if $\mu$ is $AN$-invariant and $g_*\mu=\mu$ for one $g\notin N_H(AN)$, then $\mu$ is $H$-invariant. The group generated by $AN$ and such a symmetry is dense in $H$, and the stabilizer of a finite measure is closed for the continuous action. A central element or a different component of the normalizer does not suffice.

The factor observation has an essential compactness condition. If $P$ is amenable and $\pi:X\twoheadrightarrow Y$ is a continuous equivariant map of compact Hausdorff $H$-spaces, every $P$-invariant probability on $Y$ has a $P$-invariant lift: the set of lifts is nonempty compact convex, so amenability supplies a fixed point. Consequently stiffness of $X$ implies stiffness of $Y$. The nontrivial flag space $H/P$ is not stiff, because its point mass at $eP$ is $P$-invariant and not $H$-invariant. Hence a compact stiff $H$-space has no such factor when $P$ is amenable. Arbitrary noncompact factor maps need not have the compact lift set, and general parabolic subgroups need not be amenable. These diagnostics do not classify all stiff actions.

Short English statement

The supplied literature reports stiffness for the stated unit-area differential strata. The additional results are diagnostics: extra negative-root symmetries force full invariance, one symmetry outside the $AN$ normalizer suffices for $SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})$, and amenability lifts measures through compact factors. The flag-space obstruction uses those compactness and amenability assumptions and is not an unrestricted factor theorem.
