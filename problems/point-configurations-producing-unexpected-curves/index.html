Point Configurations Producing Unexpected Curves

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied question asks for a characterization of finite point configurations producing unexpected curves. It does not specify the ambient space, ground field, characteristic, curve degree, or fat-point multiplicity. The following gives a precise conditional plane-curve formulation; these additional parameters are not asserted to be hypotheses supplied by the source.

Suppose the intended ambient space is ${\mathbb{P}}^2_k$, where $k$ is a specified algebraically closed field. Let $Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^2_k$ be a finite set of distinct points, regarded as a reduced subscheme, and let $\mathcal I_Z$ be its ideal sheaf. For integers $d\geq 0$ and $m\geq 1$, and a point $P\notin Z$, define the fat point $mP$ by the ideal sheaf $\mathcal I_P^m$. Set \[ V_d(Z)=H^0\left({\mathbb{P}}^2_k,\mathcal I_Z(d)\right),\qquad V_{d,m}(Z,P)=H^0\left({\mathbb{P}}^2_k,(\mathcal I_Z\cap\mathcal I_P^m)(d)\right). \] Thus $V_{d,m}(Z,P)$ consists of degree-$d$ homogeneous forms vanishing on $Z$ and to order at least $m$ at $P$. The restriction map \[ \rho_{Z,P}^{d,m}:V_d(Z)\longrightarrow H^0\left(mP,\mathcal O_{mP}(d)\right) \] has kernel $V_{d,m}(Z,P)$, and its target has dimension $\binom{m+1}{2}$. The expected vector-space dimension is therefore \[ e_{d,m}(Z)=\max\left\{0,\dim_k V_d(Z)-\binom{m+1}{2}\right\}. \] For these specified parameters, define the unexpectedness condition by \[ \dim_k V_{d,m}(Z,P)>e_{d,m}(Z) \quad\text{for general }P\in{\mathbb{P}}^2_k\setminus Z. \] Here "general" means that the inequality holds for every $P$ in some nonempty Zariski-open subset of ${\mathbb{P}}^2_k\setminus Z$. Equivalently, the restriction map fails to have maximal rank for general $P$.

Requested characterization, subject to unresolved scope. Characterize the finite point sets $Z$ satisfying this unexpectedness condition in the ambient setting and degree--multiplicity regime intended by the source. The supplied record does not determine whether $d,m$ are fixed, whether existence of a pair $(d,m)$ is intended, or whether a prescribed relation between them is required. It also does not establish that the conditional plane-curve setting above is the complete intended setting. Consequently, a unique fully specified classification problem cannot be recovered from the supplied wording alone.

Short English statement

Characterize finite point configurations for which requiring a curve through the configuration to have a specified multiplicity at a general additional point leaves more curves than the expected dimension predicts. A precise plane-curve interpretation is given conditionally; the source leaves the ground field, ambient space, and degree--multiplicity quantifiers unspecified.
