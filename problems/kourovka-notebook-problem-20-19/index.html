Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.19

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For subgroups $K\leq H$, let $[H:K]$ denote the number of left cosets of $K$ in $H$. A subgroup $H\leq G$ is commensurated in $G$ if \[ \forall g\in G,\qquad [H:H\cap gHg^{-1}]<\infty. \] A free group is non-abelian if it has a free basis with at least two elements. The source imposes no explicit finite-rank hypothesis. Here a hyperbolic group is understood in the usual word-hyperbolic sense: it has a finite generating set whose Cayley graph has uniformly thin geodesic triangles. It is non-elementary if it is neither finite nor virtually cyclic, where virtually cyclic means having a cyclic subgroup of finite index.

Consider separately the classes \[ \mathcal C_1=\{G:G\text{ is a non-abelian free group}\},\qquad \mathcal C_2=\{G:G\text{ is a non-elementary hyperbolic group}\}. \] For each $i\in\{1,2\}$, does there exist $G\in\mathcal C_i$ containing subgroups $A,B\leq G$ such that \[ |A|=|B|=\infty,\qquad A\cap B=\{1_G\}, \] and \[ \forall g\in G,\qquad [A:A\cap gAg^{-1}]<\infty \quad\text{and}\quad [B:B\cap gBg^{-1}]<\infty? \] The supplied source reports that the answer is negative if either $A$ or $B$ is normal in $G$. This reported qualification is retained without independent verification. The source does not specify whether its free-group question is intended to be restricted to finite rank.

Short English statement

Can a non-abelian free group, or a non-elementary hyperbolic group, contain two infinite commensurated subgroups whose intersection is trivial? The source reports that this cannot happen when either subgroup is normal. Its registry status is supplied as open.
