Problem 2.12 — Let r be a positive integer and let Γr be the (normal) subgroup of Modg generated by the rth powers of all Dehn twists.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

For a positive integer $r$, let \[ \Gamma_r=\langle T_c^r:c\text{ is a simple closed curve defining a Dehn twist in }\operatorname{Mod}_g\rangle. \] This is the source's normal subgroup generated by the $r$-th powers of all Dehn twists, including all curve types intended by that surface convention. For a fixed nonseparating curve $a$, set \[ N_r=\langle\!\langle T_a^r\rangle\!\rangle_{\operatorname{Mod}_g}. \] The genus range and surface convention are not stated in the excerpt.

Problem 2.12. Is $[\operatorname{Mod}_g:\Gamma_r]=\infty$? Determine the answer for the intended genera and positive exponents, retaining the known exceptions reported in the source. It attributes to Humphries [17] that $N_r$ has finite index for $r=2$ in every intended genus and for $(g,r)=(2,3)$. Since $N_r\subseteq\Gamma_r$, these give negative answers to the infinite-index question in those cases.

It further states, citing [17] and [10], that $N_r$ has infinite index if $r\notin\{2,3,4,6,8,12\}$. This statement requires an omitted range qualification: as written it includes $r=1$, whereas in the usual mapping-class setting nonseparating twists normally generate the group. The draft preserves the reported exclusion set but does not treat that unrestricted claim as verified. Also, infinite index of the smaller subgroup $N_r$ alone does not imply infinite index of $\Gamma_r$; the two subgroups must not be identified.

The extraction then gives separate context: $\operatorname{PMod}_{g,p}^{b}$ consists of mapping classes fixing each puncture, and for $g\geq4$ it states \[ H_2(\operatorname{PMod}_{g,p}^{b};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{p+1}, \] citing [13], [39], [33], with the warning that [33] interchanges the subscript $p$ and superscript $b$. The exact boundary/puncture conventions and the scope of that homology statement must be checked. This is adjacent context, not a further condition on the power-subgroup question.

Short English statement

Ask when the subgroup generated by all $r$-th powers of Dehn twists has infinite index. The source gives finite-index exceptions via the normal closure of one nonseparating twist, but its other normal-closure range has a missing $r=1$ qualification and does not itself settle the larger subgroup.
