Math-1312

A multiplier-repetition obstruction for long prime chains

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

A prime chain is a finite sequence $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ of primes with \[ p_j=m_jp_{j-1}+1,\qquad m_j\in{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\},\quad2\leq j\leq k. \] It is increasing. Let $K(x)$ be the maximum chain length with $p_k\leq x$.

Problem 6 (K.B. Ford). The elementary bound is $K(x)=O(\log x)$. Prove or disprove \[ K(x)=o(\log x)\qquad(x\to\infty). \] This is a uniform assertion over all chains, not just a selected chain family.

Related problem in the source. H.L. Montgomery recalls an Erdős question asking whether only finitely many integers $n$ have $n-2^j$ prime for every positive integer $j$ with $2^j<n$. R.C. Vaughan recalls Erdős's belief that $n=105$ is largest. The extracted record prints flattened "$2j$" rather than an explicit exponent and says "all integers" without a lower bound. The displayed power-of-two/positive-index reading is an explicit reconstruction consistent with the stated example; if $j=0$ were included, $105-1=104$ would already contradict that example. The source's recollection is not a proof of finiteness or maximality.

Reported multiplier-repetition bound. For a chain with $k\geq3$, put $n=k-2$ and \[ R=\max_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\#\{j\in\{3,\ldots,k\}:m_j=m\}. \] All these tail multipliers are positive even integers, since $p_2,\ldots,p_k$ are odd. The aid asserts the exact inequality \[ p_k>\left(\frac{2n}{eR}\right)^n. \] Consequently, along any family with $k\to\infty$ and $R=o(k)$ one has $k=o(\log p_k)$. In particular, if $R$ is bounded independently of the chain, \[ k\leq(1+o(1))\frac{\log p_k}{\log\log p_k}. \] These are conditional restrictions on repetition in the tail; they do not bound $R$ for all prime chains.

Conversely, fix $\eta>0$ and suppose an unbounded family of chains satisfies $n\geq\eta\log p_k$. The report says there are constants $c_\eta>0$ and $B_\eta<\infty$ such that each such chain has a positive even multiplier $m\leq B_\eta$ occurring at least $c_\eta n$ times among $m_3,\ldots,m_k$. Passing to a subsequence makes that multiplier fixed. For every fixed word length, the aid also reports an infinitely recurring fixed word of bounded even multipliers after passage to a subsequence. The word and bound may depend on its length and $\eta$; this does not assert arbitrarily long consecutive runs of one multiplier.

Thus a linear-logarithmic counterexample to Ford's target would require structured multiplier reuse. The report does not rule out that reuse, settle $K(x)=o(\log x)$, or resolve the related Erdős problem.

Short English statement

Ford asks whether every prime chain formed by repeatedly taking a prime one above a multiple of the previous prime has sublogarithmic length. The aid proves a sharp restriction in terms of multiplier repetition: low repetition forces short chains, while a hypothetical long family must repeatedly use bounded even multipliers. The separate powers-of-two question is retained with its extraction ambiguity.
