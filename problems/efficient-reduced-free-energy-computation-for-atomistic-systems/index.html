Definitions and conventions.
$X_r\in\mathbb R^{dN_r}$ are retained representative-atom coordinates and $X_c\in\mathbb R^{dN_c}$ are the coordinates to be integrated out; $N=N_r+N_c$ and $\beta>0$ is inverse temperature. $V$ is the atomistic potential energy and the integral is conditional on the fixed retained coordinates. The normalized free energy $A(X_r)/N_c$ is measured per eliminated particle. Exact Gaussian elimination with retained atoms.
Restrict to a quadratic potential in the eliminated vector $y\in\mathbb R^n$, where $n=dN_c$: \[ V(x,y)=\tfrac12y^TKy+b(x)^Ty+c(x),\qquad K=K^T>0. \] Completing the square and changing variables gives \[ A(x)=c(x)-\tfrac12b(x)^TK^{-1}b(x) +\frac1{2\beta}\log\det K-\frac n{2\beta}\log(2\pi/\beta). \] The positivity hypothesis is essential. If $K$ has a zero mode with no confining higher-order term, the integral along that direction diverges, irrespective of whether the linear coefficient there vanishes.
A precise computational subcase.
For a fixed-block-bandwidth positive-definite $K$ with block size $d$ fixed, band Cholesky elimination needs a number of arithmetic operations proportional to $N_c$: each row updates only a bounded number of blocks. It obtains $\log\det K$ from twice the sum of logarithms of its positive Cholesky diagonal and obtains the quadratic form by triangular solves. This is an exact-arithmetic operation count, not a uniform floating-point error theorem; ill-conditioning can demand additional precision.
Beyond the harmonic case.
The formula handles multidimensional atom positions and makes the confinement convention explicit. It supplies no uniform thermodynamic limit for arbitrary sequences $K,b,c$, and no efficient procedure for generic nonlinear atomistic potentials. The source already has chain transfer-operator methods and closely repeats 30001115. The first gap is a controlled nonlinear approximation whose error per removed atom and computational cost remain bounded at the specified tolerance, with the retained-coordinate scaling fixed.
