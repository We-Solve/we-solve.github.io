Math-3449

Turán Densities of Hypergraph Daisies

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $r\ge 2$ and $n\ge r$ be integers, let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and write $\binom{X}{k}$ for the family of all $k$-element subsets of a finite set $X$. For disjoint sets $P,Q$ with $|P|=r-2$ and $|Q|=4$, define the $r$-daisy by \[ D_r(P,Q)=\{P\cup B:B\in\tbinom{Q}{2}\}. \] This is an $r$-uniform hypergraph with six edges on $r+2$ vertices; $D_r$ denotes its isomorphism type. In particular, $P=\varnothing$ is allowed when $r=2$.

A family $\mathcal A\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}$ is $D_r$-free if it contains no copy of these six edges. Explicitly, for every $P\in\binom{[n]}{r-2}$ and every $Q\in\binom{[n]\setminus P}{4}$, there is a $B\in\binom{Q}{2}$ such that $P\cup B\notin\mathcal A$. Containment here is ordinary hypergraph containment, with no induced-subhypergraph requirement.

Define \[ \operatorname{ex}(n,D_r)=\max\left\{|\mathcal A|:\mathcal A\subseteq\tbinom{[n]}{r},\ \mathcal A\text{ is }D_r\text{-free}\right\}. \] The problem asks to determine how large such a family can be, equivalently to determine $\operatorname{ex}(n,D_r)$ as a function of $n$ and $r$. When $r\le n<r+2$, the forbidden configuration cannot fit on the ground set, so the condition is vacuous and the maximum is $\binom nr$.

For the density questions mentioned in the supplied title and literature assessment, the associated Turán density is \[ \pi(D_r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{ex}(n,D_r)}{\binom nr}, \] where $r$ is fixed in this limit. The source question does not specify whether an exact finite-$n$ formula, an asymptotic determination, or bounds are the intended target.

The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and attributes to Ellis, Ivan, and Leader a disproof of the conjecture $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(D_r)=0$. It also reports that the extremal-function and exact-density questions, including the value of $\pi(D_3)$, remain open. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified assertions in this draft.

Short English statement

For each integer $r\ge2$, an $r$-daisy consists of all six ways to add two elements of a fixed four-element set to a disjoint fixed $(r-2)$-element set. Determine the maximum number of $r$-element subsets of an $n$-element set that can be chosen without containing all six members of any such daisy. The source also discusses the associated asymptotic densities; its partially solved status is supplied dated curation.
