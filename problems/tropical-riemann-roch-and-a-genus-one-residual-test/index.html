Tropical Riemann--Roch and a genus-one residual test

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

David Eisenbud's A.7 asks what Riemann--Roch, nodal plane curves, and residual intersection should mean for tropical plane curves. It suggests either line bundles/coherent sheaves or a geometric Brill--Noether approach. Classically, at a common point $x$ of curves $A,B,C$, with local ring $\mathcal O_x$ on $A$ and local equations $f_B,f_C$, it records \[ ((f_B):f_C)=\{g\in\mathcal O_x:gf_C\in(f_B)\} \] as the residual ideal. Proper intersections and any containment needed for literal effective subtraction are not specified. At a smooth curve point multiplicities describe intersection divisors, but subtracting a larger multiplicity need not produce an effective residual cycle.

The source literally says "a Gorenstein ring for any smooth curve, no matter how singular". Its verification identifies this as internally contradictory and suggests replacing "smooth" by "plane", since a local plane-curve ring is a hypersurface quotient of a regular local ring. That replacement is an unverified inference, not adopted as an exact source correction. The record's clean-statement field contains this correction discussion rather than the actual full question.

The aid addresses the divisor question on a compact metric graph, not a tropical local colon-ideal construction. A divisor is a finite integral sum $D=\sum n_p[p]$, effective when every $n_p\ge0$, with $\deg D=\sum n_p$. Linear equivalence is generated by principal divisors of continuous piecewise-linear functions with integer slopes. Its rank $r(D)$ is $-1$ if its class has no effective representative; otherwise it is the greatest $r\ge0$ such that $D-E$ is equivalent to an effective divisor for every effective $E$ of degree $r$. In the metric-graph Riemann--Roch framework, \[ r(D)-r(K-D)=\deg D+1-g, \qquad K=\sum_v(\operatorname{val}(v)-2)[v]. \] This states the framework invoked by the aid, with valence-two subdivision immaterial.

For the compact circle skeleton $\Gamma$ of a smooth tropical cubic, let its circumference be $L>0$, choose an orientation and origin, and write $s(p)\in{\mathbb{R}}/L{\mathbb{Z}}$ for arc-length coordinate. For a degree-zero divisor define \[ a(D)=\sum_p n_p s(p)\in{\mathbb{R}}/L{\mathbb{Z}}. \] The aid gives the exact genus-one rule \[ r(D)= \begin{cases} -1,&\deg D<0,\\ \deg D-1,&\deg D>0,\\ 0,&\deg D=0\text{ and }a(D)=0,\\ -1,&\deg D=0\text{ and }a(D)\ne0. \end{cases} \] For degree zero, $a(D)=0$ is also the test for principality; it is independent of the chosen origin, and reversing orientation does not change its vanishing.

For stable-intersection divisors on the tropical cubic, restrict the following test to divisors $D_B,D_C$ on $\Gamma$, or explicitly push them to $\Gamma$ by a specified skeleton retraction. Put $D=D_B-D_C$. Three different notions must be separated:

  1. A literal effective residual cycle requires coefficientwise $D_B\ge D_C$.
  2. An effective representative of the class exists exactly when $r(D)\ge0$, as given by the displayed degree and coordinate test.
  3. A principal difference requires $\deg D=0$ and $a(D)=0$.
For degree zero an effective divisor itself must be zero, whereas a nonzero signed principal divisor can represent the effective zero class. The aid reports a length-13 example of this distinction, but does not give its points or coefficients. Retraction and divisor-class information do not construct a local tropical scheme or its colon ideal. The treatment of ordinary nodes, arbitrary singular tropical plane curves, and coherent-sheaf residuation therefore remains outside this reported genus-one partial result.

Short English statement

The question seeks tropical Riemann--Roch and a meaningful residual intersection for tropical plane curves. The aid gives an exact divisor-class test on a metric circle, distinguishing literal effectivity, effectivity up to linear equivalence, and principality. It does not construct a tropical analogue of the classical local colon ideal, and the proposed correction of a contradictory source sentence remains unverified.
