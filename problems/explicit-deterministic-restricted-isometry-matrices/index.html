Literature and context check

The primary report's compressed-sensing discussion asks for deterministic small restricted-isometry constants in compressed measurement regimes. The target does not fix a numerical optimal tradeoff. This attempt rederives an explicit finite-field graph construction and then audits the limitation of the coherence proof used to certify it. It does not claim that the classical square-root-scale construction solves the larger-sparsity agenda.

Research attempt: An explicit polynomial graph matrix and the coherence barrier

An explicit deterministic family
Let $q$ be prime and $1\leq r<q$. Index rows by $(x,y)\in\mathbb F_q^2$ and columns by the $q^{r+1}$ polynomials $p$ of degree at most $r$ over $\mathbb F_q$. Define \[ A_{(x,y),p}=\begin{cases}q^{-1/2}&y=p(x),\\0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \] Each column has exactly $q$ nonzero entries and norm one. Distinct polynomials agree at at most $r$ field points, so their inner product is at most $r/q$. For completeness, the root bound follows by induction on degree: a root $a$ gives polynomial division by $X-a$, and every other distinct root is a root of the quotient. Thus $m=q^2$ measurements and $N=q^{r+1}$ columns have coherence $\mu\leq r/q$ and are explicitly listed by field evaluation.

For an $s$-sparse complex coefficient vector $z$, expansion of its squared image norm gives \[ \left|\lVert Az\rVert_2^2-\lVert z\rVert_2^2\right| \leq\mu\sum_{i\ne j}|z_i||z_j| =\mu\left(\left(\sum_i|z_i|\right)^2-\sum_i|z_i|^2\right) \leq\mu(s-1)\lVert z\rVert_2^2. \] The last inequality is Cauchy's inequality on at most $s$ nonzero entries. In particular the restricted isometry constant obeys $\delta_s\leq(s-1)r/q$. With $r=2$ and $q>2$ this has $N=q^3>m=q^2$ and certifies $\delta_s\leq\delta$ for $s\leq1+\delta q/2$. This is a genuine compressed family, but its certified sparsity grows only like $\sqrt m$ for fixed $\delta$.

Why this certificate cannot by itself pass the square-root scale
For any $m\times N$ matrix of unit columns with $N>m$, its Gram matrix $G=A^*A$ has trace $N$, at most $m$ nonzero nonnegative eigenvalues, and \[ \operatorname{tr}(G^2)\geq N^2/m \] by Cauchy's inequality on those eigenvalues. Its diagonal contributes $N$, and the $N(N-1)$ off-diagonal squared absolute entries are each at most $\mu^2$. Therefore \[ \mu^2\geq\frac{N-m}{m(N-1)}. \] Any proof using only the sufficient condition $(s-1)\mu\leq\delta$ must consequently have \[ s\leq1+\delta\sqrt{\frac{m(N-1)}{N-m}}. \] When $N/m$ is bounded away from one, this limits that proof method to a square-root order. It is a barrier to this certificate, not a lower bound on the actual restricted isometry of all deterministic matrices.

To see concretely that the coherence estimate can be loose, take unit columns $a_1=e_1$, $a_2=\mu e_1+\sqrt{1-\mu^2}e_2$, and $a_i=e_i$ for $i\geq3$, with $0<\mu<1$. Their Gram matrix has just one nonzero off-diagonal pair. Any support containing the first two columns has extremal eigenvalues $1\pm\mu$, and every other support is orthonormal. Thus $\delta_s=\mu$ for every $2\leq s\leq N$, whereas the generic certificate is $(s-1)\mu$. This example is square, not a proposed compressed solution; it disproves only the inference that the coherence bound must be sharp.

Verification and adversarial audit

The construction requires $r<q$, so distinct coefficient polynomials cannot coincide as functions on all field points. The complex-vector proof uses absolute values and is not restricted to nonnegative coefficients. The rank-based bound assumes $N>m$ and only constrains the coherence sufficient condition. The final example explicitly separates an analysis limitation from a construction meeting the full compressed target.
