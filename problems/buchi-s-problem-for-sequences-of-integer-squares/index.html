Büchi's Problem for Sequences of Integer Squares

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $S=\{n^2:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}$ be the set of integer squares. For a positive integer $M$, a sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_M)\in S^M$ has constant second difference $2$ if \[ a_{i+2}-2a_{i+1}+a_i=2 \qquad\text{for every integer }i\text{ with }1\le i\le M-2. \] Call such a sequence consecutive-square in form if there is an integer $x$ such that $a_i=(x+i)^2$ for every $1\le i\le M$. This concerns the square values; no choice of signs for their square roots is prescribed.

The supplied formulation of Büchi's problem asks whether \[ \exists M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge1}\;\forall(a_1,\ldots,a_M)\in S^M,\qquad \left[ \forall i\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad 1\le i\le M-2\ \Longrightarrow\ a_{i+2}-2a_{i+1}+a_i=2 \right] \Longrightarrow \left[ \exists x\in{\mathbb{Z}}\;\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,M\},\quad a_i=(x+i)^2 \right]. \] Here $M$ must be uniform over all sequences of that length, whereas $x$ may depend on the sequence.

Unresolved qualification. The supplied statement allows every positive length $M$, including lengths below $3$, for which the second-difference condition has an empty index range. This leaves a substantive short-length issue in the stated target. A lower bound ensuring a nonempty second-difference condition is not supplied; none has been silently added. The intended nonvacuous formulation requires human mathematical review.

Short English statement

The supplied question asks for a fixed length $M$ such that every sequence of $M$ integer squares with second differences $2$ consists of squares of consecutive integers. The allowed short lengths leave an unresolved formulation issue. The supplied partially solved label and dated literature assessment are curation claims, not independently verified conclusions.
