Conjecture 4.3 — If every closed incompressible surface in M is parallel to ∂M, then S2,∞(M ) is torsion free.

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the standard Kauffman-bracket convention, let $R=\mathbb Z[A,A^{-1}]$ and let $S_{2,\infty}(M)$ be the $R$-module generated by isotopy classes of unoriented framed links in a three-manifold $M$, including the empty link, modulo the local relations \[ [L_\times]=A[L_0]+A^{-1}[L_\infty],\qquad [L\sqcup O]=-(A^2+A^{-2})[L], \] where the first triple differs only in a ball by a crossing and its two smoothings, and $O$ is a disjoint trivial zero-framed circle. The excerpt names the module but does not reproduce the coefficient convention; this is the usual interpretation requiring confirmation.

A closed embedded surface is boundary parallel if it cobounds a product with a boundary component. Incompressibility is the intended absence of a compression disk; the treatment of spheres, one-sided surfaces and orientability must be fixed in the source's manifold/surface category.

The conjecture asserts: if every closed incompressible surface in $M$ is parallel to $\partial M$, then $S_{2,\infty}(M)$ is torsion free. Over the displayed ring this means \[ \forall r\in R\setminus\{0\}\ \forall x\in S_{2,\infty}(M): \quad rx=0\Longrightarrow x=0. \] It does not merely mean absence of integer torsion, nor does it assert freeness or finite generation. The excerpt does not state compactness, orientability, irreducibility or boundary conventions on $M$; no additional manifold hypothesis is inserted from customary examples.

The supplied summary calls the claim open/triage and not obviously resolved, without a precise theorem citation. The historical conjecture and later assessment remain source attributions pending primary-literature review.

Short English statement

If all closed incompressible surfaces are boundary parallel, must the Kauffman-bracket skein module have no coefficient-ring torsion? The coefficient ring and manifold/incompressibility conventions need confirmation from the original context.
