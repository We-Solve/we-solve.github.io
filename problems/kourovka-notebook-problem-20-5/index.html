Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.5

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. Its integral group ring is \[ \mathbb ZG=\left\{\sum_{g\in G}a_g g:a_g\in\mathbb Z\right\}, \] with multiplication induced by the group operation and distributivity. Define the augmentation homomorphism by \[ \varepsilon:\mathbb ZG\longrightarrow\mathbb Z, \qquad \varepsilon\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)=\sum_{g\in G}a_g. \] The group of normalized units is \[ \mathrm V(\mathbb ZG)=\{u\in(\mathbb ZG)^\times:\varepsilon(u)=1\}. \] For any group $H$, define its finite-order spectrum by \[ \operatorname{Spec}(H)=\{n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\text{there exists }h\in H\text{ with }\operatorname{ord}(h)=n\}. \] Here $\operatorname{ord}(h)=n$ means that $n$ is the least positive integer with $h^n=1$. Infinite orders are excluded from this spectrum, consistently with the source's explicit formulation in terms of integer orders.

Problem. Is it true that, for every finite group $G$, \[ \operatorname{Spec}(G)=\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathrm V(\mathbb ZG)\right)? \] Equivalently, for every finite group $G$ and every $n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, is \[ \left(\exists u\in\mathrm V(\mathbb ZG):\operatorname{ord}(u)=n\right) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(\exists g\in G:\operatorname{ord}(g)=n\right)? \] The source says "for any integer $n$"; positive integers are used here because finite element orders are positive. The record describes this as a well-known problem and labels it open. It also reports an affirmative answer when $G$ is solvable, attributed to M. Hertweck, Comm. Algebra 36 (2008), 3585--3588. These are supplied source claims, not independently verified assessments. The proposer attribution is corrupted in the supplied record and cannot be reliably reconstructed.

Short English statement

Does every finite-order normalized unit of the integral group ring of a finite group have an order that also occurs among elements of the group, and conversely? The supplied record reports an affirmative answer for solvable groups and labels the general problem open; its proposer attribution is damaged.
