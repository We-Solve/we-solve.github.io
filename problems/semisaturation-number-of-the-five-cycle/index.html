Math-7372

Semisaturation Number of the Five-Cycle

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Use the standard convention that graphs are finite, simple, and undirected, and that copies of cycles are not required to be induced. These conventions are not explicitly specified in the supplied record and require confirmation against the references. For an integer $k\ge 3$, let $C_k$ denote the cycle on $k$ vertices. A graph $G$ is $C_k$-semisaturated if, for every pair of distinct nonadjacent vertices $u,v$, adding the edge $uv$ creates a copy of $C_k$ containing that edge. Equivalently, adding any missing edge strictly increases the number of copies of $C_k$. The graph $G$ may already contain copies of $C_k$. Define \[ \operatorname{ssat}(n,C_k)=\min\left\{|E(G)|: |V(G)|=n,\ G\text{ is }C_k\text{-semisaturated}\right\}. \] For comparison, $\operatorname{sat}(n,C_k)$ is the corresponding minimum restricted to graphs containing no copy of $C_k$.

The self-contained conjecture selected by the supplied corrected statement is \[ \operatorname{ssat}(n,C_5)=\frac{11}{8}n+O(1)\qquad(n\to\infty). \] Precisely, does there exist a real constant $M\ge 0$ and an integer $N\ge 5$ such that, for every integer $n\ge N$, \[ \left|\operatorname{ssat}(n,C_5)-\frac{11}{8}n\right|\le M? \] The original statement additionally conjectures that certain stated constructions for $\operatorname{sat}(n,C_k)$ and $\operatorname{ssat}(n,C_k)$ are optimal whenever $n>n_0(k)$. The supplied record does not describe those constructions, their edge counts, or their applicable values of $k$. Consequently, that additional assertion cannot be formulated precisely here and remains an unresolved part of the original statement.

Short English statement

Is the minimum number of edges in an $n$-vertex graph for which every added missing edge creates a new five-cycle within a bounded additive distance of $11n/8$, uniformly for all sufficiently large integers $n$? Existing five-cycles are allowed. The original record also contains a construction-dependent optimality conjecture whose required constructions are not supplied.
