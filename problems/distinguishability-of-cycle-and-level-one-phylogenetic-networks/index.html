Math-7050

Distinguishability of Cycle and Level-One Phylogenetic Networks

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

The supplied record asks for a classification of phylogenetic network topologies by their distinguishability under three group-based models: the Cavender--Farris--Neyman model (CFN), the Kimura two-parameter model (K2P), and the Kimura three-parameter model (K3P). CFN has a two-state alphabet, conventionally indexed by ${\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}$; K2P and K3P have four-state alphabets, conventionally indexed by $({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^2$. These model names alone do not specify the stochastic construction on a network.

Let $X$ be a fixed leaf-label set with $|X|=n$. Write $\mathcal C_k(X)$ for the intended class of $k$-cycle phylogenetic networks on $X$, and $\mathcal L_1(X)$ for the intended class of level-one phylogenetic networks on $X$. Here interpreting $n$ as the number of labelled leaves follows the usual phylogenetic reading; the supplied statement does not explicitly define it. The precise network conventions remain unresolved. In a common rooted convention, level one means that each biconnected component of the underlying undirected graph contains at most one reticulation vertex; a single-cycle network has one such cycle, whose length may be denoted by $k$. The record does not establish that these are its intended conventions, or specify binaryness, directions retained in the topology, suppression of degree-two vertices, or admissible cycle lengths.

To make the possible meaning of distinguishability explicit, suppose a network parameterization has been fixed. For a model $M$ with state alphabet $A_M$, let \[ \phi_{N,M}\colon\Theta_{N,M}\longrightarrow\Delta(A_M^X),\qquad \Delta(A_M^X)=\left\{(p_a)_{a\in A_M^X}:p_a\geq0,\ \sum_{a\in A_M^X}p_a=1\right\}, \] where $\Theta_{N,M}$ is the chosen stochastic parameter domain. Define the attainable distribution set and its complex Zariski closure by \[ \mathcal P_M(N)=\phi_{N,M}(\Theta_{N,M}),\qquad V_M(N)=\overline{\mathcal P_M(N)}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{A_M^X}. \] The choice of $\Theta_{N,M}$, including root distributions, edge constraints, reticulation mixing, and inclusion of boundary parameters, is not supplied. Thus these expressions describe a framework for specifying the problem, rather than a completed choice of network model.

One possible algebraic distinguishability relation is mutual noncontainment: \[ D_M^{\mathrm{nc}}(N,N')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_M(N)\not\subseteq V_M(N')\ \text{and}\ V_M(N')\not\subseteq V_M(N). \] Other possible meanings include merely $V_M(N)\neq V_M(N')$, inequality of attainable stochastic distribution sets, or a specified generic statistical identifiability condition. These criteria must not be silently identified; the supplied record does not select one.

Requested classification, subject to these unresolved specifications. For each fixed admissible $n$, each admissible cycle length $k$, and each \[ M\in\{\mathrm{CFN},\mathrm{K2P},\mathrm{K3P}\}, \] give necessary and sufficient conditions on a pair $N,N'\in\mathcal C_k(X)$ for distinguishability under $M$. Extend the classification to all pairs $N,N'\in\mathcal L_1(X)$. Equivalently, once an intended relation $D_M$ and network conventions are fixed, determine exactly the sets \[ \{(N,N')\in\mathcal C_k(X)^2:D_M(N,N')\} \quad\text{and}\quad \{(N,N')\in\mathcal L_1(X)^2:D_M(N,N')\}. \] No numerical range for $n$ or $k$, or treatment of comparisons between different cycle lengths, is supplied. A unique fully specified formulation therefore requires clarification of the network classes, parameterizations, and distinguishability criterion.

Short English statement

For a fixed number of leaves, classify which pairs of cycle phylogenetic networks can be distinguished under each of CFN, K2P, and K3P, and extend the classification to level-one networks. The source leaves the network conventions, parameter restrictions, and precise meaning of distinguishability unspecified. Its partially solved status and reported special cases are supplied dated curation requiring independent verification.
