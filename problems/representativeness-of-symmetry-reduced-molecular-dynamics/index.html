Math-15433

Representativeness of Symmetry-Reduced Molecular Dynamics

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $\Phi_t$ denote the molecular-dynamics flow in the intended phase space $\mathcal X$, with an action of the intended spatial/particle-label symmetry group. When this action commutes with the flow, a configuration in $\operatorname{Fix}(H)$ remains $H$-symmetric. A group-generated simulation represents such a configuration by data on a fundamental domain and reconstructs its other particles through the group action. This need not mean only translational periodicity.

The source asks how representative these symmetry-reduced solutions are of generic solutions with suitably restricted initial conditions. In particular, for a sequence of larger fundamental domains and correspondingly smaller symmetry subgroups, does the reconstructed simulation converge to the full dynamics in an appropriate average sense?

To turn that request into a convergence statement one must specify reduced initial data $x_N$, admissible full initial data $x$, relevant observables or average operators $\mathcal A_N,\mathcal A$, and a limit such as \[ \mathcal A_N(\Phi_t(x_N))-\mathcal A(\Phi_t(x))\longrightarrow0 \qquad(N\to\infty) \] in the intended time regime and topology. This is a schematic description of the requested approximation, not an asserted convergence law or a choice of spatial, temporal or ensemble averaging.

The record supplies neither the interaction potential and particle model, the precise group-generated constraint, the subgroup/domain limit, the initial-data restrictions, nor the meaning of genericity and average approximation. It also does not specify whether a finite-time, long-time or equilibrium claim is sought. Those choices are essential to the modeling and validation question; exact equivariance alone does not state the requested representativeness.

Short English statement

Do symmetry-generated molecular-dynamics simulations become representative of generic admissible dynamics, in an average sense, as their fundamental domains grow and symmetry constraints weaken? The model, initial-data class, observable and averaging limit remain unspecified.
