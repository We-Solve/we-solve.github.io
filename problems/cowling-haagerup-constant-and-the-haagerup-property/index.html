Math-1090

Cowling–Haagerup Constant and the Haagerup Property

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $G$ be a countable group with the usual discrete-group interpretation. For a function $\phi:G\to{\mathbb{C}}$, its Herz--Schur norm $\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}$ is the completely bounded norm of the Schur multiplier on $B(\ell^2G)$ given on matrix entries by \[ (a_{s,t})\longmapsto(\phi(s^{-1}t)a_{s,t}). \] The Cowling--Haagerup constant is \[ \Lambda(G)=\inf\left\{\sup_i\|\phi_i\|_{\mathrm{cb}}: \phi_i\text{ finitely supported},\quad \phi_i(g)\to1\text{ for every }g\in G\right\}, \] with value infinity when no uniformly bounded approximating family exists. In particular, $\Lambda(G)=1$ asks for bounds arbitrarily close to one; it does not silently require that the infimum be attained by an approximating family of norm exactly one.

The Haagerup property, equivalently a-T-menability for these groups, means that $G$ has a proper affine isometric action on a Hilbert space. In displacement terms, for some point $v$, each set \[ \{g\in G:\|g\cdot v-v\|\leq R\} \] is finite for every finite $R\geq0$.

The question is the universal implication \[ \forall G\text{ countable},\qquad \Lambda(G)=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\text{ has the Haagerup property}. \] The equivalence in the source identifies Haagerup property with a-T-menability; it does not add the reverse implication from that property to $\Lambda(G)=1$. The assessment separately reports an older Cowling equivalence disproved and leaves this indicated direction as the remaining question.

Short English statement

Must every countable group with Cowling--Haagerup constant one admit a proper affine isometric Hilbert-space action? The source asks this direction, not a new assertion of the historical two-way conjecture.
