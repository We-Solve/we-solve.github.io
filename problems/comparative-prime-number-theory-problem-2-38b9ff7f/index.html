Comparative Prime Number Theory — Problem 2

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

For the conventional interpretation as races between primes in arithmetic progressions, let $q\ge 2$ be an integer and let $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ be pairwise distinct reduced residue classes modulo $q$, where $3\le r\le\varphi(q)$ and $\varphi(q)=\#({\mathbb{Z}}/q{\mathbb{Z}})^\times$. Define \[ \pi(x;q,a)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ is prime and }p\equiv a\pmod q\} \] and the ordering event \[ R(q;a_1,\ldots,a_r)=\{x\ge2:\pi(x;q,a_1)>\cdots>\pi(x;q,a_r)\}. \] When the limit exists, define its logarithmic density by \[ \delta(q;a_1,\ldots,a_r)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log X}\int_2^X\mathbf 1_{R(q;a_1,\ldots,a_r)}(x)\,\frac{\mathrm dx}{x}. \] Here $\mathbf 1_R$ denotes the indicator of $R$. This is an explicit conventional framework; the supplied excerpt does not itself define the races, the density convention, or hypotheses ensuring existence of these limits.

Requested task. Numerically compute further three-way races, or races involving more competitors. In particular, determine, for example, the $100$ most biased three-way races. The reference value for a three-way ordering density is $1/6$. The excerpt does not specify whether "most biased" means the largest ordering densities, the largest absolute deviations from $1/6$, or another statistic. It also does not specify the admissible moduli, restrictions on the competitors, or equivalences under which two races count as the same. Consequently, a unique ranked list cannot be specified from this excerpt alone; these choices must be supplied before a precise exhaustive-ranking task is fixed.

The source additionally highlights the potentially harder task of bounding three-way densities sufficiently sharply to certify that such a list is exhaustive. It reports that Fiorilli and Martin [FiM] determined the $117$ most biased two-way races. It attributes to Lamzouri [Lam] a convergence rate $O(1/\log q)$ toward $1/6$ for three-way race densities, compared with a rate $q^{-1/2+o(1)}$ toward $1/2$ for two-way densities. These are supplied background claims: their hypotheses, ranges of uniformity, and precise bibliographic references are absent from the record. They are not asserted here as unconditional statements for every tuple defined above.

Short English statement

Compute more races among primes in three or more residue classes, and identify, for example, the $100$ most biased three-way races. The source also raises the challenge of proving that the resulting list is exhaustive. The meaning of bias, the search domain, the treatment of equivalent races, and the assumptions needed for the densities remain unspecified.
