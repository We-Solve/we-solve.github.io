Merkurjev–Suslin Theory for Higher Massey Products

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

For context, the usual degree-two norm-residue formulation of the Merkurjev--Suslin theorem is the isomorphism \[ K_2^M(F)/mK_2^M(F)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H^2(G_F,\mu_m^{\otimes 2}), \qquad \{a,b\}\longmapsto (a)\smile(b), \] for a field $F$ and an integer $m\geq 2$ invertible in $F$. Here $G_F=\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{sep}}/F)$, $\mu_m$ is the Galois module of $m$-th roots of unity, cohomology is continuous Galois cohomology, and $(a)\in H^1(G_F,\mu_m)$ is the Kummer class of $a\in F^\times$. The group $K_2^M(F)$ is generated by bilinear symbols $\{a,b\}$ subject to the Steinberg relations $\{a,1-a\}=0$ for $a\neq 0,1$. This recalls the conventional theorem mentioned in the question; it does not specify the intended higher analogue.

For a standard degree-one convention for Massey products, let $A^\bullet$ be a differential graded algebra with differential $d$, and let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in H^1(A^\bullet)$, where $n\geq 2$. A defining system consists of elements $a_{ij}\in A^1$, indexed by $1\leq i<j\leq n+1$ with $(i,j)\neq(1,n+1)$, such that $a_{i,i+1}$ represents $\alpha_i$ and \[ d a_{ij}=\sum_{i<k<j}a_{ik}a_{kj}. \] With this sign convention, the Massey product is the set \[ \langle\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\rangle = \left\{ \left[\sum_{k=2}^{n}a_{1k}a_{k,n+1}\right] :\ (a_{ij})\text{ is a defining system} \right\}\subseteq H^2(A^\bullet). \] It is defined when a defining system exists, and it vanishes when it contains zero. For $n=2$, it is the singleton containing $\alpha_1\smile\alpha_2$. These definitions explain one conventional setting; the supplied record does not specify the degrees, coefficients, or differential graded algebra intended in its question.

Short English statement

The question asks for an analogue of the Merkurjev--Suslin theorem involving higher Massey products. Such products can have several values and require defining systems.
