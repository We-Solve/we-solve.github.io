Strongly Balanced Almost-Uniform Vector Permutations

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb C$ denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively. All additional parameters and hypotheses are specified in the question.
Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does every finite set of $m$-dimensional vectors of Euclidean norm at most $1$, whose vector sum has norm at most $1$, admit a permutation whose every prefix sum has norm at most $\sqrt{2.7m}$ and whose block sums are uniformly bounded by an absolute constant?

Formulation references: [S1], [S2].

Short English statement

Does every finite set of $m$-dimensional vectors of Euclidean norm at most $1$, whose vector sum has norm at most $1$, admit a permutation whose every prefix sum has norm at most $\sqrt{2.7m}$ and whose block sums are uniformly bounded by an absolute constant?

Strongly Balanced Almost-Uniform Vector Permutations

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 097; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that one signing controls every prescribed-order prefix by $C\sqrt d$, and every zero-sum Euclidean unit-ball family has an ordering with that bound.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For all integers $d,N\ge1$ and vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_N\in\mathbb R^d$ of Euclidean norm at most one, one absolute $C$ is claimed to permit signs with \[ \max_{0\le k\le N}\|\sum_{i=1}^k\varepsilon_iv_i\|_2\le C\sqrt d. \] The prescribed vector order is retained and the same signs control every prefix. If additionally $\sum_iv_i=0$, the asserted Steinitz--Bergström consequence instead gives a permutation $\pi$ with \[ \max_{0\le k\le N}\|\sum_{i=1}^kv_{\pi(i)}\|_2\le C\sqrt d. \] The constant is independent of dimension, vector count and the particular family.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 097; 2026-09-24), a filtered signed walk balances contractions against imposed linear constraints. Matrix trace budgets bound accumulated energy, and Brownian slab estimates turn low energy into shifted signs; a sign-to-ordering reduction gives the zero-sum conclusion.
