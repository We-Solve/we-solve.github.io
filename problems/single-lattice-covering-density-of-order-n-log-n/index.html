Human contribution summary on Math-2646: Scott Duke Kominers

Human Contribution: Scott Duke Kominers

Multi-covering extension of OpenAI Result 092

Paper and attribution.
Scott Duke Kominers, Multi-coverings by lattice translates of convex bodies, hexagon:2610.00165v1, is a preliminary manuscript dated October 8, 2026. It develops the single-lattice construction in OpenAI's Result 092 into a bound for arbitrary covering multiplicity. The author credits substantial LLM assistance, principally GPT-6 Astra, while taking responsibility for the problem, methods, exposition, and any errors. This record is a summary of that authored contribution.
Lattice multi-coverings (Theorem 1.1).
For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ and a full-rank lattice $\Lambda$, write \[ N_{\Lambda,K}(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbf 1_K(x-\lambda), \qquad \rho=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(K)}{\det\Lambda}. \] A lattice $k$-fold covering requires $N_{\Lambda,K}(x)\geq k$ at every $x$, with one translate for each distinct lattice point. Its density $\rho$ is the mean multiplicity. Let $\theta_{\mathrm{lat},k}(K)$ be the infimum of these densities. Theorem 1.1 states that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a constant $C_\varepsilon$ depending only on $\varepsilon$ gives \[ \theta_{\mathrm{lat},k}(K) \leq \max\{(1+\varepsilon)k,\ C_\varepsilon n\log n\} \quad(n\geq2,\ k\geq1). \] No symmetry assumption is imposed on $K$. In particular, the density approaches the unavoidable lower bound $k$ uniformly over bodies and dimensions when $k/(n\log n)$ tends to infinity.
Prescribed density and simultaneous coverings (Theorem 1.2).
For one body and $0<\varepsilon<1$, every prescribed density $\rho\geq A_\varepsilon n\log n$ can be realized by a lattice satisfying $N_{\Lambda,K}(x)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\rho$ everywhere, in every dimension $n\geq2$. In sufficiently large dimension, the theorem also constructs one lattice of a prescribed covolume $\Delta$ for up to $\exp(n^{c_0})$ bodies, for an absolute $c_0>0$. This simultaneous statement assumes that their uniform distributions share a covariance matrix and that each mean $\rho_j=\operatorname{vol}_n(K_j)/\Delta$ exceeds the threshold. The bodies can have different volumes; their means need not agree. There is no matching pointwise upper bound on multiplicity or claim about a typical random lattice.
Worst-case order (Corollary 1.3).
Combining Theorem 1.1 with Li--Liu's earlier one-fold lattice lower bound, the paper obtains worst-case density of order $k+n\log n$, both over all convex bodies and over origin-symmetric convex bodies. This is a worst-case lattice statement; it does not identify the optimal one-fold translative density for a particular body such as a ball.
Outline of the argument.
The proof adapts OpenAI's horizontal--vertical decomposition of a lattice. Approximately Gaussian section volumes provide layer weights, and averaging a bounded terminal block over its cosets retains almost all of the mean while keeping individual selected weights small. Independent spatial thinning turns a mean-hole estimate into a Laplace-transform bound for section counts. A random shear combines those estimates to make the region of insufficient multiplicity very small. A small dilation eliminates that region; a final lattice rescaling gives the prescribed mean. Equal covariance permits a common normalization in the simultaneous version.
Scope relative to this problem.
The recorded question asks for a single lattice covering any convex body at density $O(n\log n)$ in every dimension $n\geq2$. Taking $k=1$ and, for example, $\varepsilon=1$ in Theorem 1.1 gives this conclusion with an absolute constant, so the paper includes the full one-fold statement and extends it to multi-coverings. Starting from an ordinary covering and taking an index-$k$ superlattice would instead give density $O(kn\log n)$; the new bound pays the dimension-dependent cost once. The simultaneous result has the extra covariance and large-dimension hypotheses stated above.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
An absolute constant $C>0$ is claimed such that for every dimension $n\ge2$ and every convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ there is one full-rank lattice $\Lambda$ with \[ K+\Lambda=\mathbb R^n,\qquad |K|/\det\Lambda\le Cn\log n. \] The ratio is lattice covering density. Neither central symmetry nor smooth boundary is needed, and the covering uses one lattice rather than a union of independently translated lattices.
