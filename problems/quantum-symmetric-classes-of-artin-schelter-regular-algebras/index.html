A concrete twist calculation.
Let $k$ be a field, $q\in k\setminus\{0\}$, and $A=k[x,y]$ with its usual grading. Define the graded automorphism $\sigma(x)=x,\ \sigma(y)=qy$. On the underlying graded vector space define, for homogeneous $a$, \[ a*b=a\,\sigma^{\deg a}(b). \] If $\deg a=r$ and $\deg b=s$, then \[ (a*b)*c=a\sigma^r(b)\sigma^{r+s}(c) =a*(b*c). \] Bilinearity proves associativity for arbitrary polynomials. The unit remains $1$. The new degree-one generators satisfy $x*y=q(y*x)$, and repeated ordered products are nonzero scalar multiples of $x^iy^j$. Consequently they span and are linearly independent; the degree-$n$ component still has dimension $n+1$.
What this tests.
For $q\ne1$, the twisted algebra is noncommutative whereas $A$ is commutative, so the two are not ordinarily isomorphic. Nevertheless their underlying graded vector spaces and Hilbert series coincide. This directly exhibits why matching growth data or enumerating ordinary isomorphism classes is too weak a proposed route to the requested quantum-symmetric classification.
