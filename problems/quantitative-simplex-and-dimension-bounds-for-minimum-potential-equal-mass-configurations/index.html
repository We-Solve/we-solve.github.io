Quantitative simplex and dimension bounds for minimum-potential equal-mass configurations

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. A central configuration has $\nabla_{q_i}U=-\lambda m_iq_i$ in center-of-mass coordinates for a common scalar $\lambda$. After fixing $I=1$, it is a critical point of $U$ on the collision-free inertia ellipsoid. Congruent configurations and scales are factored out for a finiteness count. A relative equilibrium is a rigidly rotating solution with time-independent shape. For equal masses, a geometric symmetry can combine an orthogonal transformation with a permutation of particle labels, preserving the normalized configuration. Minimizing the positive Newtonian function $U$ on $I=1$ is the source’s convention; physical potential energy is $-U$.

The maximal-dimensional equal-mass case.
Normalize all masses to one, center of mass zero and $I=\sum_i|q_i|^2=1$. Let $M=n(n-1)/2$. Expanding squares gives $\sum_{i<j}|q_i-q_j|^2=n$. The function $x\mapsto x^{-1/2}$ is strictly convex on positive numbers, so Jensen's inequality yields \[U=\sum_{i<j}|q_i-q_j|^{-1}\ge M\left(\frac nM\right)^{-1/2}=\frac{M^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}. \] Equality holds exactly when every mutual distance is $\sqrt{2/(n-1)}$. Such configurations are regular simplices and exist when $d\ge n-1$: take the vectors $e_i-n^{-1}\mathbf1$ in the sum-zero subspace of $\mathbb R^n$, rescaled to inertia one. They have all permutation symmetries realized by orthogonal maps.
Why the low-dimensional problem remains.
If $d<n-1$, n distinct equidistant points cannot exist: their centered Gram matrix has rank $n-1$, as follows by expanding their equal squared distances. The Jensen bound is then unattainable. It provides no information on whether the actual minimizing configuration has a nontrivial symmetry. A minimizer does exist on the normalized compact sphere because $U$ diverges at collisions, but compactness does not constrain its stabilizer.
Scope.
This known simplex calculation answers only a dimension range in which the equality geometry is available. It does not verify or reject the source's reported n=47 indication, whose dimension and symmetry convention matter. Proving a symmetry theorem or certifying an asymmetric minimizer in the intended lower-dimensional setting remains the first gap. The physical sign $-U$ is not confused with the source's minimization of positive $U$.
