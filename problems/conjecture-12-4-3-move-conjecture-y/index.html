Conjecture 12.4 — (3-move conjecture, Y.

Status Solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Solved from source status

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedSolvedSolved

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

In the usual unframed link setting, a link is a finite disjoint union of embedded circles in $S^3$, up to ambient isotopy. A trivial link is an unlink: its components bound pairwise disjoint embedded disks. A standard $3$-move replaces inside a ball a trivial two-strand tangle by three consecutive half-twists, or performs the inverse replacement, leaving the link outside the ball fixed. The precise local picture and orientation convention must agree with the cited source.

Conjecture 12.4, the $3$-move conjecture attributed to Y. Nakanishi [305], asserts: \[ \forall\text{ links }L\ \exists\text{ an unlink }U:\quad L\text{ is related to }U\text{ by finitely many }3\text{-moves and isotopies}. \] The statement does not require the unlink to have a prescribed number of components. The supplied research assessment reports that this universal assertion is false: there exists a link that cannot be related by such a sequence to any trivial link. It attributes the negative resolution to Dabkowski--Przytycki (2002), using a Burnside-group obstruction. This is a source-reported resolution; no counterexample or proof is reconstructed here. The generic background note calling the source item open is retained as conflicting status evidence, not used to overwrite the explicit negative assessment.

Short English statement

The conjecture claimed that every link becomes an unlink through finitely many $3$-moves. The supplied registry marks it solved negatively and cites a 2002 Burnside-group obstruction. This draft records the original universal claim and its reported disproof, with the local-move convention still requiring source review.
