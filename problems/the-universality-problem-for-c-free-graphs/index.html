Math-4086

The universality problem for C-free graphs

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

All graphs are simple and undirected. Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite set of finite graphs. A graph $G$ is $\mathcal C$-free if no subgraph of $G$ is isomorphic to a member of $\mathcal C$; the excluded subgraphs need not be induced. Write $\mathcal G_{\mathcal C}$ for the class of all countable $\mathcal C$-free graphs. A strong embedding $e:H\to G$ is an injective map on vertices such that two vertices of $H$ are adjacent if and only if their images are adjacent in $G$. A universal member $U\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal C}$ must admit a strong embedding of every $H\in\mathcal G_{\mathcal C}$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Characterize all finite sets $\mathcal C$ of finite graphs for which $\mathcal G_{\mathcal C}$ has a universal member under strong embeddings. [S2]

Short English statement

Which finite lists of forbidden finite subgraphs permit a countable graph containing every countable graph avoiding that list as an induced subgraph?
