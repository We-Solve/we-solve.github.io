Kourovka Notebook Problem 18.116

Status Open · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedOpenOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ of ${\mathbb{Q}}$. For a finite set $S$ of rational primes, let $M_S\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}$ be the compositum of all finite Galois extensions $L/{\mathbb{Q}}$ whose degrees are powers of $2$ and which are unramified at every finite prime outside $S$. Thus $M_S$ is the maximal Galois $2$-extension of ${\mathbb{Q}}$ unramified outside $S\cup\{\infty\}$; ramification at the infinite place is allowed, and the prime $2$ is permitted in $S$.

The source refers to the "absolute Galois group of the maximal $2$-extension." Its literal meaning and the relative Galois group of that extension are, respectively, \[ H_S^{\mathrm{abs}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/M_S), \qquad H_S^{\mathrm{rel}}=\operatorname{Gal}(M_S/{\mathbb{Q}}). \] These are different groups. The supplied record does not clarify whether its wording is literal or whether the relative group is intended.

For an explicitly chosen interpretation $H_S$, the precise question is: for every integer $n\geq 0$ and every finite group $G$ of order $2^n$, does there exist a finite set $S$ of rational primes and a continuous surjective homomorphism \[ \pi:H_S\twoheadrightarrow G, \qquad |S|\leq n, \] where $G$ has the discrete topology? The literal formulation takes $H_S=H_S^{\mathrm{abs}}$. The alternative formulation takes $H_S=H_S^{\mathrm{rel}}$; equivalently, it asks for a finite Galois extension $K/{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(K/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong G$ that is unramified at every finite prime outside some set $S$ with $|S|\leq n$. The intended choice remains unresolved in this draft.

The supplied source further states that the corresponding assertion for finite $p$-groups, with $p$ odd, is true, as noted by Serre. This is retained as a source-attributed assertion; the record gives neither a separate citation for it nor a clarification of the same Galois-group terminology.

Short English statement

The question asks whether every finite group of order $2^n$ can occur as a continuous finite quotient of a specified Galois group using at most $n$ finite ramification primes, with ramification at infinity allowed. The source's wording leaves unresolved whether the group is the absolute Galois group of the maximal extension or its Galois group over ${\mathbb{Q}}$. The supplied open label and the odd-prime assertion attributed to Serre are not independently verified here.
