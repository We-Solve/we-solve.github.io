Monoidal Invariance of Hopf-Algebra Cohomological Dimension

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Top Open Problems Not reported cataloguedPartially solvedOpen

Statement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $k$ be a common base field and let $A$ and $B$ be Hopf algebras over $k$. No restriction on the characteristic is stated for the general question. For a unital $k$-algebra $H$, put $H^e=H\otimes_k H^{\mathrm{op}}$ and define its Hochschild cohomological dimension by \[ \operatorname{cd}(H)=\operatorname{pd}_{H^e}H =\sup\{n\geq 0:\operatorname{Ext}_{H^e}^{n}(H,M)\neq 0\text{ for some }H\text{-bimodule }M\}. \] The value $+\infty$ is allowed. The source also identifies this invariant, for the Hopf algebras in question, with global dimension, namely the supremum of projective dimensions of modules.

Fix a convention $\sigma$ specifying left or right comodules and whether all comodules or only finite-dimensional comodules are considered. Write $\mathcal C_\sigma(H)$ for the resulting $k$-linear tensor category, with tensor product induced by the Hopf algebra structure. A $k$-linear monoidal equivalence is an equivalence equipped with coherent invertible tensor-product and unit constraints.

The question attributed to Julien Bichon's talk is whether, for every such pair $A,B$, \[ \mathcal C_\sigma(A)\simeq_{k,\otimes}\mathcal C_\sigma(B) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{cd}(A)=\operatorname{cd}(B). \] The supplied excerpt uses $\mathcal M^A$ and $\mathcal M^B$, but does not explicitly specify the comodule convention. Thus this display records precise candidate readings with the same fixed convention on both sides; it does not resolve which convention the source intends. Neither finite-dimensionality, bijectivity of antipodes, nor finiteness of the dimensions is imposed in the general question.

The excerpt reports positive answers in the following special cases, qualified there by "to the best of my knowledge":

  1. Both antipodes are bijective and both Hopf algebras are smooth, attributed to internal reference [2]. The excerpt does not define smoothness. A usual interpretation is homological smoothness: the algebra, as a module over its enveloping algebra, has a finite resolution by finitely generated projective modules; this interpretation requires confirmation against the cited source.
  2. Both Hopf algebras are cosemisimple and $S_A^4=\mathrm{id}_A$, $S_B^4=\mathrm{id}_B$, attributed to [1]. Here cosemisimplicity means semisimplicity of the comodule category.
  3. Both Hopf algebras are cosemisimple and $\operatorname{cd}(A),\operatorname{cd}(B)<\infty$, attributed to [2].
  4. Both Hopf algebras are finite-dimensional, with a characteristic condition transcribed as "zero, $\phi(d)$ or satisfies $p>d^2$", where $d=\dim_k A$, attributed to [2]. The occurrence of $\phi(d)$ is unexplained and the condition is not sufficiently intact to state the full special case reliably. The explicit alternatives characteristic zero and characteristic $p>d^2$ are retained as supplied, without reconstructing the intervening text.
  5. Both Hopf algebras are finite-dimensional and $A^*$ is unimodular, attributed to [2]. Here $A^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,k)$ is the dual Hopf algebra; unimodularity means that its spaces of left and right integrals coincide.
The excerpt describes a proposed general method using an $A$-$B$ bi-Galois object $R$, whose existence it attributes to Schauenburg [6], and seeking \[ \operatorname{cd}(A)=\operatorname{cd}(R)=\operatorname{cd}(B). \] It says the smooth case follows arguments of Yu [7]. Its subsequent projective-dimension formula has corrupted category subscripts and cannot be reconstructed reliably from the supplied text. These contextual claims and special cases are supplied source reports, not independently verified results in this draft.

Short English statement

The question asks whether a linear tensor equivalence between the comodule categories of two Hopf algebras over the same field forces equality of their Hochschild cohomological dimensions, allowing infinite dimension. The supplied record labels the problem partially solved and lists qualified positive cases. The comodule convention, the meaning of smoothness, and a corrupted characteristic condition require source review.
