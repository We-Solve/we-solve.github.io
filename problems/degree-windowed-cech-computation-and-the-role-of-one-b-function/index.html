Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Computing local cohomology via D-modules

The current method for computing local cohomology requires the iterated computation of Bernstein--Sato polynomials at every step of the Cech complex.

Can computing just the necessary steps of the Cech complex speed up the computation of local cohomology? What about computing just the Budur--Mustata--Saito b-function just once?

Attempt and results

The source asks for faster local-cohomology computations and proposes a single b-function calculation. Its reconstruction flag is retained. We test a route that discards unnecessary terms, and solve the elementary coordinate case without any b-function. This is known auxiliary material, not a new general local-cohomology algorithm.
What a degree request actually needs.
In any cochain complex $(C^\bullet,d)$, the module $H^q=\ker(d^q:C^q\to C^{q+1})/\operatorname{im}(d^{q-1}:C^{q-1}\to C^q)$ depends only on these two maps and three terms, with missing endpoint terms interpreted as zero. Computing several requested degrees needs the union of these windows. This identity justifies omitting other material only when the needed localizations and maps can be constructed directly. It does not show that a recursively generated D-module presentation can bypass its prerequisites. Dropping the incoming map changes the answer: over $R=k[x]$, the Cech complex $R\hookrightarrow R_x$ has $H^1=R_x/R$, whereas retaining only $R_x$ gives $R_x$. These are not even isomorphic $R$-modules: every class in $R_x/R$ is annihilated by some power of $x$, while $1\in R_x$ is not.
An exact computation for coordinate ideals.
Let $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, $1\le r\le n$, and $I=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$. Use the Cech complex \[ C^q=\bigoplus_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\},\ |J|=q} R_{\prod_{j\in J}x_j}, \qquad 0\le q\le r, \] with the usual alternating inclusion maps. Give variables their separate $\mathbb Z^n$ degrees. Fix an exponent vector $a\in\mathbb Z^n$. If any $a_i<0$ for $i>r$, this strand is zero. Otherwise put $N=\{i\le r:a_i<0\}$. The strand has a one-dimensional summand, with basis $x^a$, precisely for those faces $J$ containing $N$; its differential adds one vertex with the standard exterior sign. Identify the strand with the span of exterior symbols $e_J$ for $J\supseteq N$. Its differential is left exterior multiplication by $u=\sum_{i=1}^r e_i$, where terms with repeated indices vanish. If $N$ is proper, choose $j\notin N$ and let $h$ be contraction by the coordinate functional taking $e_j$ to one and all other $e_i$ to zero. Removing $j$ preserves the condition $J\supseteq N$, so $h$ stays in the strand. On a wedge $e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_q}$, contraction removes each $e_j$ with sign $(-1)^{l-1}$ at its position $l$. Expanding this formula gives $h(u\wedge v)=v-u\wedge h(v)$, because the functional has value one on $u$. Thus $hd+dh=1$, proving the strand has zero cohomology over every field, including characteristic two. If $N=\{1,\ldots,r\}$, only the face of size $r$ occurs, so there is one cohomology class in degree $r$. Each Laurent polynomial is a finite sum of homogeneous monomials, and all maps preserve degrees. Kernels and images therefore decompose into these strands. We have proved that the cohomology vanishes outside degree $r$, and that its degree-$r$ vector-space basis is \[ x_1^{-b_1}\cdots x_r^{-b_r}x_{r+1}^{c_{r+1}}\cdots x_n^{c_n}, \qquad b_i\ge1,\quad c_i\ge0. \] This computes the Cech cohomology; its standard identification with local cohomology is the framework supplied in the original definition, not a new identification theorem proved here.
Why this shortcut does not generalize as stated.
The contracting operator depends on a monomial basis with a single copy in each permitted face. For a general generator $f$, localization at $f$ does not preserve those exponent strands: in $k[x,y]$, the relation $(x+y)/(x+y)=1$ couples the numerators $x$ and $y$. Thus the coordinate-strand test cannot even assign the same face membership rule to fractions with equivalent presentations. Replacing the localizations by coordinate Laurent monomials is the first invalid inference in this route. A single b-polynomial might provide bounds or certificates while retaining all generator data; the present obstruction does not disprove that possibility.
