The exactly quadratic attractive interaction.
We try the source problem at $\alpha=2$ and prove a restricted large-mass result. Let $\rho$ be admissible with finite energy and mass $m$. The quadratic pair interaction implies a finite second moment after choosing an origin: by Fubini there is a point $y_0$ with $\int|x-y_0|^2\rho(x)\,dx<\infty$. Its barycenter $b=m^{-1}\int x\rho(x)\,dx$ is therefore defined, and direct expansion gives \[ \frac12\iint|x-y|^2\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy =m\int|x-b|^2\rho(x)\,dx. \] Translate so that $b=0$. Let $B$ be the centered ball of radius $R=(3m/(4\pi))^{1/3}$ and set $\sigma=\rho-\mathbf1_B$. Both densities have mass $m$ and barycenter zero.
Coulomb positivity and the ball potential.
For a real bounded integrable $\sigma$, its Coulomb quadratic form is nonnegative: \[ \frac12\iint\frac{\sigma(x)\sigma(y)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy\geq0. \] For smooth compactly supported $\sigma$, define $U_\sigma=|\cdot|^{-1}*\sigma$. The identity $-\Delta U_\sigma=4\pi\sigma$ and integration by parts on expanding balls give the quadratic form as $(8\pi)^{-1}\int|\nabla U_\sigma|^2$. The boundary term tends to zero since $U_\sigma=O(|x|^{-1})$ and $\nabla U_\sigma=O(|x|^{-2})$. Truncation and smoothing extend nonnegativity to bounded integrable $\sigma$: the kernel on $|x-y|<\delta$ has integral $2\pi\delta^2$, while the complementary kernel is bounded by $1/\delta$, so convergence in $L^1$ with a uniform $L^\infty$ bound suffices.

The ball potential, obtained by integrating its radial Poisson equation and matching at $R$, is \[ U_B(r)=\begin{cases}2\pi(R^2-r^2/3),&r\leq R,\\m/r,&r\geq R.\end{cases} \] Expansion of the Coulomb energy and the moment identity yield \[ E_2[\rho]-E_2[\mathbf1_B] =\int W(|x|)\sigma(x)\,dx +\frac12\iint\frac{\sigma(x)\sigma(y)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy, \qquad W(r)=U_B(r)+mr^2. \] For $m>2\pi/3$, $W'(r)=2(m-2\pi/3)r>0$ on $0<r<R$. Outside, $W'(r)=m(2r-r^{-2})>0$ because $R^3=3m/(4\pi)>1/2$.

The density constraint closes this special case.
Since $\int\sigma=0$, replace $W$ by $W-W(R)$ in the first integral. Inside $B$, both $W-W(R)$ and $\sigma=\rho-1$ are nonpositive; outside they are nonnegative. Thus both displayed energy terms are nonnegative. Strict monotonicity makes the first term zero only if $\rho=1$ almost everywhere inside $B$ and $\rho=0$ almost everywhere outside. The ball is therefore a global minimizer, and every minimizer is its translate, for $\alpha=2$ and $m>2\pi/3$.
What does not extend.
The proof uses the exact barycentric second-moment identity. For general $\alpha>0$, the attractive energy is not that one-body moment, and its difference cannot be absorbed into this monotone effective potential by the argument above. The source asks for every exponent, not just $\alpha=2$. This elementary special-case reconstruction has not been checked for novelty and supplies no threshold for the remaining exponents; the full-source completion estimate remains zero.
