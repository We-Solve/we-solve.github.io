Multivariable Motivic Poles and Monodromy

Status Partially solved · unsolvedmath.comLLM status Open unverified

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_r):({\mathbb{C}}^n,0)\to({\mathbb{C}}^r,0)$ be a holomorphic germ and put $f=\prod_{i=1}^r f_i$. The supplied record does not explicitly exclude identically zero components. The following standard definitions require $f\not\equiv0$; this necessary qualification must be checked against the source before accepting the formulation.

Write $\mathbf s=(s_1,\ldots,s_r)$, $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$, and $F^{\mathbf s}=\prod_i f_i^{s_i}$, interpreted as a formal symbol over $\mathcal O_{{\mathbb{C}}^n,0}[f^{-1}][\mathbf s]$. Let $\mathcal D_{{\mathbb{C}}^n,0}$ denote the ring of germs of holomorphic differential operators. Using the simultaneous-shift convention, define the local Bernstein--Sato ideal by \[ B_F=\left\{b(\mathbf s)\in{\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf s]: b(\mathbf s)F^{\mathbf s}=P(\mathbf s)F^{\mathbf s+\mathbf1} \text{ for some }P(\mathbf s)\in\mathcal D_{{\mathbb{C}}^n,0}[\mathbf s]\right\}. \] Here differentiation acts through \[ \partial_{x_j}F^{\mathbf s} =\left(\sum_{i=1}^r s_i\frac{\partial_{x_j}f_i}{f_i}\right)F^{\mathbf s}. \] Set \[ Z(B_F)=\{\mathbf s\in{\mathbb{C}}^r:b(\mathbf s)=0\text{ for every }b\in B_F\}, \qquad \operatorname{Exp}(\mathbf s)=(e^{2\pi i s_1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i s_r}). \] The supplied record does not explicitly specify the shift convention for $B_F$.

For a sufficiently small representative ball $B_\epsilon$ about the origin, let $U_\epsilon=B_\epsilon\setminus f^{-1}(0)$. For $\boldsymbol\lambda\in({\mathbb{C}}^*)^r$, let $\mathcal L_{\boldsymbol\lambda}$ be the rank-one complex local system on $({\mathbb{C}}^*)^r$ whose monodromy around the $i$-th coordinate circle is $\lambda_i$. A precise support locus for the complement at the origin is \[ V_{F,0}=\left\{\boldsymbol\lambda\in({\mathbb{C}}^*)^r: H^q\!\left(U_\epsilon,(F|_{U_\epsilon})^*\mathcal L_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\right)\ne0 \text{ for some integer }q\ge0\right\}. \] The source denotes a cohomology-support locus by $V_F$, but the supplied material does not determine whether it means this locus at the origin or a union of analogous local loci over nearby points of $f^{-1}(0)$. Accordingly, $V_F$ below retains the source's unresolved convention.

The original source record presents the following three assertions for every germ satisfying the intended hypotheses:

  1. There are finitely many generators $g_1,\ldots,g_m$ of $B_F$, each of which is a finite product of affine forms \[ a_1s_1+\cdots+a_rs_r+b, \qquad a_i\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ge0},\quad b\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}. \] The supplied coefficient conditions do not require $(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\ne0$.
  2. The equality \[ \operatorname{Exp}(Z(B_F))=V_F \] holds with the source's intended definition of $V_F$.
  3. Every polar hyperplane $H$ of the multivariable motivic zeta function associated with $F$ satisfies \[ H\subseteq Z(B_F). \]
The cleaned statement focuses on the third assertion and asks additionally whether the corresponding multivariable monodromy conjecture holds. If the intended monodromy assertion is support-locus containment, its formulation is \[ \operatorname{Exp}(H)\subseteq V_F \quad\text{for every such polar hyperplane }H. \] This interpretation is conditional: the supplied record does not specify the multivariable motivic zeta function, its local or nearby-point convention, the coefficient ring and meaning of a pole, or the precise corresponding monodromy conjecture. In particular, actual polar hyperplanes must not silently be replaced by candidate hyperplanes from a resolution. Thus the displayed pole assertions are formulation schemas pending these source definitions.

The supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and attributes the exponentiated zero-locus/support-locus equality to Budur--van der Veer--Wu--Zhou, while describing the ideal-generation assertion and the associated multivariable motivic-monodromy conjecture as unresolved in general. This is a report of the supplied assessment, not an independently verified theorem statement or current status determination.

Short English statement

For a tuple of holomorphic germs, the source asks whether its Bernstein--Sato ideal has generators built from rational affine forms, whether its exponentiated zero locus equals an appropriate local-system cohomology-support locus, and whether every motivic polar hyperplane lies in that zero locus. The cleaned target emphasizes motivic poles and monodromy. The nonzero-component hypothesis, support-locus convention, and precise meaning of motivic poles and multivariable monodromy require source clarification. Supplied dated curation reports partial progress; this draft requires human mathematical review.
