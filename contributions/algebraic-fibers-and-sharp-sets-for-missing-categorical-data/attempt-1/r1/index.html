A concrete identifiability attempt

The original question specifies no missingness mechanism, model, loss or estimand. We first try to recover a binary missing value law from the observed categorical probabilities alone, then test whether an algebraic model can narrow the answer. These restrictions are explicit and do not replace the broad source question by a claimed solved problem.
The entire binary completion fiber.
Let $R=1$ mean that binary $Y$ is observed. The observed law gives \[ a=\Pr(R=1,Y=1),\quad b=\Pr(R=1,Y=0),\quad m=\Pr(R=0),\qquad a,b,m\ge0,\quad a+b+m=1. \] Every compatible full law is uniquely described by $t=\Pr(R=0,Y=1)\in[0,m]$, with the four joint cells $(a,b,t,m-t)$. These numbers are nonnegative and sum to one for every such $t$, so the interval is both necessary and sufficient, not merely a relaxation. The marginal success probability ranges sharply over $[a,a+m]$. If $m>0$, the missing-case success probability is $t/m$ and ranges over all of $[0,1]$.

In particular $t=0$ and $t=m$ produce exactly the same observed data law, while missing cases have opposite deterministic outcomes. No single data-dependent imputation rule can reconstruct both full laws. The respondent fraction $a/(a+b)$, when $a+b>0$, determines the missing fraction only after a condition such as $Y\perp R$ is added. Under that condition, $a=\Pr(R=1)\Pr(Y=1)$ yields $\Pr(Y=1)=a/(a+b)$, and $t=m a/(a+b)$; this is an added assumption, not a consequence of the observed table.

An explicit algebraic restriction across observed strata.
Suppose a finite covariate $X=i$ is always observed. Let the known stratum mass be $w_i>0$, observed cells $a_i=\Pr(X=i,R=1,Y=1)$ and $b_i=\Pr(X=i,R=1,Y=0)$, and missing mass $m_i=w_i-a_i-b_i\ge0$. Arbitrary completions form the product $t_i\in[0,m_i]$. Impose the additional model $Y\perp X$ while allowing the response mechanism to depend arbitrarily on $X,Y$. Writing $\theta=\Pr(Y=1)$, independence is equivalent to \[ a_i+t_i=w_i\theta\quad\hbox{for every }i. \] Hence compatibility with independence is equivalent to the interval \[ L:=\max_i a_i/w_i\ \le\theta\le\ U:=\min_i(1-b_i/w_i). \] Necessity follows by $0\le t_i\le m_i$. Conversely any $\theta$ in this interval defines $t_i=w_i\theta-a_i\in[0,m_i]$ and therefore a full joint law with the required observed cells and common stratum success probability. Thus $L\le U$ is a complete feasibility test and $[L,U]$ is the sharp marginal identified interval in this specified model. It can remain wide; equations alone need not choose one imputation distribution. If $L>U$ the model is contradicted by the observed probabilities, rather than repaired by negative missing cells.

First gap and model-assisted audit

The attempted inference from an observed contingency table to a unique missing-case law fails at the explicit interval above. Independence adds tractable polynomial constraints but supplies a justified imputation only if that scientific assumption and a choice within the remaining fiber are defended. The primary AIM Question 19 was inspected, and no missing mechanism was supplied there. The audit checked boundary masses, strata with no respondents, sharpness in both directions, and the distinction between recovering a law and choosing a loss-optimal action. No simulation, dataset fit or formal computation was performed. These are elementary known identification arguments, not a new general imputation improvement or an assessment of multiple-imputation coverage.
