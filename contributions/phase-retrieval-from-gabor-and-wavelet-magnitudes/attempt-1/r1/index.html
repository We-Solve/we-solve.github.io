Phase retrieval: exact finite criteria and two obstructions to uniform guarantees

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A frame $(\phi_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ in a Hilbert space $H$ satisfies $A\|f\|^2\leq\sum_\lambda|\langle f,\phi_\lambda\rangle|^2\leq B\|f\|^2$ for constants $0<A\leq B<\infty$. Magnitudes cannot distinguish $f$ from $cf$ for $|c|=1$, so uniqueness is understood modulo this global phase. Stability bounds reconstruction error in this quotient by measurement error. Gabor systems use translations and modulations of a window; wavelet systems use translations and dilations.

The source's parameter-dependent question

The primary report, printed pages3025--3027, distinguishes signal spaces, windows, sampling sets, uniqueness modulo phase, and stability. It already reports both positive results on restricted signal classes and nonuniqueness for sampled Gaussian Gabor measurements on all of $L^2$. We do not replace those choices by a universal assertion about frames. We attempt an elementary span criterion and a global stability argument, then identify the limitations through explicit examples.

A complete finite real criterion

For a finite family $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_M\in\mathbb R^d$, phase retrieval up to sign holds exactly when, for every subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$, either its vectors or the vectors in its complement span $\mathbb R^d$. If both spans are proper, choose nonzero $u$ perpendicular to the first and nonzero $v$ perpendicular to the second. Then $f=u+v$ and $g=u-v$ have equal measurement magnitudes: on the first subset only the $v$ term contributes, with opposite signs, and on the complement only the $u$ term contributes. They are not equal up to sign, since either equality would force $u=0$ or $v=0$. Thus the condition is necessary. Conversely if $f,g$ have equal real magnitudes, then for every index \[ \langle f-g,\phi_i\rangle\langle f+g,\phi_i\rangle=0. \] Take $I$ to consist of those indices where the first factor is zero. On its complement the second is zero. The spanning condition forces $f-g=0$ or $f+g=0$, proving sufficiency.

This real argument fails for complex phases. For instance $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ have the real spanning property in dimension two, but complex vectors $(1,i)$ and $(1,-i)$ have the same three magnitudes and are not global phase multiples: the first coordinate would force the multiplier to be one. The factorization into two real linear factors is exactly the step that fails.

A continuous Gabor frame with explicit nonuniqueness

Take a nonzero bounded window $g$ supported in $[-R,R]$, $R>0$, and two unit signals $u,v$ with compact supports separated by more than $2R$. No translate of the window intersects both supports. Thus for every time-frequency point, at most one of $V_gu,V_gv$ is nonzero, and \[ |V_g(u+v)(x,\omega)|=|V_g(u-v)(x,\omega)|. \] The signals are orthogonal by their disjoint supports. For any $|c|=1$, \[ \|(u+v)-c(u-v)\|_2^2 =|1-c|^2+|1+c|^2=4, \] so they are not phase-equivalent.

This remains a frame example, not just an arbitrary deficient measurement system. With $V_gf(x,\omega)=\int f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\omega t}\,dt$, Plancherel in $\omega$ and Tonelli give \[ \int_{\mathbb R^2}|V_gf(x,\omega)|^2\,dx\,d\omega =\int_{\mathbb R}|f(t)|^2 \left(\int_{\mathbb R}|g(t-x)|^2dx\right)dt =\|g\|_2^2\|f\|_2^2. \] The product $f(t)g(t-x)$ is in $L^2$ for each $x$ by boundedness of $g$, justifying Plancherel; the equality extends with the usual Fourier $L^2$ interpretation. Hence the continuous Gabor system is a tight frame for linear measurements even though its magnitudes fail uniqueness. This does not address noncompact Gaussian windows or the source's bandlimited classes.

No uniform Lipschitz inverse for a countable infinite frame

Let $H$ be an infinite-dimensional real or complex Hilbert space and $(\phi_j)_{j\ge1}$ a frame with finite upper bound. We show that no dimension-free positive lower Lipschitz bound for the magnitude map holds on all unit vectors modulo phase. Fix a unit vector $u$ and $\eta>0$. Its analysis coefficients are square-summable, so a finite set $I$ satisfies \[ \sum_{j\notin I}|\langle u,\phi_j\rangle|^2<\eta^2. \] Choose a unit $v$ orthogonal to $u$ and all $\phi_j$ with $j\in I$; this is possible because their span is finite-dimensional and $H$ is infinite-dimensional. Set $f=u+v,g=u-v$. They have quotient distance two by the preceding orthogonality calculation. For complex numbers $a,b$, the reverse triangle inequality applied after cancelling either term gives \[ \left||a+b|-|a-b|\right|\le2\min(|a|,|b|). \] The measurement difference is zero on $I$ and at most $2|\langle u,\phi_j\rangle|$ off $I$. Therefore \[ \left\|\left(|\langle f,\phi_j\rangle| -|\langle g,\phi_j\rangle|\right)_j\right\|_{\ell_2}<2\eta. \] Dividing $f,g$ by $\sqrt2$ makes them unit vectors, with quotient distance $\sqrt2$ and measurement distance below $\sqrt2\eta$. Since $\eta$ is arbitrary, there is no uniform positive lower Lipschitz constant. The argument allows the frame to be phase-retrieving: lack of a uniform stable inverse does not by itself imply a pair with exactly equal magnitudes.
