Bose–Einstein Condensation in Quantum Boltzmann Dynamics

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In the isotropic Bose quantum Boltzmann model, $x\in[0,\infty)$ is kinetic energy. A measure solution $F_t$ is a positive finite mass-and-energy measure, with $\sup_{0\le t<T}\int(1+x)\,dF_t<\infty$, satisfying the source's weak collision equation against $C_b^2$ test functions. Its Lebesgue decomposition is $dF_t(x)=f(x,t)\sqrt{x}\,dx+d\mu_t(x)$. A condensate is the atom $\mu_t(\{0\})>0$ at zero energy. The source begins with no singular part, $dF_0=f_0(x)\sqrt{x}\,dx$, and seeks suitable low-temperature data with $f_0$ large near zero.

When uniform concentration already forces an atom.
Suppose $t\mapsto F_t$ is a narrowly continuous family of finite positive measures on $[0,\infty)$ for $0\leq t\leq t_0$, with $F_0(\{0\})=0$. Assume the source's weaker quantity is positive. Then some $c>0$, sequence $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$ and times $t_j\in[0,t_0]$ satisfy $F_{t_j}([0,\epsilon_j])\geq c$. Extract a subsequence with $t_j\to t_*$. For any $\delta>0$, choose a continuous compactly supported cutoff $\chi_\delta$ equal to one on $[0,\delta/2]$, between zero and one, and supported in $[0,\delta]$. For large $j$, \[ \int\chi_\delta\,dF_{t_j}\geq c, \quad\text{hence}\quad F_{t_*}([0,\delta])\geq c. \] The limit uses time continuity. Taking $\delta\downarrow0$ and continuity from above of the finite measure gives $F_{t_*}(\{0\})\geq c$. Since $F_0$ has no atom, $t_*>0$.
Strength of the extra hypothesis.
Only continuity against these cutoffs is needed; smooth $C_b^2$ cutoffs can be used. But it must hold on the whole compact interval, including any candidate condensation time. Without time compactness or continuity, narrow spikes may concentrate along times that do not yield an admissible limiting state. The proof does not establish the positive lower bound $c$ from the collision equation.
