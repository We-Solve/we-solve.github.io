FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #135; reconstruction, 2026-09-23. Suppose $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ has $n$ points and every four distinct points determine at least five distinct distances. Must $P$ determine $\gg n^2$ distances? We reconstruct the known negative answer.

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

Tao's 2024 paper resolves this by randomizing a finite-field parabola and deleting the remaining forbidden quadruples. We prove the probability calculation and alteration argument below. Two explicitly identified literature inputs are used: the count of non-parallelogram forbidden quadruples in a square grid, and the Landau--Ramanujan bound for sums of two squares. Neither is claimed as reproved here.

WORK

Let $Q=\{0,\ldots,M-1\}^2$ and choose a prime $4M<p<8M$. We use these external counting facts, in the form recorded in Tao's primary exposition:
  • Among four-element subsets of $Q$ having at most four distances, all except parallelograms number $O(M^5)$ in total. This is the forbidden-pattern classification and counting result of Dumitrescu.
  • The number of positive integers at most $2M^2$ representable as a sum of two integer squares is $O(M^2/\sqrt{\log M})$.
The second bound controls the number of distances in every subset of $Q$. We construct a valid subset of size at least a constant times $M$.

Choose $(a,b,c,d)$ uniformly with $ad-bc\ne0$ in $\mathbb F_p$, and choose $e$ uniformly and independently. Let \[ S=\{(x,y)\in Q:(ax+by)^2=cx+dy+e\pmod p\}. \] This is the intersection of the grid with an invertible affine image of the parabola $\{(t,t^2):t\in\mathbb F_p\}$. It contains no three collinear field points. It also contains no four distinct points forming a parallelogram: if $t_1+t_4=t_2+t_3$ and $t_1^2+t_4^2=t_2^2+t_3^2$, then the unordered pairs $\{t_1,t_4\}$ and $\{t_2,t_3\}$ are equal, by their sum and product. Any ordinary grid parallelogram would give such an equality modulo $p$, with its four points still distinct. Thus $S$ has no parallelograms.

Every fixed grid point belongs to $S$ with probability exactly $1/p$, since its defining equation specifies exactly one value of $e$. Consequently $\mathbb E|S|=M^2/p\ge M/8$. We next prove that every four distinct field points lie in $S$ with probability $O(p^{-4})$. If three are collinear this probability is zero. Otherwise affine invariance permits the normalization \[ (0,0),\ (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (s,t),\qquad st\ne0. \] Affine invariance holds because an invertible affine change of variables permutes our parameter tuples: a constant in the first linear form can be absorbed into the second after expanding the square, preserving the determinant. Membership of the first three points forces $e=0,c=a^2,d=b^2$. The fourth then imposes \[ (s^2-s)a^2+2stab+(t^2-t)b^2=0. \tag{1} \] This is a nonzero polynomial of degree two, because $2st\ne0$. It has at most $2p$ zeros in $\mathbb F_p^2$, by the elementary polynomial zero bound. There are $p(p^2-1)(p^2-p)\asymp p^5$ parameter tuples altogether, so the probability is $O(p/p^5)=O(p^{-4})$, as asserted.

Independently retain each point of $S$ with probability $\theta$, a small positive constant, to obtain $T$. Its expected size is at least $\theta M/8$. Let $Z$ count its forbidden four-element subsets. Parallelograms are absent; the counting input and $1$ give \[ \mathbb EZ\le C M^5\theta^4p^{-4}\le C'\theta^4M. \] Choose fixed $\theta$ sufficiently small that $C'\theta^4\le\theta/16$. Then $\mathbb E(|T|-Z)\ge\theta M/16$, so some outcome attains this bound. Delete at most one vertex for each forbidden quadruple, processing the original list. Deletion creates none, and leaves a set $P$ with \[ |P|\ge\theta M/16, \] all of whose four-element subsets have at least five distances. Select exactly $n=\lfloor\theta M/16\rfloor$ remaining points if necessary. Their distances are among those in the original grid, giving \[ D(P)=O(M^2/\sqrt{\log M})=O(n^2/\sqrt{\log n})=o(n^2). \] As $M\to\infty$, these examples disprove a universal quadratic lower bound.$\square$

VERIFICATION

The extra thinning is necessary: size and forbidden-quadruple counts before thinning are both of order $M$, with uncontrolled constants. The proof uses the expectation of their difference, so no independence between these two counts or concentration theorem is assumed. Every forbidden object is a four-element set, giving exactly the thinning factor $\theta^4$.
