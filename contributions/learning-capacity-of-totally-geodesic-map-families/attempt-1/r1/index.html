Totally geodesic capacity: an explicit circle shattering construction

Source scope and test of the finite-parameter route

The primary report supplies squared geodesic distance as regression loss, but the final classification question still does not select a formal capacity notion. We test strict threshold shattering of that loss class, a standard pseudodimension formulation: points $(x_i,y_i)$ and real thresholds $r_i$ are shattered if every binary pattern is realized by losses strictly above or below the corresponding thresholds. This is an explicit interpretation for the following auxiliary results, not a silent full specification of the catalogue's broad classification.
Euclidean totally geodesic maps are affine and have finite loss capacity.
If $F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^q$ is smooth and totally geodesic with affine geodesic parameter, every line has affine image, so $D^2F(x)[v,v]=0$ for every $x,v$. Polarizing the symmetric bilinear second derivative gives $2D^2F(x)[u,v]=D^2F(x)[u+v,u+v]-D^2F(x)[u,u]-D^2F(x)[v,v]=0$. Thus each first derivative is constant along line segments, and $F(x)=Ax+b$. Conversely affine maps take affine-parameterized lines to such lines and are totally geodesic. The loss $\|Ax+b-y\|_2^2$ is a polynomial of total degree at most two in $z=(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{d+q}$. All such losses lie in the span of $p=\binom{d+q+2}{2}$ monomial features $\psi(z)$. Any real linear feature class with $p$ features has strict threshold-shattering dimension at most $p$. Indeed, among more than $p$ feature vectors choose a nonzero relation $\sum_i\alpha_i\psi(z_i)=0$. For fixed thresholds $r_i$, put $s=\sum_i\alpha_i r_i$. If $s\ge0$, demand that $g(z_i)-r_i$ have the same strict sign as $\alpha_i$ wherever it is nonzero; then $\sum_i\alpha_i(g(z_i)-r_i)>0$, contradicting its value $-s\le0$. If $s<0$, demand opposite signs, producing a negative sum equal to $-s>0$. Indices with $\alpha_i=0$ may have either label. Thus not all patterns are possible. The squared-loss subclass inherits this finite bound.
Circle maps shatter arbitrarily many loss samples at one fixed margin.
Identify the circle with $\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ and use its geodesic distance. For every nonnegative integer $K$, the map $F_K(x)=Kx\pmod{2\pi}$ is totally geodesic: an affine lift $x_0+vt$ of any local geodesic maps to the affine lift $Kx_0+Kvt$. For any $m\ge1$, choose distinct inputs $x_i=2\pi/4^i$, outputs $y_i=0$, and thresholds $r_i=\pi^2/4$, $1\le i\le m$. For a prescribed binary pattern $(b_1,\ldots,b_m)$, choose $K=\sum_{j=1}^m2b_j4^{j-1}$, and add $4^m$ if a positive $K$ is desired. The fractional part of $K/4^i$ equals \[ \frac{b_i}{2}+\sum_{j<i}2b_j4^{j-1-i}, \qquad 0\le\sum_{j<i}2b_j4^{j-1-i}<1/6. \] If $b_i=0$, the image angle is in $[0,\pi/3)$ and its distance to zero is less than $\pi/3$. If $b_i=1$, the angle is in $[\pi,4\pi/3)$ and that distance is greater than $2\pi/3$ (or equals $\pi$ at the left endpoint). Therefore the squared loss is respectively less than $\pi^2/9$ or greater than $4\pi^2/9$. It is below or above $\pi^2/4$ according to the desired bit, with margin at least $\pi^2/10$. This proves infinite pseudodimension, and even infinite fixed-margin shattering dimension at that stated margin, for this circle loss class. Only one integer parameter was varied. Finite-dimensional or one-parameter parametrization alone therefore cannot imply finite statistical capacity.
The source's universal interpolation sentence needs qualifications.
The displayed source theorem speaks of arbitrary training data for maps $Kx+\delta\pmod{2\pi}$. Literally, the distinct inputs $0,\pi$ with targets $0,\pi/2$ cannot be approximated to error $\varepsilon<\pi/4$ by such a map. Their image difference is $K\pi$, either zero or $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$, and its distance from the target difference $\pi/2$ is $\pi/2$. If both individual errors were at most $\varepsilon$, the triangle inequality and translation invariance of circle distance would bound this difference error by $2\varepsilon<\pi/2$, a contradiction. Thus some nonresonance or sampling qualification would be needed for that universal wording. The explicit shattering proof above avoids assuming it. This discrepancy does not refute the source's broader infinite-capacity insight.

Adversarial audit and classification gap

The audit checked affine parameterization, global circle well-definedness (integer $K$), every base-four digit bound, and the use of the actual squared geodesic loss. These are complete special-case proofs, not a classification of all manifold pairs. They recover known finite/infinite examples and invalidate the attempted finite-parameter criterion. A specified capacity notion and new geometric conditions for general $M,N$ remain necessary.
