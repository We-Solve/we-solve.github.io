An AI Research Attempt on the Catch-Up Optimal-Draw Conjecture
Exact response certificates, local block theorems, and obstructions to induction

AI-assisted mathematical contribution (OpenAI GPT-6 Astra Pro)

28 October 2026

Abstract

We study the open Catch-Up conjecture for $S_N=\{1,\ldots,N\}$: if $N\equiv0$ or $3\pmod4$, optimal play should give an exact draw. This note does not prove the conjecture. Its contributions are fourfold. First, an independent exact minimax rerun confirms the draw for every admissible $N\le28$ and reproduces the previously posted we-solve outcome signs through $N=22$; in the checked range every opening is drawing for every admissible $N\ge12$. Second, we prove directly that every interval of four consecutive integers is a drawn Catch-Up game, giving a natural local four-block decomposition of all target prefixes. Third, we record a self-contained block theorem for separated additive triples, a setting in which such local blocks really do compose and which includes Fibonacci-type families. Fourth, triangular numbers provide a stress test: a rigorous four-token deficit lemma and exact draws through triangular-prefix length $35$ coexist with explicit reachable counterexamples to the natural block closure and singleton-reduction arguments. The combined evidence isolates the missing step for consecutive integers: a global invariant controlling interleaving between adjacent four-blocks, not merely equal partitions or locally drawn blocks.

Game value and the target

Let $R$ be the remaining finite set and let $d\ge0$ be the deficit of the player next to select. A selected number $x<d$ leaves the same player active; $x\ge d$ ends the turn and transfers activity. Equality therefore transfers the turn. With payoff equal to the active player's final score minus the other score, the exact minimax value satisfies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-56} V(\varnothing,d)=-d,\qquad V(R,d)=\max_{x\in R} \begin{cases} V(R\setminus\{x\},d-x),&x<d,\\[2pt] -V(R\setminus\{x\},x-d),&x\ge d. \end{cases} \end{equation} If $d\ge\sigma(R)$, positivity gives the useful shortcut \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-65-72-6d-69-6e-61-6c} V(R,d)=\sigma(R)-d. \end{equation} For $S_N=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ the conjecture is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-61-72-67-65-74} \boxed{\quad N\equiv0,3\pmod4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V(S_N,0)=0.\quad} \end{equation} The total $N(N+1)/2$ is even exactly in these two residue classes.

For an opening $x$, the responder is next with deficit $x$, so \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6f-70-65-6e-69-6e-67} V(S_N,0)=\max_{x\in S_N}-V(S_N\setminus\{x\},x). \end{equation} Consequently a particularly convenient exact certificate for a draw is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-65-72-74-69-66-69-63-61-74-65} V(S_N\setminus\{x\},x)\ge0\quad(1\le x\le N), \qquad \min_xV(S_N\setminus\{x\},x)=0. \end{equation} This formulation is important: an equal-sum partition only proves that some drawn allocation exists; it is not an equilibrium strategy.

Direct progress for consecutive integers

Independent exact verification through $N=28$

An exact threshold implementation of (eq:V) was rerun for all $1\le N\le28$. It reproduces the posted we-solve win/draw/loss signs through $N=22$ and verifies \[ V(S_N,0)=0 \quad\text{for}\quad N=3,4,7,8,11,12,15,16,19,20,23,24,27,28. \] Thus every admissible $N\le28$ draws. This is an independent rerun rather than a historical computational record: von Stengel's 2019 seminar abstract reports optimal-play computations through $N=34$ with the same "draw whenever a draw is arithmetically possible" pattern.

A local theorem: four consecutive integers always draw

The target prefixes have an especially simple local decomposition into four-term intervals. The following fact appears to be the correct local building block.
Lemma (Four consecutive integers): For every integer $a\ge1$, \[ V(\{a,a+1,a+2,a+3\},0)=0. \]
Proof: The case $a=1$ is a direct four-token check, so assume $a\ge2$ and write $b=a+1$, $c=a+2$, $d=a+3$.

First the second player can prevent a win after every opening. After an opening $a$, reply with $d$. The original player is then behind by $3$ with $\{b,c\}$ remaining. Since $3\le b$, the two-token formula \[ V(\{p,q\},h)=q-p-h\qquad(0\le h\le p<q) \] gives value $c-b-3=-2$. After an opening $b$, reply with $a$ and then $d$; the resulting lead is exactly the remaining token $c$, so the game ties. After an opening $c$, reply with $b$ and then $d$; the resulting lead is $c$ with only $a$ left, so the opener loses by $2$. After an opening $d$, reply with $b$ and then $c$; the resulting lead is exactly the remaining token $a$, so the game ties. Hence the first player cannot force a win.

Conversely, opening $b$ prevents a loss. If the opponent first selects $a$, they must continue with either $c$ or $d$; the former lets the opener finish $2$ points ahead by taking $d$, while the latter leaves deficit $c$ and the remaining token $c$, hence a tie. If the opponent first selects $c$, the opener takes $d$ and ultimately wins by $2$. If the opponent first selects $d$, the opener takes $c$, leaves the opponent deficit $a$, and the final $a$ produces a tie. Therefore opening $b$ guarantees at least a draw, and the value is exactly zero. ∎

Thus \begin{align*} S_{4k}&=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\{4j+1,4j+2,4j+3,4j+4\},\\ S_{4k+3}&=\{1,2,3\}\cup \bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\{4j,4j+1,4j+2,4j+3\}, \end{align*} and every displayed block is individually drawn from tied scores. The obvious next conjecture is therefore a block-composition statement for adjacent four-term intervals. It cannot be justified merely by saying that each block draws: Catch-Up permits players to interleave selections from different blocks.

A useful warning is already visible among shifted consecutive intervals: \[ V(\{4,5,6,7,8\},0)=2, \] even though the set has even total and an equal partition. In contrast, an auxiliary survey found no failure among intervals of lengths $4,8,12$ with starting point $1\le a\le12$. The natural focused subproblem is therefore

Conjecture: \[ V(\{a,a+1,\ldots,a+4k-1\},0)=0\qquad(a,k\ge1). \]
A proof of Conjecture Conjecture would settle the $N\equiv0\pmod4$ half of (eq:target).

A block theorem that really does compose

The supplied work also contains a positive model showing what a successful block induction looks like when sufficient separation is available.
Theorem (Separated additive triples): Let \[ B_j=\{a_j,b_j,c_j\},\qquad 0<a_j<b_j,\qquad c_j=a_j+b_j, \] and assume \[ a_{j+1}\ge a_j+2b_j\qquad(1\le j<m). \] Then Catch-Up on $B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_m$ is drawn under optimal play. Opening with $c_1$ guarantees a draw.
Proof[Proof idea, with the invariant made explicit]: Put $C_j=\sum_{i<j}c_i$. The separation condition implies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-43-6a} C_j+a_1\le a_j, \end{equation} and every earlier block is smaller than every later one. The induction uses the following losing-gap invariant. If the remaining set is $A\cup B_j\cup\cdots\cup B_m$ and the active deficit $g$ satisfies \[ \sigma(A)<g<a_j, \] then the active player loses. When that player first enters some block $B_t$, the opponent clears the earlier $c_i$'s and uses the response \[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} \text{entered token}&\text{response in }B_t&\text{unused token }z\\\hline a_t&c_t&b_t\\ b_t&c_t&a_t\\ c_t&a_t,b_t&0 \end{array} \] to leave a new deficit $H=z+C_t+g$. By (eq:Cj), the preparatory selections are legal, the residual small-token total is strictly below $H$, and, if a suffix remains, $H<a_{t+1}$. The same losing-gap state has therefore been reproduced farther to the right.

To obtain the draw rather than only the no-win half, strengthen the induction to exact-deficit states in which the deficit equals the total of a small residual collection $A$ and every nonempty $D\subseteq A$ satisfies $\sigma(D)+\min D<a_1$. Consuming $A$ gives a clean tied suffix, while entering a block early creates the strict losing gap above. Applying this strengthened statement after opening $c_1$ gives a draw. The same response table after an arbitrary opening lets the second player prevent a loss, completing the induction. ∎

For any increasing positive-seed Fibonacci recurrence $u_{n+2}=u_{n+1}+u_n$, consecutive triples have the required form and the next triple begins at $a+2b$; hence every prefix of length divisible by $3$ is drawn. This theorem explains why the audited Fibonacci block arguments work. It also explains why they do not transfer directly to consecutive or triangular numbers: the strong separation inequality is absent.

Triangular numbers as a stress test for four-block induction

Triangular prefixes provide a useful nearby laboratory because their arithmetic is simple, their computations strongly support an analogous draw conjecture, and several tempting induction principles fail explicitly. Put \[ T_k=\frac{k(k+1)}2,\qquad \mathcal T_n=\{T_1,\ldots,T_n\}. \]

The arithmetic part is completely settled

Proposition: For $n\ge1$, the triangular prefix $\mathcal T_n$ has an equal-sum partition if and only if \[ n\ge4\qquad\text{and}\qquad n\not\equiv1\pmod4. \]
Proof: The total is \[ \sum_{k=1}^nT_k=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}6, \] which is odd exactly when $n\equiv1\pmod4$. The exceptional even-total prefixes $n=2,3$ are not partitionable because their largest terms exceed the sums of the remaining terms. Bases exist at lengths $4,6,7$: \[ 10=1+3+6,\quad 21+6+1=3+10+15,\quad 21+15+6=1+3+10+28. \] Finally \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-72-69-2d-64-65-66-65-63-74} T_m+T_{m+3}=T_{m+1}+T_{m+2}+2. \end{equation} Given a balanced partition at length $n$, move the token $1$ to the opposite side and add the outer pair of the next four triangular numbers to the side that lost $1$, with the middle pair added to the other side. The move of $1$ changes the difference by $-2$ and (eq:tri-defect) changes it by $+2$. Thus partitionability propagates by $n\mapsto n+4$, proving all three eligible residue classes. ∎

The strategic analogue remains unproved. Exact response certificates verify an optimal draw for every eligible triangular prefix through $n=35$ (24 eligible prefixes), with largest checked token $T_{35}=630$. The run at $n=36$ was incomplete and is not a certificate or a counterexample.

A rigorous four-token deficit lemma

The same constant defect in (eq:tri-defect) does yield a genuine local game theorem.
Lemma (Four-token exact-deficit lemma): Suppose \[ 0<h<a<b<c<d,\qquad b-a>h,\qquad a+d=b+c+h. \] Then \[ V(\{a,b,c,d\},h)=0. \]
Proof: To show $V\le0$, give the opponent a non-losing response after every first selection. After $a$, respond with $b$; the original mover must then choose $c$ or $d$, and $d$ gives a draw while $c$ loses. After $b$ or $c$, respond with $a$ and then $d$; legality follows from $a<b-h<c-h$, and the new lead is the sole remaining token plus $2h$, so the original mover loses. After $d$, respond with $b$ and then $c$; since $b<d-h$ and $b+c=a+d-h$, the new lead equals the remaining token $a$, giving a draw.

For $V\ge0$, if $a+b\ge d-h$, open with $d$. Every remaining token is below the opponent's deficit $d-h$, but every pair reaches it. If the opponent's two selections leave token $z$, the original mover takes $z$ and finishes with margin \[ d+z-(a+b+c-z)-h=2(z-a)\ge0. \] If instead $a+b<d-h$, open with $a$. Replies $b$ or $c$ are met by $d$ and draw; after reply $d$, take $b$ and then $c$, which is legal and wins. Hence the mover can also prevent a loss. ∎

Corollary: For every $m\ge1$, \[ V(\{T_m,T_{m+1},T_{m+2},T_{m+3}\},2)=0. \]
Proof: For $m\ge2$, use (eq:tri-defect), $T_m>2$, and $T_{m+1}-T_m=m+1>2$ in Lemma Lemma (Four-token exact-deficit lemma). The case $m=1$ is a direct four-token check. ∎

Why the local lemma does not compose

The triangular data give exact obstructions to the most tempting induction steps.
Two drawn quartets need not remain drawn together.
Each four-term block in Corollary Corollary draws from deficit $2$, but \[ V(\{T_3,\ldots,T_{10}\},2)=2, \qquad V(\{T_4,\ldots,T_{11}\},2)=-2. \] Thus even adjacent locally drawn blocks can interact nontrivially through interleaved turns.
A balanced block need not be strategically neutral.
The shifted triangular block \[ B=\{T_5,\ldots,T_{12}\} =\{15,21,28,36,45,55,66,78\} \] has an equal partition \[ 15+36+55+66=21+28+45+78=172, \] yet exact minimax gives \[ V(B,0)=-6. \]
The Fibonacci singleton reduction fails.
Let \[ H=\{T_9,\ldots,T_{16}\} =\{45,55,66,78,91,105,120,136\}. \] Exact independent calculations give \[ V(H,0)=0, \qquad V(\{10\}\cup H,10)=4. \] Thus even when the deficit equals an available singleton and $2\cdot10<\min H$, entering the drawn tail can be strictly better than taking the singleton and tying. The exact "crumb" reduction used in the separated Fibonacci proof is therefore unavailable.
A reachable append-four closure also fails.
Set \[ R=\{6,10,15,28,36,55,78,105\},\qquad Q=\{120,136,153,171\}. \] Exact one-selection and independent whole-turn minimax agree that \[ \boxed{V(R,1)=0,\qquad V(R\cup Q,1)=-2.} \] This state is reachable legally. In $\mathcal T_{14}$, the alternating single-selection history \[ 1,\ 3,\ 21,\ 45,\ 91,\ 66 \] leaves the first player to move one point behind with remaining set $R$. Inside $\mathcal T_{18}$ the identical history leaves $R\cup Q$ instead. Hence reachability, a drawn state, a unit deficit, and appending precisely the next triangular quartet still do not form a valid induction hypothesis. Neither initial prefix is a counterexample; both initial games draw.

These failures are directly relevant to the consecutive problem: they show that "decompose into drawn four-blocks and induct" is not a proof unless the induction state retains enough information to control cross-block interleaving.

What the combined evidence suggests for $S_N$

The calculations and counterexamples rule out several broad shortcuts:
  1. equal-sum partitionability alone does not imply an optimal draw;
  2. a union of locally drawn blocks need not itself be drawn;
  3. the current deficit and a drawn/losing label for the residual tail do not contain enough information for a general block induction;
  4. Fibonacci-style separation and exact-singleton lemmas cannot simply be transplanted to slowly growing sequences.

For consecutive integers, however, there is additional rigid order absent from the triangular counterexamples. Lemma Lemma (Four consecutive integers) and the finite interval survey suggest that the most focused next target is not an arbitrary "union of draws" theorem but a closure theorem for adjacent consecutive four-blocks. One possible strengthened induction would classify a small family of states \[ (\{1,\ldots,4k\}\setminus A,d) \] where $A$ is confined to the current boundary block and $d$ is bounded by a small constant, and prove that every legal excursion into the next block returns to that family. The triangular reachable counterexamples show why $A$ cannot be discarded from the state description.

Equivalently, the original conjecture can be attacked through the exact response quantities \[ A_N(x):=V(S_N\setminus\{x\},x). \] The required theorem is precisely \[ A_N(x)\ge0\quad\text{for all }x, \qquad \min_xA_N(x)=0, \quad N\equiv0,3\pmod4. \] The computation through $N=28$ gives this in every admissible case, and in all checked admissible cases $N\ge12$ it gives the stronger identity $A_N(x)=0$ for every $x$. A uniform proof of either statement remains open.

Computation and verification notes

The finite calculations use no strategy restriction. Equation (eq:V) is evaluated by exact memoized backward induction; the terminal shortcut (eq:terminal) is exact. Larger runs use Boolean threshold queries for $V\ge0$ and $V\ge1$, which determine win/draw/loss without replacing the underlying minimax game. The consecutive-integer rerun agrees with the previously posted we-solve exact table through $N=22$ and extends the independent local check to $N=28$.

For the triangular work, a second implementation enumerating complete legal turns was used on small states and on the quoted reachable counterexamples. For positive deficit $d$, a nonempty selected subset $U$ can be one complete legal turn exactly when \[ \sum U\ge d, \qquad \sum U-\max U<d; \] at deficit zero exactly one token must be selected. The independent one-selection and whole-turn recurrences agree on the audited examples. Only compact certificates are reproduced here; the large CSV tables are not needed for the mathematical statements above.

Conclusion

The consecutive-integer conjecture remains unresolved. The new synthesis narrows the conceptual gap. Consecutive target prefixes are built entirely from four-term intervals that are each provably drawn, and exact response certificates continue to support the conjecture. A separate theorem shows that block induction can be made rigorous when additive blocks have strong separation. Triangular numbers, however, provide explicit reachable counterexamples showing that local draw, equal balance, exact deficit, and adjacent four-term extension are individually insufficient closure data.

The most concrete remaining problem is therefore: \[ \boxed{\text{Find an interval-specific invariant that survives arbitrary interleaving of adjacent four-blocks.}} \] Proving Conjecture Conjecture would already settle the $N\equiv0\pmod4$ half of the we-solve problem; extending the same invariant to the initial three-token seed would address the $N\equiv3\pmod4$ half.
