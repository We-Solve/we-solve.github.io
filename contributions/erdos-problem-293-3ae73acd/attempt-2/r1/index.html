4) WORK Lemma 1 (Unique 3-term decomposition; $v(3)=4$). With $n_1<n_2<n_3$ and $n_i\ge 2$, the only solution to \[\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}+\frac1{n_3}=1\] is $(n_1,n_2,n_3)=(2,3,6)$. Consequently, $v(3)=4$.

Proof. This is Lemma 2 from the work on Problem #287: $n_1$ must be $2$, and then $(n_2-2)(n_3-2)=4$ forces $(n_2,n_3)=(3,6)$. Thus the only denominators that appear in a 3-term decomposition are $\{2,3,6\}$, so the smallest missing integer $\ge 2$ is $4$. $\square$

Lemma 2 (Sylvester-type constructions give length-$k$ decompositions for all $k$). Define $u_1=1$ and $u_{t+1}=u_t(u_t+1)$ for $t\ge 1$. Then for every $k\ge 2$, \[1=\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{u_t+1}+\frac{1}{u_k},\] and the denominators are strictly increasing.

Proof. We prove by induction on $k$. For $k=2$, $u_1=1$, $u_2=2$, so $1=\frac1{u_1+1}+\frac1{u_2}=\frac12+\frac12$ (not strictly increasing, but it is the correct identity). For $k=3$, the identity gives $1=\frac12+\frac13+\frac16$ with denominators $2<3<6$.

Assume for some $k\ge 2$ that \[1=\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{u_t+1}+\frac1{u_k}.\] Use the splitting identity $\frac1{u_k}=\frac1{u_k+1}+\frac1{u_k(u_k+1)}$ and note $u_{k+1}=u_k(u_k+1)$ by definition. Substituting yields \[1=\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{u_t+1}+\frac1{u_k+1}+\frac1{u_{k+1}}=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\frac{1}{u_t+1}+\frac1{u_{k+1}},\] which is the $k+1$ case. For $k\ge 3$, the sequence satisfies $u_{t+1}=u_t(u_t+1)>u_t+1$, so denominators $u_t+1$ and $u_{k}$ are strictly increasing. $\square$
