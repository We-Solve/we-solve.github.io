Two exact model endpoints, without inventing an evaporation rule

The primary report already states particular ant-colony runtime results, including a high-evaporation relation to the $(1+1)$ evolutionary algorithm and a change near an inverse-linear evaporation scale. The extract does not define the complete pheromone update rule. We therefore cannot interpolate between evaporation factors merely from its symbol $\rho$. We test two explicitly defined sampling processes on OneMax, as auxiliary endpoints rather than an asserted complete ACO model.
Proposition: If every evaluation is an independent uniform bit string in $\{0,1\}^n$, the expected number of evaluations through the first optimum is $2^n$. For $n\ge2$, the elitist $(1+1)$ process that flips each bit independently with probability $1/n$ and accepts a child whenever its OneMax value is no smaller than its parent's has expected optimization time at most $enH_n$ mutation evaluations from any initial string, where $H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n1/j$.
Proof: Uniform sampling hits the unique all-ones string with probability $2^{-n}$ independently at every evaluation. Its first-success time has tail $(1-2^{-n})^t$ after $t$ evaluations, so summing the geometric tail gives mean $2^n$. For the elitist process, if the current string has $j>0$ zeros, flipping exactly one of those zeros and no other bit improves the objective. Its probability is \[ p_j=\frac jn(1-1/n)^{n-1}\ge\frac j{en}. \] To justify the inequality, $-\log(1-1/n)=\int_0^{1/n}(1-t)^{-1}dt\le1/(n-1)$; exponentiating gives $(1-1/n)^{n-1}\ge e^{-1}$. While at level $j$, even if accepted equal-fitness moves change the string, each independent mutation has probability at least $p_j$ of leaving toward a better level. The probability of spending at least $t$ further unsuccessful iterations at that level is therefore at most $(1-p_j)^t$, by conditional multiplication. Its expected residence time is at most $1/p_j\le en/j$. Elitism prevents return to a worse level, and skipping levels only decreases this sum, giving the bound $enH_n$. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

For $n=2,\ldots,8$, the exact transition probabilities between OneMax levels were computed by summing all bit-flip patterns, and the triangular hitting-time equations were solved with rational arithmetic. Their worst initial-state expectations satisfy the displayed bound. The no-learning uniform sampler has exponential mean, whereas the specified elitist process has a polynomial bound. These processes do not define every intermediate ACO update or identify an evaporation threshold. In particular, assigning the first process the label $\rho=0$ would additionally require a fixed uniform initialization and a rule leaving it unchanged, assumptions not supplied by the original extract. The attempted continuum classification stops at the missing update dynamics, not at a proved threshold.
