Construction of K-Matrices for Coideal Subalgebras

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An $R$-matrix encodes the exchange relation of an integrable model. A boundary $K$-matrix solves the corresponding reflection equation; one common additive-parameter convention is $R_{12}(u-v)K_1(u)R_{21}(u+v)K_2(v)=K_2(v)R_{12}(u+v)K_1(u)R_{21}(u-v)$. Subscripts mark tensor legs and $R_{21}=PR_{12}P$. A coideal subalgebra $B$ of a Hopf algebra $A$ has coproduct taking values in $B\otimes A$ or $A\otimes B$, according to convention.

A rational scalar-boundary check.
On $V\otimes V$ let $P(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x$ and take $R(u)=uI+\eta P$, with fixed scalar $\eta$. Since $P^2=I$, all $R(u)$ commute and $R_{21}(u)=R(u)$. For any scalar function $k(u)$ the boundary matrix $K(u)=k(u)I_V$ satisfies the displayed ordinary reflection equation: both sides equal $k(u)k(v)R(u-v)R(u+v)$. This verifies a rational family, including normalization choices $k(0)=1$ if desired.
A less trivial constant ansatz.
Let $K$ be a constant matrix with $K^2=cI$. Put $A=K\otimes I$, $B=I\otimes K$, so $AB=BA$, $PA=BP$ and $PB=AP$. Expanding the two sides for $R(x)=xI+\eta P$, $x=u-v$, $y=u+v$, their difference reduces to $\eta x(A^2-B^2)P$, which vanishes under $K^2=cI$. The terms proportional to $xy$ and $\eta^2$ cancel using these identities, and the other mixed terms coincide. Singular $K$ is allowed algebraically, although a physical boundary may require invertibility.
Why this is not the requested method.
These are ordinary rational reflection-equation checks, with no coideal intertwining, twist or trigonometric parameter dependence. A solution of this matrix equation need not arise from the specified twisted Yangian or quantum symmetric pair. The first gap is relating representation-theoretic coideal data to a normalized spectral-parameter family in both source cases. The elementary ansätze do not settle that general construction problem.
