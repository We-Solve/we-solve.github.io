Definitions and conventions.
For a lattice $\Gamma$ with finite-dimensional site Hilbert spaces, $\Phi(X)$ is a short-range interaction supported on finite $X\subseteq\Gamma$, and $H_\Lambda=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}\Phi(X)$ is the local Hamiltonian. Infinite-volume ground states are thermodynamic-limit states on the quasi-local observable algebra. In the source, two ground-state sets belong to the same gapped phase when a differentiable path $\Phi_s$, $s\in[0,1]$, connects their interactions, has the required uniform locality bounds, and has a common positive lower bound $\gamma>0$ for the local ground-state spectral gap.

An explicit uniformly gapped product-state path.
Fix a site space of finite dimension at least two and two unit vectors $v,w$. Choose a unitary $U$ with $Uv=w$, diagonalize it, and choose a self-adjoint logarithm $K$ so $U=e^{iK}$. Put $v_s=e^{isK}v$ and define the on-site interaction $h_s=I-|v_s\rangle\langle v_s|$, $0\leq s\leq1$. For any finite nonempty region $\Lambda$, \[ H_{\Lambda,s}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h_s^{(x)} \] is a sum of commuting projections. Its unique ground vector is $v_s^{\otimes|\Lambda|}$, and every nonzero eigenvalue is a positive integer. The gap is exactly one, uniformly in $s$ and $\Lambda$. The path is differentiable with uniformly bounded on-site derivative $i[K,h_s]$.
Infinite-volume interpretation.
The product states have consistent local density matrices, so define states on local observables and extend by norm continuity to the quasi-local algebra. The constructed path places all such unconstrained on-site product ground states in the same phase under the source definition. No finite-volume gap is inferred from a numerical finite-size sequence; it was computed exactly for every volume.
Limits of classification.
An imposed symmetry could forbid the chosen $U_s$, and degenerate, entangled or topologically ordered ground-state sets need not admit this on-site path. The source already defines gapped phases and supplies spectral flow; this calculation is just a verified elementary phase class, not a new definition or classification. The first gap is a complete invariant and a constructive gapped interpolation criterion for the broader interaction class, respecting any chosen symmetries and ground degeneracies.
