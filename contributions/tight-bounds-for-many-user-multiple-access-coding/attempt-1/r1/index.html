Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

In a Gaussian multiple-access channel, $K_n$ users transmit length-$n$ codewords and the receiver observes their sum plus Gaussian noise. The many-user regime has $K_n/n\to\mu>0$, where $\mu$ is user density. An achievable bound constructs codes meeting the energy-per-information-bit and target-error constraints; a converse rules out performance beyond a bound for every permitted code. In random access, activity of users is itself uncertain and must be accounted for in decoding.

Research attempt: A per-user Fano converse and the block-error obstruction

Let $K$ independent messages $W_i$ be uniform on $[M]$, $M\geq2$. User $i$ transmits a deterministic $n$-dimensional vector $X_i(W_i)$ with $\mathbb E\|X_i\|_2^2\leq E$. Let $Y=\sum_iX_i+Z$, with independent $Z\sim N(0,I_n)$. Let $\epsilon_i=\Pr\{\widehat W_i(Y)\ne W_i\}$ and $\bar\epsilon=K^{-1}\sum_i\epsilon_i$. All logarithms in this calculation are natural. Define $h_2(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)$.

Introduce the error indicator for user $i$. Given $Y$, the true message is determined on the no-error event and has at most $M-1$ possibilities on the error event. Entropy chain rules therefore give \[ H(W_i\mid Y)\leq h_2(\epsilon_i)+\epsilon_i\log(M-1). \] Here conditioning reduces entropy because the difference is conditional mutual information, an average of nonnegative relative entropies; nonnegativity follows by summing $-\log t\geq1-t$. Summing conditional entropies bounds joint conditional entropy, and concavity of $h_2$ (its second derivative is $-1/[t(1-t)]$) gives \[ I(W_1,\ldots,W_K;Y)\geq K\{\log M-h_2(\bar\epsilon)-\bar\epsilon\log(M-1)\}. \] For an imposed bound $\bar\epsilon\leq\epsilon\leq1-1/M$, the right side with $\epsilon$ in place of $\bar\epsilon$ is still a lower bound, since $h_2(t)+t\log(M-1)$ is increasing up to $1-1/M$.

Independence of the users implies \[ \operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(Y) =n+\sum_i\mathbb E\|X_i-\mathbb EX_i\|_2^2\leq n+KE. \] To justify the entropy bound directly, compare the density of $Y$ with the Gaussian having the same mean and covariance $s^2I_n$, where $ns^2=\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(Y)$. Nonnegativity of relative entropy implies $h(Y)\leq (n/2)\log(2\pi e s^2)$: integrate the Gaussian log density and use its quadratic expectation $n$. Also $h(Y\mid W_1,\ldots,W_K)=h(Z)=(n/2)\log(2\pi e)$ by translation. Consequently \[ K\{\log M-h_2(\bar\epsilon)-\bar\epsilon\log(M-1)\} \leq {n\over2}\log(1+KE/n). \tag{1} \] For $K/n\to\mu$ and fixed $M,E,\epsilon$, $1$ gives the necessary condition \[ \mu\{\log M-h_2(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log(M-1)\} \leq\tfrac12\log(1+\mu E) \] in the stated target-error range.

One tempting strengthening applies block-message Fano with the same $\epsilon$. That substitution is invalid. For example, $K$ independent error events of probability $\epsilon\in(0,1)$ have average per-user error $\epsilon$ and block error $1-(1-\epsilon)^K\to1$. This example concerns the relation between error metrics; it is not asserted to arise from every code. A per-user error constraint alone cannot justify replacing the block error by $\epsilon$.
