Euclidean Extension of Hierarchical Susceptibility Asymptotics

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

For a finite system, susceptibility measures the integrated connected two-point correlation, equivalently the linear response to a uniform field. The hierarchical four-dimensional $|\phi|^4$ model organizes interactions by nested blocks. The Euclidean counterpart uses the ordinary periodic lattice. Finite-size scaling concerns the joint dependence on the system size and distance to criticality.

An exact zero-mode scaling calculation.
Let $V>0$ denote volume, $g>0$ a quartic coefficient and $t\in\mathbb R$ a mass parameter. In the scalar constant-mode integral with density proportional to $\exp[-V(tm^2/2+gm^4/4)]$, define susceptibility $\chi_0=V\langle m^2\rangle$. Substituting $m=(Vg)^{-1/4}s$ gives \[ \chi_0=V^{1/2}g^{-1/2}\,F(t\sqrt{V/g}),\qquad F(z)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb R}s^2e^{-zs^2/2-s^4/4}ds}{\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-zs^2/2-s^4/4}ds}. \] Both integrals converge for every real $z$, since the quartic term dominates; the denominator is strictly positive. The candidate window is therefore $t$ of order $\sqrt{g/V}$, without changing any integration contour.
The missing comparison.
Integrating out nonconstant Euclidean lattice modes generally changes the effective mass, quartic coupling and higher interactions. Even a small pointwise remainder need not be small after rescaling throughout the growing integration region. A rigorous transfer requires an error bound dominated by an integrable quartic tail and uniform in the source scaling variable, together with the correct running-coupling normalization.
