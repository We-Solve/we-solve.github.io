Four-Dimensional Standard-Subspace Nets with Minimal Length

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A closed real subspace $H$ of a complex Hilbert space is standard when $H\cap iH=\{0\}$ and $\overline{H+iH}$ is the whole space. A local net assigns such subspaces to spacetime regions, is isotone under inclusion, and makes spacelike-separated subspaces orthogonal for the symplectic form $\operatorname{Im}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. A double cone is the intersection of a future and a past light cone in Minkowski spacetime.

A tempting threshold construction and its defect.
Start with a local isotone real-subspace net $K(O)$ and choose a radius $\ell>0$ for a restricted family of nested standard double cones. Define $H(O)=0$ for radius below $\ell$ and $H(O)=K(O)$ above it. On that nested family isotony and locality survive: replacing subspaces by zero cannot violate symplectic orthogonality, and crossing the threshold only enlarges a subspace.
Standardness immediately fails below threshold.
In a nonzero ambient complex Hilbert space, the zero real subspace satisfies $0\cap i0=0$ but $\overline{0+i0}=0\ne\mathcal H$, so it is not standard. Thus this artificial cutoff does not meet a literal requirement of standard subspaces for every double cone. It can also break additivity: if a large region is covered by small regions assigned zero, their generated subspace is zero while the large-region assignment may be nonzero.
What remains to formulate and prove.
The source's positive minimal length may refer to a subtler localization property, and the catalogue does not define it precisely. The preceding construction is therefore a failed toy route, not a contradiction in the original problem. One must recover the intended minimal-length condition and covariance requirements and then construct a four-dimensional net satisfying them without silently weakening standardness or additivity. No naturalness criterion or Poincaré-covariant extension of the radius cutoff is claimed.
