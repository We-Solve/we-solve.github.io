Research attempt: The recovered information inequality on Gaussian inputs

Let $X$ be Gaussian with mean $m$ and positive semidefinite covariance $\Sigma$, and let $G$ be an independent standard Gaussian in $\mathbb R^n$. Its log moment generating function at $g$ is $\langle g,m\rangle+\tfrac12g^T\Sigma g$, so \[ E_G\log E_X e^{\langle G,X\rangle}=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\Sigma. \] This follows from $EG=0$ and $E(G_iG_j)=\mathbf1_{i=j}$. The Gaussian moment-generating formula follows by completing the square in each independent normal coordinate after diagonalizing the covariance; zero-eigenvalue coordinates are deterministic.

The mutual information is \[ I(X+G;X)=h(X+G)-h(G)=\tfrac12\log\det(I+\Sigma). \] For completeness, the Gaussian entropy formula follows by taking the negative log of its density and averaging the quadratic form, whose expected value equals the dimension. The difference identity here follows by averaging $\log[p_{X+G\mid X}(Y\mid X)/p_{X+G}(Y)]$: conditionally, Y is a translate of G and has the same entropy. It does not require a density for X itself, so singular $\Sigma$ is allowed. If $\lambda_i\ge0$ are the covariance eigenvalues, then $\log(1+\lambda_i)\le\lambda_i$, because integrating $1/(1+t)\le1$ from zero to $\lambda_i$ proves the inequality. Summing establishes the proposed information inequality with $c=1$ throughout this Gaussian subclass.

The constant cannot be universally less than one even just in one dimension: for variance $t>0$, the ratio of the two sides before multiplying by c is $\log(1+t)/t$, which tends to one as $t\downarrow0$ by the integral formula and the bounds $1/(1+t)\le\log(1+t)/t\le1$. Thus the Gaussian test gives a necessary lower bound on any universal constant.

A tempting pointwise strengthening fails. For deterministic $X=m\ne0$, mutual information is zero, while the unaveraged log moment generating function is $\langle G,m\rangle$, negative with positive probability. No positive constant can make $0\le c\langle G,m\rangle$ hold almost surely. Its expectation is zero, so this is not a counterexample to the proposed averaged inequality. It shows why removing the expectation or ignoring translation cancellation changes the claim.
