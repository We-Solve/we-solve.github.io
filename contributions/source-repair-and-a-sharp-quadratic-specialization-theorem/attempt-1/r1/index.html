Research attempt: A norm-specific quadratic distance calculation

For an affine quadratic $f(x,y)$ homogenize with a new variable $t$ and write $F(x,y,t)=w^TQw$, $w=(x,y,t)^T$, with $Q$ a symmetric $3\times3$ matrix. Choose the norm $\|F\|=\|Q\|_F$, the Frobenius matrix norm; off-diagonal coefficients are therefore weighted differently from a plain monomial-coefficient Euclidean norm. Permit homogeneous products of two linear forms, including degenerate cases, so that their dehomogenizations have degree at most two.

A homogeneous quadratic splits into two linear forms over $\mathbb C$ if and only if its symmetric matrix has rank at most two. In one direction, $F=(u^Tw)(v^Tw)$ has matrix $(uv^T+vu^T)/2$, of rank at most two. Conversely, a symmetric bilinear form over $\mathbb C$ can be reduced by completing squares: if a diagonal entry is nonzero, eliminate its row and column by a linear change; if all diagonal entries vanish but some entry is nonzero, the sum of its two basis vectors has nonzero self-pairing and becomes the first basis vector. Repeat. This gives a sum of $r$ independent squares, where $r$ is the rank. For $r=2$ it factors as $(z_1+iz_2)(z_1-iz_2)$, and rank one or zero is immediate. Thus the degree-two target is exactly $\det Q=0$.

For a real symmetric input $Q$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3$, the distance, in the chosen norm, to complex symmetric singular matrices is \[ \min_j|\lambda_j|. \] Indeed let $B$ be any singular complex symmetric matrix and choose a unit complex vector $v$ with $Bv=0$. Then $\|Q-B\|_F\ge\|(Q-B)v\|=\|Qv\|\ge\min_j|\lambda_j|$, the last inequality following by expressing $v$ in an orthonormal real eigenbasis of $Q$. If $e_j$ is a unit eigenvector of smallest absolute eigenvalue, the real symmetric matrix $B=Q-\lambda_je_je_j^T$ is singular and has exactly that Frobenius distance. This proves attainment as well as the formula.

For example $f=x^2+y^2+1$ has $Q=I_3$, so the distance is one. The polynomial $x^2+y^2=(x+iy)(x-iy)$ attains it by removing the $t^2$ coefficient. This actually solves the stated restricted optimization, including possible complex perturbations; it is elementary linear algebra, with no novelty claim.

The attempted generalization would identify arbitrary complete factorization with one symmetric-matrix rank constraint. This is where it stops: the representation by a quadratic form used the exact degree two, and no analogous rank characterization or distance formula has been established for the higher-degree products in the source. Changing the coefficient norm also changes the optimization, so the formula cannot be substituted into the unqualified original question.
