Accelerated Information Propagation in Bosonic Lattices

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A bosonic lattice has unbounded local occupation numbers. For local observables $A$ and $B$, propagation can be measured by $[A(t),B]$, where $A(t)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}$. An effective light cone bounds the spatial range where this commutator can be appreciable. Acceleration means faster spreading than a uniform finite-velocity cone in the source's state-dependent setting.

Large hopping matrix elements do not alone prove acceleration.
In a two-site free bosonic system let $a_j,a_j^*$ obey $[a_i,a_j^*]=\delta_{ij}$ and take $H=J(a_1^*a_2+a_2^*a_1)$ with real $J\ne0$. On occupation vectors the transition matrix element from $|n,n\rangle$ to $|n+1,n-1\rangle$ is $J\sqrt{n(n+1)}$. This grows without bound, tempting an occupation-dependent speed conclusion.
Exact countercheck.
The Heisenberg equations instead close: \[ \dot a_1(t)=-iJ a_2(t),\qquad a_1(t)=\cos(Jt)a_1-i\sin(Jt)a_2. \] The analogous formula exchanges the indices. In the state $|N,0\rangle$, with $N\geq1$, one obtains \[ \langle a_2^*(t)a_2(t)\rangle=N\sin^2(Jt). \] The fraction transferred and the first complete transfer time $\pi/(2|J|)$ are independent of $N$. This follows directly by substituting the linear solution; no unbounded-operator norm estimate is taken. The finite-particle sectors justify the differentiations.
Remaining propagation problem.
These two sites provide no spatial light-cone theorem. They show that increasing individual occupation-basis matrix elements is insufficient evidence for accelerated transmission of a fixed fraction of a signal. The source question still requires admissible state classes, a quantitative information observable and spatially extended dynamics; interactions or long-range hopping may change the conclusion. Neither the nearest-neighbor nor the long-range source regime is classified by this free calculation.
