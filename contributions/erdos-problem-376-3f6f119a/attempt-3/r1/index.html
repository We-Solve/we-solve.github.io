Problem 376. Infinitely many $n$ with $\gcd\!\left(\binom{2n}{n},105\right)=1$? --- Round 2

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): isolate a fatal obstruction in the strongest currently available fractal-intersection strategy, and correct an overoptimistic reading of the literature.

Round 1 already gave the exact Kummer digit reformulation and the unconditional two-prime theorem. What remained unclear was whether recent fractal technology gives an unconditional power-saving upper bound for the specific triple $(3,5,7)$. In this round I show that the answer is no: the existing method needs an extra linear-independence input which is not currently known for $(3,5,7)$.

This is strict progress because it identifies the exact missing hypothesis needed to push current methods onto Problem 376.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 facts.
  • By Kummer's theorem, \[ \gcd\!\left(\binom{2n}{n},105\right)=1 \] if and only if all base-$3$ digits of $n$ lie in $\{0,1\}$, all base-$5$ digits lie in $\{0,1,2\}$, and all base-$7$ digits lie in $\{0,1,2,3\}$.
  • Erdős--Graham--Ruzsa--Straus proved infinitude for any two odd primes.
  • Round 1 recorded that the three-prime problem for $105$ remains open.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

The new tool is a careful reading of Burrell--Yu's three-base intersection theorem.

Their method does give a power-saving upper bound for the counting function \[ G_{3,5,7}(N):=\#\left\{n\le N:\gcd\!\left(\binom{2n}{n},105\right)=1\right\}, \] but only under the extra hypothesis that \[ 1,\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 5},\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 7} \] are linearly independent over the rationals (equivalently, that $(3,5,7)$ is strongly multiplicatively independent in their terminology).

Unconditionally, they prove only that at least one of the triples \[ (3,5,7),\qquad (3,5,11),\qquad (3,5,13) \] has the required independence property. That existential statement is not enough to say anything unconditional about $105$ itself.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

The exact gap after Round 1 is this:
Can one apply current three-Cantor-set intersection theorems directly to the specific triple $(3,5,7)$?

I will answer this in two steps.

  1. State precisely the extra hypothesis Burrell--Yu need in order to get a power-saving upper bound for $(3,5,7)$.
  2. Show that their unconditional theorem is only existential in the third prime $q\in\{7,11,13\}$, so it cannot be specialized to $q=7$.
This isolates the exact obstruction in the present proof strategy.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Proposition 5.1 (conditional power-saving for Problem 376)

Assume that \[ 1,\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 5},\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 7} \] are linearly independent over the rationals. Then \[ G_{3,5,7}(N)\ll N^{\theta_{3,5,7}+o(1)}, \qquad \theta_{3,5,7}:=\frac{\log 2}{\log 3}+\frac{\log 3}{\log 5}+\frac{\log 4}{\log 7}-2. \] In particular, \[ G_{3,5,7}(N)\ll N^{0.026} \] for all sufficiently large $N$.
Justification.
Burrell--Yu's three-base intersection theorem applies to digit-restricted sets in strongly multiplicatively independent bases. For Problem 376 the allowed digit counts are \[ 2 \text{ in base }3,\qquad 3 \text{ in base }5,\qquad 4 \text{ in base }7. \] Hence the relevant dimension sum is \[ \frac{\log 2}{\log 3}+\frac{\log 3}{\log 5}+\frac{\log 4}{\log 7}, \] and their theorem yields an upper bound of exponent equal to this quantity minus $2$. Numerically, \[ \theta_{3,5,7}\approx 0.0259<0.026. \] So under the displayed linear-independence hypothesis one gets \[ G_{3,5,7}(N)\ll N^{0.026}. \] Under Schanuel's conjecture this linear independence is available, so the same conditional upper bound follows.

Proposition 5.2 (the exact unconditional obstruction)

Burrell--Yu's unconditional argument does not imply any power-saving upper bound for the specific set $G_{3,5,7}(N)$.
Proof.
Their notion of strong multiplicative independence for three bases $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$ is exactly the linear independence over the rationals of \[ 1,\ \frac{\log p_1}{\log p_2},\ \frac{\log p_1}{\log p_3}. \] For the triple $(3,5,7)$ this becomes \[ 1,\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 5},\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 7}. \] Burrell--Yu do not prove this unconditionally.

What they do prove unconditionally is weaker: by a six-exponentials argument they show that at least one of \[ (3,5,7),\qquad (3,5,11),\qquad (3,5,13) \] is strongly multiplicatively independent. Consequently, one gets a power-saving upper bound for at least one of the three counting functions \[ G_{3,5,7}(N),\qquad G_{3,5,11}(N),\qquad G_{3,5,13}(N), \] but not necessarily for the first one.

In the worst case the independent triple is only $(3,5,13)$, whose exponent is \[ \theta_{3,5,13} = \frac{\log 2}{\log 3}+\frac{\log 3}{\log 5}+\frac{\log 7}{\log 13}-2 \approx 0.0722<0.073. \] Thus the unconditional output of that paper is merely: \[ \text{for at least one } q\in\{7,11,13\},\quad G_{3,5,q}(N)\ll N^{0.073}, \] and this gives no unconditional upper bound for Problem 376 itself.

Corollary 5.3 (a sharply formulated obstruction)

The present three-fractal strategy would settle the upper-bound side of Problem 376 if one could prove \[ 1,\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 5},\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 7} \] are linearly independent over the rationals. At present this is the exact missing hypothesis blocking that route.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

Potential failure modes:
  • Did I accidentally turn an existential theorem into a theorem for $q=7$? No. This is precisely what I ruled out. The unconditional theorem says "for at least one $q\in\{7,11,13\}$", not "for each such $q$".

  • Is the exponent $0.026$ unconditional for $105$? No. In the argument above it is explicitly conditional on the displayed linear independence (for example via Schanuel).

  • Does this settle the original infinitude problem? No. Even a power-saving upper bound still allows infinitely many solutions. This round isolates an obstruction in one proof strategy; it does not prove finiteness or infinitude.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

The strict Round-2 advance is a precise obstruction theorem: current three-base fractal methods do not give an unconditional upper bound for the specific problem with $105$. They would yield \[ G_{3,5,7}(N)\ll N^{0.026} \] under the extra hypothesis that \[ 1,\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 5},\ \frac{\log 3}{\log 7} \] are linearly independent over the rationals, but unconditionally they prove only that one of the triples $(3,5,7)$, $(3,5,11)$, $(3,5,13)$ enjoys a power-saving upper bound. So the exact missing ingredient for this route is now sharply identified.
