OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE REVIEW

Erdős #376; independent attempt, 2026-09-23. Are there infinitely many positive $n$ with $\gcd(\binom{2n}{n},105)=1$? I read all of 376.tex. It carefully labels the proposed very small exponent conditional, but its broader assertion that no unconditional power-saving upper bound is available is false literally: one base already provides one. I make that distinction precise and independently enumerate a bounded range.

WORK: AN UNCONDITIONAL BASELINE AND EXACT CHECK

By the digit characterization, every admissible $n<3^k$ has only digits zero or one in its $k$ base-three positions. There are exactly $2^k$ such nonnegative integers. Consequently \[ G_{3,5,7}(N)\le2(N+1)^{\log_3 2}. \] To justify the digit condition directly, Legendre's factorial valuation gives $v_p(\binom{2n}{n})=\sum_{j\ge1}(\lfloor2n/p^j\rfloor-2\lfloor n/p^j\rfloor)$, the number of carries on doubling $n$ in base $p$. There are no carries exactly when every digit is at most $(p-1)/2$. The saved program verification/batch_3_digits_records.py generates all $2^{20}$ zero-one base-three strings and tests their base-five and base-seven digits. It finds exactly 14 positive solutions below $3^{20}=3486784401$: \[ 1,10,756,757,3160,3186,3187,3250,7560,7561,7651, 20007,59548377,59548401. \] The list and bounds are saved in its JSON output. This is an exact bounded reproduction check, not a claimed record search.

ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

Leading zero digits are permitted, ensuring every integer below the cutoff is considered. Zero is explicitly excluded from the displayed list. The unconditional exponent $\log_3 2$ is much larger than the source's conditional $0.026$; it does not validate that stronger assertion. Neither any power-saving upper bound nor a finite list proves finiteness or infinitude. Constructing arbitrarily large simultaneous digit-restricted integers remains the exact gap.
