Sidorenko's Conjecture for Bipartite Graphons

OpenAI's claim

Sidorenko and forcing counterexamples: A connected bipartite graph with 35 vertices and 66 edges is claimed to violate Sidorenko’s homomorphism-density lower bound in a finite host. The same pattern is also claimed not to force quasirandomness when the edge and pattern densities match their random values.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $H$ be the incidence graph of the explicitly tabulated $22$ triples on $13$ points: it is connected and bipartite with $35$ vertices and $66$ edges. For $t(H,G)=\operatorname{hom}(H,G)/|V(G)|^{35}$ and $p(G)=2|E(G)|/|V(G)|^2$, a finite simple graph $G$ with $p(G)>0$ is claimed to satisfy $t(H,G)<p(G)^{66}$. Moreover, for every $c>0$ another graph $F$ has $0<t(K_2,F)$ and $t(H,F)<c\,p(F)^{66}$. The forcing corollary claims some $p\in(0,1)$ and a nonconstant symmetric measurable graphon $W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]$ with \[t(K_2,W)=p,\qquad t(H,W)=p^{66},\qquad t(H,W)=\int_{[0,1]^{35}}\prod_{ij\in E(H)}W(x_i,x_j)\,dx.\] Thus equality at that density does not force this cyclic bipartite graph to be quasirandom.

Outline of the argument

Sidorenko and forcing counterexamples: The construction combines rank and discriminant relations for symmetric matrices with counts of symplectic intersections. Quadratic Gauss-sum estimates control joint signs, while type scores distinguish the desired vertex roles. Singular configurations are estimated separately so that the signed rank-layer correlations survive normalization and transfer to a finite host graph.
