When constrained minimizers gain symmetry

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. A symmetry class consists of loops invariant under a specified combined action on time, space and labels. A loop’s full stabilizer may contain this prescribed group strictly. Extra symmetry means this strict enlargement; it need not follow merely from existence in the admissible class.

A mechanism from uniqueness.
Let a group $G$ preserve a functional $\mathcal A$ and an admissible set $X$. If $q$ is its unique minimizer in $X$, then $gq=q$ for every $g\in G$: invariance makes $gq$ another minimizer, so uniqueness forces equality. Applied to a class fixed by a smaller group $H$, this gives extra symmetry when a larger normalizing group preserves that class and the minimizer is unique. If minimizers are unique only modulo time translation or rotation, the conclusion is invariance of that orbit, and an actual pointwise stabilizer requires choosing and checking representatives.
Why symmetry alone does not suffice.
The smooth even function $f(x)=(x^2-1)^2$ is invariant under $x\mapsto-x$, but its two minimizers $\pm1$ are exchanged and neither is fixed. Moreover averaging symmetric loops cannot generally rescue the argument for Newtonian action: the Hessian of $|x|^{-1}$ is $3xx^T/|x|^5-I/|x|^3$, with negative tangential eigenvalues. Thus the potential is not convex, and averaging can increase action or even create collisions.
