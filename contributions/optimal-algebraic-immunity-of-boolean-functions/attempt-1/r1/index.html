Graph algebraic immunity: a full scalar construction and a failed block extension

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

The graph of $f$ is $\{(x,f(x)):x\in\mathbb F_2^n\}$. Its algebraic immunity is the least degree of a nonzero Boolean polynomial in the input and output variables that vanishes on this graph; Boolean polynomials are taken modulo $z_i^2-z_i$.

A Boolean function maps $\{0,1\}^n$ to $\{0,1\}$. A Boolean circuit is an acyclic network of logical gates; a formula is a circuit with no sharing of intermediate gate outputs. Size and depth measure its number of gates and longest gate path.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For every $1\leq m\leq n$, there should exist a Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^m$ whose algebraic immunity equals $d^*(n,m)$, the least $d$ such that $\sum_{i=0}^d\binom{n+m}{i}>2^n$.

Attempt and results

All polynomials here are over $\mathbb F_2$ modulo $z_i^2-z_i$, so they have unique squarefree representatives. The immunity is that of the graph, not the scalar annihilator convention often used elsewhere. We prove the scalar majority case and test a natural attempt to combine it into several outputs. The report already gives scalar constructions; no novelty or advance on the full multi-output conjecture is claimed.
Dimension gives the stated upper bound.
The squarefree monomials of degree at most $d$ in $n+m$ variables form a basis of dimension $D_d=\sum_{i=0}^d\binom{n+m}{i}$. To see independence as functions on the Boolean cube, if $\sum_A c_A\prod_{i\in A}z_i$ vanishes everywhere, evaluate at indicator vectors of subsets in increasing cardinality. The empty evaluation first gives $c_\varnothing=0$, and evaluation at a subset $S$ then gives $c_S=0$ after all proper-subset coefficients have vanished. Thus all coefficients vanish. Evaluation on the $2^n$ graph points is a linear map to a $2^n$-dimensional space. If $D_d>2^n$, it has a nontrivial kernel, giving a nonzero graph annihilator. Hence immunity is at most $d^*(n,m)$ exactly as defined.
A useful low-weight interpolation lemma.
If a squarefree polynomial $A(x)$ of degree at most $k$ vanishes on every Boolean point of Hamming weight at most $k$, then $A=0$. The same induction on subsets used above determines every coefficient of degree at most $k$; there are no other coefficients. Substitution $x_i\mapsto1-z_i$ cannot increase degree, so vanishing on all points with at most $k$ zero coordinates has the same consequence for a degree-at-most-$k$ polynomial.
The scalar construction in every input dimension.
Put $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and define $f(x)=1$ exactly when $\operatorname{wt}(x)>k$. Suppose a graph annihilator $P(x,y)$ has degree at most $k$. Because $y^2=y$, it is uniquely $A(x)+yB(x)$ with $\deg A\le k$ and $\deg B\le k-1$ (take $B=0$ when $k=0$). On points of weight at most $k$, $f=0$, so $A=0$ by the lemma. On points of weight at least $k+1$, it follows that $B=0$. If $n=2k$, those points become all points of weight at most $k-1$ after complementing the coordinates; if $n=2k+1$, they become all points of weight at most $k$, which includes the needed weight-at-most-$k-1$ set. The lemma applied to $B(1-z)$ yields $B=0$. Thus there is no nonzero graph annihilator of degree at most $k$, and immunity is at least $k+1$. For $n=2k$, symmetry of binomial coefficients in odd dimension $2k+1$ gives $\sum_{i=0}^k\binom{2k+1}{i}=2^{2k}$, so $d^*=k+1$. For $n=2k+1$, the sum through $k$ in dimension $2k+2$ is strictly below half the total, and adding the positive central coefficient makes it strictly above half. Hence again $d^*=k+1$. The upper bound therefore proves equality for the scalar construction, including $n=1$.
A direct-sum route to multiple outputs fails.
Take $n=8,m=2$, and define $f_1$ as strict majority on $x_1,\ldots,x_4$, and $f_2$ as strict majority on $x_5,\ldots,x_8$. Each separate four-input scalar function has optimal graph immunity three, as proved above. But the two-output graph has the nonzero annihilator \[ P(x,y_1,y_2)=y_1(1+x_1)(1+x_2), \] of degree three. If $y_1=0$ it vanishes; if $y_1=1$, at least three of the first four inputs equal one, so $x_1,x_2$ cannot both be zero and the product again vanishes. On the other hand $\sum_{i=0}^3\binom{10}{i}=176\le256$ and $\sum_{i=0}^4\binom{10}{i}=386>256$, so $d^*(8,2)=4$. Thus combining optimal independent scalar blocks is not a proof of optimal multi-output immunity. Copying the same scalar output would be even worse, since $y_1+y_2$ is then a nonzero degree-one annihilator.
