Future Restrictions of Inner Automorphic Dilations

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A Markov semigroup $T_t$ is a continuous semigroup of unital completely positive maps on the specified operator algebra $B$. A dilation consists of an embedding $i:B\to A$, a conditional expectation or compression $p:A\to B$, and endomorphisms $\vartheta_t$ with $T_t=p\vartheta_t i$. The GNS construction for $p$ gives a Hilbert $B$-module $E$ and a represented algebra in $\mathcal B^a(E)$. An automorphic dilation has invertible $*$-automorphisms; an inner one is implemented by unitaries. With cyclic reference vector $\xi$, the future module is the closed span $E_+=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\vartheta_t(i(b))\xi:t\ge0,\ b\in B\}$ in the source's construction. A proper $E_0$-semigroup here is the resulting genuinely endomorphic future dynamics rather than the original two-sided automorphic flow. A unital embedding of $\mathcal B^a(E_+)$ into $\mathcal B^a(E)$ must be compatible with the given flow, not merely an abstract inclusion.

Compression can destroy multiplicativity.
Let $\mathcal H_+\subset\mathcal H$ be a proper closed subspace with orthogonal projection $P$. Even if a unitary flow is defined on $\mathcal H$, compression $X\mapsto PXP|_{\mathcal H_+}$ is generally not a homomorphism. On $\mathbb C^2$, take $P=|e_1\rangle\langle e_1|$ and $X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Then $PXP=0$ but $PX^2P=P\ne0$. Thus a future restriction cannot be obtained by arbitrary compression of the original automorphism algebra.
A finite-dimensional embedding obstruction.
If a unital $*$-embedding $M_m(\mathbb C)\hookrightarrow M_n(\mathbb C)$ exists, the images of the $m$ diagonal matrix units are orthogonal projections summing to $I_n$. Off-diagonal matrix units give partial isometries between their ranges, so all ranges have the same dimension $k$. Hence $n=mk$. For example a two-dimensional future subspace inside $\mathbb C^3$ cannot yield a unital embedding of its full operator algebra into $M_3$, despite the obvious Hilbert-space inclusion.
Where the source problem starts.
These examples are not minimal inner Markov dilations satisfying all the source assumptions. They show that future-subspace inclusion alone supplies neither an endomorphism nor the required unital compatible embedding; a factorization or equivalent representation structure is essential. Establishing it under meaningful minimality, extending the future dynamics, and analyzing the suggested two-sided $q$-Fock shift remain separate gaps. No $q$-Fock factorization is inferred from its tensor-Fock analogue.
