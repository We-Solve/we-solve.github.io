With logits $Z=WH$, balanced one-hot targets give cross-entropy $L=-k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\log(e^{Z_{jj}}/\sum_{i=1}^ke^{Z_{ij}})$. Gradient flow differentiates both factor matrices. Neural-collapse geometry concerns aligned class features and classifiers with centered simplex-like class means.

A feed-forward neural network composes affine maps and specified nonlinear activations. ReLU is $\sigma(t)=\max\{0,t\}$. Empirical risk averages a loss on training samples; population risk is its expectation on the target distribution, and generalization compares these risks.

A general-initialization calculation

The report's Hadamard analysis is a restricted invariant family. We attempt to extend a convergence argument by identifying quantities available for every initialization. Use exactly the catalogue mean loss, let $S$ be columnwise softmax, and set \[ Z=WH,\qquad E=\nabla_Z L=\frac1k(S(Z)-I),\qquad \dot W=-EH^T,\quad\dot H=-W^TE. \] The factor $1/k$ fixes the time normalization.
Proposition: Along any solution, $W^TW-HH^T$ and $\mathbf1^TW$ are constant, and \[ \dot L=-\|EH^T\|_F^2-\|W^TE\|_F^2\le0. \] There nevertheless exist nonzero stationary initializations with no nondegenerate centered neural-collapse geometry.
Proof: Differentiating $W^TW$ gives $-HE^TW-W^TEH^T$, and differentiating $HH^T$ gives the same expression. Since every softmax column sums to one, $\mathbf1^TE=0$, implying $\mathbf1^T\dot W=0$. The chain rule and the negative-gradient equations give the loss identity. For nonzero vectors $a,b\in\mathbb R^d$, put $W=\mathbf1a^T$ and $H=b\mathbf1^T$. All logits equal $a^Tb$, so $S=\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/k$. This $E$ has both zero row sums and zero column sums. Hence $EH^T=E\mathbf1b^T=0$ and $W^TE=a\mathbf1^TE=0$. Both matrices stay fixed. All class-feature columns are identical, so after centering they are zero, and cannot form a nondegenerate simplex frame for $k\ge2$. Nonzero matrix normalization does not repair the vanished centered class differences. ∎
The attempted inference from decreasing loss and conserved balance to universal neural collapse therefore fails: the dissipation can vanish at an undesired stationary state. Even the parameter norm need not increase monotonically. At $W=I_k,H=-I_k$, put $s=e^{-1}/(e^{-1}+k-1)$. Then \[ \frac{d}{dt}\|W\|_F^2=-2\langle Z,E\rangle_F=-2(1-s)<0\quad(k\ge2). \] Here the diagonal entries of $E$ are $(s-1)/k$, which proves the formula directly.

Verification and remaining gaps

For $k=d=3$, $a=e_1,b=e_2$ gives nonzero matrices, zero logits and exactly zero flow. A second numerical check at $(I_3,-I_3)$ confirms the negative norm derivative. The stationary family refutes an unqualified all-initialization convergence claim, but the original question asks for the initialization-dependent classification; generic basins, nonstationary limits and useful monotone collapse metrics remain unclassified.
