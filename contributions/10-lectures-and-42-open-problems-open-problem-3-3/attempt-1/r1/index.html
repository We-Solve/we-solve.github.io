Exact-k expansion: boundary calculations and failure of an index substitution

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For an undirected graph with nonnegative symmetric edge weights $W$, write $d_v=\sum_uW_{vu}$ and $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_v)$. In the positive-degree convention, the normalized Laplacian is $L=I-D^{-1/2}WD^{-1/2}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{|V|}$. For a positive-volume subset $S$, let $\phi(S)=w(S,V\setminus S)/\sum_{v\in S}d_v$. The $k$-way expansion $\rho_G(k)$ is the minimum of $\max_i\phi(S_i)$ over $k$ disjoint nonempty vertex sets. A polylogarithmic factor is bounded by a fixed power of $\log(k+1)$, up to an absolute constant.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Let ${G=(V,E,W)}$ be a graph and ${k}$ a positive integer, is the following true? $\rho_G(k) \leq \mathrm{polylog}(k) \sqrt{\lambda_k}. \ \ \ \ \ (2)$

Attempt and results

We use finite undirected graphs with nonnegative weights and strictly positive degrees, as in the preserved definition. Sets are disjoint, nonempty and need not cover the graph. The question concerns every $1\le k\le n$ with a constant independent of $G,n,k$.
The zero-eigenvalue case.
If $G$ has at least $k$ connected components, choose $k$ whole components. Each has positive volume and zero boundary, whence $\rho_G(k)=0$. Conversely, the identity \[ f^TLf=\sum_{\{u,v\}}W_{uv} \left(\frac{f_u}{\sqrt{d_u}}-\frac{f_v}{\sqrt{d_v}}\right)^2 \] shows that a kernel vector has $f_u/\sqrt{d_u}$ constant on each connected component. One constant per component is free. Thus the multiplicity of zero is the number of components, and $\lambda_k=0$ implies this exact easy case. Loops, if permitted, make zero contributions to the displayed energy; connectivity uses edges of positive weight between distinct vertices.
Complete graphs give a uniform boundary check.
For the loopless complete graph $K_n$, $n\ge2$, a set of size $t$ has $\phi(S)=(n-t)/(n-1)$. In any $k$ disjoint nonempty sets, the smallest size is at most $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$, so \[ \rho_{K_n}(k)\ge\frac{n-\lfloor n/k\rfloor}{n-1}. \] A partition into $k$ parts whose sizes differ by at most one attains equality. Since $L=\frac{n}{n-1}I-\frac1{n-1}J$, its eigenvalues are zero and $n/(n-1)$ with multiplicity $n-1$. Therefore $k=1$ gives zero on both sides, and $k\ge2$ satisfies $\rho_{K_n}(k)\le1\le\sqrt{\lambda_k}$. This verifies a family but supplies no bound for general graphs.
A failed route through $\lambda_{2k}$.
The lecture notes distinguish bounds with index $k$ from bounds with index $2k$. An attempt to transfer the latter by a universal estimate $\lambda_{2k}\le C(\log(k+1))^a\lambda_k$ fails even for a union of $k$ disjoint unit-weight edges. Its normalized Laplacian is block diagonal with blocks $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. There are $k$ zeros and $k$ eigenvalues two. Thus $\lambda_k=0$ and $\lambda_{2k}=2$. The target is nonetheless true for this example because $\rho_G(k)=0$. This is a counterexample to the proposed intermediate estimate, not to the original Cheeger question.
