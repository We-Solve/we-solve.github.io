Sharp robust dynamic revenue ratios with one or multiple buyers

Version 2. The full catalogue problem remains unresolved.

Target and restricted model

The catalogue asks for robustly truthful dynamic mechanisms and revenue guarantees relative to the optimum under each known law. We retain the complete one-buyer argument from Version 1 and extend its sharp guarantee to competing buyers with arbitrarily correlated exogenous type paths. A fresh unit is available each period, and there is no cross-period resource constraint. The multi-buyer extension is stated separately so that its positive-benchmark ambiguity restriction is explicit. Known-marginal ambiguity, shared capacity and allocation-dependent transitions remain outside the result.

Fix $T\geq1$, $0<\beta\leq1$, $D=\sum_{t=1}^T\beta^{t-1}$, and \[ 0<v_1<\cdots<v_m\leq1,\qquad v_0=0,\qquad H=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{v_j-v_{j-1}}{v_j}. \tag{1} \] The buyer learns $\theta_t\in\{v_1,\ldots,v_m\}$ each period. Each unit can be allocated with probability in $[0,1]$, utility is $\sum_t\beta^{t-1}(\theta_t q_t-p_t)$, and stage transfers satisfy $|p_t|\leq1$. The seller observes reports and its own public outcomes, but not true types. Type evolution is independent of allocations and mechanism randomization.

Let $\mathcal P$ contain all full-support probability laws on the finite set of type paths. Mechanisms have commitment, cannot condition on the unknown law, and must satisfy truthful continuation optimality against every adapted reporting-and-exit strategy under every law. Exit is available each period, gives zero future utility, and leaves past payments sunk. At every off-path report history impose continuation incentive compatibility and participation for every possible future type path; the mechanism constructed below meets this stronger convention. For the upper bound only initial incentive compatibility and participation are used. For each known $P$, define $\mathrm{OPT}_P$ using the same allocation, payment, observation, and participation restrictions, but requiring incentive compatibility only under $P$ (and the stated off-path convention).

The research agenda concerning weaker information assumptions is discussed by Bergemann and Välimäki [BV]. Randomized pricing under limited information has a substantial existing literature, including competitive-ratio analysis [EM]. The proof below makes no novelty claim; it supplies a sharp finite-grid dynamic subcase and identifies why full ambiguity eliminates any ratio gain from intertemporal screening.

The attainable guarantee

Define $\alpha_j=(v_j-v_{j-1})/(Hv_j)$. In each period independently select index $j$ with probability $\alpha_j$. Allocate that period's unit and charge $v_j$ if and only if the current report is at least $v_j$. The random index may be drawn after the report. Neither subsequent prices nor allocations depend on past reports or purchases.
Lemma (Pathwise incentives and exact revenue): The displayed finite randomized mechanism is feasible, robustly truthful, and voluntarily continued after every private and public history under the prescribed convention. For every type path $(\theta_t)$ its expected discounted revenue is \[ \frac1H\sum_{t=1}^T\beta^{t-1}\theta_t. \tag{2} \] For every $P\in\mathcal P$, $0<\mathrm{OPT}_P\leq{\mathbb{E}}_P\sum_t\beta^{t-1}\theta_t$, and consequently this mechanism guarantees revenue ratio at least $1/H$.
Proof: Fix the realized price in a period. If it is below the true value, purchase weakly dominates refusal; if it is above the true value, refusal weakly dominates purchase; at equality both yield zero. A truthful report makes precisely these choices. This comparison is pathwise, including for prices drawn after reporting. Because current reports and allocations never affect future opportunities or the exogenous type process, summing these stagewise comparisons proves optimality against arbitrary adapted reporting strategies. Every truthful stage surplus is nonnegative, so exit cannot improve any continuation. The same reasoning applies at every off-path report history and for every future type path. Payments are between zero and one.

If the current value is $v_k$, expected revenue is $\sum_{j\leq k}\alpha_jv_j=\sum_{j\leq k}(v_j-v_{j-1})/H=v_k/H$. Sum with discount weights to obtain (2).

Any feasible known-law mechanism has nonnegative initial expected buyer utility by participation. Its expected discounted payments are therefore at most its expected discounted allocated value, itself at most ${\mathbb{E}}_P\sum_t\beta^{t-1}\theta_t$. A per-period posted price $v_1/2$ is feasible, is strictly accepted by every type, and earns $Dv_1/2>0$. Thus the benchmark is positive and the ratio follows. ∎

The converse from nearly constant type paths

The next argument permits arbitrary history dependence and randomization in the candidate mechanism. It uses only bounded transfers, the reporting interface, and robustness under full-support laws.
Lemma (Constant-path menus implied by robust dynamic IC): Fix any feasible robustly truthful mechanism $M$. For each $j$, run it on the deterministic true type path $(v_j,\ldots,v_j)$ with all reports truthful, integrating its own randomness. Let $Dx_j$ and $Dt_j$ be its expected discounted allocation and payment, respectively. Then $0\leq x_j\leq1$ and \[ v_jx_j-t_j\geq0,\qquad v_jx_j-t_j\geq v_jx_k-t_k\quad\text{for every }j,k. \tag{3} \] These conclusions hold even though deterministic type paths are not themselves in $\mathcal P$.
Proof: For each $j$ and $\epsilon>0$, use the full-support independent law under which each period's value has distribution $(1-\epsilon)\delta_{v_j}+\epsilon\nu$, where $\nu$ gives positive mass to every grid value. Conditional on the first value being $v_j$, the future path converges in probability to the constant $v_j$ path as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

Initial robust incentive compatibility compares truth with the admissible deviation that always reports $v_k$ and never exits. There are finitely many type paths, and payments and allocations are bounded. Expected payoffs therefore converge, as finite weighted sums of bounded pathwise expected payoffs, to the corresponding payoffs on the constant true path. Under the deviation, the mechanism receives exactly the report path $(v_k,\ldots,v_k)$. Its observable random outcomes and transfers have the same distribution as when that is the true path and reports are truthful: the mechanism observes reports, not the hidden true values. On the constant true $v_j$ path the deviation consequently yields utility $D(v_jx_k-t_k)$, whereas truth yields $D(v_jx_j-t_j)$. Taking the limit proves the incentive inequalities in (3). Comparing truthful continuation initially with immediate exit gives the participation inequality by the same argument. Finally, each allocation is in $[0,1]$, so its discounted sum lies in $[0,D]$. ∎

Lemma (A finite-grid upper bound): Every menu satisfying (3) obeys \[ \min_j\frac{t_j}{v_j}\leq\frac1H. \tag{4} \]
Proof: Participation gives $t_1/v_1\leq x_1$. For $j\geq2$, the incentive inequality for type $j$ against $j-1$ gives $(t_j-t_{j-1})/v_j\leq x_j-x_{j-1}$. Adding and collecting coefficients yields \[ \sum_{j<m}\left(\frac1{v_j}-\frac1{v_{j+1}}\right)t_j +\frac{t_m}{v_m}\leq x_m\leq1. \] All displayed coefficients are positive, and their scalar product with $(v_j)$ is $H$. If all payment/value ratios were larger than $1/H$, their weighted sum would exceed one. This proves (4); negative transfers, if present, do not affect the argument. ∎
Theorem (Exact minimax ratio for the restricted dynamic problem): For the domain and the full-support ambiguity set specified above, \[ \sup_{M\ \mathrm{robustly\ truthful}} \inf_{P\in\mathcal P}\frac{\mathrm{Rev}_P(M)}{\mathrm{OPT}_P} =\frac1H. \tag{5} \] The lower bound is attained by the mechanism in Lemma Lemma (Pathwise incentives and exact revenue); the upper bound applies to all admissible committed history-dependent mechanisms. The value is independent of $T$ and $\beta$.
Proof: The lower bound follows from Lemma Lemma (Pathwise incentives and exact revenue). For the converse fix $M$ and its menu $(x_j,t_j)$ from Lemma Lemma (Constant-path menus implied by robust dynamic IC). Let $P_j^\epsilon$ be the independent full-support laws used there. Unconditional truthful revenue converges to $Dt_j$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

Under the known law $P_j^\epsilon$, charging $v_j$ independently in each period with the direct allocation rule "allocate iff reported value is at least the price" is truthful and gives nonnegative continuation utility on every path. It earns at least $Dv_j(1-\epsilon)$. The welfare upper bound in Lemma Lemma (Pathwise incentives and exact revenue) tends to $Dv_j$. Hence $\mathrm{OPT}_{P_j^\epsilon}\to Dv_j$. These positive limits imply \[ \inf_{P\in\mathcal P}\frac{\mathrm{Rev}_P(M)}{\mathrm{OPT}_P} \leq\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} \frac{\mathrm{Rev}_{P_j^\epsilon}(M)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{P_j^\epsilon}} =\frac{t_j}{v_j} \] for every $j$. Lemma Lemma (A finite-grid upper bound) bounds the minimum of these ratios by $1/H$. Since $M$ was arbitrary, this proves (5). ∎

Corollary (No grid-independent positive guarantee): Even with one buyer and one period, bounded values in $(0,1]$, bounded payments, and full-support laws, no positive revenue ratio works uniformly over all finite positive value grids. For the geometric grid $v_j=2^{j-m}$, the ratio in (5) is $2/(m+1)$.
Proof: The first summand of $H$ is one. Every subsequent summand is $(v_j-v_{j-1})/v_j=1/2$, so $H=(m+1)/2$. Letting $m\to\infty$ proves the claim. For each fixed grid the benchmark remains uniformly bounded away from zero by $Dv_1/2$, as required; this lower bound is not uniform across grids. ∎

Multiple buyers: the same sharp guarantee

We now change the one-buyer model explicitly. There are $n\geq1$ buyers, one indivisible unit available in each of the $T$ periods, and value grid $\{0,v_1,\ldots,v_m\}$ with the same positive values and $H$ as in (1). Each buyer's allocation is in $[0,1]$ and their allocations sum to at most one in each period. Per-buyer transfers satisfy $|p_{it}|\leq1$. Discounted quasilinear utility, period-by-period exit, sunk past payments, and the reporting interface are as above. The entire array of types is exogenous: neither allocations, reports nor mechanism randomization change its law. Buyers observe their own current and past types and the declared public history, but not other buyers' unreported types. Robust incentives compare truth with every adapted reporting-and-exit strategy while other buyers report truthfully. At off-path reports use the same stronger pathwise continuation convention as above.

Fix $0<\eta<1$. Let $\mathcal P_\eta$ be all full-support laws on the finite type arrays satisfying \[ \Pr_P\{\max_i\theta_{it}\geq v_1\}\geq\eta \qquad(t=1,\ldots,T). \] These restrictions keep the benchmark uniformly positive. They impose no independence across buyers or dates. The seller knows the set but not its member. Define $\mathrm{OPT}^{(n)}_P$ with the same allocation, payment, observation and participation conventions as the candidate mechanisms, except that the type law is known. Put \[ S_P={\mathbb{E}}_P\sum_{t=1}^T\beta^{t-1}\max_i\theta_{it}. \]

Lemma (A random reserve gives a pathwise welfare guarantee): Independently in each period draw reserve $v_j$ with probability $\alpha_j=(v_j-v_{j-1})/(Hv_j)$. Run a second-price auction with this reserve: the highest report wins if it reaches the reserve, with a fixed public tie rule, and pays the larger of the reserve and the highest opponent report; all others pay zero. Future auctions ignore earlier reports and allocations. This mechanism is feasible, robustly truthful and voluntarily continued. Its revenue satisfies \[ \mathrm{Rev}_P\geq S_P/H, \qquad D\eta v_1/2\leq\mathrm{OPT}^{(n)}_P\leq S_P \quad(P\in\mathcal P_\eta). \]
Proof: For fixed other reports and reserve, winning costs the bidder a threshold independent of its own report. A value above that threshold weakly prefers to win; one below it weakly prefers to lose; at equality either outcome has zero surplus. The fixed tie rule does not change those inequalities. A truthful report therefore maximizes that period's utility for every realization of opponents' reports and the reserve. Future opportunities are unchanged by current reports, and true future types are exogenous. Adding these stagewise inequalities along any type array and random-price sequence proves optimality against all adapted deviations. Truthful surplus is nonnegative in every period, so exit cannot improve any continuation. This reasoning also applies at every off-path report history. Allocations are feasible and each payment is between zero and one.

Fix a type array. In a period with maximum value $v_k$, a sale occurs for every reserve $v_j\leq v_k$, and its price is at least $v_j$. Expected revenue in that period is therefore at least $\sum_{j\leq k}\alpha_jv_j=v_k/H$. With maximum zero both sides are zero. Integrating and discounting proves the revenue lower bound.

For any feasible known-law mechanism, each buyer's initial participation implies nonnegative expected utility, after averaging over its initial private type. Sum these inequalities over buyers. Total expected payments cannot exceed total expected allocated value, which is at most $S_P$ because at most one unit is allocated at each date. Conversely, a second-price auction with reserve $v_1/2$ sells and collects at least $v_1/2$ whenever the maximum value is positive. It is truthful and pathwise individually rational by the preceding argument. Its expected discounted revenue is at least $D\eta v_1/2$, proving the uniform lower bound on the benchmark. ∎

Lemma (A single active buyer still bounds all dynamic mechanisms): Fix any robustly truthful $n$-buyer mechanism $M$. At the deterministic type array where buyer 1 has value $v_j$ at every date and every other buyer has value zero, let $Dx_j$ and $Dt_j$ be buyer 1's expected discounted allocation and payment under truthful reports. Then the menu $(x_j,t_j)$ satisfies (3), and the other buyers' total expected payment at this array is nonpositive.
Proof: For a fixed $j$, choose a full-support distribution $\nu$ on the value grid. Independently across all buyers and dates, give buyer 1 the value law $(1-\epsilon)\delta_{v_j}+\epsilon\nu$ and every other buyer the law $(1-\epsilon)\delta_0+\epsilon\nu$. Call the resulting law $P_j^\epsilon$. For sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$ it belongs to $\mathcal P_\eta$, since each period's maximum is positive with probability at least $1-\epsilon$. These independent laws are members of the ambiguity set; no independence assumption is imposed on its other members.

Conditional on buyer 1's initial type being $v_j$, the entire type array converges in probability to the stated deterministic array. Robust initial incentives compare truth with the deviation that always reports $v_k$ and never exits. Because the mechanism observes reports rather than hidden values, the distribution of its outcomes under this deviation converges to the same distribution it has when buyer 1's reported path is constantly $v_k$ and the others report zero. All pathwise expected allocations and transfers are bounded, and there are finitely many arrays. Passing to the limit gives $v_jx_j-t_j\geq v_jx_k-t_k$. Initial participation gives $v_jx_j-t_j\geq0$, and allocation feasibility gives $0\leq x_j\leq1$. Thus (3) holds.

For any other buyer $i$, condition instead on its initial type being zero. The true array again converges to the same deterministic one. At its limit that buyer's value for every allocation is zero. Initial participation and bounded-payoff convergence therefore require its expected discounted payment to be nonpositive. Sum over $i\ne1$. Only initial on-path participation is passed to the limit; no limiting claim about arbitrary off-path beliefs is needed. ∎

Theorem (Exact minimax ratio with competing buyers): Under the preceding multi-buyer assumptions, \[ \sup_{M\ \mathrm{robustly\ truthful}} \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_\eta} \frac{\mathrm{Rev}_P(M)}{\mathrm{OPT}^{(n)}_P} =\frac1H. \] The random-reserve second-price mechanism attains the lower bound. The value is independent of $n$, $T$, $\beta$ and $\eta\in(0,1)$.
Proof: The first lemma proves the lower bound, including positivity of every benchmark. For the converse apply the preceding menu lemma and Lemma Lemma (A finite-grid upper bound) to choose $j$ with $t_j/v_j\leq1/H$. Under $P_j^\epsilon$, total revenue converges to the total payment at the limiting array, which is at most $Dt_j$.

A known-law seller can offer the unit solely to buyer 1 at price $v_j$ each period. This is a feasible pathwise truthful mechanism with voluntary continuation and revenue at least $Dv_j(1-\epsilon)$. The welfare upper bound $S_{P_j^\epsilon}$ tends to $Dv_j$, because the maximum value tends to $v_j$ in every period and is bounded. Consequently $\mathrm{OPT}^{(n)}_{P_j^\epsilon}\to Dv_j>0$. The limiting revenue ratio is at most $t_j/v_j$, and the infimum over admissible laws is no larger than this limit. Taking the supremum over $M$ gives the upper bound. Deterministic arrays are only limiting devices; every law used in the infimum has full support and satisfies its positive-sale-probability restriction. ∎

The multi-buyer theorem is the substantive addition in Version 2. Competition can raise realized auction revenue, but an ambiguity set containing nearly single-active-buyer arrays prevents a uniformly better competitive ratio. This is a theorem about this ambiguity set and the specified known-law benchmark, not about environments with known value marginals or a guaranteed number of active bidders.

Verification and unresolved boundary

For $m=1$, the formula gives $H=1$: charging the sole possible value each period extracts all surplus under truthful implementation. Indifference is allowed by the target's truthful-reporting revenue convention. If adverse equilibrium selection were added, a slight price discount would give the same supremal ratio, but that would be a different formulation. For $(v_1,v_2)=(1/2,1)$, $H=3/2$, and the independent price lottery uses probabilities $(2/3,1/3)$ in every period.

Full support is respected throughout the upper bound: the deterministic paths enter only as limits of explicitly specified admissible laws. There is no inference from unconditional convergence to an arbitrary off-path conditional belief. Only initial incentives conditional on the observed first type are passed to the limit. This is why the proof does not need a uniform lower bound on transition probabilities.

Unresolved boundary. The first gap for known-marginal ambiguity is that the nearly constant-path laws used above generally violate the prescribed marginals. Neither the upper bound nor its conclusion that history does not improve the ratio automatically survives that restriction. The new theorem addresses multiple buyers under broad correlation ambiguity. Persistent allocation effects on type transitions and shared intertemporal capacity still invalidate its stagewise lower bound or limiting menu reduction. Those remain substantive parts of the catalogue agenda not solved here. The work is an analytic restricted solution with complete proofs, without a claim of prior unknownness or experimental validation.

Completion Estimate

35%

This is a heuristic estimate for the full catalogue target. The sharp guarantee now covers competing buyers and arbitrary exogenous correlations, with complete upper and lower bounds. Known marginals, allocation-dependent transitions and shared intertemporal resources remain untreated, so the general dynamic-mechanism target is unresolved.
