A sharp obstruction to two-process asymptotic coupling
under shifted weak convergence

Model-assisted research contribution

28 October 2026

Abstract

We construct one binary process whose shifted finite-window laws eventually equal those of independent fair bits, but which cannot be coupled to that limiting process with asymptotically vanishing coordinate disagreement probability. For every coupling preserving the two entire process laws, the limsup of the coordinate disagreement probabilities is at least one half, and this constant is sharp. The proportion of disagreements on specified growing blocks also has almost-sure liminf at least one half. Consequently the two shifted paths cannot approach one another even in probability in any compatible product metric. This refutes the particular metric-convergence suggestion in Thorisson's Problem 3.3; it does not supply the more general coupling characterization requested there.

Question and scope

Let $\mathcal X=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_0}$, and write $\theta_n x=(x_{n+k})_{k\geq0}$. Equip $\mathcal X$ with its product topology and the metric \[ d(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k-1}\mathbf 1\{x_k\ne y_k\}. \] A coupling of processes $X,Y$ is any joint law having the prescribed entire marginal laws on $\mathcal X$. No adaptedness, Markovianity, stationarity of the joint law, or independence is required.

Thorisson's Problem 3.3 [thorisson, p. 4] asks for a characterization of $\theta_nX\Rightarrow Y$ involving only a joint construction of $X$ and $Y$, and specifically proposes metric convergence between $\theta_nX$ and $\theta_nY$. The archived formulation [archive] explicitly asks for almost-sure convergence. We give a counterexample to that proposal and, more strongly, to convergence in probability. The full characterization question remains open in this note.

The construction combines classical de Bruijn cycles with a direct concentration estimate. Neither cyclic-word existence nor the concentration inequality is claimed as new. The issue is that finite-window laws can become exactly correct while the set of possible long blocks remains too small to track a fair independent sequence under any coupling. This is different from constructing a separately coupled copy of each shifted law, as in the Skorohod representation and the widening-window results discussed in [windows, Section 1]. Those results allow a family of process copies and do not impose the single-process consistency required here.

Construction and theorem

For $j\geq1$, choose a binary cyclic word $w_j$ of length $2^j$ in which each binary word of length $j$ occurs exactly once as a cyclic consecutive window. A short proof of existence is given below. Extend $w_j$ periodically to all integer indices. Set \[ L_j=4^j,\qquad s_1=0,\qquad s_{j+1}=s_j+L_j, \qquad I_j=\{s_j,\ldots,s_j+L_j-1\}. \] Let $U_j$, $j\geq1$, be independent, each uniform on $\{0,\ldots,2^j-1\}$. Define the entire process $X$ by \[ X_{s_j+t}=w_j(U_j+t),\qquad 0\leq t<L_j. \] Let $\nu$ be the law of an independent fair binary sequence $Y$. The construction specifies $\mu=\mathcal L(X)$; subsequently $X,Y$ may be coupled in any way.

Theorem: The laws $\mu,\nu$ have the following properties.
  1. For every $m\geq1$, every $n\geq s_m$, and every $a\in\{0,1\}^m$, \[ \mathbb P\big((X_n,\ldots,X_{n+m-1})=a\big)=2^{-m}. \] In particular, $\mathcal L(\theta_nX)\Rightarrow\nu$.
  2. For every coupling of $\mu,\nu$, \[ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P(X_n\ne Y_n)\geq\frac12. \] The bound is sharp: the independent coupling has disagreement probability exactly $1/2$ at every coordinate. Thus the infimum over all couplings of the displayed limsup equals $1/2$.
  3. In every coupling, the block disagreement proportions \[ H_j=\frac1{L_j}\sum_{t\in I_j}\mathbf 1\{X_t\ne Y_t\} \] satisfy $\liminf_{j\to\infty}H_j\geq1/2$ almost surely.
  4. For every metric $\widetilde d$ compatible with the product topology on $\mathcal X$, no coupling can satisfy $\widetilde d(\theta_nX,\theta_nY)\to0$ in probability. In particular, almost-sure convergence is impossible.

Cyclic words and finite windows

Lemma: The words $w_j$ exist. If their phase is uniform, every cyclic window of length $r\leq j$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^r$.
Proof: Use the directed graph whose vertices are binary words of length $j-1$ and whose edges are binary words of length $j$; an edge goes from its length-$j-1$ prefix to its length-$j-1$ suffix. For $j=1$ this is one vertex with two loops. Every vertex has indegree and outdegree two. For $j>1$, appending the symbols of any desired vertex provides a directed path to it, so the graph is strongly connected. A finite connected balanced directed graph has an Euler tour: follow unused outgoing edges to close a walk, and splice in further such closed walks until every edge is used. Reading the overlapping edge labels along this tour gives a cyclic binary word whose length-$j$ windows are exactly the edges, once each.

Each fixed binary word of length $r\leq j$ is the prefix of exactly $2^{j-r}$ length-$j$ words. Hence it occurs at exactly $2^{j-r}$ of the $2^j$ phases. Uniform phase therefore gives probability $2^{-r}$, including windows that cross the end of the written cycle. ∎

Fix $m$ and $n\geq s_m$. Every block meeting the length-$m$ window beginning at $n$ has index at least $m$ and length at least $m$. The window therefore meets either one block or two consecutive blocks. In one block, Lemma Lemma gives the uniform law. In two blocks, the two pieces have lengths at most $m$, and are independently uniform because their phases are independent. Their concatenation is again uniform. This proves the first displayed assertion of Theorem Theorem.

To obtain weak convergence, take a continuous function on the compact space $\mathcal X$. Uniform continuity allows uniform approximation by a function depending on a finite prefix, for example by setting all later coordinates to zero. Expectations of such finite-prefix functions are exactly those under $\nu$ for all sufficiently large shifts. The approximation proves convergence of expectations of every continuous function, hence weak convergence.

A bound uniform over all couplings

Let $\mathcal C_j\subset\{0,1\}^{L_j}$ be the possible words of $X$ on block $I_j$. There are at most $2^j$ such words, one for each phase. This small deterministic set is the only property of the $X$-law needed in this section.

Lemma: For $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, every coupling satisfies \[ \mathbb P\left(H_j\leq\frac12-\varepsilon\right) \leq 2^j\exp\big(-2\varepsilon^2 4^j\big). \]
Proof: For a fixed deterministic $c\in\mathcal C_j$, the variables $\mathbf 1\{c_t\ne Y_{s_j+t}\}$, $0\leq t<L_j$, are independent Bernoulli variables of parameter $1/2$. This follows from the marginal law of $Y$, regardless of its dependence on $X$. If their sum is $S$, then for $\lambda>0$, \[ \mathbb E e^{-\lambda(S-L_j/2)} =\cosh(\lambda/2)^{L_j} \leq e^{L_j\lambda^2/8}. \] Here $\cosh u\leq e^{u^2/2}$, since $\tanh u\leq u$ for $u\geq0$ and both sides are even. The exponential Markov inequality and $\lambda=4\varepsilon$ give \[ \mathbb P\big(S\leq L_j(1/2-\varepsilon)\big) \leq e^{-\lambda\varepsilon L_j+L_j\lambda^2/8} =e^{-2\varepsilon^2L_j}. \] Since $X|_{I_j}\in\mathcal C_j$ almost surely, a small value of $H_j$ implies that at least one deterministic $c\in\mathcal C_j$ has that small disagreement with $Y|_{I_j}$. A union bound over at most $2^j$ words gives the claim. No conditioning on the random word $X|_{I_j}$, which could destroy independence of the bits of $Y$, has been used. ∎

For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the bound in Lemma Lemma is summable in $j$. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma implies that $H_j>1/2-\varepsilon$ eventually almost surely. Intersecting the probability-one events for $\varepsilon=1/r$, $r\geq3$, proves assertion 3 of Theorem Theorem.

The same estimate gives \[ \mathbb E H_j\geq(1/2-\varepsilon) \left(1-2^j e^{-2\varepsilon^2 4^j}\right). \] Consequently $\liminf_j\mathbb E H_j\geq1/2$. But $\mathbb E H_j$ is the arithmetic mean over $I_j$ of the coordinate disagreement probabilities. If their limsup were less than $1/2$, all sufficiently late block means would also be bounded by a constant less than $1/2$, a contradiction. Each $X_n$ is fair by Lemma Lemma, so coupling $X$ independently to $Y$ gives disagreement probability $1/2$ at every coordinate. This proves assertion 2 including sharpness.

For the displayed metric $d$, \[ d(\theta_nX,\theta_nY)\geq\tfrac12\mathbf 1\{X_n\ne Y_n\}, \] so convergence in probability contradicts assertion 2. For any other compatible metric $\widetilde d$, the disjoint sets $A_0=\{x:x_0=0\}$ and $A_1=\{x:x_0=1\}$ are compact. The continuous distance function attains a positive minimum on $A_0\times A_1$; call it $a$. Thus $\widetilde d(\theta_nX,\theta_nY)\geq a$ whenever $X_n\ne Y_n$, and the same contradiction applies. Almost-sure convergence would imply convergence in probability. This completes the proof.

Consequences and limitations

Even eventual exactness of every fixed finite-window law does not permit two full process laws to be coupled with asymptotically vanishing coordinate disagreement. The obstruction applies to every coupling, not only to adapted or Markovian ones. It therefore rules out the specific proposed metric criterion in the stated generality.

The process $X$ is deliberately nonstationary; its limit law is stationary. We do not give a substitute necessary-and-sufficient two-process criterion, nor a result for additional classes such as time-homogeneous Markov processes. We make no assertion of setwise or total-variation convergence and do not resolve the neighboring setwise questions in [thorisson].

Targeted searches and comparison with the cited primary problem and widening-window statement did not identify this exact counterexample. That bounded check is not a comprehensive novelty certification. The proof is self-contained apart from elementary finite graph, probability, and compactness facts explained above. All mathematical work and checking for this manuscript are model-assisted; no human or formal-proof verification is claimed.
