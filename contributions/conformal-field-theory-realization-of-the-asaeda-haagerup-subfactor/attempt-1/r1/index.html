Conformal Field Theory Realization of the Asaeda–Haagerup Subfactor

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A subfactor is an inclusion $N\subset M$ of von Neumann algebras with trivial centers. Its index and standard invariant encode the size and higher relative-commutant structure of the inclusion. In an operator-algebraic conformal field theory, interval or local observable algebras and sectors can produce subfactor inclusions. To say a subfactor arises in conformal field theory requires identifying the inclusion or invariant that the construction realizes. The Asaeda--Haagerup subfactor is the exceptional finite-index subfactor named in the source; its standard invariant is the specific datum whose conformal realization is sought.

Testing a pointed-only extension ansatz.
The supplied Grossman--Izumi--Snyder source [S3], whose HTML introduction was consulted, describes an algebra object $1\oplus X$ yielding the Asaeda--Haagerup index $(5+\sqrt{17})/2$, with $d(X)=(3+\sqrt{17})/2$. Put $d=(3+\sqrt{17})/2$. Direct multiplication gives $d^2=3d+2$. Its stated fusion relation $X\otimes X\cong1\oplus X\oplus Y$ therefore forces \[ d(Y)=d^2-d-1=2d+1=4+\sqrt{17}. \] This is a quick numerical consistency check on the noninvertible sector required by that construction.
Why the simplest simple-current route fails.
In a unitary pointed tensor category all simple objects are invertible and have positive dimension one: multiplicativity gives $d(g)d(g^{-1})=1$, and unitarity identifies the two dimensions. Any finite direct sum of such objects has integral dimension. It therefore cannot be tensor-equivalent to a category containing the above $X$ with irrational dimension, nor realize the specified algebra object merely as a sum of invertible sectors. Adding only a finite set of simple currents is an insufficient ansatz for that direct realization.
Remaining gap.
The primary source supplies tensor-category and subfactor constructions, not a conformal net construction used here. A CFT can have noninvertible sectors, and Morita/orbifold constructions can change the presentation, so the pointed obstruction is narrow. The first missing step is a positive-energy local conformal theory with the appropriate noninvertible tensor data and a verified realization of the actual Asaeda--Haagerup standard invariant. Matching the index or an unrelated exceptional subfactor is insufficient.
