Maker winning strategy for Snaky on the infinite square lattice

OpenAI's claim

Snaky within 21 Maker claims: The paper claims a Maker strategy for Snaky using at most 21 Maker moves against every legal Breaker response. It can operate on a fixed 251-cell region inside a $17\times17$ square.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
On the initially empty board $\mathbb Z^2$, Maker moves first and each player then claims one unowned cell per turn. For \[S=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(3,1),(4,1)\},\] targets are $t+RS$ with $t\in\mathbb Z^2$ and $R$ a signed permutation matrix, so rotations and reflections but no scaling are allowed. The claim supplies one globally legal Maker policy which, against every legal Breaker continuation, owns an entire target within at most $21$ Maker claims. It therefore asserts a winning strategy for the ordinary unbiased Snaky game.

Outline of the argument

Snaky within 21 Maker claims: Finite winning conditions list required Maker cells, a Breaker-free envelope, and a bound on remaining moves. Backward references combine child conditions after one chosen Maker claim, using envelope intersections to ensure that one Breaker move cannot kill every continuation. A 728-condition certificate closes at the empty initial position; induction extracts a legal policy with the stated move bound.
