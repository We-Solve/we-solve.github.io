A sample lower bound even with interaction

Let the verifier's only access to an unknown distribution be at most $m$ independent samples. Its task is to accept $P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ with probability at least $2/3$ with an honest prover, while accepting $Q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\varepsilon)$ with probability at most $1/3$ against every prover, where $0<\varepsilon\le1/4$. Other random coins and public parameters do not depend on the unknown distribution. The dishonest prover may simulate the honest $P$ prover, including fair-coin samples from $P$.
Proposition: Any such protocol, with arbitrary interaction, satisfies $m\ge1/(18\varepsilon^2)$.
Proof: Under $Q$ use a prover that follows the honest strategy for $P$. Predraw an array of $m$ genuine verifier samples, padding unused entries if the protocol stops earlier. Both complete experiments are obtained from these arrays by the same randomized transformation: the verifier algorithm and the fixed simulated prover strategy. Thus their acceptance-probability difference is at most $\operatorname{TV}(P^m,Q^m)$. To see the contraction directly, conditional acceptance given array $z$ is a common number $h(z)\in[0,1]$; then $|\sum_z h(z)(P^m(z)-Q^m(z))|\le\operatorname{TV}(P^m,Q^m)$ by splitting the signed differences into positive and negative parts.

For any finite distributions $U,V$ with affinity $\rho=\sum_z\sqrt{U(z)V(z)}$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives \[ \operatorname{TV}(U,V)=\frac12\sum_z|\sqrt{U(z)}-\sqrt{V(z)}|(\sqrt{U(z)}+\sqrt{V(z)})\le\sqrt{1-\rho^2}. \] Here the one-sample affinity is $\rho=(\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon}+\sqrt{1-2\varepsilon})/2$, so \[ \rho^2=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\varepsilon^2}}2\ge1-2\varepsilon^2. \] The inequality follows from $\sqrt{1-u}\ge1-u$ for $u\in[0,1]$, obtained by squaring its nonnegative sides. Affinities multiply for independent product distributions by expanding the product sums. Therefore \[ \operatorname{TV}(P^m,Q^m)\le\sqrt{1-\rho^{2m}}\le\sqrt{2m}\,\varepsilon, \] using $(1-v)^m\ge1-mv$ for $v\in[0,1]$, proved by induction. Completeness and soundness require an acceptance gap of at least $1/3$, giving the claim. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps
