Erdős Problem #920: chromatic growth without a fixed clique

Statement and current result

Let $f_k(n)$ be the largest chromatic number of an $n$-vertex $K_k$-free graph. For each fixed $k\geq4$, the requested lower bound holds. More precisely, \[ f_k(n)\gg_k \frac{n^{1-1/(k-1)}}{(\log n)^{(2k-4)/(k-1)}}. \tag{1} \] We give an elementary probabilistic construction first, then prove $1$ from the exact current Ramsey input, with constants and rounding controlled. This updates the older local attempt, which preceded the relevant 2026 theorem.

A self-contained, weaker construction

Fix $k\geq4$, write $b=\binom{k}{2}$, and choose a constant $c>0$ so small that $2^kc^b\leq1/8$. In the random graph on $2n$ vertices with edge probability $p=cn^{-2/k}$, the expected number of copies of $K_k$ is at most \[ (2n)^kp^b\leq n/8. \] By Markov's inequality, the probability of more than $n$ such cliques is at most $1/8$.

Set $t=\lceil6p^{-1}\log(2n)\rceil$. For large $n$, $t\leq2n$, and the expected number of independent $t$-sets is at most \[ \binom{2n}{t}(1-p)^{\binom t2} \leq \exp\left(t\log\frac{2en}{t}-\frac p2t(t-1)\right)=o(1). \] For example, $p(t-1)\geq6\log(2n)-p$, while $\log(2en/t)\leq\log(2en)$, so the exponent tends to negative infinity. Some graph has at most $n$ forbidden cliques and no independent $t$-set. Delete one vertex from each surviving forbidden clique. At least $n$ vertices remain, the graph is $K_k$-free, and its independence number is still less than $t$. Restrict to exactly $n$ vertices. Every proper colour class has at most $t-1$ vertices, and therefore \[ f_k(n)\geq\frac{n}{t-1}\gg_k\frac{n^{1-2/k}}{\log n}. \tag{2} \] This proof is independent of the deeper input below, but its exponent does not yet give $1$.

The exact external Ramsey theorem

Theorem 1.1 of D. Bradač, Off-diagonal Ramsey numbers, arXiv:2605.28793v3, dated 16 June 2026, states that for each fixed $s\geq3$ there is $c_s>0$ such that \[ R(s,t)\geq c_s\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{2s-4}}\qquad(t\geq2). \tag{3} \] Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28793v3. Version 1 had a smaller exponent; the version and formula in $3$ are essential. The construction and container estimates proving $3$ are external inputs, not reproved here.

An exact-size transfer lemma

If $n<R(k,t)$, there is a $K_k$-free graph on exactly $n$ vertices with independence number less than $t$. Indeed, the definition gives such a graph on $R(k,t)-1$ vertices, and taking an induced $n$-vertex subgraph preserves both restrictions. Counting vertices in proper colour classes then gives \[ f_k(n)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rceil. \tag{4} \]

Put $a=k-1$, $b=2k-4$, and choose $D>0$ with $c_kD^a\geq2$. For sufficiently large $n$, define \[ t=\left\lceil D n^{1/a}(\log n)^{b/a}\right\rceil. \] Then $t\leq n$, so $\log t\leq\log n$, and the ceiling gives \[ R(k,t)\geq c_k\frac{t^a}{(\log t)^b} \geq c_kD^a n\geq2n>n. \] Also $t-1<D n^{1/a}(\log n)^{b/a}$. Applying $4$ proves $1$. The spare factor two ensures the strict Ramsey inequality; a bound $R(k,t)\geq c n$ with $c<1$ would not justify $4$.

For $k=4$, this gives $f_4(n)\gg n^{2/3}/(\log n)^{4/3}$. For general $k$, one may take $c_k$ in the problem's logarithmic exponent to be $(2k-4)/(k-1)$; this exponent notation is unrelated to the positive constant named $c_k$ in $3$.
