Conformal Field Theory Realizations of Subfactors

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A subfactor is an inclusion $N\subset M$ of von Neumann algebras with trivial centers. Its index and standard invariant encode the size and higher relative-commutant structure of the inclusion. In an operator-algebraic conformal field theory, interval or local observable algebras and sectors can produce subfactor inclusions. To say a subfactor arises in conformal field theory requires identifying the inclusion or invariant that the construction realizes. Finite index restricts the inclusion to the finite Jones-index case. A conformal net is an interval-indexed family of local observable algebras with conformal covariance, while a vertex operator algebra is an algebraic chiral-field structure. A direct construction is to recover such a theory from the given subfactor data, not merely exhibit a categorical analogy.

Testing a direct constant-algebra construction.
Suppose one tries to turn a given nontrivial factor $M$ from a subfactor inclusion into a conformal net by declaring $\mathcal A(I)=M$ for every interval $I$, acting on one fixed Hilbert space. Isotony holds automatically, but locality fails. Choose disjoint intervals $I,J$. Locality requires \[ [\mathcal A(I),\mathcal A(J)]=0, \] which in this construction says $[M,M]=0$. An abelian factor equals $\mathbb C I$, since its center is all of itself and a factor has scalar center. Thus this construction can realize only the trivial algebra, regardless of the index or standard invariant of $N\subset M$.
Why tensor copies are not yet a repair.
On a finite ordered collection of sites, separate tensor copies do give commuting disjoint algebras. However a continuum interval net needs inclusions compatible under every subdivision together with a strongly continuous positive-energy covariance representation. A finite tensor layout provides neither this refinement system nor its analytic covariance; freely choosing copies can erase the original inclusion's standard invariant.
