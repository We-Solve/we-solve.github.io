Physical Regularity Criteria for Massless Scattering Theory

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Haag--Ruelle scattering theory constructs asymptotic multiparticle states from local observables in a relativistic quantum field theory with a vacuum and translation spectrum. A sharp-mass particle has a one-particle mass shell even when other excitations accumulate near it. Herbst's regularity assumption controls the unwanted spectral component near this mass shell sufficiently strongly for the scattering limits. The report concerns a theory that also has massless particles, while the scattered massive particles retain sharp masses.
Source scope and qualifications.
The report excludes electrically charged infraparticles through the sharp-mass hypothesis. The exact quantitative Herbst assumption is in the cited scattering article rather than in this short report passage; no invented exponent or spectral bound is substituted here.

An abstract spectral test.
Let $K\geq0$ be a self-adjoint operator measuring spectral distance from a chosen sharp shell, let $P$ project onto its kernel, and let $\xi=(I-P)A\Omega$. Write $\mu(B)=\langle\xi,1_B(K)\xi\rangle$. This is an auxiliary abstraction, not a claimed transcription of Herbst's exact condition. If for some $s>0$ \[ \int_{(0,1)}r^{-2s}\,d\mu(r)<\infty, \] then $\|1_{(0,\delta)}(K)\xi\|\leq C\delta^s$ for $0<\delta<1$: on that interval $1\leq\delta^{2s}r^{-2s}$, and integration proves the assertion.
Counterexample to a naive energy criterion.
Finite positive energy moments alone imply no such power law. On $(0,e^{-1})$ put $d\mu(r)=dr/[r(\log(1/r))^2]$. It is a probability measure and \[ \mu((0,\delta))=1/\log(1/\delta),\qquad 0<\delta<e^{-1}. \] All positive moments are finite because $r$ is bounded; nevertheless this mass exceeds every fixed multiple of $\delta^{2s}$ for all sufficiently small $\delta$. Realize the example by multiplication by $r$ on $L^2(\mu)$ with $\xi=1$, and adjoin a one-dimensional zero eigenspace to provide a sharp eigenvalue. The added shell does not improve the orthogonal component.
Source-scope limit.
The example is a spectral model, not a local relativistic quantum field theory, and says nothing about charged infraparticles, excluded in the source. It rules out the purely abstract implication "sharp shell plus finite energy moments implies power-law spectral regularity". Deriving a stronger near-shell bound from physically meaningful locality or dynamical assumptions, and matching it to the exact scattering estimates of the cited article, are the unresolved steps.
