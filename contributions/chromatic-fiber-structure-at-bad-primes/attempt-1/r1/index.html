Chromatic Fiber Structure at Bad Primes

OpenAI's claim

The canonical weak-splitting unit is claimed to have no retraction at height p for p at least five and height p+1 for p at least seven, witnessed by $\beta_1^{(p-1)^2}$. The cited manuscript argues for that result; its relation to this problem is specified below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $p\ge5$, $S=S_p^\wedge$, $L_j=L_{E(j)}$ and $L_{K(j)}$ be chromatic localizations. Put \[ d_p=2p(p-1)-2,\qquad k_p=(p-1)^2,\qquad z_p=\beta_1^{k_p}\in\pi_{d_pk_p}S, \] where $\beta_1$ is the first classical beta element. OpenAI claims that the image of $z_p$ in $\pi_{d_pk_p}L_{p-1}S$ is nonzero but is killed by the unit-induced map \[ i_{p,S}:L_{p-1}S\longrightarrow L_{p-1}L_{K(p)}S. \] Hence this map has no homotopy retraction. The finite input is the sphere and the height equals the prime; this does not assert such failure at every height.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 318, 2026-09-27): The named beta power survives under the height-$p-1$ unit by an ordinary homotopy-fixed-point detection calculation. At each upper height, a separated multiplicative filtration puts beta_1 in degree at least two and vanishes beyond a dimension-controlled bound, killing that same power. Completion and naturality identify a nonzero lower-localized class in the unit's kernel, obstructing a retraction.
