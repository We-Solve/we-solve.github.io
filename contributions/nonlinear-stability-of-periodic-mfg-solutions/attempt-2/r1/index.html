Periodic MFG stability as a boundary problem: the unavoidable neutral-phase obstruction

Problem ID: C73-40. Research attempt; independent review pending.

Target and scope

Let $(v_*,m_*,\lambda_*)$ be a smooth, genuinely time-periodic solution of period $P$ on the unit-volume torus ${\mathbb{T}}^d$: \[ \lambda_*-v_{*,t}-\nu\Delta v_*+\tfrac12|Dv_*|^2=f(x,m_*), \qquad m_{*,t}-\nu\Delta m_*-{\operatorname{div}}(m_*Dv_*)=0, \] where $\nu>0$, $m_*>0$, and $\int m_*=1$. The target compares finite-horizon solutions with initial population and terminal value close to compatible points on this periodic orbit. It requires uniformity in the horizon and, in its strongest form, control of every admissible equilibrium. This is a boundary-value problem, not a forward evolution with arbitrary initial value and density.

Version 1 gave a conditional contraction theorem based on a uniform inverse of the centered linear boundary operator, a conserved bilinear form, and a stationary homogeneous example with singular horizons. We retain their statements and proofs for context. Version 2 proves that the stated uniform inverse is impossible for every genuinely periodic reference: slowly modulated phase variations have order-one size, zero boundary data and residual of order $1/T$. Thus that conditional theorem cannot establish stability of the periodic orbits in the target. This identifies a necessary correction to the proposed method, not nonlinear orbital instability. A successful inverse-based method must allow an appropriate varying phase or otherwise remove the neutral direction. The full catalogue problem remains unresolved.

Version 1 records a check of the distinction between known periodic examples and their further qualitative questions in [CD, Section 3.3] and [CN, Section 6, especially Remark 6.4] on 9 October 2026. We retain those references without claiming a fresh literature search. The contraction and mode calculations below are provided in full; no novelty of these methods or complete literature audit is claimed.

Linear variations do not define a dissipative forward flow

For a phase $\theta$, write $b(t,x)=Dv_*(t+\theta,x)$, $\bar m(t,x)=m_*(t+\theta,x)$, and $a(t,x)=f_m(x,\bar m(t,x))$. Uncentered linear variations $(w,\rho)$ solve \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6c-69-6e} \begin{cases} -w_t-\nu\Delta w+b\cdot Dw=a\rho,\\ \rho_t-\nu\Delta\rho-{\operatorname{div}}(\rho b+\bar m Dw)=0, \qquad\int\rho=0. \end{cases} \end{equation}
Proposition: If $(w_1,\rho_1)$ and $(w_2,\rho_2)$ are two $C^{1,2}$ solutions of (lin) on an interval, then \[ \Omega(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}(w_1\rho_2-w_2\rho_1)dx \] is constant. For a single solution, \[ \frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}w\rho\,dx =-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}a\rho^2dx-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}\bar m|Dw|^2dx. \] If the reference solution is differentiable one additional time in the classical sense, $(v_{*,t}(t+\theta),m_{*,t}(t+\theta))$ solves (lin); it is the phase variation.
Proof: Substitute (lin) in the derivative of $\int w_1\rho_2$. The two Laplace terms cancel after spatial integration by parts, and the terms involving $b$ cancel as well. The result is \[ \frac{d}{dt}\int w_1\rho_2 =-\int a\rho_1\rho_2-\int\bar m Dw_1\cdot Dw_2. \] The right side is symmetric in the two solutions, proving conservation of $\Omega$. Taking identical solutions gives the second formula. Differentiating the reference PDEs in time, with constant $\lambda_*$, gives the phase-variation assertion directly. ∎

The coefficient $a$ can be negative, so the displayed quadratic form is not generally dissipative. No bounded forward solution operator or Floquet monodromy for the full PDE is inferred from this identity. The backward heat component of (lin) requires that distinction.

A conditional nonlinear theorem with an explicit selection

Here are the precise spaces and additional regularity assumptions. Fix $0<\alpha<1$, assume the reference solution and $f$ are smooth, and fix $\zeta>0$ with $\bar m\geq2\zeta$ at every phase. For $T\geq1$ let $X_T$ be the Banach space of pairs $(w,\rho)$ of parabolic Hölder class $C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}$ on $[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}}^d$, both of spatial mean zero at every time. Use the sum of their usual parabolic norms, with Hölder seminorms computed on time intervals of length at most one and their supremum over such intervals. This norm controls $\|Dw\|_\infty+\|\rho\|_\infty$. The local-in-time norm convention avoids a horizon factor in product estimates. It is equivalent to the usual norm for each fixed $T$.

Let $Y_T$ be the product of two spatially mean-zero residual spaces $C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}$, with the same interval convention, and two mean-zero boundary spaces $C^{2+\alpha}({\mathbb{T}}^d)$. Write $\Pi r=r-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}r$ for spatial mean projection and define \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-4c} L_{T,\theta}(w,\rho)= \left(\Pi[-w_t-\nu\Delta w+b\cdot Dw-a\rho],\, \rho_t-\nu\Delta\rho-{\operatorname{div}}(\rho b+\bar m Dw),\, \rho(0),\,w(T)\right). \end{equation} Centering the first equation matters: an uncentered constant residual would produce a value correction proportional to $T-t$, which has no uniform bound in a norm measuring the value itself.

Lemma (Uniform quadratic remainder): Under the smoothness and positivity assumptions just stated, there are $r_0>0$ and $C<\infty$, independent of $T\geq1$ and $\theta$, such that $r_0\leq\zeta$ and the nonlinear residual \[ N_{T,\theta}(w,\rho)= \left(\Pi\left[\tfrac12|Dw|^2- \{f(x,\bar m+\rho)-f(x,\bar m)-a\rho\}\right], -{\operatorname{div}}(\rho Dw),0,0\right) \] satisfies, whenever $\|x\|_{X_T},\|y\|_{X_T}\leq r_0$, \[ \|N(x)\|_{Y_T}\leq C\|x\|_{X_T}^2, \qquad \|N(x)-N(y)\|_{Y_T} \leq C(\|x\|_{X_T}+\|y\|_{X_T})\|x-y\|_{X_T}. \] Here $x$ and $y$ denote pairs of perturbations, not spatial points.
Proof: On the indicated ball, $\bar m+\rho\geq\zeta$ and all densities lie in a fixed compact positive interval. The derivatives of $f$ needed below, through third order in $m$ and the corresponding spatial Hölder bounds, are uniformly bounded on that interval. Reference norms are uniform in phase and horizon because the reference is smooth and periodic. Taylor's integral formula gives \[ f(x,\bar m+\rho)-f(x,\bar m)-a\rho =\rho^2\int_0^1(1-s)f_{mm}(x,\bar m+s\rho)ds. \] The multiplier on the right has uniformly bounded Hölder norm on $r_0$ balls and is Lipschitz in $\rho$ in that norm, by the bounded third derivative and the composition rule obtained by the mean-value formula. For scalar functions the elementary product estimate $[uv]_\alpha\leq\|u\|_\infty[v]_\alpha+ \|v\|_\infty[u]_\alpha$ applies to space and time differences. Together with $u^2-v^2=(u-v)(u+v)$, it proves both estimates for the Taylor remainder and $|Dw|^2$. For the second component expand ${\operatorname{div}}(\rho Dw)=D\rho\cdot Dw+\rho\Delta w$ and use the same product estimate, followed by subtraction of the two products for the Lipschitz bound. The $X_T$ norm controls every derivative used. Projection $\Pi$ is bounded in each residual norm. All estimates are local on time intervals of length at most one, so taking their supremum introduces no $T$ factor. Increasing one finite constant $C$ gives the announced bounds. ∎
Theorem (Selected orbital shadowing from a uniform inverse): In addition to the preceding assumptions, suppose every $L_{T,\theta}:X_T\to Y_T$ is a bounded linear isomorphism and \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-69-6e-76-65-72-73-65} \sup_{T\geq1,\,\theta\in[0,P)}\|L_{T,\theta}^{-1}\|\leq K<\infty. \end{equation} Let $C,r_0$ be as in Lemma Lemma (Uniform quadratic remainder). For boundary data define \[ d_0=m_0-m_*(\theta),\qquad d_T=\Pi(h-v_*(T+\theta)),\qquad \delta=\|d_0\|_{C^{2+\alpha}}+\|d_T\|_{C^{2+\alpha}}, \] with $\int m_0=1$. If \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-73-6d-61-6c-6c} \delta\leq\min\{r_0/(2K),\,1/(8CK^2)\}, \end{equation} then there is exactly one centered perturbation $(w,\rho)$ in the closed $X_T$ ball of radius $2K\delta$ producing a classical finite-horizon MFG solution with $m(0)=m_0$, $u(T)=h$. It satisfies \[ \|w\|_{X_T\text{ component}}+\|\rho\|_{X_T\text{ component}} \leq2K\delta,\qquad m\geq\zeta, \] and, for the orbit distance in the catalogue, \[ \sup_{0\leq t\leq T} \inf_{s\in[0,P)}\left(\|Du(t)-Dv_*(s)\|_\infty +\|m(t)-m_*(s)\|_1\right) \leq2K\delta. \] Selection is defined by this unique small fixed point. For two boundary perturbations satisfying a common smallness bound, their selected centered solutions differ by at most $2K$ times the $Y_T$ norm of the difference of the boundary vectors.
Proof: Put $d=(0,0,d_0,d_T)$ and consider the map $\mathcal A(x)=L_{T,\theta}^{-1}(d-N_{T,\theta}(x))$. For $R=2K\delta\leq r_0$ and $\|x\|\leq R$, \[ \|\mathcal A(x)\|\leq K\delta+KCR^2 \leq K\delta+\tfrac12K\delta<R \] when $\delta>0$, by (small); for $\delta=0$ the zero vector is the sole point of the stated ball. For $\|x\|,\|y\|\leq R$, \[ \|\mathcal A(x)-\mathcal A(y)\| \leq2KCR\|x-y\|\leq\tfrac12\|x-y\|. \] The complete closed ball therefore has a unique fixed point by the contraction theorem (whose proof is the convergence of iterates under this geometric distance bound). It solves $Lx+N(x)=d$. The density $m=\bar m+\rho$ has mass one and is at least $2\zeta-r_0\geq\zeta$.

To recover the actual value, set $u_{\rm ref}(t,x)=v_*(t+\theta,x)-\lambda_*t$. The first residual of $u_{\rm ref}+w$ with density $m$ has zero spatial projection and is therefore a scalar function $r(t)$. Choose a scalar $c(t)$ by $c'(t)=r(t)$ and $c(T)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^d}(h-u_{\rm ref}(T))dx$. Then $u=u_{\rm ref}+w+c$ satisfies the unprojected HJB equation because its residual is $r-c'=0$, and its terminal value is $h$. The Fokker--Planck equation and initial condition already follow from the fixed-point equation. This also proves nonemptiness for every allowed perturbation; no vacuous stability bound is used.

At time $t$, use orbit phase $s=t+\theta$ modulo $P$. The difference of gradients is $Dw$ and the difference of densities is $\rho$. The stated $X_T$ norm and unit torus volume bound their required norms by $\|x\|\leq2K\delta$, proving shadowing. For two fixed points, subtract their fixed-point equations and use the same $1/2$ Lipschitz bound for the nonlinear part to obtain $\|x-x'\|\leq K\|d-d'\|+\tfrac12\|x-x'\|$. This proves the final assertion and completes the proof. ∎

This theorem measures perturbations in a stronger boundary topology than the catalogue's $L^1$ and gradient-uniform topology and selects the nearby branch. It does not control far-away equilibria with the same data. More strongly, Theorem Theorem (Linear growth of any phase-fixed inverse) below proves that its hypothesis (inverse) fails for every genuine periodic reference under the present norms. The theorem remains a valid conditional implication, but is not an applicable stability result for those orbits.

Exact singular horizons in the homogeneous linear model

Proposition: Linearize the homogeneous quadratic MFG around $v=0,m=1$, with $a=f'(1)$. Let $q>0$ be a spatial Laplace eigenvalue and suppose $a<-\nu^2q$. Put $\omega=\sqrt{-q(\nu^2q+a)}$. For the corresponding mode amplitudes $(U,R)$, impose $R(0)=0$ and $U(T)=d$. Define \[ D(T)=\cos(\omega T)+\frac{\nu q}{\omega}\sin(\omega T),\qquad T_j=\frac{j\pi-\arctan(\omega/(\nu q))}{\omega},\quad j=1,2,\ldots. \] If $D(T)\ne0$, the unique mode solution has $U(0)=d/D(T)$. At every $T_j$, zero boundary data have a nonzero linear solution, and boundary data with $d\ne0$ have no linear solution. Furthermore $|D'(T_j)|=\sqrt{-aq}>0$, so the boundary response gain is unbounded as $T\to T_j$. Hence a bound of the form (inverse) over all $T\geq1$ fails for this homogeneous reference.
Proof: The mode system is \[ \binom{U'}{R'}= A\binom U R,\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}\nu q&-a\\-q&-\nu q\end{pmatrix}, \qquad A^2=-\omega^2 I. \] Thus $e^{tA}=I\cos(\omega t)+(A/\omega)\sin(\omega t)$. Starting at $(U(0),0)$ gives \[ U(t)=D(t)U(0),\qquad R(t)=-\frac q\omega\sin(\omega t)U(0). \] The terminal condition therefore is the scalar equation $D(T)U(0)=d$. The listed $T_j$ are exactly the positive zeros of $D$. At a zero, $\cos(\omega T_j)=-(\nu q/\omega)\sin(\omega T_j)$, and $\sin(\omega T_j)\ne0$. Direct differentiation gives \[ |D'(T_j)|=\sqrt{\omega^2+\nu^2q^2}=\sqrt{-aq}. \] This proves both the singularity and the divergent inverse gain. There are arbitrarily large $T_j$, so restricting to $T\geq1$ does not remove the obstruction. A smooth spatial eigenfunction embeds each mode in all the Hölder spaces used above, so it obstructs a bounded inverse for the PDE operator as well. ∎

The uniform inverse fails along every genuine periodic orbit

This section is the substantive new result of Version 2. It applies to the genuine periodic reference in the target, whereas Proposition Proposition concerns a stationary reference only. Use exactly the centered spaces $X_T,Y_T$ and operator $L_{T,\theta}$ from (L); in particular their time Hölder norms are taken locally on intervals of length at most one. We assume the reference is smooth, as in the conditional nonlinear section, so its phase derivative belongs to all these spaces.
Lemma (The centered phase solution): Set \[ W_\theta(t,x)=\Pi\partial_t v_*(t+\theta,x),\qquad R_\theta(t,x)=\partial_t m_*(t+\theta,x). \] Then both fields have spatial mean zero and satisfy the two interior components of $L_{T,\theta}(W_\theta,R_\theta)=0$ on every time interval. Their local Hölder norms of every required order are bounded independently of $T$ and $\theta$. Moreover \[ c_*:=\max_{s\in[0,P]} \left(\|\Pi\partial_t v_*(s)\|_\infty +\|\partial_t m_*(s)\|_\infty\right)>0. \]
Proof: The density mean is constant, so $\int\partial_t m_*=0$. Differentiating the autonomous reference equations yields the uncentered linear system (lin) for $(\partial_t v_*,\partial_t m_*)$. Subtracting the spatial mean of $\partial_t v_*$ does not change its spatial derivatives. In the first equation it changes only a spatially constant time derivative, which is removed by $\Pi$ in (L). The second equation depends on the value perturbation only through its spatial derivatives. This proves the asserted interior equations and centering. Smoothness and time periodicity give every uniform local Hölder bound. If $c_*=0$, then $\partial_t m_*=0$ and $D\partial_t v_*=D\Pi\partial_t v_*=0$, so both $m_*$ and $Dv_*$ are independent of time. This contradicts genuine periodicity. ∎

Choose once and for all a smooth function $\chi:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ that is zero on neighborhoods of $0$ and $1$ and equals one on $[1/3,2/3]$. For $T\geq1$ define \[ x_{T,\theta}(t,x)=\chi(t/T) (W_\theta(t,x),R_\theta(t,x)). \]

Theorem (Linear growth of any phase-fixed inverse): There is a constant $C_*<\infty$, depending on the fixed smooth periodic reference, $\alpha$ and $\chi$, but not on $T$ or $\theta$, such that for every $T\geq\max\{1,3P\}$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-71-75-61-73-69-6b-65-72-6e-65-6c} \|x_{T,\theta}\|_{X_T}\geq c_*,\qquad \|L_{T,\theta}x_{T,\theta}\|_{Y_T}\leq\frac{C_*}{T}. \end{equation} The boundary components of $L_{T,\theta}x_{T,\theta}$ vanish exactly. If $L_{T,\theta}$ is an isomorphism, then \[ \|L_{T,\theta}^{-1}\|\geq\frac{c_*}{C_*}T. \] Consequently the horizon-independent inverse assumption (inverse) of Version 1 is false for every smooth genuinely periodic reference in the target. The same test rules out a uniform estimate $\|x\|_{X_T}\leq K\|L_{T,\theta}x\|_{Y_T}$ even if no surjectivity assumption is made.
Proof: Multiplication by $\chi(t/T)$ preserves spatial means and the required regularity. Lemma Lemma (The centered phase solution), linearity, and the product rule give exactly \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-70-68-61-73-65-66-6f-72-63-69-6e-67} L_{T,\theta}x_{T,\theta} =\left(-\frac{\chi'(t/T)}T W_\theta, \frac{\chi'(t/T)}T R_\theta,\ 0,\ 0\right). \end{equation} The signs come from the backward derivative $-w_t$ and forward derivative $\rho_t$. Projection of the first displayed term changes nothing because $W_\theta$ has spatial mean zero. The two boundary components vanish because $\chi$ vanishes near both endpoints.

We spell out the uniform Hölder estimate in (phaseforcing). Its scalar time multiplier has supremum at most $\|\chi'\|_\infty/T$. On an interval of length at most one, \[ \frac{|\chi'(t/T)/T-\chi'(s/T)/T|}{|t-s|^{\alpha/2}} \leq\frac{\|\chi''\|_\infty}{T^2}|t-s|^{1-\alpha/2} \leq\frac{\|\chi''\|_\infty}{T^2}. \] There is no spatial variation in this multiplier. Apply the elementary product Hölder inequality to it and to the uniformly bounded periodic fields of Lemma Lemma (The centered phase solution). Each residual $C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}$ norm is at most a constant times $1/T+1/T^2\leq2/T$. Their sum gives the upper bound in (quasikernel) with $C_*$ independent of the horizon and phase.

The interval $[T/3,2T/3]$ has length at least $P$ and $\chi(t/T)=1$ there. It therefore contains, after adding any $\theta$, every phase of the periodic orbit. In particular there is a time in this interval where the sum of the two supremum norms in Lemma Lemma (The centered phase solution) equals $c_*$. The $X_T$ norm controls that sum. This proves the lower bound in (quasikernel).

If an inverse exists, applying it to $L_{T,\theta}x_{T,\theta}$ gives $c_*\leq\|L_{T,\theta}^{-1}\|C_*/T$, proving its lower bound. If the residual happens to be zero for some horizon, the nonzero $x_{T,\theta}$ instead proves noninjectivity directly. Finally, the same substitution into a proposed uniform coercive estimate would give $c_*\leq KC_*/T$ for arbitrarily large $T$, which is impossible. ∎

Proposition (The obstruction is compatible with zero orbital error): For $|\delta|\leq1$ put \[ \phi(t)=t+\theta+\delta\chi(t/T),\qquad \widetilde m(t,x)=m_*(\phi(t),x),\qquad \widetilde u(t,x)=v_*(\phi(t),x)-\lambda_*t. \] These smooth fields have exactly the reference boundary data $\widetilde m(0)=m_*(\theta)$ and $\widetilde u(T)=v_*(T+\theta)-\lambda_*T$. Their HJB and Fokker--Planck residuals are, respectively, \begin{align*} -\widetilde u_t-\nu\Delta\widetilde u +\tfrac12|D\widetilde u|^2-f(x,\widetilde m) &=-\frac{\delta\chi'(t/T)}T\partial_t v_*(\phi(t),x),\\ \widetilde m_t-\nu\Delta\widetilde m -{\operatorname{div}}(\widetilde m D\widetilde u) &=\frac{\delta\chi'(t/T)}T\partial_t m_*(\phi(t),x). \end{align*} Their local $C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}$ norms are bounded by $C|\delta|/T$, uniformly for $T\geq1$, $\theta$ and $|\delta|\leq1$. At every time their orbit distance in the catalogue is exactly zero. They are approximate solutions; the proposition does not assert that their nonzero residual can be corrected by a comparably small exact solution.
Proof: The endpoint equalities follow from $\chi(0)=\chi(1)=0$. At phase $\phi(t)$, the reference PDEs cancel all terms that do not contain $\phi'(t)-1=\delta\chi'(t/T)/T$. The chain rule therefore gives precisely the two residual formulas. Their local Hölder bounds follow by the same product estimate used in Theorem Theorem (Linear growth of any phase-fixed inverse): the phase map has derivative bounded by $1+\|\chi'\|_\infty$, and the smooth periodic reference and its required derivatives consequently have uniform local Hölder norms after composition. Finally $(D\widetilde u(t),\widetilde m(t))= (Dv_*(\phi(t)),m_*(\phi(t)))$ belongs to $\mathcal O_*$ by its definition. The infimum defining the orbit distance is therefore zero. ∎

The phase derivative supplies a neutral direction because the equations are autonomous. Theorem Theorem (Linear growth of any phase-fixed inverse) proves that spatial centering alone does not remove this direction; even zero boundary perturbations admit a large response to slowly varying interior forcing. Proposition Proposition (The obstruction is compatible with zero orbital error) explains why this is not a proof of failure of the catalogue's orbital stability. An estimate for transverse errors, with a permitted time-dependent phase and a justified equation for that phase, would be a different statement and could avoid this obstruction. No such estimate is assumed or proved here.

Exact remaining issue

The homogeneous state in Proposition Proposition is stationary; that proposition is not a nonlinear instability theorem for an existing genuine periodic orbit. It demonstrates why a horizon-independent boundary inverse cannot be presumed from imaginary mode eigenvalues. For every genuinely periodic solution, Theorem Theorem (Linear growth of any phase-fixed inverse) now rules out the full uniform inverse proposed in Version 1. One must instead establish a suitable phase-adapted estimate or another nonlinear selection mechanism, handle possible additional conjugate horizons, and, for the strongest catalogue formulation, control every admissible equilibrium in the weaker boundary topology. Those steps are not proved here. The retained contraction theorem is a correct but inapplicable conditional implication for these orbits; the new conclusions are the universal phase obstruction and its precise compatibility with zero orbital distance for the modulated approximate trajectory.

Completion Estimate

25%

This is a heuristic assessment of the full catalogue target. The version establishes a universal neutral-phase obstruction to the previous uniform-inverse approach and explains why that obstruction does not imply orbital instability. It does not prove nonlinear stability or instability of an actual periodic equilibrium, provide a valid transverse selection theorem, or control every equilibrium with nearby boundary data.
