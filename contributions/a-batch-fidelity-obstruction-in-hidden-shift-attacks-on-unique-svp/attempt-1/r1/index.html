Research attempt: Accumulated state error and the unique-SVP promise gap

Suppose, as an auxiliary reduction model, an ideal quantum routine needs $t$ independent copies of a unit vector $|\psi\rangle$, but preparation supplies independent copies of $|\phi\rangle$ with $|\langle\psi\mid\phi\rangle|^2=1-\varepsilon$. The squared overlap of the batches is $(1-\varepsilon)^t$, not $1-\varepsilon$. Their trace distance is \[ D_t=\sqrt{1-(1-\varepsilon)^t}. \tag{1} \] For completeness, for any two unit vectors $u,v$, adjust the phase of $v$ to write $v=cu+\sqrt{1-c^2}w$, where $c=|\langle u,v\rangle|$ and $w\perp u$. On their span, $uu^*-vv^*$ has trace zero and determinant $-(1-c^2)$, so its eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{1-c^2}$. Half of its trace norm is therefore $\sqrt{1-c^2}$. Tensor-product overlaps give $1$.

This quantity has a direct operational meaning requiring no unproved inference about a particular sieve. If a measurement event has effect $0\leq M\leq I$, diagonalize the trace-zero Hermitian difference $\Delta$ into positive and negative parts. Then $|\operatorname{tr}(M\Delta)|\leq\operatorname{tr}\Delta_+=\|\Delta\|_1/2$, and equality is attained by the projector onto the positive eigenspace. Thus $1$ is an exact worst-case difference in event probability, rather than merely a norm chosen for convenience.

The elementary inequalities $1-(1-\varepsilon)^t\leq t\varepsilon$ and $(1-\varepsilon)^t\leq e^{-t\varepsilon}$ imply \[ \sqrt{1-e^{-t\varepsilon}}\leq D_t\leq\sqrt{t\varepsilon}. \] The upper bound follows by induction from $(1-\varepsilon)^t\geq1-t\varepsilon$; the lower bound uses $\log(1-\varepsilon)\leq-\varepsilon$. To guarantee $D_t\leq\eta$ uniformly by this route, $\varepsilon\leq\eta^2/t$ is sufficient, whereas $1$ also requires $\varepsilon\leq1-(1-\eta^2)^{1/t}$. A fixed single-copy infidelity does not give fixed total error as $t$ grows. Conversely, a particular algorithm might tolerate more error; $1$ does not prove every hidden-shift routine fails.

There is a separate lattice-promise obstruction. For $0<\delta<1$, the two-dimensional lattice generated by $(1,0)$ and $(0,1+\delta)$ has $\lambda_1=1$ and second successive minimum $\lambda_2=1+\delta$. Indeed every vector is $(m,(1+\delta)n)$; a vector independent of $(1,0)$ has $n\ne0$ and length at least $1+\delta$, attained by the second basis vector. For any approximation factor $\gamma>1$, choose $\delta<\min(1,\gamma-1)$. Returning the second basis vector is then a valid $\gamma$-approximate answer, but its gap $\lambda_2/\lambda_1$ can be arbitrarily close to one. Thus a general approximation-factor specification supplies no fixed uniqueness gap exceeding one. A reduction that needs such a gap must construct or prove it separately.

Together these checks locate two concrete missing parameters in a runtime estimate: the cost to prepare each state at accuracy scaling with the total number of consumed states, and a justified reduction from the stated lattice promise. Counting only the subsequent ideal hidden-shift operations omits both.
