Exact chain gluing and a sharp cyclic incompatibility certificate

For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. A three-way table is a nonnegative array $x_{ijk}$, with two-way margins obtained by summing over one index. Holes mark entries or margins that have not been observed. Completing these data requires compatibility of the specified counts and margins, followed by estimation within the intended statistical model.

Research attempt

Literature and scope

Problem 56 combines incomplete three-way tables, margins and likelihood without a missingness mechanism. I chose two fixed, ignorable observation patterns: one independent cohort observes $(X,Y)$ and another observes $(Y,Z)$, under the model $X\perp Z\mid Y$. This is an explicitly restricted likelihood model. I tested the route of gluing the two empirical pair margins without first fitting their common $Y$ distribution.
Proposition: For strictly positive pair counts $n_{xy}$ and $m_{yz}$ of respective totals $N_1,N_2$, the unique interior MLE in the model $p_{xyz}=\pi_y a_{x\mid y}b_{z\mid y}$ is \[ \widehat\pi_y=\frac{n_{+y}+m_{y+}}{N_1+N_2},\quad \widehat a_{x\mid y}=\frac{n_{xy}}{n_{+y}},\quad \widehat b_{z\mid y}=\frac{m_{yz}}{m_{y+}}. \]
Proof: The two observed margins are $\pi_ya_{x\mid y}$ and $\pi_yb_{z\mid y}$. Their independent multinomial likelihood therefore has log, up to constants, \[ \sum_y(n_{+y}+m_{y+})\log\pi_y+ \sum_{x,y}n_{xy}\log a_{x\mid y}+ \sum_{y,z}m_{yz}\log b_{z\mid y}. \] The probability vectors in its three groups are constrained separately. For positive counts $c_i$ of total $C$, the vector $r_i=c_i/C$ uniquely maximizes $\sum c_i\log p_i$: the difference from its value at $r$ is $C\sum r_i\log(p_i/r_i)\le C\sum r_i(p_i/r_i-1)=0$, using $\log s\le s-1$, with equality only when $p_i=r_i$ for all $i$. Applying this to each group proves the formulas and uniqueness. ∎

Failed route and verification

Take $n=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6&1\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with rows $X$ and columns $Y$, and $m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\2&6\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with rows $Y$ and columns $Z$. Both totals are ten, but the empirical $Y$ distributions are $(4/5,1/5)$ and $(1/5,4/5)$. No joint distribution can have both empirical margins, since marginalizing either would give contradictory $Y$ laws. The MLE instead uses pooled $\widehat\pi=(1/2,1/2)$. Exact rational substitution gives joint probabilities, in lexicographic $xyz$ order, $(3,3,1,3,1,1,1,3)/16$, summing to one.

Original-scope comparison and remaining gaps

This is the familiar chain factorization under a specified sampling mechanism. It does not address arbitrary holes, informative missingness, cyclic interactions or algebraic components at zero margins. The failed inference is that independently observed empirical margins must be compatible exact constraints. No general solution to the original missing-table agenda is claimed.
