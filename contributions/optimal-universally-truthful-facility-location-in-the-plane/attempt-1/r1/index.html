Random axes and the limits of angular reweighting

Problem ID: D82-151. Status: unresolved.

Target and scope

The target is the best worst-case expected Euclidean social-cost ratio over all universally truthful planar facility mechanisms, uniformly in the population size. Barak [Barak, Sections 2--4] establishes $4/\pi$ for randomly rotated coordinate medians and leaves the unrestricted optimum open. We give a self-contained upper-bound proof and a minimax reduction: changing the report-independent distribution of axes cannot improve the worst-case ratio of a fixed coordinate-median rule. This is a restriction on a proposed direction of improvement, not a lower bound for every universally truthful mechanism.

For $n\ge2$ set $k=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor$. The deterministic rule $C_n$ returns the $k$th order statistic in each of the two coordinates; in particular it uses the lower median when $n$ is even. Let $R_\theta$ denote rotation through $\theta$, and define \[ C_{n,\theta}(x)=R_{-\theta}C_n(R_\theta x_1,\ldots,R_\theta x_n). \] All random angles below are independent of the reports. These maps are Borel measurable since order statistics are continuous.

Truthfulness and the average norm bound

Lemma (The one-dimensional option interval): Fix $n-1$ real reports and an order rank $1\le k\le n$. The $k$th order statistic after inserting a report $z$ is the projection of $z$ onto a fixed closed interval, allowing an infinite endpoint. Consequently, for every true location $a$, its truthful output minimizes distance from $a$ over all obtainable outputs.
Proof: Order the other reports as $b_1\le\cdots\le b_{n-1}$, set $b_0=-\infty,b_n=+\infty$, and inspect the three cases $z\le b_{k-1}$, $b_{k-1}\le z\le b_k$, and $z\ge b_k$. The output is respectively $b_{k-1},z,b_k$, with the evident interpretation at infinite endpoints. This is exactly projection onto $[b_{k-1},b_k]$. Projection is the closest point in that interval to $a$. ∎
Theorem: Every $C_{n,\theta}$ is truthful for Euclidean distance and unanimous. For uniform $\Theta$ on $[0,2\pi)$, the resulting mechanism has finite expected costs and satisfies, for every profile $x$, \[ {\mathbb{E}}{\operatorname{SC}}(x,C_{n,\Theta}(x))\le\frac4\pi{\operatorname{OPT}}(x). \]
Proof: In the rotated coordinate system, Lemma Lemma (The one-dimensional option interval) shows that a truthful report weakly minimizes each of the two absolute coordinate errors. Squaring and adding these two inequalities proves Euclidean truthfulness; rotations preserve distances. Unanimity follows immediately.

Write $\|\cdot\|_1$ for the sum of absolute coordinates. Coordinate medians minimize $\sum_i\|R_\theta x_i-z\|_1$: for a scalar coordinate, the slope of the convex sum of absolute deviations changes from nonpositive to nonnegative on the median interval. Thus, for a Euclidean minimizer $y^*$, \begin{align*} {\operatorname{SC}}(x,C_{n,\theta}(x)) &\le\sum_i\|R_\theta x_i-C_n(R_\theta x)\|_1\\ &\le\sum_i\|R_\theta(x_i-y^*)\|_1. \end{align*} A minimizer exists because the social cost is continuous and tends to infinity when $\|y\|_2$ tends to infinity. For every fixed vector $v$, \[ \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\|R_\theta v\|_1\,d\theta =\|v\|_2\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} (|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|)\,d\theta =\frac4\pi\|v\|_2. \] Integrating the preceding pointwise inequality proves the result and finiteness. At unanimous profiles both sides are zero. ∎

This proof is a reconstruction of the standard random-projection norm argument underlying the cited bound, not a claim of a new upper bound.

A minimax obstruction to choosing better axes

For a Borel probability measure $\nu$ on the circle define \[ W_n(\nu)=\sup_{x:{\operatorname{OPT}}(x)>0} \frac{\int{\operatorname{SC}}(x,C_{n,\theta}(x))\,d\nu(\theta)}{{\operatorname{OPT}}(x)}. \] Let $u$ be uniform angular measure. The next assertion does not assume that $\nu$ has a density or finite support.
Theorem (Uniform angular mixing is minimax): For every $n\ge2$ and every $\nu$, $W_n(\nu)\ge W_n(u)$. Consequently, for arbitrary population-dependent measures $(\nu_n)$, \[ \sup_{n\ge2}W_n(\nu_n)\ge\sup_{n\ge2}W_n(u). \]
Proof: Fix a nonunanimous profile $x$ and let $h_x(\theta)={\operatorname{SC}}(x,C_{n,\theta}(x))/{\operatorname{OPT}}(x)$. The identity \[ C_{n,\theta}(R_\varphi x)=R_\varphi C_{n,\theta+\varphi}(x) \] follows by multiplying the rotations. Both social cost and the optimum are rotation-invariant, so the ratio on the rotated profile is $\int h_x(\theta+\varphi)\,d\nu(\theta)$. Every such value is at most $W_n(\nu)$. Integrate over uniform $\varphi$. Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative integrand, and translation-invariance of uniform measure gives \[ W_n(\nu)\ge \int\!\int h_x(\theta+\varphi)\,du(\varphi)d\nu(\theta) =\int h_x(\psi)\,du(\psi). \] Taking the supremum over $x$ proves the first assertion, and taking the supremum over $n$ proves the second. ∎
The argument applies to any fixed measurable base mechanism and its rotation conjugates whenever its cost ratio is defined. Thus it is more than a statement about discrete angle grids. Any strict improvement over the uniform rotation guarantee must change the base family, use additional mechanisms, or exploit structure other than reweighting absolute axes.

The missing unrestricted lower bound

For completeness, fixed report-independent mixtures of truthful rules remain universally truthful by their definition as distributions over seeds; Theorem Theorem (Uniform angular mixing is minimax) covers only mixtures of the displayed orbit. It does not compare this orbit with a mixture of distinct base rules. Nor does it allow the angle distribution to depend on reports: such a choice requires a fresh incentive proof.

The cited source proves tightness of its random-rotation mechanism. We have not reproved that lower-bound construction here, and we do not infer from it that the global optimum is $4/\pi$. The precise remaining task is a lower bound that applies to every deterministic truthful rule used as a seed, or a universally truthful construction outside the above orbit with a smaller uniform worst-case ratio. No finite computation is used. The HTML source was consulted on 9 October 2026, including the universal truthfulness statement and the planar upper bound.
