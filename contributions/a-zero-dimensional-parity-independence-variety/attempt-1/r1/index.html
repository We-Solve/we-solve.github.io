A fixed parity component: a zero-dimensional CI calculation with all probability points checked

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For finite discrete variables, conditional-independence constraints can be expressed by minors of conditional probability tables. Their algebraic zero set depends on the chosen constraints and ambient coordinates. A zero-dimensional algebraic variety has finitely many points over an algebraic closure. A matroid rank function $r$ assigns nonnegative integer ranks to subsets; its rank conditional-independence relation for disjoint $A,B,C$ is $r(A\cup C)+r(B\cup C)=r(C)+r(A\cup B\cup C)$.

Why the undefined ambient class matters

The source explicitly makes its conjecture conditional on independence varieties being properly defined, including functional relations. With only ordinary disjoint-set CI equations on finite variables, every product distribution satisfies every such equation. If at least one variable has two levels, fix all other marginals and vary that variable's probability $t$. This gives a nonconstant affine line of normalized complex product tables, and its positive real interval lies in the probability simplex. Hence an ordinary CI locus of this unrestricted kind cannot be zero-dimensional. A support or functional restriction is essential to the intended route. The catalogue's parity title is therefore treated as a test case, not as the general original target.
A fully specified finite calculation.
Take binary $X,Y,Z$ with the fixed functional law $Z=X\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}Y$. In cell order $000,011,101,110$, let the probabilities be $a,b,c,d$, with $a+b+c+d=1$ and all other cells zero. Impose pairwise independence of each two variables. Their three $2$ by $2$ marginal determinants are, up to sign, \[ ab-cd=0,\qquad ac-bd=0,\qquad ad-bc=0. \] For example, the $(X,Y)$ table is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, giving $ad-bc$; the $(X,Z)$ table is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\d&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and the $(Y,Z)$ table is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\d&b\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. These verify every equation directly.

We solve this normalized system over $\mathbb C$, not just over the nonnegative reals. If one coordinate is zero, all pair products of the remaining three vanish; this follows by substitution in the three equations (the equations are symmetric in the four coordinates as a set). Thus at most one coordinate is nonzero, and normalization forces one of the four coordinate vertices. If all are nonzero, dividing the first equation by the second gives $b/c=c/b$, so $b=\epsilon c$ with $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. Equivalently, solving the three equations gives \[ b=\eta a,\qquad c=\xi a,\qquad d=\eta\xi a, \qquad \eta,\xi\in\{1,-1\}. \] To verify completeness, $ad=bc$ first gives $d=bc/a$; substitution in $ab=cd$ gives $c^2=a^2$, and substitution in $ac=bd$ gives $b^2=a^2$. The displayed signs then follow. Normalization is $a(1+\eta)(1+\xi)=1$, so both signs must be $+1$ and $a=1/4$. The normalized complex zero set therefore consists exactly of the four vertices and $(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)$. It is finite, hence zero-dimensional as a set; no assertion about multiplicity or a scheme being reduced is needed. All five points are probability laws.

Exact CI structures at all five points.
At each vertex all three variables are constant, so every conditional independence holds. This is the rank-zero matroid on three elements. At the uniform parity point each singleton has entropy one bit, each pair has entropy two bits, and the triple has entropy two bits. More directly, each pair is independent and uniform; each singleton is a nonconstant function of the other pair; and given any one variable, the other two are related by equality or complementation and are therefore dependent. These exhaust the nontrivial disjoint CI triples on three variables. For the rank function $r(A)=\min(|A|,2)$, the rank-CI difference is zero for two unconditional singletons, one for a singleton against the other pair, and one for the two remaining singletons given the third. Thus the exact ordinary CI structure is that of $U_{2,3}$. The stated functional dependencies also match: any two variables determine the third, and no singleton determines another. Every probability point of this particular finite variety satisfies the proposed matroid conclusion.

Attempted generalization and audit

The computation fixes one deterministic parity map. General functional dependence constraints can have many support components; their normalizations, complex points and probability points must be analyzed separately. Nothing in the five-point calculation proves that every zero-dimensional component for every possible system has a matroid CI structure. The original class of varieties is also not fully defined in the source. The primary AIM Conjecture 23 and its preceding qualification were inspected. The audit checked complex signs, normalization, zero coordinates, all exact CI relations and the difference between finitely many nonnegative points and a genuinely finite complex zero set. All calculations were by hand; no Groebner basis or formal algebra package was run. This is an elementary test case with no novelty claim and no resolution of the general conditional conjecture.
