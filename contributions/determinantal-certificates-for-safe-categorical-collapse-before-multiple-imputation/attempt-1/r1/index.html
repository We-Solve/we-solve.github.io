A specified preservation criterion

The original question does not define quality, a missingness mechanism, or the inferential target. We examine preservation of the coarsened population law, which is a necessary basic property of one saturated imputation strategy. No statement about finite-sample coverage, variance, or all possible estimands is inferred from it. Let $X$ be an observed finite covariate, $Z=g(X)$ a proposed collapse, $Y$ a categorical outcome, and $R$ its response indicator. Assume fine conditional independence $Y\perp R\mid X$ and response probabilities strictly between zero and one. Fix one value $z$ of positive probability, and write, within its fiber, \[ \pi_i=\Pr(X=i\mid Z=z),\quad \rho_i=\Pr(R=1\mid X=i),\quad q_{iy}=\Pr(Y=y\mid X=i). \] Only $i$ with $\pi_i>0$ need be retained. Put $\bar\rho=\sum_i\pi_i\rho_i$, so $0<\bar\rho<1$.
Exact law and imputation bias.
By conditioning and fine-level independence, the population, respondent and missing-case outcome probabilities in the collapsed cell are \[ q_y=\sum_i\pi_iq_{iy},\quad q_y^R=\frac{\sum_i\pi_i\rho_iq_{iy}}{\bar\rho},\quad q_y^M=\frac{\sum_i\pi_i(1-\rho_i)q_{iy}}{1-\bar\rho}. \] If missing outcomes are imputed from the collapsed respondent law $q^R$, the resulting population-limit completed law is $\bar\rho q^R+(1-\bar\rho)q^R=q^R$. Its bias for the coarsened law is \[ q_y^R-q_y= \frac{\sum_i\pi_i\rho_iq_{iy} -(\sum_i\pi_i\rho_i)(\sum_i\pi_iq_{iy})}{\bar\rho} =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi}(\rho_i,q_{iy})}{\bar\rho}. \] Moreover $q^R=q^M$ holds exactly when this covariance vanishes for every $y$, since $q=\bar\rho q^R+(1-\bar\rho)q^M$. Thus fine-level independence does not automatically remain independence after collapsing. Multiplying by the positive denominators gives explicit polynomial equalities in the underlying cell probabilities; positivity of the denominators is part of the argument.
A complete two-level test and a counterexample.
For a fiber consisting of two levels, direct expansion of the covariance reduces it to \[ \pi_1\pi_2(\rho_1-\rho_2)(q_{1y}-q_{2y}). \] Since $\pi_1\pi_2>0$, all the required equalities hold exactly when $\rho_1=\rho_2$ or the two full outcome distributions coincide. This is both necessary and sufficient in the stated two-level setting. It does not assert the same dichotomy for larger fibers, where covariances can cancel. Take $\pi_1=\pi_2=1/2$, $(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(3/4,1/4)$, and binary success probabilities $(q_{11},q_{21})=(3/4,1/4)$. Construct $Y$ and $R$ independently within each value of $X$; this realizes all the assumptions with positive probabilities. Then $\bar\rho=1/2$, $q_1=1/2$, $q_1^R=5/8$ and $q_1^M=3/8$. Collapsed respondent imputation therefore has success-probability bias $1/8$. The explicit numbers follow from $\sum_i\pi_i\rho_iq_{i1}=5/16$. By contrast, retaining $X$ and imputing from its true conditional laws $q_i$ reproduces each stratum law and hence, after collapsing, exactly $\sum_i\pi_iq_i=q$. This comparison concerns the population laws, not estimated finite-sample distributions.

Attempted inference and remaining gap

The failed inference is that reducing categories preserves a fine-level missing-at-random condition merely because it pools more observations. The counterexample disproves it with strictly positive response rates. A useful data-driven choice of collapse would additionally need a target, assumptions identifying $q_i$, and an estimation/uncertainty criterion. The primary AIM Question 20 was checked; these ingredients are not fixed there. The audit checked the conditional weights after selection, respondent versus missing-case laws, the exact two-level quantifier, and probability preservation versus quality of multiple-imputation inference. No numerical experiment was run. The displayed covariance calculation is a known elementary selection effect, not a novel general safe-collapse method or a solution of the original broad question.
