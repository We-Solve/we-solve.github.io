For the stated interacting continuum models, the selected sources bound the fraction occupying the constant orbital away from zero along every thermodynamic sequence. This establishes Bose condensation by the macroscopic-mode criterion, including positive temperatures in the specified dilute regime. The target additionally asks for a pointwise infinite-distance limit of the thermodynamic one-particle density kernel; that limit is not asserted by these occupation-fraction theorems. Bose-Einstein Condensation in Continuum Models

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: For fixed bounded repulsive finite-range potentials, the constant-mode condensate fraction is claimed uniformly positive at sufficiently small density and temperatures up to the density squared; For sufficiently small fixed hard-sphere density, a positive volume-independent temperature is claimed to give a positive thermodynamic constant-mode condensate fraction; Every ground state of a sufficiently dilute fixed-density three-dimensional hard-sphere gas is claimed to place a uniform positive particle fraction in the constant orbital; The hard-sphere ground state is claimed to have the full scaled Bogoliubov momentum law, with depletion $\frac{8}{3\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{\rho a^3}+o(\sqrt{\rho a^3})$; The same leading depletion coefficient is claimed for every fixed bounded nonnegative radial finite-range potential of positive scattering length, uniformly over ground-state density matrices. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Take a bounded measurable radial potential $v:\mathbb R^3\to[0,\infty)$, supported in a ball of finite radius and nonzero on a set of positive measure. On $(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)^3$, use the symmetric $N$-particle Hamiltonian \[ H_{N,L}=-\sum_i\Delta_i+\sum_{i<j}v_L(x_i-x_j),\qquad v_L(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^3}v(x+Lk). \] Let $\gamma^{(1)}_{N,L,T}$ be the canonical state's one-particle matrix, of trace $N$, and $u_0=L^{-3/2}$. The claim provides $\rho_*(v),c_*(v)>0$ such that every $0<\rho<\rho_*$ has a finite $L_0(\rho,v)$, with \[ L\ge L_0,\quad\rho/2\le N/L^3\le2\rho,\quad0\le T\le\rho^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\langle u_0,\gamma^{(1)}_{N,L,T}u_0\rangle}{N}\ge c_*. \] The positive fraction is uniform throughout the indicated dilute density range.
Result 2.
Fix exclusion distance $a>0$. On the three-dimensional torus of side $L$, the hard-sphere Hamiltonian is the symmetric Dirichlet kinetic operator on configurations with every interparticle torus distance greater than $a$. Let $\gamma^{(1)}_{N,L,T}$ be the canonical Gibbs one-particle matrix, of trace $N$, and $u_{0,L}=L^{-3/2}$. The source asserts that some $\rho_*(a)>0$ has the property \[ 0<\rho<\rho_*(a)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \exists T(a,\rho)>0:\quad \liminf_{L\to\infty,\ N/L^3\to\rho} \frac{\langle u_{0,L},\gamma^{(1)}_{N,L,T}u_{0,L}\rangle}{N}>0. \] Here $a,\rho,T$ remain fixed during the thermodynamic limit.
Result 3.
For the three-dimensional torus hard-sphere Bose Hamiltonian, impose Dirichlet exclusion at pair distance $a$, with kinetic energy $-\sum_i\Delta_i$. For a normalized symmetric ground vector $\Psi$, put \[ B(\Psi)=N^{-1}\langle L^{-3/2},\gamma^{(1)}_\Psi L^{-3/2}\rangle, \qquad\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^{(1)}_\Psi=N. \] The claimed universal constants $\varepsilon_0,c_0>0$ give \[ \rho a^3<\varepsilon_0,\quad N_k,L_k\to\infty,\quad N_k/L_k^3\to\rho \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\liminf_k B(\Psi_k)\ge c_0 \] for every choice of ground vectors, allowing complex vectors and degeneracy.
Result 4.
For the three-dimensional periodic hard-sphere Bose gas of exclusion distance $a>0$, let $\mathcal G_{N,L,a}$ be all trace-one states supported on the lowest eigenspace, and $n_{\Gamma,L}(k)$ the occupation of $L^{-3/2}e^{ik\cdot x}$. Define \[ g(t)=\frac{|t|^2+1}{2\sqrt{|t|^4+2|t|^2}}-\frac12,\qquad d\nu_{\mathrm{Bog}}(t)=\frac{(8\pi)^{3/2}}{(2\pi)^3}g(t)\,dt. \] For each bounded continuous real $f$ and $\varepsilon>0$, the source gives $\rho_0(a,f,\varepsilon)>0$. If $0<\rho<\rho_0$, $\eta=\rho a^3$, and $N_j,L_j\to\infty$, $N_j/L_j^3\to\rho$, then \[ \limsup_j\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal G_{N_j,L_j,a}} \left|\frac{\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L_j)\mathbb Z^3,\ k\ne0} f(k/\sqrt{8\pi\rho a})n_{\Gamma,L_j}(k)}{N_j\sqrt\eta} -\int f\,d\nu_{\mathrm{Bog}}\right|\le\varepsilon. \] The measure has total mass $8/(3\sqrt\pi)$.
Result 5.
Let $v\ge0$ be bounded, measurable, radial, compactly supported, and nonzero on a positive-measure set. Its scattering length $a_v>0$ is determined by $(-\Delta+v/2)f=0$, with $f(x)=1-a_v/|x|$ outside the support. For the periodic symmetric Hamiltonian $-\sum_i\Delta_i+\sum_{i<j}v_L(x_i-x_j)$, let $\mathcal G^v_{N,L}$ contain all ground-space trace-one states and $B_\Gamma=N^{-1}\langle L^{-3/2},\gamma^{(1)}_\Gamma L^{-3/2}\rangle$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, some $\rho_0(v,\varepsilon)>0$ satisfies \[ 0<\rho<\rho_0,\ N_k,L_k\to\infty,\ N_k/L_k^3\to\rho \ \Longrightarrow\ \limsup_k\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal G^v_{N_k,L_k}} \left|\frac{1-B_\Gamma}{\sqrt{\rho a_v^3}}-\frac8{3\sqrt\pi}\right|\le\varepsilon. \] Volume and particle number tend to infinity while density remains fixed.

Outline of the argument

Energy and free-energy estimates control cell occupancy and isolation. A short imaginary-time slab supplies joint trajectory bounds; defective-cell handling and insertion comparisons connect distant cells and preserve a density-uniform coherence lower bound. Scaled kinetic and particle-count bounds localize the canonical state. Thermal-slab trajectory estimates permit moving paths between adjacent cells; environment exposure retains movable groups and turns their long-range coherence into the condensate lower bound.

Energy and spatial bounds produce stationary hard-core path samples. Neighbor-cell moves, retained movable groups, and routes through sparse obstacles compare phase coherence across the volume, giving a constant-mode lower bound also for complex ground states. Local energy estimates keep excitations instead of discarding them. One-body covariance and short-trajectory certificates are compared through smoothed deletion measures, while random encounter routes connect local momentum data to the thermodynamic occupation measure before the dilute limit.

Local density tails and ground-state path bounds control occupations at the relevant scattering scale. Spatial comparison transports these local estimates to the constant orbital, identifying every thermodynamic accumulation value before density tends to zero.
