Erdős Problem #43 (Round 5)

1) ROUND-5 OBJECTIVE

Path chosen: (C) --- obstruction/correction, with full proof of the corrected asymptotic statement.

Round 4 already produced an explicit infinite-family disproof of the second (equal-size) question, but only on an infinite subsequence and without extracting the full asymptotic consequence for all $N$. The most promising Round 5 direction is therefore to turn that disproof into a precise corrected theorem.

The new goal is to prove that the correct equal-size asymptotic is \[ M(N):=\max\left\{m:\exists\text{ Sidon }A,B\subset[N],\ |A|=|B|=m,\ (A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}\right\} =\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N, \] and then to extend this to the unrestricted extremal quantity \[ g(N):=\max\left\{\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}: A,B\subset[N]\ \text{Sidon},\ (A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}\right\}. \] This will show that the second question is false in the strongest possible asymptotic sense, and that the first question is asymptotically correct up to an $o(N)$ error, leaving only the fine $O(1)$ scale unresolved.

2) Round-4 FOUNDATION USED

I explicitly use the following Round 4 results.

  • Round 4 parity-splitting construction. For every odd prime power $q$, with \[ N_q:=\frac{q^2-1}{2}, \] there exist Sidon sets $A_q,B_q\subset[N_q]$ with $|A_q|=|B_q|=:m_q$, $(A_q-A_q)\cap(B_q-B_q)=\{0\}$, and \[ m_q\ge \frac{q-\sqrt{2q-3}}{2} = \frac q2-O(\sqrt q). \]
  • Round 3 corrected convolution inequality. If $A,B\subset[N]$ are Sidon and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$, then for every integer $H\ge 1$, \[ \frac{(|A|^2+|B|^2)H^2}{N+H}\le (|A|+|B|)H+H^2. \]
  • Round 4 status correction. The second question is false; what remained open after Round 4 was to identify the correct asymptotic statement and to advance the first question beyond finite computations.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-5)

There are three genuinely new ingredients in this round.

  • A strengthened upper bound that extends the Round 4 equal-size estimate to arbitrary Sidon pairs: \[ g(N)\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}). \]
  • A monotonicity + prime-number-theorem upgrade of the Round 4 prime-power construction, which promotes the lower bound from an infinite subsequence to all sufficiently large $N$.
  • Combining these yields a full corrected asymptotic theorem: \[ M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N, \qquad D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N):=2\binom{M(N)}2=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N, \] and also \[ g(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N=\binom{f(N)}2+o(N). \]

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-5)

Exact gap left after Round 4. Round 4 settled the equal-size question only on an infinite subsequence and did not extract a theorem for the unrestricted extremal function $g(N)$. The remaining tasks are:

  1. prove a nontrivial upper bound for all feasible pairs $(A,B)$, not just the equal-size case;
  2. upgrade the Round 4 lower construction from the prime-power subsequence $N=(q^2-1)/2$ to all large $N$;
  3. deduce the exact asymptotic scale of both $M(N)$ and $g(N)$.

Why this overcomes the earlier obstacle. The Round 3 corrected inequality is already strong enough to force an $N/2+O(N^{3/4})$ upper bound once one controls $|A|+|B|$. Meanwhile the Round 4 parity-splitting construction already gives near-extremizers on a dense-enough subsequence; the prime number theorem allows us to pull those constructions down to arbitrary large $N$ by monotonicity.

5) WORK (ROUND-5)

5.1 Definitions and monotonicity

Define \[ M(N):=\max\left\{m:\exists\text{ Sidon }A,B\subset[N],\ |A|=|B|=m,\ (A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}\right\}, \] and \[ g(N):=\max\left\{\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}: A,B\subset[N]\ \text{Sidon},\ (A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}\right\}. \] Both $M(N)$ and $g(N)$ are nondecreasing in $N$, because any feasible pair inside $[N']$ is also feasible inside $[N]$ whenever $N'\le N$.

For later use, note that if $S\subset[N]$ is Sidon and $|S|=s$, then its positive differences are distinct elements of $\{1,\dots,N-1\}$, so \[ \binom{s}{2}\le N-1. \] Hence every Sidon subset of $[N]$ has size \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-69-64-6f-6e-2d-73-69-7a-65-2d-74-72-69-76-69-61-6c} s\le \frac{1+\sqrt{8N-7}}{2}=\sqrt{2N}+O(1). \end{equation}

5.2 A new upper bound for arbitrary pairs

Proposition: Let $A,B\subset[N]$ be Sidon sets with $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$. Write \[ a:=|A|,\qquad b:=|B|,\qquad S:=a^2+b^2,\qquad T:=a+b. \] Then \[ S\le N+O(N^{3/4}). \] Consequently, \[ \binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}), \] and therefore \[ g(N)\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}). \]
Proof: We use the Round 3 corrected convolution inequality: \[ \frac{SH^2}{N+H}\le TH+H^2 \qquad (H\ge 1,\ H\in\mathbb Z). \] Rearranging gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-53-2d-62-61-73-69-63} S\le \frac{(T+H)(N+H)}{H}=N+T+H+\frac{TN}{H}. \end{equation}

By (eq:sidon-size-trivial), each of $a,b$ is $O(\sqrt N)$, hence \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-54-2d-75-70-70-65-72} T=O(\sqrt N). \end{equation} If $T=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose \[ H:=\left\lceil\sqrt{TN}\right\rceil. \] Then \[ H\le \sqrt{TN}+1, \qquad \frac{TN}{H}\le \sqrt{TN}. \] Substituting into (eq:S-basic), \[ S\le N+T+2\sqrt{TN}+1. \] Using (eq:T-upper), we have $T=O(\sqrt N)$ and therefore $\sqrt{TN}=O(N^{3/4})$. Hence \[ S\le N+O(N^{3/4}). \] Finally, \[ \binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2} =\frac{a(a-1)+b(b-1)}{2} =\frac{S-T}{2} \le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}), \] proving the proposition. ∎

Corollary: For the equal-size extremal function one has \[ M(N)\le \frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt N+O(N^{1/4}). \]
Proof: If $|A|=|B|=m$, then Proposition Proposition gives \[ 2m^2\le N+O(N^{3/4}), \] whence \[ m\le \frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt N+O(N^{1/4}). \square \] ∎

5.3 Upgrading the Round 4 lower bound to all large $N$

The Round 4 construction gives large equal-size pairs only for $N_q=(q^2-1)/2$ with $q$ an odd prime power. To pass to arbitrary $N$, we use monotonicity together with the prime number theorem.

Lemma: For every real $x\to\infty$, there exists an odd prime $q\le x$ such that \[ q=(1-o(1))x. \]
Proof: Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. By the prime number theorem, \[ \pi(x)-\pi((1-\varepsilon)x)\sim \frac{\varepsilon x}{\log x}\to\infty \qquad (x\to\infty). \] Therefore, for all sufficiently large $x$, the interval $[(1-\varepsilon)x,x]$ contains a prime. Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, there exists a prime $q\le x$ with $q=(1-o(1))x$. For large $x$ this prime is automatically odd. ∎
Proposition: One has \[ M(N)\ge \left(\frac1{\sqrt2}-o(1)\right)\sqrt N. \] In particular, \[ M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N. \]
Proof: Set \[ x:=\sqrt{2N+1}. \] By Lemma Lemma, choose an odd prime $q\le x$ such that $q=(1-o(1))x$. Since every prime is a prime power, we may apply the Round 4 parity-splitting construction at this $q$. Let \[ N_q:=\frac{q^2-1}{2}\le \frac{x^2-1}{2}=N. \] Round 4 then supplies Sidon sets $A_q,B_q\subset[N_q]$ with \[ |A_q|=|B_q|=:m_q\ge \frac{q-\sqrt{2q-3}}{2} = \frac q2-O(\sqrt q). \] By monotonicity of $M$, \[ M(N)\ge M(N_q)\ge m_q. \] Because $q=(1-o(1))x=(1-o(1))\sqrt{2N+1}$ and $\sqrt q=o(\sqrt N)$, we conclude that \[ M(N)\ge \frac q2-o(\sqrt N) =\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}-o(1)\right)\sqrt N. \] Combining this with Corollary Corollary yields \[ M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N. \] ∎

5.4 The corrected asymptotic theorem

Theorem (Equal-size case): Let \[ D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N):=2\binom{M(N)}{2}=M(N)(M(N)-1). \] Then \[ D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] Equivalently, the sharp asymptotic constant in the equal-size problem is $1/\sqrt2$, and there is no fixed positive gap below it.
Proof: By Proposition Proposition, \[ M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N. \] Therefore \[ D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N)=M(N)(M(N)-1)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] This proves the claim. ∎
Theorem (Unrestricted case): One has \[ g(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] Since $f(N)\sim \sqrt N$, this is equivalent to \[ g(N)=\binom{f(N)}{2}+o(N). \]
Proof: The lower bound follows from the equal-size case: \[ g(N)\ge D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] The upper bound is Proposition Proposition, namely \[ g(N)\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4})=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] Hence \[ g(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] Finally, the classical asymptotic $f(N)\sim \sqrt N$ implies \[ \binom{f(N)}2=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N, \] so indeed \[ g(N)=\binom{f(N)}2+o(N). \square \] ∎

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

(i) Did Round 5 improperly reprove Round 4 material?
No. The Round 4 parity-splitting construction is used as a black box in Proposition Proposition. The only proof repeated from earlier rounds is the convolution-based upper estimate, and there it is strengthened from the equal-size setting to arbitrary pairs, which is explicitly permitted.
(ii) Hidden dependence on prime powers?
There is none. Proposition Proposition uses primes, and every prime is a prime power, so the Round 4 construction applies unchanged.
(iii) Monotonicity check.
If $A,B\subset[N_q]$ are feasible, then $A,B\subset[N]$ for every $N\ge N_q$. Thus both $M(N)$ and $g(N)$ are indeed nondecreasing. No rescaling or translation is required.
(iv) Prime-number-theorem quantifiers.
Lemma Lemma is uniform in the needed sense: for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $x$, a prime exists in $[(1-\varepsilon)x,x]$. This is exactly what is needed to make the multiplicative loss $o(1)$.
(v) Does Proposition Proposition really give an $o(N)$ error?
Yes. The proof yields $g(N)\le N/2+O(N^{3/4})$, and $N^{3/4}=o(N)$. Hence Theorem Theorem (Unrestricted case) is rigorous.
(vi) What remains open after Round 5?
The original first question asked for the much sharper estimate \[ g(N)\le \binom{f(N)}2+O(1). \] Round 5 proves only \[ g(N)=\binom{f(N)}2+o(N), \] so the fine error term remains unresolved. The second question, however, is fully settled negatively, and its corrected asymptotic form is now proved.

7) FINAL

FULL SOLUTION --- FULL PROOF.

The second question is false, and the corrected asymptotic statement is now proved in full: \[ M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N, \qquad D_{\mathrm{eq}}(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N. \] Equivalently, in the equal-size case the sharp asymptotic constant is exactly $1/\sqrt2$, so no fixed positive gap $c>0$ can occur.

Moreover, for the unrestricted extremal quantity, \[ g(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N=\binom{f(N)}2+o(N). \] Thus the asymptotic form of the first question is true, but the original $O(1)$ refinement remains open.
