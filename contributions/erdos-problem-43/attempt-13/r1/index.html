Erdős Problem #43 (Round 11)

Round 10 exhausted the translation-invariant support-size quadratic-form route, but it still did not isolate the combinatorial core of the first question. In this round I attempted a full proof or a counterexample for \[ \binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}\le \binom{f(N)}{2}+O(1), \] for Sidon sets $A,B\subset [N]$ with $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$.

I do not have a gap-free proof or disproof of this first question. The strict new progress in this round is an exact reformulation of the problem as a Sidon problem in two residue classes modulo $3$, together with an exact Fourier identity at the cube roots of unity. This isolates the remaining difficulty more sharply than any previous round: after Round 10's kernel barriers, the unresolved issue is now exactly a fixed-frequency extremal problem for Sidon sets lying in two congruence classes.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-11)

There are two genuinely new ingredients in this round.

(i) Minimal mod-$3$ Sidon embedding.
The pair condition is exactly equivalent to the statement that \[ C:=3A\cup (3B+1) \] is a Sidon set. Thus Problem 43 can be reformulated as a problem about Sidon subsets of $[3N+1]$ supported on the two residue classes $0,1\pmod 3$.
(ii) Exact objective formula at the cube roots of unity.
If $C\subset [3N+1]$ is such a Sidon set, write \[ a:=|C\cap 3\mathbb Z|, \qquad b:=|C\cap (3\mathbb Z+1)|, \qquad s:=|C|=a+b, \qquad \omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}. \] Then \[ \binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2} = \#\{d>0:d\in C-C,\ 3\mid d\} = \frac{s^2-3s+2\left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2}{6}. \] So the first question is equivalent to bounding a single Fourier coefficient at frequency $1/3$ for Sidon sets occupying two residue classes mod $3$.

This is strictly new: previous rounds ruled out averaged kernel methods, but they did not identify this exact residue-class/Fourier reformulation.

5) WORK (ROUND-11)

Throughout this section, $[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$.

5.1 The exact mod-$3$ embedding theorem

Theorem (Minimal residue-class embedding): Let $A,B\subset \mathbb Z$ be finite sets. The following are equivalent:
  1. $A$ and $B$ are Sidon and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$.
  2. The set \[ C:=3A\cup(3B+1) \] is Sidon.
More generally, the same equivalence holds with $3$ replaced by any integer $m\ge 3$ and $3B+1$ replaced by $mB+1$.
Proof: Assume first that $A$ and $B$ are Sidon and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$. By the Round 2 cross-sum injectivity lemma, the map $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$ on $A\times B$ is injective.

We show that $C=3A\cup(3B+1)$ is Sidon. Suppose \[ x_1+x_2=x_3+x_4 \qquad (x_i\in C). \] Reducing modulo $3$, the unordered pair-types are distinguished:

  • two elements from $3A$ give sum $0\pmod 3$;
  • one element from $3A$ and one from $3B+1$ give sum $1\pmod 3$;
  • two elements from $3B+1$ give sum $2\pmod 3$.
Hence the two representations have the same type.

If both are of type $3A+3A$, then dividing by $3$ gives an equality of two sums in $A+A$, and Sidonicity of $A$ forces the same unordered pair. The type $3B+1+3B+1$ is identical.

If both are of mixed type, we have \[ 3a_1+(3b_1+1)=3a_2+(3b_2+1), \] so $a_1+b_1=a_2+b_2$. Since $A+B$ is injective, $(a_1,b_1)=(a_2,b_2)$. Thus the mixed representation is unique as well. Therefore $C$ is Sidon.

Conversely, assume that $C=3A\cup(3B+1)$ is Sidon.

First, $A$ is Sidon: if $a_1+a_2=a_3+a_4$, then \[ 3a_1+3a_2=3a_3+3a_4, \] and since $C$ is Sidon, the two unordered pairs in $3A$ are equal; dividing by $3$ gives $\{a_1,a_2\}=\{a_3,a_4\}$. The same argument shows that $B$ is Sidon.

Next, $A+B$ is injective. If $a_1+b_1=a_2+b_2$, then \[ 3a_1+(3b_1+1)=3a_2+(3b_2+1). \] Since $C$ is Sidon and each side is a mixed representation (one residue $0\pmod 3$, one residue $1\pmod 3$), the two ordered pairs coincide; hence $a_1=a_2$ and $b_1=b_2$.

Finally, if $d\in (A-A)\cap(B-B)$, say $d=a_1-a_2=b_2-b_1$, then \[ a_1+b_1=a_2+b_2. \] Injectivity of $A+B$ implies $a_1=a_2$ and $b_1=b_2$, so $d=0$. Thus $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$.

The proof for $mA\cup(mB+1)$ with any $m\ge 3$ is identical, since the sum-types have residues $0,1,2\pmod m$ and are therefore distinct. ∎

Remark: The modulus $3$ is minimal. For $m=2$, the same-colour sum-types $0+0$ and $1+1$ are both $0\pmod 2$, so the clean residue separation fails.

5.2 Exact reformulation of the first question

For a finite set $C\subset \mathbb Z$, define \[ D_0(C):=\#\{d>0:d\in C-C,\ 3\mid d\}. \]

Corollary (Problem 43 as a mod-$3$ Sidon problem): The first question of Problem 43 is equivalent to the following statement:
If $C\subset [3N+1]$ is Sidon and $C\subset 3\mathbb Z\cup (3\mathbb Z+1)$, then \[ D_0(C)\le \binom{f(N)}{2}+O(1). \]
Moreover, if $C=3A\cup(3B+1)$, then \[ D_0(C)=\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}. \]
Proof: If $(A,B)$ is feasible, Theorem Theorem (Minimal residue-class embedding) produces a Sidon set $C=3A\cup(3B+1)\subset [3N+1]$.

A positive difference of $C$ is divisible by $3$ if and only if it comes from two elements of the same residue class mod $3$, i.e. either from two elements of $3A$ or from two elements of $3B+1$. Since $A$ and $B$ are Sidon, these positive differences are all distinct within each class, and because the original difference sets are disjoint, they are distinct across the two classes as well. Thus \[ D_0(C)=\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}. \]

Conversely, if $C\subset [3N+1]$ is Sidon and lies in $3\mathbb Z\cup(3\mathbb Z+1)$, define \[ A:=\{a:3a\in C\}, \qquad B:=\{b:3b+1\in C\}. \] Then $C=3A\cup(3B+1)$, so Theorem Theorem (Minimal residue-class embedding) shows that $(A,B)$ is feasible, and the same counting argument gives \[ D_0(C)=\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}. \] This is exactly the original optimisation problem. ∎

5.3 Fourier identity at the cube roots of unity

Let $\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}$, so $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$ and \[ \Re(\omega)=-\frac12. \]

Proposition (Exact Fourier formula): Let $C\subset [3N+1]$ be Sidon and assume $C\subset 3\mathbb Z\cup(3\mathbb Z+1)$. Write \[ a:=|C\cap 3\mathbb Z|, \qquad b:=|C\cap (3\mathbb Z+1)|, \qquad s:=|C|=a+b. \] Then \[ \sum_{c\in C}\omega^c = a+\omega b, \qquad \left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2 = a^2+b^2-ab. \] Consequently, \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-66-6f-75-72-69-65-72-44} D_0(C)=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2} =\frac{s^2-3s+2\left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2}{6}. \end{equation}
Proof: Every element of $C\cap 3\mathbb Z$ contributes $1$ to $\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c$, and every element of $C\cap(3\mathbb Z+1)$ contributes $\omega$. Hence \[ \sum_{c\in C}\omega^c=a+\omega b. \] Taking squared modulus and using $\omega+\overline\omega=-1$ gives \[ \left|a+\omega b\right|^2 =a^2+b^2+ab(\omega+\overline\omega) =a^2+b^2-ab. \]

Let \[ M:=\left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2. \] Then \[ M=a^2+b^2-ab, \qquad s^2=(a+b)^2=a^2+b^2+2ab. \] Subtracting yields \[ 3(a^2+b^2)=s^2+2M. \] Therefore \[ a^2+b^2=\frac{s^2+2M}{3}. \] Finally, \[ D_0(C)=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2}=\frac{a^2+b^2-a-b}{2} =\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{s^2+2M}{3}-s\right) =\frac{s^2-3s+2M}{6}, \] which is exactly (eq:fourierD). ∎

Corollary (Equal-size condition as an exact Fourier constraint): With notation as above, \[ a=b \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|=\frac{|C|}{2}. \]
Proof: By Proposition Proposition (Exact Fourier formula), \[ \left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2=a^2+b^2-ab. \] Also, \[ \frac{|C|^2}{4}=\frac{(a+b)^2}{4}. \] Subtracting, \[ a^2+b^2-ab-\frac{(a+b)^2}{4} =\frac{3(a-b)^2}{4}. \] Thus \[ \left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2=\frac{|C|^2}{4} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=b. \] Taking square roots proves the claim. ∎

5.4 The remaining open inequality, rewritten exactly

By Corollary cor:mod3reform and Proposition Proposition (Exact Fourier formula), the first question of Problem 43 is equivalent to the statement that for every Sidon set $C\subset [3N+1]$ contained in $3\mathbb Z\cup(3\mathbb Z+1)$, \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-72-65-77-72-69-74-74-65-6e-2d-6f-70-65-6e} \frac{|C|^2-3|C|+2\left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|^2}{6} \le \binom{f(N)}{2}+O(1). \end{equation}

This is now the exact unresolved core left after Round 10. The point is that previous barrier theorems only controlled support size and total mass via averaged kernels, whereas (eq:rewritten-open) shows that the decisive statistic is a single rigid Fourier coefficient at frequency $1/3$, together with the Sidon constraint and the restriction to two residue classes.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

(i) The naive mod-$2$ embedding really fails.
Take the small feasible pair \[ A=\{1,2,5\}, \qquad B=\{1,3,8\}. \] Then $A$ and $B$ are Sidon and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$, but \[ (2A-1)\cup 2B = \{1,2,3,6,9,16\} \] is not Sidon, since \[ 1+3=2+2. \] So parity is the wrong ambient modulus; the mod-$3$ embedding theorem above is genuinely new and genuinely minimal.
(ii) No hidden use of the unresolved first question.
Theorem Theorem (Minimal residue-class embedding), Corollary cor:mod3reform, and Proposition Proposition (Exact Fourier formula) are all exact elementary equivalences and identities. None of them assumes the desired $O(1)$ bound.
(iii) Why this is beyond Round 10.
Round 10 classified support-length kernel lower bounds. The new mod-$3$ reformulation shows that even after those barriers, there remains a very specific unresolved statistic, namely \[ \left|\sum_{c\in C}\omega^c\right|, \] for Sidon sets $C$ in two residue classes. This isolates the obstruction much more sharply than "some stronger kernel is needed."
(iv) What a future proof or disproof would now have to do.
A proof of the first question must control the fixed-frequency quantity in (eq:rewritten-open) for Sidon sets in two residue classes. A disproof would need an infinite family of such sets for which the left-hand side of (eq:rewritten-open) exceeds $\binom{f(N)}{2}$ by an unbounded amount. I do not have either in this round.

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

I cannot honestly upgrade Problem 43 to a full 100% resolution in this round: the first question remains open in my work here. The strict new progress beyond Round 10 is an exact and minimal reformulation:

  • feasible pairs $(A,B)$ are exactly Sidon sets of the form $C=3A\cup(3B+1)$;
  • the target quantity is exactly the number of positive $0\pmod 3$ differences of $C$;
  • equivalently, \[ \binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2} = \frac{|C|^2-3|C|+2\left|\sum_{c\in C}e^{2\pi i c/3}\right|^2}{6}. \]
This isolates the remaining unresolved core as a fixed-frequency extremal problem for Sidon sets in two residue classes modulo $3$.
