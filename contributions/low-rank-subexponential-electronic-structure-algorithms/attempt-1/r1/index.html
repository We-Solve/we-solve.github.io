Research attempt: A tensor-rank obstruction and the importance of representation

An exact rank requirement across a fixed cut
For an integer $m\geq1$, on $2m$ two-state factors consider \[ \Psi_m=2^{-m/2}\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^m}|b\rangle\otimes|b\rangle. \] Its coefficient matrix across the first-$m$/last-$m$ cut is $M=I_D/\sqrt D$, where $D=2^m$, and its squared norm is one. For an integer $0\leq r\leq D$, any approximation whose matrix across this cut has rank at most $r$ satisfies \[ \lVert M-B\rVert_F^2\geq1-r/D. \tag{1} \] Indeed $\ker B$ has dimension at least $D-r$. Choose that many orthonormal kernel vectors and extend to an orthonormal basis. The Frobenius norm squared is the sum of squared images on this basis; on each kernel vector $(M-B)u=Mu$ has squared norm $1/D$. This proves $1$, with equality achieved by retaining any $r$ diagonal entries of $M$. Thus norm error at most $\varepsilon<1$ requires $r\geq(1-\varepsilon^2)2^m$.

Any tensor train whose central bond has dimension $r$ yields a matrix of rank at most $r$ across this cut: contracting all tensors to the left and right expresses the matrix as a sum of $r$ column-times-row products. Consequently this ordering forces an exponential central bond for fixed error. The argument does not assume a particular optimization algorithm for finding the approximation.

The state has a compact, exactly solvable Hamiltonian
Let $\Phi=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and define \[ H=\sum_{i=1}^m\left(I-|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|\right)_{i,m+i}. \] The terms act on disjoint pairs, are commuting nonnegative projectors, and each pair has a one-dimensional zero eigenspace. In a tensor product of their eigenbases, every energy is the number of pairs outside that zero eigenspace. Thus $\Psi_m$ is the unique ground state and the first excited energy is one. The Hamiltonian has $m$ explicitly specified two-factor terms, and its ground energy and factorized pair state are known without exponential computation.

If the tensor factors are reordered as $(1,m+1),(2,m+2),\ldots$, then every cut either separates whole pairs, where rank is one, or cuts one pair, where rank is two. A tensor train of bond dimension at most two follows directly by writing each Bell pair as its two-term expansion and multiplying the independent pair factors. Therefore the exponential rank obstruction depends on the chosen ordering and cut, not on an intrinsic computational difficulty of this example.

What this does and does not say about the source route
This auxiliary Hamiltonian is not asserted to be a fermionic Coulomb electronic Hamiltonian in the source's class. Its role is to disprove a blanket inference from a short two-body Hamiltonian description to small tensor rank in every fixed ordering. Conversely its exact solvability disproves the inference from exponential rank in one format to exponential runtime for every method. A general electronic-structure result would need a rank bound in a specified admissible representation, a way to find that representation, controlled contraction costs, and a relation between tensor error and the requested energy or wavefunction accuracy. None follows from the word "low-rank" alone.
