FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #370; explicit elementary construction, 2026-09-24. Writing $P(a)$ for the largest prime factor of $a>1$, prove that infinitely many positive integers $n$ satisfy \[ P(n)<\sqrt n,\qquad P(n+1)<\sqrt{n+1}. \]

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

The current problem page records an affirmative resolution and points out the simple square-neighbour construction. The inequalities are strict; merely taking $n+1$ to be the square of a prime would fail. The following progression of composite square roots avoids that issue.

OBJECTIVE AND ATTACK PLAN

Factor $m^2-1$ into $m-1$ and $m+1$. Make $m$ composite and give $m+1$ a small fixed divisor so that neither factor can contribute a prime as large as $\sqrt{m^2-1}$.

WORK

For any integer $t\geq1$, put \[ m=6t+2,\qquad n=m^2-1. \] Then $m\geq8$ is even, so every prime factor of $m$ is at most $m/2$: this follows by writing $m=2(m/2)$, with the factor $2$ also at most $m/2$. Therefore \[ P(n+1)=P(m^2)=P(m)\leq m/2<m=\sqrt{n+1}. \tag{1} \] On the other hand, \[ n=(m-1)(m+1),\qquad m+1=3(2t+1). \] The two factors of $m+1$ are at most $(m+1)/3$, since $2t+1\geq3$. Hence \[ P(m+1)\leq(m+1)/3\leq m-1, \qquad P(m-1)\leq m-1. \] It follows that \[ P(n)=\max(P(m-1),P(m+1))\leq m-1<\sqrt{m^2-1}, \tag{2} \] because $(m-1)^2<m^2-1$ for $m>1$. Equations $1$--$2$ prove both required inequalities. The constructed $n$ increase strictly with $t$, proving infinitude.

The construction also gives a quantitative lower bound. For $X\geq63$, every \[ 1\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{X+1}-2}{6}\right\rfloor \] gives a distinct valid $n\leq X$. Thus there are at least $\sqrt X/6+O(1)$ such integers. For example $t=1$ gives $n=63$, with $P(63)=7<\sqrt{63}$ and $P(64)=2<8$.

RESULT AND LIMITATIONS

Attempt status: solved. Both strict inequalities and infinitely many distinct examples are proved without an external analytic theorem. The counting bound is a lower bound for this explicit family, not an asymptotic count of all solutions. This elementary reconstruction does not claim novelty.
