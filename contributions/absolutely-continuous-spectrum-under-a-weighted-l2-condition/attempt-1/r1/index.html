Absolutely Continuous Spectrum Under a Weighted L2 Condition

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
The operator $-\Delta+V$ is a continuum Schrödinger operator on $L^2(\mathbb R^\nu)$, realized self-adjointly for a real potential under the source’s standing operator-domain assumptions. The displayed integral is a weighted square-integrability condition, with weight $|x|^{1-\nu}$. The absolutely continuous part is characterized by spectral measures having Lebesgue densities; its multiplicity is the number of independent spectral channels almost everywhere.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Let $V$ be a function on $\mathbb R^\nu$, $\nu\geq2$, satisfying $\int |x|^{-\nu+1}|V(x)|^2\,d^\nu x<\infty$. Prove that $-\Delta+V$ has absolutely continuous spectrum of infinite multiplicity on $[0,\infty)$.
Scope and source qualification.
The brief catalogue statement does not specify local regularity or the self-adjoint realization. Those operator-domain hypotheses require consultation of the primary source, and are not invented here. Multiplicity on an interval is understood almost everywhere in spectral theory.

Short English statement

Determine whether the stated weighted square-integrability condition forces infinitely many absolutely continuous spectral channels at nonnegative energies.

The free infinite-multiplicity benchmark.
For $V=0$, the Fourier transform turns $-\Delta$ into multiplication by $|\xi|^2$. In polar coordinates $\xi=r\theta$, followed by $E=r^2$, \[L^2(\mathbb R^\nu)\simeq L^2\left((0,\infty),\tfrac12E^{(\nu-2)/2}dE;L^2(S^{\nu-1})\right). \] Multiplication by the square root of the strictly positive density makes the base measure ordinary Lebesgue measure on $(0,\infty)$. For $\nu\ge2$, the angular Hilbert space is infinite dimensional. The free operator therefore has absolutely continuous spectrum with infinite multiplicity almost everywhere on the positive axis. The single endpoint zero has no bearing on an almost-everywhere multiplicity assertion.
What the proposed hypothesis actually controls.
The weighted condition becomes exactly \[\int_0^\infty\int_{S^{\nu-1}}|V(r\theta)|^2\,d\theta\,dr<\infty.\] It is a radial $L^2$ average of angular $L^2$ norms, not a uniform angular decay bound. To see this concretely, fix a smooth bounded radial function $a$ supported in $1<r<2$, and let $b_M$ be a smooth angular bump of height $M$ supported on a patch of area at most $M^{-4}$. Then $V_M(r\theta)=a(r)b_M(\theta)$ has weighted square norm at most $\|a\|_2^2M^{-2}$, while its supremum grows like $M$. Each individual example is a bounded smooth compactly supported potential, so no operator-domain pathology is needed for this warning.
Failed perturbative step.
A proof based on smallness of the multiplication-operator norm cannot follow from the stated weighted norm: the bump family explicitly contradicts such a bound. Transferring the free angular channels to the perturbed operator requires a scattering or spectral estimate controlled by the weighted norm itself. This estimate is not proved here. The supplied warning about self-adjoint realization is retained, and the free case and bump test do not settle the general continuum assertion.
