Facial Structure of Classical and Quantum Correlation Sets

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Fix finite numbers of parties, measurement settings, outcomes, and a distribution of joint settings. A correlation is the array $p(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf x)$ of conditional outcome probabilities. The local polytope $P$ is the convex hull of deterministic local response arrays, equivalently the arrays obtained using a shared classical hidden variable. The quantum set $Q$ consists of arrays $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\bigotimes_i M^{(i)}_{a_i\mid x_i})$ from a shared quantum state and local measurements in the report's framework. The nonsignalling polytope $R$ consists of nonnegative normalized arrays whose marginal at any group of parties is independent of the other parties' settings. A face is the intersection with a supporting hyperplane. The report calls a local face a Bell inequality when its supporting constraint admits a quantum violation.
Source scope and qualifications.
Use the report's meaning of a quantum-violable Bell face; merely calling every supporting local inequality a Bell inequality would make the first question tautological. Minimal local dimensions, pure-state realizations, and rank-one projectors are bundled requirements. The report's historical treatment of Q must not be silently replaced by a modern closure or commuting-operator correlation set.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Very little is known about the structure of $P$ and $Q$. Are all faces of $P$ that are not contained in faces of $R$ Bell inequalities? Is the boundary of $Q$ covered by probability distributions arising from projective measurements on pure states in Hilbert spaces of minimal dimension, with all projectors having rank one?

The smallest nontrivial face test.
Restrict to two settings and two outcomes $\pm1$ per party and write $C=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}$. For deterministic local outputs $a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1\in\{\pm1\}$, \[ C=a_0(b_0+b_1)+a_1(b_0-b_1)\in\{-2,2\}. \] Convex mixtures thus satisfy $C\leq2$. Nonsignalling arrays can instead have uniform single-party marginals and correlation signs $E_{xy}=(-1)^{xy}$; explicitly put probability $1/2$ on each output pair satisfying $ab=(-1)^{xy}$. This gives $C=4$, so the supporting constraint is not a supporting constraint of the nonsignalling set.
A pure rank-one boundary realization.
On $|\Phi\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$, take $A_0=Z,A_1=X,B_0=(Z+X)/\sqrt2,B_1=(Z-X)/\sqrt2$. The four expectations give $C=2\sqrt2$. Each measurement has rank-one spectral projections. This is a quantum boundary point: for arbitrary self-adjoint involutions the Bell operator $T=A_0\otimes(B_0+B_1)+A_1\otimes(B_0-B_1)$ satisfies \[ T^2=4I-[A_0,A_1]\otimes[B_0,B_1],\qquad \|T\|\leq2\sqrt2, \] since each commutator has norm at most two. The same bound for binary contractions follows by adjoining a two-dimensional dilation with involution block matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&\sqrt{I-A^2}\\\sqrt{I-A^2}&-A\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. A one-dimensional local Hilbert space cannot violate the local bound, so local dimension two is minimal for this example.
