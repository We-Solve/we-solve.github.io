Directionwise normalized cylinder-covering bound

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 100; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that a regular tetrahedron has a finite covering by triangular-base cylinders whose total perpendicular area is strictly less than half its minimum projection area; In manuscript 2 (family 100; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that radial segment sweeps have finite triangular-base cylinder realizations whose area converges to the weighted parameter cost, including a tetrahedral cover below the half-area bound; In manuscript 3 (family 100; 2026-09-27), the asserted result is that varying the axes of the standard two-cylinder tetrahedral cover yields a finite cover below the half-area threshold, also violating the directionwise normalized bound.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Put $H=\sqrt2$, $K=\{(x,y,Hs):0\le s\le1,\ |x|\le1-s,\ |y|\le s\}$ and $d(q)=(q^2+q^4)/16$; this tetrahedron has least projection area $H$. For small $\tau>0$, choose any finite partition $-1=q_0<\cdots<q_m=1$, coefficients $\alpha_j,\beta_j$ and positive continuous caps $R_j$ on $I_j=[q_{j-1},q_j]$. Require continuity of the piecewise affine $L(q)=\alpha_j-q\beta_j$ and $L(\pm1)=0$, and uniform estimates \[ \beta_j=q/2+O(\tau^2),\quad\alpha_j=(1+q^2)/4+O(\tau^2),\quad R_j(q)=1/2+\tau^2(d(q)+\eta)+O(\tau^4),\quad0<\eta<1/480. \] The constants are independent of $\tau,j,q$. Define $P_j^-=\{(0,qS,HS):q\in I_j,0\le S\le R_j(q)\}$, $P_j^+=\{(qT,0,H(1-T)):q\in I_j,0\le T\le R_j(q)\}$, and directions $v_j^-=(1,\tau\alpha_j,H\tau\beta_j)$, $v_j^+=(-\tau\alpha_j,1,H\tau\beta_j)$. The claimed finite cover is $K\subseteq\bigcup_j(P_j^-+\mathbb Rv_j^-)\cup(P_j^++\mathbb Rv_j^+)$, with compact perpendicular bases and total area \[ C_{\tau}/H=1/2+(2\eta-1/240)\tau^2+O(\tau^4)<1/2 \] for sufficiently small $\tau$. Constant caps give triangular bases; the threshold is independent of the number of intervals.
Manuscript 2.
Choose a Euclidean plane $P$ with unit normal $e$, independent $a,b\in P$, $J=[\alpha,\beta]$ with $\alpha<\beta$, continuous $R\ge0$, a $C^1$ map $w:J\to P$, $p_0\in P$ and $L>0$. Set \[ \mathcal S=\{p_0+\rho(a+tb)+s(e+w(t)):t\in J,0\le\rho\le R(t),|s|\le L\}, \quad\mathcal A=|\det_P(a,b)|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{R(t)^2}{2\sqrt{1+\|w(t)\|^2}}\,dt. \] If $w'(t)=\lambda(t)(a+tb)$ for continuous real $\lambda$, every tagged finite partition gives one covering cylinder per interval, with frozen axis $e+w(c_i)$ and nondegenerate compact triangular perpendicular base, whose total base area tends uniformly to $\mathcal A$ as the mesh tends to zero. Frozen projections of the actual interval sweeps give the same limit. The tetrahedral consequence claims, for each regular tetrahedron $K$ of positive edge length, a finite triangular-base cover with \[ \sum_i|B_i|<\tfrac12 A_{\min}(K),\qquad \sum_i\frac{|B_i|}{|\pi_{u_i^{\perp}}K|}<\tfrac12. \]
Manuscript 3.
For every regular tetrahedron $K\subset\mathbb R^3$ of positive edge length, the claim supplies finitely many cylinders $B_i+\mathbb Ru_i$, with unit $u_i$, compact nondegenerate parallelogram bases $B_i\subset u_i^{\perp}$, covering $K$ and satisfying \[ \sum_i|B_i|<\tfrac12\min_{|u|=1}|\pi_{u^{\perp}}K|, \qquad\sum_i\frac{|B_i|}{|\pi_{u_i^{\perp}}K|}<\tfrac12. \] It also claims such finite covers with bounded open measurable bases. Both sums count base areas with multiplicity.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 100; 2026-09-27), angularly tilted radial families receive a cubic buffer that preserves coverage. Secant-sector calculations give a negative quadratic area correction, and finite triangular enclosures retain the strict saving when the angular mesh is small.

For manuscript 2 (family 100; 2026-09-27), one triangular enclosure is assigned to each interval of a tagged partition. Complementary shortened beams leave a strip covered by horizontal cylinders; exact overlap and strip calculations cancel the first-order cost and leave a strict second-order saving.

For manuscript 3 (family 100; 2026-09-27), tangential angular and rational slope fields preserve the relevant wedges. Complementary label displacements have a controlled second-order overlap surplus, so a small padding secures coverage while tilt lowers perpendicular area by a larger amount.
