Erdős problem #342

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT. Define the Ulam sequence $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ by $a_1=1$, $a_2=2$, and for $n\ge 2$, let $a_{n+1}$ be the smallest integer $>a_n$ that can be expressed as $a_i+a_j$ in exactly one way with $1\le i<j\le n$.

The questions in the statement include:

  • Does $A$ have positive density? what is $|A\cap[1,x]|$?
  • Are the successive differences $a_{n+1}-a_n$ eventually periodic?
  • Are there infinitely many twin pairs $a, a+2\in A$?

2) QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK. The statement gives initial terms \[ 1,2,3,4,6,8,11,13,16,18,26,28,36,38,47,48,\dots \] and reports that numerical evidence suggests about $7\%$ of integers lie in $A$ and that no periodicity is known.

3) ATTACK PLAN.

  • Prove basic structural facts about the greedy/unique-representation construction (infinity, permanence of uniqueness).
  • Compute a moderately long prefix to check twin pairs and density heuristics.

4) WORK.

Lemma 342.1 (the Ulam sequence is infinite). For every $n\ge 2$, the integer $a_n+a_{n-1}$ has a unique representation as a sum $a_i+a_j$ with $1\le i<j\le n$. In particular, $a_{n+1}$ exists for all $n$.

Proof. Consider $s:=a_n+a_{n-1}$. It is representable as $a_{n-1}+a_n$. We claim there is no other representation using indices $\le n$. Any sum $a_i+a_j$ with $j\le n-1$ is at most $a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}<a_{n-1}+a_n=s$ because $a_n>a_{n-2}$. Thus any representation of $s$ must involve $a_n$. But if $a_n+a_i=s$, then $a_i=a_{n-1}$, giving the same pair. Hence $s$ has exactly one representation among pairs from $\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$. Since $s>a_n$, the greedy rule has at least one eligible candidate above $a_n$, so $a_{n+1}$ is well-defined. $\square$

Lemma 342.2 (uniqueness is permanent). For each $m\ge 3$, the representation of $a_m$ as $a_i+a_j$ with $1\le i<j<m$ is unique not only at the time $a_m$ is chosen, but forever: there is no other pair $(i',j')$ with $1\le i'<j'<m$ such that $a_m=a_{i'}+a_{j'}$.

Proof. By definition of the construction, when $a_m$ is chosen it has exactly one representation as $a_i+a_j$ with $i<j<m$. If there were another representation $a_m=a_{i'}+a_{j'}$ with $i'<j'<m$, it would already exist at the time $a_m$ is selected (since it uses only earlier terms), contradicting the selection rule. No later terms can create new representations of $a_m$ because all later terms are positive and larger than $a_m$. $\square$

FAST REALITY CHECK (computed prefix). A brute-force generation of the first $200$ Ulam numbers produced:

  • The first $30$ terms match the statement: \[1,2,3,4,6,8,11,13,16,18,26,28,36,38,47,48,53,57,62,69,72,77,82,87,97,99,102,106,114,126.\]
  • Among the first $200$ terms, there are $54$ twin pairs $a,a+2\in A$ (counted by scanning the set).
  • The $200$th term is $a_{200}=1792$, so $|A\cap[1,1792]|/1792\approx 0.1116$ at this small scale.
  • No perfect period $\le 50$ was detected in the last $200$ successive differences among the first $500$ terms (a finite check; not a proof).

5) VERIFICATION. Lemmas 342.1--342.2 are proved by direct inequalities using the strict increase of $a_n$. The computation is an exact implementation maintaining counts of representations by distinct pairs.

6) FINAL.

UNRESOLVED

(i) Strongest fully proved partial result obtained here. We proved that the Ulam sequence is well-defined and infinite (Lemma 342.1) and that each selected term has a permanently unique representation by earlier terms (Lemma 342.2). Computations up to $200$ terms show many twin pairs in that range.

(ii) Exact first gap. We did not prove any asymptotic statement about density, growth of $a_n$, periodicity of differences, or infinitude of twin pairs. The first gap is controlling representation counts globally as $n$ grows.

(iii) Top 3 next moves (concrete targets).

  1. Prove any nontrivial bound on $|A\cap[1,x]|$ (even $x^c$ upper/lower bounds) by estimating how many integers have exactly one representation by current pairs.
  2. Develop a "local limit" heuristic/lemma: show that representation counts around scale $a_n$ behave like a random model, then try to make at least one implication rigorous (e.g. infinitely many twins or positive density).
  3. Search for and prove modular constraints (if any) on $A$ that could force or preclude eventual periodicity of differences.

(iv) Minimal counterexample structure. A counterexample to positive density would be evidence that $|A\cap[1,x]|/x\to 0$, meaning that as $x$ grows, integers with exactly one representation as $a_i+a_j$ become sparse. A counterexample to "infinitely many twins" would mean there exists $X$ such that no twin pair occurs above $X$, forcing strong constraints on which even gaps can appear.
