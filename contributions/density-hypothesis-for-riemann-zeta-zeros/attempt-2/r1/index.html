Density Hypothesis for Riemann-Zeta Zeros

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that zeta, Dirichlet functions, and finite-order Eisenstein-field Hecke families admit a zero-free region with boundary $11/12$; In manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), the asserted result is that zeta, Dirichlet $L$-functions, and finite-order Hecke $L$-functions for $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ have no zeros with real part greater than $7/8$, with poles excluded.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and every finite-order Hecke character $\chi$ over $K$, the claimed zero-free assertion, with meromorphic poles excluded from evaluation, is \[ \operatorname{Re}s>11/12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_K(s,\chi)\ne0. \] It includes every Dirichlet $L$-function and the Riemann zeta function. The accompanying prime-progression claim uses $\pi(x;q,a)$ and asserts, with an absolute effective constant, \[ \sup_{\substack{1\le q\le x\$a,q)=1}} \left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\int_2^x\frac{dt}{\log t}\right| \ll x^{11/12}\log x\quad(x\ge2). \] The critical-line requirement $\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2$ is not a consequence of this half-plane claim.
Manuscript 2.
For every finite-order Hecke character of $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, every Dirichlet character and the zeta function, the principal claim is \[ \operatorname{Re}s>7/8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(s)\ne0, \] where a principal character's pole at $s=1$ is allowed. The weaker displayed $11/12$ theorem follows within this scope. The cited arithmetic consequence gives absolute $A,C>0$ such that every odd prime $p$ has least positive quadratic nonresidue \[ n(p)\le C(\log p)^A. \] Thus $n(p)\ll_{\delta} p^{\delta}$ for each fixed $\delta>0$. Given binary $p$ and $a\in\mathbb F_p$, a deterministic algorithm of time polynomial in $\log p$ is further claimed to return a square root of $a$ or correctly declare it nonexistent. The half-plane assertion leaves zeros between the critical line and $7/8$ untreated.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 003; 2026-10-05), twisted Möbius sums are enlarged to a family before Poisson summation. A recursive estimate for the dual mean square, followed by inversion of cube factors, supplies the saving used to exclude a reciprocal-function singularity.

For manuscript 2 (family 003; 2026-09-30), smoothed character sums are estimated in two ways: directly by large-sieve and recursive moment bounds, and through a Mellin transform involving the reciprocal of an $L$-function. Poisson summation and sextic residue characters align the two expressions so that their savings force continuation of the reciprocal.
