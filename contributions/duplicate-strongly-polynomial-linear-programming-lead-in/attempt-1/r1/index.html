Attempted route: repeated feasibility tests recover the optimum

This is contextual commentary, not a second formal LP conjecture. To investigate its feasibility-versus-optimization distinction, consider an explicitly restricted oracle reduction. An unknown optimum $q$ belongs to $\{j2^{-B}:0\le j\le2^B\}$. A query at $t$ returns only the bit indicating whether a feasible point with objective at least $t$ exists, equivalently whether $q\ge t$. The reduction must identify $q$ exactly.
Proposition: Every deterministic comparison tree for this task has worst-case depth at least $\lceil\log_2(2^B+1)\rceil=B+1$.
Proof: There are $2^B+1$ distinct values requiring different outputs. A binary tree of depth at most $r$ has at most $2^r$ leaves, by induction on depth. Two candidate optima ending at the same leaf would give the same output and one would be wrong. Thus $2^r\ge2^B+1$. ∎
The threshold question can arise from optimizing $x$ over $0\le x\le q$. But when this LP is supplied explicitly, $q$ is already a coefficient and can be read directly. The lower bound concerns the chosen black-box conversion and emphatically is not an LP lower bound. It identifies why a naive threshold-bisection reduction does not inherit a strongly polynomial operation count from a feasibility oracle: the reduction may spend a number of calls depending on precision.
