Erdős Problem 1000: vanishing average of new reduced denominators

Statement and attribution

For a strictly increasing positive integer sequence $A=(n_k)$, let $\phi_A(k)$ count those $1\le m\le n_k$ for which $n_k/\gcd(m,n_k)$ is unequal to every earlier $n_j$. We construct $A$ with \[ \frac1N\sum_{k\le N}\frac{\phi_A(k)}{n_k}\longrightarrow0. \] Haight's thesis is credited with the known resolution. The explicit prime-times-divisors construction below is reconstructed from the public proof linked in the problem discussion, Erdos1000.lean in plby/lean-proofs; that source credits Aristotle for formalization. We prove the mathematical construction here and do not claim to have run Lean or to have a new result.

One finite block

Let $r$ be prime and $q$ a positive integer coprime to $r$. Suppose $n_k=rq$ and every $rd$ with $d\mid q$, $d<q$, has occurred earlier. If an admissible numerator $m$ is not divisible by $r$ and has $\gcd(m,q)>1$, its reduced denominator is \[ \frac{rq}{\gcd(m,q)}=rd \] for a proper divisor $d$ of $q$, contradicting admissibility. Thus every counted numerator either is coprime to $rq$, or is divisible by $r$. There are at most $\phi(rq)+q$ such numerators, giving \[ \frac{\phi_A(k)}{rq}\le \frac{\phi(q)}q+\frac1r. \tag{1} \] Additional denominators from earlier blocks can only decrease the left side. This inequality, rather than an unjustified exact invariance under scaling, is what the construction needs.

A divisor average tending to zero

Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be all primes, $Q_t=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_i$, and \[ a_t=\frac1{2^t}\sum_{d\mid Q_t}\frac{\phi(d)}d. \] Squarefree divisors correspond to subsets of the first $t$ primes, so multiplicativity gives \[ a_t=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{1+(1-1/p_i)}2 =\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac1{2p_i}\right)\longrightarrow0. \tag{2} \] For completeness, $\sum_p1/p$ diverges: if it converged, the finite Euler products $\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p)^{-1}$ would stay bounded, since $-\log(1-1/p)\le1/p+C/p^2$. But expanding these products shows that they dominate $\sum_{n\le x}1/n$, a contradiction. Applying $\log(1-u)\le-u$ proves $2$.

Ordering all blocks and controlling every prefix

Choose primes $r_t$ recursively so that \[ r_t>\max(Q_t,\ r_{t-1}Q_{t-1},\ 1/a_t) \] (omit the middle term at $t=1$). Infinitely many primes guarantee these choices. The $t$-th block consists of all $2^t$ numbers \[ \{r_td:d\mid Q_t\}, \] listed increasingly. Every number in this block exceeds all numbers in preceding blocks; its proper-divisor companions occur earlier within the same block. Concatenating the blocks therefore produces a strictly increasing infinite sequence. By $1$--$2$, the sum of $\phi_A(k)/n_k$ over the whole $t$-th block is at most \[ 2^t(a_t+1/r_t)\le2^{t+1}a_t. \tag{3} \] Let a prefix end anywhere in block $t$. For $t\ge2$ its length is at least $\sum_{j<t}2^j=2^t-2$. All summands are nonnegative, so even an unfinished block contributes no more than its full-block bound. Thus the prefix average is at most \[ \frac{\sum_{j\le t}2^{j+1}a_j}{2^t-2}. \tag{4} \] This tends to zero. Indeed, given $\epsilon>0$, choose $J$ with $a_j<\epsilon$ for $j\ge J$. The finite sum over $j<J$, divided by $2^t-2$, vanishes; the remaining geometric sum in $4$ is at most $(4+o(1))\epsilon$. Since $\epsilon$ is arbitrary, every prefix average tends to zero, not just the block-end subsequence.

Why this does not force individual terms to vanish

If $\liminf\phi_A(k)/n_k=0$, then $\liminf\phi(n_k)/n_k=0$, so arbitrarily large primes divide members of $A$. For such a prime $p$, let $n_k$ be its first occurrence. Whenever $p\nmid m$, the reduced denominator $n_k/\gcd(m,n_k)$ is still divisible by $p$ and cannot equal any earlier $n_j$. Hence $\phi_A(k)/n_k\ge1-1/p$. Thus the known obstruction $\limsup\phi_A(k)/n_k=1$ is fully compatible with our vanishing Cesàro average.

Conclusion and sources

The required sequence has been constructed and every-prefix convergence proved.
