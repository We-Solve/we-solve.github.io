Structural-variant calling

Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.

Target and scope

Catalogue definitions and assumptions.
A diploid genome is represented by two oriented sequence paths through a finite reference or pangenome graph. A structural event changes a path by deletion, insertion, duplication, inversion or translocation. Reads $R$ follow a specified length and platform-error model, with coverage and mapping bias recorded. Let $S$ denote event descriptions, and quotient by descriptions that produce the same haplotype paths; event history is not always uniquely inferable from final sequence.
Original target.
Recover paths and structural-variant uncertainty from noisy reads, with guarantees on identifiable classes: \[\widehat S=\arg\max_{S\in\mathcal S}P(R\mid S)P(S),\qquad \mathbb{P}\{d_{\rm path}(\widehat S,S_0)\le\varepsilon\}\ge1-\alpha.\] The discrepancy $d_{\rm path}$ counts misreconstructed sequence and breakpoint errors after resolving equivalent descriptions. Determine coverage and linkage conditions giving reliable complex-allele reconstruction, and characterize cases where several variant configurations remain observationally indistinguishable.
Catalogue alternative formulations.
(i) Call germline variants in diploid unique-sequence regions against a validated reference, using independent assemblies as truth. (ii) Infer low-frequency mosaic rearrangements in mixed cells, with clone fractions and contamination explicitly modelled. This adds a mixture-identifiability issue absent from ordinary diploid calling.

Attempted answer

Proposition: In a marker-only mixture experiment, clone fraction $f$ and within-clone marker chance $r$ give no-marker probability $(1-fr)^n$. Any zero-false-positive caller has at least this false-negative probability; the marker-presence rule attains it.
Proof: Independent reads have marker probability $fr$. The uninformative observation occurs surely under the null, so a zero-false-positive rule cannot call there. This forces the bound under the alternative. ∎

What remains unresolved

Errors, richer linkage and equivalent structural histories need a broader model; no general caller guarantee is proved.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
No data-dependent shortcut.
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family, a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
