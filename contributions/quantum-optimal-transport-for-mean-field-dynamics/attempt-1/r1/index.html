Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A quantum state is a positive trace-one operator. A coupling of two quantum states is a bipartite state with those partial traces. Quantum optimal transport seeks a cost or variational principle on such states analogous to classical optimal transport. A Benamou--Brenier formula in classical transport minimizes a kinetic action over density and velocity curves subject to a continuity equation. The source pseudo-metric for mean-field comparison has its own cost operator and need not be a genuine metric with zero self-distance.

A self-distance obstruction to a naive dynamic action.
The qualification in [S2] says the mean-field cost may have nonzero self-distance. Here is an exact finite-dimensional diagnostic. For a unit vector $u$ let $\rho=|u\rangle\langle u|$. Any positive bipartite density matrix $\Gamma$ with both marginals $\rho$ equals $\rho\otimes\rho$. Indeed $\operatorname{Tr}((I-\rho)\otimes I)\Gamma=0$; writing this as the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm of $((I-\rho)\otimes I)\Gamma^{1/2}$ proves support in $\mathbb Cu\otimes\mathcal H$, and the other marginal reduces it to $\mathbb Cu\otimes\mathbb Cu$. For any self-adjoint matrix $A$, the cost $C=(A\otimes I-I\otimes A)^2$ therefore satisfies \[ \inf_{\Gamma\mapsto(\rho,\rho)}\operatorname{Tr}(C\Gamma) =2\big(\langle u,A^2u\rangle-\langle u,Au\rangle^2\big). \] Take $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $u=(1,1)/\sqrt2$: the answer is $2$.
