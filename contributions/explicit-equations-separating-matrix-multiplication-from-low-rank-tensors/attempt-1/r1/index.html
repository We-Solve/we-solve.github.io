An explicit flattening equation reaches only a basic rank threshold

The primary passage is a rhetorical transition immediately followed by representation-theoretic methods. It does not specify a new residual equation-construction target. We work out a simple instance of the proposed method and locate its limitation. Over $\mathbb C$, write the matrix-multiplication tensor as \[ T_n=\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^n e_{ij}\otimes e_{j\ell}\otimes e_{\ell i}. \] Flatten a tensor by using its first factor as row index and the pair of remaining factors as column index. This is an $n^2$ by $n^4$ matrix.
Proposition: The determinant of the $n^2$ by $n^2$ submatrix using columns $(e_{j1},e_{1i})$, indexed by pairs $(i,j)$, vanishes on all tensors of border rank at most $n^2-1$, but equals 1 at $T_n$.
Proof: A simple tensor $a\otimes b\otimes c$ flattens to the rank-at-most-one matrix $a(b\otimes c)^T$. A sum of $r$ simple tensors therefore has flattening rank at most $r$, and every $(r+1)$-minor vanishes. The same holds for border rank at most $r$, since these minors are polynomials, hence continuous, and vanish on every approximating rank-at-most-$r$ tensor. In $T_n$, the coefficient in row $(i',j')$ and selected column $(e_{j1},e_{1i})$ is 1 exactly when $i'=i$ and $j'=j$, and is otherwise 0. The selected submatrix is therefore the identity when its rows and columns use the same pair order. Its determinant is 1, proving the assertion. ∎
The failed extension is trying to obtain larger lower bounds from minors of this same flattening. It has only $n^2$ rows, so no minor of order greater than $n^2$ exists. For a proposed border-rank threshold $r\ge n^2$, this particular rank test imposes no further constraint. The source already discusses substantially stronger methods; the displayed determinant is a basic known equation, not an improvement on matrix-multiplication bounds.
