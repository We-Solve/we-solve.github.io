Casimir-Connection Monodromy and Quantum Weyl Groups

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Let $\mathfrak g$ be a complex simple Lie algebra, $\mathfrak h$ a Cartan subalgebra, and $W$ its Weyl group. The regular torus removes the root hypertori from the torus with Lie algebra $\mathfrak h$; the fundamental group of its Weyl quotient is the affine braid group in the report. The trigonometric Casimir connection is the flat Weyl-equivariant connection defined there with values in the Yangian $Y(\mathfrak g)$. Its monodromy gives a braid-group representation. The quantum loop algebra $U_\hbar(L\mathfrak g)$ deforms the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak g[z,z^{-1}]$, and its quantum Weyl operators give a second braid-group action. Equivalence asks for an intertwiner between these representations under the source's module comparison.
Source scope and qualifications.
The identification of the finite-dimensional Yangian and quantum-loop module data is part of the proposed comparison. Equality of abstract braid groups alone is insufficient, and a result for sl_2 alone does not settle every simple Lie algebra.

A local monodromy necessary test.
For a fixed finite-dimensional matrix $R$ and formal or small complex parameter $\hbar$, consider the pure logarithmic equation $dF/dz=(\hbar R/z)F$. On a chosen logarithm branch, $F(z)=\exp(\hbar R\log z)$, verified by differentiating the exponential of the fixed matrix. One positive circuit about zero changes it by \[ M_R=\exp(2\pi i\hbar R)=I+2\pi i\hbar R+O(\hbar^2). \] Its determinant is $\exp(2\pi i\hbar\operatorname{Tr}R)$, as follows by triangularizing $R$ and multiplying the diagonal exponentials.
Constraints on a proposed intertwiner.
If two braid actions have generator matrices $M_i=I+\hbar A_i+O(\hbar^2)$ and $Q_i=I+\hbar B_i+O(\hbar^2)$, and an intertwiner is $C=C_0+O(\hbar)$ with invertible $C_0$, then $CM_iC^{-1}=Q_i$ forces $C_0A_iC_0^{-1}=B_i$ for every generator. In particular their traces and eigenvalue multisets must match simultaneously. An intertwiner with $C_0=I$ cannot repair any first-order residue mismatch.
Where the attempt stops.
The trigonometric Casimir connection has several singular loci and noncommuting residues, together with a nontrivial Yangian/quantum-loop module comparison. The pure one-residue equation is not its global solution, and the preceding test is not sufficient for equivalence: higher braid words and higher orders may differ even after the tests pass. The first gap is an explicit module identification and simultaneous comparison of the source generator matrices beyond this local check. No general-simple-Lie-algebra result is inferred from a rank-one calculation.
