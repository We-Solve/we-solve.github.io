Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a polynomial optimization instance $\inf_{x\in K}f(x)$ with $K=\{x:g_j(x)\geq0\}$, a representing measure $\mu$ has moments $y_\alpha=\int_K x^\alpha\,d\mu(x)$. Moment relaxations retain finitely many moments and require moment and localizing matrices to be positive semidefinite. Their SOS duals seek certificates of the form $f-\gamma=\sigma_0+\sum_j\sigma_jg_j$, with sum-of-squares polynomials and bounded degrees. The generalized moment problem also permits the source linear measure constraints. Strong duality means equality of the primal and dual optimal values under the appropriate attainment conventions. Convergence of moments to an optimal measure and convergence rates of relaxation values are separate targets.

Research attempt: A finite-support exact hierarchy and obstructions to measure convergence

An exact finite-support moment relaxation.
Let $K=\{t_1,\ldots,t_s\}\subset\mathbb R$ consist of distinct points, $s\geq1$, and let $h(t)=\prod_i(t-t_i)$. For a polynomial objective $f$ of degree at most $d\geq0$, choose an integer \[ r\geq\max\{s-1,\lceil s/2\rceil,\lceil d/2\rceil\}. \] Consider a linear functional $L$ on polynomials of degree at most $2r$ satisfying $L(1)=1$, $L(q^2)\geq0$ for all $\deg q\leq r$, and $L(hq)=0$ whenever $\deg(hq)\leq2r$. These are the moment PSD and truncated equality-ideal constraints. Define the Lagrange polynomials \[ \ell_i(t)=\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{t-t_j}{t_i-t_j}, \qquad \ell_i(t_j)=\mathbf1_{i=j}. \] They have degree at most $s-1\leq r$. The polynomial $\ell_i^2-\ell_i$ vanishes at every $t_j$, so is divisible by $h$, by successive applications of the factor theorem for distinct roots. Its degree is at most $2s-2\leq2r$. Thus $w_i=L(\ell_i)=L(\ell_i^2)\geq0$. The identity $\sum_i\ell_i=1$ follows because both sides have degree at most $s-1$ and agree at $s$ distinct points; their difference must be zero, again by the factor theorem. Hence $\sum_iw_i=1$. For every $p$ of degree at most $2r$, $p-\sum_i p(t_i)\ell_i$ is divisible by $h$ and has degree at most $2r$, so \[ L(p)=\sum_iw_i p(t_i). \] Every feasible truncated moment functional is therefore exactly a probability measure on $K$ at this level. Conversely any such measure satisfies the constraints. Minimizing $L(f)$ gives $\min_i f(t_i)$.
A matching explicit dual certificate.
Put $f_*=\min_i f(t_i)$ and $c_i=f(t_i)-f_*\geq0$. Then \[ f-f_*=\sum_i c_i\ell_i^2+hq \] for a polynomial $q$, because the difference vanishes at every root of $h$. The SOS terms have degree at most $2s-2\leq2r$ and the whole difference has degree at most $\max(d,2s-2)\leq2r$, so the equality term is allowed as well. Each $c_i\ell_i^2$ is a square after multiplying $\ell_i$ by $\sqrt{c_i}$. This is a real certificate; rational square coefficients are not claimed for arbitrary real input data. The point mass at a minimizing $t_i$ is a primal feasible solution of value $f_*$, and the certificate is a dual feasible solution of the same value. Weak duality follows by applying $L$ to any SOS-plus-ideal representation. Thus there is no duality gap in this explicitly stated hierarchy, with finite convergence at the displayed degree and no Slater argument required.
Optimal values do not force a chosen sequence of measures to converge.
Take $K=\{-1,1\}$ and $f=0$. Both $\delta_{-1}$ and $\delta_1$ are optimal probability measures at every sufficiently large level, and their moment sequences are feasible at every level. Choosing them alternately produces first moments $-1,1,-1,1,\ldots$, so neither the first moments nor the corresponding measures converge weakly. The objective values are all exactly zero. This disproves convergence of every selected optimizer sequence without a uniqueness or selection condition; it does not disprove subsequence convergence on a compact set.

Without compactness or tightness, even a limiting optimal measure can be absent. On the closed semialgebraic set $K=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:xy=1\}$, minimize $f(x,y)=x^2$ over probability measures. Point masses at $(1/j,j)$ have objective $1/j^2\to0$ and are feasible at every degree. But $x^2>0$ everywhere on $K$, so its integral under any probability measure on $K$ is strictly positive (or infinite): the sets $\{x^2\geq1/k\}$ cover $K$, and at least one has positive measure. No optimal probability measure attaining zero exists. These point masses also escape every bounded set, so there is no weakly convergent probability subsequence: any candidate probability limit gives positive mass to some compact ball, whereas continuous bounded bump functions on that ball eventually have integral zero under the sequence.

Scope and remaining gaps

The manuscript proves exact finite convergence and strong duality only for an explicit univariate finite set with its defining equality polynomial. It does not derive general hierarchy convergence rates or strong duality for arbitrary moment problems. The examples specify why convergence of values, subsequences, and entire selected optimizer sequences require different hypotheses.
