Erdős Problem 446

Statement and result.
Let $\delta(n)$ be the natural density of integers with a divisor in $(n,2n)$, and $\delta_r(n)$ the density of those with exactly $r$ such divisors. The known answer is \[ \delta(n)\asymp \frac1{(\log n)^\alpha(\log\log n)^{3/2}},\qquad \alpha=1-\frac{1+\log\log2}{\log2}, \tag{1} \] and, for each fixed $r\geq1$, \[ \delta_r(n)\gg_r\delta(n). \tag{2} \] In particular $\delta_1(n)=o(\delta(n))$ is false. We give a complete deduction from Ford's interval-divisor theorems, including the open-endpoint convention and the existence of all densities.
Exact finite formulas.
Let $D_n=\{n+1,\ldots,2n-1\}$, with integer $n\geq2$, and $L=\operatorname{lcm}(D_n)$. The number $T_n(m)=|\{d\in D_n:d\mid m\}|$ is periodic modulo $L$. Hence all the stated natural densities exist and are rational. Inclusion-exclusion gives \[ \delta(n)= \sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq D_n} \frac{(-1)^{|S|+1}}{\operatorname{lcm}(S)}. \tag{3} \] More generally, binomial inversion gives, for $r\geq1$, \[ \delta_r(n)= \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq D_n\\|S|\geq r}} \frac{(-1)^{|S|-r}\binom{|S|}{r}}{\operatorname{lcm}(S)}. \tag{4} \] To verify $4$, for any integer with $t$ divisors in $D_n$, its contribution before averaging is $\sum_{j=r}^{t}(-1)^{j-r}\binom jr\binom tj$. This is $1$ if $t=r$ and zero otherwise, by $\binom tj\binom jr=\binom tr\binom{t-r}{j-r}$. Averaging a divisibility event over one period gives the reciprocal of its least common multiple, proving both formulas.
The explicit external input.
In The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval, Ford defines $\varepsilon(y,z)$ and $\varepsilon_r(y,z)$ using the half-open interval $(y,z]$. His Corollary 2 gives $1$ for $\varepsilon(n,2n)$. His Corollary 7 gives $\varepsilon_r(n,2n)\gg_r\varepsilon(n,2n)$ for each fixed $r$. These deep theorems are external inputs; their analytic proofs are not reconstructed here.
Transfer to the exact statement.
The only difference between $(n,2n)$ and $(n,2n]$ is the divisor $2n$. Thus the indicator for having any divisor, and the indicator for having exactly $r$ divisors, can change only on multiples of $2n$. Consequently \[ |\delta(n)-\varepsilon(n,2n)|\leq\frac1{2n},\qquad |\delta_r(n)-\varepsilon_r(n,2n)|\leq\frac1{2n}. \tag{5} \] The error $1/n$ is negligible compared with the function in $1$. Applying the external bounds and then $5$ proves $1$--$2$. In particular there is a fixed $c>0$ such that $\delta_1(n)/\delta(n)\geq c$ for all sufficiently large $n$; the ratio cannot tend to zero.
A useful check on the interpretation.
The period average of $T_n$ is exactly \[ \sum_{d=n+1}^{2n-1}\frac1d=\log2+o(1). \] Conditioning on $T_n\geq1$, the mean is this sum divided by $\delta(n)$, and therefore tends to infinity. This is consistent with $2$: a positive conditional proportion can have exactly one divisor while the conditional mean diverges. Conflating these two statements would lead to the incorrect conjecture.
