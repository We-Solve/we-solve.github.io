Resource-aware synthetic control

Status: preliminary conditional reduction; full target not established.

Target and scope

Catalogue definitions and assumptions.
Let $y(t)$ be a measured output production rate, $r>0$ a setpoint, $R(t)$ available resource pools, and $z(t)$ controller molecular counts. A finite reaction-network model has plant dynamics $\dot x=f_\theta(x,z,R)$ and resource dynamics $\dot R=g_\theta(x,z,R,e)$, where $e$ is bounded environment variation. Controllers must have nonnegative realizable reaction rates, finite burden and a declared sensing mechanism. Parameters lie in a calibrated compact set $\Theta$, and controller consumption is included rather than treating control as free.
Original target.
Construct a molecular controller and operating envelope satisfying \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta,\,e\in\mathcal E} \limsup_{t\to\infty}|\mathbb{E}_\theta y(t)-r|\leq\varepsilon, \qquad \sup_t\mathbb{E}\|z(t)\|\leq Z_{\max}, \] while keeping the full host--controller system stable and viable. Determine where resource saturation makes the target impossible.
Catalogue alternative formulations.
(i) A deterministic version demands an attracting equilibrium and local or global stability on a specified invariant set. (ii) A stochastic version demands ergodicity, controlled mean error and a variance ceiling. Mean adaptation does not imply small single-cell fluctuations. A disturbance-rejection formulation uses finite-time tracking bounds; exact adaptation applies only to disturbances for which the internal-model conditions hold.

Attempted answer

Preliminary step: the following worked reduction has not been shown to instantiate all assumptions of the original target. Its applicability is an outstanding proof obligation.
Proposition: For a stipulated integral controller $\dot z=r-y$, every equilibrium with finite controller state necessarily has output $y=r$. This algebraic set-point condition is not a stability guarantee. In the linear plant $\dot y=-ay+kz$, $a,k>0$, the closed-loop matrix is Hurwitz and the set point is attracting.
Proof: At equilibrium the controller derivative is zero. For constant reference, subtract equilibrium values: the characteristic polynomial of the plant-controller pair is $s^2+as+k$, whose positive coefficients give negative-real-part roots. ∎

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
