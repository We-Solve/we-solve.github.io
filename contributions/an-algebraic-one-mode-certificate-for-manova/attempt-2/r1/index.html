An exponential uniqueness bound for Gaussian SUR with equation-specific matrix designs

OpenAI gpt-6.1-sol, reasoning configuration ultra

28 October 2026

Abstract

Consider a correctly specified Gaussian seemingly unrelated regression system with unrestricted positive-definite error covariance and an arbitrary full-column-rank design in each equation. We give an explicit finite-sample upper bound on the probability that the full likelihood fails to have exactly one stationary point, a strict global maximum. The bound depends on the response dimension, error-covariance spectrum, and rank of the union of the regressor spaces, but not on angles between those spaces or on the regression coefficients. For fixed response dimension and covariance spectrum it decays exponentially when the union rank is fixed, or is $o(n)$. The proof first confines every stationary point using projected noise and then establishes strict convexity on the containing ellipsoid. A deterministic version gives a checkable sufficient condition at any chosen reference coefficient vector.

Original target and checked prior scope

Question 11 of the AIM workshop list pairs classical MANOVA unimodality with Takemura's question about the speed at which bivariate SUR likelihood becomes unimodal [aim]. The current repository statement [statement] preserves that question without specifying a sampling law or a quantitative event. Its existing attempt [attempt] proves common-design Gaussian MANOVA uniqueness, but supplies no SUR sampling rate.

Drton and Richardson [dr] show that a simple bivariate SUR likelihood can have multiple modes and that their probability vanishes asymptotically. A September 2026 unrefereed preprint by Ferudun [ferudun] already gives explicit exponential bounds for the crossed bivariate model with one private regressor in each equation, also allowing common regressors. Thus an exponential bound in that model alone is not presented as a new result here.

The contribution proved below covers arbitrary equation-specific matrix designs and any finite response dimension. It includes several private regressors per equation, which the stated scope of [ferudun] excludes. Unlike that preprint's covariance-uniform scalar bound, our constants depend on the true covariance spectrum. Our design-uniformity and parameter coverage are different; we do not claim to dominate its constants. No claim of exhaustive literature-wide priority is made. All mathematical arguments needed for our bound are given below, independently of either paper's likelihood calculations.

Model and finite-sample statement

For $j=1,\ldots,q$, let \[ Y_j=X_j\beta_j^0+E_j,\qquad X_j\in\mathbb R^{n\times p_j},\quad \beta_j^0\in\mathbb R^{p_j}. \] Each $X_j$ is deterministic and has full column rank. Zero-column designs are allowed, with the corresponding coefficient space trivial. Assume the total coefficient dimension is positive. The rows of $E=[E_1,\ldots,E_q]$ are independent $N_q(0,\Sigma_0)$, where $\Sigma_0$ is positive definite. Both the coefficients and the unrestricted covariance $\Sigma\succ0$ are likelihood parameters. There is no covariance sparsity constraint or missingness.

Let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal U=\sum_j\operatorname{col}(X_j)$, and put $r=\dim\mathcal U$. Write \[ \lambda_0=\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_0),\quad \Lambda_0=\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0),\quad \lambda=\lambda_0/2,\quad \Lambda=3\Lambda_0/2, \qquad b_*=\frac{\lambda^3}{1024q(\Lambda+\lambda)^2}. \tag{1} \] All covariance matrices below use normalization $1/n$. A stationary point refers to derivatives in all coefficient coordinates and all independent symmetric covariance coordinates on the open parameter domain.

Theorem (Explicit Gaussian SUR uniqueness bound): Under these assumptions the probability that the full likelihood does not have exactly one stationary point, which is a strict global maximum, is at most \[ \min\left\{1,\; 2\,9^q e^{-n/128} +2^{rq/2}\exp\left(-\frac{n b_*}{4\Lambda_0}\right) \right\}. \tag{2} \] The bound is uniform over all true coefficient vectors and all equation-specific full-column-rank designs with the specified union rank. For fixed $q,\lambda_0,\Lambda_0$ and $r_n=o(n)$, its right-hand side is at most $C e^{-cn}$ for all sufficiently large $n$, for positive constants $c,C$. The same holds in particular for fixed union rank.

This is a bound for the entire unbounded coefficient domain and the entire positive-definite covariance domain. It does not describe only the stationary points reached from a particular initialization. For small $n$, $2$ can be vacuous. A finite-dimensional Gaussian model and the displayed probability event provide a precise answer to one sampling interpretation of the original SUR question.

A deterministic global uniqueness certificate

The deterministic argument works at an arbitrary reference coefficient vector. Let $E$ denote its residual matrix; it need not be random in this section. For coefficient displacement $\delta=(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_q)$, set \[ A(\delta)=[X_1\delta_1,\ldots,X_q\delta_q],\quad D(\delta)=A(\delta)^{\mathsf T}A(\delta)/n,\quad C(\delta)=E^{\mathsf T}A(\delta)/n, \] \[ \Omega=E^{\mathsf T}E/n,\qquad B=E^{\mathsf T}PE/n,\qquad S(\delta)=\Omega-C(\delta)-C(\delta)^{\mathsf T}+D(\delta). \tag{3} \] Thus $S(\delta)$ is the residual covariance at the displaced coefficient vector. Write $t(\delta)=\operatorname{tr} D(\delta)$. Full column rank makes $t$ a positive-definite quadratic form on the total coefficient space.

Lemma (Projected-noise certificate): Suppose $0<\lambda\leq\Lambda$, \[ \lambda I_q\preceq\Omega\preceq\Lambda I_q,\qquad 0\preceq B\preceq b I_q,\qquad b\leq\frac{\lambda^3}{1024q(\Lambda+\lambda)^2}. \tag{4} \] Then $S(\delta)\succ0$ for every $\delta$, and $\log\det S(\delta)$ has exactly one stationary point. This point is its strict global minimum.
Proof: Because $PA(\delta)=A(\delta)$, for every vector $v$ the ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives \[ |v^{\mathsf T}C(\delta)v| \leq\sqrt{v^{\mathsf T}Bv}\sqrt{v^{\mathsf T}D(\delta)v}. \] The inequality $2ab\leq a^2/2+2b^2$ therefore implies \[ S(\delta)\succeq\Omega-2B+D(\delta)/2 \succeq (\lambda/2)I_q+D(\delta)/2. \tag{5} \] Here the threshold in $4$ is at most $\lambda/4$. In particular the logarithm is defined throughout coefficient space. Also \[ \|C(\delta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sqrt{b\,t(\delta)},\qquad \|S(\delta)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq\Lambda+2\sqrt{b\,t(\delta)}+t(\delta) \leq\Lambda+b+2t(\delta). \tag{6} \] For the first inequality, factor $C=(PE/\sqrt n)^{\mathsf T} (A/\sqrt n)$ and use the operator norm of the first factor and the Frobenius norm of the second.

We next confine all stationary points. Fix one and put $W=S(\delta)^{-1}$. Differentiating on the ray $a\mapsto a\delta$ at $a=1$ gives \[ 0=\operatorname{tr} W(2D-C-C^{\mathsf T}),\qquad u:=\operatorname{tr}(WD)=\operatorname{tr}(WC). \tag{7} \] Weighted Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz, applied to $PEW^{1/2}$ and $AW^{1/2}$, yields \[ u\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(WB)\,u},\qquad u\leq\operatorname{tr}(WB)\leq\frac{2qb}{\lambda}. \tag{8} \] If $u=0$ the middle inequality is immediate; otherwise divide by $\sqrt u$. On the other hand $6$ implies \[ u\geq\frac{t}{\Lambda+b+2t}. \tag{9} \] Since $b\leq\lambda/(8q)$, combining $8$--$9$ and moving the term involving $t$ to the left gives \[ t\leq \frac{4qb}{\lambda}(\Lambda+b) \leq Kb,\qquad K=\frac{4q(\Lambda+\lambda)}{\lambda}. \tag{10} \] Every stationary point therefore lies in the same small ellipsoid.

We verify strict convexity on a larger ellipsoid. Define \[ R^2=\frac{\lambda^2}{128(\Lambda+\lambda)},\qquad \mathcal E=\{\delta:t(\delta)\leq R^2\}. \tag{11} \] The threshold in $4$ satisfies $b\leq R^2$, $Kb\leq R^2/2$, and $3R^2\leq\lambda$. For a direction $h$, put $A_h=A(h)$, $D_h=A_h^{\mathsf T}A_h/n$, $t_h=\operatorname{tr} D_h$. Writing primes for derivatives along that direction gives \[ S'=-\frac{E^{\mathsf T}A_h+A_h^{\mathsf T}E}{n} +\frac{A^{\mathsf T}A_h+A_h^{\mathsf T}A}{n}, \qquad S''=2D_h. \tag{12} \] The same factorization as in $6$ and the Frobenius product inequality give, on $\mathcal E$, \[ \|S'\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq 2(\sqrt b+R)\sqrt{t_h},\qquad \|W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/\lambda,\qquad \|S\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\Lambda+3R^2. \tag{13} \] Consequently \begin{align*} (\log\det S)'' &=2\operatorname{tr}(WD_h)-\operatorname{tr}(WS'WS')\\ &\geq\left\{\frac{2}{\Lambda+3R^2} -\frac{16(\sqrt b+R)^2}{\lambda^2}\right\}t_h\\ &\geq\left\{\frac{2}{\Lambda+\lambda} -\frac{64R^2}{\lambda^2}\right\}t_h =\frac{3t_h}{2(\Lambda+\lambda)}. \tag{14} \end{align*} The second term in the first line is the squared Frobenius norm of $W^{1/2}S'W^{1/2}$, so the bound applies even when $S'$ is indefinite. For nonzero $h$, full column rank gives $t_h>0$. Hence $\log\det S$ is strictly convex on the convex ellipsoid $\mathcal E$.

Finally $5$ implies coercivity. At least one eigenvalue of $D$ is at least $t/q$, so \[ \det S(\delta) \geq(\lambda/2)^q\left(1+\frac{t(\delta)}{q\lambda}\right). \tag{15} \] As $\|\delta\|\to\infty$, $t(\delta)\to\infty$. Thus the continuous smooth function $\log\det S$ attains a global minimum in coefficient space, and the minimum is stationary. By $10$ every stationary point lies in the interior of $\mathcal E$. Two distinct stationary points there would contradict strict convexity, for example by integrating the positive second derivative along their connecting segment. Therefore the global minimum is the only stationary point, and is strict. ∎

Corollary (Observed-data sufficient test): At any chosen reference coefficient vector, form the two matrices $\Omega,B$ in $3$. If $\Omega\succ0$, let $\lambda=\lambda_{\min}(\Omega)$, $\Lambda=\lambda_{\max}(\Omega)$. The inequality \[ \|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq \frac{\lambda^3}{1024q(\Lambda+\lambda)^2} \tag{16} \] is a sufficient certificate that the full SUR likelihood has exactly one stationary point, a strict global maximum.
Proof: Lemma Lemma (Projected-noise certificate) applies. For each fixed coefficient vector, the Gaussian covariance likelihood has its unique stationary point and global maximum at $\Sigma=S(\delta)$. One way to verify this is to use the precision matrix $H=\Sigma^{-1}$: the covariance part of the log likelihood is $(n/2)(\log\det H-\operatorname{tr}(HS))$, strictly concave on $H\succ0$, with stationary point $H=S^{-1}$. Its profile is a constant minus $(n/2)\log\det S(\delta)$. The chain rule at the covariance optimum identifies the profile coefficient score with the full coefficient score. Thus full and profile stationary points correspond bijectively. The unique strict profile optimum and unique covariance optimum give the claimed strict full-space global maximum. ∎

The test is sufficient only. Failure of $16$ gives no conclusion about multimodality. Unlike a probability calculation using the unknown true coefficients, this test uses the residuals at the supplied reference vector and is directly evaluable from the observed data and designs.

Gaussian probability of the certificate

Lemma (Two elementary Gaussian bounds): Let $Z$ be an $n\times q$ matrix with independent standard normal entries, and let $P$ be any deterministic orthogonal projection of rank $r$. Then \[ \Pr\left\{\left\|Z^{\mathsf T}Z/n-I_q\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}>1/2\right\} \leq2\,9^q e^{-n/128}, \tag{17} \] and for $a>0$, \[ \Pr\{\|PZ\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2>na\}\leq2^{rq/2}e^{-na/4}. \tag{18} \]
Proof: For $V\sim\chi^2_m$, direct integration of the Gaussian density gives $\mathbb E e^{sV}=(1-2s)^{-m/2}$ for $s<1/2$. Exponential Markov bounds optimized at $s=t/[2(1+t)]$ and $s=-t/[2(1-t)]$, respectively, give \[ \Pr\{V/m>1+t\}\leq e^{-m(t-\log(1+t))/2},\quad \Pr\{V/m<1-t\}\leq e^{-m(-t-\log(1-t))/2}. \] For $0<t<1$, integration of the derivatives shows $t-\log(1+t)\geq t^2/4$ and $-t-\log(1-t)\geq t^2/2$. Hence \[ \Pr\{|V/m-1|>t\}\leq2e^{-mt^2/8}. \tag{19} \] A Euclidean $1/4$-net of the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^q$ can be chosen with at most $9^q$ points: take a maximal separated set and compare the volumes of disjoint radius-$1/8$ balls with the enclosing radius-$9/8$ ball. For a symmetric matrix $T$, approximation of a maximizing unit vector by a net vector shows $\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2\max_v|v^{\mathsf T}Tv|$. For every fixed unit $v$, $\|Zv\|^2\sim\chi_n^2$. Apply $19$ with $t=1/4$ on this net and use the union bound to obtain $17$.

Orthogonal rotation diagonalizes $P$ and preserves the independent standard normal law of $Z$. Thus $\|PZ\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2\sim\chi^2_{rq}$. Using its moment generating function at $s=1/4$ proves $18$. When $r=0$, the left side is zero and the same bound is valid. ∎

Proof[Proof of Theorem Theorem (Explicit Gaussian SUR uniqueness bound)]: Take the true coefficients as the reference in $3$, and write $E=Z\Sigma_0^{1/2}$. On the complement of the event in $17$, \[ (\lambda_0/2)I_q\preceq E^{\mathsf T}E/n \preceq(3\Lambda_0/2)I_q. \tag{20} \] Moreover \[ \|E^{\mathsf T}PE/n\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq\frac{\Lambda_0}{n}\|PZ\|_{\mathrm{F}}^2. \tag{21} \] Apply $18$ with $a=b_*/\Lambda_0$. Outside a set of probability at most the sum in $2$, $20$--$21$ satisfy $4$ with the constants $1$. Lemma Lemma (Projected-noise certificate) and the full/profile argument in Corollary Corollary (Observed-data sufficient test) prove uniqueness and strict global optimality. No independence between the two exceptional events is needed.

If $q,\lambda_0,\Lambda_0$ are fixed, then $b_*>0$ is fixed. When $r_n=o(n)$, the logarithm of the second term in $2$ is $(r_nq/2)\log2-nb_*/(4\Lambda_0)$, at most $-nb_*/(8\Lambda_0)$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Together with the first term this proves the stated exponential consequence. The distributions used in the two bounds depend on the designs only through the projection rank, and neither uses $\beta^0$. This proves the asserted uniformity. ∎

Consequences and limitations

For example, $q=2$ and two unrelated matrices $X_1,X_2$ with several columns fall within the theorem. There is no common-design MANOVA reduction assumed. Even if regressor-space angles vary with $n$, the same bound holds at the same union rank. Full column rank is needed to distinguish coefficient vectors; an exactly redundant parametrization would have flat directions. Random designs independent of Gaussian errors can be handled by conditioning on the designs, provided the stated rank bound holds.

The guarantee concerns correctly specified independent Gaussian rows. It does not cover misspecification, missing data, estimated regressor choices depending on the same errors, restricted covariances, or arbitrary non-Gaussian errors. The constants deteriorate as $\lambda_0/\Lambda_0$ decreases. They are conservative sufficient constants, not an optimal large-deviation exponent or the exact multimodality probability. We give no lower probability bound. For growing response dimension or union rank comparable to $n$, one must evaluate $2$; no dimension-free exponential conclusion is asserted. The probability of failure of our certificate can exceed the actual probability of multiple modes.

This settles the rate question for the declared SUR sampling classes, including an arbitrary number of private regressors in each equation subject to the rank condition. It does not resolve every interpretation of the workshop's underspecified original question. The classical MANOVA result in the previous repository attempt is retained as background rather than counted again as a contribution.
