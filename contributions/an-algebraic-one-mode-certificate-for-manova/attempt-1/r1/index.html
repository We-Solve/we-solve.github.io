MANOVA likelihood: algebraic uniqueness under rank hypotheses and the unresolved SUR rate

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. In seemingly unrelated regression (SUR), several response equations have separate regression coefficients and a correlated multivariate error vector. MANOVA allows every predictor to enter every response equation. Unimodality asks whether the likelihood has only one mode, with attention to its stated parameter domain.

A specified MANOVA model

Let $Y$ be an $n$ by $q$ response matrix, $X$ a fixed $n$ by $k$ design of full column rank, and use independent Gaussian rows with mean $XB$ and common positive-definite covariance $\Sigma$. Set $\widehat B=(X^TX)^{-1}X^TY$ and $S_0=(Y-X\widehat B)^T(Y-X\widehat B)$. Assume $S_0\succ0$. These hypotheses specify a standard subproblem; the source does not provide a sampling law for its separate SUR convergence-rate question.
Algebraic residual decomposition.
The normal equations $X^T(Y-X\widehat B)=0$ eliminate cross terms and give, for every $B$, \[ S(B)=(Y-XB)^T(Y-XB) =S_0+(B-\widehat B)^TX^TX(B-\widehat B). \] The second term is positive semidefinite and is zero exactly when $B=\widehat B$, since $X^TX\succ0$. For fixed $B$, the negative twice-log-likelihood, up to constants, is $n\log\det\Sigma+\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}S(B))$. Write $S=S(B)\succ0$ and $T=S^{1/2}\Sigma^{-1}S^{1/2}/n$. The expression becomes a constant depending on $S$ plus $n\operatorname{tr}(T)-n\log\det T$. Each positive eigenvalue $t$ of $T$ satisfies $t-\log t\ge1$, with equality exactly at $t=1$, by differentiation. Thus the unique optimum over $\Sigma$ is $\Sigma=S(B)/n$. The profile likelihood is maximized by minimizing $\det S(B)$. Conjugating its residual decomposition by $S_0^{-1/2}$ gives $\det S(B)=\det S_0\det(I+H)$ with $H\succeq0$. Every eigenvalue of $I+H$ is at least one, and equality of the product to one forces $H=0$. Hence the unique global maximum is $(\widehat B,S_0/n)$.
There are no other interior stationary points.
The $B$ score is $X^T(Y-XB)\Sigma^{-1}=0$. Since $\Sigma$ is invertible and $X^TX$ nonsingular, it forces $B=\widehat B$. The covariance score then forces $\Sigma=S_0/n$, as is also seen by the preceding change of variables. Thus there is exactly one interior stationary point, and it is the global maximum. If $S_0$ is singular, evaluating at $\widehat B$ and shrinking covariance eigenvalues along the residual nullspace sends the log likelihood to $+\infty$: the trace term has no component in those directions while $-n\log\det\Sigma/2$ diverges. If the design is rank deficient, different coefficients can give the same fitted mean. Both hypotheses are therefore substantive; universal uniqueness for arbitrary data fails.
Why this does not give a SUR sample-size rate.
The shared design is essential to one matrix normal equation. With different designs in separate response equations, cross-response covariance weights couple different coefficient spaces; the displayed residual decomposition does not hold with one common $\widehat B$. Increasing $n$ without specifying design sequences, a distribution, identifiability and a convergence event cannot yield a rate statement. No such assumptions are silently supplied for the separate source question.
