Erdős Problem 795: sets with distinct subset products

Statement and claim.
Let $g(n)$ be the largest size of $A\subseteq[1,n]\cap\mathbb N$ whose subset products, including the empty product, are all distinct. The requested upper bound is true. We apply Raghavan's established upper theorem, verify its asymptotic implication, and reconstruct his explicit improvement to the lower bound. Source: R. Raghavan, Sharp Bounds for Sets with Distinct Subset Products, Theorems 1.3--1.4 and Section 5, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02695, version 2. See https://www.erdosproblems.com/795. The $x^{1/2}$ in the problem's displayed error is understood as $n^{1/2}$, since $n$ is its only size parameter.
The external upper bound.
Theorem 1.3 of the cited version proves \[ g(n)=\pi(n)+\pi(\sqrt n)+O(n^{5/12}). \tag{1} \] This structural upper estimate is the main external input; its graph-pruning proof is not reproduced here. Because \[ \frac{n^{5/12}}{\sqrt n/\log n}=n^{-1/12}\log n\longrightarrow0, \] $1$ gives precisely the asserted upper bound. The elementary set consisting of primes $p\leq n$ and squares $p^2\leq n$ already attains $\pi(n)+\pi(\sqrt n)$: at each prime the selected exponent is $0,1,2,$ or $3$, and these four values uniquely determine which of $p,p^2$ was used.
A seven-element block on three primes.
Let $p,q,r$ be distinct primes at most $n^{1/3}$. All seven numbers \[ p^2q,\quad p^2r,\quad p^2,\quad qr,\quad p^3,\quad q^3,\quad r^3 \tag{2} \] are at most $n$. To prove their subset products distinct, subtract the exponent vectors of two putatively equal products. Let $a,b,c,d,e,f,h\in\{-1,0,1\}$ be the resulting coefficients of the seven entries in $2$. The $q$- and $r$-coordinates give \[ a+d+3f=0,\qquad b+d+3h=0. \] As $|a+d|,|b+d|\leq2$, these force $f=h=0$ and $a=b=-d$. The $p$-coordinate becomes \[ -4d+2c+3e=0. \] Its parity forces $e=0$, and then $c=2d$. Since $c,d\in\{-1,0,1\}$, we have $c=d=0$, followed by $a=b=0$. Every coefficient is zero, proving the claim.
Combining independent prime blocks.
Partition the primes at most $n^{1/3}$ into triples, leaving at most two ungrouped. For each triple use its seven-element block $2$. For primes in $(n^{1/3},\sqrt n]$, use both $p,p^2$; for primes above $\sqrt n$, use just $p$. Different blocks have disjoint prime supports. An equality between two global subset products restricts, prime by prime, to an equality within each block, which has just been proved impossible unless the subsets agree. Thus the construction has distinct subset products and size \[ \pi(n)-\pi(\sqrt n) +2\left(\pi(\sqrt n)-\pi(n^{1/3})\right) +7\left\lfloor\frac{\pi(n^{1/3})}{3}\right\rfloor = \pi(n)+\pi(\sqrt n)+\frac13\pi(n^{1/3})-O(1). \tag{3} \] This explains concretely why optimizing prime powers independently cannot describe all lower-order terms.
Scope.
The requested upper estimate follows completely from the explicitly cited external theorem. The baseline lower bound and the stronger seven-element construction are fully verified here. No new proof of the structural upper theorem or novel extremal construction is claimed.
