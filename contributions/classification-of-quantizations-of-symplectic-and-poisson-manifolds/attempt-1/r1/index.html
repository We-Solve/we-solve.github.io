Classification of Quantizations of Symplectic and Poisson Manifolds

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A symplectic manifold carries a closed nondegenerate two-form; a Poisson manifold carries a bracket on functions satisfying the Leibniz and Jacobi identities. A deformation quantization is an associative product $f\star g=fg+\sum_{j\geq1}\hbar^j C_j(f,g)$ with $(f\star g-g\star f)/(i\hbar)$ equal to the Poisson bracket to leading order. Classification is relative to an equivalence notion, such as formal changes of variables preserving the classical limit.
Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Given a symplectic or Poisson manifold, how can all possible quantizations be classified, including deformation quantizations of its algebra of functions?
Source scope and qualification.
Formal, strict analytic, geometric and representation-theoretic quantizations are distinct. The broad question does not prescribe one equivalence relation for every such notion, and known formal classifications must not be mistaken for resolution of all requested quantizations.

Classify quantizations of symplectic and Poisson manifolds, including their deformation products.

Constant-Poisson construction.
On the $d$-torus let $\Pi$ be a fixed real antisymmetric $d\times d$ matrix and define $\{f,g\}=\sum_{a,b}\Pi_{ab}(\partial_af)(\partial_bg)$. For Fourier modes $e_k(x)=e^{ik\cdot x}$, $k\in\mathbb Z^d$, set formally \[ e_k\star e_l=\exp\left(-\frac{i\hbar}{2}k^T\Pi l\right)e_{k+l}. \] We work with formal power series in $\hbar$ whose coefficients are trigonometric polynomials, so every coefficient of a product is a finite sum. Bilinearity gives \[ k^T\Pi l+(k+l)^T\Pi m =l^T\Pi m+k^T\Pi(l+m), \] which proves associativity on modes and hence on this algebra. The constant mode is a unit. Also \[ \frac{e_k\star e_l-e_l\star e_k}{i\hbar} =-k^T\Pi l\,e_{k+l}+O(\hbar^2) =\{e_k,e_l\}+O(\hbar^2). \] This verifies the required classical bracket directly, including degenerate $\Pi$.
Testing what is actually classified.
Let $D$ be a differential operator preserving trigonometric polynomials with $D1=0$, and let $T=I+\hbar D$. Its inverse exists formally as $\sum_{j\geq0}(-\hbar D)^j$. Transport the product by $f\star' g=T^{-1}((Tf)\star(Tg))$. Associativity and the unit are preserved. If $C_1$ is the first coefficient of $\star$, expansion gives \[ C'_1(f,g)=C_1(f,g)+(Df)g+f(Dg)-D(fg). \] The added expression is symmetric in $f,g$, so the antisymmetric first-order bracket cannot distinguish these products. For example, $D=a\partial_1^2$ gives the change $-2a(\partial_1f)(\partial_1g)$ for any real $a$. Infinitely many displayed coefficient formulas can thus represent the same formal equivalence class.
First obstruction to a classification.
The construction supplies one family on a constant-Poisson torus and exhibits explicit redundancy; it does not show every deformation is equivalent to one of these products. Nor does a formal inverse or formal exponential establish convergence at nonzero $\hbar$, a norm completion, positivity of states, or a classification of representations. The broader source question distinguishes formal, strict analytic, geometric and representation-theoretic quantizations. A classification statement needs a chosen category and equivalence relation before an invariant can be proved complete. No assertion that the established formal theory in [S3] is unresolved is intended.
