$L^2$-Cohomology of Strongly Centered Monopole Moduli Spaces

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A finite-energy monopole on $\mathbb R^3$ consists of a connection $A$ and Higgs field $\Phi$ for the gauge group, in the Bogomolny/monopole setting of the source. Framed moduli divide by gauge transformations tending to the identity at infinity. Magnetic charge is determined by the asymptotic Higgs field: one positive integer $k$ for $SU(2)$, or a vector of $N-1$ charges for maximally broken $SU(N)$. The moduli metric is the $L^2$ inner product on infinitesimal field variations perpendicular to gauge directions. The strongly centered $SU(2)$ moduli space removes the center-of-mass translation and total-phase degrees of freedom, with the cover/discrete quotient fixed in the Sen-conjecture convention. Its reduced $L^2$ cohomology is represented by square-integrable harmonic differential forms for this metric. The charge and residual cyclic-group character sectors are relevant to the dimension question, not merely the dimension of ordinary topological cohomology.

A cutoff test for degree-zero square-integrable cohomology.
Let $(M,g)$ be a connected complete Riemannian manifold with smooth or Lipschitz cutoffs $\chi_R$, equal to one on a radius-$R$ ball, compactly supported, and $|d\chi_R|\leq C/R$. Suppose a smooth function $u\in L^2(M)$ is harmonic. Multiplying $\Delta u=0$ by $\chi_R^2\overline u$, integrating by parts and taking real parts gives \[ \int\chi_R^2|du|^2 \leq2\left(\int\chi_R^2|du|^2\right)^{1/2} \left(\int|d\chi_R|^2|u|^2\right)^{1/2}. \] Hence the left side is at most $4C^2R^{-2}\|u\|_2^2$. Letting $R\to\infty$ forces $du=0$, so $u$ is constant. If $M$ has infinite volume, square integrability then forces $u=0$.
What the argument would give for monopoles.
Once completeness, the cutoff control and infinite volume of a chosen strongly centered component are established, this excludes degree-zero $L^2$ harmonic forms there. It does not compute the middle-degree forms relevant to the source. For a general differential form, the analogous integration includes connection/curvature structure, and no sign forcing vanishing has been obtained here.
