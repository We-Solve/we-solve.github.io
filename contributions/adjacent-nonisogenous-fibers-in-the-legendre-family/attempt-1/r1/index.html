Adjacent Legendre fibers: a moment proof of existence and the search-time gap

An elliptic curve over $\mathbb F_p$ is a nonsingular genus-one curve with a chosen rational point. Its rational-point count is $\#E(\mathbb F_p)$. The source seeks adjacent parameters in a nonconstant family with unequal point counts, corresponding to nonisogenous fibers over the stated finite field.

A concrete family and an actual attempted route

The original target concerns nonconstant elliptic families, with small degree and sufficiently large characteristic mentioned but not quantified in its remarks. We work first with the Legendre family $E_\lambda:y^2=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$ for odd primes $p\ge5$ and $\lambda\notin\{0,1\}$. These fibers are smooth because the cubic has three distinct roots. The goal of this route is to prove the point counts are not constant on the consecutive smooth parameters, then find an adjacent change. It does not give a general-family or bit-polynomial search theorem.
Elementary character identities.
Let $\chi$ be the quadratic character on $\mathbb F_p$, with $\chi(0)=0$. Nonzero squares form an index-two subgroup: the squaring map has exactly two preimages $\pm u$ for each square, and its image is closed under products and inverses. Assigning signs to its two cosets proves multiplicativity and $\sum_t\chi(t)=0$. For distinct $x,y$, \[ \sum_\lambda\chi((x-\lambda)(y-\lambda))=-1. \] Indeed, for $\lambda\ne y$ the summand equals $\chi((x-\lambda)/(y-\lambda))$, since the omitted factor is a nonzero square. The fractional transformation is a bijection from $\mathbb F_p\setminus\{y\}$ to $\mathbb F_p\setminus\{1\}$: solving its equation gives a unique $\lambda$ unless the ratio is one. The sum is therefore $-\chi(1)=-1$; the omitted summand was zero. The case $x=0,y=1$ also gives $\sum_x\chi(x(x-1))=-1$.
First and second moments with singular parameters removed.
Define for every $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p$, $S_\lambda=\sum_x\chi(x(x-1)(x-\lambda))$. For smooth fibers the number of affine $y$ values above each $x$ is $1+\chi(x(x-1)(x-\lambda))$, and there is one point at infinity. Hence $\#E_\lambda(\mathbb F_p)=p+1+S_\lambda$. Summing first over $\lambda$ gives $\sum_\lambda S_\lambda=0$, because $\sum_\lambda\chi(x-\lambda)=0$. At the two singular parameters, direct summation yields $S_0=-\chi(-1)$ and $S_1=-1$: cancel the square factor and subtract the omitted $x=0$ or $x=1$ term from a complete character sum. Consequently \[ \sum_{\lambda\ne0,1}S_\lambda=1+\chi(-1). \] Put $a_x=\chi(x(x-1))$. Then $\sum_xa_x=-1$ and $\sum_xa_x^2=p-2$. Expanding the second moment, the diagonal inner character sums equal $p-1$ and the off-diagonal ones equal $-1$ by the preceding identity. Therefore \[ \sum_\lambda S_\lambda^2 =(p-1)\sum_xa_x^2-\sum_{x\ne y}a_xa_y =p(p-2)-1. \] Removing $S_0^2=S_1^2=1$ gives \[ \sum_{\lambda\ne0,1}S_\lambda^2=(p-3)(p+1)>0. \]

If all smooth $S_\lambda$ were a common integer $c$, their first moment would give $(p-2)c=1+\chi(-1)$. If $\chi(-1)=1$, this is impossible since $p-2\ge3$ does not divide two. If $\chi(-1)=-1$, it forces $c=0$, contradicting the positive second moment. Thus the smooth counts are not all equal. The parameter list $2,3,\ldots,p-1$ is a connected chain under adding one, so some $\lambda\in\{2,\ldots,p-2\}$ has $S_\lambda\ne S_{\lambda+1}$. Both fibers are smooth, proving existence of a pair with the source's unequal-point-count criterion for every $p\ge5$.

Why this route still falls short.
One may precompute squares modulo $p$ and all character values with $O(p)$ field operations, then compute the $p$ sums in $O(p^2)$ operations and scan the adjacent pairs. This is deterministic but exponential in $\log p$; the moment proof does not locate the change in a set of only polynomially many bit-length candidates. Nor does nonconstancy alone ensure a useful rational-parameter family without the source's degree restriction. For example, replace the Legendre parameter by $\lambda(t)=t^p-t+2$. At every $t\in\mathbb F_p$ this equals two, so all finite rational fibers are the same smooth curve. Over the rational function field the family is nonconstant: its usual Legendre invariant $256(\lambda^2-\lambda+1)^3/[\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2]$ has a pole at a zero of $t^p-t+2$ and is not constant. The parameter polynomial has derivative $-1$, so such zeros exist and are simple over the algebraic closure. This is a degree-growing warning, not a counterexample to a fixed-small-degree hypothesis or an assertion that the fiber at infinity is smooth.

Audit and gap

The AIM Question 5 and its small-degree remark were inspected. The proof above uses unequal point counts, exactly the criterion in the source, without importing a new isogeny classification. The audit checked singular fibers, all odd primes at least five, character-sum signs and the difference between existence and efficient location. No point-count experiment or numerical search was run. The elementary Legendre moment result is a known-type special-case argument, and no new general-family or polynomial-in-logarithm algorithm is claimed.
