Popularity probability under uncertain house-allocation preferences: independent sets and #P-hardness

Abstract

For a perfect matching $M$, we show that popularity under a realised profile depends only on which applicants receive their top choice, and on the houses ranked above $M(a)$ by the others. This reduces $\pi_I(M)$ to a hard-core (independent-set) partition function. Consequences: (1) in the independent-lottery encoding, with two orders per applicant and probability $1/2$ each, $\pi_I(M)=i(G)/2^n$, where $i(G)$ counts independent sets of an arbitrary graph; (2) in the uniform-tie-refinement encoding, $\pi_I(M)$ is a nondegenerate two-spin partition function, which polynomial interpolation reduces to $i(G)$. Exact evaluation is therefore #P-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions in both compact encodings, and lies in $\mathrm{FP}^{\#\mathrm P}$. The threshold problem lies in PP and is #P-hard under Turing reductions. The explicit joint encoding is polynomial. As a by-product, approximation in the lottery encoding inherits the hard-core model's computational threshold at maximum degree $6$.

1. A characterisation for perfect matchings

Add a last-resort house at the end of each list, as in [aikm]. For a profile $P$, let $f(a)$ be $a$'s first choice, $F=\{f(a)\}$, and $s(a)$ the best house on $a$'s list outside $F$. By [aikm, Thm. 2.1], an applicant-complete matching is popular iff every $f$-house is matched and $M(a)\in\{f(a),s(a)\}$ for all $a$. Write $G_P=\{a:f(a)=M(a)\}$ for the top applicants, $T_P=M(G_P)$, and $\mathrm{Up}_a=\{h:h\succ_aM(a)\}$.
Lemma: Suppose $|A|=|H|$ and $M$ is perfect. Then $M$ is popular for $P$ iff $\mathrm{Up}_a\subseteq T_P$ for every $a\notin G_P$.
Proof: All houses are matched, so we need only $M(a)=s(a)$ for $a\notin G_P$. This holds iff $\mathrm{Up}_a\subseteq F$ and $M(a)\notin F$. ($\Leftarrow$) $T_P\subseteq F$, so $\mathrm{Up}_a\subseteq F$. Suppose $M(a)=f(b)$; then $b\ne a$. If $b\in G_P$, then $f(b)=M(b)\ne M(a)$. If $b\notin G_P$, then $f(b)\in\mathrm{Up}_b\subseteq T_P$, so $M(a)\in T_P$ and $a\in G_P$. Both cases are contradictions. ($\Rightarrow$) Let $h\in\mathrm{Up}_a\subseteq F$ and $h=M(c)$. If $c\notin G_P$, then $M(c)=s(c)\notin F$, a contradiction. Hence $h\in T_P$. ∎
Equivalently, let $S=A\setminus G_P$ and $O_a=M^{-1}(\mathrm{Up}_a)$. Then $M$ is popular iff $O_a\cap S=\varnothing$ for every $a\in S$.

2. Independent lotteries: independent sets

Given a graph $G=(V,E)$, create applicant $a_v$ and house $h_v$ for each $v$, and let $M=\{a_vh_v\}$. Applicant $a_v$ finds $\{h_v\}\cup\{h_u:u\in N(v)\}$ acceptable. With probability $1/2$ each, it uses order (top) $h_v\succ$ neighbours, or order (non-top) neighbours $\succ h_v$.
Theorem: $\pi_I(M)=i(G)/2^{|V|}$, where $i(G)$ is the number of independent sets of $G$.
Proof: Isolated vertices are always top. Otherwise, $a_v\in S$ iff it uses the non-top order, and then $O_{a_v}=N(v)$. By Lemma Lemma, $M$ is popular iff $S$ is independent. Each $S$ has probability $2^{-|V|}$. ∎
Counting independent sets is #P-complete [pb]. Hence evaluating $\pi_I$ in the independent-lottery encoding is #P-hard. The threshold problem "$\pi_I(M)\ge\tau$" is in PP: scale by the product of denominators, and verify popularity for each profile choice in polynomial time [aikm]. It is #P-hard under Turing reductions, since binary search over $\tau$ recovers $i(G)$. It is therefore not in P unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{PP}$.
Corollary (Approximation): In this encoding, with $\deg(v)+1$ acceptable houses per applicant, approximating $\pi_I(M)$ is approximating the hard-core partition function at activity $\lambda=1$. An FPTAS exists when every list has length at most $6$ (maximum degree $\le5$) [weitz]. For lists of length $7$ there is no FPRAS unless $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}$ [sly], because $\lambda=1$ exceeds the uniqueness threshold $\lambda_c(6)=5^5/4^6$.

3. Uniform refinements of weak orders

Given a multigraph $G$, create for each vertex $v$ an applicant $a_v$ with tie $[h_v\sim g_v]$, and a dummy $z_v$ whose only acceptable house is $g_v$. For each edge $e=uv$, create an applicant $a_e$ with tie $[h_u\sim h_v\sim h_e]$. Let $M$ match $a_v h_v$, $z_v g_v$ and $a_e h_e$.
Lemma: $\pi_I(M)=2^{-|V|}\sum_{X\in\{0,1\}^V}\prod_{uv\in E}w(X_u,X_v)$, where $X_v=1$ means $a_v$ is top, and $w(0,0)=1/3$, $w(0,1)=w(1,0)=1/2$, $w(1,1)=1$.
Proof: Each $z_v$ is always top. If $a_v$ is not top, $O_{a_v}=\{z_v\}$, so it imposes no constraint. The applicant $a_e$ is top with probability $1/3$. Otherwise $\mathrm{Up}_{a_e}$ is $\{h_u\}$, $\{h_v\}$ or $\{h_u,h_v\}$ with probabilities $1/6,1/6,1/3$. It requires the corresponding vertex applicants to be top. Refinements are independent, and the formula follows from Lemma Lemma. ∎
Theorem: Evaluating $\pi_I$ in the uniform-refinement encoding is #P-hard under Turing reductions.
Proof: Replace each edge of a simple graph $G$ by $k$ parallel edges, giving $G^{(k)}$. With $S=\{X=0\}$ and $T=\{X=1\}$, \[ 2^{|V|}\,2^{k|E|}\ \pi_I^{(k)}(M)=\sum_{a,b}N(a,b)\,c_{ab}^{\,k},\qquad c_{ab}=(2/3)^a2^b. \] This follows because an edge contributes $(1/3)^k$, $(1/2)^k$ or $1$ according to whether it lies inside $S$, crosses, or lies inside $T$. Here $N(a,b)$ counts assignments with $a$ edges of $G$ inside $S$ and $b$ inside $T$. The numbers $c_{ab}=2^{a+b}3^{-a}$ are distinct for distinct $(a,b)$. Evaluating at $k=1,\dots,(|E|+1)^2$ and solving the Vandermonde system recovers every $N(a,b)$. Then $i(G)=\sum_bN(0,b)$, because $S$ must be independent. ∎

4. Explicit joint lotteries and verification

For an explicit list of profiles, sum the weights of the support profiles at which $M$ passes the polynomial popularity test [aikm]. Both problems are then in P. We verified Theorem Theorem and the two-spin formula by brute force on small graphs, where popularity was checked against all matchings rather than through the characterisation. Lottery encoding: five random graphs on $3$--$4$ vertices; for example $i=10,8,4,6$ matched exactly. Tie encoding: four graphs; for example $\pi=53/144$, $55/216$ and $7/12$ matched exactly.

What remains

(i) Whether the threshold problem is PP-complete under many-one reductions. (ii) The complexity when $M$ is not perfect. Unmatched houses then add the constraint that no first choice is unmatched, which is again a hard-core-type event. (iii) Natural bounded restrictions in the tie encoding: hardness above uses ties of size $3$ and unbounded multiplicity.

Completion Estimate

86%

This is a post hoc, heuristic estimate for the full catalogue target. Independent-set and two-spin reductions establish compact-encoding exact counting hardness, while threshold upper bounds and joint-support evaluation cover all three representations. Tight threshold classifications and some approximation and nonperfect-matching claims remain incomplete beyond these strong hardness results.
