$\mathbb T^d=\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$ is the flat periodic torus, $d\ge1$, and $n(x,t)$ is a nonnegative particle density. $\nabla$, $\operatorname{div}$, and $\Delta$ are spatial gradient, divergence, and Laplacian. Linearization tests the maximum-principle route.
Around a positive constant $n_*>0$, put $n=n_*+\epsilon u$. Expanding $\sqrt n=\sqrt{n_*}+\epsilon u/(2\sqrt{n_*})+O(\epsilon^2)$ in a smooth norm gives $n\nabla(\Delta\sqrt n/\sqrt n)=\epsilon\nabla\Delta u/2+O(\epsilon^2)$. The linearized equation is therefore $u_t=-\Delta^2u/2$.
The linear semigroup does not preserve positivity.
On the period-one circle take $u_0(x)=(1-\cos(2\pi x))^2\geq0$. At $x=0$, $u_0=0$ and $\partial_x^4u_0(0)=6(2\pi)^4$, so the exact finite-Fourier-mode solution has $\partial_tu(0,0)=-3(2\pi)^4<0$. Consequently it is negative at that point for all sufficiently small positive times. This follows from ordinary Taylor expansion in time of its finite sum of decaying Fourier modes.
Why the nonlinear problem is different.
Linearization around $n_*>0$ describes small signed perturbations of a positive background. Sending the background to zero invalidates the expansion, so the preceding sign change is not a counterexample to nonnegative nonlinear weak solutions. It does prove that a second-order maximum-principle argument cannot be imported merely from the principal fourth-order symbol. The first gap is an estimate controlling the vacuum set in the nonlinear entropy solution class. Positive total mass and the distinction between pointwise and almost-everywhere positivity remain necessary; zero initial mass is the trivial excluded boundary case.
