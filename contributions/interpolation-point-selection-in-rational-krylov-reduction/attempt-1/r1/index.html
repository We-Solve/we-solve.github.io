Interpolation-Point Selection in Rational Krylov Reduction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For a linear system with state matrix $A$, rational Krylov spaces use shifted resolvents such as $(A-\sigma I)^{-1}b$. Projection reduces the state to a subspace; interpolation points are the shifts or transfer-function frequencies used to construct that subspace.

A two-point rational Krylov interpolation that loses stability

The source leaves the desired properties unspecified and discusses projection spaces built from resolvents. We select one concrete desired property, asymptotic stability, and test whether interpolation at positive real points guarantees it. Consider \[ A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\3\end{pmatrix},\quad C=(1,0),\quad D=0, \quad V=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix},\quad W^T=(1/2,1/2). \] The right interpolation point is 2 and the left one is 3.
Proposition: These vectors form the report's one-dimensional two-sided rational Krylov projection. The full system is asymptotically stable; its reduced system is unstable, although the transfer functions agree at both chosen interpolation points.
Proof: Direct inversion gives $(2I-A)^{-1}B=(1,1)^T=V$ and $C(3I-A)^{-1}=(1/4,1/4)=\overline W^T$. Since $\overline W^TV=1/2$, the normalization in the source gives $W^T=2\overline W^T$, and $W^TV=1$. Projection yields $\widehat A=W^TAV=1$, $\widehat B=W^TB=1$, and $\widehat C=CV=1$. The full state matrix has both eigenvalues $-1$ and $e^{At}=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&4t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, which tends to zero. Its transfer function is \[ H(s)=C(sI-A)^{-1}B=\frac{11-s}{(s+1)^2}, \qquad \widehat H(s)=\frac1{s-1}. \] Both equal 1 at $s=2$ and $1/2$ at $s=3$. The reduced zero-input state evolves as $e^t\widehat x(0)$ and is unstable. Neither chosen point is a pole of either transfer function, so the interpolation equalities are well-defined. ∎
The attempted inference that good interpolation-point placement alone automatically preserves stability therefore fails for this permitted construction. The original controllability and observability assumptions do not rescue this example: $[B,AB]$ has determinant $-36$, and the observability matrix with rows $C,CA$ has determinant 4, both nonzero. Additional restrictions on the projection, the original system or the selected objective are needed.
