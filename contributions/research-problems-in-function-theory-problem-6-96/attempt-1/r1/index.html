Claim: literature report

Summary of a claim published in openai/math; not a claim by this platform.

The proposed spectrum formula requires $B(-1)=1/4$. The inverse-first-power theorem asserts an upper growth exponent strictly below $1/4$, uniformly over normalized univalent maps, and hence also for bounded images. Rescaling a bounded map into the disk changes an integral mean by a constant factor and preserves its limiting exponent. Thus this result contradicts the spectrum identity in the precise disk-to-disk class recorded here. One failed parameter is enough to disprove the proposed universal identity; the theorem does not compute the exact remaining spectrum values. The Brennan paper provides a separate inverse-square result.

Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.96

OpenAI's claim

Inverse first-power means: OpenAI announces a universal growth exponent below $1/4$ for circular means of the inverse derivative of normalized injective holomorphic disk maps; applied to bounded maps, this gives a strict inequality at the spectrum parameter $-1$, contrary to Kraetzer’s proposed value.

Brennan integrability: The announced integrability range for a conformal map onto the disk is $4/3<s<4$ for the area integral of the $s$th derivative power; the paper also identifies the inverse-square integral-means exponent for the schlicht class as one.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Write $\mathcal S=\{f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C:\ f\text{ holomorphic and injective},\ f(0)=0,\ f^\prime(0)=1\}$ and $M_p(f,r)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^\prime(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta$. The claim is that some $0<\epsilon<1/4$ and finite $C$, independent of $f$ and $r$, satisfy \[M_{-1}(f,r)\le C(1-r)^{-1/4+\epsilon}\quad(f\in\mathcal S,\ 1/2\le r<1).\] In particular the bounded-image subclass has spectrum $B_b(-1)=\sup_f\limsup_{r\uparrow1}\log M_{-1}(f,r)/\log(1/(1-r))<1/4$. The theorem bounds this exponent; it does not determine its exact value.
Mathematical claim 2.
For every $\epsilon>0$ there is a finite $C_\epsilon$ such that all injective holomorphic $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$ and $f^\prime(0)=1$ satisfy \[\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^\prime(re^{i\theta})|^{-2}\,d\theta\le C_\epsilon(1-r)^{-1-\epsilon}\quad(1/2\le r<1).\] The supremum over these maps of the limiting logarithmic growth exponent is exactly $1$. If $W\subset\mathbb C$ is simply connected, its spherical boundary has at least two points, and $\varphi:W\to\mathbb D$ is conformal and bijective, then \[\int_W|\varphi^\prime|^s\,dA<\infty\quad(4/3<s<4).\] Equivalently $\int_{\mathbb D}|f^\prime|^t\,dA<\infty$ for every injective holomorphic $f$ and $-2<t<2/3$. These are strict intervals, with no endpoint-integrability assertion. The sharp-endpoint corollary exhibits the Koebe map $k(z)=z/(1-z)^2$, for which $\int_{\mathbb D}|k^\prime|^{-2}\,dA=\int_{\mathbb D}|k^\prime|^{2/3}\,dA=\infty$, so both Brennan endpoints fail for its inverse.

Outline of the argument

Inverse first-power means: Affine rebasings of upper-half-plane maps define a positive averaging operator. Hypothetically excessive growth produces a scale-compatible weighted probability law and identities for logarithmic-derivative moments. Critical points of a target potential are paired geometrically; their signed Jacobians have an incompatible expected sign, and the equality case is separately eliminated.

Brennan integrability: The proof represents inverse-square derivative growth by a positive operator acting on normalized half-plane maps. An eigenmeasure is averaged over translations and scales to produce a weighted rebasing law. Moment identities from this law are compared with a pairing of critical points of target potentials, ruling out any growth exponent larger than one; change of variables then gives the stated derivative integrability.
