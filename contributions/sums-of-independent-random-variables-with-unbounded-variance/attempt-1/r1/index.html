Sums of independent random variables with unbounded variance

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $n\ge1$, $\mu>0$ and $\delta>0$. Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent nonnegative real random variables on a probability space, with finite expectations satisfying $\mathbb E X_i\le\mu$ for each $i$. They need not have identical distributions, and their variances may be infinite. Write $S=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$, so $\mathbb E S=\sum_i\mathbb E X_i$ is finite.

The conjectured uniform lower bound is \[ \mathbb P(S-\mathbb E S<\delta\mu) \ge \min\left\{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta},\ e^{-1}\right\}. \] The event uses a strict inequality, which matters when the variables have atoms. The centering is the actual expectation of the sum, rather than the upper bound $n\mu$. The proposed right-hand side depends only on $\delta$, independently of $n$, $\mu$, and all the distributions. Dividing each $X_i$ by $\mu$ gives the equivalent normalization $\mu=1$; requiring $\mu>0$ avoids a degenerate strict event at zero. Establish the inequality for every admissible family and every $\delta>0$, or give a fully specified independent family and parameter violating it. An assumption of bounded variance or bounded support would restrict the stated problem.

Short English statement

How small can the probability be that a sum of independent nonnegative variables lies less than a prescribed amount above its own mean?

Research attempt

Normalize by the supplied $\mu>0$, so each mean is at most one and the desired event is $S-\mathbb ES<\delta$. The attempted proof route was to replace each marginal distribution by two conditional means on the two sides of its individual threshold. Such a replacement preserves that marginal's expectation and threshold probability. It does not preserve, or decrease, the lower-tail probability of the sum; the exact counterexample below blocks this route to an extremal reduction.
Proposition: There are independent mean-one nonnegative $X_1,X_2$ and independent mean-one two-point $Y_1,Y_2$ for which \[ \mathbb P(X_i<2)=\mathbb P(Y_i<2)=2/3\quad(i=1,2), \] but \[ \mathbb P(X_1+X_2<3)=2/3<8/9= \mathbb P(Y_1+Y_2<3). \] Each $Y_i$ is obtained from $X_i$ by replacing the parts below and at least two by their conditional means.
Proof: Take each $X_i$ uniform on $\{0,1,2\}$, independently. Its expectation is one; below two its conditional mean is $1/2$, and at least two its conditional mean is two. Thus $Y_i$ is $1/2$ with probability $2/3$ and two with probability $1/3$, also with expectation one. The ordered pairs of $X$ values with sum below three are $(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)$, six of nine equally likely pairs. For $Y$, every pair except $(2,2)$ has sum below three, with total probability $1-(1/3)^2=8/9$. These calculations prove every assertion. ∎

At $n=2$ and $\delta=1$, the global threshold in the original event is exactly three, whereas the individual thresholds are two. A valid minimization argument would require replacing marginals without increasing the global lower tail, so the direction in this example is fatal to the proposed step. It does not disprove the conjecture, and it does not refute other rigorously established reductions to two-point laws.

An elementary case and sharpness tests.
For $n=1$, write $a=\mathbb EX\le1$. Nonnegativity gives \[ a\ge(a+\delta)\mathbb P(X\ge a+\delta), \quad\text{so}\quad \mathbb P(X<a+\delta)\ge\frac\delta{a+\delta} \ge\frac\delta{1+\delta}. \] This proves the full one-variable case, including atoms at the threshold. If $a=0$, nonnegativity implies $X=0$ almost surely, and the event has probability one.

For every $\delta>0$, the mean-one law taking $1+\delta$ with probability $1/(1+\delta)$ and zero otherwise has lower-tail probability $\delta/(1+\delta)$. Adding deterministic variables equal to one gives the same value at every larger $n$. Alternatively let all $n$ independent variables take $n+\delta$ with probability $1/(n+\delta)$ and zero otherwise. Their means are one; the strict event holds exactly when all vanish, with probability $(1-1/(n+\delta))^n\to e^{-1}$. Thus both branches of the proposed dimension-free constant are genuine upper constraints on a universal lower bound. These standard examples do not establish that no worse law exists.

Recent primary sources were also found. Fu, Han, Wang, Yan, Zhang and Zhou, arXiv:2607.23980v1, Theorem 1.1, prove a sharp finite-$n$ bound for $\delta\ge1$ and a weaker bound for $0<\delta<1$; its displayed theorem and scope discussion were inspected. The theorem statements and surrounding scope were inspected; the entire proof was not audited here. Verification and remaining gaps

Exact rational enumeration searched $9261$ independent three-variable two-point product laws for each $\delta\in\{1/4,1/2,3/4,1,2\}$: each variable took height $h\in\{1,5/4,\ldots,6\}$ with probability $1/h$, and zero otherwise. All $2^3$ outcomes were evaluated with the strict inequality. The minima were, respectively, \[ 1/5,\quad1/3,\quad1331/3375,\quad27/64,\quad64/125. \] No value below the conjectured bound occurred in this finite grid. This does not exhaust arbitrary heights, nonzero lower support points, larger $n$, or infinite-variance laws.

The self-audit kept the strict threshold, actual expectation centering and the direction of the extremal reduction. The first gap in this attempt is a valid global extremal argument for general independent marginals; the failed compression cannot supply it. External papers may address that gap, but their full proofs and publication acceptance have not been independently checked here.
