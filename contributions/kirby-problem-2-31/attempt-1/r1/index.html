Exact rational stable commutator length on a two-generator retract of every surface group

**Problem:** prb_950ef52a14fd4d36b1703d3470b0a85d

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For every closed oriented surface group of genus≥2, every element in the commutator subgroup of the explicit retract generated by its first handle has rational scl equal to its free-group scl; equality holds already for each finite commutator length.

The named element regime

Write the closed surface group as

$$G=\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g\mid\prod_{i=1}^g[a_i,b_i]=1\rangle,\qquad g\ge2.$$

Let $H$ be the subgroup generated by $a_1,b_1$. For every word $w\in[H,H]$, its stable commutator length computed in the whole surface group is rational. It equals the stable commutator length in the free group on those two generators. The allowed words have arbitrary length and need not be single commutators.

The distinction “computed in the whole group” is essential in the prompt. A subgroup inclusion alone gives only one inequality; new commutator factorizations using the other handles could, in principle, shorten the length. The retract below supplies the reverse inequality.

Build and verify the retraction

Let $F=\langle A,B\rangle$ be the abstract free group. Define a map from the surface generators to $F$ by

$$a_1\mapsto A,\quad b_1\mapsto B,\quad
a_2\mapsto B,\quad b_2\mapsto A,$$

and send all remaining generators to the identity. The surface relator maps to $[A,B][B,A]=1$. Thus this defines a homomorphism $r:G\to F$.

There is also a homomorphism $j:F\to G$ taking $A,B$ to $a_1,b_1$. The composite $rj$ is the identity on the free generators, hence on $F$. Therefore $j$ is injective, its image is exactly $H$, and $r$ is a retraction after this identification. This simultaneously proves that the designated subgroup really is free and that the required reverse map exists.

Equality of the lengths before taking limits

Any product of $k$ commutators in $F$ maps under $j$ to a product of $k$ commutators in $G$. Hence $\operatorname{cl}_G(jw)\le\operatorname{cl}_F(w)$. Conversely any commutator decomposition of $jw$ in $G$ maps under $r$ to one of $w$ in $F$ of the same or shorter length. Thus

$$\operatorname{cl}_G(jw)=\operatorname{cl}_F(w).$$

Apply this argument to $w^n$ for every $n\ge1$, divide by $n$, and take the subadditive limits. It follows that

$$\operatorname{scl}_G(jw)=\operatorname{scl}_F(w).$$

Calegari’s Rationality Theorem proves that the right-hand value is rational for every $w\in[F,F]$. His theorem also supplies extremal surfaces and a rational linear-programming framework; this is an exact existence result, not a limit inferred from a finite list of rational approximations.

Scope and algorithmic boundary

The report gives a genuine infinite element class inside every genus-$g$ group, together with a reason that allowing all ambient commutators cannot change its answer. Conjugating the subgroup or transporting it by an automorphism of $G$ gives the same conclusion, since commutator length is preserved by conjugation and automorphisms.

An arbitrary free subgroup of a surface group need not be supplied with such a retraction. Nor does an arbitrary homologically trivial surface word lie in the commutator subgroup of this first-handle retract. The report therefore does not claim rationality for every element of $[G,G]$. It does not equate rationality of all finite ratios with rationality of their infimum. The universal step in this regime is the exact equality for every finite commutator length plus the precisely credited free-group theorem.
