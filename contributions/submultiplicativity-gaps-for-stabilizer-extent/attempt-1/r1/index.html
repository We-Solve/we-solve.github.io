Stabilizer extent: a measurement bound and the failed amplification inference

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a normalized pure $n$-qubit state $\psi$, its stabilizer extent is $\xi(\psi)=\min\{(\sum_j|c_j|)^2:\psi=\sum_jc_j\phi_j,\ \phi_j\text{ normalized stabilizer states}\}$. A stabilizer state is a common eigenstate of a maximal commuting subgroup of the qubit Pauli group. Product decompositions give $\xi(\phi\otimes\psi)\leq\xi(\phi)\xi(\psi)$. The gap may be described additively or by the ratio $\xi(\phi)\xi(\psi)/\xi(\phi\otimes\psi)$.

Atomic norm and the measurement closure lemma

For an arbitrary vector $v$ on $n$ qubits define \[ a_n(v)=\inf\left\{\sum_j|c_j|:v=\sum_j c_jS_j, \ S_j\text{ normalized stabilizer states}\right\}. \] The computational basis makes the feasible set nonempty. The triangle inequality for the Euclidean norm and then Cauchy--Schwarz in a computational-basis expansion give \[ \|v\|_2\le a_n(v)\le2^{n/2}\|v\|_2. \] Combining expansions proves the triangle inequality; scaling coefficients and then scaling back proves $a_n(zv)=|z|a_n(v)$. For normalized $v$, $a_n(v)^2=\xi(v)$. Taking infima suffices throughout, so attainment is not needed.

We will use the following closure fact: measuring any subset of qubits of a normalized stabilizer state in the computational basis leaves, on every nonzero branch, a scalar times a normalized stabilizer state on the other qubits. Here is a full algebraic proof. An $N$-qubit stabilizer state has a group $G$ of $2^N$ commuting Hermitian Pauli operators fixing it and not containing $-I$. The average $2^{-N}\sum_{g\in G}g$ is a projector: squaring it counts each group element $2^N$ times, and it is Hermitian. Its trace is one because only $I$ has nonzero trace. Thus its range is the one-dimensional stabilized space. The same argument shows that a commuting Pauli group of size $2^{N+1}$ with no $-I$ on $N$ qubits is impossible, because its average would be a projector of trace $1/2$.

Measure the Pauli $Z$ of one qubit. If $Z$ or $-Z$ belongs to $G$, the outcome is deterministic. Otherwise some generator anticommutes with $Z$: if all commuted, adjoining $Z$ would give the impossible larger group just described. Choose such a generator $g_1$. By multiplying any other anticommuting generator by $g_1$, take the remaining $N-1$ independent generators to commute with $Z$. For either outcome sign $z\in\{-1,1\}$, replace $g_1$ by $zZ$. These $N$ generators are independent: a relation involving $Z$ would place $\pm Z$ in $G$, and a relation without it contradicts the original independence. They commute. The projected vector is nonzero because $\langle Z\rangle=\langle g_1Zg_1\rangle=-\langle Z\rangle=0$, so each outcome has probability $1/2$, and the new generators fix that vector. Thus they cannot generate $-I$.

In either case choose $zZ$ as one generator. Every other generator commutes with $Z$ and hence has only $I$ or $Z$ on this qubit; multiplying by $zZ$ removes any $Z$ there. Restricting these $N-1$ independent generators to the remaining qubits preserves independence and the absence of $-I$. Their unique stabilized vector is the residual state. The measured qubit is fixed to its computational basis outcome. Iterating proves the stated closure lemma. For a normalized joint stabilizer $S_j$, write its branch vectors as $\alpha_{jx}s_{jx}$; orthogonality of measurement outcomes gives $\sum_x|\alpha_{jx}|^2=1$.

A complete non-sharp bound on both gap conventions

Let $\phi,\psi$ be normalized $n$- and $m$-qubit states. Product expansions into stabilizers are stabilizer expansions, since the tensor products of the two independent generator groups have the required number of commuting independent generators. Taking infima proves the usual upper bound. For the lower bound, start with any joint expansion \[ \phi\otimes\psi=\sum_j c_jS_j. \] Measure the last $m$ qubits. The outcome $x$ gives $\psi_x\phi=\sum_jc_j\alpha_{jx}s_{jx}$, so \[ |\psi_x|a_n(\phi)\le\sum_j|c_j||\alpha_{jx}|. \] Square and sum over outcomes. Normalization of $\psi$ and Cauchy--Schwarz for each pair of branch-amplitude vectors imply \[ \xi(\phi)\le \sum_{i,j}|c_ic_j|\sum_x|\alpha_{ix}||\alpha_{jx}| \le\left(\sum_j|c_j|\right)^2. \] Infimize and exchange the factors to obtain \[ \max\{\xi(\phi),\xi(\psi)\} \le\xi(\phi\otimes\psi)\le\xi(\phi)\xi(\psi). \] The norm bounds above give $1\le\xi(\phi)\le2^n$ and $1\le\xi(\psi)\le2^m$. Consequently the multiplicative ratio and additive difference satisfy \[ 1\le\frac{\xi(\phi)\xi(\psi)}{\xi(\phi\otimes\psi)} \le\min\{\xi(\phi),\xi(\psi)\}\le2^{\min(n,m)}, \] \[ 0\le\xi(\phi)\xi(\psi)-\xi(\phi\otimes\psi) \le2^{n+m}-2^{\max(n,m)}. \] For the last inequality, $ab-\max(a,b)$ is nondecreasing in each variable for $a,b\ge1$: on either side of $a=b$ its slope in either variable is either the other variable or the other variable minus one. Substitute $a\le2^n,b\le2^m$. If one factor is a stabilizer, its extent is one by the one-term expansion and the norm lower bound, so the two-sided inequality proves exact equality of the product extent with the other extent. The statements include a zero-qubit scalar factor, with extent one.

Why a strict example does not amplify automatically

A natural attempted route to a large gap takes tensor powers of a known strictly submultiplicative pair. Write \[ A_t=\xi(\phi^{\otimes t}),\quad B_t=\xi(\psi^{\otimes t}),\quad C_t=\xi((\phi\otimes\psi)^{\otimes t}). \] Submultiplicativity gives $A_t\le A_1^t$, $B_t\le B_1^t$ and $C_t\le C_1^t$. The numerator estimates are upper bounds, so they do not imply $A_tB_t/C_t\ge(A_1B_1/C_1)^t$. For an exact check of the logical failure, the abstract constant sequences $A_t=B_t=2$, $C_t=3$ obey all these submultiplicative inequalities, monotonicity in $t$, and $\max(A_t,B_t)\le C_t\le A_tB_t$, but their ratio is always $4/3$. These sequences are not claimed to be realized by states; they show that the displayed inequalities alone do not amplify a strict gap. Additional control of the factor tensor powers or an explicit growing family is missing.
