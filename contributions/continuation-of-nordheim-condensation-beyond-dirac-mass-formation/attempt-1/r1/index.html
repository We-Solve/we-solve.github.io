Continuation of Nordheim Condensation Beyond Dirac-Mass Formation

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

The Nordheim equation describes a spatially homogeneous Bose gas through an energy distribution. The energy variable is $\omega\geq0$. Condensation corresponds to a measure with an atom at zero energy; $n(t)$ is its mass. In the source normalization the particle measure uses the weight $\sqrt\omega\,d\omega$, so the expression $\delta_0/\sqrt\omega$ is a symbolic way to describe that atom, not ordinary multiplication of distributions.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
After finite-time formation of a Dirac mass at $\omega=0$ in the Nordheim equation, determine whether the solution can be continued as $f(t,\omega)=n(t)\delta_0/\sqrt\omega+h(t,\omega)$ with $h(t,\cdot)$ a function.
Scope and source qualification.
A measure-valued weak formulation is necessary at the atom. Conservation laws, positivity and any uniqueness claim must be justified in that formulation.

Short English statement

Determine whether the Nordheim evolution can continue after condensation as a zero-energy atom plus a regular energy-density component.

Testing continuation through conserved moments.
Work with the particle measure $G=n\delta_0+\sqrt\omega h(\omega)\,d\omega$, rather than multiplying the atom by an undefined reciprocal square root. Fix particle number $M>0$ and energy $E>0$. For any $0\leq n<M$, set $a=(M-n)/E$ and \[ g_n(\omega)=(M-n)a e^{-a\omega},\qquad G_n=n\delta_0+g_n(\omega)\,d\omega. \] Integration gives $\int dG_n=M$ and $\int\omega\,dG_n=E$. The corresponding regular occupation is $h_n=g_n/\sqrt\omega$ on $\omega>0$; its singularity at zero is locally integrable even without the particle weight. Thus conservation, positivity and an atom-plus-function decomposition allow every value of the atom mass below $M$.
Failure of moment closure.
Any differentiable path $n(t)\in[0,M)$ inserted in this formula preserves these two moments. Paths with increasing, decreasing or constant condensate mass all pass this test. They are not asserted to solve the Nordheim equation: the calculation disproves the attempted inference of a condensate evolution law from conserved moments alone. At $E=0$, by contrast, a positive measure supported on $[0,\infty)$ with zero first moment is supported at zero, so $G=M\delta_0$.
