Research attempt: A finite triangle-free certificate with an explicit stability bound

An exact local-to-global certificate.
Let $G$ be a finite simple triangle-free graph with $n\geq1$ vertices and $m$ edges. Write $d_v$ for vertex degrees. For an edge $uv$, the neighborhoods $N(u)$ and $N(v)$ are disjoint: a vertex in both would make a triangle. Thus $d_u+d_v\leq n$. Summing over edges counts $d_v$ exactly $d_v$ times, so \[ \sum_v d_v^2\leq nm. \] Also $\sum_vd_v=2m$ and $0\leq\sum_v(d_v-2m/n)^2=\sum_vd_v^2-4m^2/n$. If $m>0$, the two inequalities imply $m\leq n^2/4$; if $m=0$ this is immediate. Integrality gives $m\leq\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$. A complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes $\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil$ is triangle-free and has exactly that many edges. This proves the sharp bound for this specified auxiliary problem.
Extracting stability rather than only an extremal number.
Assume $m\geq n^2/4-t$ for some real $t\geq0$. Choose a maximum-degree vertex $v$, write $a=d_v$, set $A=N(v)$ and $B=V(G)\setminus A$. The set $A$ is independent. Let $e_B$ count edges inside $B$ and $e_{AB}$ count cross edges. Every vertex in $B$ has degree at most $a$, so \[ e_{AB}+2e_B=\sum_{u\in B}d_u\leq a(n-a). \] Since $m=e_{AB}+e_B$, it follows that \[ m\leq a(n-a)-e_B\leq n^2/4-e_B. \] Hence $e_B\leq t$ and $(a-n/2)^2\leq t$. The number of missing cross edges is \[ a(n-a)-e_{AB}=a(n-a)-m+e_B\leq t+e_B\leq2t. \] Deleting all $e_B$ internal edges and adding the missing cross edges changes at most $3t$ edges and produces the complete bipartite graph on $(A,B)$, whose two part sizes differ by at most $2\sqrt t$. All of this follows from explicitly summed local inequalities and the displayed square identity; there is no reliance on a numerical SDP output.
A precise limitation of the route.
The local inequality $d_u+d_v\leq n$ uses triangle-freeness itself. It cannot be reused just because some other graph is forbidden: the complete graph on three vertices has an edge with degree sum $4>3$, while it avoids every forbidden graph having at least four vertices. Likewise an upper bound obtained from a relaxation does not automatically yield a stability statement; the argument above required choosing an actual vertex partition and quantifying both its internal edges and missing cross edges. No such partition follows merely from the abstract nesting $\Phi_{k+1}\subseteq\Phi_k$ in the source.

Verification and adversarial audit

The proof separates $m=0$ before division. The stability argument remains valid for an edgeless graph: one may take $a=0$, and its hypothesis then forces $t\geq n^2/4$, making the part-size inequality valid. For odd $n$ the bound $t=0$ is unattainable, so no false equality assertion is made. The complete bipartite examples attain the rounded extremal bound.
