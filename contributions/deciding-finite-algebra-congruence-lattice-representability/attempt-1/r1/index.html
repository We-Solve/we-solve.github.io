Deciding finite-algebra congruence-lattice representability

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents a counterexample in the selected manuscript. A colored-graph criterion is claimed to characterize realizable finite congruence lattices, with an undecidable recognition problem; recognition of finite subgroup intervals is also claimed to be undecidable.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
For a nonempty finite lattice $L$, let $\mathcal R(L)$ mean that $L\cong\operatorname{Con}(A)$ for some finite algebra $A$. The claimed finite colored-graph criterion is equivalent to $\mathcal R(L)$, with a finite decision procedure once the candidate graph size is fixed. Nevertheless, \[ \text{no total algorithm decides }\mathcal R(L)\text{ from the order table of }L. \] The source also specifies a nonrepresentable lattice $L_0$ of least cardinality, with $|L_0|>7$ and every smaller finite lattice representable. Its group-interval assertion is \[ \text{no total algorithm decides }\exists G\text{ finite},\ H\le G:\ L\cong[H,G]. \] The undecidability theorem additionally rules out a total computable bound, depending only on $|L|$, on the smallest carrier size among representable lattices.

Outline of the argument

Finite colored graphs encode the equivalences permitted by all operations of an algebra. Small witness configurations are assembled into lattice representation constraints, and arithmetic divisor obstructions rule out bounded algebraic realizations; a reduction turns the characterization into the claimed undecidability result.

Formal verification

Undecidability, nonrepresentation, and subgroup-interval conclusions are not contained in that selected graph equivalence. The listed Comparator files are FiniteCongruenceGraph.lean.
