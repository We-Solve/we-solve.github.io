Fourier obstructions to unequally phased single-trace motions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. A generalized choreography has $q_i(t)=q(t+\theta_i)$ on a common periodic curve, with phase shifts distinct modulo the period. They need not be equally spaced. A collision-free solution must satisfy the different mass-weighted equations at every shifted time.

A positivity obstruction for rich Fourier traces.
Let $q_i(t)=q(t+\theta_i)$, with distinct phases modulo $P$, positive masses $m_i$, and set $z_i=e^{2\pi i\theta_i/P}$, $M=\sum m_i$. If each Fourier coefficient $\widehat q_\ell$ for $1\le\ell\le n-1$ is nonzero, constancy of the center of mass forces $\sum_im_i z_i^\ell=0$ in that range. Conjugation gives the corresponding negative moments. Let $V$ be the square matrix $V_{ai}=z_i^a$, $0\le a<n$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(m_i)$. The moment equations say $VDV^*=MI$. Hence $M^{-1/2}VD^{1/2}$ is unitary. Its column norms give $nm_i=M$, so all masses are equal. Orthogonality of distinct columns gives \[\sum_{a=0}^{n-1}(\overline z_i z_j)^a=0. \] Since $z_i\ne z_j$, the geometric sum implies $(\overline z_i z_j)^n=1$. The phases therefore form a rotated regular n-gon.
Consequence and missing construction.
A genuinely unequal-phase choreography must omit at least one of these first $n-1$ Fourier harmonics, or evade one of the explicitly stated hypotheses. This is a necessary condition, not a nonexistence theorem for all smooth traces: a circle already lacks most harmonics. Conversely choosing sparse Fourier support satisfies only a center-of-mass compatibility condition and gives no force balance.
