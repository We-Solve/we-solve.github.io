Erdős Problem 77: the exponential rate of diagonal Ramsey numbers

1. Statement and scope

Let $R(k)$ be the least order forcing a monochromatic $K_k$ in every two-colouring of complete-graph edges. Does $R(k)^{1/k}$ converge, and to what value? We reconstruct the classical exponential bounds and examine exactly what the natural graph-product construction proves. The existence of the limit remains unresolved. More recent upper bounds on its limsup, recorded on the problem page, are not reconstructed here.

2. Elementary upper and lower bounds

For the asymmetric Ramsey numbers, \[ R(s,t)\le R(s-1,t)+R(s,t-1),\qquad R(1,t)=R(s,1)=1. \] To prove the recurrence, take a vertex in a colouring of the indicated order. Either its red neighbourhood has at least $R(s-1,t)$ vertices, or its blue neighbourhood has at least $R(s,t-1)$ vertices, because the two neighbourhood sizes sum to one less than the order. A homogeneous set there either already has the required size or gains one vertex by including the chosen vertex. Pascal's identity and induction give \[ R(s,t)\le\binom{s+t-2}{s-1},\qquad R(k)\le4^{k-1}. \] For the lower bound, take $N=\lfloor2^{k/2}\rfloor$ and colour edges independently and uniformly. The expected number of monochromatic $k$-sets is \[ 2\binom Nk2^{-\binom k2} \le2\left(\frac{e\sqrt2}{k}\right)^k<1\qquad(k\ge9). \] Here $\binom Nk\le(eN/k)^k$, following from $k!\ge(k/e)^k$. A nonnegative integer random variable with expectation below one is zero for some outcome. Thus $R(k)>N$, and integrality implies $R(k)>2^{k/2}$. Consequently \[ \sqrt2\le\liminf R(k)^{1/k}\le\limsup R(k)^{1/k}\le4. \tag{1} \]

3. What lexicographic products actually give

Choose graphs $G,H$ of orders $R(s)-1,R(t)-1$ with both clique and independence numbers at most $s-1,t-1$, respectively. Form $G[H]$: each vertex of $G$ is replaced by a copy of $H$, and two distinct copies are completely joined exactly when their vertices in $G$ are adjacent. A clique uses copies indexed by a clique of $G$, and inside each copy uses a clique of $H$. Conversely, maximum such choices attain the product. Thus $\omega(G[H])=\omega(G)\omega(H)$. The same reasoning with nonadjacent copies gives $\alpha(G[H])=\alpha(G)\alpha(H)$. Hence \[ R((s-1)(t-1)+1)>(R(s)-1)(R(t)-1). \tag{2} \] The clique parameter in $2$ multiplies; it does not add. Therefore this construction does not supply an additive supermultiplicative or submultiplicative inequality to which the usual Fekete argument for $\log R(k)/k$ would apply. For example, two copies of the five-cycle construction give a $25$-vertex graph with clique and independence numbers at most four, a valid finite illustration of $2$, without addressing the limiting rate.

4. Monotonicity and exponential bounds are insufficient

For comparison define the increasing integer sequence \[ u_k=2^{\,2^{\lceil\log_2 k\rceil}}\qquad(k\ge1). \] It satisfies $2^k\le u_k\le4^k$. At $k=2^j$, $u_k^{1/k}=2$, whereas at $k=2^{j-1}+1$ the roots tend to four. Thus even monotonicity and fixed exponential bounds cannot alone prove root convergence. This auxiliary sequence is not a construction of Ramsey numbers or evidence of their nonconvergence; it identifies a missing logical ingredient in a proposed limit argument.

5. Assessment and attribution

The bounds $1$, product inequality $2$, and the obstruction to the indicated shortcuts are proved. The missing step is a relation controlling the exponential rate across all large clique sizes, or upper and lower bounds with the same rate. This is a checked reconstruction and extension of earlier GPT-5.2 attempts, not a claim of new Ramsey bounds. Source: https://www.erdosproblems.com/77.
