Erdős Problem #276

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): incorporate the 2025 comments and separate the already-solved formal existence statement from the stronger informal question they are actually discussing.

Round-1 already gave a full proof of the displayed existence statement using Vsemirnov's explicit pair. The genuine remaining task is therefore not to re-prove existence, but to identify the precise unresolved strengthening suggested by the comments and to make the obstruction mathematically sharp.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 results.

  1. Round-1 gave a gap-free proof that the Fibonacci-like sequence with \[ x_0=106276436867,\qquad x_1=35256392432 \] has all terms composite and has no nontrivial common divisor.
  2. Round-1 proved the standard identity that $\gcd(x_n,x_{n+1})$ is constant along any Fibonacci-like sequence.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

There are two new points.

  1. The current problem page is not self-consistent if read literally. Its displayed statement asks only for an infinite composite Fibonacci-like sequence with no common divisor $>1$, but the page text itself says that Graham already proved the existence of such a sequence, and Round-1 re-proved this with a much smaller example.
  2. The comments show that the intended stronger question is really about excluding a finite prime / covering-congruence explanation of compositeness, at least for the odd-indexed terms in the Ismailescu--Son construction.

The key Round-2 tool is the following formalization of that comment-based obstruction.

Odd-subsequence finite-prime obstruction
\[ (\star_{\mathrm{odd}})\qquad \forall m>1\ \exists n\ge 0\ \mbox{such that}\ \gcd(m,x_{2n+1})=1. \]

This says exactly that the odd-indexed terms do not admit any finite set of primes hitting every odd term.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

The exact gap after Round-1 was that the formal existence statement had already been settled, but the comment-based strengthened question had not been isolated precisely.

I will now:

  1. prove that the displayed statement is already solved and therefore cannot be the genuine remaining issue;
  2. prove that $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$ is equivalent to "the odd-indexed terms have no finite prime cover";
  3. prove that any Graham-type covering-congruence explanation of compositeness would force such a finite prime cover, so $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$ is a sufficient sharp obstruction;
  4. explain exactly where Ismailescu--Son stop: they prove compositeness of all terms, but they do not prove $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

Step 1: the displayed statement is already solved.
Round-1 already proved the displayed formal statement by exhibiting the Vsemirnov pair \[ x_0=106276436867,\qquad x_1=35256392432 \] and checking that the resulting sequence has all terms composite and has no common divisor greater than $1$.

So the literal statement on the current page is not the real unresolved issue.

Step 2: a precise formulation of the comment-based strengthening.
Define:
The odd terms have a finite prime cover

if there exists a finite set of primes $P$ such that for every $n\ge 0$ there is some $p\in P$ with \[ p\mid x_{2n+1}. \]

Claim 1.
For any integer sequence $(x_n)$, the following are equivalent:
  1. (i) the odd terms have a finite prime cover;
  2. (ii) there exists an integer $m>1$ such that \[ \gcd(m,x_{2n+1})>1 \qquad \mbox{for every }n\ge 0. \]
Proof.
If (i) holds, let $m$ be the product of the distinct primes in $P$. Then every odd term $x_{2n+1}$ shares at least one prime factor with $m$, so $\gcd(m,x_{2n+1})>1$ for all $n$.

Conversely, if (ii) holds and $P$ is the finite set of prime divisors of $m$, then for each $n$ some prime $p\in P$ divides $\gcd(m,x_{2n+1})$, hence divides $x_{2n+1}$. So the odd terms have a finite prime cover. This proves the equivalence.

Therefore $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$ is exactly the negation of "the odd terms have a finite prime cover".

Step 3: why finite prime covers really do produce covering congruences.
Fix a prime $p$ and consider the state sequence modulo $p$, \[ v_n=(x_n,x_{n+1}) \in (\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^2. \] The recurrence gives \[ v_{n+1}=T(v_n),\qquad T(a,b)=(b,a+b). \] The map $T$ is bijective modulo $p$ because it has inverse \[ T^{-1}(a,b)=(b-a,a). \] Since $(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^2$ is finite, the orbit $(v_n)$ is purely periodic from $n=0$.

Hence, for each fixed prime $p$, the set \[ S_p=\{n\ge 0:\ p\mid x_n\} \] is a finite union of residue classes modulo some period $\pi_p$.

It follows that the set \[ S^{\mathrm{odd}}_p=\{n\ge 0:\ p\mid x_{2n+1}\} \] is also a finite union of residue classes modulo $\pi_p$ (or, after simplification, modulo a divisor of $\pi_p$).

Therefore, if the odd terms have a finite prime cover by primes in $P$, then the sets \[ S^{\mathrm{odd}}_p \qquad (p\in P) \] together give a finite covering of the odd indices by congruence classes.

So a finite prime cover of the odd terms automatically yields an "underlying covering system of congruences" in the natural Graham-type sense.

Step 4: the Ismailescu--Son candidate and the exact unresolved point.
Ismailescu and Son construct a Fibonacci-like sequence by taking \[ x_0=p^2+q^2,\qquad x_1=2pq+q^2, \] with $p=1$ and an explicit $129$-digit integer $q$.

They prove two different compositeness mechanisms:

  1. every odd term factors explicitly as \[ x_{2n+1}=(pF_n+qF_{n+1})(pL_n+qL_{n+1}), \] so all odd terms are composite;
  2. every even term is divisible by one of finitely many primes coming from a finite partial covering system, so all even terms are composite.

They also prove \[ \gcd(x_0,x_1)=1. \]

Thus they produce a bona fide composite Fibonacci-like sequence with no common divisor $>1$.

However, as the 2025 comments correctly emphasize, they do not prove $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$. Equivalently, they do not prove that the odd-indexed subsequence has no finite prime cover.

This is the exact unresolved obstruction left by the comments.

Step 5: why the stronger all-terms version is too strong.
A natural but incorrect sharpening would be \[ \forall m>1\ \exists n\ge 0\ \mbox{such that}\ \gcd(m,x_n)=1. \] That is too strong for the Ismailescu--Son candidate, because their even terms were deliberately built to have a finite prime cover. So the comments cannot be formalized using the whole sequence; they must focus on the odd terms (or on an equivalent weakening).

This identifies the minimal sharp formalization suggested by the comments.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

Possible failure modes:

  1. Is $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$ really the same as "no finite prime cover"? Yes, by Claim 1.
  2. Does a finite prime cover really imply covering congruences? Yes. For each fixed prime, divisibility positions are periodic because the two-dimensional state recursion modulo $p$ is a bijection on a finite set.
  3. Is $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$ the unique correct formalization of the comments? No. It is a minimal sharp formalization of the exact issue highlighted in the comments: the unknown existence of an integer $m>1$ with $\gcd(m,x_{2n+1})>1$ for every $n$.
  4. Have we accidentally weakened the problem too much? No. Any Graham-type finite covering explanation of the whole sequence would in particular force a finite prime cover of the odd terms, hence violate $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$.
  5. Have we accidentally claimed Ismailescu--Son solved the comment-based strengthening? No. The whole point of the section is that they solve the compositeness problem but not the finite-prime-cover obstruction for the odd subsequence.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

The displayed formal existence statement was already solved in Round-1 and in the literature. After incorporating the 2025 comments, the real remaining issue is the sharper condition $(\star_{\mathrm{odd}})$: whether one can obtain a composite Fibonacci-like sequence whose odd-indexed subsequence has no finite prime cover, equivalently no finite Graham-type covering-congruence explanation. That sharpened statement remains open to me.
