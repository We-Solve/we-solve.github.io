Research attempt: A sparse-family generalization fails under Frobenius iteration

The supplied prior preparation treats the familiar family $T^p-T-a$ using trace. An attempted enlargement replaces $p$ by $p^k$ while keeping a three-term description, hoping that a nonzero base-field trace would still certify irreducibility. It already fails over the prime field.

Let $a\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ and $f(T)=T^{p^k}-T-a$. If $\alpha$ is any root in an algebraic closure, then $\alpha^{p^k}=\alpha+a$. Inductively, \[ \alpha^{p^{jk}}=\alpha+ja\qquad(j\ge1), \] since Frobenius fixes $a$; at $j=p$ this yields $\alpha^{p^{pk}}=\alpha$. Thus its orbit under the ordinary $p$-Frobenius has length at most $pk$. To see directly that its minimal polynomial has degree at most this length, multiply $T-\alpha^{p^i}$ over one complete orbit. Frobenius permutes the factors and fixes the coefficients; an algebraic-closure element fixed by $c\mapsto c^p$ lies in $\mathbb F_p$, since $T^p-T$ has exactly its $p$ prime-field roots. The orbit product is therefore a polynomial over $\mathbb F_p$ annihilating $\alpha$. This proves the degree bound without assuming irreducibility of $f$.

Consequently if $p^k>pk$, $f$ cannot be irreducible: any root has a nonconstant minimal polynomial of smaller degree dividing $f$ (division with remainder and evaluation proves divisibility). This is a systematic obstruction, not a single accidental factor. In the first example $p=2,k=3,a=1$, \[ T^8+T+1=(T^2+T+1)(T^6+T^5+T^3+T^2+1) \quad\hbox{over }\mathbb F_2. \] The trace of $a$ to the prime field is $1$, yet the polynomial is reducible. Multiplying the displayed factors verifies every coefficient.

For $k=1$ the degree obstruction does not apply; it says $p\le p$. For $a=0$ the polynomial has the evident root zero, a separate boundary case. Therefore this calculation rejects only the proposed larger sparse family, not the known prime-degree trace criterion. Short sparse encoding alone does not preserve a valid irreducibility certificate as exponents grow.

The other bundled equation-solving question is untouched: a factorization obstruction for this family provides neither a promised fraction of solvable instances nor a never-incorrect polynomial-time solver for quadratic linearized expressions over the extension field.
