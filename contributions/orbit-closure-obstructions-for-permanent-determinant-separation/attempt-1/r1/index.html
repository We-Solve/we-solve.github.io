Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

The determinant and padded permanent are homogeneous polynomials acted on by linear variable changes. Equivariance of a ring map means it commutes with this group action; a surjection is a map onto the whole target ring.

For a group action on a vector space, $Gp$ is the orbit of $p$ and $\overline{Gp}$ is its algebraic closure. Its coordinate ring consists of polynomial functions modulo those vanishing on the orbit closure. Representation multiplicities count copies of a given irreducible representation in a graded component.

Research attempt: Small permanent degenerations and an unfulfilled weight obstruction

Why an arbitrary linear substitution gives a degeneration
Let $V$ be the vector space of the $n^2$ matrix-entry variables and $F=\det_n$ its polynomial. For any linear map $L:V\to V$, the polynomial $F\circ L$ belongs to the closure of the orbit under invertible linear substitutions. Indeed $\det(L+tI)$ is a nonzero monic polynomial in $t$, so $L+tI$ is invertible except for finitely many scalars. The coefficient vector of $F\circ(L+tI)$ tends to that of $F\circ L$ as $t\to0$. Every polynomial relation vanishing on the orbit also vanishes at this limit by continuity, proving membership in its Zariski closure.

For permanent size one and $n\geq2$, substitute the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(x,z,\ldots,z)$ to obtain $z^{n-1}x$. For permanent size two and $n\geq3$, substitute the block diagonal matrix with first block \[ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-c&d\end{pmatrix} \] and remaining diagonal entries all $z$. Its determinant is $z^{n-2}(ad+bc)=z^{n-2}\operatorname{per}_2$. The cases $n=m=1$ are immediate, and $n=m=2$ use the same two-by-two block without $z$. In that last case merely changing the sign of one input variable is invertible, so the permanent and determinant actually have the same orbit.

Let $X$ be the determinant orbit closure and $q$ one of these padded permanents. Since $X$ is closed and invariant under the group, it contains $Y=\overline{Gq}$. In the common ambient polynomial-coordinate ring, $I(X)\subseteq I(Y)$, because a function vanishing on the larger set also vanishes on the smaller one. Quotienting gives a surjective restriction map \[ \mathbb C[X]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[Y], \] which commutes with the group action. These low-size cases therefore cannot provide the obstruction demanded by the asymptotic conjecture. They do not refute it, since its quantifier asks for arbitrarily large permanent sizes.

A necessary finite-dimensional weight test
Suppose a surjective linear map $\phi:U\to W$ commutes with a diagonal torus action, and both spaces split into finitely many character spaces. Then its restriction to each character space is onto the corresponding character space of $W$. For a direct proof choose a torus element whose eigenvalues on the finitely many distinct characters are distinct; such a choice exists because it avoids finitely many nonzero character-difference equations. For any one eigenvalue, Lagrange interpolation gives a polynomial in that torus operator equal to its eigenspace projection. Equivariance makes $\phi$ commute with this projection. Project a preimage of a target character vector to get a preimage of the same character. In particular a character occurring on the right but not the left obstructs surjectivity, and larger character-space dimension on the right also obstructs it.

This test applies to finite-dimensional homogeneous pieces when the proposed map respects them. For these degree-$n$ form orbit closures, central scalar substitutions act on coordinate functions of homogeneous degree $d$ with character $t^{-nd}$ under the usual pullback convention. Distinct degrees give distinct characters, so a group-equivariant linear map respects the grading. The asserted scalar character follows because scaling the input variables by $t$ scales a degree-$n$ form by $t^n$, and pullback uses the inverse action. Each homogeneous coordinate-ring piece is a quotient of finitely many degree-$d$ coordinate monomials and is finite-dimensional.

The missing step is to find and prove a discrepancy for the actual determinant and padded-permanent coordinate rings at unbounded sizes and every polynomial padding bound. The small degenerations above supply no such discrepancy, and mere knowledge that the two polynomials have different displayed monomials does not determine their orbit-closure coordinate-ring weights. No representation occurrence or multiplicity is asserted without calculation.
