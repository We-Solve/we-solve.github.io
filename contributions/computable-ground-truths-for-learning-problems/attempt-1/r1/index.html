Verbatim supplied catalogue question

When is the ground truth of a learning task a computable function, and how does that relate to approximation by computable neural networks?

Scope and the attempted equivalence

The source discussion, printed pages3030--3032, already connects computability from point samples with network approximation and asks how to choose meaningful computable ground truths. This record overlaps sourceID30004929, but here we focus on the quantifier order and effective convergence rather than sampling noise. We use rational-name representations of real inputs and rational-weight finite ReLU networks. We prove a restricted constructive equivalence and show why neither individual approximation existence nor a computable convergent sequence without a rate suffices.

Effective uniform networks imply a computable target

Suppose an algorithm given $k$ returns a rational-weight ReLU network $N_k$ with $\|N_k-f\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\le2^{-k}$. Each such finite network can be evaluated effectively on a real input supplied by rational approximations. Explicitly, the activation is $1$-Lipschitz, and each rational affine layer has a rational Lipschitz upper bound in the maximum norm equal to the largest absolute row sum of its matrix. Their product is a computable upper bound $L_k$ for the network. Obtain a rational input $q$ with $|q-x|\le2^{-j}$, choose $j$ so $L_k2^{-j}$ is below a prescribed rational tolerance, and evaluate the rational network at $q$ exactly. If $L_k=0$, no input refinement is necessary. This computes $N_k(x)$ to arbitrary accuracy.

To compute $f(x)$ within $2^{-n}$, choose $k=n+2$ and evaluate $N_k(x)$ to accuracy $2^{-n-2}$. The triangle inequality gives total error at most $2^{-n-1}<2^{-n}$. Thus a uniformly generated approximation family with a known error rate makes the target computable in this representation.

A converse with explicit effective continuity data

Assume evaluations of $f$ at dyadic rationals can be computed to arbitrary rational accuracy, and a computable modulus $m(k)$ is supplied such that \[ |x-y|\le2^{-m(k)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |f(x)-f(y)|\le2^{-k} \] for $x,y\in[0,1]$. To produce an approximation at requested precision $2^{-n}$, take the dyadic grid of mesh $2^{-m(n+2)}$ and approximate each function value rationally to error at most $2^{-n-2}$. Linear interpolation of these rational samples has error at most $2^{-n-1}$: each endpoint value differs from $f(x)$ by at most $2^{-n-2}$ before sampling noise, and the interpolant is a convex combination. The interpolant is a rational-weight ReLU network: if its successive interval slopes are $s_j$ at knots $t_j$, write \[ N(x)=N(0)+s_0x+\sum_{j\ge1}(s_j-s_{j-1})\max(x-t_j,0), \] with a finite sum over interior knots. Slopes telescope and values agree at the first knot, proving exact representation. All operations and sample precisions are effective, so this is a uniform algorithm producing the promised family. The modulus and evaluation assumptions are explicit; no general inference of them from finite samples is claimed.

Non-effective approximants can exist for a noncomputable target

Let $K\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ be the noncomputable halting set in a fixed effective program enumeration and put \[ \alpha=\sum_{e\in K}4^{-e}. \] Then $\alpha$ is not computable. Indeed, if its value were computable, decide membership in increasing order of $e$. After subtracting the already determined contributions before $e$ and multiplying by $4^e$, the result lies in $[0,1/3]$ if $e\notin K$ and in $[1,4/3]$ if $e\in K$, because $\sum_{j>e}4^{e-j}=1/3$. Approximate $\alpha$ within $4^{-e}/6$ and compare the scaled remainder to $2/3$. The two possible approximate ranges remain separated, so this procedure would decide $K$, a contradiction.

The constant function $f(x)=\alpha$ is therefore noncomputable, but for every $\epsilon>0$ there exists a rational constant $q$ within $\epsilon$ of $\alpha$. The one-bias network $x\mapsto q$ is a computable network with that uniform error. Thus the bare statement that arbitrarily accurate computable networks exist does not imply a computable target. It is the uniform effective selection with a certified rate that matters.

There is even a computable sequence of such networks converging uniformly without any computable convergence rate. Let \[ q_s=\sum_{\substack{1\le e\le s\\ \text{program }e\text{ halts within }s\text{ steps}}}4^{-e}. \] Each rational $q_s$ is effectively obtained by finitely many bounded simulations; the sequence is monotone and bounded. It converges to $\alpha$: for any tolerance, first bound the geometric tail by choosing finitely many indices, then wait until all halting programs among those indices have appeared. The latter waiting time exists but need not be computably bounded. If a computable function $S(n)$ guaranteed $|q_s-\alpha|\le2^{-n}$ for all $s\ge S(n)$, computing $q_{S(n)}$ would compute $\alpha$, contradicting the preceding proof. Therefore uniform convergence of an effectively generated network sequence, without an effective rate, is insufficient.
