Kasner Singularities Beyond the Subcritical Regime

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A generalized Kasner background has spatial scale factors $t^{\widetilde q_I}$ as proper time $t$ approaches zero. The exponents are those of the vacuum or Einstein--scalar-field background in the report. Asymptotically velocity-term-dominated behavior means that spatial derivative terms become negligible relative to time evolution. The subcritical condition is the displayed strict inequality in the target.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For general sufficiently small perturbations, possibly without symmetry, of generalized Kasner solutions of the Einstein equations, what behavior occurs near the singularity when the sub-criticality condition $\max_{I<J,B}(\widetilde q_I+\widetilde q_J-\widetilde q_B)<1$ fails?
Scope and source qualification.
The failure of the strict inequality includes the borderline equality case. Oscillatory behavior is a heuristic possibility, rather than an added asserted conclusion.

Where the subcritical power arises.
Let $X_I$ be a time-independent spatial frame with $[X_I,X_J]=\sum_B c_{IJ}^B X_B$, and rescale it by Kasner powers $e_I=t^{-q_I}X_I$. Since the powers depend only on time, \[ [e_I,e_J]=\sum_B c_{IJ}^B t^{q_B-q_I-q_J}e_B. \] Relative to the time-derivative scale $t^{-1}$, a nonzero structure coefficient therefore has size $t^{1-(q_I+q_J-q_B)}$. It decays when the exponent in parentheses is less than one, remains order one at equality, and grows when it exceeds one.
Borderline accumulation.
Even a coefficient of small amplitude cannot be dismissed merely for being small if its time integral is borderline: $\int_t^1\varepsilon s^{-1}ds=\varepsilon\log(1/t)$ becomes order one at $t=e^{-1/\varepsilon}$. For powers $s^{-b}$, $b>1$, accumulation is stronger, equal to $\varepsilon(t^{1-b}-1)/(b-1)$. These exact integrations identify why extending a subcritical integrability estimate fails.
