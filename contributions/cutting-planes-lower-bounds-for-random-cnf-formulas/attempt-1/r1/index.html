Research attempt: Density extremes and an explicit short Cutting Planes refutation

A specified random model.
Let $1\le k\le n$, and draw $m$ clauses independently with replacement from all \[ Q=2^k\binom nk \] clauses using exactly $k$ distinct variables and arbitrary signs. A fixed assignment satisfies one random clause with probability $1-2^{-k}$, so if $Z$ counts satisfying assignments, \[ \mathbb EZ=2^n(1-2^{-k})^m\le\exp(n\log2-m2^{-k}). \] Markov's inequality gives $\Pr(Z>0)\le e^{-t}$ whenever $m\ge2^k(n\log2+t)$. This proves an unsatisfiability probability, not any proof-length lower bound. The attempted inference from "unlikely to have a solution" to "hard to certify" has no support in this calculation.
At much greater density a short certificate appears.
Fix one set $S$ of $k$ variables. Each of its $2^k$ sign clauses has probability $1/Q$ per draw. A union bound gives \[ \Pr(\text{some sign clause on }S\text{ is missing}) \le 2^k(1-1/Q)^m\le 2^k e^{-m/Q}. \] For $m\ge Q(k\log2+t)$, all of them occur with probability at least $1-e^{-t}$. This complete block has a short explicit Cutting Planes refutation. Encode a positive literal by $x$, a negative literal by $1-x$, and a clause by the inequality that the sum of its literals is at least $1$, with Boolean bounds $0\le x\le1$.

Pair clauses with identical other literals $C$ and opposite last literal. Their inequalities are \[ L_C+x\ge1,\qquad L_C+1-x\ge1. \] Adding gives $2L_C\ge1$. Since $L_C$ is integral, division by two with upward rounding gives $L_C\ge1$. Repeating for each remaining variable yields $0\ge1$. There are $2^k-1$ shortened clauses in this binary tree, each requiring one addition and one division. The coefficients in these derivations are at most two in literal notation; conversion to variable notation has integer constants of size $O(k)$. Thus the refutation has at most $2(2^k-1)$ new lines, besides its input clauses and bounds.

For fixed $k$ the certificate size is constant with respect to $n$, and for $k\le C\log_2n$ it is polynomial in $n$. Its presence at this high density prevents a lower bound asserted uniformly over all clause counts. This does not contradict the source's lower bound, which concerns a particular distributional parameter regime.

What the route actually establishes.
A first-moment proof of unsatisfiability and a coupon-style proof of an easy local contradiction can both apply to random formulas, depending on their parameters. Neither identifies a hard intermediate regime. The exponential size of the universe $Q$ also matters: this high-density example is not claimed to use the linear clause density relevant to many fixed-width hardness questions.

Verification and adversarial audit

The proof was checked at $k=1$, where the block is $x,\neg x$ and two Cutting Planes steps suffice. The constructed short derivation uses rounding legally because every literal sum is integer. It bounds lines and coefficient encodings, rather than treating large integer coefficients as free.
