Research attempt: What two torsion images determine, and what they do not

Let $\ell$ be prime and take two abstract torsion spaces $T,T'\cong\mathbb F_\ell^2$ with specified ordered bases $(P,Q)$ and $(P',Q')$. A group homomorphism $\varphi:T\to T'$ is $\mathbb F_\ell$-linear: for an integer representative $a$ of a field element, additivity gives $\varphi(aP)=a\varphi(P)$, and the value is unchanged on adding $\ell$. Therefore the two images \[ \varphi(P)=aP'+bQ',\qquad \varphi(Q)=cP'+dQ' \] determine its value on every $uP+vQ$ as $(au+cv)P'+(bu+dv)Q'$. Its matrix is $M=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\b&d\end{pmatrix}$. There are $\ell^4$ such maps. If it is promised to be an isomorphism, its first column may be any nonzero vector and its second any vector outside the first column's span, giving $(\ell^2-1)(\ell^2-\ell)$ choices. The images distinguish all of these maps, algebraically.

To see the information lost by recording only a scalar pairing invariant, fix a formal primitive $\ell$th root $\zeta$ and define the alternating pairing \[ e((u,v),(u',v'))=\zeta^{uv'-vu'}. \] Expanding a two-by-two determinant proves $e(Mx,My)=e(x,y)^{\det M}$. Thus the pairing of the two published basis images determines only $\det M$. For every $t\in\mathbb F_\ell$, the matrix $M_t=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant one, while $M_tQ=tP'+Q'$ distinguishes the $\ell$ possibilities. Even knowing the pairing relation exactly therefore does not identify the complete torsion action supplied by the two points.

This calculation also locates the first algorithmic gap. A published point such as $\varphi(P)$ does not automatically provide its coordinates $a,b$ in a chosen basis of the target torsion group. The uniqueness of coordinates is not an efficient discrete-logarithm algorithm. Exhaustively enumerating all $\ell^2$ combinations of $P',Q'$ will find them, but costs that many candidate operations in this elementary implementation. Furthermore, distinct global isogenies can require additional analysis even when their restrictions to a finite torsion group coincide. No lifting theorem from a torsion matrix to a unique secret isogeny has been proved here.

The failed abstraction is concrete: treating the public transcript only as an endpoint curve, or compressing its extra points to one pairing determinant, removes finite-group data that can distinguish many different restrictions. The successful use of that data in a complete SIDH attack is a separate, known algebraic construction, not supplied by this counting argument.
