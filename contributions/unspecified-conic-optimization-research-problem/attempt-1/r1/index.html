Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Abstract. From a mathematical perspective, optimization is the science of proving inequalities. In this sense, computational optimization is a method for computer-assisted proofs. Conic (linear) optimization is the problem of minimizing a linear func- tional over the intersection of a convex cone with an affine subspace of a topological vector space. For many cones this problem is computationally tractable, and as a result there is a growing number of computer-assisted proofs using conic optimization in discrete geometry, (extremal) graph the- ory, numerical analysis, and other fields, the most famous example perhaps being the proof of the Kepler Conjecture. The aim of this workshop was to bring researchers from these diverse fields together to work towards expanding the current scope of conic optimization as a method of generating proofs, and to identify problems and challenges to work on together. Mathematics Subject Classification $2020$: 90Cxx, 90C22, 52-xx, 14Pxx, 65-xx, 05Cxx. [S2]

Research attempt: Residual-aware semidefinite proof certification

A concrete certification route.
Consider real symmetric matrices and the optimization problem \[ p_* =\inf\{\langle C,X\rangle:\mathcal A(X)=b,\ X\succeq0\}, \qquad \langle C,X\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(CX). \] Suppose every feasible $X$ satisfies $\operatorname{tr}X\leq R$ for a known $R\geq0$. Given a vector $y$, a genuinely positive semidefinite matrix $S$, and a certified residual bound \[ E=C-\mathcal A^*y-S,\qquad \|E\|_{\rm op}\leq\varepsilon, \] we obtain the rigorous lower bound \[ p_*\geq b^Ty-\varepsilon R. \] Indeed, $\langle C,X\rangle=b^Ty+\langle S,X\rangle+\langle E,X\rangle$. Diagonalize $X=\sum_i\lambda_i u_i u_i^T$ with $\lambda_i\geq0$. Then $\langle S,X\rangle=\sum_i\lambda_i u_i^TSu_i\geq0$ and $\langle E,X\rangle\geq-\varepsilon\sum_i\lambda_i\geq-\varepsilon R$. No dual attainment or Slater condition is needed for this elementary weak-duality certificate. With an exactly feasible candidate $X_0$, the same calculation gives a certified interval $[b^Ty-\varepsilon R,\langle C,X_0\rangle]$ containing $p_*$. A rational factorization $S=LL^T$ is one possible exact PSD certificate; verifying its entries suffices because $z^TSz=\|L^Tz\|^2\geq0$.
Why small floating residual alone fails.
In dimension one, let $C=0$, impose $X=1/\varepsilon$ with rational $\varepsilon>0$, and take $y=\varepsilon$, $S=0$. The true optimum is zero, while the uncorrected proposed lower bound is $b y=1$. Its dual residual is only $E=-\varepsilon$. The trace factor is $R=1/\varepsilon$, so the corrected bound is exactly zero. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the residual tends to zero but the false lower-bound error stays one. Therefore no error guarantee depending only on residual size and tending to zero can hold uniformly without a feasible-scale bound. If the feasible set is unbounded, even the one-dimensional problem $\inf_{X\geq0}(-\varepsilon X)=-\infty$ has the approximate certificate $y=0,S=0,E=-\varepsilon$. A small residual cannot certify a finite lower bound.
PSD rounding must also be certified.
The rank-one PSD matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ becomes \[ S_\delta=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1-\delta\end{pmatrix} \] after an entry perturbation of size $\delta>0$. The vector $(1,-1)$ has quadratic form $-\delta$, so $S_\delta$ is not PSD, however small the perturbation. One valid repair is to establish a certified $S_\delta\succeq-\eta I$ and then use $S_\delta+\eta I\succeq0$, charging the resulting residual $\eta I$ to the same trace bound. Such a repair must be proved for the particular numerical output; proximity to a floating PSD matrix is not an exact certificate.
