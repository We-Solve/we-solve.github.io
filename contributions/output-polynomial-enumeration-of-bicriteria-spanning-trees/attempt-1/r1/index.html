Bicriteria enumeration: exponential output versus exponential wasted search

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Output-polynomial time is polynomial in the input length plus the total encoded output length. A Pareto-optimal tree is distinct from its cost pair: different trees may have the same pair.

In a bicriteria minimization problem each feasible solution has two costs. A cost pair is Pareto-optimal if no feasible pair is no larger in both coordinates and strictly smaller in one.

A spanning tree of a connected finite graph is an acyclic connected subgraph containing every vertex. Its cost is the sum of its edge costs.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For bicriteria minimum spanning trees, can all Pareto-optimal solutions be enumerated in time polynomial in the number of solutions?

Output conventions and attempted enumeration

The report asks for time polynomial in the number of Pareto-optimal solutions. We use the usual input-plus-output interpretation and distinguish trees from distinct cost pairs. Two explicit simple-graph families show why large output is legitimate but exhaustive tree search is not an output-polynomial argument.
A linear-size family with exponentially many distinct pairs.
Build a chain of $k$ triangles, consecutive triangles meeting only at one articulation vertex. In triangle $i$, for $0\le i<k$, give the three edges two-coordinate costs \[ (1,1+2^i),\qquad(1+2^i,1),\qquad(1,1). \] A spanning tree must select exactly two edges in each triangle. Indeed all three would form a cycle, while at most one disconnects a vertex or separates its two articulation endpoints; other blocks cannot bypass it because the block-incidence structure is a chain. Conversely choosing any two in every triangle gives a connected acyclic graph by repeatedly attaching a tree at a single shared vertex. Thus there are $3^k$ trees.

The two choices containing the $(1,1)$ edge have block totals $(2,2+2^i)$ and $(2+2^i,2)$. The third has $(2+2^i,2+2^i)$ and is strictly dominated in at least one coordinate by either of those choices. Replacing this third choice within its block preserves a tree and strictly improves a coordinate without worsening the other. No Pareto tree uses it. The remaining $2^k$ trees have distinct pairs \[ \left(2k+s,\ 2k+(2^k-1)-s\right),\qquad0\le s<2^k, \] because binary expansion identifies the selected powers of two. All pairs have the same coordinate sum and are distinct, so none dominates another. They are exactly the Pareto frontier. The graph has $2k+1$ vertices and $3k$ edges; the total encoding length of the powers is $O(k^2)$. Exponential output is therefore possible even for distinct pairs, and is permitted by an output-sensitive bound.

The same search space can have a single answer.
On the same chain give two edges of each triangle cost pair $(1,1)$ and its third edge pair $(2,2)$. The unique tree using only the cheaper two edges in every triangle has pair $(2k,2k)$. Every other tree uses a more expensive edge in at least one block, increasing both total coordinates, and is dominated by that tree. Thus there is exactly one Pareto tree and one Pareto pair, yet an algorithm that first lists all spanning trees examines $3^k$ objects. Its time is exponential in $k$, whereas input and output have polynomial length in $k$. Exhaustive enumeration followed by pruning therefore fails the desired complexity guarantee on this family.
Weighted-sum enumeration also misses some answers.
For a single triangle with edge pairs $(6,0),(2,2),(0,6)$, the tree pairs are $(8,2),(6,6),(2,8)$, all undominated. For $(6,6)$ to minimize the second coordinate plus $\lambda$ times the first requires both $\lambda\ge2$ and $\lambda\le1/2$, by comparison with the two endpoint pairs. No nonnegative multiplier does this. Hence enumerating supported optima by scalarized MST calls does not enumerate the entire frontier either. This hand computation is independent of the distinction between repeated and distinct pairs.
