Testing the linear-algebra mechanism.
Let $B(u,v)=u^TAv$ over $\mathbb C$. If $A^T=-A$, then $B(v,v)=-B(v,v)$, hence $B(v,v)=0$. Thus a diagonal isotropy constraint is automatic for an alternating form, while for symmetric $A$ it imposes the quadratic equation $v^TAv=0$. These are genuinely different projective conditions and cannot be exchanged simply by changing a sign convention. For a four-by-four alternating matrix with upper entries $a_{ij}$, direct determinant expansion gives \[ \det A=(a_{12}a_{34}-a_{13}a_{24}+a_{14}a_{23})^2. \] Consequently degeneracy is controlled by a quadratic Pfaffian relation, including its alternating signs. For example, when $a_{13}=a_{14}=a_{23}=a_{24}=0$, the matrix splits into two two-by-two blocks and the formula reduces to $a_{12}^2a_{34}^2$, checking the normalization.
