Multitask Hamiltonian Learning across Levels of Theory

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A learned Hamiltonian is an effective operator whose parameters are fitted to electronic-structure data. Distinct levels of theory supply tasks with different approximations and errors. Multitask learning shares selected parameters or representations while allowing task-dependent outputs. Transfer benefit is assessed against corresponding single-task prediction and sample requirements.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Develop and analyze a multitask learning framework for Hamiltonians trained on data generated at different levels of electronic-structure theory. Determine how information should be shared across tasks and when transfer improves accuracy and sample efficiency.
Scope and source qualification.
The source gives a learning-framework question, not a universal theorem that sharing always improves every task.

Short English statement

Develop Hamiltonian learning that shares information across electronic-structure approximations and determine when that sharing improves prediction and sample efficiency.

A one-parameter Hamiltonian transfer calculation.
Fix a known Hermitian matrix $A$ with Frobenius norm one and let task $j$ have Hamiltonian $H_j=\theta_j A$. Suppose $n$ independent scalar observations per task equal $\theta_j$ plus centered noise of variance $\sigma^2$, independently between tasks. Their sample means $\bar Y_j$ have variance $v=\sigma^2/n$. Estimate task one by $\widehat\theta_1=(1-\lambda)\bar Y_1+\lambda\bar Y_2$, $0\leq\lambda\leq1$. With $\Delta=\theta_2-\theta_1$, direct bias/variance decomposition gives \[ \mathbb E\|\widehat\theta_1A-H_1\|_F^2 =v(1-\lambda)^2+v\lambda^2+\lambda^2\Delta^2. \] Equal pooling, $\lambda=1/2$, improves on task-one-only risk $v$ exactly when $\Delta^2<2v$.
Oracle sharing and negative transfer.
If $v=\Delta=0$, every $\lambda$ has zero risk. Otherwise differentiation of the quadratic risk yields $\lambda_*=v/(2v+\Delta^2)$. This is an oracle choice because $\Delta$ is unknown. If $\Delta=0$, pooling halves the risk. If the task discrepancy is fixed and $n\to\infty$, equal pooling retains bias $\Delta^2/4$ while the independent estimator becomes accurate, so unconditional sharing eventually harms the target task.
Full learning gap.
The example preserves Hermiticity but assumes direct coefficient observations in a known common basis. Electronic-structure data are usually more indirect and task discrepancy can vary with configuration and operator component. The first missing step is identifying estimable shared structure with a data-dependent transfer rule and error control under those observation maps. No empirical comparison or general multitask Hamiltonian-learning theorem is supplied; the calculation only establishes a transparent sharing-versus-bias diagnostic.
