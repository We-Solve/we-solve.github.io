Restricted Models of Vertex-Distribution-Free Testing

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

In vertex-distribution-free testing, vertices are sampled from an unknown distribution. Distance weights changed adjacency pairs by the product of their vertex probabilities. Restricting atom weights limits how heavily or lightly individual vertices may be sampled.

Atom bounds compare distances but do not give the missing oracle

The preserved source limitation is substantive: the report already states its large-graph, small-maximum-atom and lower-atom theorems. We do not relabel that framing paragraph as a new unresolved classification. Instead we test a simple quantitative transfer under a stronger, explicit hypothesis. On an $n$-vertex set suppose \[ \frac c n\le D(v)\le\frac C n\quad\hbox{for all }v, \qquad 0<c\le1\le C. \] For a graph property admitting at least one graph on this vertex set, let $d_D(G,\mathcal P)$ denote the minimum weighted unordered-edge edit distance, and let $d_U$ use the uniform distribution.
Proposition: One has $c^2d_U(G,\mathcal P)\le d_D(G,\mathcal P)\le C^2d_U(G,\mathcal P)$. If an additional oracle gives exact values of $D(v)$ and $n$ is known, rejection sampling turns $D$-samples into uniform samples with expected $1/c$ trials per accepted sample.
Proof: Every changed unordered pair has weight between $c^2/n^2$ and $C^2/n^2$. Summing proves the two inequalities for each candidate edited graph. Taking minima proves the lower inequality, and choosing a uniform-distance minimizer proves the upper inequality; the finite set of graphs guarantees a minimizer. For rejection sampling, accept a sample $v$ with probability $c/(nD(v))$, which lies in $[0,1]$. The joint probability of drawing and accepting $v$ is exactly $c/n$, hence the total success probability is $c$ and its conditional distribution is uniform. Independent trials give mean waiting time $1/c$ by summing the geometric tail. ∎
This yields an explicit attempted transfer: $\varepsilon$-far in $D$ implies $\varepsilon/C^2$-far in uniform distance, then simulate a uniform tester by rejection. The first step is valid; the second requires extra information. The actual VDF model supplies samples from an unknown $D$, not exact atom evaluations, and it does not provide $n$. The displayed simulation is therefore not an algorithm in that model. Moreover an expected sample count alone is not the source's always-bounded query guarantee without a separate truncation/error analysis.
