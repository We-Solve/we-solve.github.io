Both zero-surgery assertions for every knot in the three-sphere

Problem: prb_3ce404d9a67742478f8d6f6f2d5010ef

Claim type: Literature report

Exact scope: For Y=S3 and every smooth knot K, the two assertions in Kirby Problem 1.13 hold: fibering of zero surgery implies fibering of the exterior in the prescribed primitive class, and capping a taut Seifert surface preserves tautness. The reverse fibering and norm statements use Gabai’s published theorems, with generator classes and the unknot case checked explicitly.

The established three-sphere results

Let K be a smooth knot in S^3, X its exterior and M=S^3_0(K). Gabai [1, Corollary 8.19] proves that K is fibered if and only if M fibers over S^1. For a nontrivial K, his Theorem 8.8 gives the Thurston norm of a generator a in H_2(M;Z) as 2g(K)-2. These are the deep foliation-theoretic inputs; their proofs are not reproduced here.

We explain why these theorems imply the exact two assertions, including their class conditions, rather than just nearby existence statements.

Homology classes under capping

Alexander duality gives H_1(X;Z)=Z generated by the meridian and H_2(X;Z)=0. Poincare–Lefschetz duality gives H_2(X,partial X;Z)=Z. Every oriented Seifert surface F whose boundary is the preferred oriented longitude represents its generator z; intersection with a meridian detects the coefficient one.

Mayer–Vietoris for the zero filling gives H_1(M;Z)=Z and H_2(M;Z)=Z. Capping the longitude boundary of F by the filling meridian disk produces F-hat, and this oriented surface has intersection number one with the core of the attached solid torus. Consequently [F-hat] is a primitive generator a. The capping correspondence sends z to a and respects signs. All Seifert surfaces of the same oriented K determine the same z and a, even if their genera differ.

Assertion (1): fibering in the prescribed class

Suppose M fibers over S^1 with fiber class [F-hat]. Gabai's theorem supplies a fibration of X by Seifert surfaces G. Its fiber class is one of the two primitive generators of H_2(X,partial X). Choose the orientation of the base circle so that [G]=[F]=z; reversing that orientation does not change the existence of a bundle structure. Its capped class is then [F-hat]=a. Thus the conclusion supplies exactly the prescribed relative class, not an unrelated class of a larger-rank homology group.

For completeness, the forward implication needs no deep theorem: a fibered knot exterior is the mapping torus of a monodromy of a one-boundary-component surface, taken to be the identity near its boundary. Extend that monodromy over a capping disk by the identity. Its closed-surface mapping torus is precisely the longitudinal zero filling. This also confirms the longitude convention used in the argument.

Assertion (2): capping a taut Seifert surface

If K is nontrivial, the Thurston norm of z in X is 2g(K)-1. A connected Seifert surface has one boundary component and chi_-(F)=2g(F)-1. Therefore tautness forces g(F)=g(K). Capping gives a connected closed surface of genus g(K), whose chi_- is max(0,2g(K)-2). Gabai's generator norm theorem gives precisely this value for a. Hence F-hat minimizes the norm in its nonzero class and is taut under the prompt's convention.

If K is the unknot, a taut Seifert surface is a disk: its generator has norm zero, whereas every connected positive-genus Seifert surface has chi_->0. Zero surgery is S^2 x S^1 and the cap is a sphere. Spheres are explicitly taut by the prompt's convention. This covers the exception to the nontrivial-knot hypothesis in Theorem 8.8. For g(K)=1 the cap is a torus in a nonzero class with norm zero; that is also taut, and is not confused with a homologically trivial torus.

Scope and verification

The prompt lists no prior attempts. This is the entire Y=S^3 subcase of both assertions, credited to Gabai with the class and low-genus translations supplied. The ambient hypothesis cannot be deleted: a general closed Y can have additional homology classes, and the generator and norm calculations above no longer apply unchanged. No universal null-homotopic-knot theorem is claimed.

The source's foliation arguments and minimal-surface conclusions have not been reimplemented or tested numerically. Homology bookkeeping, orientation choices and genus-zero/one boundary cases were checked directly in this report.
