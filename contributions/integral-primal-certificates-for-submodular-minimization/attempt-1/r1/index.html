Integral Primal Certificates for Submodular Minimization

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For normalized $f(\varnothing)=0$, its base polyhedron is $B(f)=\{y\in\mathbb R^V:y(S)\leq f(S)\ (S\subseteq V),\ y(V)=f(V)\}$ with $y(S)=\sum_{e\in S}y_e$. The associated primal objective is $\max_{y\in B(f)}\sum_e\min(y_e,0)$. Greedy vertices take successive value differences along permutations. A convex combination is $y=\sum_i\lambda_i v_i$ with $\lambda_i\geq0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$.

A set function $f:2^V\to\mathbb R$ is submodular if $f(A)+f(B)\geq f(A\cup B)+f(A\cap B)$ for all $A,B\subseteq V$. It is monotone if inclusion never decreases its value and normalized if $f(\varnothing)=0$.

A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed affine half-spaces; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Its faces are its intersections with supporting hyperplanes.

Testing integrality assumptions and greedy-vertex search

The source states a submodular minimization duality involving the base polyhedron, then asks for a direct construction of an integral dual optimum and its greedy decomposition. For a normalized submodular function $f$ on $E$, use \[ B(f)=\{y\in\mathbb R^E:y(S)\le f(S)\ (S\subseteq E),\ y(E)=f(E)\}, \qquad g(y)=\sum_{e\in E}\min(y_e,0). \] If $f$ is allowed to be arbitrary real valued, an integral base point need not exist: on a singleton set take $f(\varnothing)=0,f(\{1\})=1/2$. Submodularity is immediate, and $B(f)=\{1/2\}$. This only identifies a missing hypothesis in the literal extraction; it is not a counterexample to an intended integer-valued theorem.
Proposition: Even for integer-valued submodular $f$, maximizing $g$ by inspecting only greedy vertices can fail.
Proof: Take $E=\{1,2\}$, $f(\varnothing)=f(E)=0$ and $f(\{1\})=f(\{2\})=1$. The only nonnested submodularity inequality is $1+1\ge0+0$, and nested pairs give equality, so $f$ is submodular. Its base polyhedron is precisely $y=(t,-t)$ with $-1\le t\le1$: the singleton upper bounds and zero total give these inequalities. The two greedy orders have increments $(1,-1)$ and $(-1,1)$, respectively. On the segment, \[ g(t,-t)=\min(t,0)+\min(-t,0)=-|t|. \] The unique maximizer is the integral point $(0,0)$ with value zero, whereas both greedy vertices have value $-1$. The optimum has the explicit convex decomposition $\frac12(1,-1)+\frac12(-1,1)$. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

All submodularity inequalities and both greedy orders on the two-element example were checked exactly. The failed inference is that a maximizer must be a vertex because the feasible region is a polyhedron: here the objective is concave piecewise linear rather than linear, and its maximum lies in the interior of an edge. Convex combinations of greedy bases are therefore essential even in this small case. No direct polynomial algorithm or general integral-optimum decomposition procedure is supplied.
