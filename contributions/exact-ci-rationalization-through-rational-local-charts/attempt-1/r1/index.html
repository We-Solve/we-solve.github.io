Finite uniform CI representations: a rationalization route and its unproved step

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A conditional-independence (CI) structure records exactly which relations $X_A\perp X_B\mid X_C$ hold for disjoint index sets. It is probabilistically representable when it is the CI structure of some random vector. A finite uniform probability space gives each elementary outcome the same probability, while the random vector may identify multiple elementary outcomes.

The exact representation requirement

The source asks for exactly the same complete CI structure, not merely for a distribution satisfying selected independences. Uniformity refers to elementary outcomes, not to distinct vector values. We first make this arithmetic requirement explicit, then try rational approximation within a specified conditional-independence parametrization. The source does not supply a finite-alphabet restriction on all initial representations; our finite-alphabet argument does not establish such a reduction.
Finite uniform spaces and rational probability tables.
A random vector on a uniform space of $N$ points has cell probabilities $k_x/N$, where $k_x$ counts elementary outcomes with value $x$. Conversely, if a finite table has rational nonnegative entries summing to one, choose a common positive denominator $N$ and make $Np_x$ distinct elementary outcomes for each cell $x$, all of mass $1/N$. Their total number is $N$ and the induced vector has exactly the specified law. Consequently a rational finite table suffices for the conjectured representation; equal positive masses at distinct vector values are not necessary.
A rationalization theorem for a positive three-block chain.
Let $X,Y,Z$ have fixed finite alphabets, a strictly positive joint table, and $X\perp Z\mid Y$. Write \[ p(x,y,z)=r_y a_{x\mid y}b_{z\mid y}, \] where $r$ and each conditional vector $a,b$ are positive and sum to one. Choose rational positive approximations to all but the last entry of each vector and set the last entry to one minus their sum. Sufficiently close approximations stay positive, and this polynomial parametrization then produces rational tables converging to $p$, all satisfying $X\perp Z\mid Y$ exactly. To preserve the complete CI pattern, include every additional CI that actually holds as a constraint on the parameter choice; the preceding unrestricted approximation does not generally do that. Thus the theorem proved by this argument is the following precisely limited one: if the complete CI structure of $p$ has no additional nontrivial CIs beyond those forced by $X\perp Z\mid Y$, then a rational table with that same complete structure exists. Here is the missing non-CI part of the proof. On fixed finite alphabets there are finitely many disjoint-set CI statements. Each is expressed by finitely many polynomial equations in table entries, for example $p_{abc}p_c-p_{ac}p_{bc}=0$ for every assignment, with these symbols meaning the corresponding marginal probabilities. At every absent CI, at least one such polynomial is nonzero at $p$. Choose one witness for each absent statement. By continuity, all the finitely many witnesses remain nonzero in a sufficiently small neighborhood. The rational parameter approximations can be taken in that neighborhood. All forced CIs remain true by factorization and all absent CIs remain false by the witnesses, proving the restricted claim. Trivial empty-set CIs are automatically preserved.
General chart criterion and the exact unproved step.
The same proof applies whenever a rational map, defined on an open parameter neighborhood, takes that whole neighborhood into the asserted CI locus and the probability simplex, takes one parameter point to the given exact representation, has nonvanishing denominators there, and has rational parameter points arbitrarily nearby. Rational outputs preserve the asserted equations and finitely many nonzero witnesses preserve every absent CI. This is a sufficient criterion, proved by those two continuity arguments. It is not a proof that every CI locus has such a chart.

For example, even the displayed chain approximation fails to preserve an additional marginal independence unless its parameters satisfy the additional polynomial exactly. General rational approximation in the ambient simplex is still less adequate. The strictly positive normalized set \[ u^2=2v^2,\qquad u+v=1,\qquad u,v>0 \] has a real point $v=1/(1+\sqrt2)$ but no rational point: rational $u,v$ with $v>0$ would imply a rational square root of two. Its irrationality follows by reducing $a^2=2b^2$ to coprime integers, forcing both $a$ and $b$ even. This set is not asserted to be a CI variety or a counterexample to the conjecture. It disproves only the proposed general inference from rational defining polynomials and a real point to rational points on the same locus.
