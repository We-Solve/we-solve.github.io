Testing a visualization certificate based on sampled agreement

An online visualization collection needs to distinguish a displayed approximation from a certified mathematical object. Test the route of certifying a scalar graph by agreement on finitely many sample abscissas $x_1,\ldots,x_m$. Assume these are distinct real numbers.
Proposition: Sample agreement alone does not identify even a polynomial function. If instead a function is known to be $L$-Lipschitz on a sampled interval and adjacent sample gaps are at most $h$, its nearest-sample approximation has error at most $Lh/2$.
Proof: The polynomials $f(x)=0$ and $g(x)=\prod_{j=1}^m(x-x_j)^2$ agree at every sample, but $g(x)>0$ at every real $x$ outside the sample set. Multiplying $g$ by an arbitrary positive constant makes the discrepancy at any such point arbitrarily large. For the second claim assume the interval endpoints are included among the samples. Every point lies between two adjacent samples at distance at most $h$; its distance to the nearer endpoint is at most $h/2$. Lipschitz continuity gives $|f(x)-f(x_j)|\le L|x-x_j|\le Lh/2$ for that sample. ∎
Thus a finite screenshot or sample table by itself cannot certify all features between sampled points. A bound on regularity changes the available guarantee, but verifying the bound is a separate obligation. The result is restricted to one-dimensional scalar graphs; it does not automatically validate topological, geometric or interactive visualizations.
