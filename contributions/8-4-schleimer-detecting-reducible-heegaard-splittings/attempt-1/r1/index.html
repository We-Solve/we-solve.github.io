Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a closed orientable three-manifold $M$, a Heegaard splitting is a decomposition $M=V\cup_\Sigma W$ into two handlebodies with common boundary $\Sigma=\partial V=\partial W$, a closed connected orientable surface. A handlebody is a regular neighborhood of a finite connected graph. A Heegaard diagram records finite systems of meridian curves bounding disks in $V$ and $W$; it supplies a finite combinatorial input for an algorithm.

An essential simple closed curve on $\Sigma$ is one that does not bound a disk in $\Sigma$. The splitting is reducible if there exist properly embedded disks $D\subset V$ and $E\subset W$ with the same essential boundary $\partial D=\partial E=\gamma\subset\Sigma$. Such a $\gamma$ is a reducing curve. The requested algorithm must terminate on every finite input diagram, decide whether such a curve exists, and produce an encoded reducing curve whenever the answer is affirmative. No polynomial-time bound is required by the supplied question.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Is there an algorithm to detect whether a Heegaard splitting is reducible and, if so, find a reducing curve? [S2]

Research attempt: A homological obstruction misses separating reducing curves

For a genus-$g$ splitting let \[ L_V=\ker(H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(V;\mathbb Z)),\qquad L_W=\ker(H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(W;\mathbb Z)). \] If a reducing curve $\gamma$ exists, then $[\gamma]\in L_V\cap L_W$ because it bounds a disk in each side. It is tempting to conclude that a zero intersection of these subgroups rules out reducing curves. That step fails because an essential separating curve can have zero homology class.

Here is a concrete genus-two construction realizing the failure. Form each handlebody as the boundary connected sum of two solid tori. The connecting one-handle has a co-core disk, whose boundary $\gamma$ separates the boundary surface into two once-punctured tori. Glue the two handlebodies so these curves agree, and on each punctured-torus block take meridian systems represented by $a_i$ on the first side and $b_i$ on the second, where \[ H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)= \mathbb Z a_1\oplus\mathbb Z b_1\oplus \mathbb Z a_2\oplus\mathbb Z b_2, \qquad a_i\cdot b_j=\delta_{ij}. \] Such a gluing exists: the interchange of the two generators on each punctured-torus block is induced by a homeomorphism of that block (rotate a square torus model, adjusting orientation as necessary), and the boundary maps can be joined across the connecting annulus. The side interchange may be chosen orientation reversing so the resulting manifold is orientable.

Each solid torus retracts onto its longitude circle and its meridian bounds a disk. Retraction of their boundary connected sum onto the wedge of the two core circles therefore gives \[ L_V=\mathbb Z a_1\oplus\mathbb Z a_2,\qquad L_W=\mathbb Z b_1\oplus\mathbb Z b_2, \quad L_V\cap L_W=0. \] Nevertheless the two co-core disks have common boundary $\gamma$. This curve is essential on $\Sigma$: either side of it is a punctured torus, rather than a disk. Its homology class is zero since it is the oriented boundary of either punctured-torus subsurface. Thus the splitting is reducible despite the zero intersection calculation.

The failure is not merely loss of a representative: the necessary invariant itself vanishes on a valid witness. Conversely a common homology vector would not automatically construct embedded disks with the same simple boundary. The algorithmic route needs control of actual disk boundaries up to isotopy, not only their abelianized classes. Checking visible common meridian curves in an input diagram is a sound positive test but may miss curves not present in those finite systems.

Scope and remaining gaps

The first gap is deciding whether the two infinite sets of essential embedded disk boundaries intersect, including separating boundaries annihilated by homology, and producing a curve. The constructed splitting only refutes a linear-algebra decision shortcut; it does not show undecidability.
