Cloud-Organization Limit Cycles

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A limit cycle is an isolated periodic orbit of a dynamical model of cloud organization. Its size, period and amplitude describe different observables. Quasistatic response means tracking the family of equilibrated or periodic states under slow forcing. Diurnal radiation and open boundaries alter the forcing and exchange of mass and energy.

A cycle-response benchmark.
Use the auxiliary complex normal form $\dot z=(\mu+i\omega)z-|z|^2z$, with $\mu>0$, $\omega\ne0$. Writing $z=re^{i\theta}$ gives $\dot r=\mu r-r^3$, $\dot\theta=\omega$. The circle $r_* =\sqrt\mu$ is attracting because the radial linearization is $-2\mu$, and its period is $2\pi/|\omega|$. Amplitude, frequency and radial recovery time are already distinct quantities in this minimal model.
Failure of automatic quasistatic tracking.
Let $\mu(t)=\mu_0+\delta\cos(\Omega t)$, $\mu_0>0$, with small $\delta$, and write $r=\sqrt{\mu_0}+\delta q+O(\delta^2)$. The linear response solves \[ \dot q+2\mu_0q=\sqrt{\mu_0}\cos(\Omega t). \] Its periodic solution has amplitude $\sqrt{\mu_0}/\sqrt{4\mu_0^2+\Omega^2}$ and lag $\arctan(\Omega/(2\mu_0))$. The quasistatic prediction instead gives amplitude $1/(2\sqrt{\mu_0})$ and zero lag. Agreement therefore requires $|\Omega|\ll2\mu_0$, and this requirement becomes restrictive near cycle onset.
