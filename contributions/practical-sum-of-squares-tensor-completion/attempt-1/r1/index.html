Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A polynomial is a sum of squares (SOS) if it is $\sum_j q_j^2$ for polynomials $q_j$. Moment-SOS hierarchies impose positivity through increasingly large truncated moment matrices and corresponding degree-bounded SOS certificates.

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. Matrix completion reconstructs a low-rank matrix from a subset of its entries; matrix sensing reconstructs it from specified linear measurements. Tensor decomposition represents a multiway array as a sum of simple outer products.

Research attempt: Whitening changes the observation model

A fully solvable rank-one observation pattern.
Let $T_{ijk}=a_i b_j c_k$ be a real tensor with $T_{111}\ne0$. If the three coordinate fibers through $(1,1,1)$ are observed, then every entry is determined by \[ T_{ijk}=\frac{T_{i11}\,T_{1j1}\,T_{11k}}{T_{111}^2}. \] Indeed the numerator is $a_i b_j c_k(a_1b_1c_1)^2$. There are $n_1+n_2+n_3-2$ distinct observed entries, and each desired entry is reconstructed by a constant number of arithmetic operations after reading them. For this next boundary example assume all three mode sizes are at least two. Nonzero anchoring is essential: if only these fibers are observed and all their values are zero, the zero tensor and a spike $e_i\otimes e_j\otimes e_k$ with $i,j,k>1$ are indistinguishable. This is a deterministic sampling pattern, not the random-entry theorem in the source, and rank one is not a substantive extension beyond orthogonality.
Why arbitrary rank and arbitrary masks need assumptions.
For any missing coordinate $(i,j,k)$, the zero tensor and $\lambda e_i\otimes e_j\otimes e_k$ give the same observed entries, for every scalar $\lambda$. Even a rank-at-most-one guarantee therefore cannot yield universal recovery from every proper coordinate mask. The source's incoherence and random sampling cannot be dropped merely because a more general nonorthogonal factorization is proposed.
The attempted whitening reduction loses entry samples.
Suppose an invertible mode transformation $W$ were available which made some component vectors orthogonal. Mode-one multiplication gives \[ \widetilde T_{ijk}=\sum_a W_{ia}T_{ajk}. \] A single observed entry of $T$ is generally not an observed entry of $\widetilde T$. With \[ W=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \widetilde T_{1jk}=T_{1jk}+T_{2jk}, \] observing the first slice alone does not determine the transformed first slice. For example, the two rank-one tensors with mode-one vectors $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ and the same nonzero remaining factors agree on the observed first slice, but their transformed first slices differ by a factor of two. Equivalently, the original observations become linear combinations of transformed coordinates involving $W^{-1}$, rather than independent coordinate samples. Thus even known, exact whitening does not put the data into the hypotheses of an entry-sampling completion theorem. Unknown factor-dependent whitening has an additional circularity.
Near parallel factors introduce conditioning as well.
Let $u=e_1$ and $v=e_1+\epsilon e_2$, with $\epsilon>0$. Then \[ \|v^{\otimes3}-u^{\otimes3}\|_F^2=(1+\epsilon^2)^3-1. \] This follows from $\|a^{\otimes3}\|_F^2=\|a\|^6$ and $\langle a^{\otimes3},b^{\otimes3}\rangle=\langle a,b\rangle^3$. Changing two component coefficients by $(1,-1)$ changes the tensor by a quantity tending to zero with $\epsilon$, while the coefficient change has norm $\sqrt2$. Hence a uniform stable decomposition estimate cannot ignore separation of these factors. This does not itself preclude stable recovery of the tensor as a whole: completion and decomposition are different outputs. It does show why a reduction that explicitly recovers and whitens components needs conditioning assumptions absent from a bare nonorthogonality claim.
