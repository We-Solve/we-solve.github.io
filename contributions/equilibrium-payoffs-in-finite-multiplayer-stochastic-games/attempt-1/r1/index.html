On Equilibrium Payoffs in Finite Multiplayer Stochastic Games Partial Results, Explicit Obstructions, and a Conditional Reduction

Abstract

The existence of uniform, limiting-average, or liminf-average Nash equilibrium payoffs in arbitrary finite multiplayer stochastic games remains an open problem. This note collects and critically audits several attempts to address it. We prove a sufficient condition based on a state-independent vanishing-discount equilibrium payoff; exhibit elementary counterexamples to a proposed additive-eigenpair criterion and to naive concatenation of exact finite-horizon equilibria; and formulate a history-dependent finite-deviation criterion. Using a finite-prefix deviator-identification theorem, we prove a conditional conversion from approximate acceptability and small finite-deviation regret to approximate liminf-average Nash equilibrium. For each fixed finite deviation horizon we establish the two required properties simultaneously. The missing step is to produce one profile satisfying the deviation inequalities for every finite horizon. An example shows why pointwise compactness of strategies cannot supply this step automatically. No general existence proof or counterexample is claimed. Statements proved in this note are distinguished from unproved candidate principles and unsuccessful approaches.

Status. Research-progress note; not a solution of the general open problem.

The question and precise payoff conventions

A finite stochastic game is a tuple \[ \Gamma=(I,S,(A_i(s))_{i\in I,s\in S},q,(g_i)_{i\in I}), \] where $I=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and $S$ are nonempty finite sets, each action set $A_i(s)$ is finite and nonempty, $q(\cdot\mid s,a)$ is a probability distribution on $S$, and $g_i(s,a)\in\mathbb R$ is a bounded stage payoff. At stage $t$ the current state $s_t$ is publicly observed, the players independently randomize their actions $a_{i,t}$ conditional on the publicly observed history, receive $g_i(s_t,a_t)$, and the next state is sampled from $q(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$. Actions and states are publicly observed after the stage. Unless stated otherwise, strategies are arbitrary behavioral strategies with perfect recall and no external mediator.

Write \[ \bar g_{i,n}=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n g_i(s_t,a_t),\qquad f_i^-(\omega)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar g_{i,n}(\omega),\qquad f_i^+(\omega)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bar g_{i,n}(\omega). \] We use the expected liminf payoff $u_i^-(s,{\sigma})={\mathbb{E}}_{s,{\sigma}}f_i^-$, with analogous notation $u_i^+$. One also encounters $\liminf_n{\mathbb{E}}_{s,{\sigma}}\bar g_{i,n}$ and $\lim_n{\mathbb{E}}_{s,{\sigma}}\bar g_{i,n}$ (when the latter exists). The expected liminf and liminf of expectations are different: interchanging expectation and liminf is not justified in general. A behavioral profile ${\sigma}$ is an ${\varepsilon}$-equilibrium for $u^-$ from state $s$ when \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-69-6d-69-6e-66-65-71} u_i^-(s,\tau_i,{\sigma}_{-i})\le u_i^-(s,{\sigma})+{\varepsilon} \quad\text{for every player }i\text{ and every behavioral }\tau_i. \end{equation} A liminf-average equilibrium payoff is a vector $c$ such that for every ${\varepsilon}>0$ there is an ${\varepsilon}$-equilibrium (from the specified initial state) with payoff within ${\varepsilon}$ of $c$. This is an approximate equilibrium-payoff concept, not an assertion that a zero-error equilibrium exists.

A profile is a uniform ${\varepsilon}$-equilibrium when, for every sufficiently large finite horizon $n$, its expected $n$-stage average admits no unilateral gain above ${\varepsilon}$, and its payoff is also nearly constant across all sufficiently large horizons; one may additionally require the analogous inequality for every sufficiently small normalized discount factor $\lambda$, where \[ \gamma_i^\lambda(s,{\sigma}) ={\mathbb{E}}_{s,{\sigma}}\sum_{t=1}^\infty \lambda(1-\lambda)^{t-1}g_i(s_t,a_t). \] A uniform equilibrium payoff $c$ is realized asymptotically by uniform approximate equilibria with their finite-horizon and discounted payoffs converging to $c$. The formulations above are made precise in the statement and proof of Theorem Theorem (State-flat sufficient condition). We distinguish these concepts rather than silently identifying them.

Question (General multiplayer existence problem): For every finite stochastic game, and every initial state, does there exist (a) a uniform equilibrium payoff; or at least (b) a limiting-average equilibrium payoff; or at least (c) a liminf-average equilibrium payoff in the sense of (eq:liminfeq)?
Two-player finite stochastic games have uniform equilibrium payoffs by work of Vieille; corresponding assertions for arbitrary finite numbers of players are not established by the references reviewed here [vieille2002,fleschsolan2023,solanvieille2025,aps2026].

Established background and a map of the attempts

For each $\lambda\in(0,1]$, finite discounted stochastic games have stationary Nash equilibria [fink1964]. Their payoff vectors generally depend on the starting state, and a discounted equilibrium need not remain approximately optimal under a limiting-average criterion. In the general-payoff framework, Flesch and Solan prove the existence of minmax-acceptable profiles in all subgames and of approximate equilibria in at least one subgame, not necessarily at an arbitrarily prescribed initial state [fleschsolan2023]. Deviator identification for nearly certain target events is available by Alon, Gunby, He, Shmaya, and Solan [alon2024].

A valid conditional vanishing-discount theorem

Choose a semialgebraic branch $\lambda\mapsto(x^\lambda,v^\lambda)$ of stationary discounted Nash equilibria for all sufficiently small $\lambda>0$. Such a branch can be selected because the finite-dimensional discounted stationary equilibrium correspondence has a semialgebraic graph; a stationary equilibrium exists for every positive $\lambda$. At finitely many other discount parameters, choose arbitrary stationary equilibria. Assume $|g_i|\le M$.
Theorem (State-flat sufficient condition): Suppose there are constants $c_i$ such that \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-66-6c-61-74} \lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}v_i^\lambda(s)=c_i \quad\text{for every }i\in I,\ s\in S. \end{equation} Thus the limit is independent of the starting state for each player. Then $c=(c_i)_{i\in I}$ is a uniform equilibrium payoff. The same profiles also yield equilibrium payoffs $c$ for the expected liminf-average criterion and for the corresponding limsup-average criterion and the standard finite-horizon limit-payoff formulations.
Proof: For player $i$, state $s$, and pure action $b_i$, optimality against $x_{-i}^\lambda$ implies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-65-6c-6c-6d-61-6e-64-69-73-63} v_i^\lambda(s)\ge\lambda g_i(s,b_i,x_{-i}^\lambda(s)) +(1-\lambda)Q_{b_i,x_{-i}^\lambda(s)}v_i^\lambda. \end{equation} Here $Q_{b_i,x_{-i}}v=\sum_{s'}q(s'\mid s,b_i,x_{-i})v(s')$. Equality holds on averaging over $x_i^\lambda(s)$.

With $r\in\mathbb N$, set $v_{i,r}=v_i^{1/r}$ and $x_r=x^{1/r}$. Multiplying (eq:bellmandisc) by $r$ yields \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-65-6c-6c-6d-61-6e-72} r v_{i,r}(s)\ge g_i(s,b_i,x_{-i,r}(s)) +(r-1)Q_{b_i,x_{-i,r}(s)}v_{i,r}. \end{equation} Let $\pi^N$ prescribe $x_r$ when $r$ stages remain in a block of length $N$. Define \[ \Delta_r=\max_{i,s}|v_{i,r-1}(s)-v_{i,r}(s)|,\qquad E_N=\sum_{r=2}^N(r-1)\Delta_r. \] Induction on $r$, using (eq:bellmanr), gives for all starting states and every unilateral deviation during the block \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-6c-6f-63-6b-73} {\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_i,\pi^N_{-i},s}\sum_{t=1}^N g_i \le N v_{i,N}(s)+E_N, \end{equation} and, using equality under the prescribed mixed actions, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-6c-6f-63-6b-62-61-73-65-6c-69-6e-65} \left|{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^N,s}\sum_{t=1}^N g_i-Nv_{i,N}(s)\right| \le E_N. \end{equation} The induction works for arbitrary within-block history-dependent deviations: a best-response continuation can be optimized at each successor history, while $\Delta_r$ bounds the error from replacing $v_{i,r-1}$ by $v_{i,r}$.

A bounded one-variable semialgebraic function with a finite limit has a Puiseux expansion near zero. In particular, for some $\alpha>0$ and $C<\infty$, \[ \left\|\frac{{\mathrm d} v_i^\lambda}{{\mathrm d}\lambda}\right\|_\infty \le C\lambda^{\alpha-1} \] for sufficiently small $\lambda>0$. Therefore $\Delta_r=O(r^{-1-\alpha})$ and \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-72-72-6f-72} E_N=O\left(\sum_{r=2}^Nr^{-\alpha}\right)+O(1)=o(N). \end{equation} Under (eq:flat), the errors \[ d_N=\max_{i,s}|v_{i,N}(s)-c_i|+E_N/N \] converge to zero. Consequently every $N$-block, from every initial state, has equilibrium expected average within $d_N$ of $c_i$, and a deviator's expected average is at most $c_i+d_N$.

Repeat $\pi^N$ periodically, restarting only the countdown after each block; the physical state is not reset. Call this profile ${\sigma}^N$. Conditioning at the start of each block and summing the bounds gives, for all $n$, \begin{align} {\mathbb{E}}_{s,{\sigma}^N}\bar g_{i,n}&\ge c_i-d_N-2MN/n,\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-6e-69-66-6f-72-6d-6c-6f-77-65-72}\\ {\mathbb{E}}_{s,\tau_i,{\sigma}^N_{-i}}\bar g_{i,n} &\le c_i+d_N+2MN/n.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-6e-69-66-6f-72-6d-75-70-70-65-72} \end{align} The same argument gives an upper bound on the prescribed payoff. Since $N$ is fixed while $n\to\infty$, this proves the long finite-horizon part of uniform equilibrium.

For completeness, the discounted criterion follows from the same block bounds. Let $\theta\downarrow0$ be an evaluation discount factor, distinct from the within-block parameters $1/r$. The normalized discount weights of successive complete blocks satisfy \[ \frac{\theta\sum_{t=kN+1}^{(k+1)N}(1-\theta)^{t-1}g_t} {\theta\sum_{t=kN+1}^{(k+1)N}(1-\theta)^{t-1}} =\frac1N\sum_{t=kN+1}^{(k+1)N}g_t+O(MN\theta), \] uniformly in the block index and realization; the geometric weights within one block have relative variation $O(N\theta)$. The block inequalities then imply that discounted prescribed payoffs are within $d_N+O(MN\theta)$ of $c_i$ and deviations yield at most $c_i+d_N+O(MN\theta)$. First take $N$ large, then $\theta$ small.

For the pathwise liminf/limsup criteria, let $Y_{i,k}$ be the total reward in block $k$. Under either prescribed play or one fixed arbitrary unilateral deviation, the bounded martingale differences \[ Y_{i,k}-{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{i,k}\mid{\mathcal H}_{kN}] \] have averages converging almost surely to zero. Under prescribed play, all conditional block expectations are within $Nd_N$ of $Nc_i$; under a unilateral deviation, they are at most $N(c_i+d_N)$. Thus almost surely under ${\sigma}^N$, \[ c_i-d_N\le\liminf_n\bar g_{i,n} \le\limsup_n\bar g_{i,n}\le c_i+d_N, \] and under any $\tau_i$, $\limsup_n\bar g_{i,n}\le c_i+d_N$ almost surely. Bounded convergence inequalities then show that ${\sigma}^N$ is a $2d_N$-equilibrium for both ${\mathbb{E}}\liminf$ and ${\mathbb{E}}\limsup$, with payoffs within $d_N$ of $c$. For formulations using $\liminf_n{\mathbb{E}}\bar g_{i,n}$ or $\limsup_n{\mathbb{E}}\bar g_{i,n}$, the same conditional-block estimates apply directly. For the prescribed periodic finite-memory profile, the ordinary limit $\lim_n{\mathbb{E}}\bar g_{i,n}$ exists by finite-state Markov-chain ergodic theory. An arbitrary deviating strategy need not have a well-defined ordinary limit of expected average payoffs; no unqualified assertion is made here for a criterion that is undefined under such deviations. This completes the claimed approximate-payoff conclusions. ∎

Remark: The state-flat hypothesis is essential to this proof. Without it, (eq:blocks) bounds the payoff in block $k$ by $N v_{i,N}(S_{kN+1})+E_N$. A deviator can change the next block's starting-state distribution. The state-dependent benchmarks need not telescope or be bounded by one common target. Thus Theorem Theorem (State-flat sufficient condition) is a sufficient result, not a proof of Question Question (General multiplayer existence problem).

Two false generalizations of the discounted argument

A state-independent additive eigenpair need not exist

An earlier attempt asked for a constant $c\in\mathbb R^m$, a potential $\Phi:S\to\mathbb R^m$, a length $N$, and Nash equilibrium payoffs $y(s)$ in the $N$-stage game with terminal payment $\Phi(S_{N+1})$ such that \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-41-45} \max_{s\in S}\norm{y(s)-\Phi(s)-Nc}_\infty\le N\eta. \tag{AE} \end{equation} If valid, the terminal potentials would telescope across blocks. But the assertion that (eq:AE) holds for every finite stochastic game and arbitrarily small $\eta$ is false.
Proposition: For $\eta<\tfrac12$, there is a finite stochastic game for which no $N$, $c$, or $\Phi$ satisfies (eq:AE).
Proof: Take two absorbing states $z_0,z_1$, no meaningful choices, and reward $0$ per stage at $z_0$ and reward $1$ per stage at $z_1$ for every player. From $z_j$ the only terminal-reward payoff is $y_i(z_j)=Nj+\Phi_i(z_j)$. Condition (eq:AE) would imply $|c_i|\le\eta$ and $|1-c_i|\le\eta$, a contradiction. ∎
Remark: This is not a counterexample to equilibrium existence: every profile is an equilibrium. It only rules out a state-independent additive-eigenpair principle of the stated form.

Exact countdown blocks fail in the Big Match

Consider the two-player constant-sum Big Match, with player 1's reward shown below and player 2's reward equal to $1-g_1$. A star denotes permanent absorption at the indicated payoff: \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-42-4d} \begin{array}{c|cc} & L&R\\\hline T&1^{*}&0^{*}\\ B&0&1 \end{array}. \end{equation} At $r$ remaining stages, if the value of the continuation with $r-1$ stages is $(r-1)/2$, the matrix of total continuation rewards is \[ \begin{array}{c|cc} &L&R\\\hline T&r&0\\ B&(r-1)/2&(r+1)/2 \end{array}. \] Player 1 plays $T$ with probability $p_r=1/(r+1)$ and player 2 mixes $L,R$ equally. Direct indifference checks and induction show that this is an exact equilibrium of the $N$-stage game with value $N/2$. It agrees with the countdown $x^{1/r}$ of stationary discounted equilibria: \[ v^\lambda=\tfrac12\quad\text{at the nonabsorbing state}, \qquad p^\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}. \] The state-flat hypothesis fails: the two absorbing states have discounted payoffs $0$ and $1$.
Proposition (Exact blocks, fixed profitable deviation): Repeat the exact $N$-stage equilibrium countdown block forever, restarting the countdown after each $N$ stages but not resetting the physical state. For every $N$, player 2 has a unilateral deviation increasing expected liminf-average payoff by $1/2$.
Proof: Conditional on entering a block without prior absorption, the probability of surviving its $N$ stages is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-75-72-76-69-76-61-6c} \prod_{r=1}^N\left(1-\frac1{r+1}\right) =\prod_{r=1}^N\frac r{r+1}=\frac1{N+1}. \end{equation} Thus absorption occurs almost surely during the infinite concatenation. Under prescribed play, the absorbing column is fair, so both players' expected limiting average payoffs equal $1/2$. If player 2 plays $R$ forever, the stopping hazard is unchanged, but absorption always yields player 1 reward $0$ and player 2 reward $1$. The deviation gain of player 2 is exactly $1/2$ for every $N$. ∎
A quantitative finite-horizon version makes the failure especially clear. Against the deviation $R$ forever, player 1's expected total reward in each reached block equals $N/2$. By (eq:survival), the expected total reward over all time is $(N/2)\sum_{k\ge0}(N+1)^{-k}=(N+1)/2$. Therefore ${\mathbb{E}}\bar g_{1,n}\le(N+1)/(2n)$, whereas the prescribed profile yields expected average $1/2$. Exact block Nash optimality does not prevent deviations with effects that persist after the nominal block.
Proposition (Predetermined stopping hazards are insufficient): Suppose player 1's stopping probability at date $t$, conditional on nonabsorption, is a fixed number $p_t$, independent of observed play. Then player 2 can force player 1's expected liminf-average reward to be arbitrarily close to $0$.
Proof: If $\sum_t p_t=\infty$, playing $R$ forever causes absorption at payoff $0$ almost surely. If $\sum_t p_t<\infty$, choose $K$ with $\sum_{t>K}p_t<\delta$; player 2 plays $R$ through $K$ and $L$ thereafter. Absorption before $K$ yields $0$, while positive limiting payoff is possible only if player 1 stops after $K$. The probability of that event is at most $\sum_{t>K}p_t<\delta$. Since payoffs lie in $[0,1]$, player 1's expected liminf-average payoff is at most $\delta$. ∎
The Big Match itself has approximate long-run equilibria; the propositions refute time-only implementations, not its equilibrium existence [bigmatchspace].

Discounted equilibrium limits need not be uniform payoffs

Even without the block-concatenation error, a direct assertion that every vanishing-discount Nash payoff limit is uniform is false: Sorin's example, discussed by Renault and Ziliotto, has a nonempty discounted equilibrium payoff limit set disjoint from the set of uniform equilibrium payoffs [renaultziliotto]. This is a distinct reason why a universal vanishing-discount proof needs additional hypotheses or a different target.

A history-dependent, finite-deviation conversion theorem

The previous failures suggest avoiding one state-independent payoff target at every intermediate date. We now let the continuation benchmark be an actual payoff conditional on the entire finite public history. Normalize rewards in this and the remaining sections to $[0,1]$.

For a profile ${\sigma}$, history $h$, and player $i$, put \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-56} V_i^{\sigma}(h)={\mathbb{E}}_{h,{\sigma}}f_i^-. \end{equation} Let \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6d-69-6e-6d-61-78} v_i(s)=\inf_{\rho_{-i}}\sup_{\tau_i} {\mathbb{E}}_{s,\tau_i,\rho_{-i}}f_i^- \end{equation} be the individual minmax level. Opponents in $\rho_{-i}$ use an ordinary joint profile of independently randomized behavioral strategies, rather than a mediator. Since $f_i^-$ is shift-invariant, the minmax level after a finite history depends only on the current state. By the definition of the infimum, for every $\zeta>0$ there is a joint-profile punishment against $i$ yielding at most $v_i(s)+\zeta$ against any strategy of $i$.

Let $\tau_i\star_N{\sigma}_i$ be a strategy that may differ from ${\sigma}_i$ through stage $N$ and equals ${\sigma}_i$ at all subsequent histories. Define \begin{align} A({\sigma})&=\sup_{i,h}[v_i(s_h)-V_i^{\sigma}(h)]_+,\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-41}\\ R_N({\sigma})&=\max_i\sup_{\tau_i}\left[ u_i^-(s_0,\tau_i\star_N{\sigma}_i,{\sigma}_{-i}) -u_i^-(s_0,{\sigma})\right],\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-52-4e}\\ R_{\mathrm{fin}}({\sigma})&=\sup_{N\ge1}R_N({\sigma}).\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-52-66-69-6e} \end{align} These $R_N$ use infinite-horizon tail payoffs; only the length of the deviation is bounded. This is not an $N$-stage expected-payoff test.

Theorem (Finite-deviation conversion; conditional on a starting profile): Suppose $A({\sigma})\le\alpha$ and $R_{\mathrm{fin}}({\sigma})\le\beta$. For every $\rho,\delta\in(0,1)$ there is an ordinary behavioral profile $\widehat{\sigma}$ such that, from $s_0$, \begin{align} |u_i^-(\widehat{\sigma})-u_i^-({\sigma})|&\le\delta &&\text{for every }i,\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-6e-76-70-61-79}\\ \sup_{\tau_i}\left[ u_i^-(\tau_i,\widehat{\sigma}_{-i})-u_i^-(\widehat{\sigma}) \right] &\le\alpha+\beta+\rho+2\sqrt{m\delta}+\delta &&\text{for every }i.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-6e-76-65-70-73} \end{align} Here $m=|I|$; the estimate uses the finite-prefix identification form of [alon2024, Theorem 2.6 and Section 4], with stochastic transitions treated as prescribed moves of Nature in an alternating perfect-information expansion. In particular, the conclusion does not add an actual mediator to the game.
Proof: Step 1: a continuation supermartingale. For a finite history $h$ define \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-57} W_i(h)=\sup_{N\ge |h|}\sup_{\tau_i\text{ through }N} {\mathbb{E}}_{h,\tau_i,{\sigma}_{-i}}\! \left[V_i^{\sigma}(H_N)\right]. \end{equation} The player returns to ${\sigma}_i$ after $N$. (The indexing of $H_N$ may be shifted by one without changing the argument.) Then \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-57-62-6f-75-6e-64} 0\le V_i^{\sigma}(h)\le W_i(h)\le1, \qquad W_i(s_0)\le u_i^-({\sigma})+\beta. \end{equation} Concatenating a current pure deviation with almost optimal bounded-time deviations from the finitely many successor histories, using one common terminal date, shows \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-57-73-75-70-65-72} {\mathbb{E}}\left[W_i(H_{t+1})\mid H_t=h,a_i,{\sigma}_{-i}(h)\right] \le W_i(h). \end{equation} Thus, when the opponents use ${\sigma}_{-i}$, $W_i(H_t)$ is a bounded supermartingale under every unilateral deviation. The common-date argument is legitimate because at any stage there are finitely many successor public histories.

Step 2: a compact full-payoff-realization set. Under ${\sigma}$, the bounded martingale $V_i^{\sigma}(H_t)={\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}[f_i^-\mid{\mathcal H}_t]$ converges almost surely to $f_i^-$. Accordingly, if \[ G=\{\omega: V_i^{\sigma}(H_t(\omega))\to f_i^-(\omega) \text{ for every }i\}, \] then ${\mathbb{P}}_{\sigma}(G)=1$. The infinite-play space is compact metrizable; regularity of its probability measures gives a compact $K\subset G$ with ${\mathbb{P}}_{\sigma}(K)>1-\delta$. Let $T$ be the first finite public history whose cylinder has empty intersection with $K$. Closedness of $K$ implies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-78-69-74} \{T=\infty\}=K. \end{equation}

Step 3: identify an offending player at a finite exit prefix. Apply the closed-target finite-prefix blame theorem [alon2024, Section 4] to the prescribed law and target $K$. The standard reduction of stochastic transitions to an additional non-strategic Nature agent is performed by splitting each stage into (i) simultaneous player actions and (ii) Nature's publicly observed state draw according to $q$. Nature has a prescribed behavioral strategy and does not deviate. There are $m+1$ agents in the identification problem; map any attribution to Nature to an arbitrary genuine player. We obtain a finite-exit-prefix-measurable blame assignment $j(H_T)\in I$ such that, for each genuine sole deviator $i$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-6c-61-6d-65} {\mathbb{P}}_{\tau_i,{\sigma}_{-i}}\{T<\infty,\ j(H_T)\ne i\} \le 2\sqrt{m\delta}. \end{equation} At $T$, modify the continuation: the players other than $j(H_T)$ switch permanently to a $\rho$-minmax punishment of that player from the current state. Call the resulting profile $\widehat{\sigma}$. The event $T<\infty$ has prescribed probability at most $\delta$; therefore boundedness of $f_i^-$ gives (eq:convpay).

Step 4: bound an arbitrary infinite deviation. Fix $i$ and an unrestricted $\tau_i$. Before $T$, the opponents use ${\sigma}_{-i}$; hence $W_i(H_{t\wedge T})$ is a bounded supermartingale, converging almost surely to some $Z_i$ with ${\mathbb{E}} Z_i\le W_i(s_0)$. On $\{T=\infty\}$, the path lies in $K$, so \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6f-6e-4b} f_i^-=\lim_tV_i^{\sigma}(H_t) \le\lim_tW_i(H_t)=Z_i. \end{equation} On $\{T<\infty,j(H_T)=i\}$, the punishment yields conditional expected payoff at most \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-72-72-65-63-74-62-6c-61-6d-65} v_i(s_{H_T})+\rho \le V_i^{\sigma}(H_T)+\alpha+\rho \le W_i(H_T)+\alpha+\rho. \end{equation} On incorrect blame use the payoff bound $1$ and (eq:blame). Because the law of the stopped history is unaffected by the punishment that follows the exit, \[ u_i^-(\tau_i,\widehat{\sigma}_{-i}) \le{\mathbb{E}} Z_i+\alpha+\rho+2\sqrt{m\delta} \le u_i^-({\sigma})+\beta+\alpha+\rho+2\sqrt{m\delta}. \] Together with (eq:convpay), this proves (eq:conveps). ∎

Remark (Nature and observability): The identification step requires public observation of realized actions and states and a known transition law. It should not be applied verbatim to games with hidden actions, private states, or an endogenous unobserved mediator. The alternating Nature construction is a device for applying a finite-prefix testing theorem; it does not expand the feasible deviations in the original game.

The Big Match passes the finite-deviation test

In (eq:BM), prescribe player 1 to play $B$ forever and player 2 to choose independent fair $L/R$ forever. At every unabsorbed history, \[ V^{\sigma}(h)=(1/2,1/2), \] and at absorbing histories $V^{\sigma}$ is the absorbing reward vector. The minmax value to each player is $1/2$ in the nonabsorbing state and the fixed terminal reward after absorption; hence $A({\sigma})=0$. Every bounded-duration deviation of player 1 either leads to an absorption whose expected payoff is $1/2$, or returns to continuation payoff $1/2$. Every bounded-duration deviation of player 2 affects only finitely many rewards, because player 1 never stops. Thus \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-42-4d-63-72-69-74-65-72-69-61} A({\sigma})=R_{\mathrm{fin}}({\sigma})=0. \end{equation} Yet this ${\sigma}$ is not itself an equilibrium: player 2 can play $L$ forever to obtain payoff $1$. Theorem Theorem (Finite-deviation conversion; conditional on a starting profile) yields approximate equilibria by testing deviations and assigning punishment. This is a consistency check on the conditional conversion theorem, not a new solution of the Big Match.

A fixed-cutoff construction in every finite game

The following is the strongest unconditional step from the finite-deviation approach.
Theorem (Finite-prefix construction): For every finite stochastic game, every $\alpha>0$, and every integer $N\ge1$, there is a behavioral strategy profile ${\sigma}^{(N)}$ satisfying \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-72-65-66-69-78} A({\sigma}^{(N)})\le\alpha, \qquad R_N({\sigma}^{(N)})=0. \end{equation}
Proof: The minmax-acceptability theorem of Flesch and Solan [fleschsolan2023, Theorem 4.4] supplies a profile $\pi$ with \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-63-63-65-70-74-61-62-6c-65-70-69} V_i^\pi(h)\ge v_i(s_h)-\alpha \qquad\text{for all players and all finite histories }h. \end{equation} Construct a finite extensive-form game for the first $N$ stages, with terminal payoff $V_i^\pi(h_N)$ at each date-$N$ history; after date $N$ prescribe $\pi$. Choose a mixed Nash equilibrium in every finite continuation game by backward induction and call the full profile ${\sigma}^{(N)}$.

A deviation ending by date $N$ cannot improve player $i$'s expected tail payoff: conditional on the date-$N$ history, the remaining payoff is precisely $V_i^\pi(h_N)$. Thus $R_N({\sigma}^{(N)})=0$.

To propagate acceptability backward, use the dynamic-programming identity for the state-dependent minmax level with shift-invariant payoff: \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6d-69-6e-6d-61-78-42-65-6c-6c-6d-61-6e} v_i(s)=\min_{x_{-i}\in\prod_{j\ne i}\Delta(A_j(s))} \max_{a_i\in A_i(s)} \sum_{a_{-i},s'}x_{-i}(a_{-i})q(s'\mid s,a_i,a_{-i})v_i(s'). \end{equation} For clarity, (eq:minmaxBellman) uses product mixed actions of the opponents. It follows by decomposing an arbitrary opponent strategy into its first-stage product mixed action and continuation profiles and using the shift-invariance of $f_i^-$. The upper bound concatenates near-minmax opponent continuation profiles after each observed action-state history; the lower bound responds optimally at the first stage and then nearly best-responds to the opponents' continuations. Only finitely many first-stage successors arise, so the approximation errors can be made arbitrarily small.

If all next-history continuation values $y_i(h')$ satisfy $y_i(h')\ge v_i(s_{h'})-\alpha$, the current one-shot Nash equilibrium with opponent product mixed action $x_{-i}$ gives \[ \begin{split} y_i(h) &\ge\max_{a_i}{\mathbb{E}}[y_i(H_{t+1})\mid h,a_i,x_{-i}]\\ &\ge\max_{a_i}{\mathbb{E}}[v_i(S_{t+1})\mid h,a_i,x_{-i}]-\alpha\\ &\ge v_i(s_h)-\alpha, \end{split} \] by (eq:minmaxBellman). This inducts backward from (eq:acceptablepi). After date $N$, acceptability again follows from (eq:acceptablepi). ∎

The remaining quantifier gap and a compactness warning

By Theorem Theorem (Finite-deviation conversion; conditional on a starting profile), the following unproved selection principle would settle the liminf-average part of Question Question (General multiplayer existence problem):
Question (Simultaneous finite-deviation criterion): Does every finite stochastic game, for each initial state $s_0$ and $\eta>0$, admit a single profile ${\sigma}$ such that \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6d-69-73-73-69-6e-67} A({\sigma})\le\eta, \qquad \sup_{N\ge1}R_N({\sigma})\le\eta\ ? \tag{FD} \end{equation}
If (eq:missing) held for arbitrarily small $\eta$, the conversion theorem would give approximate liminf-average equilibria. Compactness of the numerical payoff vectors (not of their strategy realizations) would then yield a liminf-average equilibrium payoff in the approximate sense defined in Section sec:model.

Theorem Theorem (Finite-prefix construction) proves \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-71-75-61-6e-74-69-66-69-65-72-73} \forall\eta>0\ \forall N\ \exists{\sigma}^{(N)}: \quad A({\sigma}^{(N)})\le\eta,\ R_N({\sigma}^{(N)})=0, \end{equation} whereas (eq:missing) asks for \[ \forall\eta>0\ \exists{\sigma}\ \forall N: \quad A({\sigma})\le\eta,\ R_N({\sigma})\le\eta. \] The quantifier interchange is not justified.

Proposition (Tail payoffs need not survive pointwise strategy limits): There is a one-player two-state stochastic game and a sequence of exact equilibrium strategies ${\sigma}^{(N)}$ converging pointwise at every fixed history to a strategy ${\sigma}^\infty$, such that \[ u^-({\sigma}^{(N)})=1\quad\forall N, \qquad u^-({\sigma}^\infty)=0. \]
Proof: At nonabsorbing state $s$, wait yields reward $0$ and remains in $s$, while stop moves to an absorbing state of permanent reward $1$. Let ${\sigma}^{(N)}$ wait through date $N-1$ and stop at date $N$; after any off-path history that remains unabsorbed at or after that date, stop immediately. Every such strategy is optimal and has tail payoff $1$ from every history. On each fixed finite history, however, as $N\to\infty$ its prescribed action is eventually wait. The pointwise limit waits forever and gets tail payoff $0$. ∎
This example does not disprove (eq:missing): choosing stop immediately satisfies it. It proves only that an arbitrary strategy-limit argument fails. The key requirement is to construct a profile for which actual realized tail payoffs and all finite-deviation incentive inequalities survive simultaneously.

Research directions and boundaries of the present note

  1. History-dependent selection. Find a direct construction or a compactness theorem tailored to actual continuation payoffs $V_i^{\sigma}(h)$ and the entire sequence $(R_N)_{N\ge1}$. A mere subsequence of behavior profiles does not suffice by Proposition Proposition (Tail payoffs need not survive pointwise strategy limits).
  2. Tests before profitable steering. Develop a deviation test that limits the probability of reaching higher individual minmax levels before the player receives effective punishment. This is the central state-dependent continuation obstruction highlighted in [fleschsolan2023].
  3. Do not conflate solution concepts. Correlated equilibrium, existence from some initial state, and existence at every initial state are different assertions. A general correlated-existence theorem does not supply the desired ordinary Nash profile.
  4. Restricted positive results. Specialized approaches for absorbing and quitting games are informative but do not answer the arbitrary finite-state multiplayer question. Recent examples include [solanvieille2025,aps2026].
Remark (What is and is not claimed): Theorem Theorem (State-flat sufficient condition), Propositions Proposition, Proposition (Exact blocks, fixed profitable deviation), Proposition (Predetermined stopping hazards are insufficient), Theorem Theorem (Finite-deviation conversion; conditional on a starting profile), and Theorem Theorem (Finite-prefix construction) are mathematical statements proved here, the conversion theorem relying on the published deviator-identification and acceptability results explicitly cited. The condition (eq:missing) remains unproved. Neither the state-flat result nor the finite-cutoff construction, taken alone or together, resolves Question Question (General multiplayer existence problem). No counterexample to the open existence statement is exhibited here.
