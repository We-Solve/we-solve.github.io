Uniform Equilibrium in Finite Stochastic Games Partial Results, a Four-Player Quitting-Game Audit, and Obstructions to General Proof Strategies

Abstract

Does every stochastic game with finitely many players, states, and actions admit a uniform Nash equilibrium payoff? This note consolidates a sequence of proof and counterexample attempts, but does not settle that open question. It records two sufficient verification devices: a survival-weighted Bellman-defect criterion for quitting games and a continuation-potential certificate for general finite stochastic games. It proves a local-degree sufficient condition yielding stationary uniform approximate equilibria for a class of quitting games, and applies it to disqualify a previously proposed four-player counterexample matrix for every completion of its simultaneous-quitting payoffs. The matrix audit includes exact complementary-support calculations and an explicit rational stationary equilibrium for one completion. Two unsuccessful general approaches are explained rigorously: predetermined schedules of stationary discounted equilibria fail even in the Big Match, and minmax-acceptable profiles cannot in general be enforced merely by post-deviation punishments. Continuous absorption-path screening and the remaining index-one regime are clearly identified as unresolved, not as proofs of nonexistence. The arguments collected here have not undergone independent peer review; no originality or priority claim is asserted for them.

Status and scope. As of the date above, neither a proof of universal uniform-equilibrium existence nor a finite counterexample is presented here. Statements explicitly marked as a theorem, proposition or lemma are proved in this note; externally established results are cited; computational and heuristic observations are labelled separately.

The open problem and precise definitions

A finite stochastic game consists of a finite player set $I=\{1,\dots,n\}$, a finite state set $S$, finite nonempty action sets $A_i(s)$, bounded stage payoff functions $g_i(s,a)$, and a transition kernel $P(\cdot\mid s,a)\in\Delta(S)$. Stages are simultaneous move; states and past action profiles are publicly observed (perfect monitoring). A behavioral strategy $\sigma_i$ maps every finite public history ending in state $s$ to $\Delta(A_i(s))$. Under initial state $s$ and profile $\sigma$, let \[ \gamma_i^N(s,\sigma)={\mathbb{E}}_{s,\sigma}\left[\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}g_i(s_t,a_t)\right]. \]
Definition (Uniform equilibrium payoff): A family $v=(v_i(s))_{i\in I,s\in S}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{I\times S}$ is a uniform equilibrium payoff if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exist a strategy profile $\sigma^\varepsilon$ and an integer $N_\varepsilon$ such that for each initial state $s$, each player $i$, each behavioral deviation $\tau_i$, and every integer $N\ge N_\varepsilon$, \begin{align} \left|\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)-v_i(s)\right|&\le\varepsilon,\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-6e-69-66-6f-72-6d-2d-70-61-79-6f-66-66}\\ \gamma_i^N(s,\tau_i,\sigma^\varepsilon_{-i})-\gamma_i^N(s,\sigma^\varepsilon)&\le\varepsilon.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-6e-69-66-6f-72-6d-2d-69-6e-63-65-6e-74-69-76-65} \end{align} The same profile works for all sufficiently long horizons $N$; it may depend on $\varepsilon$, but not on $N$.

The main question is whether every game of this kind has such a vector $v$. It is important not to conflate this statement with existence of Nash equilibria for each fixed discount factor, a single limiting-average Nash equilibrium, exact ($\varepsilon=0$) equilibrium, subgame-perfect equilibrium, or a correlated/sunspot equilibrium. Two-player finite stochastic games and a range of structured multiplayer cases have positive results; the unrestricted multiplayer question remains unresolved in the sources consulted [sv2001,akrs2024,aps2026,sv2025]. Two- and three-player quitting games have positive results, whereas the corresponding general existence question for four or more players is expressly discussed as unresolved in [aps2026].

Quitting games and the payoff convention

A standard $n$-player quitting game has a single nonabsorbing state, with actions $C_i$ (continue) and $Q_i$ (quit) for every player. If the first nonempty set of quitters is $T\subseteq I$, the state becomes absorbing with perpetual stage payoff $r(T)\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$; prior to absorption the common stage-payoff vector is $b\in{\mathbb{R}}^n$. If nobody ever quits, the stage payoff remains $b$. All $2^n-1$ nonempty quitting sets have separate terminal payoff vectors. Write $\theta$ for the absorption stage, with $\theta=\infty$ if no quit ever occurs.

The terminal-payoff functional is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-65-72-6d-69-6e-61-6c-2d-66-75-6e-63-74-69-6f-6e-61-6c} \Gamma_i^\infty(\sigma)={\mathbb{E}}_\sigma\left[r_i(T_\theta){\mathbf{1}}_{\{\theta<\infty\}}+b_i{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\theta=\infty\}}\right]. \end{equation} An approximate equilibrium for (eq:terminal-functional) is not, without additional tail control, automatically a uniform equilibrium for the $N$-stage averages. Sections sec:weighted and sec:index establish the required tail estimates explicitly. Whenever singleton quits are under discussion, subtracting the constant $r_i(\{i\})$ from every payoff of player $i$ permits the normalization \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6e-6f-72-6d-61-6c-69-7a-65} r_i(\{i\})=0,\qquad M_{ij}:=r_i(\{j\}). \end{equation} Here the $j$th column of $M$ is the payoff vector when player $j$ quits alone. If these four vectors are displayed as rows of a matrix $R$, then $M=R^{\mathsf T}$.

Established approaches and the small unresolved core

The equilibrium-existence theory for two-player stochastic games, zero-sum stochastic games, and many special classes is extensive; see the bibliography of [akrs2024,sv2025]. We rely here on the following narrower assertions, with references tied to each statement.
  • Solan and Vieille [sv2001] obtain uniform approximate equilibria for quitting games satisfying additional assumptions, and [sv2002example] exhibits a four-player game whose simplest equilibrium uses a two-stage cycle.
  • Solan and Solan [solansolan2020] prove that every quitting game has sunspot approximate equilibria; they also establish uniform ordinary approximate equilibria under a non-$Q$-matrix condition on the relevant complementarity matrix.
  • Ashkenazi-Golan, Krasikov, Rainer, and Solan [akrs2024] compactify quitting strategies by absorption paths, which allow both continuous small-hazard segments and discrete jumps. Sequentially perfect absorption paths characterize approximate terminal equilibria in the precise formulation of their article.
  • The APS paper [aps2026] studies a restricted family of paths (SFAPs) and its associated successor graph and operators; emptiness of that subclass does not establish absence of all equilibria.
  • Solan and Vieille [sv2025] prove an existence theorem for positive recursive absorbing games subject to an explicitly nonrectangular absorption structure, rather than for all finite stochastic games.
  • Solan and Vieille [solanvieille2002corr] establish uniform correlated equilibrium payoffs with an autonomous correlating device. Correlation and public sunspots are stronger information resources than the independent randomization permitted in the original Nash problem.

A negative example among four-player quitting games would settle the original problem negatively, but a proof for quitting games alone would not settle arbitrary finite stochastic games positively. This distinction guides both the matrix and the general-game approaches below.

A survival-weighted Bellman-defect criterion

The first attempt replaced a global fixed point by small one-stage incentive defects accumulated along surviving paths. This section states a precise sufficient criterion; it is not an unconditional construction.

For this section only, assume the nonabsorbing payoff is $b=0$ and $|r_i(T)|\le L$ for every $i$ and nonempty $T$. A strategy in a standard quitting game, conditional on survival to stage $t$, is a vector $p_t=(p_{1,t},\dots,p_{n,t})\in[0,1]^n$. For any player $i$ put \[ \mu_{-i,t}(T)=\prod_{j\in T}p_{j,t} \prod_{\substack{j\notin T\\j\ne i}}(1-p_{j,t}),\quad T\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}. \] Assume the eventual absorbing payoff is well defined under the prescribed profile, and let $u_{i,t}$ be its conditional expectation given survival to $t$. Define the values of quitting now and of continuing for exactly one stage followed by the prescribed strategy: \begin{align} Q_{i,t}&=\sum_{T\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}\mu_{-i,t}(T)r_i(T\cup\{i\}),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-71-2d-73-74-61-67-65}\\ C_{i,t}&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}\mu_{-i,t}(T)r_i(T) +\mu_{-i,t}(\varnothing)u_{i,t+1}.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-2d-73-74-61-67-65} \end{align} By the prescribed mixing rule, \[ u_{i,t}=p_{i,t}Q_{i,t}+(1-p_{i,t})C_{i,t}. \] Set \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-65-66-65-63-74} d_{i,t}=\max\{Q_{i,t},C_{i,t}\}-u_{i,t}\ge0,\quad \rho_{i,1}=1,\quad \rho_{i,t}=\prod_{k<t}\mu_{-i,k}(\varnothing). \end{equation} The number $\rho_{i,t}$ is the chance that all opponents of $i$ have continued through stage $t-1$, independent of what $i$ does.

Proposition (Weighted-defect verification): Suppose $\rho_{i,t}\to0$ for every $i$ and \[ D_i:=\sum_{t=1}^\infty\rho_{i,t}d_{i,t}<\infty. \] Then every unilateral behavioral deviation $\tau_i$ satisfies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-77-65-69-67-68-74-65-64-2d-74-65-72-6d-69-6e-61-6c} \Gamma_i^\infty(\tau_i,p_{-i})\le u_{i,1}+D_i. \end{equation} For every $N$ its $N$-stage average-payoff gain is at most \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-77-65-69-67-68-74-65-64-2d-66-69-6e-69-74-65} D_i+2\kappa_{i,N},\qquad \kappa_{i,N}:=L\left(\rho_{i,N+1}+\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N\rho_{i,t}\right)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0. \end{equation} Consequently a profile with sufficiently small $D_i$ for every $i$ is a uniform approximate equilibrium, with equilibrium payoff $u_{\cdot,1}$ (or with a convergent sequence of such initial payoff vectors).
Proof: At a reached stage $t$, under either action of player $i$, the one-step payoff plus the continuation potential $u_{i,t+1}$ is at most $u_{i,t}+d_{i,t}$. Summing through stage $T$ telescopes the continuation-potential terms and yields \[ {\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_i,p_{-i}}\left[r_i(T_\theta){\mathbf{1}}_{\{\theta\le T\}}+u_{i,T+1}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\theta>T\}}\right]-u_{i,1} \le\sum_{t=1}^T\rho_{i,t}d_{i,t}. \] The probability of reaching stage $T+1$ is bounded by $\rho_{i,T+1}\to0$, uniformly over $i$'s deviation, so bounded convergence gives (eq:weighted-terminal). In the game with $b=0$, replacing the finite average on any path by the terminal payoff costs at most $L$ times the probability of absorption after $N$, plus $L(\theta-1)/N$ when absorption occurs by $N$. As ${\mathbb{P}}(\theta\ge t)\le\rho_{i,t}$, \[ \left|\gamma_i^N(\tau_i,p_{-i})-\Gamma_i^\infty(\tau_i,p_{-i})\right|\le\kappa_{i,N}. \] The same holds under compliance. Applying the estimate twice proves (eq:weighted-finite). Cesàro convergence gives $\kappa_{i,N}\to0$. ∎

If $d_{i,t}=0$, the stagewise complementary-slackness conditions are \[ \begin{array}{ccl} p_{i,t}=0&\Longrightarrow&C_{i,t}\ge Q_{i,t},\\ 0<p_{i,t}<1&\Longrightarrow&C_{i,t}=Q_{i,t},\\ p_{i,t}=1&\Longrightarrow&Q_{i,t}\ge C_{i,t}. \end{array} \] The missing implication is universal existence of sequences satisfying arbitrarily small $D_i$ and the tail condition. The criterion should be treated as a verification tool, not as a solution of the open problem.

Four-player counterexample search: unsuccessful candidates

Empty restricted path classes do not show nonexistence

A four-player singleton-payoff matrix occurring in the precursor discussion of the APS method [apslecture, Example 3.5] is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-52-7a-65-72-6f} R_0=\begin{pmatrix} 0&4&-\frac12&-1\\ -1&0&3&1\\ -\frac18&-1&0&4\\ 4&\frac12&-1&0 \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation} That source establishes emptiness of a restricted path-payoff class, not nonexistence of ordinary uniform equilibrium. The initial search overlooked this logical distinction. As a further finite calculation, for $M_0=R_0^{\mathsf T}$, the LCP with \[ q=(-4,-4,-4,-1)^{\mathsf T} \] has no solution: on each of its sixteen possible positive supports, the complementary equations are either inconsistent or fail nonnegativity. Therefore $M_0$ is not a $Q$-matrix. The non-$Q$ theorem of [solansolan2020], with its associated normal-player reduction, supplies a positive existence result in this setting. In particular, a restricted-path obstruction cannot be presented as a four-player counterexample.

A stronger proposed matrix and its heuristic continuous-path test

The subsequent search proposed \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-52-64-61-67} R^\dagger=\begin{pmatrix} 0&6&-3&-5\\ 6&0&3&-4\\ -2&4&0&3\\ 4&-2&3&0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad M^\dagger=(R^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}. \end{equation} Its singleton sign graph frustrates various one-quitter-at-a-time constructions. There was also a tentative continuous-path calculation: for a nonnegative absolutely continuous curve \[ X(u)=\int_0^u\alpha(t)R^\dagger\,dt, \quad \alpha(t)\in\Delta(I), \quad \alpha_i(t)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ X_i(t)=0\ \text{a.e.}, \] absolute continuity implies $X_i'(t)=0$ for almost every $t$ at which $X_i(t)=0$. Writing $A(t)={\operatorname{supp}}\alpha(t)$ then forces \[ \alpha_{A(t)}(t)^{\mathsf T}R^\dagger_{A(t),A(t)}=0\quad\text{a.e.} \] Since all principal submatrices of order at least two are nonsingular, $\alpha(t)$ must be concentrated on at most one player almost everywhere.

Scope warning: these hypotheses describe a simplified absolutely continuous part of an absorption-path model, not the full definition of a sequentially perfect absorption path. Switching on accumulation sets and especially discrete absorption jumps are not excluded by this calculation. The earlier claim of an almost-complete counterexample proof was unjustified. In fact, the next section gives a stronger, decisive positive result: the entire $R^\dagger$ family has uniform equilibrium payoffs, regardless of its completion.

A local-degree theorem ruling out the proposed matrix

This is the principal positive contribution of the audit. The argument is elementary apart from standard properties of Brouwer degree [gowda2016]. The theorem concerns all numbers of players; the worked certificate concerns four players.

The complementarity map and global projected degree

For a normalized singleton matrix $M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}$ let \[ \phi_M(z):=\min\{z,Mz\} \] coordinatewise. We call $M$ an $R_0$-matrix when $\phi_M^{-1}(0)=\{0\}$. By positive homogeneity, the local degree \[ d(M):=\deg(\phi_M,B_\rho(0),0) \] is defined and independent of $\rho>0$.
Theorem (Degree-based sufficient condition): Suppose a normalized quitting game satisfies
  1. $M$ is $R_0$;
  2. $d(M)\ne1$;
  3. for each player $i$, an opponent $j\ne i$ has $M_{ij}<0$.
Then every assignment of payoffs to simultaneous quit sets, and every nonabsorbing payoff vector $b$, has a uniform equilibrium payoff. Stationary strategy profiles supply the uniform approximate equilibria.
Proof: For $x\in[0,1]^n$, denote the distribution of opponents' quit sets by \[ \mu_{-i,x}(T)=\prod_{j\in T}x_j\prod_{\substack{j\notin T\\j\ne i}}(1-x_j). \] Put \begin{align} a_i(x)&=1-\prod_{j\ne i}(1-x_j),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-69}\\ B_i(x)&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}\mu_{-i,x}(T)r_i(T),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-42-69}\\ Q_i(x)&=\sum_{T\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}\mu_{-i,x}(T)r_i(T\cup\{i\}),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-51-69}\\ F_i(x)&=B_i(x)-a_i(x)Q_i(x).\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-46-69} \end{align} Whenever $a_i(x)>0$, the terminal payoff obtained by continuing forever is $C_i(x)=B_i(x)/a_i(x)$, while quitting immediately gives $Q_i(x)$. Therefore $F_i(x)=a_i(x)(C_i(x)-Q_i(x))$. All $F_i$ are polynomial and independent of $x_i$. Since singleton-quitter self-payoffs are normalized to zero, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6a-61-63} F(x)=Mx+O(\|x\|^2)\qquad (x\to0). \end{equation} The box complementarity conditions for stationary best responses are \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-62-6f-78} F_i(x)\ \begin{cases}\ge0,&x_i=0,\\=0,&0<x_i<1,\\\le0,&x_i=1.\end{cases} \end{equation} With $K=[0,1]^n$ and Euclidean coordinatewise projection $P_K$, define $H(x)=x-P_K(x-F(x))$, extending $F$ polynomially beyond $K$. Its zeros are exactly the solutions of (eq:box), all lying in $K$. On $\Omega=(-1,2)^n$, the homotopy \[ H_t(x)=x-\big((1-t)P_K(x-F(x))+t(1/2,\dots,1/2)\big) \] never vanishes on the boundary, so \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-6c-6f-62-61-6c-64} \deg(H,\Omega,0)=1. \end{equation} Near the origin, upper projection is inactive, and $H(x)=\min\{x,F(x)\}=\phi_M(x)+O(\|x\|^2)$. The $R_0$ property yields $\min_{\|u\|=1}\|\phi_M(u)\|>0$, so zero is isolated and has local index ${\operatorname{ind}}(H,0)=d(M)$. Additivity of degree and $d(M)\ne1$ force a nonzero root $x^*$ of (eq:box).

Suppose first that two or more coordinates of $x^*$ are positive. Every player then faces strictly positive opponent hazard $a_i(x^*)$. A player deviating from stationarity while opponents remain stationary can select any stopping time (including infinity); quitting at the first opportunity gives $Q_i$, continuing indefinitely gives $C_i$, and delaying $t-1$ rounds gives \[ \big[1-(1-a_i)^{t-1}\big]C_i+(1-a_i)^{t-1}Q_i. \] Thus no behavioral deviation exceeds $\max\{C_i,Q_i\}$. Conditions (eq:box) make the prescribed action optimal, so $x^*$ is an exact equilibrium for the terminal-payoff functional. Its payoff is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-74-61-74-69-6f-6e-61-72-79-56} V_i(x)=\frac{x_iQ_i(x)+(1-x_i)B_i(x)}{x_i+(1-x_i)a_i(x)}. \end{equation}

It remains possible that only one coordinate of $x^*$ is positive, say $x_i^*=p>0$. For $k\ne i$, (eq:box) yields $r_k(\{i\})\ge p\,r_k(\{i,k\})$, so every other player is best responding at $x^*$. The sole quitter's response to never quitting may involve $b_i$, and must not be ignored. Pick $j\ne i$ with $r_i(\{j\})<0$ and, for $\delta>0$, set \[ x_i^\delta=p,\quad x_j^\delta=\delta,\quad x_k^\delta=0\ (k\notin\{i,j\}). \] For $i$, $Q_i(x^\delta)=\delta r_i(\{i,j\})\to0$ and $C_i(x^\delta)=r_i(\{j\})<0$, while $V_i(x^\delta)\to0$. For each $k\ne i$ the opponent hazard remains at least $p$, so the prescribed terminal payoff and best-response payoff depend continuously on $\delta$ and their difference tends to zero. Therefore $x^\delta$ gives terminal regret $o(1)$ and $V(x^\delta)\to r(\{i\})$.

To convert these terminal equilibria into uniform equilibria, let $x$ have at least two positive quitting coordinates, let $a_*:=\min_i a_i(x)>0$, and take $L\ge\max\{|b_i|,|r_i(T)|\}$. Under any unilateral deviation, the probability of survival through $t$ is at most $(1-a_*)^t$. Hence \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-65-72-6d-69-6e-61-6c-4e} \sup_{\tau_i}\left|\gamma_i^N(\tau_i,x_{-i})-\Gamma_i^\infty(\tau_i,x_{-i})\right|\le\frac{2L}{Na_*}. \end{equation} The prescribed profile has the same bound. If its terminal regret is $\eta$, then \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-75-6e-69-66-6f-72-6d-62-6f-75-6e-64} \sup_{\tau_i}\left[\gamma_i^N(\tau_i,x_{-i})-\gamma_i^N(x)\right] \le\eta+\frac{4L}{Na_*}. \end{equation} For a root with at least two positive coordinates, use $x^*$ and $v=V(x^*)$; for a singleton root, use sufficiently small $\delta$ and $v=r(\{i\})$. Equations (eq:terminalN)--(eq:uniformbound) give both requirements of Definition Definition (Uniform equilibrium payoff) for all sufficiently large $N$, and absorption makes $b$ immaterial in the limit.$\square$ ∎

Remark: The theorem's strict negative-entry assumption is stronger than needed for the perturbation step: a nonpositive entry also suffices in many cases. No claim of novelty or priority is made for this index argument; it is recorded as a self-contained sufficient condition and warrants independent review before being cited as a published result.

Where the degree argument stops

Within the $R_0$ and negative-incoming class of Theorem Theorem (Degree-based sufficient condition), a counterexample must satisfy \[ \boxed{d(M)=1.} \] This condition is necessary only within that class, not for all quitting games. A concrete degree-one example is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-4d-6f-6e-65} M_1=\begin{pmatrix} 0&3&-1&-1\\3&0&-1&-1\\-1&-1&0&3\\-1&-1&3&0 \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation} It is $R_0$; at $q=(1,1,1,1)$ its exact LCP index sum is $1-4+4=1$. This is the singleton matrix associated with the Solan--Vieille four-player period-two example [sv2002example]. Its existence of a nonstationary equilibrium makes the central point: failure of stationary methods is not failure of uniform equilibrium. Degree one offers no nonzero-root conclusion by the present global-local index comparison.

A general-game verification theorem using continuation potentials

We next abandon quitting-game structure. A public history $h_t$ includes its stage and current state. For a prescribed profile $\sigma$, let $r_i(h,a_i,\sigma_{-i}(h))$ denote the expected current stage payoff if player $i$ uses pure action $a_i$ while opponents follow $\sigma$, and let $h^+$ be the random next public history. (Expectations below include transition and opponents' randomization.)
Theorem (Potential--correction verification): Suppose for each $i$ there are a bounded function $G_i$ and a function $B_i$ on finite public histories such that, for every history $h$ and unilateral pure action $a_i$, \begin{align} {\mathbb{E}}_{a_i,\sigma_{-i}(h)}G_i(h^+)&\le G_i(h),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-47-73-75-70-65-72}\\ r_i(h,a_i,\sigma_{-i}(h))+ {\mathbb{E}}_{a_i,\sigma_{-i}(h)}B_i(h^+)&\le G_i(h)+B_i(h)+\eta.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-42-75-70-70-65-72} \end{align} Under compliance, suppose also \begin{align} {\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma(h)}G_i(h^+)&=G_i(h),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-47-6d-61-72-74}\\ \left|r_i(h,\sigma(h))+{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma(h)}B_i(h^+)-G_i(h)-B_i(h)\right|&\le\eta.\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-42-6c-6f-77-65-72} \end{align} Assume, uniformly over histories $h$ at stage $N+1$ reachable from a given initial history $h_1$, \[ \kappa_i(N):=\frac{|B_i(h_1)|+\sup_{h:\,|h|=N+1}|B_i(h)|}{N}\longrightarrow0. \] Then, for every unilateral deviation $\tau_i$, \begin{align} \gamma_i^N(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})-\gamma_i^N(\sigma)&\le2\eta+2\kappa_i(N),\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-6f-74-2d-72-65-67-72-65-74}\\ \left|\gamma_i^N(\sigma)-G_i(h_1)\right|&\le\eta+\kappa_i(N).\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-6f-74-2d-70-61-79-6f-66-66} \end{align}
Proof: For all deviations, (eq:Gsuper) implies ${\mathbb{E}} G_i(h_t)\le G_i(h_1)$ by iteration. Telescoping (eq:Bupper) and using this bound gives \[ {\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}}\sum_{t=1}^N r_i(s_t,a_t) \le \sum_{t=1}^N{\mathbb{E}} G_i(h_t)+B_i(h_1)-{\mathbb{E}} B_i(h_{N+1})+N\eta \le NG_i(h_1)+N\eta+N\kappa_i(N). \] Under compliance, (eq:Gmart) keeps ${\mathbb{E}} G_i(h_t)=G_i(h_1)$, while summing (eq:Blower) gives (eq:pot-payoff). Subtracting the prescribed-payoff lower bound from the deviating-payoff upper bound proves (eq:pot-regret). ∎

This supplies a sufficient certificate for a uniform equilibrium when $\eta\downarrow0$, with initial targets $G_i(h_1)$ converging to one payoff vector for all states. The hard unsolved step is jointly constructing the same independent strategy profile and the supermartingale/sublinear-correction functions for every player. The existence of such objects is not a consequence of discounted Nash equilibrium alone.

An exact Big Match obstruction to discount scheduling

For each discount parameter $\lambda\in(0,1]$, consider the zero-sum Big Match with Player 1's payoff table \[ \begin{array}{c|cc} &L&R\\\hline T&1^*&0^*\\ B&0&1 \end{array} \] where $*$ denotes immediate absorption at the indicated payoff. Player 1 maximizes; Player 2 minimizes. The discounted stationary value is $1/2$, and the effective current-stage discounted game at that value is \[ \begin{pmatrix}1&0\$1-\lambda)/2&(1+\lambda)/2\end{pmatrix}. \] Its unique stationary equilibrium is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-69-73-63-6f-75-6e-74-2d-61-63-74-69-6f-6e-73} \Pr_\lambda(T)=p_\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}, \qquad\Pr_\lambda(L)=q_\lambda=\frac12. \end{equation} These probabilities are smooth rational functions of $\lambda$, and the value is constant. Nevertheless their predetermined scheduling fails.
Proposition (Failure of every predetermined discount schedule): For any deterministic sequence $\lambda_t\in(0,1]$, let the players use (eq:discount-actions) at stage $t$ conditional on survival. The resulting profile is not a uniform $\varepsilon$-equilibrium for any $\varepsilon<1/2$.
Proof: Under the prescribed strategy of Player 2, $L$ and $R$ are each played with probability $1/2$. Every stage, including the conditional absorbing payoff, has expected payoff $1/2$ for Player 1; hence \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-68-61-6c-66} \gamma_1^N(\sigma)=\frac12\qquad\text{for every }N. \end{equation} Let $\theta$ be Player 1's first stage choosing $T$, and set \[ w_t=p_t\prod_{k<t}(1-p_k),\qquad \beta_T={\mathbb{P}}(T<\theta<\infty)=\sum_{t>T}w_t\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}0. \] The distribution of $\theta$ is independent of Player 2's deviation, because Player 1's quit probabilities depend only on the calendar date. Player 2 chooses $R$ through stage $T$ and $L$ afterward. Absorption at or before $T$ produces payoff zero for Player 1; later absorption produces payoff one, while all nonabsorbing payoffs after $T$ are zero. The first $T$ stages can contribute at most $T/N$ to the $N$-stage average, so for $N>T$, \[ \gamma_1^N(\sigma_1,\tau_2^T)\le\frac{T}{N}+\beta_T. \] In the zero-sum game Player 2's gain is Player 1's loss, thus by (eq:half) \[ \gamma_2^N(\sigma_1,\tau_2^T)-\gamma_2^N(\sigma) \ge\frac12-\beta_T-\frac{T}{N}. \] For any $\varepsilon<1/2$, first choose $T$ so that $\beta_T<(1/2-\varepsilon)/2$, then choose an arbitrarily large $N$ so that $T/N<(1/2-\varepsilon)/2$. Such a profitable deviation violates the uniform-equilibrium inequality. ∎

In the language of Theorem Theorem (Potential--correction verification), Player 2's discounted continuation targets are $G_2(s)=-1/2$, $G_2(1^*)=-1$, and $G_2(0^*)=0$. If Player 2 chooses $R$ in the live state, then \[ {\mathbb{E}}[G_2(h^+)]-G_2(h)=\frac{p_t}{2}>0. \] Thus the crucial supermartingale condition (eq:Gsuper) is not inherited from the discounted Bellman inequalities. These positive drifts can accumulate. This example is not a counterexample to uniform-equilibrium existence: the Big Match has uniform value, but successful strategies depend on the history and cannot be obtained by a fixed, calendar-only schedule of the stationary discounted equilibria.

Why minmax acceptability alone cannot enforce incentives

Flesch and Solan [fleschsolan2023] establish several positive results for stochastic games with general bounded Borel payoff functionals, including approximately minmax-acceptable strategy profiles in every subgame and extensive-form correlated approximate equilibria. These are important, but the logical implication "an individually rational profile can always be enforced by threats" fails without more structure.

A two-player game chooses an absorbing payoff at the first stage: \[ \begin{array}{c|cc} & C&D\\\hline C&(3,3)^*&(0,5)^*\\ D&(5,0)^*&(1,1)^* \end{array}. \] Each player's minmax value is $1$. The profile $(C,C)$ gives both players $3$, strictly above that bound; every subsequently absorbing subgame trivially meets its own minmax benchmark. Nonetheless either player can change only their first action from $C$ to $D$ and raise their payoff to $5$. Because the game has already absorbed, no continuation punishment is available.

The game itself does have a uniform equilibrium, namely $(D,D)$. The point of the example is limited and precise: subgamewise acceptable payoffs do not automatically supply the off-path incentive inequalities required in Definition Definition (Uniform equilibrium payoff).

What remains open and actionable

The attempts above delimit three distinct unsolved constructions.
  1. Quitting games, index-one regime. The degree argument excludes $R_0$ matrices of degree other than one with negative incoming entries. It gives no conclusion for degree-one matrices such as (eq:Mone), matrices outside $R_0$, or cases lacking the negative-incoming property. A counterexample would require a complete simultaneous-quitting payoff table and a proof excluding all strategy profiles, including paths with discrete jumps and accumulation phenomena. Failure of stationarity or of SFAPs is insufficient.
  2. Small Bellman defects. Proposition Proposition (Weighted-defect verification) would yield uniform approximate equilibria from profiles whose opponent-survival-weighted defects tend to zero with controlled tails. No universal selection theorem producing such profiles has been established here.
  3. Arbitrary finite-state games. Theorem Theorem (Potential--correction verification) yields uniform equilibrium from common independent strategies and playerwise continuation-potential certificates, but neither vanishing-discount limits nor minmax acceptable profiles alone construct the required certificates. History-dependent deviation control remains the missing step.

Final conclusion. The unrestricted existence problem remains unsolved in this note. The genuine achievements are the two verification lemmas, an explicit degree-based sufficient class and its exact four-player certificate, and precise counterexamples to two natural methods of proof. None is a counterexample to existence of uniform equilibrium in finite stochastic games.

Reproducibility and limitations of the arithmetic audit

The matrix arithmetic in Sections sec:search and sec:index is rational. For the proposed matrix (eq:Rdag), the complete sixteen-support enumeration, all principal minors, the terminal equilibrium, the payoff table, and the uniform-regret constant were checked with exact SymPy arithmetic in the earlier research audit. The diagnostic code is not included with this manuscript; the key numerical certificates are fully displayed above. The analytic degree and strategy arguments are mathematical proofs, not formally machine-verified proofs. Before an external research claim is made, an independent mathematical audit is appropriate.
