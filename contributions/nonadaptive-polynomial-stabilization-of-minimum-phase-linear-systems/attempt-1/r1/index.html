Nonadaptive Polynomial Stabilization of Minimum-Phase Linear Systems

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

$I$ is the identity matrix. $W^{1,\infty}$ consists of essentially bounded functions with essentially bounded weak derivative. The funnel restricts the output norm by the prescribed time-dependent bound; $\xi$ is the feedback controller's internal state.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Consider the single-input, single-output system $\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+bu(t)$, $y(t)=cx(t)$, where $A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $b,c^\top,x^0\in\mathbb{R}^n$ are unknown. Assume that, for some known $r\in\mathbb{N}$, there is a $\rho\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$ such that $cA^ib=0$ for $i=1,\ldots,\rho-2$ and $cA^{\rho-1}b>0$, and that $$\det\begin{bmatrix}sI-A&b\\c&0\end{bmatrix}\ne0\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s\geq0).$$ For an arbitrary performance funnel $$\mathcal{F}_\varphi=\{(t,e)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}\times\mathbb{R}^m:\varphi(t)\lVert e\rVert<1\},$$ where $\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq0},\mathbb{R})$, $\varphi(0)=0$, $\varphi(s)>0$ for $s>0$, and $\liminf_{s\to\infty}\varphi(s)>0$, construct a feedback law $$u(t)=-\gamma_r(k(t),y(t),\xi(t)),\qquad k(t)=\left[1-(\varphi(t)\lVert y(t)\rVert)^2\right]^{-1},\qquad \dot\xi(t)=f(y(t),u(t),\xi(t)),$$ with polynomial $\gamma_r$ and $f$, such that $y$ remains in $\mathcal{F}_\varphi$. [S2]

A complete scalar barrier calculation and its extension gap

The source asks for every admissible unknown-order plant, with only a relative-degree upper bound known. We test the simplest permitted scalar subclass \[ \dot y=ay+\beta u,\qquad a\in\mathbb R,\quad\beta>0. \] This is a first-order minimum-phase plant. For example $b=\beta,c=1$ gives Rosenbrock determinant $-\beta\ne0$. Set $u=-ky$ with $k=(1-\varphi^2y^2)^{-1}$, so $\gamma_1(k,y)=ky$ is polynomial and no controller state is needed. Use the continuous Lipschitz representative of the prescribed $W^{1,\infty}$ function.
Proposition: For every scalar plant just specified, every finite initial value and every source-admissible $\varphi$, this feedback has a global solution satisfying $\varphi(t)|y(t)|<1$ for all $t\ge0$. The controller does not need to know $a$ or $\beta$.
Proof: The right-hand side is continuous in time and locally Lipschitz in $y$ on the open funnel, giving a unique local solution. Write $w=\varphi^2y^2$. While $w<1$, $k\ge1$ and the scalar equation implies $|y(t)|\le |y(0)|e^{a_+t}$, where $a_+=\max(a,0)$; this follows directly by integrating the coefficient $a-\beta k$, with the zero solution handled separately. If $L=\|\varphi'\|_\infty$, then $\varphi(t)\le Lt$ because $\varphi(0)=0$. Choose $\delta>0$ so small that $L^2\delta^2|y(0)|^2e^{2a_+\delta}<1/2$. Thus $w<1/2$ up to $\delta$ wherever the solution exists. On this interval $k\le2$ and $y$ is bounded, so local continuation extends the solution to $\delta$. Positivity, continuity and the positive limit inferior of $\varphi$ imply $m=\inf_{t\ge\delta}\varphi(t)>0$: use a compact interval up to a time after which the limit-inferior bound applies. Set $M=a_++L/m$. Almost everywhere for $t\ge\delta$ within the funnel, \[ \dot w=2\left(\frac{\varphi'}{\varphi}+a-\frac{\beta}{1-w}\right)w \le2\left(M-\frac{\beta}{1-w}\right)w. \] Choose $q\in(w(\delta),1)$ with $\beta/(1-q)>M+1$. Whenever $w\ge q$, its derivative is at most $-2w$. The positive part $(w-q)_+$ is absolutely continuous and has nonpositive derivative almost everywhere, starting at zero; hence it stays zero. Therefore $w\le q<1$ after $\delta$. In particular $k\le(1-q)^{-1}$ and $|y|\le\sqrt q/m$. The solution stays a fixed distance from the funnel singularity and remains bounded, so the local differential equation continues for all time. Every choice of constants in this proof is for analysis only; the feedback formula contains none of them. ∎
The attempted extension to relative degree two loses the scalar inward-pointing inequality. For $\dot y=v,\dot v=u$ at a time when $\varphi=1$ and $\varphi'=0$, the state $(y,v)=(1/2,100)$ has $\dot w=2yv=100$ independently of the instantaneous $u$. This does not prove that a dynamic controller cannot work; it identifies the missing control of unmeasured derivatives in this proof.
