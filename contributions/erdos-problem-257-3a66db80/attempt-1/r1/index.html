OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE REVIEW

Erdős #257; independent attempt, 2026-09-23. Is $S_A=\sum_{n\in A}(2^n-1)^{-1}$ irrational for every infinite $A\subset\mathbb N$? I read the complete 257.tex, whose periodic special case imports a Lambert-series theorem. Here is a separate elementary criterion covering irregular sparse sets, with an explicit obstruction for rational sums.

WORK: A NECESSARY GAP INEQUALITY

Enumerate $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$. If $S_A=p/q$ is rational, let $Q_j=\prod_{i\le j}(2^{a_i}-1)$. The positive remainder after $j$ terms is a nonzero rational with denominator dividing $qQ_j$, whence \[ S_A-\sum_{i\le j}\frac1{2^{a_i}-1}\ge\frac1{qQ_j} >\frac{2^{-\sum_{i\le j}a_i}}q. \] On the other hand, using $1/(2^n-1)\le2^{1-n}$ and distinctness of the remaining exponents, \[ S_A-\sum_{i\le j}\frac1{2^{a_i}-1} \le\sum_{n\ge a_{j+1}}2^{1-n}=2^{2-a_{j+1}}. \] Consequently rationality forces, for every $j$, \[ a_{j+1}-\sum_{i\le j}a_i<2+\log_2q. \] In particular, an unbounded limsup of the expression on the left proves irrationality. For example $a_j=3^j$ satisfies the criterion, giving an aperiodic family handled independently of the periodic theorem. More generally $a_{j+1}\ge(2+\delta)a_j$ eventually suffices: the earlier terms have sum at most $(1+1/(1+\delta))a_j+O(1)$, leaving a difference tending to infinity.

ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

The denominator used need not be reduced; this makes the rational lower bound weaker but valid. Infinite positivity ensures the remainder is nonzero. The geometric tail bound uses the fact that $A$ is a set, so exponents do not repeat. For dense or moderately sparse sets the gap expression can be negative, and the criterion says nothing. This is a standard denominator-tail mechanism, not a claim of new literature progress. It does not decide arbitrary aperiodic $A$.
