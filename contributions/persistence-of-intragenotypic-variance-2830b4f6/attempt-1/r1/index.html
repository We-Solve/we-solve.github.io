Persistence of intragenotypic variance

Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.

Target and scope

Catalogue definitions and assumptions.
Within a verified isogenic lineage, individuals occupy phenotypic states $1,\ldots,k$ in nominally common conditions. A heritable regulatory parameter $\alpha$ sets switching matrix $Q_\alpha$ and state-specific growth multipliers $w_j(e)$. Environment $e_t$ follows a known stationary ergodic process; microenvironment variation and measurement error are explicitly separated from switching noise. Phenotype fractions evolve by nonnegative matrix $A_\alpha(e_t)$ combining growth and switching. Define long-run lineage growth $\Lambda(\alpha)=\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\log\|A_\alpha(e_T)\cdots A_\alpha(e_1)\|$ under assumptions guaranteeing the limit. Genotypic identity is verified at the relevant resolution, and parent-of-origin or stable epigenetic inheritance is separately recorded. Measuring several descendants from each founder helps distinguish within-lineage switching from a mixture of persistent, unmeasured heritable states.
Original target.
Predict when an invasion-resistant regulatory rule maintains nonzero within-genotype variation: \[ \Lambda(\alpha_*)\ge\Lambda(\alpha)\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathcal A, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid g,e_t)]\ge v_0>0, \] where $Y$ is a declared quantitative phenotype and $\mathcal A$ is a molecularly feasible compact rule class. Test the predicted variance and growth advantage in independent environmental sequences.
Catalogue alternative formulations.
(i) In constant environments estimate residual nonadaptive noise and its fidelity cost; persistent variance need not be beneficial. (ii) In unpredictable switching environments compare stochastic diversification with cue-dependent plasticity, matching sensing and switching costs. Long-run growth optimization is distinct from maximizing mean one-generation output.

Attempted answer

Proposition: For richness birth rate $\lambda S+\nu$ and death rate $\mu S$, mean and variance satisfy $m\prime=(\lambda-\mu)m+\nu$ and $V\prime=2(\lambda-\mu)V+(\lambda+\mu)m+\nu$. Mean history alone cannot separate speciation and extinction rates when their difference is fixed.
Proof: Apply the jump generator first to $S$ and then to $S^2$, using increments $\pm1$. Subtract the derivative of $m^2$ to obtain the variance equation. The mean equation contains only the difference of the two per-species rates. ∎

What remains unresolved

Full trees and fossil observations can carry information beyond the mean. Sampling, time-varying rates and habitat histories require a separate likelihood; no observed macrodiversity mechanism is identified.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
