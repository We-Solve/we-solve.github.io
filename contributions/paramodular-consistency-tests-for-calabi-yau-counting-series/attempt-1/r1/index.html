Definitions and notation.
A paramodular group is an arithmetic subgroup acting on degree-two Siegel upper half-space, associated with a polarization type or level. A Calabi--Yau counting series packages enumerative invariants into a generating function. A paramodular interpretation must identify the group, weight, variables and transformation law of that series.

A level-one Fourier consistency test.
Consider a degree-two Siegel variable $Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&z\\z&\sigma\end{pmatrix}$ with positive definite imaginary part, and a holomorphic Fourier series \[F(Z)=\sum_{n,r,m}a(n,r,m)e^{2\pi i(n\tau+rz+m\sigma)}. \] In the level-one symplectic group, the block matrix induced by $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ acts by $Z\mapsto AZA^T$, interchanging $\tau$ and $\sigma$. For an integer-weight form satisfying $F((AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1})=\det(CZ+D)^kF(Z)$, this element has factor $(-1)^k$. Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients therefore forces \[a(n,r,m)=(-1)^k a(m,r,n). \] For even weight this is symmetry in the two degree indices. A candidate counting series violating it cannot have the claimed full level-one transformation law with that weight and trivial character.
Why this is only a test.
Passing one generator relation is far from proving modularity. Moreover a general paramodular level has a different lattice and may have an involution only after adjoining extra symmetries or a character. The coefficient swap above cannot be imposed without checking the actual group. It is not a theorem that all Calabi--Yau counting series satisfy it.
