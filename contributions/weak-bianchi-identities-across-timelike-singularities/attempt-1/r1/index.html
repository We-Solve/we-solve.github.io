Weak Bianchi Identities Across Timelike Singularities

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

The Einstein tensor is $G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12R g_{\mu\nu}$, where $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $R$ are Ricci and scalar curvature. The derivative $\nabla$ and volume measure $dV_g$ are those of $g$. A weak identity means that the integral against every compactly supported smooth test vector field is well-defined and vanishes. ZAS denotes a zero-area singularity, with regularity and mass defined as in the report's Bray--Jauregui convention.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Does the weak contracted second Bianchi identity $\int_M(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12R g_{\mu\nu})\nabla^\mu\psi^\nu\,dV_g=0$ hold for every spacetime whose timelike singularity restricts on spacelike slices to a regular zero-area singularity with negative ZAS mass in the sense of Bray and Jauregui, and for every compactly supported vector field $\psi$?
Scope and source qualification.
The cited work proves the weak twice-contracted second Bianchi identity for a restricted static spherically symmetric class. Its extension to all admissible timelike singularities is the remaining conjecture. Integrability and the treatment of the singular locus are part of the question; the classical pointwise identity alone does not justify this weak formula.

Short English statement

Decide whether the distributional conservation identity survives the stated class of timelike negative-mass zero-area singularities.

Excision identifies the missing boundary term.
Remove a radius-$\varepsilon$ tube around a timelike singular locus and call the regular remainder $M_\varepsilon$. Assume the smooth Einstein tensor is divergence free there and the test field $\psi$ has compact support. Integration by parts gives \[ \int_{M_\varepsilon}G_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\mu\psi^\nu\,dV_g =\int_{\partial M_\varepsilon}G_{\mu\nu}n^\mu\psi^\nu\,dS_g. \] Thus local integrability of the interior integrand and vanishing of the boundary flux are sufficient for the desired weak identity. For instance, a bound $\int_{\partial M_\varepsilon}|G(n,\cdot)|dS_g\to0$, in a fixed controlled frame over the compact test support, would suffice.
Why pointwise conservation does not settle it.
The elementary Euclidean vector field $F=x/|x|^3$ has divergence zero off the origin but flux $4\pi$ through every centered sphere. For a smooth test $\varphi$, its small-sphere flux tends to $4\pi\varphi(0)$, so distributionally $\operatorname{div}F=4\pi\delta_0$. A persistent singular flux can therefore survive excision despite the smooth identity everywhere else.

The reported static spherical result does not automatically furnish those estimates in the nonsymmetric class.
