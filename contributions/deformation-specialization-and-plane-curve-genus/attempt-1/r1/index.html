Problem/

The three-part scope

Question 17 asks about $\ell$-adic deformation, reducing Betti-number computation to positive characteristic, and polynomial-time genus computation in the degree. It specifies neither a complete input encoding nor the admissible singularities and reductions. We attempt the simplest degree-based genus shortcut, compute exactly why it fails for a singular curve, and examine the smoothness issue that a reduction argument would have to control. The three broad questions are not resolved by these calculations.
What degree alone actually determines for a plane equation.
Let $F\in k[X,Y,Z]$ be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$, and $R=k[X,Y,Z]/(F)$. For $t\ge d$, multiplication by $F$ is injective on the degree-$t-d$ piece, because the polynomial ring over a field is an integral domain. Its image is exactly the degree-$t$ part of the principal ideal. Counting monomials therefore gives \[ \dim_k R_t=\binom{t+2}{2}-\binom{t-d+2}{2} =dt+1-\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}2. \] With the convention that the arithmetic genus is one minus the constant coefficient of this linear Hilbert polynomial, it is $(d-1)(d-2)/2$. This proof computes the arithmetic invariant from the equation. It has not proved that the genus of the normalization of a singular curve equals that number.
An explicit failure of that substitution.
Over $\mathbb Q$ consider the projective cubic \[ C:\quad Y^2Z=X^2(X+Z). \] It is irreducible. In the affine chart $Z=1$, reducibility of $y^2-x^2(x+1)$ in $\mathbb Q[x,y]$ would, by division in $\mathbb Q(x)[y]$, force $x+1$ to be a square in $\mathbb Q(x)$. This is impossible: after writing a rational square in lowest terms, the exponent of the irreducible factor $x+1$ is even, whereas it is one for $x+1$. Any putative homogeneous factor disappearing on this affine chart would be a power of $Z$, which does not divide the equation. Thus the projective curve is irreducible as well.

Away from $x=0$, put $u=y/x$. The equation becomes $u^2=x+1$, so $x=u^2-1$ and $y=u(u^2-1)$. These rational substitutions are inverse and identify the function field of $C$ with $\mathbb Q(u)$. More explicitly the projective map \[ [U:V]\longmapsto[(U^2-V^2)V:U(U^2-V^2):V^3] \] has no simultaneous zero coordinates: at $V=0$ the middle coordinate is nonzero, and at $V\ne0$ the last is nonzero. Its generic inverse is $u=y/x$. Thus it is a birational parametrization by the projective line, and the nonsingular projective model of the function field is $\mathbb P^1$.

For clarity, the genus of this model is zero directly: a regular differential on the affine line has form $h(u)du$ with polynomial $h$, because a rational function with no finite pole has constant denominator after cancellation. At infinity set $v=1/u$. Then $u^jdu=-v^{-j-2}dv$, so every nonzero polynomial $h$ produces a pole there. There are no nonzero global regular differentials. The normalization genus, defined as their dimension, is therefore zero, whereas the preceding Hilbert computation gives arithmetic genus one. The two preimages $u=1,-1$ of the affine point $(0,0)$ also exhibit the two branches responsible for the failed shortcut. This is a counterexample to substituting the plane degree formula for normalization genus, not to polynomial-time genus computation.

A deformation check that cannot be omitted.
The family $F_t=y^2-x^2(x+1)-t$ has a singular affine fiber only when $2y=0$ and $x(3x+2)=0$. In characteristic zero these equations give $(x,y)=(0,0)$ or $(-2/3,0)$, and substitution gives $t=0$ or $t=-4/27$. The projective point at infinity is $[0:1:0]$; the partial derivative with respect to $Z$ of $Y^2Z-X^3-X^2Z-tZ^3$ equals one there, so it is never singular. Hence a chosen parameter can move between a singular fiber and a smooth one even in this elementary degree-three family. Likewise reduction of coefficients requires checking the resulting derivatives in the new characteristic; division by 2 or 3 in the preceding calculation cannot simply be copied into those characteristics. No claim about the actual Betti numbers of the singular cubic is inferred from its normalization genus: those are different invariants.

Audit and remaining questions

The primary Question 17 was inspected. The attempted fast algorithm "read the degree and output the plane genus formula" has a completely checked counterexample. A correct normalization-genus algorithm must account for singularities, and a deformation or reduction proof must state the hypotheses preserving the particular cohomological invariant. The degree parameter alone also leaves field representation and coefficient bit size unspecified; this observation is not a lower bound excluding algorithms in a stated arithmetic model. No general Betti-number comparison theorem, $\ell$-adic algorithm, normalization algorithm, or complexity bound is established here. All calculations are symbolic hand calculations, with no machine geometry computation.
