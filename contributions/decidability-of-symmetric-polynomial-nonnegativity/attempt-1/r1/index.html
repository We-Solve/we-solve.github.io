Research attempt: Uniform quadratic symmetric forms and finite-dimension blindness

For every $n\geq1$ put $p_1(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$ and $p_2(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2$. Consider the same rational coefficients $a,b$ in all dimensions: \[ F_n(x)=a p_2(x)+b p_1(x)^2. \] On all of $\mathbb R^n$, nonnegativity for every $n$ is equivalent to $a\geq0$ and $b\geq0$. Sufficiency is immediate from the squares. For necessity use $x=(1,-1)$ in dimension two to obtain $a\geq0$, and $x=(1,\ldots,1)$ in dimension $n$ to obtain $an+bn^2\geq0$. Dividing by $n^2$ and allowing arbitrary $n$ forces $b\geq0$.

For a specified cutoff $N\geq2$, nonnegativity in all dimensions $1\leq n\leq N$ is instead equivalent to $a\geq0$ and $a+Nb\geq0$. To see this, split $x$ into its component along the all-one vector and its orthogonal complement. The form has eigenvalues $a+nb$ on the first component and $a$ on the second. If $b<0$, the first value is minimized at $n=N$; if $b\geq0$, $a\geq0$ suffices. Necessity follows from the same test vectors. In particular \[ a=1,\qquad b=-\frac1{N+1/2} \] passes every test in dimensions at most $N$, but at $n=N+1$ the all-one vector has negative value. Therefore no fixed dimension cutoff independent of input coefficients can decide even this quadratic family by testing only those dimensions. This does not rule out a computable coefficient-dependent cutoff: the family itself is decided by the two sign tests already proved.

If the designated domain is instead the nonnegative orthant, the uniform criterion changes to $b\geq0$ and $a+b\geq0$. Necessity follows from all-one vectors of arbitrarily large dimension and from one nonzero coordinate. For sufficiency, if $a\geq0$ both terms are nonnegative; if $a<0$, the inequality $p_2\leq p_1^2$ for nonnegative coordinates gives $F_n\geq(a+b)p_1^2\geq0$. Thus the domain cannot be omitted.

One-sided search for a general negative witness.
For a rational polynomial expression in finitely many power sums interpreted in each dimension, enumerate dimensions and rational vectors in those dimensions, dovetailing the enumerations. Exact rational evaluation detects any negative value. If a negative real vector exists, continuity gives an open neighborhood of negative values containing a rational vector, so this search eventually detects it. This proves semidecidability of failure in this explicit encoding. It supplies no halting criterion for a uniformly nonnegative input; replacing that missing criterion by an unbounded run is not a decision algorithm.
