Research attempt: Residual certificates and a conditional truncation estimate

What a small residual does certify
Let $H$ be a finite Hermitian matrix, $v$ a unit vector, $E=v^*Hv$ and $r=(H-EI)v$. An orthonormal eigenbasis gives \[ \lVert r\rVert^2=\sum_j|c_j|^2(\lambda_j-E)^2. \] Since $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$, some eigenvalue lies within $\lVert r\rVert$ of $E$. This proves proximity to the spectrum, not specifically to its ground energy. The matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ with $v=(0,1)$ has residual zero and energy one although its ground energy is zero. A residual-only stopping rule therefore cannot identify the ground state without further spectral information.

Suppose in addition $\lambda_0$ is the lowest eigenvalue and every other eigenvalue is at least a certified $L>E$. The eigenvalues of $(H-\lambda_0I)(H-LI)$ are nonnegative, so \[ 0\leq v^*(H-\lambda_0I)(H-LI)v =\lVert r\rVert^2-(E-\lambda_0)(L-E). \] The Rayleigh quotient is a convex combination of eigenvalues and hence $\lambda_0\leq E$. Combining these facts yields the explicit enclosure \[ E-\frac{\lVert r\rVert^2}{L-E}\leq\lambda_0\leq E. \] The certified lower bound on the rest of the spectrum is an additional assumption, not a number inferred from the same trial vector.

A conditional localization-to-truncation guarantee
Index matrix entries by vertices of a graph with maximum degree $\Delta\geq3$, and suppose \[ |H_{ij}|\leq C e^{-\alpha\operatorname{dist}(i,j)},\qquad \alpha>\log(\Delta-1). \] Let $H_R$ retain entries at graph distance at most the nonnegative integer $R$ and set the others to zero symmetrically. There are at most $\Delta(\Delta-1)^{k-1}$ vertices at distance $k\geq1$ from a given vertex: enumerate paths without immediate backtracking, which include at least one path to every such vertex. With $\beta=\alpha-\log(\Delta-1)>0$, every absolute row sum of $H-H_R$ is at most \[ \delta_R=\frac{C\Delta}{\Delta-1}\, \frac{e^{-\beta(R+1)}}{1-e^{-\beta}}. \] For a Hermitian matrix with absolute row sums at most $\delta_R$, any eigenvector coordinate of maximal absolute value gives $|\lambda|\leq\delta_R$ from its eigenvalue equation. The operator norm is the largest absolute eigenvalue, so $\lVert H-H_R\rVert_2\leq\delta_R$. Taking minimum Rayleigh quotients in both directions proves \[ |\lambda_0(H)-\lambda_0(H_R)|\leq\delta_R. \] Each row of $H_R$ has at most $1+\Delta\sum_{k=1}^R(\Delta-1)^{k-1}$ entries. Thus a matrix-vector multiplication is linear in the matrix dimension for fixed $R,\Delta$, and the displayed error determines a sufficient $R$ for a prescribed tolerance. This counts a multiplication, not the number of eigensolver iterations or the cost of constructing the entries.
The unresolved complexity step
A many-electron discretization can itself have dimension exponential in the number of physical particles or orbitals. Linear cost in that dimension is therefore not automatically a fast physical-size algorithm. Nor do Coulomb interactions and a chosen many-body basis automatically give the assumed decay with an exponent exceeding the graph-growth rate. Finally a gap certificate needed for the ground-state residual enclosure may be costly. These are concrete missing hypotheses in the localization/residual route, rather than consequences of sparse arithmetic.

Verification and adversarial audit

The residual counterexample is an exact excited eigenstate. The ground-state enclosure checks the sign using $L>E$ and requires a bound on all other eigenvalues. The truncation proof counts graph shells and states the strict decay-versus-growth condition; at equality its geometric tail bound no longer converges. Symmetric truncation preserves Hermitian structure, which is used in the norm and Rayleigh arguments.
