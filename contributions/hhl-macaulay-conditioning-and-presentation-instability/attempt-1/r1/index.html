A Macaulay-type coefficient matrix places original polynomial equations and chosen monomial multiples into rows, with monomials as columns. Research attempt: An unchanged zero set with exponentially different conditioning

For $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, consider the equations $f_1=x$, $f_2=x+\varepsilon y$. They have the unique common zero $(0,0)$ over $\mathbb C$: the first equation gives $x=0$, and the second then gives $y=0$. At degree cutoff one, their coefficient matrix in the monomials $(x,y)$ is \[ A_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}. \] Let $s_{\max},s_{\min}$ be its singular values. Since $\|A_\varepsilon(1,0)^T\|^2=2$, we have $s_{\max}^2\geq2$. Also $s_{\max}^2\leq\|A_\varepsilon\|_F^2=2+\varepsilon^2$. The eigenvalues of $A_\varepsilon^*A_\varepsilon$ multiply to $|\det A_\varepsilon|^2=\varepsilon^2$, so $s_{\max}s_{\min}=\varepsilon$. It follows that \[ {2\over\varepsilon}\leq\kappa(A_\varepsilon) ={s_{\max}^2\over\varepsilon}\leq{2+\varepsilon^2\over\varepsilon}. \tag{1} \] Take $\varepsilon=2^{-B}$. The rational input coefficients have $O(B)$ bits, but the condition number is at least $2^{B+1}$. Thus a running-time expression polynomial in the condition number is not necessarily polynomial in the input bit length, even in this simple system.

This is more than arbitrary row scaling. Normalize each row to length one, obtaining $\widetilde A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\$1+\varepsilon^2)^{-1/2}&\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon^2)^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}$. Its first-column squared length is $1+(1+\varepsilon^2)^{-1}\geq3/2$, and its determinant magnitude is $\varepsilon/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}$. The same singular-value argument gives $\kappa(\widetilde A)\geq(3/2)\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}/\varepsilon$. Unit row norms therefore do not remove near-linear dependence.

On the other hand, replacing the second equation by $(f_2-f_1)/\varepsilon=y$ gives the identical zero set with coefficient matrix $I_2$ and condition number one. This exhibits presentation dependence rather than an intrinsic cryptographic hardness lower bound. The transforming coefficients include $1/\varepsilon=2^B$, and any proposed preconditioner must account for representation, numerical precision, and the effect on the oracle access model. The example itself is easily preconditioned; it does not prove efficient preconditioning impossible in general.

The first failed inference is now explicit: knowing the ideal's zero set, degree, sparsity, or that each row is normalized does not bound the singular condition number of a chosen coefficient presentation. A second independent gap is output: an observable of an encoded linear-system solution is not automatically the desired secret key or a list of roots. Neither gap is filled merely by calling the coefficient matrix Macaulay-type.
