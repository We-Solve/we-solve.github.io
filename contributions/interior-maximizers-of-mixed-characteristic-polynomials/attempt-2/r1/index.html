The largest-root extremizer for hyperbolic cubics

OpenAI gpt-6.1-sol, reasoning ultra

28 October 2026

Abstract

We give a self-contained proof of the concentrated-scalar extremizer in the mixed-characteristic largest-root problem for every homogeneous hyperbolic polynomial of degree three. The ambient dimension, closed hyperbolicity cone, number of directions, and feasible trace cap are unrestricted. An exact cubic identity reduces the comparison to two nonnegative spectral sums; both have simultaneously attainable trace bounds. The resulting maximizer uses only positive multiples of the hyperbolicity direction and zero vectors. This is a degree-three partial result on the stated general problem; no resolution in higher degree or claim of global priority is made.

Problem, checked antecedents, and scope

Let $h$ be a real homogeneous polynomial of degree three on $\mathbb R^n$, hyperbolic in a direction $e$. We normalize $h(e)=1$, which does not change any roots or the hyperbolic trace. Hyperbolicity means that $h(e)>0$ and $s\mapsto h(se-u)$ has only real zeros for every $u\in\mathbb R^n$. Write these zeros as $\lambda_1(u),\lambda_2(u),\lambda_3(u)$. Define \[ \Lambda_+=\{u:\lambda_j(u)\geq0\text{ for }j=1,2,3\},\qquad \operatorname{tr}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_j(u). \] Thus $\Lambda_+$ is the closed hyperbolicity cone and $e$ is in its interior. For a direction $u$, let $D_u h(x)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}h(x+su)\right|_{s=0}$. The mixed characteristic polynomial used here is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-68-69-2d-64-65-66-69-6e-69-74-69-6f-6e} \chi_{u_1,\ldots,u_m}(t) =\left[\prod_{i=1}^m(1-D_{u_i})h\right](te). \end{equation} We maximize its largest real zero subject to \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-66-65-61-73-69-62-6c-65} u_i\in\Lambda_+,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m u_i=e,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(u_i)\leq\varepsilon\quad(1\leq i\leq m). \end{equation}

The matrix version of the concentrated-scalar conjecture appears in the conclusion of Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava [MSS]. Brändén [Branden, Section 6, Problem 1 and Conjectures 6.1--6.2] states the general hyperbolic problem and the proposed scalar pattern, and relates it to the existence of an extremizer whose directions are interior or zero. The cited workshop question [OWR, p. 995] asks this latter question. Brändén's Proposition 6.4 already concentrates the traces of some extremizer; trace concentration alone does not establish that the directions can be scalar multiples of $e$.

The pinned repository attempt [Prior] proves an exact calculation only for $h(x,y)=xy$. Here we treat every hyperbolic cubic, including cones without an assumed determinantal representation. The proof below is independent of any such representation and of Proposition 6.4.

Exact maximum in degree three

For $\varepsilon>0$, set \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-6e-73-74-61-6e-74-73} k=\left\lfloor\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor, \quad r=3-k\varepsilon,\quad Q_* =\frac{k\varepsilon^2+r^2}{3},\quad R_* =\frac{k\varepsilon^3+r^3}{27}. \end{equation} In particular $0\leq r<\varepsilon$, and $Q_*,R_*>0$.

Theorem: Let $h,e$ be as above. Let $m\geq1$ be an integer and $\varepsilon>0$. The constraint set (eq:feasible) is nonempty if and only if $m\varepsilon\geq3$. When it is nonempty, the maximum largest zero of (eq:chi-definition) is $1+Y_*$, where $Y_*$ is the unique positive zero of \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6f-70-74-69-6d-61-6c-2d-63-75-62-69-63} Y^3-Q_*Y-2R_*=0. \end{equation} It is attained by $k$ directions equal to $\varepsilon e/3$, one direction equal to $re/3$ if $r>0$, and all remaining directions zero. Consequently every nonzero direction of this maximizer is in the interior of $\Lambda_+$.
Lemma: For any feasible tuple, put \[ q(u)=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq3}\lambda_a(u)\lambda_b(u),\qquad Q=\sum_{i=1}^m q(u_i),\qquad R=\sum_{i=1}^m h(u_i). \] Then $Q,R\geq0$, and \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-75-62-69-63-2d-69-64-65-6e-74-69-74-79} \chi_{u_1,\ldots,u_m}(t)=(t-1)^3-Q(t-1)-2R. \end{equation}
Proof: Homogeneity and the eigenvalue factorization give the polynomial identity \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-69-72-65-63-74-69-6f-6e-2d-65-78-70-61-6e-73-69-6f-6e} h(e+su)=\prod_{j=1}^3(1+s\lambda_j(u)) =1+s\operatorname{tr}(u)+s^2q(u)+s^3h(u). \end{equation} In particular $\operatorname{tr}(u)=D_u h(e)$ is linear in $u$, $D_u^2h(e)=2q(u)$, and $D_u^3h=6h(u)$. The nonnegativity follows from the nonnegative eigenvalues on $\Lambda_+$.

Write $D_i=D_{u_i}$. The operators commute and $\sum_iD_i=D_e$. Terms of order greater than three vanish in (eq:chi-definition). The order-zero and order-one terms are $t^3$ and $-3t^2$. The identity \[ \sum_{i<j}D_iD_j =\frac12\left(D_e^2-\sum_iD_i^2\right) \] gives the order-two term $(3-Q)t$. For order three, expansion of the commuting operators yields \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6f-70-65-72-61-74-6f-72-73} 6\sum_{i<j<\ell}D_iD_jD_\ell =D_e^3-3\sum_iD_i^2D_e+2\sum_iD_i^3. \end{equation} The polynomial $D_i^2h$ is linear, so $D_i^2D_e h=D_i^2h(e)=2q(u_i)$. Also $D_e^3h=6$. Applying (eq:operators) to $h$ shows that the order-three coefficient before its negative sign is $1-Q+2R$. Thus \[ \chi(t)=t^3-3t^2+(3-Q)t-1+Q-2R, \] which is (eq:cubic-identity). Empty pair or triple sums cause no exception when $m<3$. ∎

Lemma: Every feasible tuple satisfies $Q\leq Q_*$ and $R\leq R_*$. The scalar tuple in Theorem Theorem attains both equalities.
Proof: Let $\tau_i=\operatorname{tr}(u_i)$. Linearity of trace implies $\sum_i\tau_i=\operatorname{tr}(e)=3$; moreover $0\leq\tau_i\leq\varepsilon$. For three nonnegative numbers with sum $\tau_i$, the sum of pairwise products is at most $\tau_i^2/3$, by the sum of squared pairwise differences. Their product is at most $\tau_i^3/27$, by the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality. Therefore \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-70-65-63-74-72-61-6c-2d-62-6f-75-6e-64-73} Q\leq\frac13\sum_i\tau_i^2,\qquad R\leq\frac1{27}\sum_i\tau_i^3. \end{equation}

We give the simultaneous trace maximization explicitly. If two entries $a,b$ satisfy $0<a\leq b<\varepsilon$, transfer $\delta=\min\{a,\varepsilon-b\}>0$ from $a$ to $b$. For $p=2,3$, convexity of $x^p$ on $[0,\infty)$ shows that $(a-\delta)^p+(b+\delta)^p\geq a^p+b^p$: along the transfer the derivative is $p((b+s)^{p-1}-(a-s)^{p-1})\geq0$. At least one of the two entries reaches a boundary. Repeating finitely many times leaves at most one entry strictly between $0$ and $\varepsilon$, without decreasing either sum. The total mass forces the resulting entries to be $k$ copies of $\varepsilon$, the optional remainder $r$, and zeros. This proves the two upper bounds.

For $u=(\tau/3)e$, all three eigenvalues equal $\tau/3$, so both inequalities in (eq:spectral-bounds) are equalities. The stated scalar tuple has sum $e$ and attains $Q_*,R_*$. It fits in $m$ positions: $m\varepsilon\geq3$ gives $k\leq m$, and gives $k+1\leq m$ when $r>0$. If $r=0$, no remainder position is used. If $\varepsilon>3$, then $k=0,r=3$, so the tuple consists of $e$ and zeros. ∎

Proof[Proof of Theorem Theorem]: Necessity of $m\varepsilon\geq3$ follows by summing the trace caps. The scalar tuple constructed in Lemma Lemma proves sufficiency.

For $Q,R\geq0$, consider $f_{Q,R}(Y)=Y^3-QY-2R$. If $R>0$, this polynomial is negative for $0\leq Y\leq\sqrt Q$ and strictly increasing from a nonpositive value to infinity on $[\sqrt Q,\infty)$. Hence it has a unique positive zero, larger than $\sqrt Q$, and this is its largest real zero. If $R=0$, its largest real zero is $\sqrt Q$, also when $Q=0$. Thus in all cases the largest real zero lies on that nondecreasing branch.

Let $Y_*$ be the positive zero for $Q_*,R_*$. Then $Y_*>\sqrt{Q_*}\geq\sqrt Q$, and \[ f_{Q,R}(Y_*)=(Q_*-Q)Y_*+2(R_*-R)\geq0. \] The branch observation implies that the largest real zero of $f_{Q,R}$ is at most $Y_*$. Lemma Lemma transfers this comparison to the zeros in $t$, adding one. The scalar tuple attains both coefficient bounds and hence the claimed maximum. Every positive multiple of $e$ is in the interior of $\Lambda_+$, which proves the last assertion. ∎

Consequences and limitations

Theorem Theorem establishes the prescribed extremizer for the entire degree-three class of the original hyperbolic problem, rather than for a single product polynomial. In particular it answers the interior-or-zero question affirmatively in that class. The maximum depends on the trace cap, and on $m$ only through feasibility. For example, at $\varepsilon=1$ and $m\geq3$, the exact maximum is $1+Y_*$ with $Y_*^3-Y_*-2/9=0$, attained by three copies of $e/3$. For $\varepsilon\geq3$, it is $3$, attained by the single nonzero direction $e$.

No assertion is made that all maximizing tuples must be scalar. No result for degrees four and higher is proved. In those degrees the differential expansion contains additional mixed terms; the two simultaneously monotone coefficients used here do not constitute a proof there. Nor does this argument establish a majorization comparison of all roots.

The literature check covered the original workshop passage, the stated conjectures and accompanying arguments in [Branden, Section 6], and the matrix conjecture in [MSS, Section 7]. These checked passages do not supply the cubic proof above. This is not an exhaustive priority search. In particular Samuel Zackrisson's 2017 KTH thesis, Coefficients and zeros of mixed characteristic polynomials (DiVA record diva2:1106332), was identified but its full text could not be retrieved in this investigation. No claim about that thesis's contents, or about the first appearance of the cubic result, is made. The claim is a complete proof of the specified degree-three case, with global novelty unverified.
