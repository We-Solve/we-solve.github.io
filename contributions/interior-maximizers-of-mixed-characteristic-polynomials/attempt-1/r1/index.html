An exact product-cone calculation

We work in the restricted hyperbolic example $h(x,y)=xy$ with $e=(1,1)$ and closed cone $\mathbb R_+^2$. Here $\operatorname{tr}(a,b)=a+b$. Let $m\ge1$, $\varepsilon>0$ and $m\varepsilon\ge2$. Consider $u_i=(a_i,b_i)\ge0$ with $\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=1$ and $a_i+b_i\le\varepsilon$. These are the source's constraints for this particular degree-two polynomial.
Proposition: Put $q=\lfloor2/\varepsilon\rfloor$ and $r=2-q\varepsilon$. The largest possible largest root of the mixed characteristic polynomial is \[ 1+\frac12\sqrt{q\varepsilon^2+r^2}. \] It is achieved with $q$ trace masses equal to $\varepsilon$, one further mass $r$ if $r>0$, and all remaining masses zero, by taking $u_i=(s_i/2,s_i/2)$. In particular every nonzero $u_i$ in this maximizer lies in the interior of the cone.
Proof: Only derivatives of order at most two survive in $h$. Since $\sum_i(a_i+b_i)=2$ and \[ \sum_{i<j}(a_i b_j+a_j b_i) =(\sum_i a_i)(\sum_i b_i)-\sum_i a_ib_i=1-\sum_i a_ib_i, \] the polynomial $\prod_i(1-D_{u_i})h$ evaluated at $(t,t)$ equals \[ \chi(t)=t^2-2t+1-\sum_i a_ib_i. \] Its largest root is $1+\sqrt{\sum_i a_ib_i}$. Write $s_i=a_i+b_i$. Then $a_ib_i\le s_i^2/4$, with equality at $a_i=b_i=s_i/2$. This equality choice simultaneously satisfies both total-coordinate constraints whenever $\sum_i s_i=2$. Thus it remains to maximize $\sum_i s_i^2$ under $0\le s_i\le\varepsilon$ and $\sum_i s_i=2$. If two entries lie strictly between 0 and $\varepsilon$, transfer an amount $\delta>0$ from the smaller to the larger until one reaches a boundary. The sum of their squares increases by $2\delta(s_{\rm large}-s_{\rm small})+2\delta^2>0$. Repeating eliminates at least one interior entry at each step, so a maximizer has at most one such entry. The total mass then forces $q$ entries equal to $\varepsilon$ and the optional remainder $r$. Feasibility $m\varepsilon\ge2$ ensures that enough coordinates are available: $q\le m$, with $q+1\le m$ when $r>0$. This also covers $\varepsilon>2$, where $q=0,r=2$, and the case $r=0$. The stated value follows. Each positive $s_i$ gives two positive coordinates, proving the interior assertion. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

All three-vector feasible pairs of coordinate compositions with denominator eight were enumerated at $\varepsilon=3/4$. The maximum $\sum_i a_ib_i$ is $11/32$, attained at trace masses $3/4,3/4,1/2$, matching the formula. Boundary cases $m=1,\varepsilon=2$ and $m=2,\varepsilon=1$ were also checked. The workshop already formulates the proposed general mass pattern. This calculation verifies it only for $h=xy$; it supplies no exchange inequality for higher-degree or arbitrary hyperbolic polynomials. The exact place where the route ceases to generalize is the reduction of the largest root to the scalar quadratic objective $\sum_i a_ib_i$. No general conjecture is claimed solved.
