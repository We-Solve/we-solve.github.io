A family benchmark must specify its output task

The primary Macaulay2 wiki already asks for indexed examples, reproducible calculations, algorithm details and platform/runtime information. To test why a task label matters, take the family $R_n=K[x,y]/(x^n,y^n)$ for $n\ge1$ over a field. Compare two tasks: output an explicit monomial basis, or output its dimension as an integer.
Proposition: The quotient has basis $\{x^iy^j:0\le i,j<n\}$ and dimension $n^2$. Any procedure explicitly emitting all basis monomials needs at least $n^2$ output-item emissions. The dimension task does not have that output-size lower bound.
Proof: Every monomial with an exponent at least $n$ is in the ideal, so the displayed monomials span the quotient. A linear combination of them cannot lie in the ideal unless all coefficients vanish: every monomial in every multiple of $x^n$ or $y^n$ has an exponent at least $n$, so coefficient comparison on the displayed complementary monomials gives zero. They are therefore independent, and their number is $n\cdot n$. An explicit list must emit each of these $n^2$ distinct items, giving the emission bound. The dimension can instead be represented by the single integer $n^2$ computed by multiplication, whose binary length is $O(\log n)$; this statement does not claim constant bit-cost multiplication. ∎
Consequently a benchmark comparing one routine's explicit basis time against another routine's dimension time would compare different output contracts. If $n$ is binary-encoded, the explicit output can already be exponentially large in the input's bit length. This is an output-size obstruction, not evidence of an avoidable implementation defect. It also does not prove that the easy monomial family is representative of difficult research computations.
