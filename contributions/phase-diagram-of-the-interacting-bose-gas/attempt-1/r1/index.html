Phase Diagram of the Interacting Bose Gas

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In a grand-canonical Bose gas, chemical potential $\mu$ controls particle density at fixed temperature and interaction. Correlation clustering means that separated observables become independent; exponential and power-law clustering specify the distance dependence. Bose--Einstein condensation can be expressed by a macroscopic eigenvalue of the one-particle density matrix, with off-diagonal long-range order in a homogeneous setting. The proposed phases are distinguished by these large-distance properties in the thermodynamic limit.

Establish the conjectured phase diagram for the three-dimensional interacting Bose gas: below the critical chemical potential the correlations should cluster exponentially, at criticality by a power law, and above it the system should exhibit Bose–Einstein condensation with no clustering.

A Gaussian test of the proposed three regimes.
As an auxiliary static model in three dimensions, consider the covariance of $-\Delta+m^2$, where $m\geq0$. For $r=|x|>0$, a decaying radial Green function satisfies \[ -G_m''(r)-\frac2rG_m'(r)+m^2G_m(r)=0, \qquad G_m(r)=\frac{e^{-mr}}{4\pi r}. \] Indeed, putting $v=rG_m$ reduces the equation to $v''=m^2v$. Decay removes the growing solution when $m>0$, and the delta-source normalization follows from $\lim_{r\downarrow0}-4\pi r^2G_m'(r)=1$. For $m=0$, the solution decaying at infinity is $1/(4\pi r)$, with the same normalization. Thus the massive covariance has exponential decay with correlation length $m^{-1}$; removing the mass leaves a power law. This elementary calculation explains a possible mechanism for the first two regimes, without controlling an interacting measure.
Which correlation does not cluster?
Let $\eta$ be a centered complex Gaussian generalized field with covariance $G_m$, and formally write $\psi=z+\eta$ for a constant $z\in\mathbb C$. For distinct points, or after smearing, its two-point kernel is \[ \mathbb E\left[\overline{\psi(x)}\psi(y)\right] =|z|^2+G_m(x-y). \] The connected kernel, obtained by subtracting the product of means, is still $G_m$. A nonzero constant contribution therefore coexists with clustering of the connected kernel. One can also average the phase of $z$ uniformly: the mean then vanishes while the constant two-point contribution survives. This tests the definition's qualification concerning the condensate channel, rather than asserting failure of every connected correlation.
Failure of the Gaussian continuation.
In the classical quadratic approximation with kinetic operator $-\Delta$, chemical potential enters as $m^2=-\mu$. On a finite periodic box, its constant mode has quadratic coefficient $-\mu$. For $\mu>0$ the Gaussian integral diverges in that direction, and at $\mu=0$ the unconstrained finite-volume zero-mode integral also diverges. Consequently the calculation cannot simply be continued to produce the condensed phase. Adding a prescribed $z$ is not a derivation of a condensate from interactions. An interaction-dependent critical chemical potential, the thermodynamic construction, critical exponents and spontaneous condensation all remain unproved. In particular, the kernel above is a static Gaussian surrogate, not the full quantum Bose-gas correlation function.
