Heterotic Mirror Symmetry for Bundled Calabi–Yau Manifolds

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A heterotic $(0,2)$ compactification pairs a Calabi--Yau manifold $X$ with a holomorphic vector bundle $E$ carrying the left-moving gauge data, subject to the physical consistency conditions of the model. A $(0,2)$ mirror pair is expected to exchange the combined complex, Kähler, and bundle moduli and corresponding sheaf-cohomology data, and to reduce to ordinary mirror symmetry when $E\simeq TX$. A Fourier--Mukai transform is an integral functor between derived categories with kernel on the product of two varieties; an elliptic K3 surface is a K3 surface fibred by elliptic curves.
Source scope and qualifications.
The report asks for a general meaningful formulation as well as a procedure. It does not define a unique universal mirror functor or state that all bundles meet the string anomaly and stability conditions. The elliptic-K3 proposal is a particular included question.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Construct a general formulation and construction procedure for heterotic $(0,2)$ mirror pairs of Calabi–Yau manifolds with holomorphic vector bundles; in particular, determine whether such mirror symmetry can be realized functorially by Fourier–Mukai transforms in the elliptically fibered K3 setting.

A consistency test for split-bundle constructions.
Restrict to a complex surface $X$ with $c_1(TX)=0$ and a candidate rank-two bundle $E=L\oplus L^{-1}$. Put $\ell=c_1(L)$. Multiplication of total Chern classes gives \[ c(E)=(1+\ell)(1-\ell)=1-\ell^2,\quad c_1(E)=0,\quad c_2(E)=-\ell^2. \] In the no-extra-source anomaly model imposing $c_2(E)=c_2(TX)$, a necessary condition is therefore $-\ell^2=c_2(TX)$. It is an imposed physical consistency condition in this restricted model, not an assertion that every heterotic compactification has this exact equation.
Stability obstructs the easiest ansatz.
For a Kähler class $\omega$, define slope of a rank-$r$ bundle by $\mu_\omega(F)=r^{-1}\int_Xc_1(F)\wedge\omega$. The two line summands have opposite slopes $s$ and $-s$, whereas $\mu_\omega(E)=0$. Strict slope stability requires every proper subbundle to have slope below zero. If $s\ne0$, one summand violates this; if $s=0$, both give equality and strict stability still fails. Thus this elementary split ansatz never produces a strictly stable rank-two bundle, although at zero slope it may satisfy a weaker polystability requirement.
Why the attempted shortcut stops.
Chern-number matching alone cannot turn a candidate Fourier--Mukai construction into a physical mirror: even this simple family separates topological consistency from stability. One must specify allowed stability, ensure the transformed object is an appropriate bundle or physical complex, and compare moduli and spectra. No functor implementing those requirements on elliptic K3 surfaces is constructed, and the general $(0,2)$ formulation, including reduction to the tangent-bundle case, remains beyond the calculation.
