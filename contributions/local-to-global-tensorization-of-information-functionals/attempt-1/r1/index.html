Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For finite-alphabet random variables, $H(Z)=-\sum_z p(z)\log p(z)$ is entropy, with $0\log0=0$. In the source family a channel $W$ sends $X$ to $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_k)$, and real weights $\alpha_S$ and an input penalty $\gamma$ specify \[F_{\gamma,W}(p_X)=\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\alpha_S H(Y_S)-\mathbb E[\gamma(X)].\] For two independent channels, the joint functional replaces $H(Y_S)$ by $H(Y_{1,S},Y_{2,S})$ and subtracts $\mathbb E[\gamma_1(X_1)+\gamma_2(X_2)]$, allowing correlated inputs. Local tensorization means products of local maximizers of the separate functionals are local maximizers of the joint functional, for every pair of penalties. Global tensorization means a product input attains the joint maximum for every pair of penalties.

Literature and context check

The report, pp. 689--690, defines local and global tensorization for entropy-weight functionals with arbitrary input penalties. The following proof covers nonnegative entropy weights, a standard sufficient family. It then checks exactly why the same argument does not cover signed weights or settle the report's full local-to-global conjecture.

Research attempt: Nonnegative entropy weights tensorize and a sign-based extension fails

For a finite channel with outputs $Y_S$ and a real input penalty $\gamma$, write $F(p)=\sum_S\alpha_SH_p(Y_S)-E_p\gamma(X)$. Assume all $\alpha_S\ge0$. Entropy is concave in a probability vector: each summand $-t\log t$ has second derivative $-1/t<0$ for $t>0$ and extends continuously at zero, so its concavity extends to the closed simplex. Output probabilities depend linearly on input probabilities, and the penalty is affine. Hence $F$ is concave on the input simplex.

Every local maximizer of a concave function on a convex set is global. If $F(q)>F(p)$, concavity gives $F((1-t)p+tq)\ge(1-t)F(p)+tF(q)>F(p)$ for every $t>0$, contradicting local maximality as $t\downarrow0$. This includes boundary points relative to the simplex. Continuity and compactness guarantee existence of global maximizers for finite real penalties.

For two independent channels with arbitrarily correlated inputs, entropy subadditivity gives \[ F_{12}(p_{12})\le F_1(p_1)+F_2(p_2). \] To justify the inequality, $H(U)+H(V)-H(U,V)=D(p_{UV}\Vert p_Up_V)\ge0$. For probability vectors, nonnegativity follows from $-\log t\ge1-t$: summing $p\log(p/q)$ over positive $p$ gives at least $\sum_{p>0}(p-q)\ge0$, with infinite divergence if $q=0<p$. Multiply each subadditivity inequality by its nonnegative weight; penalties depend only on their own marginals and therefore split exactly. Product inputs make the two channels' output vectors independent and attain equality. Products of individual global maximizers thus globally maximize the joint functional for every pair of penalties. Since local maxima of the individuals are global, this family also has the local tensorization property.

The same subadditivity proof cannot just ignore negative weights. For the functional $-H(X)$ on a noiseless binary channel, fix both input marginals to be uniform. The product coupling has joint value $-2\log2$, while the equal-bit coupling has $-\log2$, which is larger. Thus replacing a correlated input by the product of its marginals can decrease a signed entropy objective. This is not a counterexample to the source conjecture: uniform inputs are not local maximizers of $-H$ on the full simplex with zero penalty, and the source quantifies over all penalties rather than imposing these fixed marginals. It only falsifies the specific nonnegative-weight proof extension.
