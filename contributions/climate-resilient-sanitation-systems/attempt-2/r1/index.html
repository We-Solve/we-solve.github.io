Climate-resilient sanitation systems: a tractable robust frontier, attainment, and the limits of extrapolating failure to unobserved extremes

Abstract

For the robust sanitation design problem, we prove the following. (i) With a relative-entropy ambiguity set around a reference climate law, the worst-case expected loss has the one-dimensional dual $\inf_{\theta>0}\{\theta r+\theta\log\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{L_k/\theta}\}$. The robust frontier is therefore computable from reference-law simulations. (ii) The minimum is attained over a compact design set when losses are lower semicontinuous in the design. (iii) A dominance criterion in terms of loss distributions under $P_0$ and the radius $r$. (iv) Failure probabilities are identified only on the observed hazard support. Beyond it, the worst case is total failure, unless an engineering monotonicity-and-curvature model is imposed, for which we give the implied bound. We also show that "repair costs plus health losses" avoids double counting if repair-induced health effects are counted once.

1. Robust frontier

Let $L_k=\sum_{t=1}^T\delta^t[H_t(k,\omega_t)+R_t(k,\omega_t)]$ be the discounted monetised loss of design $k$. Let $\mathcal P_r=\{P:\mathrm{KL}(P\|P_0)\le r\}$.
Proposition: If $\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{L_k/\theta}<\infty$ for some $\theta>0$, then $\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_r}\mathbb E_PL_k=\inf_{\theta>0}\{\theta r+\theta\log\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{L_k/\theta}\}$. The supremum is attained by the exponentially tilted law $dP^*/dP_0\propto e^{L_k/\theta^*}$.
Proof: By the Donsker--Varadhan variational formula, $\log\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{L/\theta}=\sup_P\{\mathbb E_PL/\theta-\mathrm{KL}(P\|P_0)\}$. Lagrangian duality for the convex constraint $\mathrm{KL}\le r$ gives the expression, and strong duality holds by Slater's condition ($P_0$ is strictly feasible). ∎
So the robust frontier $\min_k\{C_0(k)+\inf_\theta[\theta r+\theta\log\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{L_k/\theta}]\}$ requires only Monte Carlo draws from the reference climate model. The radius $r$ indexes the trust placed in the climate model, and can be calibrated, for example, to the KL distance between competing model runs.

2. Attainment and dominance

Proposition: If the design set $\mathcal K$ is compact, $k\mapsto C_0(k)$ is lower semicontinuous, and $k\mapsto L_k(\omega)$ is lower semicontinuous for each $\omega$ and dominated by an exponentially integrable envelope, then the robust objective is lower semicontinuous, and a minimiser exists.
Proof: A supremum of lower semicontinuous functions $k\mapsto\mathbb E_PL_k$ is lower semicontinuous. Each is lsc by Fatou's lemma with the envelope, and an lsc function attains its minimum on a compact set. ∎
Proposition (Dominance): If $L_k\le_{\rm st}L_{k'}$ under $P_0$ (first-order stochastic dominance in the loss) and $C_0(k)\le C_0(k')$, then $k$ weakly beats $k'$ at every radius $r$. If $L_k$ is only smaller in mean but has a heavier right tail, the ranking reverses for $r$ large enough. The switching radius is the first $r$ at which the two dual values cross.
Proof: First-order dominance implies $\mathbb Ee^{L_k/\theta}\le\mathbb Ee^{L_{k'}/\theta}$ for all $\theta>0$. As $r\to\infty$, the dual value behaves like the essential supremum of the loss, which favours the lighter tail. ∎
This formalises why resilience designs that cost more in normal years can be robustly optimal.

3. Identification of failure and its limits

Let $p_f(k,\omega)$ be observed through installed systems over the hazard range $[0,\bar\omega_k]$ that has actually occurred.
Proposition: Suppose $p_f(k,\cdot)$ is only known to be nondecreasing in hazard intensity. Then for $\omega>\bar\omega_k$ the identified set for $p_f(k,\omega)$ is $[p_f(k,\bar\omega_k),1]$, so the worst case in $\mathcal P_r$ puts total failure on unobserved extremes. If in addition $p_f(k,\cdot)$ is convex with slope at most $\ell_k$ (an engineering fragility-curve restriction), the upper bound becomes $\min\{1,p_f(k,\bar\omega_k)+\ell_k(\omega-\bar\omega_k)\}$.
Proof: Monotone extensions are unrestricted above the last observed value. The Lipschitz bound caps the growth. ∎
Hence comparing designs under climate change, where hazards exceed historical ranges, depends on engineering fragility models as much as on field data. Health and maintenance responses face the same issue for failure modes never observed.

4. Accounting

Health losses $H_t$ must be monetised once. If repair activity itself causes health harm, for example exposure during desludging, it belongs in $H_t$ and not again in $R_t$. Population exposure changes, such as migration away from flood zones, alter $H_t$ for a fixed design, so the baseline population must be declared [afl].

5. Remaining

Sequential decisions (adaptive operation and maintenance) turn the problem into a robust MDP. Rectangularity of the ambiguity set then becomes essential for dynamic consistency, and KL balls are not rectangular in general. Spatial correlation of failures across a network requires joint loss modelling. These are open.

Completion Estimate

42%

This is a post hoc, heuristic estimate for the full catalogue target. An entropy-ambiguity dual and compact-design existence argument characterize a conditional robust frontier and expose hazard-support extrapolation limits. General ambiguity classes, empirically validated fragility and health responses, and serial or spatial adaptive decisions remain unresolved, with tilted-law attainment needing extra conditions.
