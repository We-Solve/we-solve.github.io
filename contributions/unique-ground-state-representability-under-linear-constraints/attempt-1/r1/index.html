Unique Ground-State Representability under Linear Constraints

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

For observables $B_i$ and density matrix $\Gamma$, the expectation vector is $b_i=\operatorname{Tr}(B_i\Gamma)$. Constrained search fixes $b$ while minimizing energy. Multipliers $\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)$ enter a Hamiltonian of the form $H_0+\sum_i\beta_iB_i$, with the sign convention fixed by the source. The report initially assumes a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, self-adjoint operators $H_0,B_i$, commuting $B_i$, and linear independence of $\{I,B_1,\ldots,B_m\}$. It proves uniqueness of the multiplier vector for regular expectation values and treats critical or degenerate exceptions.

Question recovered from the primary report.

An infinite-dimensional loss-of-attainment test.
Let the Hilbert space be $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$, with basis $e_n$, bounded self-adjoint $H_0e_n=n^{-1}e_n$, and observable $Be_n=e_n$ for even $n$, $Be_n=0$ for odd $n$. The operators commute and $I,B$ are linearly independent, as required by the finite-dimensional source assumptions. Prescribe $\operatorname{Tr}(B\Gamma)=1/2$ for trace-one positive density operators.
The infimum is not a ground state.
The admissible states \[ \Gamma_k=\tfrac12|e_{2k}\rangle\langle e_{2k}|+ \tfrac12|e_{2k+1}\rangle\langle e_{2k+1}| \] have energy $\tfrac12((2k)^{-1}+(2k+1)^{-1})\to0$. Every admissible density operator has strictly positive energy: its diagonal probabilities are nonnegative, sum to one, and each coefficient $1/n$ is positive. Hence the constrained infimum zero is not attained. For any real multiplier $\beta$, $H_0+\beta B$ has spectral infimum $\min(0,\beta)$, approached along one parity but never attained by an eigenvector. No multiplier repairs the missing ground state.
Implication and limit.
