Attempt and results

The broad source asks for one-way functions and multilinear maps. We test conjugation as an efficiently computable candidate, derive its exact inversion relation, and disprove a simple size-based hardness criterion. This does not supply a secure primitive or settle the multilinear-map agenda.
Fiber size and a direct inversion baseline.
For a finite group $G$ and fixed $x\in G$, let $F(g)=g^{-1}xg$ and $C=C_G(x)=\{c:cx=xc\}$. Equality $F(g)=F(h)$ holds exactly when $hg^{-1}\in C$, by multiplying the equality on the left by $h$ and on the right by $g^{-1}$. Thus the fiber of $F(g)$ is $Cg$, of size $|C|$. The image has $M=|G|/|C|$ elements, and uniform $g$ gives uniform image because every fiber has the same size. An inverter sampling independent uniform $h$ and checking $F(h)=y$ succeeds per trial with probability $1/M$; its expected number of trials is $M$, by summing the geometric series. Therefore polynomial image size gives a polynomial expected-time baseline provided sampling, operations, and equality each have polynomial cost. For larger images the argument supplies no lower bound.
Both image and fiber can be huge while inversion is elementary.
Let $G=S_{2m}$ with permutations represented by their image arrays, and put $x=(1\ 2)(3\ 4)\cdots(2m-1\ 2m)$. Its conjugates are precisely the fixed-point-free involutions: conjugation relabels the $m$ unordered pairs, and any perfect pairing can be relabelled to this standard pairing. A commuting permutation must send each $x$-pair to an $x$-pair; it can permute the $m$ pairs and independently swap endpoints inside each pair. Conversely every such choice commutes with $x$. Hence $|C|=2^m m!$ and the orbit size is $(2m)!/(2^m m!)$. The latter also equals $(2m-1)(2m-3)\cdots1$, by choosing the partner of the smallest unpaired point recursively. For growing $m$, both sizes exceed any fixed polynomial in the array length $O(m\log m)$: for example, at least $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ factors in $m!$ and in that odd product are at least $m/2$, giving lower bound $(m/2)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$ up to the harmless factor $2^m$ for the centralizer.

Nevertheless, given a promised output $y$, scan its array to list its disjoint pairs $\{u_j,v_j\}$, $1\le j\le m$, taking the smaller element first and ordering pairs by that element. Define $g(u_j)=2j-1$ and $g(v_j)=2j$. Then for either element of the $j$th pair, $g^{-1}xg$ exchanges the two, so $g^{-1}xg=y$ on every point. This constructs a valid preimage with one scan and $O(m)$ array assignments; sorting is unnecessary if the scan visits points in increasing order and skips previously visited partners. Validation that a proposed $y$ is a fixed-point-free involution also takes a scan. Thus large image and large fibers are simultaneously compatible with easy inversion. Entropy counts cannot replace a hardness argument for the chosen representation and distribution.

The attempted implication and its missing content.
A conjugation-based one-wayness assertion must concern producing any member of $Cg$ on the sampled input distribution, not identifying the planted $g$. The preceding family invalidates the proposed inference from both cardinalities being large. A proof would instead need a reduction from a specified hard average-case transporter problem, with efficient sampling and encodings. Moreover a group action alone combines a group element with one point; it supplies no operation taking two hidden action points to their combined transporter. A multilinear-map construction would need additional explicitly specified structure and a correctness/security argument; none is inferred from the existence of the conjugation map.
