Definitions and conventions.
The coupling $\lambda$ is a formal expansion parameter and $a$ is the argument of the source's boundary-value solution $I_\lambda$. Derivatives in the displayed series are ordinary derivatives with respect to $a$, and $(-\log(1+a))^n$ means the $n$th power before differentiation. Holding to every order means equality of each coefficient in the formal power series in $\lambda$, after the second sum's extra factor $\lambda$ is taken into account; this does not by itself imply convergence for nonzero coupling. The primary definition uses the one-sided Hilbert transform $H_a[f]=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_0^{\Lambda^2}f(p)/(p-a)\,dp$ with a common cutoff, followed by its prescribed cutoff removal. Its angle functions satisfy $\lambda\pi\cot\tau_b(a)=1+a+b-\lambda\log a+I_\lambda(a)$ and $I_\lambda(a)=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\infty(e^{-H_p[\tau_a]}\sin\tau_a(p)-\lambda\pi/(1+p))\,dp$, in the source boundary-value convention for $a>0$.

Coefficient bookkeeping before the boundary equation.
Put $L=\log(1+a)$, $a>0$, and denote the proposed right-hand series by $J_\lambda(a)$. Direct differentiation gives \[ J_\lambda=-\lambda L+\lambda^2\left(\frac{L}{1+a}+\frac{L}{a}\right) +\lambda^3\left(\frac{L^2/2-L}{(1+a)^2}-\frac{L}{a(1+a)}+\frac{L^2}{2a^2}\right)+O(\lambda^4). \] For clarity the cubic first-sum term is $\frac16(-L^3)''=(L^2/2-L)/(1+a)^2$, while the second-sum term is $-\frac12(L^2/a)'$. Its extra coupling factor is essential; omitting it would already corrupt the linear coefficient.
Endpoint countercheck.

No interchange of the formal coupling expansion with cutoff removal or with $a\downarrow0$ is inferred from this check.
