Definitions and conventions.
The torus $\mathbb T_L^d$ has a period proportional to $L$ under the source convention, so its Fourier lattice becomes dense as $L\to\infty$. The Fourier variables $v_k^{\nu,L}$ are the slow-time modes of the damped, forced Schrödinger evolution. Their averaged actions $n_k^{\nu,L}$ are second moments. Weak convergence to a continuous spectrum is tested against functions of the wave vector after placing the discrete actions in the source's rescaled measure. The iterated limit sends $\nu$ to zero first at fixed $L$, then sends $L$ to infinity.

The linear damped-forced normalization test.
Suppress the nonlinear interaction and let each complex Fourier mode solve $dv_k=-\gamma_kv_kd\tau+b_kdW_k$, where $\gamma_k>0$ and complex Brownian motion is normalized by $\mathbb E|dW_k|^2=2d\tau$. With $n_k=\frac12\mathbb E|v_k|^2$, direct Itô differentiation gives \[ \dot n_k=-2\gamma_kn_k+|b_k|^2. \] Under the source scaling $b_k=L^{-d/2}b_k^0$, the rescaled action $\widetilde n_k=L^dn_k$ has equation $\dot{\widetilde n}_k=-2\gamma_k\widetilde n_k+|b_k^0|^2$. Its solution is explicit, and the equilibrium is $|b_k^0|^2/(2\gamma_k)$.
A genuine weak-limit subcase.
In a convention with wave lattice $L^{-1}\mathbb Z^d$, assume $\gamma(k)$ and $b^0(k)$ are continuous, $\gamma$ is uniformly positive, and initial rescaled actions converge uniformly on compact wave-vector sets. The displayed explicit solutions then converge on compact sets for fixed times. Riemann sums $L^{-d}\sum_k\widetilde n_k\varphi(k)$ converge to $\int n(k)\varphi(k)dk$ for every compactly supported continuous $\varphi$, by the usual partition-cell estimate using uniform continuity. Global mass convergence additionally requires tail control.
