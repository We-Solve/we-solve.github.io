FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Definitions.
  • For real $x\ge 0$, define \[ V'(x)=\#\{\varphi(m): m\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\le m\le x\}, \] (the number of distinct totient values attained by inputs $m\le x$), and \[ V(x)=\#\{\varphi(m): m\in\mathbb{N},\ \varphi(m)\le x\}. \]
  • Note that $V(x)$ is the same function as in Problem #416.

Questions.

  • $Q1$ Does $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{V(x)}{V'(x)}$ exist?
  • $Q2$ If it exists, is it $>1$? (Erdős suggested it might be $+\infty$.)

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

The problem statement records the trivial inequality $V'(x)\le V(x)$ and that Erdős suggested the ratio might tend to infinity. No further results are included in the extracted statement.

ATTACK PLAN

Proof-track ideas (show a limit exists).
  • Relate $V(x)$ to $V'(x)$ by bounding the smallest $m$ such that $\varphi(m)=n$ in terms of $n$; if most totients $n\le x$ have a representative $m\le x$, then $V(x)/V'(x)$ would stay near $1$.
  • Conversely, if many totients $n\le x$ require minimal preimage $m\gg x$, one might prove $V(x)/V'(x)$ grows.
Disproof-track ideas (show the limit does not exist or is infinite).
  • Try to exhibit families of totients $n$ whose minimal preimage grows much faster than $n$; if these occupy a non-negligible fraction of totients up to $x$, the ratio could diverge.
  • Search for oscillation: ranges of $x$ where many totients have small preimages (ratio near $1$) and ranges where few do (ratio large).

Lemma 417.1 (A universal lower bound for $\varphi(n)$)

Lemma. For every $n\in\mathbb{N}$, \[ \varphi(n)\ge \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}. \] Equivalently, $n\le 2\varphi(n)^2$.

Proof. Write the prime factorisation $n=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i}$ with distinct primes $p_i$ and integers $a_i\ge 1$. Then \[ \varphi(n)=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i-1}(p_i-1). \] Divide by $\sqrt{n}=\prod_i p_i^{a_i/2}$ to obtain \[ \frac{\varphi(n)}{\sqrt{n}} = \prod_{i=1}^r (p_i-1)\,p_i^{a_i/2-1}. \] We bound each factor.

Case 1: $p_i=2$. Then the factor equals $(2-1)2^{a_i/2-1}=2^{a_i/2-1}$. If $a_i=1$ this is $2^{-1/2}=1/\sqrt{2}$; if $a_i\ge 2$ this is $\ge 1$.

Case 2: $p_i\ge 3$. First note that $(p_i-1)^2=p_i^2-2p_i+1\ge p_i$ for $p_i\ge 3$ (since $p_i(p_i-3)+1\ge 1$). Hence $p_i-1\ge \sqrt{p_i}$. Therefore \[ (p_i-1)p_i^{a_i/2-1} \ge \sqrt{p_i}\,p_i^{a_i/2-1}=p_i^{(a_i-1)/2}\ge 1, \] because $a_i\ge 1$.

Multiplying all factors, every odd prime factor contributes at least $1$, and a factor $2^1$ contributes exactly $1/\sqrt{2}$ while any higher power of $2$ contributes at least $1$. Thus \[ \frac{\varphi(n)}{\sqrt{n}} \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \] which rearranges to $\varphi(n)\ge \sqrt{n/2}$. $\square$

Lemma 417.2 (Relating $V$ and $V'$)

Lemma. For every real $x\ge 1$, \[ V'(x)\le V(x)\le V'(2x^2). \]

Proof. First inequality. Every value counted by $V'(x)$ has the form $\varphi(m)$ with $1\le m\le x$. Since $\varphi(m)\le m$ for all $m$, such a value automatically satisfies $\varphi(m)\le x$, hence it is included among the values counted by $V(x)$. Therefore $V'(x)\le V(x)$.

Second inequality. Let $n$ be any value counted by $V(x)$. Then $n=\varphi(m)\le x$ for some $m\in\mathbb{N}$. By Lemma 417.1, \[ \sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}\le \varphi(m)=n\le x, \] so $m\le 2x^2$. Thus every totient value $\le x$ occurs as $\varphi(m)$ for some $m\le 2x^2$. Therefore the set of values counted by $V(x)$ is a subset of the set of values counted by $V'(2x^2)$, proving $V(x)\le V'(2x^2)$. $\square$

VERIFICATION

  • Lemma 417.1 attains equality at $n=2$ (both sides equal $1$), and holds for $n=1$ since $\varphi(1)=1>1/\sqrt{2}$.
  • In Lemma 417.2, the implication $m\le 2x^2$ from $\varphi(m)\le x$ is checked using Lemma 417.1; there is no circularity.
  • The computational check of $V(x)$ for small $x$ uses exactly the proved bound $m\le 2x^2$.

FINAL

UNRESOLVED
  1. (i) Strongest proved partial result here. A clean comparison \[ V'(x)\le V(x)\le V'(2x^2) \] for all $x\ge 1$ (Lemma 417.2), based on the universal lower bound $\varphi(n)\ge\sqrt{n/2}$ (Lemma 417.1), plus exact computed ratios $V(x)/V'(x)$ for $x\le 1000$.
  2. (ii) First gap (crisp). Determine whether the limit \[ \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{V'(x)} \] exists (finite or infinite). In particular, prove either (a) $\frac{V(x)}{V'(x)}$ is Cauchy as $x\to\infty$, or (b) there exist sequences $x_j,y_j\to\infty$ with $V(x_j)/V'(x_j)$ and $V(y_j)/V'(y_j)$ having different limit points, or diverging.
  3. (iii) Top 3 next moves.
    • Empirically compute $V(x)$ and $V'(x)$ for much larger $x$ (e.g. $x\le 10^6$) to detect whether $V(x)/V'(x)$ trends upward, stabilises, or oscillates.
    • Study the "minimal preimage" function $m_{\min}(n)=\min\{m: \varphi(m)=n\}$ on totients $n$ and quantify how often $m_{\min}(n)\le x$ for $n\le x$.
    • Attempt to strengthen Lemma 417.1 into a tighter deterministic bound on $m$ in terms of $\varphi(m)$ (even a bound of the form $m\le \varphi(m)\,g(\varphi(m))$ with explicit $g$) to improve $V(x)\le V'(\cdot)$ comparisons.
  4. (iv) What a minimal counterexample would likely look like. If the ratio does not converge (or diverges), a minimal explanation would be an infinite family of totients $n\le x$ whose smallest preimages typically lie far beyond $x$, creating many values counted by $V(x)$ but not by $V'(x)$. Such a family would likely be "highly composite" in structure (coming from $m$ with many small prime factors, making $\varphi(m)$ much smaller than $m$) and would need to occur with non-negligible frequency among totients.
