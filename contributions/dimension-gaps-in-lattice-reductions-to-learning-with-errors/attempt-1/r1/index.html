LWE dimension comparison: padding, projection, and the lost secret component

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A lattice generated by independent vectors $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ is $L=\{\sum_i z_i b_i:z_i\in\mathbb Z\}$. SVP seeks a shortest nonzero lattice vector; SIVP seeks $d$ independent short vectors, with approximation measured against the relevant successive minimum.

Learning with errors gives noisy linear equations $b_i=\langle a_i,s\rangle+e_i\pmod q$ for a hidden vector $s$. Ring-LWE uses the analogous equations in a polynomial quotient ring, under specified sample and error distributions.

Attempt 1: pad dimensions and randomize the secret

Let $q$ be prime, write $\mathbb F_q$ for the field of residues, and suppose samples of dimension $r$ have the form $(a,b)$ with uniform independent $a\in\mathbb F_q^r$ and $b=a^Tt+e$, for a fixed secret $t$ and independent errors of some fixed distribution modulo $q$. For $n\ge r$, append independent uniform $c\in\mathbb F_q^{n-r}$ and choose one shared uniform vector $w\in\mathbb F_q^n$. Output \[ A=(a,c),\qquad B=b+A^Tw. \] Then $A$ is uniform, and $B=A^T((t,0)+w)+e$. The new secret is uniform on $\mathbb F_q^n$, independently of all sample vectors, because translation by the fixed $(t,0)$ preserves uniformity. All samples use the same new secret and unchanged error distribution. A search oracle returning it lets us subtract the known $w$ and recover $t$. For a decision reduction, uniform original $b$ remains uniform after adding $A^Tw$, so uniform input also maps to uniform output. This proves the elementary direction $\mathrm{LWE}_r\le\mathrm{LWE}_n$ under the stated sample conventions. It does not turn hardness of a lower-dimensional worst-case lattice problem into hardness for a higher-dimensional worst-case lattice problem. Reversing that reduction direction would be an unsupported inference.

Attempt 2: project public vectors to fewer coordinates

Fix a full-column-rank matrix $T\in\mathbb F_q^{n\times r}$ with $r<n$, chosen independently of the secret. For original samples $b=a^Ts+e$, keep only $a'=T^Ta$ and $b$. If $s=Tt$, this is an exact $r$-dimensional LWE sample with secret $t$. But if $s\notin\operatorname{col}(T)$, the $r+1$ linear forms in $(T^Ta,a^Ts)$ are independent. The associated linear map has rank $r+1$: a dependence would express $s$ as a linear combination of columns of $T$, or contradict the rank of $T$. Gaussian elimination gives $n-r-1$ free coordinates in each fiber, so each output has exactly $q^{n-r-1}$ preimages. Consequently $a^Ts$ is uniform independently of $a'$. Adding the independent error $e$ leaves it uniform and independent. For each such fixed $s$, all projected samples are therefore independent uniform pairs, regardless of the original noise law.

For a uniform original secret, the event $s\in\operatorname{col}(T)$ has probability $q^{r-n}$. Conditional on it, the corresponding $t$ is uniform. For any number $M$ of samples, the entire projected distribution is thus \[ \mathcal P_M=q^{r-n}\mathcal L_{r,M} +(1-q^{r-n})\mathcal U_M, \] where $\mathcal L_{r,M}$ is the ordinary $r$-dimensional LWE distribution with a shared uniform secret and $\mathcal U_M$ is uniform. For every event $E$, $|\mathcal P_M(E)-\mathcal U_M(E)| =q^{r-n}|\mathcal L_{r,M}(E)-\mathcal U_M(E)|\le q^{r-n}$. Thus the total variation distance, defined as the supremum of this difference over events, is at most $q^{r-n}$. No distinguisher can recover more advantage from these projected samples, even with arbitrarily many of them. For $r$ around $\sqrt n$ this particular map discards almost all distinguishing information. The conclusion concerns a fixed secret-independent linear projection with the original right-hand side retained. It is not a lower bound for general classical reductions, adaptive transformations, or quantum algorithms.
