Multipoint Seshadri Constants and Nagata-Type Conjectures

OpenAI's claim

Manuscript 1 (family 039, 2026-10-05): In every dimension at least three, the ordinary multipoint Seshadri constant is claimed to reach $(L^n/r)^{1/n}$ at very general points for every sufficiently large $r$; Manuscript 2 (family 039, 2026-09-23): For any polarized smooth complex projective surface, the equal-weight multipoint Seshadri constant is claimed to reach $\sqrt{L^2/r}$ for all integers beyond a polarization-dependent threshold. Manuscript 3 (family 039, 2026-10-05): The same eventual volume-bound equality for ordinary multipoint Seshadri constants is claimed in positive characteristic at geometric generic tuples, in dimensions two and higher; Manuscript 4 (family 039, 2026-09-23): At $r\ge10$ very general complex plane points, every effective curve is claimed to have total assigned multiplicity below its degree times $\sqrt r$, including reducible curves and unequal assignments.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $X$ be smooth integral projective over $\mathbb C$, with $n=\dim X\ge3$, and $L$ ample. OpenAI claims an integer $r_0(X,L)$ such that for every $r\ge r_0$ and very general ordered distinct $p_1,\ldots,p_r$, \[ \varepsilon(X,L;\mathbf p)=\left(\frac{L^n}{r}\right)^{1/n}. \] The multipoint Seshadri constant is the supremum of $t\ge0$ for which $\pi^*L-t\sum_iE_i$ is nef on the blow-up at these points. For each $r$, the exceptional tuples lie in a union, indexed by a countable set, of proper algebraic closed subsets with nonempty complement. The threshold depends on $(X,L)$ and is not specified uniformly.
Manuscript 2.
For any smooth integral projective complex surface $S$ and ample line bundle $L$, OpenAI claims $r_0(S,L)$ such that every $r\ge r_0$ and very general tuple of distinct points give, on the blow-up $\pi$ with exceptional curves $E_i$, \[ \pi^*L-\sqrt{L^2/r}\sum_{i=1}^rE_i\text{ nef},\qquad \varepsilon(S,L;\mathbf p)=\sqrt{L^2/r}. \] The constant is the supremum of $t$ making $\pi^*L-t\sum E_i$ nef. Very general excludes countably many proper closed subsets of the distinct-point configuration space; the statement is for sufficiently many points, not every prescribed small $r$.
Manuscript 3.
Let $X/k$ be smooth integral projective over a field $k$ assumed algebraically closed, with characteristic $p>0$, with dimension $n\ge3$ and ample $L$. For $r$ ordered generic distinct points over the algebraic closure $K_r$ of the configuration-space function field, define \[ \varepsilon_r(X,L)=\inf_C\frac{L_{K_r}\cdot C}{\sum_{i=1}^r\operatorname{mult}_{p_i}C}, \] over integral curves meeting at least one point. OpenAI claims a threshold $r_0(X,L)$ with $\varepsilon_r(X,L)=(L^n/r)^{1/n}$ for every integer $r\ge r_0$. This is a geometric-generic-point statement, which avoids interpreting a countable exceptional-set complement over a countable field.
Manuscript 4.
For each $r\ge10$, OpenAI claims a nonempty very-general locus of ordered distinct points $(p_1,\ldots,p_r)$ in $\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}$ such that every nonzero effective plane curve $C$ of degree $d\ge1$ and integers $m_i\ge0$ with $\operatorname{mult}_{p_i}C\ge m_i$ satisfy \[ d\sqrt r>\sum_{i=1}^r m_i. \] Very general means excluding a union, indexed by a countable set, of proper algebraic closed subsets of the distinct-point configuration space. Multiplicities may be unequal, curves may be reducible, and the strict inequality starts at ten points.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 039, 2026-10-05): Graded exponent supports are reshaped into a volume-preserving simplex compatible with section multiplication. Full density forces every sufficiently deep interior lattice point to occur, allowing torus interpolation to separate the needed jets; very-general tuples inherit all the finite tests. Manuscript 2 (family 039, 2026-09-23): Finite jet tests are transferred to an algebraic torus through nodal-curve degeneration. A varying exponent region is tuned to a primitive lattice direction and a subgroup with exactly $r$ points, giving injectivity for all required orders rather than only a fixed multiplicity.

Manuscript 3 (family 039, 2026-10-05): Formal substitutions replace the analytic degeneration used in the complex companions. A nodal equation with two distinct branches even in characteristic two permits the graded-support reshaping and finite torus interpolation to proceed over the ground field. Manuscript 4 (family 039, 2026-09-23): Incidence loci and cyclic products reduce a hypothetical failure to equal multiplicities at arbitrary configurations. Degeneration toward a cubic produces finite jet constraints; colliding the remaining points and taking limits of elliptic theta bases turns the contradiction into a full-rank polynomial evaluation problem.
