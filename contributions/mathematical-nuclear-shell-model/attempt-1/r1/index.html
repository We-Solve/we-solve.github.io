Mathematical Nuclear Shell Model

Definitions and notation.
The primary source concerns the shell model of an atom with fermionic electrons, not a nuclear shell model. Electron states are antisymmetric in position and spin. The source's Coulomb Hamiltonian is $H(N,Z)=\sum_{i=1}^N(-\Delta_i-Z/|x_i|)+\sum_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|^{-1}$ on the fermionic space. Shell descriptions group effective one-electron orbitals into energy shells; the question asks how that approximation relates to exact quantum theory.

Atomic scope and a fixed-particle large-charge test.
The supplied repair identifies an atomic electron-shell programme despite the inherited nuclear title. In the stated kinetic normalization, dilate coordinates by $y_i=Zx_i$. The unitary dilation gives \[Z^{-2}H(N,Z)=H_0+Z^{-1}W,\quad H_0=\sum_{i=1}^N(-\Delta_{y_i}-|y_i|^{-1}),\quad W=\sum_{i<j}|y_i-y_j|^{-1}\ge0.\] Keep $N$ fixed. Let $E_0$ be the infimum of $H_0$ on the fermionic space. If $\Phi$ is a normalized fermionic ground state of $H_0$ with finite $C=\langle\Phi,W\Phi\rangle$, the variational principle yields \[E_0\le Z^{-2}\inf\sigma(H(N,Z))\le E_0+C/Z.\] The assumptions on the trial state are explicit: one can use a Slater determinant of occupied bound one-electron orbitals, provided their Coulomb integrals are finite. No claim about a particular shell occupancy is needed for this conditional inequality.
A quantitative shell-subspace consequence.
Suppose additionally that $H_0$ has a gap $\Delta>0$ above its ground eigenspace, and denote its projection by $P$. For a normalized ground state $\Psi_Z$ of the interacting scaled Hamiltonian, positivity of $W$ and the gap give \[\Delta\|(I-P)\Psi_Z\|^2\le\langle\Psi_Z,(H_0-E_0)\Psi_Z\rangle\le C/Z. \] This proves concentration in the unperturbed ground subspace under the explicitly stated spectral assumptions. Degeneracy inside that subspace is allowed; the estimate does not pick an individual determinant.
First obstruction.
For neutral atoms $N$ grows with $Z$, so $C$, shell gaps and degeneracies depend on $N$; the fixed-$N$ estimate supplies no uniform control. Within a degenerate shell, the interaction may select correlated states, which the projection bound cannot distinguish. A justified shell model for the exact atomic theory therefore needs uniform many-electron estimates and an effective dynamics or energy selection inside shell spaces. The elementary scaling test is not a nuclear model or a complete atomic-shell justification.
