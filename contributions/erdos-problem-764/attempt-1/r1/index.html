Erdős Problem #764: bounded error in an additive convolution

Statement and outcome

Can $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and $c>0$ satisfy \[ S(N):=\sum_{j\leq N}1_A^{*3}(j)=cN+O(1)? \] Here the convolution counts ordered representations, with repetitions allowed. The answer is no. We prove the bounded-error assertion itself; stronger quantitative error estimates require further work and are not claimed. Attempt status: solved.

Generating functions

Suppose instead that $S(N)=cN+E_N$, where $|E_N|\leq M$ for every $N\geq0$, enlarging $M$ to include any initial exceptions. Write \[ F(z)=\sum_{a\in A}z^a,\qquad E(z)=\sum_{N\geq0}E_Nz^N. \] For $|z|<1$, absolute convergence and the cumulative-sum identity give \[ F(z)^3=\frac{cz}{1-z}+(1-z)E(z). \tag{1} \] This is valid whether the convention for $\mathbb N$ includes zero. In particular, for real $s\uparrow1$, \[ F(s)^3=\frac{cs}{1-s}+O(1),\qquad (1-s)^{1/2}F(s)^{3/2}\longrightarrow\sqrt c. \tag{2} \]

Fix a positive integer $m$, and put $H_m(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1}$. All integrals below are with respect to normalized angular measure $d\theta/(2\pi)$, with $z=re^{i\theta}$ and $0<r<1$. Let \[ J=\int |F(z)H_m(z)|^2,\qquad W=\int|H_m(z)|^2=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}r^{2j}. \] The coefficient $b_\ell$ of $F H_m$ is a nonnegative integer. Thus $b_\ell^2\geq b_\ell$, and Parseval gives \[ J=\sum_\ell b_\ell^2r^{2\ell} \geq\sum_\ell b_\ell r^{2\ell}=W F(r^2). \tag{3} \]

Applying the power-mean inequality to the probability measure $|H_m|^2\,d\theta/(2\pi W)$, we obtain \[ I:=\int|F(z)|^3|H_m(z)|^2 \geq W\left(\frac JW\right)^{3/2} \geq W F(r^2)^{3/2}. \tag{4} \] This weighted step is necessary: $|F|^3$ is not the square modulus of the original generating function.

The error gains a square root in the averaging length

Since $|H_m(z)|\leq m$, the main term in $1$ contributes at most \[ cm^2\int\frac{d\theta/(2\pi)}{|1-re^{i\theta}|} =O_c\left(m^2\log\frac{2}{1-r}\right). \tag{5} \] For completeness, $|1-re^{i\theta}|^2=(1-r)^2+4r\sin^2(\theta/2)$; for $r\geq1/2$, integrating separately over $|\theta|\leq1-r$ and its complement proves $5$.

By Cauchy--Schwarz and Parseval, \[ \int |E(z)|\,|(1-z)H_m(z)^2| \leq\left(\frac{M^2}{1-r^2}\right)^{1/2} \left(\int|(1-z)H_m(z)^2|^2\right)^{1/2} \leq\frac{M\sqrt{2m}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}. \tag{6} \] Indeed, \[ (1-z)H_m(z)^2=(1-z^m)H_m(z) =\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}z^j-\sum_{j=m}^{2m-1}z^j, \] so its squared coefficient norm is $2m$. Combining $1$, the lower estimate, $5$ and $6$ yields \[ W F(r^2)^{3/2} \leq O_c\left(m^2\log\frac{2}{1-r}\right) +\frac{M\sqrt{2m}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}. \] Multiply by $\sqrt{1-r^2}$ and let $r\uparrow1$, keeping $m$ fixed. Equation $2$, $W\to m$, and the vanishing of the logarithmic term imply \[ m\sqrt c\leq M\sqrt{2m}. \] Choosing an integer $m>2M^2/c$ is a contradiction. Thus no bounded-error example exists.

Attribution and scope

R. C. Vaughan proved a substantially stronger result in On the addition of sequences of integers, J. Number Theory 4 $1972$, 1--16, https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(72)90008-X. The proof above reconstructs only the bounded-error obstruction, using the classical generating-function method and an explicit weighted power-mean step; Vaughan's stronger theorem is not an input. The same argument, with exponent $h/2$, proves the bounded-error impossibility for every integer $h\geq2$.
