Research attempt: Compatible linear simulator interfaces and error accumulation

An exact interface criterion with a proof.
Let one simulator evolve $x'(t)=Ax(t)$ in $\mathbb R^n$ and another evolve $y'(t)=By(t)$ in $\mathbb R^m$. Let $P:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ translate states, with $y(0)=Px(0)$. If \[ BP=PA, \] then $y(t)=Px(t)$ for every $t\geq0$ and initial state. Indeed induction gives $B^jP=PA^j$ for every nonnegative integer $j$. Substitute in the absolutely convergent matrix exponential series to obtain $e^{tB}P=Pe^{tA}$. Conversely, if that equality holds for all initial states on a time interval containing zero, differentiating at zero gives $BP=PA$. Thus the criterion is both necessary and sufficient for exact linear state translation, not just a schema for an untested adapter.
A proved defect bound for approximate compatibility.
Let $E=BP-PA$. The discrepancy $z(t)=y(t)-Px(t)$ satisfies $z'(t)=Bz(t)+Ex(t)$ and $z(0)=0$. Differentiating the variation-of-constants expression verifies \[ z(t)=\int_0^t e^{(t-s)B}E e^{sA}x(0)\,ds. \] If the symmetric parts of both $A$ and $B$ are negative semidefinite, then their semigroups have Euclidean norm at most one: along a homogeneous trajectory, $\frac{d}{dt}\|x\|^2=x^T(A+A^T)x\leq0$. Consequently \[ \|z(t)\|\leq t\|E\|_{\rm op}\|x(0)\|. \] Without such a stability assumption the integral can amplify the defect exponentially, so a small interface residual alone is not a time-uniform accuracy guarantee. For three systems with adapters $P_1,P_2$, their composite defect is exactly \[ A_3P_2P_1-P_2P_1A_1 =(A_3P_2-P_2A_2)P_1+P_2(A_2P_1-P_1A_1). \] Hence its norm is at most $\|E_2\|\|P_1\|+\|P_2\|\|E_1\|$. This formula states how independently checked interface errors combine; they do not simply add without adapter norms.
Preserving constants is different from preserving mass.
A spatial transfer matrix preserving constant fields satisfies $P\mathbf1=\mathbf1$. If old and new quadrature weights are $w$ and $v$, preserving the weighted integral for every state requires instead $v^TP=w^T$. These are different equations. For two old and two new cells with uniform weights $(1/2,1/2)$, take \[ P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \] It preserves constant fields. But the old state $(1,0)^T$ has integral $1/2$, while the transferred state $(1,1)^T$ has integral one. Thus even a positivity-preserving row-stochastic adapter can double mass. A workflow validating only file formats, dimensions or constant-field tests would miss this error; the mathematical conservation condition must be supplied by the particular simulation model.

For rational finite matrices, checking $BP-PA=0$, $P\mathbf1=\mathbf1$ and $v^TP=w^T$ is an exact finite computation on entries. These are concrete reviewable conditions for this model, not evidence that arbitrary nonlinear, stochastic or multiscale simulators admit such a map $P$.
