Macroscopic Limits and Phase Diagrams of Ferroelectric Models

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Polarization is the macroscopic dipole moment per unit volume. A large-body limit relates microscopic electron-density energies to a continuum polarization functional as system size grows. In a variational limit, a lower bound controls all convergent sequences, whereas an upper bound is produced by recovery configurations. A random-field Ising-like model has local polarization variables, random fields and pair interactions; long-range interactions remain significant over many lattice spacings.

A lower-bound test using fine mixtures.
To test a tempting local lower-bound strategy, use the auxiliary scalar potential $W(p)=(p^2-1)^2$ on the unit circle. This is not asserted to be the source's microscopic electron-density energy. Fix $\bar p\in(-1,1)$ and $\theta=(1+\bar p)/2$. Divide the circle into $N$ cells of length $\ell=1/N$; in each cell take $p_\ell=1$ for a fraction $\theta$ and $p_\ell=-1$ for the rest. Then $p_\ell$ converges weakly to $\bar p$ as $\ell\to0$: for any continuous test function, replace its value within each cell by its value at the cell's left endpoint, with an error bounded by its modulus of continuity at $\ell$. Yet \[ \int_0^1W(p_\ell)dx=0, \qquad W(\bar p)=(\bar p^2-1)^2>0. \] A claimed weak lower bound by the unrelaxed potential $\int W(\bar p)$ therefore fails even in this basic local model.
An explicitly scaled gradient term does not automatically repair it.
For $\eta=\ell^2$, smooth each jump across a layer of width $\delta=\ell^2$, keeping $|p_\ell|\leq1$ and $|p_\ell'|\leq C/\delta$. For fixed $\theta\in(0,1)$ the layers fit into the cells when $N$ is sufficiently large. There are $O(\ell^{-1})$ layers. The modified sequence has the same weak limit since its difference from the step function is supported on a set of length $O(\delta/\ell)$. Its regularized energy obeys \[ \int_0^1\left(W(p_\ell)+\eta^2|p_\ell'|^2\right)dx \leq\frac C\ell\left(\delta+\frac{\eta^2}{\delta}\right) =O(\ell)\longrightarrow0. \] Thus the relative scale of interfaces and microscopic oscillations changes what lower bound can be valid. At $\bar p=\pm1$, constant configurations instead have zero energy; the strict contradiction concerns interior average polarizations.
Connection and first missing step.
The example explains why constructing a macroscopically polarized trial state proves only an upper bound: other sequences can develop fine mixtures. It does not disprove the source's proposed limit. Its electrostatic terms, microscopic constraints and long-range interactions were omitted here and may assign a substantial cost to precisely these mixtures. A real lower-bound proof must control those terms on arbitrary bounded-energy sequences and determine whether the correct local contribution is relaxed. The second source target, the phase diagram of the specified long-range random-field model for lead titanate--lead zirconate solutions, also remains untouched: the auxiliary scalar construction gives no phase boundaries, disorder scaling or identification of that Hamiltonian.
