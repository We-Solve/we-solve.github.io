Research attempt: Exact scalar minimax estimation and one robust-regularization identity

An exact bounded-signal, bounded-noise minimax value.
Let $R,\delta\geq0$, observe $y=\theta+e$ with $|\theta|\leq R$, $|e|\leq\delta$, and measure absolute error. For any admissible observation, its feasible signal interval is \[ I_y=[-R,R]\cap[y-\delta,y+\delta]. \] The midpoint of $I_y$ minimizes its maximum distance to a point in $I_y$: if the endpoints are $l\leq u$, any estimate $a$ has maximum distance at least $(u-l)/2$ by $u-l\leq|u-a|+|a-l|$, and the midpoint attains it. The interval length is at most $2\min(R,\delta)$. At $y=0$ it is exactly that length. Therefore the global deterministic minimax absolute error is $\min(R,\delta)$, and the squared-error value is its square. Values of the estimator at infeasible observations do not affect this criterion.

For a scalar linear rule $\widehat\theta=ay$, its worst absolute error is \[ \sup_{|\theta|\leq R,|e|\leq\delta}|(a-1)\theta+ae| =|a-1|R+|a|\delta. \] The triangle inequality is attained by choosing the two signs to align. For $a\in[0,1]$ this expression is $R+a(\delta-R)$; outside that interval moving toward its nearest endpoint cannot increase it. Thus $a=1$ is optimal if $\delta<R$, and $a=0$ is optimal if $R<\delta$; all $a\in[0,1]$ are optimal when $R=\delta$. For $R,\delta>0$ unequal, ridge shrinkage $a=1/(1+\lambda)$ with finite $\lambda>0$ is strictly suboptimal in this worst-case model, although it may be optimal under a different probabilistic model. This falsifies a blanket identification of bounded-noise minimax estimation with arbitrary ridge tuning.

A positive equivalence with an explicitly matched uncertainty set.
Let $X\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$, $y\in\mathbb R^m$, $m\geq1$, and $\rho\geq0$. For every coefficient vector $\beta$, \[ \sup_{\|\Delta\|_F\leq\rho}\|y-(X+\Delta)\beta\|_2 =\|y-X\beta\|_2+\rho\|\beta\|_2. \] The upper bound follows from $\|\Delta\beta\|\leq\|\Delta\|_F\|\beta\|$. If $r=y-X\beta\ne0$ and $\beta\ne0$, equality is attained by $\Delta=-\rho(r/\|r\|)(\beta/\|\beta\|)^T$. If $r=0$ and $\beta\ne0$, use any unit output vector in place of $r/\|r\|$; if $\beta=0$, both sides equal $\|y\|$. Thus minimizing this robust residual is exactly minimizing a residual norm plus an $\ell_2$ penalty. Squaring the residual before the supremum gives the square of that sum, with cross term $2\rho\|y-X\beta\|\|\beta\|$, not generally the ridge objective $\|y-X\beta\|^2+\lambda\|\beta\|^2$ for a fixed universal $\lambda$.
No unrestricted risk ordering follows from either fact.
Take losses $(0,1)$ for one estimator and $(1,0)$ for another on two cases. With $P=(3/4,1/4)$ and $Q=(1/4,3/4)$ their risks are respectively $(1/4,3/4)$ and $(3/4,1/4)$, reversing the order. As a concrete squared-error realization, the target is zero, the measurement is $0$ or $1$, and the estimators are $f(y)=y$ and $g(y)=1-y$. This shares the elementary two-case obstruction with record 30005249; it is not claimed as a second independent result. Restricted positive shift theorems need assumptions tying together the estimator class and the pair $(P,Q)$.
