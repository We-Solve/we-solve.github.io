Selected Hom degrees, reusable corners, and a decomposition obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a graded module M, a homogeneous map of degree d sends $M_j$ into $M_{j+d}$. Its endomorphism module is $\operatorname{End}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}(M,M)$. If $M=N_1\oplus N_2$, its endomorphisms have diagonal blocks $\operatorname{End}(N_i)$ and off-diagonal Hom blocks; the question asks whether desired blocks or degree slices can be computed without rebuilding the full module.

Attempt and results

We attempt the source's bottleneck reduction using exact finite linear algebra, then determine what extra data is necessary to reuse endomorphisms after a split. These are standard mechanisms already foreshadowed in the supplied summary, proved here without accepting its claims as an external theorem.
A finite degree-component kernel.
Let $R$ be a nonnegatively graded $k$-algebra, $M,N$ graded modules, and \[ \bigoplus_{l=1}^b R f_l\xrightarrow{A} \bigoplus_{j=1}^a R e_j\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0 \] a homogeneous finite presentation, with $\deg e_j=u_j$, $\deg f_l=v_l$, and $A(f_l)=\sum_j a_{jl}e_j$. A degree-$d$ map $M\to N$ is equivalent to elements $n_j\in N_{u_j+d}$ satisfying $\sum_j a_{jl}n_j=0$ for every $l$. Indeed an assignment to a free basis extends uniquely to an $R$-linear map, and it factors through the displayed cokernel exactly when it kills the displayed relations. Consequently \[ \operatorname{Hom}_R^d(M,N)=\ker\left( \bigoplus_j N_{u_j+d}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_l N_{v_l+d}\right), \quad(n_j)_j\longmapsto(\sum_j a_{jl}n_j)_l. \] If the indicated components have computed finite bases and multiplication matrices, this is a finite matrix kernel over $k$; it need not construct the whole internal Hom module. If $a=0$, then $M=0$ and every Hom component is zero; treat this case first. For $a>0$, if $N_t=0$ for $t<L$, a nonzero map must have some nonzero $n_j$, and therefore $d\ge L-\max_j u_j$. Thus degrees at most $e$ reduce to the finite integer window $L-\max_j u_j\le d\le e$. Both local finite dimensionality and an effective component representation are necessary for this algorithmic conclusion. For example, assigning degree zero to the variable in $k[x]$ makes its degree-zero component infinite dimensional; a finite-dimensional matrix computation no longer follows.
Reusing a certified direct sum.
Suppose $M=N_1\oplus N_2$ is supplied with degree-zero maps $i_j:N_j\to M$, $p_j:M\to N_j$ satisfying $p_j i_l=\delta_{jl}$ and $i_1p_1+i_2p_2=1_M$. Let $E=\operatorname{End}_R(M)$ and $e_j=i_jp_j$. The map \[ \operatorname{Hom}_R^d(N_j,N_i)\longrightarrow e_i E_d e_j, \qquad h\longmapsto i_i h p_j \] is an isomorphism with inverse $a\mapsto p_i a i_j$. Their compositions are identities by the stated split relations. Moreover, any $a\in E_d$ equals $\sum_{i,j}e_i a e_j$, and applying $p_l$ on the left and $i_t$ on the right shows the sum is direct as a vector-space decomposition. For $i=j$ it respects composition, so it recovers both endomorphism corners as algebras. If a basis of $E_d$ is already stored, applying $a\mapsto e_i a e_j$ to that basis spans the desired corner; linear dependence removal yields a basis without recomputing Hom. The projector and actual composition operations are essential inputs.
Why an endomorphism vector-space basis does not itself split a module.
Take $R=k[x]$ and $M=R/(x^2)$. Every endomorphism is multiplication by a unique $a+bx$, since its value at $1$ determines it and every such value is annihilated by $x^2$. If this endomorphism is idempotent, then $a^2=a$ and $(2a-1)b=0$. In a field, $a=0$ or $1$, and $2a-1$ is a unit in either case, including characteristic two. Hence $b=0$ and the only idempotents are zero and one. A nontrivial module direct sum would give a nontrivial projection idempotent, so none exists, although the endomorphism vector space has dimension two. Therefore replacing the search for an algebra idempotent by a vector-space split is an invalid inference. This example is an ungraded obstruction to that shortcut, not a claim that every degree-zero endomorphism algebra behaves identically.
