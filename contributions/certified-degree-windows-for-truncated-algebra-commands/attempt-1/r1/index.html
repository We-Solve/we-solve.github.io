Certified degree windows for truncated algebra commands

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For an $R$-linear map $f:M\to N$, $\ker f=\{m\in M:f(m)=0\}$. The module $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ consists of $R$-linear maps. A graded calculation can organize generators and relations by degree. A degree limit requests bounded-degree work or output according to the semantics of the particular method; it need not constitute a complete calculation of the unbounded module.

Testing same-window truncation of a kernel computation

The historical source says several commands lack DegreeLimit options. Current official documentation lists DegreeLimit for Hom and for a kernel entry with its own stated semantics. Thus the old sentence is preserved as history, not asserted as a verified present-day absence. We test a mathematical truncation hazard independently of those implementations. Let $V_D$ be the polynomials of degree at most $D$ in $K[x]$ over a field, with $D\ge0$. Multiplication by $x$ maps $V_D$ to $V_{D+1}$. A naive same-window computation instead composes it with the projection $\pi_D:V_{D+1}\to V_D$ that discards the coefficient of $x^{D+1}$.
Proposition: Multiplication by $x$ on $V_D$ has zero kernel, but $\pi_D\circ(x\cdot)$ has kernel $Kx^D$. More generally, if a homogeneous linear map has degree $r\ge0$, retaining all output degrees from zero through $D+r$ suffices to preserve its restriction to inputs of degrees at most $D$ without truncation loss.
Proof: Write $f=\sum_{i=0}^D a_ix^i$. Then $xf=\sum_{i=0}^D a_ix^{i+1}$, zero exactly when every $a_i=0$, proving injectivity. Its projection is $\sum_{i=0}^{D-1}a_ix^{i+1}$, zero exactly when $a_0,\ldots,a_{D-1}$ vanish, while $a_D$ is arbitrary. This gives the one-dimensional kernel, including $D=0$, when the projected map is zero on constants. For the general claim, a homogeneous degree-$r$ map sends an input component of degree $d$ into output degree $d+r$. For $0\le d\le D$ these outputs are all at most $D+r$; retaining that full range keeps every component and hence the restricted linear map and its kernel unchanged. This assumes nonnegative input grading and no omitted lower degrees. ∎
For $D=2$, the correct matrix in monomial bases has three columns $x,x^2,x^3$ in a four-dimensional target and rank 3. Removing its last row produces rank 2 and falsely adds $x^2$ to the kernel. The failed inference is that truncating the target at the same displayed degree as the source necessarily preserves a bounded-degree kernel.
