Degrees of freedom are the independent coordinates needed to describe a model state. An asymptotic analysis studies a sequence of models indexed by a size parameter $n$ as $n\to\infty$. A useful large-system approximation must specify the system family, observable, limit and error measure; no one such family is selected by this record.

Research attempt

The source asks for asymptotics without a specified system or error metric. This attempt tests whether a large-number approximation valid at each fixed time can be used automatically on growing time horizons. A solvable interacting system shows exactly where that inference fails.

For $N\ge1$, $\beta\in\mathbb R$, let $x_i(0)=\xi_i$ and \[ \dot x_i(t)=-x_i(t)+\beta m_N(t),\qquad m_N(t)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^Nx_i(t). \] Assume the $\xi_i$ are independent, each equal to $1$ or $-1$ with probability $1/2$. The observable of interest is the empirical mean, with the deterministic approximation zero. These choices are explicit restrictions, not implicit assertions about the DARPA agenda.

Proposition: For every $t\ge0$, \[ m_N(t)=e^{(\beta-1)t}m_N(0),\qquad x_i(t)=e^{-t}(\xi_i-m_N(0))+e^{(\beta-1)t}m_N(0). \] If $\beta>1$ and $T_N\ge0$, then \[ \mathbb E\sup_{0\le t\le T_N}|m_N(t)|^2 =\frac{e^{2(\beta-1)T_N}}N. \] At $T_N=\log N/[2(\beta-1)]$, the probability of $|m_N(T_N)|\ge1/\sqrt2$ is at least $1/12$. Thus convergence at each fixed time does not extend to that logarithmic time scale.
Proof: Averaging the differential equations gives $\dot m_N=(\beta-1)m_N$. Subtracting this from the equation for $x_i$ gives $\frac d{dt}(x_i-m_N)=-(x_i-m_N)$, proving both formulas. Since independent symmetric signs have mean zero, variance one, and zero cross products, $\mathbb E m_N(0)^2=1/N$. For $\beta>1$ the absolute value of the explicit mean is nondecreasing in time; this proves the supremum identity.

At the stated $T_N$, write $Z=m_N(T_N)=N^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_i$. Independence and symmetry give $\mathbb EZ^2=1$. In the fourth-power expansion only four equal indices or two pairs have nonzero expectation, so \[ \mathbb EZ^4=\frac{N+6\binom N2}{N^2}=3-\frac2N\le3. \] Let $A=\{|Z|\ge1/\sqrt2\}$. On its complement $Z^2<1/2$, so $\mathbb E[Z^2\mathbf1_A]\ge1/2$. Cauchy--Schwarz yields \[ \frac12\le (\mathbb EZ^4)^{1/2}\mathbb P(A)^{1/2}, \] and hence $\mathbb P(A)\ge1/(4\mathbb EZ^4)\ge1/12$. No limit theorem is needed. ∎

What the model says about scale.
For $\beta>1$, mean-square uniform approximation on $[0,T_N]$ holds exactly when $e^{2(\beta-1)T_N}/N\to0$. In particular any $T_N=\gamma\log N$ with $\gamma<1/[2(\beta-1)]$ works, but the critical coefficient fails even in probability. For $\beta\le1$, the supremum over the entire infinite time interval occurs at zero and has second moment $1/N$. Thus a stability parameter and a time horizon can change the conclusion while the number of degrees of freedom and initial law remain the same. Large $N$ alone is not a uniform-accuracy certificate.
