Symmetry Identities Beyond Renormalization-Group Methods

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A Ward identity is a relation between correlation functions or functional-integral derivatives obtained from a symmetry of the model and its measure. Renormalization-group methods compare effective descriptions at successive scales and often rely on a controlled perturbative regime. The question asks whether the symmetry identities themselves can supply information outside the regimes accessible by those conventional estimates.

An exact finite-volume identity.
To test what symmetry alone can control outside a small-coupling expansion, take spins $\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}$ on any finite graph, with real couplings $J_{ij}$, inverse temperature $\beta\ge0$, uniform field $h$, and probability proportional to \[ \exp\left(\beta\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j+h\sum_i\sigma_i\right). \] Write $M=\sum_i\sigma_i$. The bijection $\sigma\mapsto-\sigma$ proves $Z(h)=Z(-h)$, so every odd derivative of $Z$ at zero vanishes. Differentiation of this finite sum gives the exact susceptibility identity \[ \frac{d}{dh}\mathbb E_h M=\mathbb E_h M^2-(\mathbb E_h M)^2\ge0. \] No restriction on $\beta J_{ij}$ was used; at $h=0$, odd observables have zero expectation.
Why this does not close the source programme.
These identities do not bound the susceptibility uniformly in volume. Consider two states only, the all-plus and all-minus configurations, with equal probabilities at zero field. The same spin-flip symmetry holds but $\operatorname{Var}(M)=N^2$ for $N$ sites. This two-state measure is a diagnostic symmetry-compatible measure, not the finite-coupling Gibbs measure above. It demonstrates that symmetry alone cannot improve the trivial variance bound $N^2$. A control argument must use additional quantitative interaction structure.
