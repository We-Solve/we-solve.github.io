A one-coupling J-Schur certificate for projected spectral enclosures

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
For compact $K$, its approximation number is $\alpha_N(K)=\inf_{\operatorname{rank}F<N}\|K-F\|$. Write $\operatorname{spr}(A)=\sup\{|z|:z\in\sigma(A)\}$. Resonances are poles of a suitably continued resolvent; an isoresonant family has the same resonance data. The source uses a J-self-adjoint structure and refers to J as a conjugation; its exact convention must be preserved in a chosen approximation scheme.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
The record bundles source items 17.1--19.1.
  1. Devise projected-space methods for rough rigorous and sharp useful enclosures of spectra of J-self-adjoint operators, incorporating the source’s J structure, and assess consequences for their evolution problems.
  2. For Schrödinger operators $-\Delta+V$ on $\mathbb R^d$ with bounded compactly supported potentials, find spectral or pseudospectral data that distinguish members of isoresonant complex-potential families in dimensions $d\ge2$, including nontrivial smooth complex potentials with no resonances. Ask the analogous question for isospectral families in settings such as the circle.
  3. For a Banach-space operator $A$ and compact perturbation $K$ with $\alpha_N(K)\to0$, estimate discrete eigenvalue counts of $A+K$ outside disks using resolvent growth near $\sigma(A)$. In particular classify operators whose resolvent has polynomial growth toward the spectrum; classify those admitting $M\ge1$ such that $\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|\le M/\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,\sigma(A))$ for every resolvent parameter; and classify those admitting $\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|\le M/(|\lambda|-\operatorname{spr}(A))$ whenever $|\lambda|>\operatorname{spr}(A)$.

A projected-space exclusion certificate.
Let a bounded operator on an orthogonal sum be $T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}$, with the first block finite dimensional. If $D-z$ is invertible, block elimination proves that $T-z$ is invertible exactly when $S(z)=A-z-B(D-z)^{-1}C$ is invertible. If $A-z$ is invertible and \[\|(A-z)^{-1}\|\,\|B\|\,\|(D-z)^{-1}\|\,\|C\|<1, \] then factoring $S=(A-z)[I-(A-z)^{-1}B(D-z)^{-1}C]$ and summing a Neumann series certifies exclusion of $z$ from the spectrum. A finite compression by itself omits the decisive complement resolvent.
A finite-dimensional classification test.
For a complex matrix, a bound $\|(T-z)^{-1}\|\le M/\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(T))$ for every resolvent parameter holds if and only if the matrix is diagonalizable. Sufficiency follows from $T=S\Lambda S^{-1}$, with $M=\|S\|\|S^{-1}\|$. For necessity, a Jordan block of size $r\ge2$ has resolvent term proportional to $(z-\lambda)^{-r}$; applying it to the last basis vector gives a norm bounded below by a positive constant times that power near $\lambda$, even after fixed invertible similarity. This contradicts a reciprocal-distance bound.
Gap and conventions.
