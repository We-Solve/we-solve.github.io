Paley RIP: an exact clique obstruction to beyond-coherence arguments

Definitions and mathematical statement

Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ be prime and let $Q\subset\mathbb F_p^*$ be the nonzero quadratic residues. Form a matrix $A_p$ with rows $\{0\}\cup Q$ and columns $\mathbb F_p\cup\{\infty\}$ by \[ (A_p)_{0j}=p^{-1/2},\quad (A_p)_{rj}=(2/p)^{1/2}e^{2\pi i rj/p}\quad(r\in Q,j\in\mathbb F_p), \qquad (A_p)_{\cdot,\infty}=e_0. \] The vector $e_0$ is one in row zero and zero elsewhere. This is the unit-column-norm Paley equiangular tight frame, of size $(p+1)/2$ by $p+1$. Distinct columns have inner-product magnitude $p^{-1/2}$.

For $s\ge1$, set \[ \delta_s(A_p)=\max_{|J|\le s} \|(A_p)_J^*(A_p)_J-I_{|J|}\|_{\mathrm{op}}, \] where $J$ is a column subset and the norm is the largest singular value. Does there exist a fixed $\delta<1$ and an integer function $s(p)$ with $s(p)/\sqrt p\to\infty$ such that $\delta_{s(p)}(A_p)\le\delta$ for all sufficiently large such primes?

Even an unbounded logarithmic improvement over $\sqrt p$ is sought; a fixed constant multiple of $\sqrt p$ does not suffice. Equivalently, all coefficient vectors on each permissible column set must have their squared norm preserved within factors $1\pm\delta$. The question is unconditional for this specific arithmetic frame, not a random choice of Fourier rows or a conclusion assuming an unproved distribution property of quadratic residues.

Short English statement

Does the Paley frame preserve sparse-vector norms for sparsity growing faster than the square root of its size?

Attempt and results

Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ be prime and keep the frame in the original definition. Write $\chi$ for the quadratic character with $\chi(0)=0$, and $e_p(t)=e^{2\pi it/p}$. We derive its Gram entries directly, avoiding a dependence on the sign convention for the quadratic Gauss sum.
Character-sum calculation.
Set $G=\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)e_p(t)$. Half the nonzero field elements are squares: the map $x\mapsto x^2$ has exactly two preimages for every nonzero square. Thus $\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)=0$. Since $\chi(-1)=1$, complex conjugation followed by $t\mapsto-t$ shows $G$ is real. Moreover, put $x=uy$ in the double sum to obtain \[ |G|^2=\sum_{u\ne0}\chi(u)\sum_{y\ne0}e_p((u-1)y) =(p-1)-\sum_{u\ne0,1}\chi(u)=p. \] Here the inner sum equals $p-1$ at $u=1$ and $-1$ otherwise, by the finite geometric series. Hence $G=\varepsilon\sqrt p$ for some $\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}$; its sign is unnecessary below. For $i\ne j$ in $\mathbb F_p$, the given row weights give \[ \langle a_i,a_j\rangle =\frac1p\left(1+2\sum_{r\in Q}e_p(r(j-i))\right) =\frac{\chi(j-i)G}{p}. \] Indeed $2\boldsymbol1_Q(r)=1+\chi(r)$ for $r\ne0$, the nontrivial additive-character sum over the whole field is zero, and scaling its argument multiplies the character sum by $\chi(j-i)$. The infinity column has inner product $1/\sqrt p$ with every finite column. All columns have norm one.
Exact error on a clique.
If $J\subset\mathbb F_p$ is a Paley clique of size $q\ge2$, every off-diagonal character in its Gram matrix is one. Thus its Gram error equals $\varepsilon(J_q-I_q)/\sqrt p$ and has norm $(q-1)/\sqrt p$. The same conclusion holds for an independent set, with the sign reversed. In particular, if $\delta_s(A_p)\le\delta<1$ and $s>1+\delta\sqrt p$, there cannot be a clique of size exceeding $1+\delta\sqrt p$: a larger clique has a subset of size $\lfloor1+\delta\sqrt p\rfloor+1\le s$, contradicting that bound. This necessary condition does not prove RIP, even if all cliques are small; arbitrary supports with mixed signs and arbitrary complex coefficients remain.
The attempted absolute-value argument.
For every support of size $t\le s$, the preceding Gram entries and $(\sum|x_i|)^2\le t\sum|x_i|^2$ imply $|\|Ax\|^2-\|x\|^2|\le(t-1)\|x\|^2/\sqrt p$. Consequently $\delta_s\le(s-1)/\sqrt p$ is a sound elementary bound. But as $s/\sqrt p\to\infty$ it cannot supply any fixed distortion below one. The clique computation shows why replacing every sign by a favorable cancellation assumption is unjustified: some supports have perfectly aligned signs. It does not show that sufficiently large such supports exist.
