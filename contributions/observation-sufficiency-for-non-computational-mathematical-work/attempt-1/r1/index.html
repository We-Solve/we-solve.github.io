Attempt: assessing a proof certificate rather than only a final answer

The phrase non-task-oriented is not defined by the source, and its reconstruction remains unverified. I tested a restricted representation for non-computational work: a finite directed graph of claims, with edges from cited premises to conclusions, checked against declared axioms and sound, decidably checkable inference rules. Sound means that true premises imply a true conclusion in every intended interpretation. Here a leaf means a node with no incoming premise edges.
Proposition: If the graph is acyclic, every leaf is a verified axiom, and every nonleaf is a valid inference from its incoming premises, then every claim in the graph is true in each interpretation satisfying the axioms. Verification takes $O(|V|+|E|)$ graph work plus the costs of checking axioms and individual inference steps. Dropping acyclicity invalidates this guarantee.
Proof: Topologically order the finite acyclic graph. Such an order exists: a finite acyclic directed graph has a vertex with no incoming edge, since repeatedly following predecessors otherwise repeats a vertex and forms a cycle; remove such a vertex and continue. Processing each vertex and edge once with incoming-edge counts computes an order in linear time. In that order, a leaf is true by the axiom check. Every later claim has already established true premises, so soundness of its verified rule makes it true. Induction proves the result for all vertices. Local checking costs are additional and are not assumed uniformly small.

For failure without acyclicity, use a single node labeled $0=1$ with an edge to itself and the identity rule "from $P$ infer $P$." That rule is sound and its local premise/conclusion match is valid, but the resulting circular object has no axiom foundation and its conclusion is false in ordinary arithmetic. Acyclicity rejects it. ∎
