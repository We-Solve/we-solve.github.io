Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Let $G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb C)$ be reductive and closed under conjugate transpose, with a compatible rational representation $\pi$ on a Hermitian space $V$ and $v\ne0$. Set $K=G\cap U(n)$ and $M=G\cap\{x=x^\dagger>0\}\cong K\backslash G$, with metric $\langle U,W\rangle_x=\operatorname{tr}(x^{-1}Ux^{-1}W)$. The Kempf-Ness objective is $f_v(x)=\log\langle\pi(x)v,v\rangle$. Instability means $0\in\overline{\pi(G)v}$. For a unit tangent $H$ at $I$, define $f_v^\infty(H)=\lim_{t\to\infty}(f_v(e^{tH})-f_v(I))/t$. Maximum destabilization minimizes this slope; the associated real one-parameter flow is $e^{tH}$, destabilizing when $\pi(e^{tH})v\to0$. Gradient flow uses $\dot x=-\nabla f_v(x)$ in this metric, with its direction limit taken at infinity.

Research attempt: An exact finite-weight torus algorithm

Use logarithmic coordinates $z\in\mathbb R^s$ for positive real points of a diagonal torus, with Euclidean metric in these coordinates. Suppose the representation is specified by rational weight vectors $w_1,\ldots,w_m\in\mathbb Q^s$ active on the nonzero coordinates of $v$, with $c_j=|v_j|^2>0$. Then \[ f(z)=\log\sum_{j=1}^m c_j e^{w_j\cdot z}. \] For a unit direction $h$, let $a=\max_j w_j\cdot h$. The sum at $th$ is at most $(\sum c_j)e^{ta}$ and at least $c_{j_*}e^{ta}$ for a maximizing index. Dividing logarithms by $t$ proves $f^\infty(h)=a$.

Let $C=\operatorname{conv}\{w_j\}$ and let $p\in C$ minimize $\|p\|_2$. Existence follows from compactness; uniqueness follows from strict convexity of squared norm. For any $w\in C$, differentiating squared norm along the segment $p+t(w-p)$ at $t=0$ yields \[ p\cdot(w-p)\geq0. \] If $p\ne0$, the direction $h_*=-p/\|p\|$ therefore satisfies $\max_j w_j\cdot h_*\leq-\|p\|$. Conversely every unit $h$ has $\max_j w_j\cdot h\geq p\cdot h\geq-\|p\|$. Hence the minimum slope is exactly $-\|p\|$, attained at $h_*$. All active representation coordinates decay exponentially along this direction. If $p=0$, every $z$ has some $w_j\cdot z\geq0$, so the norm of the transformed vector is at least $\min_j|v_j|>0$; there is no sequence of torus elements sending it to zero. Thus instability in this model is exactly $0\notin C$.

Finite exact computation.
Enumerate all affinely independent subsets of the active weights of size at most $s+1$. For each subset, compute the closest point $p_S$ to zero in its affine hull using rational linear algebra: with one vertex $u_0$ and independent difference columns $D$, solve \[ (D^{\mathsf T}D)t=-D^{\mathsf T}u_0,\qquad p_S=u_0+Dt. \] Keep it if its barycentric coordinates in the subset are nonnegative and every weight satisfies $w_j\cdot p_S\geq\|p_S\|^2$. These inequalities imply minimality over all of $C$, since for $x\in C$, \[ \|x\|^2=\|p_S\|^2+2p_S\cdot(x-p_S)+\|x-p_S\|^2\geq\|p_S\|^2. \] At least one enumerated subset succeeds. To prove this, express $p$ as a convex combination with minimal support. That support is affinely independent: an affine dependence permits moving its positive coefficients along a dependence until one becomes zero, preserving the point and contradicting minimality. It has size at most $s+1$. Projection inequalities for each support vertex are equalities, because their positive weighted sum is $p\cdot(p-p)=0$. Thus $p$ is orthogonal to every difference in its affine hull, exactly the computed closest point.

For rational inputs of total bit length $L$, each linear solve and inequality comparison has bit cost polynomial in $s,m,L$: denominators can be cleared, and determinant expansion bounds the bit lengths of all minors by a polynomial in these parameters. There are at most $\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}\binom mj$ subsets. This is an explicit finite bound, polynomial in $m,L$ for fixed $s$, but not a polynomial bound uniform in $s$. The rational vector $-p$ exactly specifies the optimal ray; the unit vector includes the algebraic normalization $\sqrt{p\cdot p}$, so an exact output need not have rational unit coordinates.
