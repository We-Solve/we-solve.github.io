Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Arithmetic-group cohomology is the cohomology of the specified arithmetic group or associated quotient. A Hecke operator is a correspondence-induced linear action on it; eigenvalues are its scalar actions on eigenvectors.

Research attempt: Cohomology action from supplied chain maps

Conditional exact algorithm.
Let a finite cochain complex over $\mathbb Q$ be supplied by rational matrices $d^j:C^j\to C^{j+1}$ with $d^{j+1}d^j=0$. Suppose rational matrices $T^j:C^j\to C^j$ are also supplied and satisfy \[ d^jT^j=T^{j+1}d^j. \] Find by elimination a basis matrix $B$ for $\operatorname{im}d^{j-1}$ and extend it inside $\ker d^j$ to a basis $[B\ H]$. Because $T^j$ preserves cycles and boundaries, there are unique matrices $U,M$ such that \[ T^jH=BU+HM. \] The induced operator on $H^j=\ker d^j/\operatorname{im}d^{j-1}$ is exactly $M$: each column equation gives the image of one chosen cohomology class. Gaussian elimination constructs all of these rational matrices in finitely many arithmetic operations; this is not a uniform bit-complexity bound for constructing the input complex. The characteristic polynomial $\det(zI-M)$ is an exact finite representation of the multiset of algebraic eigenvalues. Its roots, with multiplicity, specify those eigenvalues without pretending that arbitrary algebraic numbers have rational closed forms. The zero-dimensional cohomology case has the empty matrix and characteristic polynomial $1$.
A computed cancellation test.
Even when a complex can be compressed, deleting the cancelled coordinates of $T$ is wrong in general. Use a chain complex $C_1=\mathbb Q$, $C_0=\mathbb Q^2$, $d(a)=(a,a)$, and chain maps \[ T_1=(2),\qquad T_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}. \] Indeed $T_0d=dT_1$. The quotient $H_0=C_0/\operatorname{im}d$ is represented by $e_2$, with projection $p(x,y)=y-x$ and inclusion $i(z)=(0,z)$. Its induced action is \[ pT_0i(z)=z. \] Simply keeping the bottom-right entry of $T_0$ instead gives $2$, a wrong eigenvalue. The missing $-x$ in the projection records the boundary relation $e_1=-e_2$.
What cohomology alone fails to determine.
Consider the one-term complex $C^0=\mathbb Q$ with zero differential. For every rational $\lambda$, the map $T^0=\lambda I$ is a chain map and induces eigenvalue $\lambda$ on the same one-dimensional cohomology. Thus the vector space, or even this entire differential complex, contains no information selecting a particular correspondence operator. This example does not assert that arbitrary $\lambda$ occurs as an arithmetic Hecke operator; it pinpoints why a procedure receiving only cohomology data cannot recover the missing correspondence. Actual double-coset representatives, transfer maps, coefficient actions, and compatible chain-level reductions must be constructed and verified before the conditional algorithm applies.
