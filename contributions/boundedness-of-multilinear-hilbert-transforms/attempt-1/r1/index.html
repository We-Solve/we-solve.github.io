Boundedness of Multilinear Hilbert Transforms

OpenAI's claim

Dyadic triangular Hilbert form: The dyadic manuscript claims a uniform bound for the sum of the absolute local triangular contributions over any finite selection of scales; the three inputs are arbitrary real $L^3$ functions, and local coefficients may vary independently with modulus at most one.

A trilinear Hilbert-transform estimate: The manuscript claims an $L^1$ output bound for three $L^3(\mathbb R)$ inputs to the Hilbert transform with slopes $1,2,3$; this is a fixed-slope symmetric-exponent case.

Annular triangular variation: The paper announces an $r$-variation estimate for every $r>2$ from two complex $L^3$ inputs to $L^{3/2}$; output-dependent partitions over positive annular scales are allowed, and the result implies maximal and principal-value conclusions.

Maximal triangular Hilbert transform: The announced symmetric triangular Hilbert estimate controls the supremum over both finite hard cutoffs for arbitrary complex $L^3$ inputs; it also gives joint principal-value convergence almost everywhere and in $L^{3/2}$.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For $k\in\mathbb Z$, let $\mathcal D_k=\{[2^km,2^k(m+1)):m\ge0\}$, let $h_I$ equal $1$ on the left half of $I$ and $-1$ on its right half, and let $\mathcal A_k$ comprise triples whose interval indices have bitwise XOR zero. Define \[L_{\mathbf I}=2^{-k}\int_{I_0\times I_1\times I_2}F_0(x_0,x_1)F_1(x_1,x_2)F_2(x_2,x_0)\prod_{v=0}^2h_{I_v}(x_v)\,dx_0\,dx_1\,dx_2.\] For real bounded compactly supported measurable $F_v$ on $[0,\infty)^2$ and any finite $S\subset\mathbb Z$, \[\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathcal A_k}|L_{\mathbf I}|\le40\prod_{v=0}^2\|F_v\|_3.\] This is the dyadic XOR model, not the continuous triangular transform.
Mathematical claim 2.
For complex $F,G\in L^3(\mathbb R^2)$ set $B_{a,b}(F,G)(x,y)=\int_{a<|t|<b}F(x+t,y)G(x,y+t)\,dt/t$. For $r>2$ define \[V_r(F,G)=\sup_{J\ge1,\ 0<t_0<\cdots<t_J}\left(\sum_{j=1}^J|B_{t_{j-1},t_j}(F,G)|^r\right)^{1/r}.\] The supremum may be taken over rational endpoints and imposes no bound on $J$. The claim is $\|V_r(F,G)\|_{3/2}\le C_r\|F\|_3\|G\|_3$, with quantities defined off one common null set.
Mathematical claim 3.
For $F,G\in L^3(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$ let \[B_*(F,G)(x,y)=\sup_{0<a<b<\infty}\left|\int_{a<|t|<b}F(x+t,y)G(x,y+t)\,\frac{dt}{t}\right|.\] There is an absolute finite $C$ with $\|B_*(F,G)\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)}\le C\|F\|_3\|G\|_3$. Both finite hard-truncation endpoints vary in the pointwise supremum; this is the balanced exponent pair $(3,3)$.
Mathematical claim 4.
For Schwartz $f_1,f_2,f_3:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ define \[T(f_1,f_2,f_3)(x)=\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}f_1(x-t)f_2(x-2t)f_3(x-3t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.\] The claim is $\|T(f_1,f_2,f_3)\|_1\le C\prod_{j=1}^3\|f_j\|_3$. It yields a unique bounded trilinear extension $(L^3)^3\to L^1$, for these three distinct shifts and balanced exponents.

Outline of the argument

Dyadic triangular Hilbert form: Fine dyadic step functions reduce the inputs to matrices between atom spaces. At each of the three variables, the two incident matrices are joined into one row operator and assigned a positive trace energy. A local bound charges the form to the energy increment between a triple and its children; telescoping and endpoint norm bounds control all selected scales. A trilinear Hilbert-transform estimate: Local forms are compressed using projection and quadratic localization tools. A base form has few active scales on each path, and curvature localization splits it into side terms and a core. Square-energy bounds control the sides, counting bounds control the core, and the final parameter choices absorb the remaining errors.

Annular triangular variation: The proof first bounds the sum of absolute contributions from finitely many disjoint annuli, selected separately at each output location. Quantitative growth control for this counting quantity yields the variation inequality. Approximation and the maximal consequence then give common almost-everywhere limits and convergence in the output norm. Maximal triangular Hilbert transform: The cyclic form is represented by matrix traces and a positive energy evolving under Gaussian smoothing. Mixed trace estimates compare energy derivatives at adjacent variables. A further bound pays for entries switched on or off at separately chosen scales, so telescoping remains uniform under pointwise selection of the cutoffs.
