Research attempt: A finite-cover obstruction to global observability

A compact negatively curved manifold and its double cover
Let $(N,g)$ be a closed connected orientable hyperbolic surface of genus two, with curvature $-1$. One standard explicit construction glues the paired sides of a regular hyperbolic octagon of interior angle $\pi/4$. Such an octagon exists because, in the disk model, its angle varies continuously from the small-polygon Euclidean limit $3\pi/4$ to zero as its vertices approach the ideal boundary. Pair the sides by hyperbolic isometries in the genus-two polygon pattern. All eight vertices are identified and their angles total $2\pi$; edges have two half-neighborhoods. Thus the quotient has smooth hyperbolic neighborhoods also at its seam and vertex. It has one face, four edges and one vertex, Euler characteristic $-2$, and is a compact genus-two surface. This standard construction is also explicitly exhibited in the consulted Goldman slides; no assertion of a new surface construction is made.

Cut $N$ along a nonseparating simple closed smooth curve around a handle. Its cut surface is connected and has two boundary copies of that curve. Take two copies of the cut surface and identify each positive boundary to the negative boundary of the other copy using the original boundary identification. The resulting $M$ is compact and connected: each copy is connected, and the crosswise boundary identifications join them. The map \[ \pi:M\longrightarrow N \] which forgets the copy is two-to-one. Away from the cut it is the identity on a chart in each copy; at the seam each glued pair of half-collars maps bijectively to a collar of the original curve. These charts give a smooth local diffeomorphism everywhere, with exactly two points in each fiber. Equip $M$ with $\widetilde g=\pi^*g$. It is a smooth positive definite metric, and local isometry to $N$ shows that its curvature is identically $-1$.

The external theorem used to produce Euclidean outputs
We invoke Nash's smooth isometric embedding theorem for compact Riemannian manifolds. In the exact form needed here, a compact smooth $n$-manifold with a smooth positive metric admits a smooth isometric embedding into some finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The original paper's Theorem 2 on printed page 59 includes $3\leq k\leq\infty$ and target dimension $n(3n+11)/2$. This theorem is an explicitly declared external dependency; its lengthy proof is not reproduced. Applied to $N$, it supplies \[ j:N\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{17},\qquad j^*\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=g. \] The exact dimension is not essential, but is allowed by the source's unrestricted finite output dimension.
A system with precisely the required Gramian
On $M$ set \[ f=0,\qquad h=j\circ\pi,\qquad T=1. \] Both maps are smooth. Every solution is stationary, $x(t)=x_0$, exists for all real time, and has output $h(x_0)$. In any local state coordinates the derivative of the zero vector field is zero, hence its fundamental matrix is $\Phi(t)=I$. The output derivative is the constant matrix $H(t)=dh_{x_0}$. Therefore the source Gramian, as a bilinear form on $T_{x_0}M$, is \[ P_{x_0}(v,w)=\int_0^1\langle dh_{x_0}v,dh_{x_0}w\rangle\,dt =g_{\pi(x_0)}(d\pi_{x_0}v,d\pi_{x_0}w) =\widetilde g_{x_0}(v,w). \tag{1} \] It is uniformly negatively curved, with sectional curvature $-1$ in this two-dimensional example.
Uniform bounds and geodesic completeness
Uniform positive definiteness and boundedness on an arbitrary manifold are understood relative to a fixed smooth background metric, or in a chosen finite atlas with compact chart cores. Both hold here. For any smooth background metric $g_0$, its unit tangent bundle over compact $M$ is compact. The positive continuous function $v\mapsto\widetilde g(v,v)$ on that unit bundle has positive minimum $a$ and finite maximum $b$. Thus \[ a\,g_0(v,v)\leq P(v,v)\leq b\,g_0(v,v) \] for all tangent vectors. Equivalently, cover $M$ by finitely many coordinate neighborhoods whose closures lie inside larger smooth charts. The matrix entries and least eigenvalues are continuous on these compact closures, giving common finite upper and positive lower coordinate bounds. No coordinate-invariant meaning can be assigned to bounds uniform under arbitrary rescaling of all possible coordinates, and no such meaning is used.

For completeness, the geodesic equation preserves speed: metric compatibility gives $d\widetilde g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)/dt=2\widetilde g(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0$. Geodesics of a fixed speed stay in the corresponding compact sphere bundle of $TM$. The smooth geodesic vector field has a uniform local existence time on that compact set, by covering it with finitely many ordinary differential-equation neighborhoods. A geodesic therefore cannot have a finite endpoint of its maximal interval of existence: the uniform existence time extends it from a sufficiently late point. The same argument in reverse time proves geodesic completeness. Zero-speed geodesics are constant.

Failure of global observability
Choose the two distinct points $x_0,x_1$ over any point of $N$. They have \[ h(x_0)=j(\pi(x_0))=j(\pi(x_1))=h(x_1). \] Their entire constant output trajectories agree on $[0,1]$, although their initial states differ. Thus the system is not globally observable. Locally, $\pi$ is a diffeomorphism and $j$ is an embedding, so $h$ is locally one-to-one, consistent with its positive Gramian. Every hypothesis of the literal general-manifold question is satisfied, and the proposed implication is false. Simple connectedness, a global Euclidean coordinate chart, and restrictions on output dimension are not hypotheses of the consulted source and are not supplied by this argument.
