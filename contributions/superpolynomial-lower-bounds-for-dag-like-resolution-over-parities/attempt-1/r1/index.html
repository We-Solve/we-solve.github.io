Superpolynomial Lower Bounds for Dag-Like Resolution over Parities

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

An affine parity equation is $\sum_{i\in S}x_i=b$ over $\mathbb F_2$, with $b\in\{0,1\}$. A parity clause is a disjunction of such equations. Resolution over parities, $\operatorname{Res}(\oplus)$, permits resolving complementary parity equations and the source linear-algebra inference rules. A refutation derives the empty clause from an unsatisfiable formula. Dag-like proofs may reuse previously derived clauses, whereas tree-like proofs duplicate them when reused. Size measures the encoded derivation under the source convention. A superpolynomial lower bound exceeds every fixed power of the input size for a suitable growing family of unsatisfiable instances.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Prove a superpolynomial lower bound on the size of dag-like $\operatorname{Res}(\oplus)$ refutations. [S2]
Scope qualification.
The target is the general dag-like system. Lower bounds for regular, tree-like, or otherwise restricted parity resolution do not settle it.

Find formulas for which every unrestricted parity-resolution refutation is larger than any polynomial in the input length.

Testing the pure parity-cycle route

The primary source distinguishes regular resolution over parities from the general system. The 2026 Itsykson--Knop paper likewise states the general size question as open while establishing size--depth tradeoffs. We use its semantic weakening convention: from a linear clause one may infer any linear clause it implies, and resolve complementary equations $f=0,f=1$. A depth tradeoff alone is not an unrestricted size lower bound. Consider $m\ge3$ variables on a cycle and unit parity clauses \[ x_i+x_{i+1}=b_i\quad(1\le i\le m),\qquad x_{m+1}=x_1, \qquad \sum_i b_i=1 \] over $\mathbb F_2$. These are already parity clauses, not an unspecified CNF encoding.
Proposition: This contradictory family has a tree-like resolution-over-parities refutation with $O(m)$ lines and $O(m^2)$ bits in an uncompressed dense representation.
Proof: From units $f=b$ and $g=c$, weaken the second to \[ (f=1-b)\ \vee\ (f+g=b+c). \] Every assignment satisfying $g=c$ satisfies this disjunction: if $f\ne b$ the first disjunct holds, and otherwise the second does. Resolve with $f=b$ to infer the unit $f+g=b+c$. Repeatedly applying this operation to the cycle equations yields after $j<m$ equations the unit $x_1+x_{j+1}=\sum_{i\le j}b_i$. The last addition gives $0=1$, which semantically implies the empty clause. Each earlier derived unit is used once, each input is used once, and each addition costs two lines. This proves the tree and line bounds. A linear equation on $m$ variables uses at most $O(m)$ bits, so even dense encodings have the stated total size. ∎
Thus an attempted lower bound based merely on global inconsistency of local parity constraints fails: this system can accumulate that inconsistency directly. This does not say all Tseitin encodings are easy in all proof systems, or identify a hard family for the general parity system.
