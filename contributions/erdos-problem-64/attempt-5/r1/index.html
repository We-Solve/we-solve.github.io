Problem 64: The Erdős--Gyárfás conjecture (power-of-two cycle lengths)

1) ROUND-4 OBJECTIVE

Path (A) --- proof, via a sharper minimal-counterexample reduction. Round 3 reduced any putative counterexample to a connected edge-minimal graph $H$ with $\delta(H)=3$ and $V_{\ge 4}(H)$ independent, and obtained the quantitative bound that at least $4/7$ of the vertices have degree exactly $3$. The most promising next step is to strengthen that reduction itself: show that edge-minimality forces interaction among the degree-$3$ vertices, not just a weak counting inequality. This succeeds. I prove that every degree-$3$ vertex of $H$ has a degree-$3$ neighbor, which upgrades the $4/7$ bound to a sharp $2/3$ bound and isolates the equality case.

2) Round-3 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round 3 results without reproving them:
  • Round-3 Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2. If a counterexample exists, then there is a connected edge-minimal counterexample $H$ such that $\delta(H)=3$, and the set \[ B:=V_{\ge 4}(H)=\{v\in V(H): d_H(v)\ge 4\} \] is independent.
  • Round-3 Lemma 5.3. With \[ A:=V_3(H)=\{v\in V(H): d_H(v)=3\}, \] one has $|A|\ge 4|V(H)|/7$.
  • Round-2/3 validated literature inputs. Any counterexample has diameter at least $3$; every $P_{13}$-free graph with minimum degree at least $3$ satisfies the conjecture; hence every counterexample contains an induced $P_{13}$; any counterexample has at least $17$ vertices; and any bipartite counterexample has at least $32$ vertices.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-4)

The new tool is a subgraph-minimality upgrade of Round 3's edge-minimality. For the chosen edge-minimal counterexample $H$, no proper subgraph of $H$ can still have minimum degree at least $3$, because every proper subgraph has fewer edges and cannot create new cycles.

This stronger minimality has three consequences.

  1. Every degree-$3$ vertex has a degree-$3$ neighbor.
  2. The induced subgraph $H[A]$ has minimum degree at least $1$.
  3. Consequently, \[ |A|\ge 2|B|, \] so at least $2/3$ of all vertices have degree exactly $3$.
Moreover, equality is rigid: if $|A|=2|B|$, then $H[A]$ is a perfect matching and every vertex of $B$ has degree exactly $4$.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-4)

After Round 3, the exact unresolved gap was that the edge-minimal core still allowed a substantial population of vertices of degree at least $4$; the bound $|A|\ge 4|V(H)|/7$ was too weak to make the counterexample class genuinely narrow.

The Round 4 plan is therefore:

  • strengthen Round 3 Lemma 5.1 from single-edge deletions to arbitrary proper subgraphs;
  • use that stronger minimality to prove that vertices of degree $3$ cannot be supported entirely by higher-degree neighbors;
  • convert this local structural fact into a global counting theorem $|A|\ge 2|B|$;
  • isolate the equality case as a sharply formulated obstruction.
This overcomes the main Round 3 obstacle by forcing the degree-$3$ vertices to form a nontrivial internal backbone.

5) WORK (ROUND-4)

Lemma 5.9 (new: subgraph-minimality of the edge-minimal counterexample).
Let $H$ be the connected edge-minimal counterexample provided by Round 3 Lemma 5.1. Then no proper subgraph $F\subsetneq H$ satisfies $\delta(F)\ge 3$.

Proof. Suppose that some proper subgraph $F\subsetneq H$ has $\delta(F)\ge 3$. Since $F$ is a proper subgraph, it has strictly fewer edges than $H$. Also, every cycle of $F$ is a cycle of $H$, because subgraphs do not create new cycles. Since $H$ has no cycle whose length is a power of $2$, neither does $F$. Therefore $F$ is also a counterexample, contradicting the choice of $H$ as a counterexample with the minimum possible number of edges. $\square$

Corollary 5.10 (new: every degree-$3$ vertex meets $A$).
Every vertex of $A$ has at least one neighbor in $A$. Equivalently, the induced subgraph $H[A]$ has minimum degree at least $1$.

Proof. Let $a\in A$. Suppose, for contradiction, that every neighbor of $a$ lies in $B$. Since $d_H(a)=3$, the graph $H-a$ is a proper subgraph of $H$. Every neighbor of $a$ had degree at least $4$ in $H$, so after deleting $a$ each of those neighbors still has degree at least $3$. All other vertex degrees are unchanged. Hence \[ \delta(H-a)\ge 3. \] This contradicts Lemma 5.9. Therefore $a$ has a neighbor in $A$. Since $a\in A$ was arbitrary, $\delta(H[A])\ge 1$. $\square$

Theorem 5.11 (new: the $2/3$ theorem for the edge-minimal core).
With $A=V_3(H)$ and $B=V_{\ge 4}(H)$ as above, \[ |A|\ge 2|B|. \] Equivalently, \[ |A|\ge \frac{2}{3}|V(H)|, \qquad |B|\le \frac{1}{3}|V(H)|. \] In particular, at least two-thirds of the vertices of $H$ have degree exactly $3$.

Proof. By Corollary 5.10, every vertex of $H[A]$ has degree at least $1$ inside $H[A]$. Hence \[ 2e_H(A)\ge |A|. \] Every vertex of $A$ has total degree $3$, so \[ 3|A| = 2e_H(A)+e_H(A,B). \] Using $2e_H(A)\ge |A|$, we obtain \[ e_H(A,B) = 3|A|-2e_H(A) \le 2|A|. \] On the other hand, Round 3 Corollary 5.2 says that $B$ is independent. Therefore every edge incident with a vertex of $B$ lies in $E_H(A,B)$, and since each vertex of $B$ has degree at least $4$, \[ e_H(A,B)=\sum_{b\in B} d_H(b)\ge 4|B|. \] Combining the two inequalities yields \[ 4|B|\le e_H(A,B)\le 2|A|, \] so $|A|\ge 2|B|$. Since $|V(H)|=|A|+|B|$, this is equivalent to \[ |A|\ge \frac{2}{3}|V(H)|, \qquad |B|\le \frac{1}{3}|V(H)|. \] $\square$

Corollary 5.12 (new: rigidity of the equality case).
Equality $|A|=2|B|$ in Theorem 5.11 holds if and only if both of the following are true:
  1. every vertex of $B$ has degree exactly $4$;
  2. the graph $H[A]$ is $1$-regular, i.e. $H[A]$ is a disjoint union of edges (a perfect matching on $A$).

Proof. If $|A|=2|B|$, then the proof of Theorem 5.11 gives \[ 4|B|\le e_H(A,B)\le 2|A|=4|B|, \] so equality holds throughout. Hence \[ e_H(A,B)=4|B|, \] which forces $d_H(b)=4$ for every $b\in B$, because each $b\in B$ has degree at least $4$ and \[ e_H(A,B)=\sum_{b\in B} d_H(b). \] Also, \[ e_H(A,B)=2|A|. \] Substituting into \[ 3|A|=2e_H(A)+e_H(A,B) \] shows that \[ 2e_H(A)=|A|. \] Since Corollary 5.10 says that every vertex of $H[A]$ has degree at least $1$, the equality $2e_H(A)=|A|$ implies that every vertex of $H[A]$ has degree exactly $1$. Thus $H[A]$ is $1$-regular, hence a perfect matching.

Conversely, if every vertex of $B$ has degree $4$ and $H[A]$ is a perfect matching, then \[ 2e_H(A)=|A|, \qquad e_H(A,B)=3|A|-2e_H(A)=2|A|, \] while also \[ e_H(A,B)=\sum_{b\in B} d_H(b)=4|B|. \] Therefore $2|A|=4|B|$, i.e. \[ |A|=2|B|. \] $\square$

Lemma 5.13 (new: no component of $H[A]$ is closed under $B$-neighbors).
Assume $H[A]$ has at least two connected components. Then for every connected component $C$ of $H[A]$, there exists a vertex $b\in B$ that has a neighbor in $C$ and a neighbor in $A\setminus V(C)$.

Proof. Fix a connected component $C$ of $H[A]$, and let \[ S:=V(C)\cup \left(N_H(V(C))\cap B\right). \] Because $C$ is a connected component of $H[A]$, every neighbor in $A$ of a vertex of $C$ also lies in $C$. Thus every vertex of $C$ has all three of its neighbors inside $S$, so it has degree $3$ in $H[S]$.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that every $b\in B\cap S$ has all its neighbors in $C$. Then each such $b$ keeps all of its neighbors in $H[S]$, and therefore has degree at least $4$ in $H[S]$. Hence \[ \delta(H[S])\ge 3. \] Since $H[A]$ has at least two connected components, there is a vertex of $A\setminus V(C)$, so $S\neq V(H)$. Therefore $H[S]$ is a proper induced subgraph of $H$, contradicting Lemma 5.9.

Thus some $b\in B\cap S$ must have a neighbor outside $C$. Since $B$ is independent, that outside neighbor must lie in $A\setminus V(C)$. $\square$

Updated obstruction profile after Round 4.
Combining the new Round 4 results with the validated inputs from Rounds 2 and 3, we obtain the following.

If a counterexample exists, then there is a connected edge-minimal counterexample $H$ such that:

  1. $\delta(H)=3$;
  2. $B=V_{\ge 4}(H)$ is independent;
  3. every vertex of $A=V_3(H)$ has a neighbor in $A$;
  4. at least $2/3$ of the vertices of $H$ lie in $A$;
  5. if equality $|A|=2|B|$ holds, then $H[A]$ is a perfect matching and every vertex of $B$ has degree exactly $4$;
  6. if $H[A]$ is disconnected, then every component of $H[A]$ is linked to another component through some vertex of $B$;
  7. $\operatorname{diam}(H)\ge 3$;
  8. $H$ contains an induced $P_{13}$;
  9. $|V(H)|\ge 17$;
  10. if $H$ is bipartite, then $|V(H)|\ge 32$.
Thus the unresolved core is now narrower than in Round 3: a putative counterexample must be degree-$3$-dominated by a full two-thirds margin, with the degree-$3$ vertices themselves forming a nontrivial internal scaffold.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Does Lemma 5.9 really strengthen Round 3 Lemma 5.1? Yes. Round 3 handled deletion of a single edge whose endpoints both had degree at least $4$. Lemma 5.9 applies to every proper subgraph. The key monotonicity fact is valid: passing to a subgraph never creates a new cycle.

  • Could $H-a$ in Corollary 5.10 be disconnected, invalidating the contradiction? No. Connectivity is irrelevant for the contradiction. A disconnected graph with minimum degree at least $3$ and no power-of-two cycle is still a counterexample, since each component has minimum degree at least $3$ and every cycle of every component is also a cycle of the whole graph.

  • Is the $2/3$ inequality sharp? The proof shows exactly where equality can occur: simultaneously $e_H(A,B)=2|A|=4|B|$ and $2e_H(A)=|A|$. Corollary 5.12 verifies that this is equivalent to the rigid configuration "all vertices of $B$ have degree $4$ and $H[A]$ is a perfect matching." No hidden assumptions are used.

  • Is Lemma 5.13 stronger than mere connectivity of $H$? Yes. Connectivity only guarantees that each component of $H[A]$ has some neighbor in $B$. Lemma 5.13 proves that no component of $H[A]$ can be sealed off by $B$-vertices that connect only to that component; otherwise one gets a proper induced subgraph with minimum degree at least $3$.

  • Interaction with Round 3. Theorem 5.11 strictly strengthens Round 3 Lemma 5.3: the lower bound on degree-$3$ vertices improves from $4|V(H)|/7$ to $2|V(H)|/3$. The earlier bound remains true but is now superseded.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED). The full Erdős--Gyárfás conjecture remains open in general, but Round 4 makes a genuine structural advance beyond Round 3:
  • it upgrades edge-minimality to full subgraph-minimality (Lemma 5.9);
  • it proves that every degree-$3$ vertex has a degree-$3$ neighbor (Corollary 5.10);
  • it strengthens the Round 3 bound from $4/7$ to $2/3$ on the proportion of degree-$3$ vertices (Theorem 5.11);
  • it characterizes the equality case $|A|=2|B|$ (Corollary 5.12);
  • it proves that no connected component of $H[A]$ can be closed under all incident $B$-neighbors unless $H[A]$ itself is connected (Lemma 5.13).
The sharpest obstruction now isolated is this: any minimal counterexample must be a connected graph of diameter at least $3$, containing an induced $P_{13}$, in which at least two-thirds of all vertices have degree exactly $3$, every such vertex has another degree-$3$ neighbor, and the extremal equality case forces the degree-$3$ vertices to form a perfect matching while all remaining vertices have degree exactly $4$.
