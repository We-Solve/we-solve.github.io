Multipartite Quantum Data Hiding with Separable States

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A multipartite state is a positive trace-one operator on $\mathcal H_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_m$, with $m>2$. A fully separable state is a convex combination of product states. Data hiding compares distinguishability by unrestricted measurements with distinguishability under an allowed local or separable measurement class. Information efficiency measures how much classical information is encoded relative to quantum-system size; hiding quality measures the gap between global and restricted distinguishability.

A broad separable ansatz that cannot hide.
Suppose two multipartite states are diagonal in the same local product basis: $\rho=\sum_{\mathbf a}p_{\mathbf a}|\mathbf a\rangle\langle\mathbf a|$ and $\sigma=\sum_{\mathbf a}q_{\mathbf a}|\mathbf a\rangle\langle\mathbf a|$. Their trace-norm difference is $\|\rho-\sigma\|_1=\sum_{\mathbf a}|p_{\mathbf a}-q_{\mathbf a}|$. Measuring each local basis and communicating the outcomes yields exactly these classical distributions. The optimal likelihood-ratio decision thus achieves the global discrimination bias $\|\rho-\sigma\|_1/2$ by LOCC. This applies for every number of parties and every local dimension.
An explicit failed parity code.
On $m$ qubits, let $\rho_b$ be the uniform mixture of computational basis strings whose bit parity is $b$. The states are fully separable and have orthogonal supports, but local bit measurements followed by a parity computation distinguish them perfectly. Encoding a global classical correlation is not sufficient for quantum data hiding.
Why adding spectators does not establish optimality.
Tensoring a bipartite hiding pair with fixed local product ancillas preserves its global trace distance. Any multipartite LOCC protocol on these states can be simulated by the original parties plus the known uncorrelated ancillas and classical communication, so it supplies no stronger concealment than the original discrimination problem. Such padding may retain a bipartite example but does not determine optimal information efficiency or hiding-quality scaling with genuine multipartite resources. The first gap is a fully separable construction using incompatible local descriptions together with matching bounds for the exact allowed measurement class. LOCC and all separable measurements remain distinct targets.
