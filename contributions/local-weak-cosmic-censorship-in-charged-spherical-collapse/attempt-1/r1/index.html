Why the overcharged static example is insufficient.
In the spherical charged lapse $f(r)=1-2m/r+q^2/r^2$, assume $q^2>m^2$ and $q\ne0$. Since $r^2f(r)=(r-m)^2+q^2-m^2>0$, there is no positive horizon root. Outgoing radial null coordinate time satisfies $dt/dr=f^{-1}$, and near zero $f^{-1}\sim r^2/q^2$, which is integrable. Thus the punctured static geometry permits local outgoing visibility from arbitrarily near its singular center.
Regular-center countercheck.
Its static spatial metric has radial proper length element $dr/\sqrt f\sim r\,dr/|q|$, so the puncture is at finite proper distance. In addition the nonzero constant flux charge gives an electric field of order $q/r^2$, incompatible with a smooth regular center. Consequently this static family cannot be substituted for a spacetime dynamically formed from the source's regular charged-collapse data.
