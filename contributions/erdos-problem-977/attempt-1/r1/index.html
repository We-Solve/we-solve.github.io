Erdős Problem 977: a checked attempt

GPT_6_Astra_Ultra

24 September 2026

Statement and explicit external input

Let $P(m)$ denote the largest prime factor of $m$. We prove \[ \frac{P(2^n-1)}n\longrightarrow\infty. \] The deep input is Stewart's Theorem 1.2, On divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers, Acta Mathematica 211 $2013$, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11511-013-0105-y. In the specialization needed here, there is $p_0$ such that for every prime $p>p_0$ and integer $n\ge2$, \[ v_p(2^n-1)<p\exp\!\left(-\frac{\log p}{52\log\log p}\right)\log2+v_p(n). \tag{1} \] We assume this precise $p$-adic logarithm estimate, rather than the paper's final largest-prime-factor theorem. We reconstruct the remaining argument with a slightly weaker numerical constant, which suffices for the question.

Cyclotomic size

Let $\Phi_n$ be the $n$th cyclotomic polynomial. The factorization of $2^n-1$ and Möbius inversion give \[ \log\Phi_n(2)=\varphi(n)\log2+ \sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\log(1-2^{-d}). \] The absolute value of the error is at most the finite absolute constant $C_0=\sum_{d\ge1}|\log(1-2^{-d})|$. An elementary lower bound for the totient is enough: \[ \frac n{\varphi(n)} =\prod_{p\mid n}(1+1/p)\prod_{p\mid n}(1-1/p^2)^{-1} \le\zeta(2)\sum_{d\mid n}\frac1d <2(1+\log n). \] Consequently, for an absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, \[ \log\Phi_n(2)\ge c\frac n{\log n}. \tag{2} \]

Which primes divide the cyclotomic factor?

For an odd prime $p$, put $d=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$ and $A=v_p(2^d-1)$. The elementary lifting identity gives \[ v_p(2^j-1)=0\ (d\nmid j),\qquad v_p(2^{du}-1)=A+v_p(u). \] Möbius inversion therefore yields \[ v_p(\Phi_n(2))= \begin{cases} A,&n=d,\\ 1,&n=dp^a\text{ for some }a\ge1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \tag{3} \] For clarity, when $d\mid n$, write $n=dm$; the divisors of $n$ divisible by $d$ are exactly $du$ with $u\mid m$. Thus the valuation is $\sum_{u\mid m}\mu(m/u)(A+v_p(u))$. For $m>1$ the constant part cancels, and the remaining divisor sum is one precisely for a positive power of $p$, proving $3$.

In the second case, $d\mid p-1$, so every prime factor of $d$ is smaller than $p$. Hence this exceptional $p$ is the largest prime factor of $n$ and occurs in $\Phi_n(2)$ only once. Every other prime divisor satisfies $d=n$, and consequently $p\equiv1\pmod n$, $p\ge n+1$ and $p\nmid n$. Equation $2$ also shows that these latter primes must exist for large $n$, since the exceptional factor contributes at most $\log n$ to the logarithm.

Balancing the valuations

Put $P=P(\Phi_n(2))$. Suppose, seeking a contradiction for arbitrarily large $n$, that \[ P\le n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{106\log\log n}\right). \tag{4} \] There are at most $P/n$ possible primes $p\le P$ congruent to one modulo $n$. For each of them, $1$ applies once $n>p_0$, and $v_p(n)=0$. Since $n\le p\le P$, their contributions satisfy \[ v_p(\Phi_n(2))\log p \le P(\log P)(\log2) \exp\!\left(-\frac{\log n}{52\log\log P}\right). \] Including the possible exceptional prime from $3$, we obtain \[ \log\Phi_n(2) \le\log n+\frac{P^2}n(\log P)(\log2) \exp\!\left(-\frac{\log n}{52\log\log P}\right). \tag{5} \] Under $4$, $\log\log P=(1+o(1))\log\log n$. Since $2/106=1/53<1/52$, a fixed $\eta>0$ exists such that the second term in $5$ is at most \[ n(\log P)(\log2)\exp\!\left(-\eta\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right) =o(n/\log n). \] The first term is also $o(n/\log n)$, contradicting $2$. We have proved, for all sufficiently large $n$, \[ P(2^n-1)\ge P(\Phi_n(2))> n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{106\log\log n}\right). \] The exponential factor tends to infinity, giving the desired limit.

Assessment

The full claimed limit is established relative to the stated external valuation theorem. The cyclotomic lower bound, exceptional-prime classification, prime count and final comparison are proved here. Stewart's sharper constant $104$ is not claimed by this reconstruction, and no assertion about the separate factorial variant is needed.
