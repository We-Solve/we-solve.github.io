Quantum-Group and Affine-Hecke Solutions of Boundary qKZ Equations

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The boundary quantum Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov (qKZ) equations are compatible difference equations for vector-valued functions of spectral parameters, with exchange operators built from $R$- and boundary $K$-matrices. An affine Hecke algebra is the deformation of an affine Weyl-group algebra subject to braid and quadratic relations. A Weyl chamber is a region determined by inequalities between root coordinates. A $q$-integral is the discrete analogue of integration adapted to multiplicative $q$-shifts.

Determine the relation between the special boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov solutions obtained as quantum correlation functions or $q$-integrals and the affine-Hecke-algebra basis characterized by its asymptotics in a Weyl chamber.

The connection coefficient is initially q-periodic.
Consider a common one-variable difference system $F(qz)=A(z)F(z)$ with $0<|q|<1$ and invertible matrix solutions $F,G$ on a $q$-stable domain. Define $C(z)=F(z)^{-1}G(z)$. Substitution gives \[ C(qz)=F(z)^{-1}A(z)^{-1}A(z)G(z)=C(z). \] Thus two fundamental solutions differ by a multiplicatively periodic matrix, not automatically a constant. The same argument in several variables yields invariance under every independent compatible shift.
A useful normalization criterion.
If $C$ extends continuously to $z=0$, then for every point whose forward $q$-orbit remains in the domain, $C(z)=C(q^nz)\to C(0)$, so it is constant there. If the two constructions have identical normalized leading asymptotics, then $C(0)=I$ and the solutions agree on that domain. Without the extension hypothesis, nonconstant log-periodic functions are possible: on the positive real axis, $C(z)=\exp(2\pi i\log z/\log q)$ for real $0<q<1$ is $q$-periodic and lacks a limit at zero.
Boundary-qKZ obstruction.
The source bases can have singular exponents, chamber-dependent asymptotics and nontrivial connection matrices. Their quantum correlation or $q$-integral normalizations have not been shown to satisfy the extension criterion after matching parameters. The first gap is analytic control of those asymptotics and of permissible $q$-periodic factors. Merely proving both families solve the same difference equations would not identify the affine-Hecke asymptotic basis.
