Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Subgroup membership asks whether an input matrix is a finite word in the two displayed generators and their inverses. The matrices have determinant one, so their products lie in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Q)$.

The group $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ consists of integer matrices with integer inverses, and $\mathrm{SL}_n$ imposes determinant one. The subgroup generated by matrices consists of finite products of them and their inverses. A centralizer consists of elements commuting with the specified element.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For an integer $K\geq2$, let $H(K)\leq\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb Q)$ be generated by

$$A(K)=\begin{pmatrix}1&K\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B(K)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\K&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Is there a sublinear-time algorithm for the subgroup membership problem in $H(K)$? [S2]

Research attempt: A bit-query obstruction on upper unipotent inputs

Exact intersection calculation.
Fix an integer $K\ge2$, and put $U(M)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&M\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for an integer $M$. Then \[ U(M)\in H(K)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\mid M. \] The reverse implication is $U(M)=A(K)^{M/K}$. For the forward direction, combine adjacent powers of the same generator and remove powers with exponent zero in any representing word. Apply the resulting alternating word to $e_1=(1,0)^T$, from right to left. A rightmost power of $A$ fixes $e_1$ and can be discarded for this calculation. If any $B$ remains, its first application gives $(1,bK)^T$, with $b\ne0$, so $|y|>|x|$ and $y\ne0$. Whenever $|y|>|x|$, a nonzero power $A^a$ gives \[ |x+aKy|\ge K|y|-|x|>|y|. \] Whenever $|x|>|y|$, a nonzero power $B^b$ similarly gives $|y+bKx|>|x|$. Thus after the first $B$, the second coordinate never vanishes: an $A$ step preserves it and a $B$ step makes its absolute value strictly positive. A word containing $B$ therefore cannot be upper triangular. A word without $B$ is $A^a$, proving the assertion without invoking a freeness theorem.
Linear input-bit lower bound.
Take the fixed value $K=3$, or any fixed odd $K\ge3$. The input is an explicitly encoded integer matrix, with random access to individual binary digits; exact membership must be correct on every valid input. For $L\ge1$, let \[ M_0=K2^L,\qquad M_i=K2^L+2^i\quad(0\le i<L). \] All matrices $U(M_i)$ have determinant one and the same bit length. Their encodings differ from $U(M_0)$ in exactly one of its bottom $L$ digits. The base matrix belongs to $H(K)$, and none of the alternatives does, since odd $K>1$ cannot divide $2^i$.

On the base input a deterministic exact algorithm must inspect all these $L$ digits. If it omits digit $i$, its entire adaptive transcript is identical on the corresponding alternative, yet the required answers differ. The total input length is $\Theta(L)$, so even this promised subclass precludes a worst-case sublinear bit-query bound.

The conclusion also holds for bounded-error randomization with a worst-case cap of $q$ bit queries. Give the base input probability $1/2$ and distribute the other probability $1/2$ uniformly over the $L$ alternatives. Couple the algorithm's random coins on the base and a random alternative. Until the changed digit is inspected, the two executions coincide. On the base path at most $q$ of the $L$ positions are inspected; consequently the probability of seeing the change is at most $q/L$. The resulting transcript distributions have total variation at most $q/L$: the probability of a differing coupled transcript bounds the difference of probabilities of every event. Under equal priors, any decision rule therefore has average error at least $(1-q/L)/2$, since its acceptance probabilities differ by at most $q/L$. Worst-case error at most $1/3$ forces $q\ge L/3$.

Why this does not settle the unqualified question.
The obstruction charges access to digits, not arithmetic operations on an already loaded integer, and concerns worst-case exact or bounded-error correctness, not generic-case rejection. It does not rule out a sublinear bound in the numerical magnitude $\max |M_{ij}|$, a promise excluding these inputs, or a word-RAM convention making long-integer operations free. For $K=2$ this particular upper-unipotent restriction merely tests parity and has a one-bit algorithm; extending the obstruction to every $K$ would require a different family. The source supplies none of the conventions needed to identify these variants.

Verification and adversarial audit

The alternating-word argument was checked for the empty word, a pure $A$ power, negative exponents, $K=2$, and a word beginning or ending with either generator. Strict dominance, rather than positive entries, is what handles cancellation. All adversarial inputs are integral determinant-one matrices with equal encoding lengths. The randomized argument uses a distribution on valid matrices and explicitly states its worst-case query cap; it is not an unexplained appeal to an information bound.
