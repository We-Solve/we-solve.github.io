Research attempt: A quadratic landscape calculation and two gradient-descent boundaries

A proved quadratic special case.
For inputs $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$, labels $y_i$, and $n\ge1$, consider \[ S=PP^T-QQ^T,\qquad F(S)=\frac1{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i^TSx_i-y_i)^2, \] where $P\in\mathbb R^{d\times a}$ and $Q\in\mathbb R^{d\times b}$, with $a,b\ge d$. This is a shallow quadratic network with fixed positive and negative output weights after absorbing their absolute square roots into the hidden weights. Every symmetric $S$ can be represented: orthogonally diagonalize it and place the square roots of its positive and negative eigenvalues in the respective factors, padding with zero columns.

Set \[ G=\nabla F(S)=\frac1n\sum_i(x_i^TSx_i-y_i)x_ix_i^T. \] The factor gradients are $2GP$ and $-2GQ$, so at a stationary point $GP=GQ=0$. If $G=0$, then for every symmetric $T$, \[ F(T)-F(S)=\operatorname{tr}(G(T-S))+ \frac1{2n}\sum_i(x_i^T(T-S)x_i)^2\ge0. \] Thus the point is globally optimal in the factor model as well.

If $G\ne0$, choose a unit eigenvector $e$ with eigenvalue $\gamma\ne0$. The stationary equations imply $e^TP=e^TQ=0$. If $\gamma>0$, $Q$ has rank at most $d-1$, so, since $b\ge d$, choose a unit vector $z\in\ker Q$. Perturb $Q$ to $Q+t ez^T$. Its first-order change in $S$ vanishes because $Qz=0$, and its exact second-order change is $-t^2ee^T$. Hence \[ \left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}F\left(S-t^2ee^T\right)\right|_{t=0}=-2\gamma<0. \] If $\gamma<0$, use a unit vector in $\ker P$ and perturb $P$ instead, giving second derivative $2\gamma<0$. Thus every stationary point with nonzero $G$ has a negative-curvature direction. Every local minimum is stationary and cannot have such a direction, so all local minima are global. This proof covers arbitrary data and permits nonzero optimal residual; it does not assert that all labels can be interpolated.

A favorable landscape is not an arbitrary-initialization theorem.
For $d=n=1$, $x_1=y_1=1$, and scalar factors $P,Q$, the loss is $(P^2-Q^2-1)^2/2$. At $(0,0)$ the gradient vanishes and ordinary gradient descent stays there for every step-size schedule, although the global minimum zero occurs at $(1,0)$. The stationary point has a negative-curvature direction as predicted. A random-initialization or perturbation theorem would need to exclude this initialization and control step sizes, boundedness and limiting behavior; those do not follow from the stationary-point classification alone. The factor map is noncoercive as well: $P=Q=t$ keeps $S=0$ and the loss constant while parameters diverge.
Changing the activation removes the conclusion.
With one positive input $x=1$ and target $y=1$, consider a bias-free shallow ReLU network $f(1)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\max(0,w_j)$. On the open region where all $w_j<0$, the output is identically zero and the squared loss is $1/2$, with gradient zero in all parameters. Every point of this open region is a local minimum, whereas choosing one $w_j=a_j=1$ and all other output weights zero gives loss zero. Width $m$ may be arbitrarily large. This is a direct counterexample to the claim that a parameter count exceeding the sample count alone guarantees a benign landscape for unspecified activations and initialization. It does not contradict the quadratic theorem or a ReLU theorem with additional data and initialization assumptions.
