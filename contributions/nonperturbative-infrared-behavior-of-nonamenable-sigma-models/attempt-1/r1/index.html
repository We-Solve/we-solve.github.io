Nonperturbative Infrared Behavior of Nonamenable Sigma Models

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
A nonlinear sigma model has fields taking values in a target manifold and an action determined by its target metric. Noncompact hyperbolic targets can have nonamenable symmetry groups, meaning that they lack an invariant mean on bounded functions. Infrared behavior concerns long spatial scales; an infrared-free perturbative RG prediction says that the effective coupling tends to zero at those scales. Nonperturbative analysis seeks conclusions about the full model rather than a formal finite-order series.

Testing what a perturbation series can decide.
Let $0<g<1$ and consider an arbitrary stationary centered Gaussian field with covariance $C_0(x-y)$ tending to zero at large separation. Add an independent, spatially constant centered Gaussian variable of variance $e^{-1/g}$. The resulting covariance is \[ C_g(x-y)=C_0(x-y)+e^{-1/g}. \] It is positive definite: for coefficients $a_j$ the additional quadratic form is $e^{-1/g}|\sum_ja_j|^2\geq0$. All derivatives of $e^{-1/g}$ at $g=0^+$ vanish, since they are that exponential times a polynomial in $1/g$. Consequently its formal expansion agrees to every order with that of $C_0$, but $\lim_{|x-y|\to\infty}C_g(x-y)=e^{-1/g}>0$ for every fixed $g>0$.
Nonuniformity is the obstruction.
If $C_0(r)=r^{-\eta}$ in a range with $\eta>0$, the added term dominates beyond $r>e^{1/(\eta g)}$. No finite-order or even full formal expansion at fixed distance rules this out. This is an explicitly constructed covariance countertest to a method, not a nonamenable-target sigma-model counterexample.
