Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
A rate-independent model has dissipation positively homogeneous of degree one in the evolution rate, so its response is invariant under suitable time reparametrization. Rate-dependent processes have a distinct time scale, often through viscous dissipation. Fracture and damage variables obey irreversibility constraints. Energetic, local and vanishing-viscosity solution concepts may differ at jumps when the energy is nonconvex. A reliable numerical scheme must identify and converge to the intended concept while resolving the coupling.

A coupled incremental energy inequality.
For scalar displacement $u$, irreversible damage variable $z$, step $h>0$, viscosity $\nu>0$, toughness $r\ge0$, and load $\ell_n$, take \[ E_n(u,z)=\tfrac12(u-z)^2+W(z)-\ell_nu. \] Assume a global minimizer $(u_n,z_n)$ exists for \[ E_n(u,z)+r(z-z_{n-1})+\frac{\nu}{2h}(u-u_{n-1})^2, \qquad z\ge z_{n-1}. \] The previous pair is an admissible competitor. Therefore, with $\Delta u_n=u_n-u_{n-1}$ and $\Delta z_n=z_n-z_{n-1}$, \[ E_n(u_n,z_n)+r\Delta z_n+\frac\nu{2h}|\Delta u_n|^2 \le E_{n-1}(u_{n-1},z_{n-1})-(\ell_n-\ell_{n-1})u_{n-1}. \] Summation telescopes the energies and displays the discrete work contribution explicitly. No differentiability of $W$ is needed for this comparison.
Why the limit is not determined.
This estimate is compatible with global-minimum jumps in $z$. If the intended evolution selects local metastable branches or a vanishing-viscosity jump cost, the inequality does not show that this globally minimizing update converges to it. Different ratios of numerical step to regularizing viscosity can retain different transition behavior.
Scope audit.
The model is a scalar coupled test, not a fracture-space discretization or an efficient algorithm. Minimizer existence, mesh compactness and limiting energy/work identities require additional coercivity and convergence proofs. The first gap is choosing the source solution concept and proving the time/space/viscosity limits select precisely it. The derived inequality alone cannot certify reliability or mechanical validity.
