Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A reconstruction model consists of a measurement map $A:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ and a learned reconstruction map $R:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal X$. Given a signal $x$ and perturbed measurement $Ax+\eta$, a worst-case loss over perturbations $\mathcal U$ is $\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal U}\ell(R(Ax+\eta),x)$. Distribution shift replaces the training joint distribution $P$ of signals and measurements by a test distribution $Q$; its reconstruction risk is $\mathbb E_{(X,Y)\sim Q}[\ell(R(Y),X)]$. These describe different robustness criteria, each requiring its own signal class, perturbation or distribution family, and loss.

Research attempt: Indistinguishability lower bounds and risk-order reversal under small shifts

A measurement obstruction that applies to every reconstruction map.
Let $\mathcal X$ be a subset of a normed signal space and let $A$ be a map into a normed measurement space. If $x_0,x_1\in\mathcal X$ satisfy $\|Ax_0-Ax_1\|\leq2\delta$, the common observation $y=(Ax_0+Ax_1)/2$ is within $\delta$ of both exact measurements. For any deterministic reconstruction $R$, \[ \max\{\|R(y)-x_0\|,\|R(y)-x_1\|\} \geq\frac12\|x_0-x_1\|, \] by the triangle inequality. Taking the supremum over such pairs gives the minimax lower bound $\frac12\omega(2\delta)$, where $\omega(t)=\sup\{\|x-x'\|:x,x'\in\mathcal X,\|Ax-Ax'\|\leq t\}$, with a supremum rather than an assumed maximizing pair. For squared norm loss, square each pairwise bound before taking the supremum. This has nothing specifically neural about it: indistinguishable noisy data preclude robustness for every estimator.

Conversely assume $\mathcal X$ is nonempty compact, $A$ is continuous, and $\|Ax-Ax'\|\geq\alpha\|x-x'\|$ on $\mathcal X$ for some $\alpha>0$. Choose $R(y)\in\arg\min_{z\in\mathcal X}\|Az-y\|$, whose existence follows from compactness. If $y=Ax+e$, $\|e\|\leq\delta$, its residual is at most $\delta$, and the triangle inequality gives $\|AR(y)-Ax\|\leq2\delta$, hence $\|R(y)-x\|\leq2\delta/\alpha$. This is an existence bound for a possibly expensive estimator, not a trained network guarantee.

Small training error does not replace a Lipschitz bound.
For a single training signal and measurement $x=y=0$, the scalar ReLU reconstruction $R_K(y)=K\max(y,0)$ has zero training loss for every $K>0$. At the allowed perturbation $e=\delta>0$, its error is $K\delta$. The source's expected worst-perturbation risk on this point distribution is therefore at least $K^2\delta^2$, unbounded while the training risk is fixed. An architecture or training constraint must control such slopes if a robustness claim is to follow from training accuracy.
Close distributions can still reverse estimator rankings.
On two test cases $z_0,z_1$, let the bounded losses of $f$ be $(0,1)$ and those of $g$ be $(1,0)$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, put $P=(1/2+\varepsilon,1/2-\varepsilon)$ and $Q=(1/2-\varepsilon,1/2+\varepsilon)$. Then $R_P(f)<R_P(g)$ but $R_Q(f)>R_Q(g)$, while $\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=2\varepsilon$ can be arbitrarily small. This can be realized as squared reconstruction loss with target zero at scalar measurements $0,1$, and affine estimators $f(y)=y$, $g(y)=1-y$. Thus it is not merely an abstract loss table. For comparison, if a loss lies in $[0,M]$, one does have $|R_P(f)-R_Q(f)|\leq M\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)$: write its expectation as $\int_0^M P\{\ell>t\}\,dt$ and use the definition $\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\sup_E|P(E)-Q(E)|$. A small absolute-risk difference therefore does not imply a monotone relation between the risks of different estimators.
