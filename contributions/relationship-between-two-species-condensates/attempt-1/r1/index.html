Relationship Between Two-Species Condensates

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
The source concerns a two-species zero-range particle process, not Bose condensation. On $L$ sites, $\eta_i(x)\in\mathbb N_0$ is the occupation of species $i=1,2$ and $\sum_x\eta_i(x)=N_i$ is conserved. A zero-range jump rate depends on the occupations at the departure site. In the thermodynamic limit $N_i/L\to\rho_i$, a condensate is an occupation of order $L$ on a set of sites of vanishing volume fraction, containing the excess mass above the critical background density. The report's stationary canonical ensemble and weight assumptions determine which density pairs have one or two condensed species.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source proves ensemble and single-site condensation facts under assumptions on its weights. The remaining relation of two condensates includes their locations and coupled mass fluctuations; the brief source does not prescribe one particular statistic or a universal rate function.

A genuinely decoupled canonical subcase.
Suppose the two-species stationary one-site weight factorizes as $w(n_1,n_2)=w_1(n_1)w_2(n_2)$, with positive weights and finite canonical partition functions. For configurations with fixed totals $N_1,N_2$, summing the product weight gives \[ Z_{L,N_1,N_2}=Z^{(1)}_{L,N_1}Z^{(2)}_{L,N_2},\qquad \pi_{L,N_1,N_2}=\pi^{(1)}_{L,N_1}\otimes\pi^{(2)}_{L,N_2}. \] This follows by separating the two independent constrained finite sums; it does not require a thermodynamic limit.
Locations and fluctuations.
Let $J_i$ be the location of the largest occupation of species $i$, resolving ties with independent uniform randomness among maximizers. Site permutation invariance gives $\Pr(J_i=x)=1/L$. The factorization and independent tie rules imply \[ \Pr(J_1=J_2)=\sum_{x=1}^L\Pr(J_1=x)\Pr(J_2=x)=1/L. \] Thus, if both species have a single macroscopic condensate with probability tending to one, their locations asymptotically differ in this subcase. For any centered functions $F$ and $G$ of the separate species, $\mathbb E[F G]=0$ exactly. In particular any separately normalized condensate-mass fluctuations are independent for every finite $L$, and any joint distributional limit with marginal limits remains their product, as follows by factoring expectations of bounded continuous product test functions.
Failure of extension.
At $L=1$ the collision probability is one, consistent with the formula. Deterministic tie-breaking would destroy uniformity, so its omission would be an error. No condensation theorem is asserted here: its occurrence was an explicit hypothesis when interpreting maxima as condensates. For coupled $w(n_1,n_2)$ the partition function no longer factors, and the first unresolved step is to compare the weights of co-located and separated macroscopic occupations. This is the relation requested in the full interacting problem.
