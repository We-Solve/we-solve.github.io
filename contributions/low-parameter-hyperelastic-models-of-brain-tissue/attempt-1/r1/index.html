Low-Parameter Hyperelastic Models of Brain Tissue

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A hyperelastic material has strain energy $W(F)$ depending on deformation gradient $F$; stress follows by differentiating this energy. Objectivity and isotropy impose invariance under appropriate rotations of $F$. Shear and axial compression or tension probe different combinations of its invariants. A low-parameter family uses few calibratable constitutive constants; validation requires comparison with independent multiaxial data, not merely interpolation of one loading curve.

An identifiability calculation.
I test the familiar two-parameter incompressible isotropic candidate $W=c_1(I_1-3)+c_2(I_2-3)$, where $I_1=\operatorname{tr}(F^TF)$, $I_2=\operatorname{tr}((F^TF)^{-1})$ when $\det F=1$. This is a candidate family, not a claimed fit to brain data. Along simple shear \[ F=\begin{pmatrix}1&k&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad I_1=I_2=3+k^2, \] so $W=(c_1+c_2)k^2$ and the work-conjugate shear stress is $2(c_1+c_2)k$. Shear observations alone cannot identify the two constants: replacing $(c_1,c_2)$ by $(c_1+a,c_2-a)$ leaves the entire shear curve unchanged.
A complementary load.
For incompressible uniaxial stretch $F=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^{-1/2},\lambda^{-1/2})$, $\lambda>0$, \[ \frac{dW}{d\lambda}=2c_1(\lambda-\lambda^{-2})+2c_2(1-\lambda^{-3}). \] The ratio of these two coefficient functions is $\lambda$ whenever $\lambda\ne1$. Thus the shear sum plus one nontrivial uniaxial measurement determine both constants algebraically, whereas the stress at $\lambda=1$ supplies no information. Near $1$ the coefficient functions nearly coincide, explaining an elementary conditioning problem.
