Attempt and results

We investigate a specified finite set of homogeneous multiplicative cell contrasts on a positive $m$-cell table. This is one precise subfamily of the source's unspecified collections of multiway odds ratios. It does not include ratios of sums formed after collapsing, or arbitrary fixed margins, without additional equations.
Logarithmic compatibility and completeness.
Let $C$ be a real $k$ by $m$ matrix with $C\boldsymbol1=0$, and prescribe $q_i=\prod_{j=1}^m p_j^{C_{ij}}>0$. Define $H=\{u\in\mathbb R^m:\sum u_j=0\}$ and $p_j(u)=e^{u_j}/\sum_l e^{u_l}$. This is a bijection from $H$ to the positive simplex: its inverse is $u_j=\log p_j-m^{-1}\sum_l\log p_l$. Homogeneity gives $\log q=C\log p=Cu$. Since adding a constant vector does not change $Cu$, the image of $C$ on $H$ is its full image. Thus the prescribed ratios are compatible precisely when $\log q\in\operatorname{im}C$. For a compatible value, the inverse image in $H$ is an affine subspace of dimension $m-1-\operatorname{rank}C$, by rank-nullity. The softmax bijection transports it to the table fiber. The specification determines a unique positive table if and only if $\operatorname{rank}C=m-1$. This is an effective finite linear-algebra criterion for the stated positive homogeneous-contrast subproblem, not a new general model theorem.
What fixed margins add in the smallest case.
For a positive $2$ by $2$ table with fixed first-row margin $r$ and first-column margin $c$, both in $(0,1)$, write $(a,b,c',d)=(t,r-t,c-t,1-r-c+t)$. The positive interval is $\max(0,r+c-1)<t<\min(r,c)$. Its odds ratio \[ F(t)=\frac{t(1-r-c+t)}{(r-t)(c-t)} \] has logarithmic derivative $1/t+1/(1-r-c+t)+1/(r-t)+1/(c-t)>0$. At the left endpoint it tends to zero and at the right endpoint to infinity, including coincident-zero cases by cancelling their positive powers. Continuity and strict monotonicity give exactly one positive table for each positive ratio and these margins. By contrast, fixing the ratio alone has rank one on a four-cell simplex and leaves dimension two.
Failed extension to arbitrary specifications.
The logarithm turns a cell product into a linear form, but it does not turn the logarithm of a block sum into a linear form in individual log cells. Nor does it apply at a zero cell. Thus the preceding rank argument does not describe collapsed odds or boundary strata. Even in two dimensions, the one extra odds contrast is complete only after margins are fixed; omitting which margins are given changes the answer. No universal classification for the broad source question follows just from this rank test.
