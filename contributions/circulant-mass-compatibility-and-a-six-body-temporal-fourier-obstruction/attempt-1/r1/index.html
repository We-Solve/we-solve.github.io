Circulant mass compatibility and a six-body temporal Fourier obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. An equal-time-shift choreography has $q_i(t)=q(t+(i-1)P/n)$ for one $P$-periodic curve, with distinct bodies avoiding collision. The masses still appear separately in the Newtonian equations. Equal phase shifts are an assumption of this source question, not of every generalized single-trace motion.

A Fourier obstruction from center of mass.
Write an equal-shift choreography as $q_i(t)=q(t+iP/n)$, $0\le i<n$, and expand its smooth periodic trace in Fourier coefficients $\widehat q_\ell$. In an inertial frame its center of mass is constant, so every nonzero temporal frequency satisfies \[\widehat q_\ell\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}m_i e^{2\pi i\ell i/n}=0. \] If for each nonzero residue $r\pmod n$ at least one coefficient $\widehat q_\ell\ne0$ occurs with $\ell\equiv r$, all nonconstant discrete Fourier coefficients of the mass vector vanish. Inverting the finite Fourier transform then forces every mass to equal their average. This proves a conditional obstruction requiring only the center-of-mass equation.
An inactive-mode test.
For a circular trace only frequencies $\pm1$ occur. With four bodies, alternating masses $(a,b,a,b)$, $a,b>0$, satisfy the center-of-mass condition. Newton's equations add an obstruction: at an a-mass vertex of radius $R$, the required angular frequency is $\omega^2=(a/4+b/\sqrt2)/R^3$; at a b-mass vertex it is $(b/4+a/\sqrt2)/R^3$. Equality implies $(a-b)(1/4-1/\sqrt2)=0$, hence $a=b$. Thus passing the Fourier test is not sufficient.
Gap.
For a general trace with missing Fourier residues, one must use the full force equations to eliminate the remaining mass modes or construct a genuine counterexample. No such argument is proved here for arbitrary n. The source's historical small-n context is not treated as a new proof, and unequal phase shifts remain the distinct question of record 20002541.
