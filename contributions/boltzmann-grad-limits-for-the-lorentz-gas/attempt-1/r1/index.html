Boltzmann–Grad Limits for the Lorentz Gas

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A Lorentz gas consists of point particles moving freely at fixed speed between elastic scatterers and reflecting specularly at collisions. The particle density is $f^\varepsilon(x,v,t)$ with $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and $v\in S^{n-1}$. The Boltzmann--Grad scaling shrinks scatterers and rescales their number or spacing to retain the collision scale. Besides random and periodic arrays, the report examines aperiodic cut-and-project quasicrystal centers. Convergence requires a topology on the particle distributions and an initial density excluding obstacle interiors.
Source scope and qualifications.
The context is quasicrystal scatterers, which the short catalogue question omits; random and periodic arrays are comparison cases. The report uses a particular radius/spacing normalization and presents simulations rather than proof for its aperiodic model. The limit must not silently be assumed to satisfy the ordinary linear Boltzmann equation.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Given a Lorentz gas in the Boltzmann–Grad limit with initial distribution $f_0(x,v)$, does $f^\varepsilon(x,v,t)$ converge as $\varepsilon\to0$ to a limit $f(x,v,t)$, and, if so, what equation does $f$ satisfy?

A memory test for the candidate limiting equation.
In an independent-flight toy process let $\Psi(a)$ be survival probability for a free path longer than age $a$, with density $-\Psi'(a)$ and hazard $h(a)=-\Psi'(a)/\Psi(a)$. If $F(x,v,a,t)$ resolves time since the last collision, free transport with that collision law gives \[ \partial_tF+v\cdot\nabla_xF+\partial_aF=-h(a)F. \] Integrating over $a\geq0$ yields for $f(x,v,t)=\int F\,da$ the loss $\int h(a)F\,da$ and a gain boundary term $F(x,v,0,t)$. The marginal closes with a constant collision rate only when the hazard is constant, or when additional special structure controls the age law.
A concrete nonexponential example.
For $\Psi(a)=(1+a)^{-2}$, the hazard is $2/(1+a)$. Two age distributions of the same total mass, one concentrated near $a=0$ and one near a large $A$, have different losses approaching $2f$ and $2f/(1+A)$. Thus a collision operator depending only on the current $f$ cannot reproduce both. Smooth narrow age densities give the same distinction without delta functions.
Quasicrystal gap.
This does not assert that the source's cut-and-project scatterers have this survival law or independent flights. It explains why importing the ordinary linear Boltzmann equation before proving flight statistics is unjustified. The first required step is convergence of collision-path data for the actual quasicrystal configuration, including correlations and a suitable extended state space. Neither convergence of $f^\epsilon$ nor its final equation follows from the toy renewal calculation.
