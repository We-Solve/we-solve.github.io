Erdős problems #655, #738, #740 --- Round 2 continuation

Problem #738

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Path (C): keep the full problem open, but convert the Round-1 reduction into genuine new positive theorems for large classes of trees, and isolate a sharper obstruction for any future counterexample.

This is the most promising direction because Round 1 already showed that the full problem is equivalent to a finite forcing statement, so the natural next step is to import the strongest finite theorems that are already known and lift them to the infinite triangle-free setting.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following vetted Round-1 ingredients.
  • Finite-to-infinite compactness principle. If a finite-tree forcing theorem holds for all sufficiently large finite triangle-free graphs, then the corresponding statement holds for every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number.
  • Induced-path consequence. Every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contains induced paths of all finite lengths.

I do not re-prove these Round-1 facts.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)

Three finite theorems can now be fed through the Round-1 compactness mechanism.
  1. Scott's induced-subdivision theorem: every bounded-clique graph of sufficiently large chromatic number contains an induced subdivision of any fixed tree [Scott97].
  2. Scott's corollary for subdivisions of stars: if $T$ is a subdivision of a star, then $\operatorname{Forb}(T)$ is $\chi$-bounded [Scott97].
  3. Nguyen--Scott--Seymour's path-induced theorem: every bounded-clique graph of sufficiently large chromatic number contains a path-induced copy of any fixed rooted tree [NguyenScottSeymour24].

This yields new infinite-chromatic consequences that were not extracted in Round 1.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)

After Round 1, the exact gap was: which nontrivial families of trees can already be forced in the infinite triangle-free setting?

I will prove four new claims.

  1. Every finite tree occurs as an induced subdivision.
  2. Every finite subdivision of a star occurs as an induced subgraph.
  3. Every finite tree of radius at most $2$ occurs as an induced subgraph.
  4. Every finite rooted tree occurs as a path-induced subgraph.

These claims together isolate the first genuinely open family: a possible counterexample tree must lie outside both the radius-$2$ family and the subdivision-of-a-star family, and any negative example must exploit the gap between induced copies and path-induced / induced-subdivision copies.

5) WORK (ROUND-2)

We write $\chi(G)$ for the chromatic number and say that $G$ is triangle-free if it contains no $K_3$.
Claim 1.
Every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contains an induced subdivision of every finite tree.
Proof.
Fix a finite tree $T$ and let $G$ be triangle-free with infinite chromatic number. By Scott's theorem [Scott97, Theorem 3], for $k=3$ there exists an integer $c(T)$ such that every finite graph $H$ with $\chi(H)\ge c(T)$ either contains $K_3$ or contains an induced subdivision of $T$.

Since $\chi(G)$ is infinite, the de Bruijn--Erdős compactness theorem gives a finite induced subgraph $G[S]$ with $\chi(G[S])\ge c(T)$. Because $G$ is triangle-free, so is $G[S]$; hence $G[S]$ cannot contain $K_3$. Therefore $G[S]$ contains an induced subdivision of $T$. Since $G[S]$ is induced, that subdivision is also induced in $G$.

So every finite tree appears in $G$ as an induced subdivision. $\square$

Claim 2.
Every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contains every finite subdivision of a star as an induced subgraph.
Proof.
Let $T$ be a subdivision of a star. Scott proved that $\operatorname{Forb}(T)$ is $\chi$-bounded [Scott97, Corollary 2]. Thus there exists a finite number $b(T)$ such that every triangle-free $T$-free graph has chromatic number at most $b(T)$.

Now let $G$ be triangle-free with infinite chromatic number. Choose a finite induced subgraph $G[S]$ with $\chi(G[S])>b(T)$. Then $G[S]$ cannot be $T$-free, so it contains an induced copy of $T$. Since $G[S]$ is induced, this copy is induced in $G$ as well. $\square$

Claim 3.
Every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contains every finite tree of radius at most $2$ as an induced subgraph.
Proof.
Let $T$ be a tree of radius at most $2$. Scott records the theorem of Gyárfás, Szemerédi, and Tuza that for every such $T$ there exists a constant $c(T)$ such that every triangle-free finite graph of chromatic number at least $c(T)$ contains an induced copy of $T$; he also records the stronger theorem of Kierstead and Penrice that $\operatorname{Forb}(T)$ is $\chi$-bounded for every radius-$2$ tree [Scott97, p. 2, Theorem B].

Applying the Round-1 compactness principle exactly as above, any triangle-free graph $G$ with infinite chromatic number has a finite induced subgraph of chromatic number at least $c(T)$, and hence contains an induced copy of $T$. $\square$

Claim 4.
Every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contains a path-induced copy of every finite rooted tree.
Proof.
Fix a finite rooted tree $(T,r)$ and let $G$ be triangle-free with infinite chromatic number. Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour proved that for every rooted tree $(T,r)$ and every integer $t\ge 1$, every graph with no clique of size $t$ and sufficiently large chromatic number admits a path-induced copy of $(T,r)$ [NguyenScottSeymour24, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 2.3].

Set $t=3$. Then there exists $p(T,r)$ such that every finite graph $H$ with no $K_3$ and $\chi(H)\ge p(T,r)$ admits a path-induced copy of $(T,r)$. Again choose a finite induced subgraph $G[S]$ with $\chi(G[S])\ge p(T,r)$. Since $G[S]$ is triangle-free, it contains a path-induced copy of $(T,r)$, and because $G[S]$ is induced, that same copy is path-induced in $G$. Thus every finite rooted tree occurs path-induced in $G$. $\square$

Sharper obstruction extracted from Claims 1--4.
Suppose the original Problem #738 were false. Then there would exist a finite tree $T$ and a triangle-free graph $G$ of infinite chromatic number such that $G$ contains no induced copy of $T$. Claims 2 and 3 show that such a tree $T$ cannot be a subdivision of a star and cannot have radius at most $2$. Therefore any minimal unresolved tree must have radius at least $3$ and at least two vertices of degree greater than $2$.

Moreover Claim 4 shows that $G$ still contains a path-induced copy of every rooted version of $T$, and Claim 1 shows that it contains an induced subdivision of $T$. So any future counterexample must exploit the difference between an induced copy of $T$ and the weaker structures "induced subdivision of $T$" and "path-induced copy of $(T,r)$".

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

  • Persistence from finite induced subgraphs. Every copy found inside $G[S]$ remains induced (or path-induced) in $G$ because $G[S]$ has exactly the edges of $G$ on $S$.
  • Triangle-free hypothesis. In Claims 1--4, the finite theorems are applied with clique bound $2$, equivalently with forbidden clique $K_3$. This matches the hypothesis exactly.
  • Why path-induced is genuinely weaker than induced. Consider the five-vertex tree with edges $ra, rb, ax, by$, rooted at $r$. Add one extra edge $xy$. The resulting graph is still triangle-free. The original tree appears as a path-induced rooted subgraph (both root-to-leaf paths $r$--$a$--$x$ and $r$--$b$--$y$ are induced), but it is not an induced subgraph because of the extra edge $xy$. So Claim 4 does not already settle the original problem.
  • Why induced subdivisions do not settle the original problem. An induced subdivision of $T$ may insert extra degree-$2$ vertices on edges of $T$; that need not give an induced copy of $T$ itself. Thus Claim 1 is strictly weaker than the target statement for general $T$.
  • Boundary of the new positive families. Claims 2 and 3 are incomparable families: radius-$2$ trees may have many branching vertices, whereas subdivisions of stars have at most one branching vertex. So together they strictly enlarge the known forced family.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)

Round 2 proves substantially more than Round 1:

  1. every finite tree appears as an induced subdivision;
  2. every finite subdivision of a star appears as an induced subgraph;
  3. every finite radius-$2$ tree appears as an induced subgraph;
  4. every finite rooted tree appears as a path-induced subgraph.

So the first genuinely open trees are those of radius at least $3$ with at least two branching vertices, and any counterexample must separate induced copies from induced subdivisions / path-induced copies. The full problem remains open [Bloom738].
