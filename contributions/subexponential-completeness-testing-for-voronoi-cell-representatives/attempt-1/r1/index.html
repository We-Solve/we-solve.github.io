Research attempt: Orbit-adjacency closure and an incomplete homology test

A conditional closure certificate.
Let a graph $X$ carry a group action by graph automorphisms. Assume the graph on vertex orbits is connected. Given a nonempty finite set $C$ of orbit representatives, suppose two additional procedures are available: an exhaustive finite list of neighbors of each representative, and an exact orbit-equivalence test. Then $C$ represents every orbit if and only if every neighbor of every member of $C$ is equivalent to a member of $C$.

Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, if an orbit outside $C$ exists, connect it to an orbit represented in $C$ by a finite path in the orbit graph. Its first edge leaving the represented set comes from some edge $(u,v)$ of $X$, with $u$ equivalent to a representative $c$. Choose a group element sending $u$ to $c$; it sends $v$ to a neighbor of $c$ in the outside orbit, contradicting the assumed exhaustive check. This proof does not require a bound on the number of outside orbits.

If $|C|=M$, the neighbor lists have length at most $D$, listing costs at most $T_N$ per representative, and one equivalence test costs at most $T_E$, the naive certificate costs at most \[ O(MT_N+M^2DT_E). \] Duplicate representatives do not invalidate the criterion, though they waste time. A canonical-form dictionary could improve the membership step, but its construction cost cannot be discarded. If the orbit graph is disconnected, the criterion fails: one whole component passes it while omitting another. In a Voronoi application, the vertices of $X$ would be the desired $d$-cells and the adjacency would need an actual connectedness theorem; an arbitrary face adjacency is not automatically suitable.

Why homological consistency is weaker than completeness.
Let $K$ be a triangular circle, with three vertices and three edges. Let $L$ be obtained by adjoining one new vertex $w$ and one edge joining $w$ to a vertex of $K$. Then $K$ is a proper subcomplex of $L$, but both have Euler characteristic zero and the same integral homology. To see this directly, in the chain complex of $L$ the boundary of the new edge is $w-v$. Replace the vertex basis vector $w$ by $w-v$. The new edge and this new vertex basis vector form a direct two-term summand with boundary multiplication by $1$, leaving the complex of $K$. That summand has zero homology. Thus even exact homology and Euler-characteristic agreement need not certify that every cell was listed. This finite example is an obstruction to a proposed criterion, not a claimed Voronoi counterexample.
The cost cannot be inferred from closure alone.
In a neighbor-query model, even connected graphs can hide a missing leaf at any unchecked listed vertex. On a base path with $M$ listed vertices, an algorithm declaring completeness without querying some vertex's entire neighbor list cannot distinguish the path from the same path with an unlisted leaf attached at that vertex. Therefore exact completeness checking on this family needs at least $M$ such queries in the worst case. This is consistent with polynomial time in an explicit list, but gives no subexponential bound in a different group-rank parameter when $M$ itself may be exponential.
