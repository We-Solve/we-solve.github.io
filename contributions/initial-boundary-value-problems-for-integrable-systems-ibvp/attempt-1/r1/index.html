initial boundary value problems for integrable systems (IBVP)

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
On $x\ge0$, initial data and Dirichlet boundary data for NLS leave $u_x(0,t)$ as dependent data in the Fokas representation. A Robin condition is $u_x(0,t)+qu(0,t)=0$. A classical solution is twice continuously differentiable in $x$ and once in $t$ in the interior. Matching conditions concern compatibility of the initial and boundary traces at $(0,0)$. Finite-gap solutions are the quasiperiodic solutions supplied by periodic integrable spectral theory.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Determine the long-time behavior of focusing NLS $iu_t=u_{xx}+2|u|^2u$ on $x\ge0$ with given $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and $u(0,t)=e^{iwt}$, $w\in\mathbb R$. In particular, explain excitation of finite-gap modes as the driving frequency varies, analogous to the source’s periodically driven Toda chain $\ddot x_k=e^{x_{k-1}-x_k}-e^{x_k-x_{k+1}}$, $x_k(0)=\dot x_k(0)=0$ for $k\ge1$, $x_0(t)=2at+h(\gamma t)$, with $h$ $2\pi$-periodic, $\gamma>0$ and $a<a_{\mathrm{crit}}$. Use the Bikbaev--Tarasov method for defocusing NLS $iu_t=u_{xx}-2|u|^2u$ with Robin boundary conditions, including the singular extension and long-time behavior. For general appropriate smooth nonlinearities, Robin data and smooth matching initial data, prove that the half-line solution is classical in the interior; more generally determine smoothness for dispersive initial-boundary problems in any dimension.

Exact boundary-driven standing waves.
Use precisely the source's focusing sign $iu_t=u_{xx}+2|u|^2u$. Seek $u(x,t)=e^{iwt}\phi(x)$, real $\phi$, with $\phi(0)=1$ and $\phi\to0$ at infinity. Then $\phi''+2\phi^3+w\phi=0$. For $\kappa>0$, direct differentiation gives \[\phi(x)=\kappa\operatorname{sech}(\kappa(x+x_0)),\qquad w=-\kappa^2. \] The boundary condition requires $\cosh(\kappa x_0)=\kappa$, so such profiles exist for $\kappa\ge1$. For $\kappa>1$ there are two choices $x_0=\pm\kappa^{-1}\operatorname{arcosh}\kappa$; their Neumann traces are $\phi'(0)=\mp\sqrt{\kappa^2-1}$. At $\kappa=1$ the two choices coincide and the trace is zero. Taking initial data equal to either profile gives an exact classical solution with the specified unit-amplitude driver.
What this tests.
The same boundary frequency can coexist with different initial profiles and different dependent traces. A rule selecting the Neumann trace from frequency alone is therefore false without a condition on the incoming initial state or the asymptotic regime. These waves have exponential spatial decay, not compact support, and are not asserted to attract arbitrary data or to describe finite-gap excitation.
