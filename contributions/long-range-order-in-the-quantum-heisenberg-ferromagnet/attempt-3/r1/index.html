Long-Range Order in the Quantum Heisenberg Ferromagnet

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: Finite-range three-dimensional Heisenberg ferromagnets are claimed to obey the exact $T^{3/2}$ spontaneous-magnetization deficit law, with the dispersion determinant fixing the coefficient; For every positive quantum spin in dimensions $d\geq3$, low-temperature nearest-neighbor Heisenberg ferromagnets are claimed to admit zero-field KMS states with magnetization at least $S/4$; For the nearest-neighbor model, magnetization deficit is claimed equal to ideal-magnon density up to $o(\beta^{-5/2})$, including the stated $\zeta(5/2)$ lattice correction; At low fixed temperature, symmetric zero-field magnetization is claimed to approach a uniformly oriented vector of deterministic positive magnitude equal to the right pressure derivative. Together they provide the following support within the scopes described below.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Result 1.
Fix $S\in\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}$. Let $J:\mathbb Z^3\to[0,\infty)$ have finite support, $J(0)=0$, $J(z)=J(-z)$, and support generating $\mathbb Z^3$. For the free-boundary Hamiltonian $H=-\tfrac12\sum_{x,y}J(y-x)\boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y$, let $m_{S,J}(\beta)$ be obtained by differentiating the limiting pressure from positive fields and evaluating at field zero. With \[ D_{S,J}=\frac S2\sum_zJ(z)zz^{\mathsf T}, \] the claimed low-temperature law is \[ \lim_{\beta\to\infty}\beta^{3/2}(S-m_{S,J}(\beta)) =\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{8\pi^{3/2}\sqrt{\det D_{S,J}}}. \] The thermodynamic limit precedes removal of the positive field.
Result 2.
For the coupling-one nearest-neighbor spin-$S$ quantum ferromagnet on $\mathbb Z^d$, with \[ H_\Lambda=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda,\ |x-y|_1=1}\boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y, \] the source claims that every integer $d\ge3$ and $S\in\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}$ admit a finite $\beta_0(d,S)>0$. At every finite $\beta\ge\beta_0$, the induced infinite-volume dynamics has a translation-invariant $\beta$-KMS state $\omega$ satisfying \[ \omega(S_0^z)\ge S/4. \] This chooses an ordered equilibrium state; it does not assign a nonzero one-site mean to the rotation-invariant state.
Result 3.
For the three-dimensional coupling-one nearest-neighbor spin-$S$ quantum ferromagnet, define spontaneous magnetization $m_S(\beta)$ by the thermodynamic pressure's right field derivative at zero. Put \[ \varepsilon_S(k)=2S\sum_{j=1}^3(1-\cos k_j),\qquad n_S^{\mathrm{sw}}(\beta)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3} \frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3(e^{\beta\varepsilon_S(k)}-1)}. \] For each fixed $S\in\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}$, the assertion is \[ \lim_{\beta\to\infty}(\beta S)^{5/2} [S-m_S(\beta)-n_S^{\mathrm{sw}}(\beta)]=0. \] Thus the full lattice spin-wave integral captures the magnetization deficit through the stated scale.
Result 4.
On the even-side torus $\Lambda_L=(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^3$, set $V=L^3$, $H_{S,L}=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\text{ nearest neighbors}}\boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y$, and $M_L^a=\sum_xS_x^a$. For each fixed $S\in\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}$, the claim provides finite $\beta_0(S)$ so that any finite $\beta\ge\beta_0$ has positive spontaneous magnetization $m=p'_{S,\beta}(0+)$. With zero-field Gibbs expectation, \[ \lim_{L\to\infty,\ L\text{ even}} \left\langle\exp\!\left(V^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^3t_aM_L^a\right)\right\rangle_{\beta,L} =\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}e^{m t\cdot n}\,d\sigma(n) =\frac{\sinh(m|t|)}{m|t|}\qquad(t\in\mathbb R^3). \] The value at $t=0$ is one. The same limit gives $V^{-2}\sum_{x,y}\langle\boldsymbol S_x\cdot\boldsymbol S_y\rangle\to m^2$.

Outline of the argument

Pinned exchange returns are compared with the one-magnon walk. Uniform sparse-pin and long-return remainder bounds isolate finite returns, whose small-momentum asymptotics produce the Bloch coefficient; volume, zero-field derivative, and temperature limits are taken in that order. Spin slots and exchange loops translate a simultaneous pin test into return-path estimates. Sparse obstacles are bypassed by local diffusion flows, and heat-flow leakage bounds close a pinning bootstrap, producing a magnetized thermodynamic equilibrium state.

Capacity comparison controls pinned return cycles on a period. Sparse-obstacle propagation and an ideal-potential profile identify comparison sources whose cancellation improves the error beyond the leading Bloch term, allowing the next dispersion coefficient to survive. Exchange-cycle pins and zero-free traces control torus comparisons at vanishing field. Removing the field across spatial scales and suppressing replica cross terms identifies the moment law; pressure tangents determine its radius without assigning joint measurements to noncommuting components.
