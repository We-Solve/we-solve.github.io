A spectral-geometry test.
Passing from an additive coordinate $u$ to $z=e^u$ changes the logarithmic differential by $dz/z=du$. The simple meromorphic kernel \[ K(u)=\frac1{e^u-1}+\frac12=\frac12\coth(u/2) \] is $2\pi i$-periodic and has expansion $K(u)=u^{-1}+O(u)$ near zero. This supplies a concrete way rational local pole behavior can coexist with trigonometric global dependence after changing the underlying spectral geometry.
An elliptic obstruction.
A doubly periodic meromorphic scalar function cannot have just one simple pole with nonzero residue in a period cell. Integrate it around a translated fundamental parallelogram avoiding poles. Opposite-edge integrals cancel by periodicity, whereas a small contour around the sole pole gives $2\pi i$ times its residue. The residue must therefore vanish, a contradiction. A naive elliptic replacement of the one-pole scalar kernel must introduce compensating poles, nontrivial quasi-periodicity or bundle-valued structure.
