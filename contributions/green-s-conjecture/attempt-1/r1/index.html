Write-up

Green’s exact vanishing threshold in genera three and four

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: Every smooth connected complex projective nonhyperelliptic curve of genus 3 or 4 satisfies the literal Green equivalence; its entire linear strand is computed from a hypersurface or (2,3) complete-intersection resolution.

Let C be such a curve. The canonical map is a closed embedding. One direct reason is Riemann–Roch: for any length-two effective divisor Z (including a double point), failure of canonical sections to separate Z is equivalent to h^0(O_C(Z))≥2. This would give a degree-two map to P¹ after removing base points, contrary to nonhyperellipticity and genus at least three. Similarly h^0(O_C(p))=1 excludes canonical base points. Thus the canonical image is smooth, nondegenerate, and has degree 2g−2.

For g=3 it is a plane curve of degree four. Its homogeneous ideal is generated by one quartic F, and its coordinate ring R has graded free resolution
\[
0\longrightarrow S(-4)\xrightarrow{F}S\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow0,
\qquad S=\mathbb C[x_0,x_1,x_2].
\]
The coordinate ring is the canonical section ring: from the hypersurface ideal sequence, the vanishing of intermediate cohomology H¹(P²,O(n−4)) identifies all restriction maps of sections as surjective, for n≥0. There is no resolution summand S(−p−1) at homological degree p≥1. Therefore K_{p,1}=0 for all p≥1. The prompt fixes Cliff(C)=1 in genus three, so its threshold g−Cliff−1=1 agrees exactly.

For g=4 the embedding lies in P³ and has degree six. There is a nonzero quadratic equation Q because dim Sym²H^0(K)=10 while h^0(2K)=9. The space of cubic equations has dimension at least 20−h^0(3K)=5. Only four dimensions consist of linear multiples of Q, so choose a cubic F not divisible by Q. The quadric Q is irreducible: otherwise its support is one or two planes, and the integral curve would lie in a plane, contradicting nondegeneracy. Thus Q,F form a regular sequence. Their complete intersection has degree six, contains C and has no embedded components; its generic multiplicity along C is one and there is no degree left for another component. Hence it is C scheme-theoretically. Equivalently an unmixed complete-intersection ideal with this single reduced generic component equals the prime ideal of C.

The Koszul resolution is
\[
0\longrightarrow S(-5)\longrightarrow S(-2)\oplus S(-3)
\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow0.
\]
The intermediate cohomology of line bundles on P³ vanishes, so its ideal sequence again identifies R with the full canonical section ring. Tensoring the polynomial Koszul resolution of C=S/(x_0,…,x_{g−1}) with R shows that its degree-(p+1) middle homology is exactly the three-term complex K_{p,1} in the literal prompt. Thus the displayed minimal graded free resolutions compute those groups: dim K_{1,1}=1 and K_{p,1}=0 for every p≥2.

For completeness Cliff(C)=1 in this genus. The quadric is smooth or a cone; C avoids a cone vertex, since at that vertex the Jacobian of Q,F could not have rank two for a smooth complete intersection. Either ruling therefore cuts out a degree-three pencil on C, so Cliff(C)≤1. Nonhyperellipticity gives the reverse inequality in this small genus without needing a generic-curve assertion. If L and K−L each have at least two sections, each has degree at least three, since degree two would give a hyperelliptic pencil. Their degrees add to six, so both have degree three. Such an L has at most two sections: a base-point-free three-dimensional subsystem would map C into P² with image of degree at most three; a conic has degree two and cannot have total degree three, while a cubic would be birational and have normalization of genus at most one. Base points would leave a degree-at-most-two pencil, already excluded. Thus deg L−2(h^0(L)−1)=3−2=1. The ruling supplies an L for which this occurs.

Consequently the genus-four threshold is 4−1−1=2, exactly the computed vanishing range. The elementary line-bundle cohomology, Riemann–Roch, degree formula for a complete intersection and the polynomial Koszul resolution are the standard inputs used in this explicit low-genus computation. Nothing here proves the equivalence for any genus at least five or infers it from a finite list of Betti tables.
