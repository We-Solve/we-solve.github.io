Identifying equilibrium-selection mixtures by repeated sessions

Problem ID: C72-489.

A finite, experimentally interpretable restriction

The general target is the identified set of selection kernels under a specified observation kernel, including common predictions in new games or contexts. We analyze a finite equilibrium dictionary and an experiment that repeatedly observes the same selected equilibrium. We derive a sharp fiber description, a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for identified predictions, an explicit repetition length guaranteeing identification, and simultaneous finite-sample uncertainty. We also show why a finite observation window cannot identify an unrestricted continuum mixture.

For each observed game and context, suppose a known list of $K$ candidate equilibria is specified. The list may be the entire equilibrium set when finite, or a maintained restriction. A session selects index $k$ with probability $q_k$, then produces an observation in a finite alphabet $\mathcal Z$ according to the known column vector $h_k$. Write $H=(h_1,\ldots,h_K)$ and $q\in\Delta_K$. The population observation law is $p=Hq$. These are maintained sampling and support assumptions, not implications of Nash existence. Historical selection proposals and noisy-information selection theorems motivate restrictions but do not identify this $q$ without observation assumptions [hs,cvd].

Sharp fibers and identified predictions

Theorem: For the unrestricted finite-mixture family $q\in\Delta_K$, the sharp identified set is the polytope \[ Q(p)=\{q\geq0:Hq=p\}. \] Normalization is automatic because the columns of $H$ and $p$ sum to one. If $Q(p)$ is nonempty, let $S=\{k:\text{some }q\in Q(p)\text{ has }q_k>0\}$. For a given vector $\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}^K$, the target $\ell^\top q$ is identified at $p$ if and only if \[ \ell_S\in\operatorname{rowspan}(H_S). \] More generally, its sharp lower and upper bounds are the minimum and maximum of $\ell^\top q$ on $Q(p)$.
Proof: Every generating mixture must satisfy $Hq=p$, and every nonnegative solution is a valid selection distribution producing $p$. This proves sharpness. The set is a closed subset of the compact simplex, so the extrema exist.

If $\ell_S=b^\top H_S$ for some $b$, then every feasible $q$ vanishes outside $S$, and $\ell^\top q=b^\top p$ is constant. Conversely, if $\ell_S$ is not in the row span, elementary finite-dimensional orthogonality gives $d\in\ker H_S$ with $\ell_S^\top d\ne0$. For each $k\in S$, choose a feasible mixture positive at $k$ and average these finitely many mixtures. The resulting $q^\circ$ is strictly positive on $S$. Since columns of $H_S$ sum to one, $H_Sd=0$ implies $\sum_{k\in S}d_k=0$. Hence for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, both $q^\circ\pm\epsilon d$, extended by zero off $S$, are feasible mixtures with different target values. This contradicts identification and proves necessity. ∎

For a held-out outcome with a maintained common-mixture rule and known prediction vector $\ell$, Theorem Theorem applies directly. The common-mixture restriction across contexts must be stated. Without it, observations in one context put no restrictions on another context's freely varying selector.

Corollary (Observationally equivalent mixtures can predict identically): Failure to identify $q$ does not preclude identifying $\ell^\top q$. If $H$ has dependent columns, any $\ell$ in its row space is still identified at every compatible population law. Conversely, an interior compatible mixture admits unidentified predictions for every $\ell$ outside that row space.
Proof: Apply Theorem Theorem; in the interior case $S=\{1,\ldots,K\}$. The first assertion follows from the same row-space representation without an interior assumption. ∎

Repeated observations of one latent equilibrium

Suppose each session retains its selected equilibrium and yields $L$ conditionally independent draws from $h_k$. Independence is conditional on the equilibrium; unconditional observations within a session are generally dependent. Let $H^{(L)}$ have columns $h_k^{\otimes L}$.
Theorem (A finite repetition design): If $h_1,\ldots,h_K$ are pairwise distinct, then $H^{(L)}$ has full column rank for every $L\geq K-1$. Thus $K-1$ repeated observations per session identify all mixture weights. If two columns are equal, no number of such repetitions distinguishes the weights within that equal-column class.
Proof: There exists a vector $f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|\mathcal Z|}$ such that the scalars $\theta_k=f^\top h_k$ are all distinct: each equality $f^\top(h_k-h_j)=0$ is a proper hyperplane, and a finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot fill the vector space. For completeness, take $f=(1,t,\ldots,t^{d-1})$ for $d=|\mathcal Z|$. Each pairwise difference becomes a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $d-1$. A finite set of their roots can be avoided by choosing an integer $t$.

From a session of length $L$, for each $r=0,\ldots,K-1$ observe the statistic $\prod_{s=1}^r f(Z_s)$, with empty product one. Its expectation conditional on $k$ is $\theta_k^r$. Thus a linear relation $\sum_k d_k h_k^{\otimes L}=0$ implies $\sum_k d_k\theta_k^r=0$ for all $r=0,\ldots,K-1$. The Vandermonde matrix is nonsingular. One direct proof uses interpolation polynomials $P_j(t)=\prod_{k\ne j}(t-\theta_k)/(\theta_j-\theta_k)$: taking the corresponding linear combination of the moment equations gives $d_j=0$. Hence all $d_k=0$, proving full column rank. Additional observations cannot reduce rank because marginalizing them recovers the length-$K-1$ law. Equal columns remain equal under every tensor power, proving the last assertion. ∎

The theorem is about a shared latent equilibrium within a session. If the equilibrium is redrawn independently at every date, the session law becomes $(Hq)^{\otimes L}$ and repetition adds no population identification beyond $Hq$. This difference is experimentally substantive.

A lower boundary for unrestricted equilibrium mixtures

Proposition: For every finite $L$, there is a finite game and two different selection distributions on its Nash equilibria that induce identical length-$L$ session laws under conditional independent play but different length-$(L+1)$ laws.
Proof: Use a two-player game where player 1 has two actions, player 2 has one, and every payoff is zero. Every Bernoulli mixed action $\theta\in[0,1]$ of player 1 is a Nash equilibrium. Select $L+2$ distinct numbers $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{L+2}$ in $(0,1)$. The $(L+1)\times(L+2)$ matrix with entries $\theta_k^r$, $r=0,\ldots,L$, has a nonzero null vector $d$. Since its first row is all ones, $\sum_kd_k=0$, so $d$ has both positive and negative components. Let $s=\sum_{d_k>0}d_k=-\sum_{d_k<0}d_k>0$, and form two probability distributions with masses $d_k^+/s$ and $d_k^-/s$ on these equilibria.

They have identical moments through degree $L$. The probability of every binary sequence of length $L$ is a polynomial $\theta^r(1-\theta)^{L-r}$ of degree at most $L$, so the induced session laws coincide. Their degree-$(L+1)$ moments differ: otherwise the square Vandermonde matrix for degrees $0,\ldots,L+1$ would annihilate $d$, contradicting nonsingularity. In particular the probability of $L+1$ successive ones differs. This proves the assertion with a valid game and a fully specified observation kernel. ∎

This establishes a precise finite-window limitation, rather than claiming that no selection rule can be learned. A finite dictionary plus persistent sessions supplies exactly the additional restriction used in Theorem Theorem (A finite repetition design).

Uncertainty that includes estimating the selector

Observe $B$ independent sessions under a fixed known finite observation matrix $H$ (possibly $H^{(L)}$). Let $d$ be its number of rows and $\widehat p$ the empirical observation law. For $0<\alpha<1$ set \[ r_B=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/\alpha)}{2B}},\qquad \widehat Q=\{q\in\Delta_K:\|Hq-\widehat p\|_\infty\leq r_B\}. \]
Theorem: With probability at least $1-\alpha$, $\widehat Q$ contains the entire population fiber $Q(p)$. Consequently the minimum and maximum of any fixed $\ell^\top q$ over $\widehat Q$ cover its entire sharp identified interval. If $H$ has full column rank and $L_HH=I_K$ is a chosen left inverse, then \[ \operatorname{diam}_{\infty}(\widehat Q) \leq2\|L_H\|_{\infty\to\infty}r_B \] whenever $\widehat Q$ is nonempty.
Proof: For each outcome cell the $B$ session indicators are independent Bernoulli variables. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give $\Pr(\|\widehat p-p\|_\infty>r_B)\leq2d e^{-2Br_B^2}=\alpha$. On the complementary event every $q$ with $Hq=p$ belongs to $\widehat Q$, proving coverage and the interval assertion. For any $q,q'\in\widehat Q$, the triangle inequality gives $\|H(q-q')\|_\infty\leq2r_B$. Multiplication by the left inverse proves the diameter bound. ∎

Poorly separated observation columns can make the inverse norm large even when population identification holds. This exposes an experimental precision issue: choosing sufficient repetition length does not itself guarantee useful sample size. An empty estimated set is a model-fit warning; the theorem bounds its probability under the maintained exact finite-mixture model. Held-out predictions using $\ell$ inherit the mixture confidence region rather than treating fitted weights as known.

Source check and unresolved scope

The Tilburg primary record for Carlsson--van Damme (1993) was inspected on 9 October 2026 and verified title, authors, journal, volume and pages; its linked full-text request failed. It was therefore a bibliographic check, not a check of that paper's perturbation theorem. Harsanyi--Selten is retained as a catalogue reference to a theoretical construction, not a newly verified empirical law. The results above have complete finite-dimensional proofs and do not invoke those theories as identification assumptions.

The unrestricted kernel family across games, equilibrium dictionaries that are unknown or continuous, departures from equilibrium support, and causal effects of randomized context changes remain outside the proved class. The finite model supplies a concrete experimental restriction and sharp prediction tests, rather than a universal behavioral selector.
