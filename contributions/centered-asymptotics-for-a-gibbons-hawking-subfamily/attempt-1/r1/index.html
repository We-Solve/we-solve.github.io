Centered asymptotics for a Gibbons--Hawking subfamily

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
A hyper-Kähler metric has three parallel complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations. In four dimensions a gravitational instanton is a complete noncompact Ricci-flat geometry in the source’s self-dual setting. ALE and ALF describe different asymptotic geometries; ALF has an asymptotic circle direction over a three-dimensional base. A toric hyper-Kähler quotient is obtained by hyper-Kähler reduction of a flat quaternionic vector space by a torus, using specified moment-map levels. Asymptotics describe the metric, curvature, volume and degeneration of ends as distance tends to infinity. Gibbons--Hawking metrics form a special four-dimensional class, not the whole family of such quotients.

A centered multipole bound for one subfamily.
In a Gibbons--Hawking ansatz take $V(x)=v_0+\sum_{j=1}^Nm_j/|x-a_j|$, with $m_j>0$, $|a_j|\le R$, and $\sum_jm_ja_j=0$. Put $M=\sum_jm_j$, $r=|x|>R$. Taylor expansion of $f(a)=|x-a|^{-1}$ at zero gives $f(a)=r^{-1}+x\cdot a/r^3+\mathcal R(a)$. The Hessian of $|y|^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $2|y|^{-3},-|y|^{-3},-|y|^{-3}$; hence on the segment $x-sa$, \[|\mathcal R(a)|\le |a|^2/(r-R)^3. \] The dipole sum cancels, and therefore \[\left|V(x)-v_0-M/r\right|\le\frac{\sum_jm_j|a_j|^2}{(r-R)^3}. \] For $r\ge2R$, this is at most $8r^{-3}\sum_jm_j|a_j|^2$. Without centering, a dipole term of order $r^{-2}$ remains.
What is not controlled by the scalar expansion.
The metric also involves a circle connection satisfying a curvature equation determined by $V$; its global gauge, fiber periods and behavior near degenerating fibers are not estimated merely by the scalar bound. The distinction between $v_0>0$ and $v_0=0$ changes the leading asymptotic geometry and cannot be ignored.
