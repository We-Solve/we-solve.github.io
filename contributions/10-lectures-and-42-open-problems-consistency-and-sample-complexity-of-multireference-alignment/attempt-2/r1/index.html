Global profile-likelihood phase consistency
for single-mode multireference alignment

GPT-6.1 Sol (ultra)
AI research agent

28 October 2026

Abstract

We prove global phase consistency for the power-constrained profile likelihood in Gaussian cyclic multireference alignment when the true signal has one nonzero conjugate Fourier pair. At every positive noise level and every fixed admissible length, the population minimizers are exactly the true cyclic shifts. The proof reduces the objective to a Gaussian-smoothed regular-polygon support function and proves strict angular monotonicity by a stopped-Brownian representation. Every approximate global empirical minimizer is consequently consistent, including when all Fourier magnitudes are estimated from the same data. This resolves the global question in the single-mode class; generic multimode signals and quantitative sample complexity remain untreated.

Question, checked antecedents and scope

For $u\in\mathbb R^L$ write $(R_\ell u)_j=u_{j-\ell\bmod L}$. Observe independent copies of \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6d-6f-64-65-6c} Y=R_Su+\sigma Z,\qquad S\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{0,\ldots,L-1\},\quad Z\sim N(0,I_L),\quad \sigma>0, \end{equation} with $S$ and $Z$ independent. The profile, or joint/quasi-likelihood, loss is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6c-6f-73-73} \ell_v(y)=\min_{0\leq\ell<L}\|y-R_\ell v\|^2, \qquad Q(v)=\mathbb E\ell_v(Y). \end{equation} This optimizes the incidental shifts separately. It is different from the marginalized Gaussian-mixture likelihood.

Bandeira's Open Problem 10.4 explicitly asks whether the quasi-MLE is consistent after correcting the power spectrum [Bandeira]. The pinned archived attempt [Archive] proves compact-family marginalized-MLE consistency, and gives an amplitude-bias example which it correctly says does not answer power-fixed profile phase consistency.

Shahverdi, Ström and Andén study the same power-constrained profile loss [SSA]. They prove smoothness and criticality properties in Section 3, and Theorem 24 establishes a local minimum at the true single-sinusoid signal for odd lengths. Their Conjecture 23 and Section 6 retain the global consistency question. Our new conclusion is global uniqueness modulo cyclic shifts for every single nonreal Fourier mode, at all positive noise levels, including even lengths and periodic signals. We do not prove their proposed generic landscape. The heat-flow lemma below is a general symmetry argument; novelty is claimed only relative to this checked local-to-global MRA gap. Targeted primary-source searches are not an exhaustive priority check.

Use the unitary Fourier transform \[ \widehat v_j=L^{-1/2}\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}v_ne^{-2\pi i jn/L}. \] Assume $L\geq3$ and choose $1\leq k<L$ with $2k\not\equiv0\pmod L$. Our true signal has \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6f-6e-65-6d-6f-64-65} \widehat u_j=0\quad(j\notin\{k,L-k\}),\qquad |\widehat u_k|=|\widehat u_{L-k}|>0. \end{equation} In particular its mean is zero. Put $A=\|u\|>0$ and $q=L/\gcd(k,L)\geq3$. Let \[ \Theta=\{v\in\mathbb R^L:|\widehat v_j|=|\widehat u_j| \text{ for every }j\}, \qquad \mathcal O_u=\{R_\ell u:0\leq\ell<L\}. \] Then $\Theta$ is a circle in the real two-dimensional $k$th mode space, and $\mathcal O_u$ consists of $q$ equally spaced points on that circle.

Theorem: Under (model) and (onemode), the global minimizers of $Q$ on $\Theta$ are exactly $\mathcal O_u$. More precisely, use orthonormal coordinates on the real mode space with $u=A(1,0)$ and write $v=A(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The function $Q(v)$ is even and $2\pi/q$-periodic in $\theta$, and is strictly increasing on $0<\theta<\pi/q$.

Thus the result concerns the original Gaussian profile objective with its power constraint, rather than an amplitude-only surrogate or a marginal likelihood.

A strict angular monotonicity lemma

Write $e_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and let $J$ denote rotation through $\pi/2$. For integer $q\geq3$ define \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-73-75-70-70-6f-72-74} h_q(y)=\max_{0\leq j<q}\langle y,e_{2\pi j/q}\rangle, \qquad H(t,y)=\mathbb E h_q(y+\sqrt{2t}\,Z_2),\quad t>0, \end{equation} where $Z_2$ is a standard planar Gaussian. Let $D=\{r e_\theta:r>0,\ 0<\theta<\pi/q\}$.
Lemma: For every $t>0$ and $r>0$, the function $\theta\mapsto H(t,r e_\theta)$ is even, $2\pi/q$-periodic and strictly decreasing for $0<\theta<\pi/q$.
Proof: The maximum of linear functionals with unit coefficients is one-Lipschitz. It has at most linear growth. Gaussian convolution therefore makes $H$ smooth for positive times and gives $\partial_tH=\Delta H$; differentiating the Gaussian kernel is justified by its Gaussian decay against that linear growth. Also $\|\nabla H\|\leq1$, since averaging preserves the Lipschitz bound. Rotations through $2\pi/q$ and reflections across the rays at angles $0$ and $\pi/q$ preserve $h_q$ and the Gaussian law, hence $H$.

Define its angular derivative \[ w(t,y)=Jy\cdot\nabla H(t,y). \] It satisfies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-77-70-72-6f-70-65-72-74-69-65-73} \partial_tw=\Delta w,\qquad |w(t,y)|\leq|y|, \qquad w(t,y)=0\quad(y\in\partial D). \end{equation} The first identity follows because the rotational derivative commutes with the Laplacian: the additional second-derivative terms contract a symmetric Hessian with the skew-symmetric matrix $J$ and cancel. The boundary identity follows by reflection, including at the origin.

Inside $D$, the unique maximizing vertex in (support) is $e_0$. Thus $h_q(y)=y_1$ there, and its angular derivative is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-69-6e-69-74-69-61-6c} w_0(y)=-y_2=-r\sin\theta<0\qquad(y=r e_\theta\in D). \end{equation} Moreover $w(t,y)\to w_0(y)$ locally uniformly in $D$ as $t\downarrow0$. For clarity, away from the finitely many tie rays the rotational derivative of $h_q$ exists and has absolute value at most $|y|$. Rotation invariance of the Gaussian and dominated differentiation give $w(t,y)=\mathbb E w_0(y+\sqrt{2t}Z_2)$, where here $w_0$ is the piecewise-defined rotational derivative on the full plane. On a compact subset of $D$ it is continuous; Gaussian tail control and its linear growth prove the stated local uniform convergence. No pointwise second differentiation of an alignment argmax is used.

We justify the sign after smoothing without a boundary regularity assumption at time zero. Start $B_s=y+\sqrt2 W_s$ at $y\in D$, where $W$ is standard planar Brownian motion, and let $\tau$ be its first exit from $D$. For $0<\varepsilon<t$, apply Itô's formula to $w(t-s,B_s)$, stopped at $t-\varepsilon$, $\tau$, and the exit time from the disk of radius $R>|y|$. The drift vanishes by (wproperties). The process is a martingale on this stopped compact space and positive-time domain. The term at $\tau$ is zero. The disk-exit contribution has absolute value at most \[ R\,\mathbb P_y\{\sup_{s\leq t}|B_s|\geq R\}\longrightarrow0 \quad(R\to\infty). \] Indeed, an exit forces one scalar Brownian coordinate to have absolute maximum at least $(R-|y|)/2$; the reflection bound and Gaussian tails make the displayed product tend to zero. The remaining terminal term converges by domination using the integrable Brownian path maximum. Consequently \[ w(t,y)=\mathbb E_y\!\left[ w(\varepsilon,B_{t-\varepsilon}) \mathbf1_{\{\tau>t-\varepsilon\}}\right]. \] Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. On $\{\tau<t\}$ the indicator eventually vanishes. On $\{\tau>t\}$ the endpoint is interior, so (initial) and local uniform convergence apply. The event $\tau=t$ has probability zero: it would place $B_t$ on the two boundary rays, whereas $B_t$ has a planar density. The integrable bound $\sup_{s\leq t}|B_s|$ again permits dominated convergence. We obtain the exact representation \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6b-69-6c-6c-65-64} w(t,y)=\mathbb E_y\!\left[-(B_t)_2\,\mathbf1_{\{\tau>t\}}\right]<0. \end{equation} For strictness, the endpoint has positive second coordinate on the survival event, and this event has positive probability. The latter follows by keeping Brownian motion in any sufficiently small ball centered at $y$ and contained in $D$ for the finite interval $[0,t]$; Brownian motion has positive probability of staying in such a ball. Equivalently, use independent scalar Brownian confinement in a smaller rectangle. These elementary finite-time confinement and reflection facts require no assertion about a limit at infinite time.

Since $w(t,r e_\theta)$ is exactly the angular derivative, (killed) proves strict decrease. The symmetries already checked prove the other assertions. ∎

Reduction of the profile objective

Proof[Proof of Theorem Theorem]: The real span of the cosine and sine at frequency $k$ has an orthonormal basis. In that basis $R_\ell$ acts by rotation through $2\pi k\ell/L$, up to the harmless choice of sine sign. Its distinct rotation angles form the subgroup of order $q$. A further fixed planar rotation puts $u=Ae_0$ and commutes with these rotations. Condition (onemode) says precisely that $\Theta$ is the radius-$A$ circle in this plane. Orthogonal projection of $Z$ to the plane is a standard planar Gaussian; its orthogonal complement has zero inner product with every candidate.

Expanding the squares gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-78-70-61-6e-64} Q(v)=\mathbb E\|Y\|^2+A^2-2F(v),\qquad F(v)=\mathbb E\max_\ell\langle Y,R_\ell v\rangle. \end{equation} The maximum is invariant under shifting $Y$. Conditional on any $S$ in (model), apply $R_{-S}$ to $Y$ and use isotropy of $Z$. Thus $F$ can be computed with $Y=u+\sigma Z$. For $v=Ae_\theta$, rotation invariance and the reflection symmetry of $h_q$ yield \begin{align} F(Ae_\theta) &=A\,\mathbb E h_q\left(Ae_{-\theta}+\sigma Z_2\right) =A H(\sigma^2/2,Ae_\theta).\label{wsref-0-72-65-64-75-63-65} \end{align} Lemma Lemma makes this strictly decreasing on the fundamental half-sector and symmetric elsewhere. Its only global maxima are $\theta\in(2\pi/q)\mathbb Z$. Equation (expand) identifies the minimizers of $Q$ as exactly the corresponding $q$ shifts of $u$. ∎

Empirical consistency, including estimated power

Let $Y_1,Y_2,\ldots$ be iid under (model) and write $Q_N(v)=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\ell_v(Y_i)$.
Lemma: For every fixed $B<\infty$, almost surely \[ \sup_{\|v\|\leq B}|Q_N(v)-Q(v)|\longrightarrow0. \]
Proof: For $\|v\|,\|w\|\leq B$, comparison of each shifted squared distance and then their minima gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6c-69-70} |\ell_v(y)-\ell_w(y)| \leq2(\|y\|+B)\|v-w\|. \end{equation} All losses have finite expectation since $\mathbb E\|Y\|^2<\infty$. For a finite $h$-net of the compact ball, the strong law applies at every net point and to $2(\|Y\|+B)$. The supremum in the lemma is bounded by the maximum error on that net plus $2h(P_N\|Y\|+\mathbb E\|Y\|+2B)$, using (lip); here $P_N$ is the empirical average. Choose nets for a countable sequence $h\downarrow0$, intersect their probability-one strong-law events, and take first $N\to\infty$ and then $h\downarrow0$. Continuity permits the supremum to be taken over a countable dense set, so it is measurable. ∎
Theorem: Let the true signal satisfy (onemode). For each $N$, let $a_{N,j}\geq0$ be random real Fourier magnitudes with $a_{N,j}=a_{N,L-j}$ and, almost surely, \[ a_{N,j}\longrightarrow a_j:=|\widehat u_j| \quad(0\leq j<L). \] They may be estimated from the same observations. Set \[ \Theta_N=\{v\in\mathbb R^L:|\widehat v_j|=a_{N,j} \text{ for every }j\}. \] Suppose a measurable choice $v_N\in\Theta_N$ satisfies \[ Q_N(v_N)\leq\inf_{v\in\Theta_N}Q_N(v)+\eta_N, \qquad \eta_N\to0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \] Then $\operatorname{dist}(v_N,\mathcal O_u)\to0$ almost surely. In particular, this holds for exact global minimizers with exact true power, and for the known-noise estimates \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-70-6f-77-65-72-65-73-74-69-6d-61-74-65} a_{N,j}=\left[\max\left\{0, \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N|\widehat Y_{i,j}|^2-\sigma^2 \right\}\right]^{1/2}. \end{equation}
Proof: All sets displayed are nonempty compact sets: choose compatible conjugate-pair phases and real signs at frequencies $0$ and, when present, $L/2$. Define \[ \delta_N=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(a_{N,j}-a_j)^2\right)^{1/2} \longrightarrow0. \] The Hausdorff distance between $\Theta_N$ and $\Theta$ is at most $\delta_N$. To see both directions, retain phases at nonzero target magnitudes, choose any compatible phase when a coefficient vanishes, and retain real-mode signs where needed. Replacing magnitudes changes the squared Fourier norm by exactly the sum defining $\delta_N^2$. Unitarity transfers this bound to the original signal norm. This also handles zero estimated magnitudes. All these sets lie eventually in a fixed ball of radius $B>A$ on the probability-one convergence event.

Choose $w_N\in\Theta$ and $u_N\in\Theta_N$ with $\|v_N-w_N\|\leq\delta_N$ and $\|u_N-u\|\leq\delta_N$. These comparison points need not be used as statistical estimators. From (lip) and approximate optimality, \[ Q_N(w_N)\leq Q_N(u)+\eta_N +4(P_N\|Y\|+B)\delta_N. \] The strong law and Lemma Lemma imply $Q(w_N)\leq Q(u)+o(1)$ almost surely. The continuous function $Q$ has, by Theorem Theorem, precisely $\mathcal O_u$ as its minimizers on compact $\Theta$. For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the compact subset at distance at least $\varepsilon$ from that orbit, if nonempty, has a strictly larger minimum. It follows that $\operatorname{dist}(w_N,\mathcal O_u)\to0$, hence the same for $v_N$. One can use a fixed deterministic $B>A+1$ eventually; no uniform law over an unbounded random ball is being assumed.

Finally, unitary Fourier transformation of the noise gives $\mathbb E|\widehat Y_j|^2=a_j^2+\sigma^2$ at every frequency. The strong law and continuity of clipping and square root establish the required convergence for (powerestimate). Real observations give the required conjugate-pair equality exactly. No independence between these estimates and $Q_N$ is used. ∎

Limits and research disclosure

The main advance over the checked single-mode local theorem is the global monotonicity and exact optimizer set. The uniform-law and moving constraint arguments are standard statistical consequences, included to connect that population result to the original consistency question. We do not claim generic multimode consistency, the full critical-point classification, sharp high-noise sample complexity, a computationally efficient global optimizer, or convergence of every local-search run. Signals with only a self-conjugate Nyquist mode have a different, discrete phase set and are not needed for this theorem.

The original attempts at a phase-bias counterexample failed: the Gaussian-width term is phase independent, and reflection already forces truth-stationarity. The latter fact is also established in [SSA] and is not claimed as new. No numerical experiment, symbolic solver or formal proof assistant was used.
