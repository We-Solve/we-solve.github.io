Full finite-level Ramanujan-Arthur decomposition

OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 014; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that cuspidal functions for split semisimple groups over function fields decompose by dual nilpotent orbits at every full finite level; for adjoint absolutely simple groups, one generic unramified component forces temperedness at every unramified place.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Let $F=\mathbb F_q(X)$ for a smooth projective geometrically connected curve, $G/\mathbb F_q$ split connected semisimple, and $D$ any effective divisor. Write $K_D=\ker(G(\mathcal O_{\mathbb A})\to G(\mathcal O_D))$ and $C_{D,\mathbb Q}$ for compactly supported rational cuspidal functions on $G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)/K_D$. For each dual-group nilpotent orbit $\mathcal O$, choose its Jacobson--Morozov $\phi_{\mathcal O}:\operatorname{SL}_2\to\widehat G$. Let $C_{D,\mathcal O,\mathbb Q}$ consist of functions whose spherical Hecke spectra, at every good point $x$, have semisimple class \[ [\phi_{\mathcal O}(\operatorname{diag}(q_x^{1/2},q_x^{-1/2}))c], \] where $c$ centralizes $\phi_{\mathcal O}$ and is algebraic and compact under every complex embedding. The same orbit is required at every good point. The claimed rational and $\ell$-adic decompositions are \[ C_{D,\mathbb Q}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal O} C_{D,\mathcal O,\mathbb Q},\qquad C_{D,\ell}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal O} C_{D,\mathcal O,\ell}, \] with $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q$ and scalar extensions from $\mathbb Q$. For split adjoint absolutely simple $G/F$, a cuspidal representation generic and unramified at one place is further claimed tempered at every unramified place. Ramified temperedness and a full multiplicity formula are not asserted here.

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 014; 2026-09-24), iwahori translation eigencharacters are analyzed before taking the Weyl quotient. Frobenius traces of central kernels and Springer tests bound the extreme Bernstein exponent, while trace identities synchronize the governing nilpotent orbit across places and coefficient embeddings.
