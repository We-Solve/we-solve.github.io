Vanishing and Uniqueness of Quantum Diffusion Solutions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Let $n$ be a nonnegative density on $\mathbb T^d$, $d\ge1$. The Hessian $\nabla^2\log n$ has entries $\partial_i\partial_j\log n$ where $n>0$, and its squared norm is $\sum_{i,j}|\partial_i\partial_j\log n|^2$. For a matrix field $M$, $\nabla^2\!:\!M=\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\partial_jM_{ij}$. At vacuum the logarithm and division by $n$ require the report's entropy-based weak interpretation. Uniqueness compares weak solutions with the same initial data in that admissible class.
Source scope and qualifications.
A zero-mass solution would make the vacuum-existence question trivial, so the intended physical question concerns nonzero density. The weak class and meaning of a vacuum point must be fixed before asking either existence or uniqueness. The source proves nonnegative weak existence but does not infer either of these two answers.
Catalogue mathematical target.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
For the sixth-order equation $\partial_t n=\operatorname{div}\!\left[n\nabla\left(\frac12|\nabla^2\log n|^2+\frac1n\nabla^2\!:\!(n\nabla^2\log n)\right)\right]$ on the $d$-dimensional torus, with $n(\cdot,0)=n_0\geq0$, do there exist weak solutions that vanish at some points? Are weak solutions unique?

The positive-background linearization.
Let $n=n_*+\epsilon u$ with constant $n_*>0$ and smooth $u$. Then $\nabla^2\log n=\epsilon\nabla^2u/n_*+O(\epsilon^2)$. The squared-Hessian term is quadratic, while \[ \frac1n\nabla^2\!:\!(n\nabla^2\log n) =\frac{\epsilon}{n_*}\Delta^2u+O(\epsilon^2). \] Multiplying its gradient by $n$ and taking divergence gives $u_t=\Delta^3u$ at first order. For a torus Fourier mode indexed by $k\in\mathbb Z^d$, this has solution $\widehat u_k(t)=e^{-(2\pi|k|)^6t}\widehat u_k(0)$.
A rigorous uniqueness calculation in the linear model.
For two smooth linear solutions with the same initial data, their difference $w$ satisfies \[ \frac12\frac d{dt}\|w\|_2^2=\int w\Delta^3w=-\|\nabla\Delta w\|_2^2\leq0. \] Periodic integrations by parts have no boundary terms, and zero initial norm forces $w=0$. The zero Fourier mode is conserved, consistent with mass conservation.
The nonlinear first obstruction.
The displayed expansion and any comparable difference estimate require control of $1/n$ and derivatives of $\log n$. Those are precisely unavailable near vacuum in the source weak class. Linear smoothing and uniqueness around a positive constant therefore establish neither existence of genuine vacuum points in a positive-mass solution nor weak uniqueness through such points. One must first find a difference estimate meaningful at zero, or a strict-positivity theorem supplying the missing lower bound. Both bundled questions remain unresolved; the identically zero solution is not counted as the intended vacuum example.
