Research attempt: Dissipative projection and an a posteriori response bound

A parameter family whose structure survives projection
Consider $\dot x=A(\mu(t))x+Bu$, $y=B^Tx$, where \[ A(\mu)=J(\mu)-R(\mu),\qquad J(\mu)^T=-J(\mu),\qquad R(\mu)\succeq\rho I, \quad\rho>0. \] Assume the displayed trajectories exist on the time interval compared; continuous bounded coefficients and locally integrable inputs suffice. Let $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}$ have orthonormal columns, and reduce by \[ \dot z=V^TA(\mu)Vz+V^TBu,\qquad \widehat y=B^TVz. \] The reduced skew part remains skew, and $V^TRV\succeq\rho I_r$. Thus for the full and reduced energy functions $E=\lVert x\rVert^2/2$ and $\widehat E=\lVert z\rVert^2/2$, \[ \dot E\leq-\rho\lVert x\rVert^2+u^Ty, \qquad \dot{\widehat E}\leq-\rho\lVert z\rVert^2+u^T\widehat y. \] These inequalities prove passivity in the input/output pairing displayed and exponential energy decay with zero input. They hold also for time-varying parameters because the energy matrix is the same identity for the whole family. Convex interpolation of such $A$ matrices preserves both algebraic inequalities: skew symmetry is linear, and a nonnegative weighted average of matrices at least $\rho I$ remains at least $\rho I$.
A residual bound valid for arbitrary specified inputs
Lift the reduced state to $\widehat x=Vz$ and define its computable residual \[ r=\dot{\widehat x}-A(\mu)\widehat x-Bu =-(I-VV^T)(A(\mu)Vz+Bu). \] For $e=x-\widehat x$ we have $\dot e=A(\mu)e-r$. Let $U(t,s)$ be the homogeneous transition operator. Its action on any initial vector obeys $d\lVert U(t,s)v\rVert^2/dt\leq-2\rho\lVert U(t,s)v\rVert^2$; multiplying by $e^{2\rho t}$ and integrating gives $\lVert U(t,s)\rVert\leq e^{-\rho(t-s)}$. Variation of constants, verified by differentiating its expression, gives \[ e(t)=U(t,0)e(0)-\int_0^tU(t,s)r(s)\,ds. \] Taking norms therefore proves \[ \lVert y(t)-\widehat y(t)\rVert \leq\lVert B\rVert\left(e^{-\rho t}\lVert e(0)\rVert+ \int_0^te^{-\rho(t-s)}\lVert r(s)\rVert\,ds\right). \tag{1} \] This is an a posteriori bound for each input and parameter trajectory; it does not predict that a chosen small subspace has a small residual. Residual evaluation can itself require full-order work, another limitation for practical large systems.
Two obstructions to extending this route blindly
A stable full matrix need not yield a stable orthogonal projection. Let \[ A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&M\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad V=2^{-1/2}(1,1)^T, \] with $M>2$. The full solution matrix is $e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&Mt\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, which tends to zero, but $V^TAV=-1+M/2>0$ makes the reduced zero-input solution grow. Thus the common dissipativity condition, or another structure-preserving mechanism, cannot be inferred from eigenvalue stability alone.

Also, for linear systems with zero initial condition, a uniform absolute error bound over all input amplitudes is impossible unless the two input/output operators agree exactly. If one input $u$ gives a nonzero output error, the input $cu$ gives $c$ times that error. Letting $c$ increase violates any fixed finite absolute tolerance. A meaningful arbitrary-input guarantee must therefore scale with an input norm, use a bounded input class, or be a bound like $1$ depending on the actual residual.
