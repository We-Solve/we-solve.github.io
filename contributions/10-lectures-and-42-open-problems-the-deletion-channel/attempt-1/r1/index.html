OpenAI's claim

OpenAI presents the following claims relevant to this question, with manuscript numbers following paper order in Sources. In manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that worst-case trace reconstruction has quasipolynomial sample bounds with logarithm $O((\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6)$ at fixed retention, including general exactly observed symbols, without an algorithmic guarantee; In manuscript 2 (family 122; 2026-10-05), the asserted result is that every binary word can be reconstructed with known rational retention probability using a uniform algorithm with quasipolynomial sample and bit bounds at fixed retention, and polynomial bounds for sufficiently small deletion; In manuscript 3 (family 122; 2026-09-24), the asserted result is that worst-case exact binary trace reconstruction needs $n^{\Omega(\log\log n)}$ samples at any fixed deletion probability, even with unrestricted computation.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
Each source symbol is retained independently with probability $p\in(0,1]$, and the output consists of the retained symbols in order. The decoder knows the length $n$ and retention probability $p$ and receives independent traces. For every $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ its reconstruction success must be at least $2/3$. For every $n\ge2$, the claimed worst-case trace sample bound is \[ N\le\exp\left(O(p^{-1}(\log n)^3(1+\log\log(2n))^6)\right). \] The constants are absolute. For $q=1-p>0$ and $\mathcal X=1+\log n/(1+\log(1/q))$, the refined bound is \[ \log N=O(p^{-1}(\log n)\mathcal X^2(1+\log\mathcal X)^6). \] For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $q\le n^{-\varepsilon}$ therefore permits polynomially many traces. Exact observation of retained symbols also allows general alphabets. This is a sample-complexity claim, with no comparable running-time assertion.
Manuscript 2.
For a binary word $x\in\{0,1\}^n$, $n\ge2$, an ordinary deletion trace independently retains each symbol with rational probability $p=a/b\in(0,1]$ and preserves order. Put $q=1-p$, let $L$ be the bit length of the reduced numerator and denominator, and define \[ X(n,p)=\begin{cases}1+\dfrac{\log n}{1+\log(1/q)},&q>0,\\1,&q=0,\end{cases} \qquad B(n,p)=p^{-1}(\log n)X(n,p)^2(1+\log X(n,p))^6. \] The claim gives absolute $C,c\ge1$ and one randomized multitape Turing machine, given $n,p$ in binary, that requests at most \[ M_C(n,p)=\lceil\exp(CB(n,p))\rceil \] independent traces. For each input word $x$, reconstruction succeeds with probability at least $2/3$. Regardless of the internal random tape and of the received stream, provided all its strings have length bounded by $n$, the running time is at most \[ (n+L+M_C(n,p))^c \] bit operations, including preprocessing, finite-precision calculations, received bits and output. Only external generation of independent traces is treated as an oracle operation.
Manuscript 3.
For deletion probability $q\in(0,1)$ and required success probability $s\in(0,1]$, let $T_{q,s}(n)$ be the minimum number of independent ordinary deletion traces sufficient to reconstruct every binary word of length $n$ with success at least $s$. For every fixed $s$ and every fixed \[ 0<c<\frac1{4\log2}, \] the claimed lower bound, along any sequence $n\to\infty$ with $q^3\log n\to\infty$, is eventually \[ T_{q,s}(n)\ge n^{c\log(q^3\log n)}. \] In particular fixed $q,s$ give $T_{q,s}(n)=n^{\Omega(\log\log n)}$ and $T_{q,s}(n)/n^A\to\infty$ for each fixed $A>0$. If $D_q(x)$ is the one-trace probability law, the cited consequence for every fixed $q\in(0,1)$ and $A>0$ is \[ n^A\min_{\substack{x\ne y\\x,y\in\{0,1\}^n}} d_{\rm TV}(D_q(x),D_q(y))\longrightarrow0. \]

Outline of the argument

For manuscript 1 (family 122; 2026-10-05), gap probes use compact spectral masks and a latest-anchor induction to keep a signal during shuffling and scale transfer. Radial compactification and stopping-stage interpolation produce separation of trace laws; the argument then reduces general symbols to the detected binary distinction.

For manuscript 2 (family 122; 2026-10-05), low-order trace moments propagate a distinguishing signal through scales using damped products and prescribed frequencies. Positive moment constraints recover prefixes, and a bounded feasibility iteration turns the statistical separation into an explicit finite-precision decoder.

For manuscript 3 (family 122; 2026-09-24), nested signed words hide fair orientation bits inside overlapping windows. An auxiliary observation retains side data while projecting to genuine traces; likelihood-score induction forces a large binary subtree for every surviving term and bounds the distance between the two trace laws.
