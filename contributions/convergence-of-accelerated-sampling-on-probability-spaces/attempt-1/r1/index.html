Research attempt: Anderson extrapolation can leave probability space

Identify Bernoulli distributions with $p\in[0,1]$ and use absolute distance (equivalently total variation). Define the contraction \[ T(p)=\frac{31}{50}+\frac9{10}\max\{p-\tfrac45,0\}. \] It maps $[0,1]$ into $[31/50,4/5]$, has Lipschitz constant $9/10$, and has fixed point $31/50$. Starting at $p_0=1$, ordinary iteration gives $p_1=T(p_0)=4/5$, with residuals \[ r_0=T(p_0)-p_0=-1/5,\qquad r_1=T(p_1)-p_1=-9/50. \] The unconstrained two-history Anderson step chooses coefficients summing to one to minimize the absolute mixed residual. Here its exact zero-residual choice is $\alpha_0=-9$, $\alpha_1=10$. The resulting proposed iterate is \[ \alpha_0T(p_0)+\alpha_1T(p_1) =-9\cdot\frac45+10\cdot\frac{31}{50}=-1. \] It is not a probability. Thus even genuine consecutive iterates of a contraction do not justify unconstrained affine mixing in probability coordinates. Smoothness alone does not repair the example: replace the maximum by $\epsilon\log(1+e^{(p-4/5)/\epsilon})$. For sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$ this smooth map still maps the interval into itself, has derivative between zero and $9/10$, and its two-step Anderson output remains negative by continuity of the residual formula at the displayed nonzero residual difference.

A conditional valid-space safeguard.
Let $(X,d)$ be complete and $T:X\to X$ be a contraction of factor $0\leq q<1$. Its unique fixed point $x_*$ exists: the ordinary iterates are Cauchy because consecutive distances are bounded by a geometric sequence, their limit is fixed by continuity, and two fixed points would satisfy $d\leq qd$. Define residual $R(x)=d(x,Tx)$. The triangle inequality gives \[ d(x,x_*)\leq R(x)+q d(x,x_*),\qquad R(Tx)\leq q R(x). \] Fix $0\leq\theta<1$. At each step accept any accelerated candidate $y\in X$ only if $R(y)\leq\theta R(x)$; otherwise use $Tx$. Then with $\rho=\max(q,\theta)<1$, \[ R(x_k)\leq\rho^kR(x_0),\qquad d(x_k,x_*)\leq\frac{\rho^kR(x_0)}{1-q}. \] This follows by induction and the first residual bound. Membership in $X$ must be checked before acceptance; the negative Bernoulli proposal fails it. This is a complete convergence proof under a specified contraction and computable exact residual, but it neither promises acceleration nor proves that a Stein sampling map satisfies those hypotheses.
