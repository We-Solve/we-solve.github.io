Erdős Problem 804: local independence need not be global

Statement and claim.
Let $F(n,s,t)$ be the minimum possible independence number of an $n$-vertex graph in which every $s$-vertex subset contains an independent $t$-set. Writing $L=\log n$, with integer roundings understood, we reconstruct the bounds \[ \frac{L^2}{\log L}\ll F(n,L^2,L)\ll L^2,\qquad F(n,L^3,L)\asymp\frac{L^2}{\log L}. \tag{1} \] They disprove both proposed larger lower bounds. The arguments follow the counting and random-graph methods of Alon--Sudakov, On graphs with subgraphs having large independence numbers, https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/erdoshajnal4.pdf. See https://www.erdosproblems.com/804. We supply the needed estimates below rather than use $1$ as an unexplained external theorem. All logarithms are natural.
A local-to-global lower bound.
Suppose $s\geq2t\geq4$, and put $u=\lfloor t/2\rfloor$. In any remaining vertex set $V$ of order $M\geq s$, every $s$-set contains an independent $2u$-set. Double counting gives at least \[ \frac{\binom Ms}{\binom{M-2u}{s-2u}} =\frac{\binom M{2u}}{\binom s{2u}} \geq(M/s)^{2u} \] independent $2u$-sets. Average over their $u$-element cores. Some independent $u$-set $X$ has at least \[ (M/s)^{2u}/\binom Mu \geq\left(\frac{Mu}{es^2}\right)^u \] distinct $u$-element extensions $Y$, disjoint from $X$, such that $X\cup Y$ is independent. Let $V'$ be the union of these extensions. There are no edges from $X$ to $V'$, and \[ \left(\frac{e|V'|}{u}\right)^u \geq\binom{|V'|}{u} \geq\left(\frac{Mu}{es^2}\right)^u. \] Therefore \[ |V'|\geq\frac{Mu^2}{e^2s^2} \geq aM,\qquad a=\frac{t^2}{16e^2s^2}. \] Repeat inside $V'$ while its order is at least $s$. The successive cores are pairwise disjoint with no edges between them. We obtain at least a constant times $\log(n/s)/\log(s/t)$ cores, and consequently \[ F(n,s,t)\gg\frac{t\log(n/s)}{\log(s/t)}. \tag{2} \] For $s=\lceil L^2\rceil$ or $\lceil L^3\rceil$ and $t=\lceil L\rceil$, this is $\Omega(L^2/\log L)$.
A random counterexample when $s=\lceil L^2\rceil$.
Take $G\sim G(n,p)$, with $t=\lceil L\rceil$ and $p=e^{-12}/t$. Let \[ b=\left\lfloor\frac{s^2}{2t}-\frac s2\right\rfloor+1. \] If some $s$-set spans at least $b$ edges, a union bound on specified edge sets gives probability at most \[ \binom ns\binom{\binom s2}{b}p^b \leq\exp(s(L+1)) \left(\frac{eps^2}{2b}\right)^b. \] For large $n$, $s\geq2t$ and $b\geq s^2/(4t)$; the parenthesized factor is at most $2ept=2e^{-11}<e^{-10}$. The resulting bound $\exp(s(L+1)-10s^2/(4t))$ tends to zero. Thus every $s$-set has at most $s^2/(2t)-s/2$ edges. Its average degree is at most $s/t-1$, so the elementary independence estimate $\alpha\geq v/(\bar d+1)$ gives an independent set of size at least $t$.

For $K=\lceil4L/p\rceil$, on the other hand, \[ \Pr(\alpha(G)\geq K) \leq\binom nK(1-p)^{\binom K2} \leq\exp\left(KL-\frac p2K(K-1)\right)=o(1). \tag{3} \] Both properties hold simultaneously, giving $F(n,L^2,L)=O(L^2)$.

A stronger random construction when $s=\lceil L^3\rceil$.
Take now $p=(\log L)/(16L)$, still with $t=\lceil L\rceil$. We show that every $s$-set has an independent $t$-set with probability tending to one. For a fixed $s$-set, repeatedly run the greedy algorithm to find a maximal independent set in the remaining graph, and remove the set it found. The algorithm need only expose edges incident with the selected independent vertices. Conditional on the history, edges between the remaining vertices are still independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $p$.

While at least $s/2$ vertices remain, the probability that a maximal independent set of size less than $t$ exists is at most \[ t s^t\exp\left(-\frac s3(1-p)^t\right). \tag{4} \] Indeed, for a specified candidate of size $j<t$, maximality requires each of at least $s/3$ outside vertices to have a neighbour in it. The probability is at most $(1-(1-p)^j)^{s/3}\leq\exp(-(s/3)(1-p)^t)$. There are at most $ts^t$ candidates. For large $n$, \[ (1-p)^t\geq e^{-2pt}\geq L^{-1/4}. \] Thus $4$ is at most $\exp(-(s/4)L^{-1/4})$, since $t\log s=o(sL^{-1/4})$.

If the fixed $s$-set contains no independent $t$-set, at least $\lfloor s/(2t)\rfloor\geq s/(3t)$ successive rounds must all produce such small maximal sets. Applying the preceding conditional probability bound at each round gives failure probability at most \[ \exp\left(-\frac{s^2}{12t}L^{-1/4}\right) =\exp(-\Omega(L^{19/4})). \] This estimate does not assume unconditional independence between rounds. There are at most $n^s=\exp(O(L^4))$ choices of the original $s$-set, so their union-bound failure probability still tends to zero.

Finally $3$, for this new $p$, gives $\alpha(G)<K=O(L^2/\log L)$ with probability tending to one. The two desired properties again hold simultaneously. Together with $2$, this proves the second assertion in $1$.

Conclusion and scope.
The first construction has global independence $O((\log n)^2)=n^{o(1)}$, disproving the suggested $n^{1/2-o(1)}$ lower bound. The second has global independence $O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)=o((\log n)^3)$, disproving the other suggestion, with the matching lower order proved in $2$. Integer ceilings do not affect any estimate. The elementary average-degree independence bound used above follows, for example, by the random-order greedy argument giving $\alpha\geq\sum_v1/(d(v)+1)\geq v/(\bar d+1)$.
Completion estimate.
