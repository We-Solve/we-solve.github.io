Local Algorithms for Semidefinite Programming

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A bisection partitions the vertices into two parts of equal size (or differing by one for odd order). Its cut consists of the edges joining the parts; minimum bisection minimizes their total weight.

A semidefinite program optimizes a linear functional of a symmetric matrix subject to affine constraints and positive semidefiniteness. The condition $X\succeq0$ means $v^TXv\geq0$ for every real vector $v$.

In the LOCAL model vertices communicate synchronously along graph edges; messages and local computation are unrestricted and complexity is the number of rounds. After $t$ rounds a vertex can depend only on its radius-$t$ neighborhood and any supplied identifiers or randomness.

Locally positive blocks need not assemble into a positive matrix

The record overlaps the more specific random-graph question under ID 30003869. Here we test a different route: enforce the semidefinite constraint only in each small neighborhood and assemble those entries globally. Take the six-cycle with adjacency matrix $A$ and put $X=I-(3/5)A$.
Proposition: The matrix $X$ has unit diagonal and every principal block on a radius-one ball is positive definite, but $X$ itself is not positive semidefinite.
Proof: Each radius-one ball induces a three-vertex path. Its block, in path order, is \[ \begin{pmatrix}1&-3/5&0\\-3/5&1&-3/5\\0&-3/5&1\end{pmatrix}. \] The path adjacency matrix has characteristic polynomial $t(t^2-2)$, so the displayed block has eigenvalues $1,1\pm3\sqrt2/5$. They are positive because $18/25<1$. However, every cycle vertex has degree two, so $X\mathbf1=(1-6/5)\mathbf1=-\mathbf1/5$. Its quadratic form on $\mathbf1$ is $-6/5<0$, disproving global positive semidefiniteness. ∎
This obstruction concerns checking and assembling overlapping local principal blocks. A local algorithm may instead construct genuine vectors and use their Gram matrix, which is globally positive semidefinite by construction. Thus the example does not prove that all local SDP algorithms fail; it pinpoints an invalid feasibility inference. The primary Fan--Montanari work indeed supplies successful local constructions for specified random graph models, and those results are not contradicted here.
