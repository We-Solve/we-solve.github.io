Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can the computational and query-complexity limitations on procedures that prevent false discovery in interactive data analysis be removed?

The source limitations and the attempted route

Ullman's report, printed pages 3067--3068, describes both computational and information-theoretic restrictions on general adaptive statistical queries. Its phrase about removing limitations is followed by negative results; it is not an assertion that privacy abolishes all limitations. The original lower-bound paper's abstract gives an earlier parameter regime than the later workshop summary. We do not equate those rates or claim to reprove either advanced lower bound. We attempt the source's privacy route using elementary composition, derive exactly what a replacement-sample argument gives, and locate the missing high-probability inference.

A complete adaptive Laplace composition calculation

Fix $k\ge1$, $\varepsilon>0$, a sample of size $n\ge1$, and predicates $\phi_i:X\to\{-1,1\}$ selected from past released answers. The analyst has randomness independent of the sample. At each step release \[ a_i=\frac1n\sum_{x\in S}\phi_i(x)+Z_i, \qquad Z_i\text{ independent with density } \frac1{2b}e^{-|z|/b},\quad b=\frac{2k}{n\varepsilon}. \] For neighboring samples differing in one entry, a fixed predicate's empirical means differ by at most $2/n$. Conditional on a fixed earlier answer history and the analyst's seed, the chosen predicate is the same on both samples. The conditional density ratio at any next answer is at most $\exp(2/(nb))=\exp(\varepsilon/k)$, by the triangle inequality for absolute values. Multiplication of the $k$ conditional density ratios gives a joint ratio at most $e^\varepsilon$. Integrating over any transcript event and then the independent analyst seed proves pure $\varepsilon$-differential privacy of the transcript. Any selected query computed from that transcript is also private, because the probability of an event about it is the probability of its preimage event for the transcript (extra independent randomness can be integrated in the same way).

Direct integration gives $\Pr(|Z_i|>u)=e^{-u/b}$ for $u\ge0$. The union bound proves simultaneous empirical accuracy \[ \max_{i\le k}|a_i-\phi_i(S)| \le\frac{2k}{n\varepsilon}\log\frac{k}{\eta} \quad\text{with probability at least }1-\eta, \qquad 0<\eta<1. \] The displayed noise scale already grows linearly with $k$; elementary pure-privacy composition alone does not remove query dependence.

What replacement stability actually proves

Let a pure $\varepsilon$-private algorithm output a random function $q:X\to[0,1]$. Take $S=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ with independent draws from $P$, and replace $X_i$ by an independent $X_i'$ to get $S^{(i)}$. For fixed neighboring datasets and fixed $x$, privacy for the events $\{q:q(x)>t\}$, integrated over $0\le t\le1$, gives \[ \mathbb E_{q\mid S}q(x) \le e^\varepsilon\mathbb E_{q\mid S^{(i)}}q(x). \] Set $x=X_i$ and average all data. The output from $S^{(i)}$ is independent of $X_i$, and $S^{(i)}$ has the same distribution as $S$. Consequently, writing $A=\mathbb E q(S)$ and $B=\mathbb E q(P)$, we get $A\le e^\varepsilon B$ after averaging over $i$. Applying the reversed neighboring-dataset inequality likewise gives $B\le e^\varepsilon A$. Since $0\le A,B\le1$, \[ |\mathbb E[q(S)-q(P)]|\le e^\varepsilon-1. \] For predicates taking values $\{-1,1\}$, translate by $q=(\phi+1)/2$ to obtain twice this bound. This is a bound on the absolute value of a signed expectation. It is not a bound on the expectation of the absolute gap.

An exact obstruction to the tempting Markov step

Let $P$ be uniform on a finite domain of size $N>n$, and let $A(S)$ be the support of the sample. A deliberately nonprivate algorithm tosses a fair independent coin and outputs either $q=\mathbf1_{A(S)}$ or $q=1-\mathbf1_{A(S)}$. The empirical-minus-population gap is respectively \[ 1-|A(S)|/N\quad\text{or}\quad-(1-|A(S)|/N). \] Its signed expectation is exactly zero, even conditional on $S$, while its absolute value is at least $1-n/N$ with probability one. Therefore the signed-mean estimate alone cannot be fed into Markov's inequality to establish high-probability generalization: Markov requires a nonnegative variable and its expectation. This example does not contradict a correct differential-privacy generalization theorem, since the example is not private. It refutes the proposed inference from the single numerical signed-mean bound, which discards essential information.

Model-assisted audit and remaining scope

The audit checked adaptive conditional densities, independent analyst coins, the replacement sample's independence, and the location of the absolute-value signs. The published privacy transfer theorem needs a stronger argument than the one just attempted; it is not derived by combining empirical noise accuracy with this signed expectation. These are known elementary mechanisms and illustrative obstructions, not novel lower bounds. No numerical experiment or theorem prover was used. We neither remove the source's computational limitations nor prove its full high-probability query tradeoff, and we do not confuse population query accuracy with an unspecified multiple-testing FDR convention.
