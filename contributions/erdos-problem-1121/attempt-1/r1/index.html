Erdős Problem 1121: covering a nonseparable family of circles

Statement and outcome

Suppose finitely many planar circles, with centres $c_i$ and positive radii $r_i$, cannot be divided into two nonempty groups by a line disjoint from all of them. Their closed disks are contained in a disk of radius \[ R=\sum_i r_i. \] We prove the stronger explicit statement that its centre can be chosen as \[ c=\frac{\sum_i r_i c_i}{R}. \] This reconstructs the Goodman--Goodman covering argument through its one-dimensional core, with an integral proof of that core. The result is classical; no novelty is claimed.

A weighted interval lemma

Let $I_i=[x_i-r_i,x_i+r_i]$ have connected union $[L,U]$, and put $R=\sum r_i$ and $\bar x=\sum r_i x_i/R$. We prove \[ [L,U]\subset[\bar x-R,\bar x+R]. \tag{1} \] Define the multiplicity function $m(t)=\sum_i1_{I_i}(t)$. Its integral is $2R$, it is at least one almost everywhere on $[L,U]$, and \[ \int t\,m(t)\,dt=2\sum_i r_i x_i=2R\bar x. \] Writing $d=U-L\le2R$, we obtain \[ \bar x-L =\frac1{2R}\int_L^U(t-L)m(t)\,dt \ge\frac{d^2}{4R}. \] Therefore \[ \bar x-(U-R)\ge R-d+\frac{d^2}{4R} =\frac{(2R-d)^2}{4R}\ge0. \] Reflection in the origin gives the other endpoint inequality $\bar x\le L+R$. This proves $1$, allowing overlaps and containment of the original intervals.

All projections are connected

Fix a unit vector $u$. The orthogonal projections of the disks, and also of their circular boundaries, are the intervals \[ I_i(u)=[\langle u,c_i\rangle-r_i,\ \langle u,c_i\rangle+r_i]. \] Their union is connected. Otherwise an open gap between two components contains a real $t$, and the line $\langle u,x\rangle=t$ misses every circle while leaving circles on each side. This contradicts the hypothesis. A tangency causes no difficulty: a single missing point is not a gap because the intervals are closed.

The interval lemma, with the same radii as weights, now shows that every point $x$ of every disk satisfies \[ |\langle u,x-c\rangle|\le R. \] This holds for every unit vector $u$. If $x\ne c$, choose $u=(x-c)/\|x-c\|$ to get $\|x-c\|\le R$; the case $x=c$ is immediate. Thus the stated disk covers all the disks, and in particular their boundaries. The same argument works in every Euclidean dimension, with separating hyperplanes.

Sharpness and assessment

Place the disks in a chain on one line, with consecutive disks externally tangent and no gaps. Their union is connected, so no disjoint line can separate it. The two extreme points are at distance $2\sum_i r_i$, forcing any covering disk to have radius at least $\sum_i r_i$. Hence the universal radius is best possible.

The theorem, the explicit centre and sharpness are proved without an external covering theorem. Sources: https://www.erdosproblems.com/1121; A. W. Goodman and R. E. Goodman, A circle covering theorem, Amer. Math. Monthly 52 $1945$, 494--498, https://doi.org/10.2307/2304537; A. Akopyan, A. Balitskiy, R. Grigorev, On the Circle Covering Theorem by A.W. Goodman and R.E. Goodman, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-017-9883-x.
