Adaptive statistical queries: fresh-block accuracy and exact-answer overfitting

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A learning problem specifies a data distribution, a hypothesis class and a loss. Statistical consistency means that the estimator approaches its population target as the sample size increases; statistical and computational requirements are distinct.

A statistical query is an expectation $\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[q(x)]$ for a bounded function $q$. Adaptive queries may depend on earlier answers, so accuracy must account for the dependence between the sample and the selected queries.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Quantified scope of the attempt

Ullman's report, printed pages 3066--3069, uses efficiently computable predicates $\phi:X\to\{-1,1\}$. Its informal statements suppress accuracy parameters; the catalogue likewise does not quantify query count. Fix $k\ge1$, error $a>0$, confidence $0<\eta<1$, and an analyst whose randomness and initial information are independent of the sample. We prove an elementary fresh-block guarantee and then attack the tempting reuse of exact empirical answers. These are baseline calculations, not improvements on the more sophisticated mechanisms described by the source.

A complete concentration estimate

If $Y\in[-1,1]$, let $g(\lambda)=\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda Y}$. Differentiation is valid because $Y$ is bounded. Its second derivative is the variance of $Y$ under the exponentially tilted distribution, at most one: $\operatorname{Var}(Y)\le\mathbb EY^2\le1$. Taylor's formula with an integral remainder, also for negative $\lambda$, therefore gives $g(\lambda)\le\lambda\mathbb EY+\lambda^2/2$. For $b$ independent copies, multiplying their centered moment-generating functions and using Markov's inequality gives, for $\lambda>0$, \[ \Pr\!\left[\frac1b\sum_{j=1}^bY_j-\mathbb EY\ge a\right] \le \exp(-\lambda ba+b\lambda^2/2). \] Take $\lambda=a$ and apply the same argument to $-Y$ to obtain the two-sided bound $2\exp(-ba^2/2)$.

A valid adaptive mechanism by sample splitting

Partition $kb$ observations into $k$ disjoint blocks of size $b$. At step $i$, answer the selected predicate by its empirical mean on block $i$ only. Condition on the analyst's random seed and the observations in all earlier blocks. The selected predicate is now fixed, and block $i$ remains an independent sample from $P$. The preceding bound therefore holds conditionally and, by taking expectations, unconditionally. A union bound over the $k$ steps shows that all answers have population error at most $a$ with probability at least $1-\eta$ whenever \[ b\ge\left\lceil\frac{2}{a^2}\log\frac{2k}{\eta}\right\rceil, \qquad n\ge kb. \] Unused observations may be discarded. Randomized analysts can equivalently draw fresh independent coins at each step; conditioning on all their coins in advance gives the same proof. With efficient predicates, the mechanism uses $b$ evaluations per answer. The linear dependence on $k$ is a clear limitation; no source-optimal sample complexity is claimed.

Why exact empirical reuse breaks the fixed-query argument

Let $n\ge2$, choose $d=\lceil2\log_2n\rceil$, and let $P$ be uniform on $X=\{0,1\}^d$ of size $N=2^d\ge n^2$. Suppose the mechanism gives the exact empirical answer on the same sample to every predicate. We recover the sample's support by prefix counts. The root prefix has known count $n$. For every prefix of positive known count at depth less than $d$, ask the predicate which is one on its left child and minus one elsewhere. If the answer is $v$, the left-child count is exactly $n(v+1)/2$; subtract from the parent count to get the right-child count. Continue only at children of positive count. At any depth there are at most $n$ occupied prefixes, so at most $nd$ queries recover every occupied leaf and its multiplicity. All queried predicates are efficient prefix tests and are exactly of the source's $\{-1,1\}$ type.

Let $A$ be the resulting support and ask the final predicate $\phi_A=2\mathbf1_A-1$, which can be evaluated from a list of at most $n$ strings. Its empirical mean is one, but \[ \mathbb E_P\phi_A=2|A|/N-1\le2n/N-1. \] Thus the final answer's population error is at least $2-2n/N\ge2-2/n$, for every realized sample, after at most $n\lceil2\log_2n\rceil+1$ queries. This exact computation is a deterministic proof, not a simulation or a claim of an optimal attack. Earlier answers need not all be inaccurate; their exact count information is what permits the final tailored query.

The attempted inference "each selected predicate has the fixed-query concentration bound, so union-bound over the selected predicates" is invalid when those predicates depend on the same observations. The fresh-block proof conditioned on a history independent of the new block. In this attack conditioning on the history reveals the very sample on which the final mean is evaluated. The conditional independence step fails. This is an obstruction to this particular empirical mechanism, not an impossibility theorem for all adaptive mechanisms.
