Attempt and results

The source's word "decodability" can mean a code presentation, a particular syndrome, or unique decoding throughout a radius. We keep these separate and prove certificate statements for specified finite problems. These are auxiliary rederivations, not a complete answer to every intended interpretation.
Positive and negative syndrome certificates.
For $H\in\mathbb F_q^{r\times n}$, $s\in\mathbb F_q^r$, and radius $t\ge0$, an error $e$ with $He=s$ and Hamming weight at most $t$ is an immediately checkable positive witness. For each support $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ of size at most $\min(t,n)$, let $H_S$ be its column submatrix. There is no error supported on $S$ exactly when $s$ is outside the span $W$ of those columns. This holds exactly when there is a row vector $\lambda_S$ satisfying \[ \lambda_SH_S=0,\qquad\lambda_S s=1. \] One direction follows by applying $\lambda_S$ to a proposed solution. For the converse, take a basis of $W$, append $s$ to that independent list, and extend to a basis of $\mathbb F_q^r$. Define a linear functional to be zero on the basis of $W$ and the other added vectors, and one on $s$. Its coordinate row is the required $\lambda_S$. Supplying these rows for every small support is therefore a complete negative certificate. Its size can be exponential, since there are $\sum_{j=0}^{\min(t,n)}\binom nj$ supports. It proves nonexistence for that syndrome and radius, not absence of every Goppa presentation.
A full degree-one binary presentation criterion.
Let $B\in\mathbb F_2^{r\times n}$ be a full row basis of a binary parity check space, with $n\ge1$. Suppose $r\ge1$ and all columns $b_i$ are nonzero and pairwise distinct. Fix an explicit field $K=\mathbb F_{2^r}$ and basis over $\mathbb F_2$, identify $b_i$ with its nonzero field element $h_i$, and put $\alpha_i=h_i^{-1}$ and $g(z)=z$. The locators are distinct and nonzero. Modulo $z$, the rational check $\sum_i c_i/(z-\alpha_i)$ equals $\sum_i c_i h_i$, since signs disappear in characteristic two. Expanding in the chosen basis gives exactly $Bc=0$. Hence this constructs a degree-one binary Goppa presentation for the code. Finite-field construction is an explicit representation input to this proof; we make no unstated bit-complexity claim about constructing that field.

Conversely, for any degree-one polynomial $g(z)=z-\beta$ over a binary extension field with distinct locators avoiding $\beta$, the expanded check columns are the coordinates of $(\beta-\alpha_i)^{-1}$. They are nonzero and pairwise distinct. Replacing this check matrix $A$ by any full row basis $B$ of its row space preserves these properties: there are matrices $U,V$ with $A=UB$ and $B=VA$, so a zero column, or equality of two columns, holds for one matrix if and only if it holds for the other. Thus nonzero distinct columns of a full row basis are necessary and sufficient for this degree-one binary Goppa representability. When $r=0$ and $n\ge1$, the full code has all columns zero and cannot meet the criterion. A zero column supplies a weight-one codeword; equal columns supply a weight-two codeword. Conversely any nonzero binary codeword of weight one or two forces one of those two defects, by expanding its syndrome. The criterion therefore also characterizes minimum distance at least three (allowing the zero code).

Why certificates of different tasks cannot be substituted.
A zero or repeated column is a short negative witness for degree-one representability as just defined. It does not disprove some other Goppa presentation or every decoder. Similarly, if a particular syndrome equals a column of $H$, its corresponding unit error proves radius-one solvability, even if another pair of columns repeats and uniqueness fails elsewhere. For a concrete binary example, $H=(1\ 1)$ and syndrome $s=1$ have two weight-one solutions. Both are valid positive decoding witnesses, while their difference $(1,1)$ witnesses failure of uniform unique radius-one decoding. Thus the source must specify which assertion its desired negative witness is meant to refute.

Context, adversarial audit, and gap
