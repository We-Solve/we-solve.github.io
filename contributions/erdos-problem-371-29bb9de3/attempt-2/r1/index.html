Problem 371

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT

For $n\ge 2$ let $P(n)$ denote the largest prime factor of $n$ (and set $P(1)=1$). Define \[ A(x) \coloneqq \#\left\{1\le n\le x : P(n)<P(n+1)\right\}. \] The problem asks to prove that the set \[ \mathcal{S}\coloneqq \{n\in\mathbb{N} : P(n)<P(n+1)\} \] has (natural/asymptotic) density $1/2$, i.e. \[ \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{x}=\frac12. \]

2) KEY DEFINITIONS AND KNOWN FACTS

  • Largest prime factor: $P(n)=\max\{p\text{ prime}: p\mid n\}$ for $n\ge2$, and $P(1)=1$.
  • No ties: For every $n\ge1$, $\gcd(n,n+1)=1$, so $n$ and $n+1$ share no prime factors. Hence $P(n)\neq P(n+1)$ for all $n\ge1$. Therefore for each $n\ge1$ exactly one of the strict inequalities $P(n)<P(n+1)$ or $P(n)>P(n+1)$ holds.
  • Indicator reformulation: Define $f(n)\in\{\pm1\}$ by \[ f(n)=\begin{cases} +1,&P(n)<P(n+1),\\ -1,&P(n)>P(n+1). \end{cases} \] Then $A(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{n\le x}(1+f(n))$, so showing $A(x)\sim x/2$ is equivalent to proving \[ \sum_{n\le x} f(n) = o(x). \]
Quick literature/context check (browsing available).
As of late 2025/early 2026 the problem is still listed as open. There are deep partial results: best known unconditional lower density for both $\{P(n)<P(n+1)\}$ and its complement is $0.2017$ (Lü--Wang, 2025). Teräväinen $2018$ proved the logarithmic density is $1/2$; Tao--Teräväinen $2019$ proved "density $1/2$ at almost all scales"; and Wang $2021$ proved the full asymptotic density statement conditional on an Elliott--Halberstam type conjecture for friable integers.

3) ATTEMPTED SOLUTION

PHASE 1 (computational reality check). I computed $A(x)/x$ for several $x$ (by sieving the largest prime factor up to $x+1$). The observed values were: \[ \begin{array}{c|c} x & A(x)/x \\\hline 10 & 0.600000 \\ 10^2 & 0.460000 \\ 10^3 & 0.502000 \\ 10^4 & 0.498700 \\ 10^5 & 0.500230 \\ 10^6 & 0.499927 \end{array} \] This is consistent with the conjectured density $1/2$, but does not constitute a proof.

PHASE 2 (proof attempt; why it stalls). The reformulation above shows that the problem is to prove cancellation in the mean of the sign sequence $f(n)$. One would like to show that $P(n)$ and $P(n+1)$ behave "independently" in a strong enough sense that the sign is equidistributed. I attempted to reduce this to two-dimensional distributional estimates for smoothness events of the form "$P(n)\le y$ and $P(n+1)\le y'$", but obtaining an $o(x)$ error term in natural density (rather than logarithmic density) appears to require deeper correlation bounds than I can currently derive.

4) DETAILED WORK (with complete reasoning)

4.1 A completely proved partial result: both inequalities occur infinitely often

  1. Infinitely many $n$ with $P(n)<P(n+1)$. Take $n=2^m$ for any integer $m\ge 1$. Then $P(n)=P(2^m)=2$. Also $n+1=2^m+1$ is an odd integer $>1$, so it has some odd prime divisor $q\ge 3$. Hence $P(n+1)\ge 3>2=P(n)$, proving $P(n)<P(n+1)$ for all $n=2^m$.

  2. Infinitely many $n$ with $P(n)>P(n+1)$. Take $n=2^m-1$ for any integer $m\ge 2$. Then $n$ is odd and $n>1$, so it has a prime divisor $q\ge 3$, hence $P(n)\ge 3$. But $n+1=2^m$ has $P(n+1)=2$. Therefore $P(n)>P(n+1)$ for all $n=2^m-1$.

4.2 Why density $1/2$ is hard

Because $P(n)\neq P(n+1)$, the two sets \[ \mathcal{S} = \{n: P(n)<P(n+1)\},\qquad \mathcal{S}^c = \{n: P(n)>P(n+1)\} \] partition $\mathbb{N}$. Thus, if both natural densities exist, they must sum to $1$. However, showing they are each $1/2$ amounts to showing that the sign sequence $f(n)$ has mean $0$.

A naive probabilistic heuristic is:

"Model $P(n)$ and $P(n+1)$ as i.i.d. random variables with the Dickman--de Bruijn distribution (coming from smooth number statistics). Then symmetry forces $\mathbb{P}(P(n)<P(n+1))=1/2$."
But turning this into a theorem requires controlling correlations of arithmetic events involving the largest prime factor at consecutive integers with error terms strong enough to pass from logarithmic density to natural density.

5) VERIFICATION

  • The claim "no ties" is verified: if $P(n)=P(n+1)=p$, then $p\mid n$ and $p\mid (n+1)$, hence $p\mid 1$, impossible. So ties never occur.
  • The constructions $n=2^m$ and $n=2^m-1$ give valid infinite subsequences realizing each inequality.
  • The numerical table is consistent and was produced by a straightforward sieve for $P$.

6) FINAL

UNRESOLVED.

(i) Strongest fully proved partial result obtained here.

  • For all $n\ge1$, $P(n)\neq P(n+1)$.
  • There are infinitely many $n$ with $P(n)<P(n+1)$ (e.g. $n=2^m$) and infinitely many $n$ with $P(n)>P(n+1)$ (e.g. $n=2^m-1$).

(ii) Weakest specific gap preventing a full proof. A full proof of density $1/2$ is equivalent to proving cancellation $\sum_{n\le x} f(n)=o(x)$ for the sign sequence $f(n)$ comparing $P(n)$ and $P(n+1)$. I do not currently have a method to control this correlation on the scale of natural density with an error term $o(x)$.

(iii) What lemmas would be needed to complete the proof. One sufficient route would be:

  1. A theorem giving asymptotic independence of the "large prime factor structure" of $n$ and $n+1$ strong enough to imply $\mathbb{P}(P(n)\le y,\ P(n+1)\le y')\approx \mathbb{P}(P(n)\le y)\mathbb{P}(P(n+1)\le y')$ uniformly for the relevant ranges of $y,y'$.
  2. A transfer principle from such two-dimensional distributional control to the sign statistic $\mathbf{1}_{P(n)<P(n+1)}$ with an $o(x)$ error.

(iv) Minimal explicit computation supporting the claim. The sieve computations above for $x\le 10^6$ give $A(10^6)/10^6\approx 0.499927$.
