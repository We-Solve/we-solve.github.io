prb_e369961459cd41bda5262b6306a68375: exact multigraded formulas for one or two generic fat points

**Claim type:** Partial result. **Scope:** Every multigraded Hilbert-function value for $s=1$ and $s=2$, arbitrary multiplicity, arbitrary number of projective factors and arbitrary factor dimensions. The general case of three or more points is not solved here. No novelty is claimed for the coordinate-monomial method or these small-support cases.

Use exactly the catalogue's ring, multigrading and ideals. Write $\boldsymbol a=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$ and $N=\sum_i(n_i-1)=\dim Y$. A factor with $n_i=1$ is $\mathbb P^0$ and causes no difficulty below. For $s=2$ assume $N\ge1$; if every factor is $\mathbb P^0$, two distinct points do not exist, so the catalogue's distinctness assumption cannot be met.

1. Formula for one fat point

For $n_i\ge2$ define

$$A_i(z)=\sum_{b=0}^{a_i}\binom{b+n_i-2}{n_i-2}z^b.$$

For $n_i=1$ instead define $A_i(z)=1$. Put

$$F_m(\boldsymbol a)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}[z^j]\prod_{i=1}^t A_i(z). \tag{1}$$

Then a single $m$-fat point has

$$H_{mP}(\boldsymbol a)=F_m(\boldsymbol a). \tag{2}$$

To prove this, independently change coordinates in each factor so that $P$ is its first coordinate point. Its multihomogeneous ideal is generated by all coordinates except the first coordinate in each block. A block-degree-$a_i$ monomial with total exponent $b$ on those vanishing coordinates has one prescribed exponent $a_i-b$ on the first coordinate, and $\binom{b+n_i-2}{n_i-2}$ choices on the others. A monomial survives modulo the $m$th power of the point ideal exactly when the sum of all these transverse exponents is less than $m$. Counting them gives (1). In a $\mathbb P^0$ factor no transverse coordinate exists, which explains the separate convention.

This proof applies to every single point, not just a generic one.

2. Formula for two equally fat generic points

For each active factor $n_i\ge2$, two general points have distinct coordinates in that factor. An independent projective transformation sends the two factor coordinates to the first and second coordinate points. This simultaneous normalization is therefore valid on a nonempty open subset of the space of pairs. Call the normalized point ideals $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak q$.

Define a bivariate block polynomial as follows. If $n_i=1$, put $B_i(u,v)=1$. If $n_i=2$, put

$$B_i(u,v)=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=a_i}u^\beta v^\alpha.$$

If $n_i\ge3$, put

$$B_i(u,v)=
\sum_{\substack{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0\\\alpha+\beta+\gamma=a_i}}
\binom{\gamma+n_i-3}{n_i-3}
u^{\beta+\gamma}v^{\alpha+\gamma}. \tag{3}$$

Here $\alpha$ is the exponent of the first block variable, $\beta$ that of the second, and $\gamma$ the total exponent on the remaining variables. The two exponents of $u,v$ record the respective orders of vanishing at the normalized points.

Set

$$O_m(\boldsymbol a)=
\sum_{p=0}^{m-1}\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}
[u^p v^q]\prod_{i=1}^t B_i(u,v). \tag{4}$$

Then the requested exact answer for two points is

$$\boxed{H_{mP+mQ}(\boldsymbol a)=2F_m(\boldsymbol a)-O_m(\boldsymbol a).} \tag{5}$$

Indeed, the two normalized ideals and their ordinary powers are monomial ideals. A monomial is outside $\mathfrak p^m\cap\mathfrak q^m$ precisely when it is outside at least one of the two powers. The two individual survivor counts both equal (1). Their overlap is exactly (4), by the orders recorded in (3). Inclusion-exclusion proves (5).

All sums in (1)–(5) are finite and explicit. They require only binomial coefficients and multiplication of truncated polynomials, with no generic matrix-rank decision. The ordinary powers here are powers of the point ideals, exactly as defined in the catalogue; we have not replaced them by powers of the intersection.

3. Consequences and checks at boundary degrees

If $a_i\ge m-1$ for every active factor, all monomials in the $N$ local transverse variables of total degree below $m$ are allowed. Hence

$$F_m(\boldsymbol a)=\binom{N+m-1}{N}. \tag{6}$$

Let $A=\sum_{i:n_i\ge2}a_i$. A monomial counted in the overlap has total vanishing orders $p,q$ satisfying

$$p+q=A+\sum_{i:n_i\ge3}\gamma_i\ge A.$$

Thus $A\ge2m-1$ makes overlap impossible when $p,q<m$. Under that condition and the block bounds for (6),

$$H_{mP+mQ}(\boldsymbol a)=2\binom{N+m-1}{N}, \tag{7}$$

the length of two $m$-fat points. These are sufficient stabilization bounds, not a claim of optimality for every product.

On $\mathbb P^1$ there is a particularly simple reduction: after normalization the ideals are $(x_1)$ and $(x_0)$, so their powered intersection is $(x_0^m x_1^m)$. Consequently

$$H_{mP+mQ}(a)=\min\{a+1,2m\},$$

in agreement with (5). On $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, two double points have value six at multidegree $(2,2)$; two triple points have value nine at $(2,2)$ and twelve at $(3,3)$. These values illustrate both a nonsaturated and a saturated multidegree.

When all $a_i=0$, formula (5) gives one, not two: the only constant monomial is counted in both individual survivor sets and removed once by overlap. For one point on a product of $\mathbb P^0$ factors, formula (2) gives one for every multidegree, as it should.

4. Exact verification and limitations

The attached standard-library program implements (1)–(5) by one- and two-dimensional coefficient convolution. Independently, it enumerates every multigraded monomial and tests membership in the normalized point-ideal powers directly by summing exponents. It compares these methods for multiplicities $1\le m\le4$, block degrees $0\le a_i\le5$, and factor-size lists

$$ (2),(3),(4),(2,2),(2,3),(3,3),(1,2,2),(2,2,2).$$

It also checks the $\mathbb P^1$ closed formula, the stated examples and stabilization conditions. Run `python3 verify_fat_points.py`; exact counts and ranges appear in `verification.json`. No approximate rank, interpolation fit, or extrapolation is used in the proof.

The formulas cover all parameter values within $s=1,2$; the finite checks only validate their implementations. They do not calculate the Hilbert function for arbitrary numbers of fat points, and no general Cohen–Macaulay assumption is used.
