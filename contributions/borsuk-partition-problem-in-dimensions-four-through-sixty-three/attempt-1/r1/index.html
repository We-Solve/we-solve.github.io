Borsuk Partition Problem in Dimensions Four Through Sixty-Three

OpenAI's claim

Nine-dimensional diameter covering: The projector configuration of lines in $\mathbb R^4$, viewed in the nine-dimensional space of trace-one symmetric matrices, is claimed to require more than ten smaller-diameter covering sets. This compact configuration refutes Borsuk’s assertion in dimension nine.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
In the nine-dimensional affine space $\{A\in\operatorname{Sym}_4(\mathbb R):\operatorname{tr}A=1\}$ with Frobenius distance, set $X=\{uu^{\mathsf T}:u\in\mathbb R^4,\ |u|=1\}$. The claim is that $X$ is compact, $\operatorname{diam}X=\sqrt2$, and there do not exist ten subsets $X_1,\ldots,X_{10}$ covering $X$ with every $\operatorname{diam}X_i<\sqrt2$. The article also claims that, for each integer $d\ge9$, there is a compact $Y_d\subset\mathbb R^d$ of diameter $\sqrt2$ admitting no cover by $d+1$ subsets of smaller diameter. The extension adjoins $d-9$ points at diameter distance from one another and from the translated projector set. The dimensions four through eight remain outside these counterexamples.

Outline of the argument

Nine-dimensional diameter covering: A smaller-diameter cover is enlarged to an open cover and converted into nonnegative coordinate functions on projective three-space. Orthogonal lines must have disjoint positive supports. Extending the map to positive semidefinite matrices produces an odd sphere map; its mod-two degree and an equality analysis of the support complex contradict the possible ten-label structure.
