A determinant route.
The source qualification does not fix the shifts or Lie type of its $Q\widetilde Q$ relation. A concrete mechanism worth testing is the determinant identity behind rank-two connection coefficients. Write $[u,v]=u_1v_2-u_2v_1$ for vectors in $\mathbb C^2$. Direct expansion gives \[ [a,b][c,d]-[a,c][b,d]+[a,d][b,c]=0. \] For example, when $[a,b]\ne0$, write $c=\alpha a+\beta b$, $d=\gamma a+\delta b$. The three terms are respectively $(\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma)[a,b]^2$, $+\beta\gamma[a,b]^2$, and $-\alpha\delta[a,b]^2$. They cancel; the same polynomial identity holds without the nonzero assumption by expansion.
Application to a restricted equation.
If $u,v$ solve $y''=V(x)y$, their Wronskian $W(u,v)=uv'-u'v$ satisfies $W'=uVv-Vuv=0$. Evaluating four solutions at a common point as vectors $(y,y')$ therefore gives the displayed relation with constant Wronskians. It is invariant under changing the evaluation point. However rescaling any solution rescales the corresponding factors: normalizations cannot be ignored when interpreting these as spectral determinants.
