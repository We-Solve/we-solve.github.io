Erdős Problem 253: a noncomplete sequence meeting every progression

1) Statement and scope

Suppose $a_{i+1}/a_i\to1$, and every infinite arithmetic progression contains infinitely many sums of distinct $a_i$. Must all sufficiently large integers be such sums? No. We reconstruct the irrational-rotation mechanism of Cassels's counterexample, with weaker quantitative bounds sufficient for the full negative answer. Our sequence itself meets every progression infinitely often, while its subset sums have upper density at most $1/4$.

2) A rotation with quantitative covering

Write $\|t\|$ for distance to the nearest integer, and put $\alpha=\sqrt2$. For every positive integer $h$, \[ \|h\alpha\|\geq\frac1{4h}. \] Indeed, if $v$ is a nearest integer, the nonzero integer $|2h^2-v^2|$ is at least one, while $h\sqrt2+v<4h$.

Fix a positive integer $u$ and let $\theta=u\alpha$. Then $\|q\theta\|\geq1/(4uq)$. We use the elementary continued-fraction fact that consecutive convergents $p/q,p'/q'$ satisfy $|\theta-p/q|<1/(qq')$ and $q'>q$ (after the initial terms). Our lower bound implies $q'<4uq$. For every sufficiently large integer $L$, choose consecutive denominators with $q\leq L<q'$. Thus $q>L/(4u)$.

Since $p,q$ are coprime, the residues $jp/q$, $0\leq j<q$, are the complete $1/q$-grid on the circle. Their distances from $j\theta$ are less than $1/q'<1/q$. Every point of the circle is therefore within $2/q<8u/L$ of some $j\theta$ with $0\leq j<L$. We will use the slightly weaker bound $12u/L$.

3) A thin set meeting every progression with short gaps

Fix $\gamma=3/4$ and define \[ D=\{n\geq1:\|\alpha n\|<n^{-\gamma}\}. \] Consider any progression $u\ell+v$, with $u\geq1$ and fixed integer $v$. For a large integer $X$, put $L=\lceil K_uX^\gamma\rceil$, where $K_u\geq24u(3u)^\gamma$. Apply the covering result to the target $-\alpha(uX+v)$ modulo one. Some $0\leq j<L$ satisfies \[ \|\alpha(u(X+j)+v)\| \leq\frac{12u}{L} \leq\frac12(3uX)^{-\gamma}. \] For large $X$, $L\leq X$ and $0<n=u(X+j)+v\leq3uX$. Hence the last quantity is strictly less than $n^{-\gamma}$, so $n\in D$. Letting $X\to\infty$ proves that every such progression contains infinitely many elements of $D$.

With $u=1,v=0$, the same argument gives an element of $D$ in every interval $[X,X+O(X^\gamma)]$ for large $X$. If $d_i$ are the increasing elements of $D$, applying it with $X=d_i+1$ gives $d_{i+1}-d_i=O(d_i^\gamma)$, and therefore $d_{i+1}/d_i\to1$.

4) The approximation errors are summable

For distinct $n,m\in D\cap[X,2X)$, \[ \frac1{4|n-m|} \leq\|\alpha(n-m)\| \leq\|\alpha n\|+\|\alpha m\|<2X^{-\gamma}. \] Thus these elements are separated by at least $X^\gamma/8$, and there are at most $1+8X^{1-\gamma}$ of them. Consequently \[ \sum_{n\in D\cap[X,2X)}\|\alpha n\| \leq X^{-\gamma}+8X^{1-2\gamma}. \] Summing over dyadic $X$ converges because $\gamma>1/2$. Choose $N_0$ so that the tail sum is less than $1/8$, and put $A=D\cap[N_0,\infty)$. Deleting this finite prefix preserves the progression property and the consecutive-ratio limit.

5) An obstruction to completeness

For every finite subset $F\subseteq A$, the triangle inequality on the circle gives \[ \left\|\alpha\sum_{a\in F}a\right\| \leq\sum_{a\in F}\|\alpha a\| <\frac18. \] The set of positive subset sums is therefore contained in $T=\{n\geq1:\|\alpha n\|<1/8\}$. Irrational rotation is uniformly distributed, so $T$ has natural density $1/4$. One standard justification is that for each nonzero integer $h$, the geometric sum $\sum_{n\leq N}e^{2\pi ih\alpha n}$ is bounded independently of $N$; Weyl's criterion then gives uniform distribution, including the two intervals defining $T$. Thus the subset sums have upper density at most $1/4$ and cannot contain all sufficiently large integers.

At the same time every progression contains infinitely many elements of $A$, each of which is already a one-term subset sum. All the hypotheses in the problem are satisfied.

(Szeged) 21 $1960$, 111--124, Section 3, https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/13906/1/math_021_fasc_003_004_111-124.pdf. Cassels proves stronger quantitative conclusions. This is a reconstruction of the known counterexample mechanism using the standard continued-fraction estimates and Weyl criterion stated above; it makes no novelty or formal-verification claim. The construction answers the full question in the negative.
