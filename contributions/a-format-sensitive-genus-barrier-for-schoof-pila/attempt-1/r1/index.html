A representation-sensitive attempted route

Question 14 mentions the roots-of-unity step in Pila's algorithm, an exponential dependence on genus, and the desire for a linear one. Its adjacent remark concerns an $\ell^{2g}$-element group and avoiding a complete projective model. It does not say that an explicit list of all those elements is required. We examine the natural shortcut of replacing enumeration by a sparse representation, keeping that distinction visible. The following results concern representations; they are not a proved improvement of Pila's algorithm.
The explicit-list barrier and a compressed example.
Fix a prime $\ell$ and $g\ge1$, and put $N=\ell^{2g}$. Over a field of characteristic different from $\ell$, the polynomial $X^N-1$ has exactly $N$ distinct roots in an algebraic closure. It splits there by the definition of algebraic closure. Its derivative is $NX^{N-1}$, which cannot vanish at a nonzero root, so none is repeated. Hence a format listing every root with at least one output symbol per entry has length at least $N$. No algorithm in that format can have total running time linear in $g$, because writing those symbols alone takes at least $N$ steps.

This lower bound disappears for a different task. The sparse expression $X^{\ell^{2g}}-1$ uses an exponent with $O(g\log\ell)$ bits. Over $\mathbb C$, one primitive root is described exactly by $\exp(2\pi i/N)$: its $k$th power is one precisely when $N\mid k$, by the period $2\pi$ of the complex exponential on the imaginary axis. The roots of exact order $N$ are also described by \[ C(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}X^{j\ell^{2g-1}}. \] Indeed $(X^{N/\ell}-1)C(X)=X^N-1$. At a root of $X^{N/\ell}-1$ the sum equals $\ell\ne0$, so the roots of $C$ are exactly the $N$th roots whose order does not divide $N/\ell$. Every divisor of the prime power $N$ is a power of $\ell$; these are therefore precisely the roots of order $N$. For fixed $\ell$ this sparse description has size $O(g)$, while its dense degree is $N-N/\ell$. We do not assert that writing this description computes an embedded finite-field root or permits all subsequent operations in time linear in $g$.

The same obstruction in a torsion-space abstraction.
If a relevant torsion group is supplied abstractly as the vector space $V=\mathbb F_\ell^{2g}$, its cardinality is $\ell^{2g}$: each of $2g$ coordinates has $\ell$ independent choices. A linear operator can instead be represented by $(2g)^2$ field entries. To see sufficiency, its values on the coordinate basis determine its value on $\sum a_ie_i$ by linearity, and any matrix defines such a map. This compression is genuine once coordinates and the operator are given. It does not construct that basis in geometric coordinates or compute the operator's entries from a curve. In particular, substituting an abstract matrix for geometric torsion is circular if recovering that matrix was the original hard step.
Where the shortcut stops.
A sparse polynomial supplies a relation, not a chosen compatible root in the required extension field with the operations and embeddings needed by the point-counting algorithm. Listing roots forces an exponential output, but the source explicitly contemplates avoiding large geometric representations. Thus the output-size argument cannot rule out the requested compressed improvement. Conversely the short formula above does not implement it: the missing step is to recover the required arithmetic action from that short representation with proved costs in $g$, the field size and $\ell$.
