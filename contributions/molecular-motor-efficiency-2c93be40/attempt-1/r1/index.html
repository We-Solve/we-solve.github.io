Molecular-motor efficiency

Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.

Target and scope

Catalogue definitions and assumptions.
Consider a motor with step length $a$, mean velocity $v$ against force $f>0$, ATP-consumption rate $J_c$, and chemical free energy per ATP $\Delta\mu>0$. A finite-state continuous-time Markov network satisfies local detailed balance and includes futile hydrolysis cycles and backward steps. At steady state define mechanical power $P=fv$, chemical power $J_c\Delta\mu$, efficiency $\eta=P/(J_c\Delta\mu)$ and displacement diffusivity $D_x=\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{Var} X_t/(2t)$.
Original target.
For a specified motor architecture and biochemical rate constraints, characterize the attainable frontier \[\max\eta\quad\text{subject to}\quad fv\ge P_0>0,\qquad 2D_x/v^2\le r_0,\qquad Q\in\mathcal Q.\] Here $r_0$ bounds long-time relative positional noise per unit time and $\mathcal Q$ specifies measured rate ranges and fuel conditions. Predict where the actual motor lies on that frontier under changing load and nucleotide concentrations.
Catalogue alternative formulations.
(i) For a tightly coupled unicyclic motor, derive analytic load and affinity trade-offs and compare maximum efficiency with efficiency at maximum power. (ii) For multicyclic motors, quantify slippage and futile fuel use using jointly measured stepping and ATP turnover. Team motion and interacting motors require a separate larger model.

Attempted answer

Proposition: For a finite stationary motor network with positive paired transition rates and local detailed balance, nonnegative entropy production implies $P_{\rm out}\leq P_{\rm chem}$ when these are the only work reservoirs and the internal free energy is stationary. If nonzero mechanical current is required, reversible equality cannot hold in this finite model.
Proof: Let $a=\pi_iq_{ij}$ and $b=\pi_jq_{ji}$. Each edge contribution $(a-b)\log(a/b)$ is nonnegative and vanishes only when $a=b$. Summation, with local detailed balance and cancellation of stationary state-potential terms, gives $(P_{\rm chem}-P_{\rm out})/T\geq0$. Equality forces all edge currents to vanish. ∎

What remains unresolved

This is an efficiency ceiling, not an attainable power--precision frontier. Hidden chemical cycles, external forcing and singular limiting models require separate accounting.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
