Positive Maps and Multipartite Bound Entanglement

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
$M_d(\mathbb C)$ is the matrix algebra, with Hilbert--Schmidt inner product $\langle F,G\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(F^\dagger G)$. A map is positive if it sends positive semidefinite matrices to positive semidefinite matrices, and completely positive if every tensor amplification by an identity map is positive. The source uses Hermitian orthonormal bases $F^{(i)}_\mu$ and real coefficients $\lambda^{(i)}_\mu$. For the four-qubit lattice states, let $\sigma_0=I_2$ and $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$ be Pauli matrices; set $|\psi_+\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b=1}^2|a,b\rangle_A|a,b\rangle_B$ and $|\psi_{\alpha\beta}\rangle=(I_4\otimes\sigma_\alpha\otimes\sigma_\beta)|\psi_+\rangle$. Here $\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}^2$ and $|I|$ is its cardinality. PPT means positivity after partial transposition across the $A|B$ partition.

The request is to generalize this family, for example by relaxing the orthonormal-basis identity, and to study the entanglement-detection properties of the states $\rho_I$.

For $i=1,2$, let $$\Lambda_i[X]=\sum_{\mu=0}^{d_i^2-1}\lambda_\mu^{(i)}F_\mu^{(i)}XF_\mu^{(i)},$$ where the $F_\mu^{(i)}$ form Hermitian, Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal bases of $M_{d_i}(\mathbb{C})$. Can this family be generalized by relaxing the orthonormal-basis hypothesis? For $$\rho_I=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in I}|\psi_{\alpha\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\alpha\beta}|,$$ determine the properties of these states and, in particular, whether their entanglement can be detected by other criteria.

A controlled relaxation of orthonormality.
Let $F_1,\ldots,F_{d^2}$ be a Hermitian Hilbert--Schmidt orthonormal basis and let $O$ be a real $N\times d^2$ matrix with $O^TO=I$, where $N\geq d^2$. Define $G_a=\sum_\mu O_{a\mu}F_\mu$. These are a possibly redundant, nonorthogonal Hermitian family and satisfy \[ \sum_aG_aXG_a=\sum_{\mu,\nu}(O^TO)_{\mu\nu}F_\mu XF_\nu =\sum_\mu F_\mu XF_\mu=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I. \] The last identity can be checked with diagonal matrix units and the symmetric/antisymmetric Hermitian combinations of off-diagonal units, and is preserved by real orthogonal basis changes. Duplicating each $F_\mu/\sqrt2$ gives a concrete redundant example. Thus any proof using only this unweighted completeness identity can retain that step for these frames. It cannot automatically retain a theorem with independently prescribed weighted coefficients: mixing introduces cross terms unless the coefficient matrix is transformed too.
A lattice-state entanglement test.
Each $|\psi_{\alpha\beta}\rangle$ is maximally entangled across $\mathbb C^4|\mathbb C^4$. For normalized product vectors $u\otimes v$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $|\langle\psi_{\alpha\beta}|u\otimes v\rangle|^2\leq1/4$. Convexity extends this to separable states. Orthogonality of Pauli matrices makes the sixteen Bell vectors orthonormal, hence \[ \langle\psi_{\alpha\beta}|\rho_I|\psi_{\alpha\beta}\rangle=1/|I|\quad((\alpha,\beta)\in I). \] Consequently $|I|<4$ is an elementary entanglement certificate. At $|I|=4$ it becomes inconclusive, not a separability proof; at $|I|=16$ the state is $I_{16}/16$ and separable.
