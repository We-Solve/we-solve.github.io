Special cubulations: finite certificates and the unbounded search gap

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A cube complex is built by gluing Euclidean cubes along faces; nonpositive curvature is expressed by the flag condition on vertex links. A special cube complex has embedded, two-sided hyperplanes without the self-intersection, self-osculation, and inter-osculation pathologies excluded in the standard definition. For a finite graph $\Gamma$, its right-angled Artin group is $A_\Gamma=\langle v\in V(\Gamma)\mid uv=vu\text{ for }uv\in E(\Gamma)\rangle$. The input triangulation gives a finite description of $M$.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Find an algorithm that, given a triangulated hyperbolic $3$-manifold $M$, decides whether $M$ is homotopy equivalent to a special cube complex; equivalently, decide whether $\pi_1(M)$ embeds in a right-angled Artin group.

The original recognition question

Futer's report, printed pages 493--495, distinguishes a special finite cover from a special cubulation of the given manifold itself. Its Problem 5 asks for the latter recognition algorithm. The title's word "virtually" cannot be used to answer that different question by invoking virtual specialness. The report also distinguishes one specific Dehn cubulation from other, unrelated cubulations of the same homotopy type. The primary paper abstract on alternating links describes explicit covers; that result supplies neither the requested decision procedure nor a bound on all candidate cubulations. We attempt a finite-certificate search and identify its missing completeness and negative-termination steps.

Checking an explicitly supplied cubical candidate

Suppose a finite cube complex $X$ is given by its cells and face maps, together with finite compatible subdivisions where needed below. For this auxiliary verifier use the strong convention excluding both direct and indirect self-osculation; this suffices for ordinary specialness, but is not asserted to include every conventionally special candidate. There are finitely many corner and midcube incidences, so the following tests terminate.

At each vertex form its finite link: its vertices are edge germs and its simplices are corners of incident cubes. First check that this is a simplicial complex. Enumerate its finite vertex subsets and test the flag condition: whenever every pair in a subset is joined by an edge, that subset must be a simplex. This tests precisely the combinatorial nonpositive-curvature condition in the problem definition; the enumeration need not be efficient in dimension.

Join opposite edge directions across every square, producing the equivalence classes dual to hyperplanes. Build the corresponding hyperplane complexes from the midcubes and their specified gluings. Cut each ambient cube at its midplanes and subdivide compatibly; one can supply explicitly the resulting simplicial maps from the hyperplane complexes to the subdivided $X$. Verify every incidence in these finite data. To test whether one such simplicial map is an embedding, test injectivity on its vertices. Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, each simplex then maps affinely injectively to the simplex spanned by its distinct image vertices. Two image simplices intersect in the face spanned by their common image vertices, and vertex injectivity makes this exactly their common domain face. Thus the entire map is injective; continuity and compactness of its finite domain make it an embedding into the Hausdorff complex $X$.

For an embedded hyperplane, each local midcube has two normal sides. Record whether a gluing preserves or reverses their signs. Assign one local sign, propagate it over the finite adjacency data, and reject if a loop forces both signs at a location. A consistent assignment chooses the normal sides globally and gives a product two-sided neighborhood; conversely such a neighborhood induces a consistent assignment. This is the usual finite sign-consistency test for two-sidedness, with lower-dimensional incidences included in the supplied local gluing data.

Finally enumerate pairs of distinct incident edge germs at each vertex. If they are dual to the same hyperplane and do not bound a square corner, reject self-osculation under the stated strong convention. For two different hyperplanes record whether they cross in any cube; if so, reject a pair of their germs that meets at a vertex without a square corner. This is exactly the forbidden inter-osculation pattern. Every event being tested is a hyperplane, cube, or edge-germ incidence in the given finite complex; the tests therefore check the declared conditions exhaustively. This verifies a particular candidate, not its relation to the input manifold and not existence of another candidate.

A completely checkable sufficient homotopy certificate

An additional finite certificate can relate a candidate to the input: give a triangulation $T$ obtained by explicitly listed simplicial subdivisions of the input triangulation, identify a subcomplex $K$ with a specified triangulation of $X$, and give elementary collapse steps taking $T$ onto $K$. All subdivision, isomorphism, and free-face conditions are finite incidence checks. Here an elementary collapse removes a maximal simplex $\sigma$ and a codimension-one face $\tau$ contained in no other maximal simplex.

For completeness, the collapse is a strong deformation retraction. Label the vertex opposite $\tau$ by 0 and the other vertices by $1,\ldots,d$. For barycentric coordinates $(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d)$ on $\sigma$, let $m=\min_{1\le i\le d}\lambda_i$. The homotopy \[ \lambda_0(t)=\lambda_0+d t m,\qquad \lambda_i(t)=\lambda_i-tm\quad(1\le i\le d),\qquad 0\le t\le1 \] preserves nonnegativity and the sum one. At $t=1$ some coordinate $\lambda_i$, $i\ge1$, is zero, so the image lies in a face other than the removed facet. All those remaining faces have $m=0$ and are fixed throughout. The freeness condition means that the interior of the removed facet touches no other simplex; the formula therefore glues to the identity on the rest of the complex. It is continuous because the minimum of finitely many continuous coordinates is continuous. This proves the claimed retraction, including $d=1$. Composing the finitely many retractions gives $|T|\simeq|K|=|X|$. Since a verified subdivision does not alter the underlying space, the input manifold is homotopy equivalent to the verified special complex $X$.

This gives a sound positive certificate of the source property for inputs admitting such a collapse description. It is not asserted that every homotopy equivalence to a special cube complex admits this particular certificate without additional expansions or constructions.

The actual failure of the proposed exhaustive algorithm

One can enumerate all finite candidate descriptions and finite certificates above by increasing description length and run their finite tests. The search halts with a justified yes whenever one of these certificates occurs. It does not give a justified no at any finite cutoff: a failure at size $N$ rules out only the finite list checked, not a certificate of size $N+1$ or a different kind of homotopy equivalence. We have supplied neither a computable bound on necessary certificate size nor a theorem that all positive instances have this restricted collapse certificate. These are separate gaps, not merely implementation details.

Likewise, checking finitely many proposed images of group generators and verifying the defining relators proves only that a homomorphism is well defined. It does not prove injectivity. For example sending all generators to the identity satisfies every relator of every presentation but kills every nontrivial element. A bounded search for kernel words cannot conclude that the kernel is trivial just because it finds none below its cutoff. The source's asserted RAAG reformulation does not eliminate this missing certificate argument.

Model-assisted audit and remaining scope

The audit checked the original versus virtual property, finite incidence conventions, the explicit collapse homotopy, and the two different ways a candidate search can be incomplete. The finite verifiers are standard elementary constructions, not a new general recognition algorithm. No finite-cover computation, normal-surface enumeration, or theorem prover was executed. The source's proposed normal-surface route still needs a certificate of the right wall structure and a termination bound; neither is proved here.
