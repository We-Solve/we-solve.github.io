Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A channel maps transmitted signals to noisy received signals. Channel capacity is the maximum reliable information rate under a specified input-power and channel-information model; spectral efficiency normalizes rate by bandwidth. Delay spread describes variation in arrival times of channel paths. Spatial focusing concentrates energy spatially, and time-reversal transmission uses a reversed channel response. Multi-user capacity is a region of simultaneously achievable user rates.

Research attempt: User-dependent spectral gains can reverse a flat-channel comparison

Take two real orthogonal tones with independent unit-variance Gaussian noise and two users, each with total power $P>0$. For Gaussian inputs, the sum mutual information on a tone with power gains $g_i$ (channel amplitudes $\sqrt{g_i}$) and input powers $p_i$ is $\tfrac12\log(1+\sum_i g_ip_i)$. This follows by subtracting the noise entropy from the output entropy; a real Gaussian of variance $v$ has entropy $\tfrac12\log(2\pi e v)$, obtained by integrating its log density.

In the flat case each user's gain is one on both tones. For any power allocation let the aggregate tone powers be $s_1,s_2$, so $s_1+s_2\le2P$. The Gaussian sum-information is $\tfrac12\log((1+s_1)(1+s_2))\le\log(1+P)$, since their product is maximized at equal powers (expand the squared difference). Equality is attainable while respecting both users' budgets.

Now give user one gains $(2,0)$ and user two gains $(0,2)$. Each user's total spectral gain is still two, matching the flat case. Put each user's full power on its own nonzero-gain tone. The resulting sum information is $\log(1+2P)>\log(1+P)$. Thus with user-specific selectivity and adaptive allocation, the assertion that frequency selectivity cannot improve this sum-rate benchmark is false. The improvement comes from orthogonal favorable frequencies; it does not arise in every selective channel.

For contrast, with one user and forced equal powers on two tones, concavity gives $\frac12\log(1+g_1P/2)+\frac12\log(1+g_2P/2) \le\log(1+(g_1+g_2)P/4)$, so equal gains maximize this different benchmark. The elementary inequality is again the product bound at fixed sum. These two opposite conclusions show why power allocation, user dependence and the preserved channel statistic must be fixed before making the source's comparison.

No assertion about arbitrary coding, finite-block reliability, training overhead, interception, or actual time-reversal hardware follows from these chosen Gaussian-input mutual-information calculations.
