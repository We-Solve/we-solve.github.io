First-Principles Derivation of Adaptive-Resolution Molecular Dynamics

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
Adaptive-resolution molecular dynamics couples an atomistic region and a coarse-grained region using a switching function $w\in[0,1]$. For molecular centers $X_\alpha,X_\beta$, the report's force interpolation has atomistic weight $w(X_\alpha)w(X_\beta)$ and coarse-grained weight $1-w(X_\alpha)w(X_\beta)$. A potential interpolation generally yields additional derivatives of the spatial weights, hence drift forces. A first-principles derivation would obtain the prescribed interpolation and statistical ensemble from microscopic statistical mechanics rather than choosing them ad hoc.
Source scope and qualifications.
The report supplies an argument against a particular potential-interpolation scheme and leaves a first-principles force derivation open. It does not establish that every conceivable energy-based coarse-graining is impossible; that stronger conclusion is not part of the target.

The drift-force calculation.
Let two molecular positions be $x,y$, let $a(x,y)=w(x)w(y)$ and set $U=aU_A(x-y)+(1-a)U_C(x-y)$. Differentiation gives \[ -\nabla_xU=aF_A+(1-a)F_C-(\nabla_xa)(U_A-U_C), \] where $F_A=-\nabla_xU_A$ and similarly for $F_C$. The desired interpolation omits the last term, which vanishes only under extra restrictions such as constant $a$ or equality of the potentials on the switching region.
An explicit integrability obstruction.
In one dimension choose $U_A(r)=r^2/2$, $U_C=0$ and smooth $w$ taking values in $[0,1]$. The prescribed pair forces are $F_x=-a(x,y)(x-y)$ and $F_y=a(x,y)(x-y)$. A twice differentiable common potential would require $\partial_yF_x=\partial_xF_y$. Subtracting yields \[ \partial_yF_x-\partial_xF_y=-(x-y)(\partial_xa+\partial_ya). \] Choose a globally smooth $[0,1]$-valued $w$ equal to $z$ on $(1/4,3/4)$. For $x,y$ in that interior switching interval, this is $-(x-y)(x+y)$, nonzero when $x\ne y$. The forces obey action-reaction but are not conservative on that open region.
Source-level limit.
This is a concrete obstruction to the specified position-weighted force law admitting an ordinary two-coordinate potential, not a theorem against every energy-based adaptive model. Deriving its stationary ensemble or compensating reservoir forces from microscopic statistical mechanics is separate. The first-principles derivation requested in the source is not obtained merely by proving nonconservativity; it remains the first substantial gap after the drift calculation.
