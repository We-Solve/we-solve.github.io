Statement recorded in the supplied source.
A.5 Kurzhanski, Alexander

Among the central topics of modern control theory are problems of control synthesis for complex systems. These include problems of control under uncertainty and conflict as well as those under complex constraints. The solutions to such problems are well formalizable within Hamiltonian techniques and Dynamic Programming ideas, being as a rule reduced to the solution of HJB and HJBI partial differential equations or/and variational inequalities. An example where such methods are successfully applied are problems of forward and backward reachability for uncertain systems with further passage to control synthesis, safety verification, measurement feedback and related issues. The advent of HJB methods for continuous systems is due to the introduction of appropriate theories for generalized viscosity-type solutions and their equivalents. Another important topic is the solution to problems in dynamics and control for set-valued systems in terms of Hamiltonian formalism and Dynamic Programming. At present there is a sharp necessity for a breakthrough in numerical methods for solving equations and variational inequalities of the HJB-HJBI types motivated by problems of the above. There is also an interest in numerical methods for the comparison principle in HJB theory, which allows to calculate upper and lower bounds to exact solutions of HJB equations. Another perspective is the calculation of set-valued solutions to problems in evolution dynamics, estimation and control. Together with my colleagues we have developed an ellipsoidal calculus aimed at such problems and closely connected to HJB theory. The calculus is applicable to systems with original linear structure, but its methods allow effective calculations in high dimensions as well as computer animation in high dimensions through computer windows. For nonlinear systems some comparison methods were indicated. 8

The discussion of such methods as well as those for stochastic dynamics are within my primary interests at the AIM workshop. [S2]

Research attempt: Directionwise exact support does not give one exact ellipsoid

For a positive semidefinite matrix $Q$, let $E(Q)=\{Q^{1/2}u:\|u\|\le1\}$, including degenerate ellipsoids. Its support in direction $l$ is $h_{E(Q)}(l)=\sqrt{l^TQl}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, attained by a vector parallel to $Q^{1/2}l$ when nonzero. The support of a Minkowski sum is the sum of supports because its two maximizations are independent.

For $a=l^TQ_1l$, $b=l^TQ_2l$ and $\lambda>0$, \[ (\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2\le(1+1/\lambda)a+(1+\lambda)b, \] since $a/\lambda+\lambda b-2\sqrt{ab}=(\sqrt{a/\lambda}-\sqrt{\lambda b})^2$. Thus $E(Q_1)+E(Q_2)$ is contained in $E(Q(\lambda))$, where $Q(\lambda)=(1+1/\lambda)Q_1+(1+\lambda)Q_2$. For a direct containment justification, every point of the sum satisfies all support inequalities of the outer ellipsoid. A point outside a closed convex ellipsoid violates one: using its nearest point, the difference vector defines a separating supporting hyperplane. The nearest-point first-order inequality follows by differentiating squared distance along segments in the ellipsoid.

If both traces are positive, differentiating $\operatorname{tr}Q(\lambda)$ shows its minimum occurs at $\lambda=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}Q_1/\operatorname{tr}Q_2}$; the derivative changes from negative to positive there. But equality of support in a direction requires $\lambda=\sqrt{a/b}$ when $a,b>0$, generally a different value. Take $Q_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $Q_2=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$. Their sum set is the square $[-1,1]^2$, whereas the trace-minimizing outer ellipsoid is the radius-$\sqrt2$ disk. It agrees with the square's support in diagonal directions and strictly overestimates it on either axis.

Therefore assembling exact directional answers or optimizing one scalar shape criterion does not produce a single exact ellipsoidal reachable set. This is already visible for the linear control system whose two independent bounded integrated inputs generate the two coordinate segments. General nonlinear reachability requires additional error propagation not addressed by this sum.
