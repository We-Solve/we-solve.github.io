Problem 15 --- Round 3

1) ROUND-3 OBJECTIVE

(C) Obstruction / correction. I continue the obstruction/correction track from Round 2. Using the Round 2 equivalence between \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{n}{p_n} \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m}, \] the natural next gap is no longer the prime-indexed series itself, but rather the exact amount of discrepancy cancellation needed for the parity sequence \[ \varepsilon_m := (-1)^{\pi(m)}. \]

The most promising Round 3 direction is therefore:

  • derive an exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion for convergence in terms of the discrepancy \[ A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x}\varepsilon_m, \] and
  • show that the Round 2 sufficient condition is essentially sharp in the model setting by constructing an explicit equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy as small as \[ O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) \] for which the weighted series \[ \sum \frac{\varepsilon_m}{m\log m} \] still diverges.

This strictly advances Round 2 in two ways: it replaces a merely sufficient discrepancy condition by an exact one, and it rules out an entire stronger class of discrepancy-only proof strategies.

2) Round-2 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round 2 results as fixed input.

  1. Round 2 Theorem 5.7 (Said--Tao equivalence). There exists a constant $C\in\mathbb R$ such that \[ \sum_{n\le x} (-1)^n \frac{n}{p_n} = \frac12 \sum_{3\le m\le x\log x} \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m} + C + o(1). \] Hence the original Erdős series converges if and only if \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m} \] converges.

  2. Round 2 Proposition 5.8 (one sufficient discrepancy criterion). If \[ A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log\log x)^{1+\varepsilon}}\right) \] for some $\varepsilon>0$, then the parity series, and hence the original Erdős series, converges.

  3. Round 2 Proposition 5.9 (equidistribution alone is insufficient). There exists an equidistributed sign sequence for which \[ \sum \frac{\varepsilon_m}{m\log m} \] diverges.

I will not re-prove these results; Round 3 begins from them.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-3)

The new ingredients are the following.

  1. Exact Abel criterion. The parity series is convergent if and only if a certain discrepancy-weighted series \[ \sum_{m\ge 3} A(m)\left(\frac1{m\log m}-\frac1{(m+1)\log(m+1)}\right) \] converges. Equivalently, it is convergent if and only if the improper integral \[ \int_3^\infty A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)\,dt \] converges.

  2. A strictly stronger general sufficient criterion. If $L(x)\to\infty$ and \[ \int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty, \] then \[ A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right) \] already implies convergence. This sharpens Round 2 Proposition 5.8.

  3. Near-sharp model obstruction. I construct an explicit equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy \[ O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) \] whose $1/(m\log m)$-weighted series diverges. Thus one cannot hope to prove convergence from any discrepancy estimate of that scale alone.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-3)

After Round 2, the remaining gap is:

What quantitative control on \[ A(x)=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^{\pi(m)} \] is actually equivalent to convergence of \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m}\,? \]

The Round 2 result only gave a sufficient condition. To advance beyond that, I will do two things.

  1. Starting from Round 2 Theorem 5.7, I will derive an exact summation-by-parts identity for the parity series, yielding a necessary-and-sufficient convergence criterion in terms of $A(x)$.

  2. I will then build an explicit model sign sequence showing that even the much stronger discrepancy bound \[ A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) \] does not force convergence in general. This strengthens the Round 2 obstruction, which only ruled out plain equidistribution.

This attacks the exact Round 2 gap: it identifies the correct kernel against which the discrepancy must cancel, and it proves that a whole class of weaker discrepancy-only strategies cannot possibly settle the original problem.

5) WORK (ROUND-3)

5.1. Exact Abel criterion for the parity series

Set \[ \varepsilon_m := (-1)^{\pi(m)},\qquad T(N):=\sum_{m=3}^N \frac{\varepsilon_m}{m\log m},\qquad A(N):=\sum_{m=3}^N \varepsilon_m \] for integers $N\ge 3$.

By Round 2 Theorem 5.7, the original Erdős series converges if and only if $T(N)$ converges as $N\to\infty$.

Lemma 6.1 (discrete Abel identity). For every integer $N\ge 3$, \[ T(N) = \frac{A(N)}{N\log N} + \sum_{m=3}^{N-1} A(m)\left( \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} \right). \]

Proof. Apply the standard summation-by-parts identity \[ \sum_{m=3}^N a_m b_m = A(N)b_N + \sum_{m=3}^{N-1} A(m)(b_m-b_{m+1}), \] where $A(m):=\sum_{j=3}^m a_j$, with \[ a_m=\varepsilon_m,\qquad b_m=\frac{1}{m\log m}. \] This gives the claimed identity immediately. $\square$

Theorem 6.2 (exact discrepancy criterion). Let \[ A(N)=\sum_{m=3}^N (-1)^{\pi(m)}. \] Then the following are equivalent:

  1. (i) the series \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{n}{p_n} \] converges;

  2. (ii) the series \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m} \] converges;

  3. (iii) the discrepancy-weighted series \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty A(m)\left( \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} \right) \] converges.

Moreover, if $A(t):=A(\lfloor t\rfloor)$ for real $t\ge 3$, then these are also equivalent to convergence of the improper integral \[ \int_3^\infty A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)\,dt. \]

Proof. The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is exactly Round 2 Theorem 5.7.

By Lemma 6.1, \[ T(N) = \frac{A(N)}{N\log N} + \sum_{m=3}^{N-1} A(m)\left( \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} \right). \] Since $|A(N)|\le N-2$, we have \[ \left|\frac{A(N)}{N\log N}\right| \le \frac{N-2}{N\log N} \le \frac{1}{\log N} \to 0. \] Hence $T(N)$ converges if and only if the partial sums of \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty A(m)\left( \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} \right) \] converge. This proves the equivalence of (ii) and (iii), hence also of (i)--(iii).

For the integral form, note that \[ \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right) = -\frac{1}{t^2\log t}-\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}. \] Therefore, for every integer $m\ge 3$, \[ \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} = \int_m^{m+1} \left( \frac{1}{t^2\log t} + \frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t} \right)\,dt. \] Because $A(t)=A(m)$ on $t\in[m,m+1)$, we obtain for every integer $N\ge 4$, \[ \sum_{m=3}^{N-1} A(m)\left( \frac{1}{m\log m} - \frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} \right) = \int_3^N A(t)\left( \frac{1}{t^2\log t} + \frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t} \right)\,dt. \] Also, \[ \int_N^{N+1} \left| A(t)\left( \frac{1}{t^2\log t} + \frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t} \right) \right|\,dt \ll N\int_N^{N+1}\frac{dt}{t^2\log t} \ll \frac{1}{N\log N}\to 0, \] so convergence of the integrals along integer endpoints $N$ is equivalent to convergence of the full improper integral. Thus the discrepancy series converges if and only if the improper integral converges. $\square$

This is an exact criterion; unlike the Round 2 Proposition 5.8, it is not merely sufficient.

5.2. A stronger general sufficient criterion

Theorem 6.2 immediately yields the following strengthening of Round 2 Proposition 5.8.

Corollary 6.3 (general discrepancy criterion). Let $L:[3,\infty)\to (0,\infty)$ be eventually increasing with $L(x)\to\infty$. Assume \[ A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right) \] and \[ \int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty. \] Then \[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n} \] converges.

Proof. By Theorem 6.2, it suffices to show that \[ \int_3^\infty A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)\,dt \] converges. From the hypothesis, \[ |A(t)| \ll \frac{t}{L(t)}. \] Hence \[ \left| A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right) \right| \ll \frac{1}{t\log t\,L(t)}. \] The right-hand side is integrable by assumption, so the integral converges absolutely. Therefore the original Erdős series converges. $\square$

Remark. Round 2 Proposition 5.8 corresponds to the special choice \[ L(t)=(\log\log t)^{1+\varepsilon}. \] But Corollary 6.3 is strictly stronger. For instance, it already gives convergence from the much weaker-looking bound \[ A(x)=O\!\left( \frac{x}{\log\log x\,(\log\log\log x)^{1+\varepsilon}} \right), \] because \[ \int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,\log\log t\,(\log\log\log t)^{1+\varepsilon}} <\infty. \]

5.3. Near-sharp model obstruction

I now strengthen the Round 2 obstruction.

Proposition 6.4 (near-sharp model counterexample). There exists an equidistributed sign sequence $\sigma(m)\in\{\pm 1\}$ such that, with \[ B(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x}\sigma(m), \] one has \[ B(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right), \] but nevertheless \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m} \] diverges (in fact, diverges to $+\infty$).

Proof. Let \[ L_k:=\log\log k\,\log\log\log k \] for $k$ sufficiently large, and define \[ n_k:=2\lfloor kL_k\rfloor+1. \] For $k$ large enough, $L_k>1$, so $n_k$ is odd and strictly increasing. Let \[ Y:=\{n_k: k\ge k_0\} \] for some sufficiently large initial index $k_0$, and define \[ \sigma(m):= \begin{cases} 1, & m\in Y,\\ (-1)^m, & m\notin Y. \end{cases} \]

Thus $\sigma$ agrees with the alternating sequence $(-1)^m$, except that at the selected odd integers $m\in Y$ we flip the value from $-1$ to $+1$.

Step 1: discrepancy bound. Let \[ Y(x):=\#\{m\in Y: m\le x\}. \] I claim that \[ Y(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right). \]

Indeed, fix $x$ large and suppose $n_k\le x$. Write \[ M(x):=\log\log x\,\log\log\log x. \] If \[ k>\frac{8x}{M(x)}, \] then for $x$ large enough one also has $k>x^{1/2}$. Hence \[ \log\log k \ge \frac12 \log\log x, \qquad \log\log\log k \ge \frac12 \log\log\log x, \] so \[ L_k=\log\log k\,\log\log\log k \ge \frac14 M(x). \] Therefore \[ n_k \ge kL_k > \frac{8x}{M(x)}\cdot \frac14 M(x) = 2x, \] contradicting $n_k\le x$. Thus necessarily \[ k\le \frac{8x}{M(x)}, \] and so \[ Y(x)\le \frac{8x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x} \] for all sufficiently large $x$, proving the claim.

Now let \[ B_0(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^m. \] Then $B_0(x)=O(1)$. Every $m\in Y$ is odd, so replacing $(-1)^m=-1$ by $+1$ changes the partial sum by $+2$. Hence \[ B(x)=B_0(x)+2Y(x), \] and therefore \[ B(x)=O(1)+O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) = O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right). \] In particular $B(x)=o(x)$, so $\sigma$ is equidistributed.

Step 2: divergence of the weighted series. We have the exact decomposition \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m} = \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^m}{m\log m} + \sum_{m\in Y} \frac{\sigma(m)-(-1)^m}{m\log m}. \] Since every $m\in Y$ is odd, $(-1)^m=-1$ and $\sigma(m)=1$, so \[ \sigma(m)-(-1)^m=2. \] Therefore \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m} = \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^m}{m\log m} + 2\sum_{m\in Y}\frac{1}{m\log m}. \] The first series converges by the alternating series test. Thus it remains to prove \[ \sum_{m\in Y}\frac{1}{m\log m}=\infty. \]

Since $m=n_k$ for $m\in Y$, it is enough to prove \[ \sum_{k\ge k_0} \frac{1}{n_k\log n_k}=\infty. \] For $k$ sufficiently large, \[ n_k = 2\lfloor kL_k\rfloor + 1 \le 3kL_k. \] Also $L_k=o(k)$, so $n_k\le k^2$ for large $k$, and hence \[ \log n_k \le 2\log k. \] Thus \[ \frac{1}{n_k\log n_k} \ge \frac{c}{k\log k\,\log\log k\,\log\log\log k} \] for some absolute constant $c>0$ and all large $k$.

Now \[ \sum_{k\ge k_0} \frac{1}{k\log k\,\log\log k\,\log\log\log k} \] diverges by the integral test: with $u=\log\log k$, one has $du = dk/(k\log k)$, and the corresponding integral becomes \[ \int^\infty \frac{du}{u\log u}, \] which diverges. Therefore \[ \sum_{k\ge k_0} \frac{1}{n_k\log n_k}=\infty, \] so \[ \sum_{m\in Y}\frac{1}{m\log m}=\infty. \] It follows that \[ \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m}=+\infty. \] This proves the proposition. $\square$

Corollary 6.5 (strategy barrier). No theorem of the form \[ B(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m}\ \text{converges} \] can hold for arbitrary sign sequences $\sigma(m)\in\{\pm1\}$.

Thus any unconditional proof of convergence for the prime-parity sequence $\sigma(m)=(-1)^{\pi(m)}$ must use either:

  • genuinely stronger cancellation than that scale, or
  • some additional prime-specific structure beyond a bare discrepancy estimate of that order.

This is a mathematically sharper obstruction than the Round 2 statement that "equidistribution alone is insufficient."

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

I now try to break the new arguments.

  1. Did Theorem 6.2 secretly assume convergence of the discrepancy series? No. The proof is a direct finite summation-by-parts identity, valid for each $N$, followed by the trivial bound \[ \left|\frac{A(N)}{N\log N}\right|\le \frac1{\log N}\to 0. \] No prior convergence assumption is used.

  2. Is the integral criterion exact or merely asymptotic? It is exact. The identity \[ \frac{1}{m\log m}-\frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)} = \int_m^{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)\,dt \] holds exactly, and $A(t)=A(m)$ on $[m,m+1)$. So the discrepancy series and the integral are term-by-term identical after grouping by unit intervals.

  3. Could Proposition 6.4 fail because the set $Y$ is not sparse enough? No. The counting argument shows \[ Y(x)\ll \frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}, \] which is $o(x)$. Hence the modified sequence is genuinely equidistributed.

  4. Could the lower bound on $1/(n_k\log n_k)$ fail? No. For large $k$, \[ n_k\le 3k\log\log k\,\log\log\log k \] and also $n_k\le k^2$, so $\log n_k\le 2\log k$. This gives the required lower bound \[ \frac{1}{n_k\log n_k} \gg \frac{1}{k\log k\,\log\log k\,\log\log\log k}. \]

  5. Does Proposition 6.4 contradict Round 2 Proposition 5.8? No. Round 2 Proposition 5.8 required the much stronger bound \[ A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{(\log\log x)^{1+\varepsilon}}\right), \] while Proposition 6.4 only produces \[ B(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right). \] Since \[ \frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x} \gg \frac{x}{(\log\log x)^{1+\varepsilon}} \] for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, there is no inconsistency.

  6. Interaction with Round 2. Round 2 isolated the prime-parity discrepancy $A(x)$ as the key quantity. Round 3 now identifies the exact kernel against which $A(x)$ must cancel, and shows that the discrepancy scale needed for a general convergence theorem is very close to the natural integrability threshold. This is a genuine strengthening, not a reformulation.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)

Round 3 makes two substantive advances beyond Round 2:

  1. It upgrades the Round 2 sufficient criterion to an exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion: the original Erdős series converges if and only if \[ \int_3^\infty A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)\,dt \] converges, where \[ A(t)=\sum_{3\le m\le t} (-1)^{\pi(m)}. \]

  2. It proves a near-sharp model obstruction: there exists an equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy \[ O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right) \] whose $1/(m\log m)$-weighted series diverges. Therefore, discrepancy-only arguments at that scale cannot settle the prime problem.

What remains open is still the prime-specific question: can one prove, unconditionally, that the actual discrepancy \[ A(x)=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^{\pi(m)} \] has enough cancellation against the kernel from Theorem 6.2 to make the integral converge?
