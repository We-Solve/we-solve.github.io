Syzygy-word parity and the angular-momentum holonomy obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. For the planar three-body problem, a shape is a configuration modulo translation, rotation and positive scaling. Its normalized shape space is a sphere, with collisions marked and collinear configurations on an equator. A syzygy is a collinear configuration; its symbol records which body is in the middle.

A parity obstruction for one clarified sequence class.
For a collision-free planar three-body periodic motion, let $A(t)=\det(q_2-q_1,q_3-q_1)$ be twice the oriented area. Suppose all syzygies in a period are isolated transverse zeros of $A$, and the chosen period endpoints are not syzygies. At each such zero the sign changes. Since $A(P)=A(0)$, the number of crossings in the period must be even. Thus a periodic word of odd length cannot be realized under these transversality and endpoint conventions. Tangential contacts, persistent collinearity or a different coding rule invalidate this particular count and are not silently excluded from the source's broad question.
Zero angular momentum does not guarantee a closed lift.
Write $q_i=r(t)e^{i\theta(t)}s_i(t)$, where $r>0$ is scale and $s_i$ is a local normalized shape representative, with $I_s=\sum m_i|s_i|^2>0$. Direct differentiation gives \[C=r^2\left(I_s\dot\theta+\operatorname{Im}\sum_i m_i\overline{s_i}\dot s_i\right). \] At $C=0$, integrating yields $\theta(P)-\theta(0)=-\int_0^P I_s^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\sum_i m_i\overline{s_i}\dot s_i\,dt$. Even if the chosen shape representative is periodic, closure of the physical configuration requires this angle to lie in $2\pi\mathbb Z$. The syzygy word alone does not determine the integral.
