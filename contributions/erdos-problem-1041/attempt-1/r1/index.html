REUSED SOURCE 1

Original attribution: gpt_pro_5.2. Original file: attacks/erdos/gpt_pro_5.2/1041.tex.

Let $f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^n(z-z_i)\in \mathbb{C}[z]$ with $\lvert z_i\rvert < 1$ for all $i$. Must there always exist a path of length less than $2$ in\[\{z: \lvert f(z)\rvert < 1\}\]which connects two of the roots of $f$? A problem of Erdős, Herzog, and Piranian [EHP58], who proved that this set always has a connected component containing at least two of the roots. References [EHP58] Erdős, P. and Herzog, F. and Piranian, G., Metric properties of polynomials . J. Analyse Math. $1958$, 125-148.

FORMAL RESTATEMENT Let f(z)=$\prod_{i=1}^n$ $z-z_i$ with all roots satisfying |z_i|<1, and let L(f) := { z in C : |f(z)| < 1 }. Question: must there exist indices i!=j and a rectifiable path gamma in L(f) connecting z_i to z_j such that the Euclidean length of gamma is strictly less than 2? (For n=1 the question is vacuous; the interesting case is n>=2.)

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK Only facts explicitly present in the problem statement are treated as established here. In particular, it is stated that L(f) always has a connected component containing at least two roots. The additional length <2 requirement is the new constraint.

ATTACK PLAN $1$ Prove the statement for n=2 exactly (this is the smallest nontrivial case). $2$ Find a clean sufficient condition guaranteeing that the straight segment between two roots stays inside L(f). $3$ Attempt to argue that such a sufficient condition must occur for some pair, or else try to build counterexamples by spreading roots.

WORK FAST REALITY CHECK. * n=2 is fully solved below: there is always a path of length |z_1-z_2|<2 (Lemma 1041.1). * For n=3,4,5,6 a random experiment (roots uniform in the unit disc) shows that the sufficient condition in Lemma 1041.2 holds for at least one pair with frequencies: n=3: about 0.641 n=4: about 0.619 n=5: about 0.540 n=6: about 0.448 This does not prove the conjecture but is a sanity check that the condition is not extremely rare for small n.

Lemma 1041.1 (the conjecture holds for n=2). Let f(z)=$z-a$$z-b$ with |a|<1 and |b|<1 and a!=b. Then there is a path in L(f) from a to b of length |a-b|<2.

Proof. Let d:=|a-b|. Since |a|,|b|<1, the triangle inequality gives d <= |a|+|b| < 2, hence d<2. Let m=$a+b$/2 be the midpoint. Consider the straight segment from a to m, parameterized by z(t) = a + t$m-a$ = a + t$b-a$/2, 0<=t<=1. Then |z(t)-a| = t d/2. Also, z(t)-b = a-b + t$b-a$/2 = -$1-t/2$$b-a$, so |z(t)-b| = $1-t/2$ d. Therefore, along this segment we have |f(z(t))| = |z(t)-a||z(t)-b| = $t d/2$ * $(1-t/2)d$ = $t(1-t/2)$ * d^2/2. For t in [0,1], the function t$1-t/2$ is increasing (its derivative is 1-t>=0), so its maximum on [0,1] is attained at t=1 and equals 1/2. Hence for all t in [0,1], |f(z(t))| <= $1/2$ * d^2/2 = d^2/4 < 1. Thus the entire segment from a to m lies in L(f). By symmetry, the same argument shows the segment from m to b also lies in L(f). Concatenating these two segments gives a path from a to b inside L(f) of total length |a-m| + |m-b| = d/2 + d/2 = d < 2.

Lemma 1041.2 (a sufficient condition: close pair implies a short connecting segment). Let n>=2 and f(z)=$\prod_{k=1}^n$ $z-z_k$ with all |z_k|<1. Suppose there exist i!=j with d:=|z_i-z_j| such that d^2 $2+d$^{n-2} < 1. Then the straight line segment [z_i,z_j] is contained in L(f). In particular, z_i and z_j can be connected inside L(f) by a path of length d $<2$.

Proof. Let z be any point on the straight segment from z_i to z_j. Then by convexity, |z-z_i| <= d and |z-z_j| <= d. Now fix any other root z_k with k not in {i,j}. Using the triangle inequality, |z-z_k| <= |z-z_i| + |z_i-z_k|. Since |z_i|<1 and |z_k|<1, we have |z_i-z_k| <= |z_i|+|z_k| < 2. Thus |z-z_k| < d+2. Combining these bounds over all factors, |f(z)| = $\prod_{k=1}^n$ |z-z_k| < d * d * $2+d$^{n-2} = d^2$2+d$^{n-2} < 1. Therefore every z on [z_i,z_j] lies in L(f), and the segment length is d<2.

VERIFICATION * Lemma 1041.1 is a direct computation along a segment and is fully rigorous. * Lemma 1041.2 is a direct product bound using only triangle inequality and the root constraints. * The random experiment numbers were computed by a minimal script; they are not used as proof.

FINAL UNRESOLVED (i) Strongest proved partial result here: the statement is true for n=2 (Lemma 1041.1). More generally, if two roots are sufficiently close in the sense d^2$2+d$^{n-2}<1, then the straight segment between them lies in {|f|<1} and yields a path of length <2 (Lemma 1041.2). (ii) First gap: show that for every n>=2 and every choice of roots with |z_i|<1, there exists some pair i!=j satisfying a condition strong enough to force a path of length <2 inside L(f); or else construct an explicit counterexample polynomial. (iii) Top 3 next moves: 1. Try to strengthen Lemma 1041.2 by replacing the crude bound |z-z_k|<2+d with something depending on where the segment lies (e.g. if the segment stays well inside the unit disc, improve the 2 constant). 2. Combine a packing argument (some pair of roots must be close) with a lower bound on how small the other factors can be along a connecting corridor; attempt to force |f|<1 along a short path. 3. Computational: for small degrees (say n<=6) attempt an explicit search over root configurations to maximize the minimal in-domain path length between roots (e.g. discretize the plane and do shortest-path computations in the sublevel set) to look for counterexamples or patterns. (iv) Minimal counterexample structure (if false): roots arranged near the unit circle and spread so that (a) the component containing multiple roots exists but (b) any connecting path must detour around regions where |f|>=1, forcing length >=2.
