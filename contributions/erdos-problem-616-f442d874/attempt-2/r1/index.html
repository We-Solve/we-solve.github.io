Problem 616

FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Fix an integer $r\ge 2$. An $r$-uniform hypergraph is a pair $G=(V,E)$ where $V$ is a finite vertex set and $E\subseteq \binom{V}{r}$.

For a vertex subset $U\subseteq V$, write $G[U]$ for the induced subhypergraph on $U$: \[ G[U] \;:=\; \left(U,\ \{e\in E:\ e\subseteq U\}\right). \]

A transversal (or vertex cover) of a hypergraph $H=(V(H),E(H))$ is a set $T\subseteq V(H)$ meeting every edge: $T\cap e\neq\varnothing$ for all $e\in E(H)$. The transversal number $\tau(H)$ is the minimum size of a transversal.

Assume $G$ satisfies the local property: \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-63-61-6c} \forall U\subseteq V\ \ (|U|\le 3r-3\ \Rightarrow\ \tau(G[U])\le 1). \end{equation} Equivalently: every induced subhypergraph on at most $3r-3$ vertices is either edge-empty or a star (all edges share a common vertex).

Define \[ t(r)\;:=\; \max\left\{\tau(G):\ G\ \text{is $r$-uniform and satisfies \eqref{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-63-61-6c}}\right\}. \] The problem asks to determine the best possible $t(r)$ (exactly or asymptotically), and in particular the smallest universal $t$ such that $\tau(G)\le t$ for all $G$ satisfying (eq:local).

QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK

The statement in the prompt quotes Erdős--Hajnal--Tuza $1991$ giving linear-in-$r$ bounds of the form \[ c_1 r + O(1)\ \le\ t(r)\ \le\ c_2 r \] with explicit constants $c_1=\tfrac{3}{16}$ and $c_2=\tfrac{1}{5}$ (as written in the prompt). The ErdosProblems page for this item lists the problem as open and does not record a determination of $t(r)$.

ATTACK PLAN

The local hypothesis (eq:local) is a Helly-type / local-to-global condition. A natural approach is:

  1. Extract structural consequences of (eq:local). In particular, apply it to unions of 2 or 3 edges to deduce strong intersection properties.
  2. Relate $\tau(G)$ to intersections of edges. If one can show that some pair of edges intersects in $\le c r$ vertices, then $\tau(G)\le c r$ would follow from triplewise intersection.
  3. If $\tau(G)$ is large, take a minimal transversal $T$ and use the standard "witness edge" construction (each $x\in T$ has an edge meeting $T$ exactly at $x$). Try to force a small-vertex induced subhypergraph whose transversal number is $\ge 2$, contradicting (eq:local).
  4. If the above is insufficient, consider fractional transversals / LP duality and attempt to convert local constraints into a global bound (this is the sort of method used in several local-to-global covering results).

WORK

Step 1: (eq:local) forces pairwise intersection (for $r\ge 3$).
Assume $r\ge 3$. Let $e,f\in E$ be two edges. Then $|e\cup f|\le 2r\le 3r-3$. Apply (eq:local) to $U=e\cup f$. Since $G[U]$ contains $e$ and $f$, the condition $\tau(G[U])\le 1$ implies there exists a vertex $v\in U$ contained in every edge of $G[U]$, in particular $v\in e\cap f$. Hence \[ \boxed{\text{If $r\ge 3$, then every two edges intersect.}} \] So $G$ is an intersecting $r$-uniform hypergraph.
Step 2: (eq:local) forces 3-wise intersection (for $r\ge 3$).
Assume again $r\ge 3$. Take any three edges $e_1,e_2,e_3\in E$. From Step 1 we know $|e_i\cap e_j|\ge 1$ for each $i\ne j$. If $e_1\cap e_2\cap e_3=\varnothing$, then by inclusion--exclusion, \[ |e_1\cup e_2\cup e_3| = |e_1|+|e_2|+|e_3| - \sum_{i<j}|e_i\cap e_j| + |e_1\cap e_2\cap e_3| \le 3r - 3. \] Applying (eq:local) to $U=e_1\cup e_2\cup e_3$ would force $\tau(G[U])\le 1$, hence would force a vertex common to all edges of $G[U]$, in particular common to $e_1,e_2,e_3$, contradicting $e_1\cap e_2\cap e_3=\varnothing$. Therefore \[ \boxed{\text{If $r\ge 3$, then every three edges have nonempty intersection.}} \] So $G$ is 3-wise intersecting.
Step 3: In a 3-wise intersecting hypergraph, the intersection of two edges is a transversal.
Let $H=(V,E)$ be any hypergraph with the property that any three edges intersect. Fix two edges $e,f\in E$ and set $I:=e\cap f$. For any third edge $g\in E$, the triple intersection condition gives $(e\cap f\cap g)\neq\varnothing$, i.e., $I\cap g\neq\varnothing$. Thus every edge $g$ meets $I$, so $I$ is a transversal. Hence \[ \boxed{\tau(H)\le |e\cap f|\ \text{for all edges $e,f\in E$.}} \] In particular, \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-61-75-2d-6d-69-6e-2d-69-6e-74-65-72-73-65-63-74-69-6f-6e} \tau(H)\ \le\ \min\left\{|e\cap f|:\ e,f\in E\right\}. \end{equation} Applying this to $G$ (Step 2) yields the same bound.
A very coarse universal bound.
Since $G$ is intersecting (Step 1), for any edge $e\in E$ the set $e$ itself is a transversal (it meets every other edge). Therefore \[ \boxed{\tau(G)\le r\quad\text{for all $r\ge 3$ under \eqref{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-63-61-6c}.}} \] This is far weaker than the best-known linear bounds, but it is immediate.
Why this does not determine $t(r)$.
The inequality (eq:tau-min-intersection) reduces the problem to bounding how large the minimum pairwise intersection $\min_{e\ne f}|e\cap f|$ can be under (eq:local). The known upper bound $t(r)\le r/5$ amounts (morally) to showing existence of a pair of edges intersecting in at most about $r/5$ vertices. I did not find a clean argument from first principles that forces such a small intersection, nor an explicit construction matching $r/5$.

VERIFICATION

  • Step 1 used only that $2r\le 3r-3$, i.e. $r\ge 3$. For $r=2$ the local condition $|U|\le 3$ is too small to force pairwise intersection, so the reduction above is specific to $r\ge 3$.
  • Step 2 is a direct inclusion--exclusion calculation plus an application of (eq:local). No hidden assumptions beyond Step 1.
  • Step 3 is a standard consequence of 3-wise intersection and is completely general.

FINAL

UNRESOLVED

  1. Where and why the attempt fails. The core missing step is an a priori bound (as a function of $r$) on the minimum pairwise intersection size $\min_{e\ne f}|e\cap f|$ for $r$-uniform hypergraphs satisfying (eq:local). While (eq:local) forces 3-wise intersection, that alone does not immediately control the size of a smallest pairwise intersection, and I did not succeed in pushing the local constraint to produce a pair with intersection $\le r/5$ or to build an example approaching the lower bound constant.

  2. Strongest partial result proved here. For $r\ge 3$, the local hypothesis (eq:local) implies:
    • $G$ is pairwise intersecting and 3-wise intersecting;
    • for any two edges $e,f$, the intersection $e\cap f$ is a transversal;
    • consequently $\tau(G)\le \min_{e,f\in E}|e\cap f|$ and in particular $\tau(G)\le r$.

  3. Most plausible next ideas. A promising route is to study minimal transversals $T$ and the associated witness edges $\{e_x:x\in T\}$ with $e_x\cap T=\{x\}$. Under 3-wise intersection, any three witness edges have a common vertex outside $T$, which imposes strong incidence constraints. One would like to convert these constraints into an upper bound on $|T|$ linear in $r$ with a sharp constant, perhaps via: $1$ a density/averaging argument on the outside vertices, $2$ a shifting/compression argument to obtain a near-extremal canonical form, or $3$ a fractional transversal/packing duality argument.

  4. Small cases / computations. I did not carry out an exhaustive computation of $t(r)$ for small $r$. The arguments above show that for $r\ge 3$ any admissible $G$ is 3-wise intersecting, which already rules out many naive constructions. Determining $t(r)$ even for small fixed $r$ seems nontrivial and would likely require a dedicated search over $r$-uniform set systems.
