Research attempt: Amplification fails to preserve a single additive output share

Repetition helps after reconstruction.
Suppose independent executions on a fixed input produce a reconstructed Boolean output with error probability at most $1/4$ each. For odd $t$, reconstructing all $t$ outputs and taking their majority has failure probability at most \[ (\sqrt3/2)^t. \] To prove this, let $E_i$ indicate an error. For $\lambda=\log3$, independence gives \[ \Pr\left(\sum_iE_i\ge t/2\right) \le 3^{-t/2}\prod_i\mathbb E3^{E_i} \le3^{-t/2}(3/2)^t. \] Thus a receiver allowed $t$ reconstructed outputs can obtain exponentially small error in $t$, assuming the required independent sharing executions and their privacy composition are available. Finite repetition does not make the error exactly zero: for a base procedure with independent error probability $p>0$, the event that all copies err still has probability $p^t>0$.
Local majority is not additive.
The desired source interface has one local bit per party, not an external majority circuit with all shares revealed. Already for three copies there are no maps $a,b:\mathbb F_2^3\to\mathbb F_2$ satisfying \[ a(u)+b(v)=\operatorname{Maj}(u+v)\quad\text{for every }u,v. \] Take $u\in\{0,e_1\}$ and $v\in\{0,e_2\}$. The XOR of the four left sides is zero, since each term occurs twice. On the right, the values at $0,e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ are $0,0,0,1$, whose XOR is one. This contradiction disproves a universal postprocessing rule that takes local share vectors to one additive majority share. It does not prove that the particular HSS key distribution contains every such vector pair, or exclude a specialized new construction; it invalidates the unrestricted black-box rule.
Simple resharing has the same algebraic barrier.
A proposed three-party extension might split one party's arithmetic input share $z$ as $a+b$ and ask the two new parties to evaluate its nonlinear local function separately. For maps on an abelian group, a decomposition $f(a+b)=g(a)+h(b)$ for all $a,b$ forces \[ f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)-f(0), \] by substituting $a=0$ and $b=0$. Conversely this identity permits $g(a)=f(a)-f(0)$, $h(b)=f(b)$. Thus it works exactly when $f-f(0)$ is additive. For $f(z_1,z_2)=z_1z_2$ on $\mathbb F_2^2$, the same four points $0,e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ violate the identity. Nonlinear branching-program evaluation therefore cannot in general be extended to another party merely by linearly splitting a share and evaluating independently.

The two obstructions identify the missing operations: privately computing a nonlinear combiner for amplification and constructing evaluation-compatible multiparty keys. Revealing shares to compute a majority would change the compact additive interface and possibly its security use; adding interaction would change round guarantees. Neither cost is removed by the probability calculation.

Verification and adversarial audit

The exponential bound uses independent executions conditional on a fixed input and a base error at most $1/4$; no independence is inferred from merely repeating the same randomness. The majority contradiction checks all four required values exactly. The affine characterization is proved in both directions and is limited to the stated universal local-postprocessing model.
