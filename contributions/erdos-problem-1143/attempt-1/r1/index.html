Erdős Problem 1143 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt

FORMAL RESTATEMENT

For primes $p_1<\cdots<p_u$ and an integer $k\geq1$, define the guaranteed number of covered integers in an interval by \[ F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)=\min_{m\geq0}\left|\{a\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+k\}:p_i\mid a\text{ for some }i\}\right|. \] Estimate this quantity, especially when $k=\alpha p_u$ for a fixed $\alpha>2$.

CONTEXT AND SCOPE

This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

The minimum makes explicit the largest guaranteed bound intended by 'at least F_k' in the official statement. A number divisible by several selected primes is counted once.

ATTACK PLAN AND WORK

Let $Q=p_1\cdots p_u$, let $w(a)$ be the indicator that some $p_i$ divides $a$, and put $C=Q-\varphi(Q)$. The indicator is $Q$-periodic and has $C$ ones per period. Writing $k=tQ+r$ with $0\le r<Q$ therefore gives the exact reduction \[F_k=tC+F_r,\qquad F_r=\min_{0\le m<Q}\sum_{j=1}^r w(m+j),\quad F_0=0.\tag{1}\] Thus there are only $Q$ interval starts to check, independently of $k$. Averaging those $Q$ starts counts each covered residue exactly $r$ times; in particular $F_k\le\lfloor kC/Q\rfloor$.

Inclusion-exclusion gives a complementary uniform lower bound. For each nonempty subset $S$ of the selected primes, the count of multiples of $d_S=\prod_{p\in S}p$ in an interval of length $k$ differs from $k/d_S$ by less than one. Adding with the inclusion-exclusion signs yields \[ \max\{0,k(1-\prod_i(1-1/p_i))-(2^u-1)\} \le F_k\le\lfloor k(1-\prod_i(1-1/p_i))\rfloor.\tag{2} \] For one prime this specializes to the sharper exact formula $F_k(p)=\lfloor k/p\rfloor$. Equations $1$--$2$ are proved without independence assumptions about divisibility. The local finite check compares $1$ with direct interval enumeration for primes 2,3,5.

FINITE COMPUTATIONAL CHECK

For primes $2,3,5$, exhaustive starts modulo 30 verify $1$--$2$ for every $0\le k\le90$. Sample values are $F_{6}=4$, $F_{10}=6$, $F_{30}=22$, $F_{60}=44$, $F_{90}=66$.

VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP

When $u$ grows and $k$ is comparable to the largest prime, the exponential error in $2$ and the product-sized period in $1$ are too expensive. A useful asymptotic estimate requires cancellation or a more efficient covering argument.
