A unitary braided fusion category is a semisimple rigid tensor category with finitely many simple objects, a unitary structure and compatible braiding. A strong tensor generator $X$ produces all objects in the stated generating sense. The local algebra on a finite region $I$ is $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal B}(X^{\otimes I})$. Superselection sectors encode inequivalent localized charge representations of that net.

Why an equivalence of underlying categories cannot decide braiding.
Consider finite-dimensional $\mathbb Z_2$-graded Hilbert spaces, with degree-preserving maps and the usual graded tensor product. There are two unitary braidings on homogeneous tensors of degrees $a,b\in\{0,1\}$: \[ c^+_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=w\otimes v, \qquad c^-_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{ab}w\otimes v. \] Naturality is immediate because maps preserve degrees. The hexagon signs agree because $(-1)^{a(b+c)}=(-1)^{ab}(-1)^{ac}$, with the analogous identity in the other variable. Both therefore give braided tensor structures on the same underlying finite semisimple $W^*$ category.
An invariant that separates them.
Let $s$ be the one-dimensional odd object. Then $s\otimes s$ is the tensor unit and $c^+_{s,s}=+I$, whereas $c^-_{s,s}=-I$. A monoidal equivalence must fix the unit and hence carry the only other simple object to itself. Its tensor comparison on $s\otimes s$ is an invertible scalar; conjugation by that scalar cannot change the sign of the self-braiding. Thus no braided equivalence identifies these two structures.
