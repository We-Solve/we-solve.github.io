Extremal Exchangeability Beyond Natural Noncommutative Products

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An algebraic probability space is a unital $*$-algebra $A$ with a positive normalized state $\phi$. Write $A^{*\,\mathbb N}$ for the unital free product of countably many copies, with canonical embeddings $j_n$. A state on it is exchangeable if invariant under every finite permutation of the copies, and extremal if not a nontrivial convex combination of exchangeable states. For $B,C\subseteq A$, the source's independence requires an extremal exchangeable state $\phi_\infty$ whose restriction to separate copies of $B$ and $C$ agrees with their original joint distribution under $\phi$. The five universal product notions named in the source are tensor, free, Boolean, monotone, and antimonotone independence. In a represented von Neumann setting the tail algebra is the intersection of algebras generated by copies with indices at least $n$ as $n\to\infty$; triviality means that this intersection is only the scalars.

A test separating shift symmetry from exchangeability.
On sequences with entries in $\{0,1\}$, put equal probability on the two alternating sequences $0101\ldots$ and $1010\ldots$. The one-sided shift swaps these atoms, so the law is shift invariant. It is extremal among shift-invariant laws supported there, and indeed among all shift-invariant laws: any decomposition must remain supported on those two atoms, where invariance forces equal weights.
Tail and permutation checks.
The initial bit is recoverable from every tail: from coordinate $n$, parity of $n$ determines coordinate one. Thus the event "initial bit is zero" belongs to the tail sigma-field and has probability $1/2$, so the tail is nontrivial. The law is not exchangeable, since it assigns probability zero to equality of the first two bits, whereas swapping coordinates two and three makes those bits equal with probability one. Therefore extremal shift invariance does not imply a trivial tail, and the difference is already visible classically.
