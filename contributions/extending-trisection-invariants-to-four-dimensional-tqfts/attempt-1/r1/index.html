Extending Trisection Invariants to Four-Dimensional TQFTs

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A trisection decomposes a smooth four-manifold into three handlebody pieces with controlled pairwise and triple intersections. A four-dimensional topological quantum field theory assigns vector-space or categorical data to boundaries and compatible maps to bordisms, obeying gluing and disjoint-union axioms. A closed-manifold invariant alone does not provide those boundary assignments.

A gluing-rank obstruction test.
Suppose a proposed finite-dimensional oriented four-dimensional TQFT $Z$ assigns $V_Y$ to a closed oriented three-manifold $Y$. For fillings $M_i:\varnothing\to Y$, let $v_i=Z(M_i)$. Reversed fillings $\overline M_j:Y\to\varnothing$ give linear functionals $\ell_j$. Gluing requires \[ K_{ji}=Z(\overline M_j\cup_Y M_i)=\ell_j(v_i). \] For any finite list of fillings the matrix $K$ factors through $V_Y$, so $\operatorname{rank}K\leq\dim V_Y$. If the source invariant yielded arbitrarily large such ranks for one $Y$, it could not extend to a TQFT with finite-dimensional $V_Y$.
A second necessary numerical test.
Gluing the two ends of the identity cylinder on $Y$ computes $Z(Y\times S^1)=\operatorname{Tr}(I_{V_Y})=\dim V_Y$. To see the trace identity directly, choose a basis $e_i$ and dual basis $e^i$; evaluation of the coevaluation tensor $\sum_i e_i\otimes e^i$ yields $\sum_i e^i(e_i)$. Therefore these particular closed-manifold values must be nonnegative integers for the usual complex-vector-space TQFT target.
