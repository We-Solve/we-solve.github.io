Definitions and conventions.
Initial data $(g,k)$ consist of a spatial Riemannian metric and second fundamental form; their constraint quantities are energy density $\mu$ and momentum density $J$. The dominant energy condition is $\mu\geq|J|_g$. On a boundary surface the outgoing expansion is $\theta=H_g+\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}k$ with a chosen outward-normal convention. Stationary vacuum data embed in a vacuum spacetime admitting the required stationary Killing field; asymptotic flatness fixes the exterior decay. Bartnik boundary data encode the source's metric, mean-curvature and extrinsic-curvature boundary components. The primary report defines $B(g,k)=(g|_\Sigma,H_g,(k-(\operatorname{tr}_gk)g)(\nu,\cdot))$, where $\nu$ is the unit normal and $\Sigma$ is the matching boundary. Its exterior is $\mathbb R^3\setminus B^3$.

A round time-symmetric exterior calculation.
Restrict to $k=0$ and a spherical exterior metric $g=dr^2/f(r)+r^2d\Omega^2$, $f>0$. Computing its scalar curvature yields $R_g=2(1-f-rf')/r^2$. Thus the time-symmetric vacuum constraint gives $(rf)'=1$, or \[ f(r)=1-2m/r. \] At a round boundary of radius $R$, the unit normal is $\sqrt f\,\partial_r$ and the area derivative gives $H=2\sqrt{f(R)}/R$. Accordingly the boundary pair $(R,H)$, with $H>0$, determines \[ m=\frac R2\left(1-\frac{H^2R^2}{4}\right). \] The radial family is asymptotically flat and has $f>0$ for $r\ge R$. The momentum boundary component is zero since $k=0$. This verifies uniqueness of the mass parameter within this restricted radial ansatz.
Limits of the reduction.
It proves neither uniqueness among nonspherical stationary exteriors nor an extension for arbitrary source boundary tensors. The source allows expansion that is merely not everywhere nonpositive, substantially weaker than the pointwise $H>0$ used here with $k=0$. Also regular dominant-energy interiors may constrain the allowed round data; none is constructed for every algebraic pair.
