Compact facility-location formulations: exact enumeration and the missing compression

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Capacitated facility location opens facilities and assigns clients while respecting capacities and minimizing opening and connection costs. Polynomial size means that the number of encoded variables and inequalities is polynomial in instance size.

A relaxation contains the integral feasible solutions. For a nonnegative minimization objective, its integrality gap is the supremum of the ratio between the integral optimum and the relaxation optimum over the specified instances.

A linear program optimizes $c^Tx$ over a polyhedron $\{x:Ax\leq b\}$. In the simplex method a pivot moves between adjacent basic feasible solutions; a pivot rule chooses the next such move.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Does there exist a polynomial-size linear-programming relaxation for the capacitated facility location problem whose integrality gap is bounded by a constant?

Attempt to encode all opening choices

We retain the source's metric capacitated problem with unit clients, integer capacities, and at most one opening per facility. Let $m$ be the number of facilities and $n\ge1$ the number of clients. Replace each capacity $U_i$ by $\min(U_i,n)$; no feasible assignment or optimum changes. For a fixed opened subset $S$, consider the assignment polytope \[ P_S=\left\{z\ge0:\ \sum_{i\in S}z_{ij}=1\ (j=1,\ldots,n),\quad \sum_j z_{ij}\le U_i\ (i\in S)\right\}. \] It is nonempty exactly when $\sum_{i\in S}U_i\ge n$: necessity follows by summing, and sufficiency follows by assigning successive clients to successive available slots. Thus feasibility of the opened subset itself is elementary; connection costs are still tied to the facility locations.
Complete assignment integrality proof.
Every point of $P_S$ is a convex combination of integral assignments. Add one dummy client row with entries $r_i=U_i-\sum_j z_{ij}$. In the resulting nonnegative matrix, each facility column sums to the integer $U_i$, each real client row sums to one, and the dummy row sums to the integer $\sum_iU_i-n$. If any entry is nonintegral, form the bipartite graph of nonintegral entries between rows and columns. At every incident vertex, there must be at least two such entries: an integer row or column sum cannot have just one nonintegral summand. A finite graph with minimum degree at least two contains a cycle (extend a nonbacktracking walk until a vertex repeats); bipartiteness makes its length even. Add an alternating increment $t,-t$ along this cycle. Both signs are feasible for sufficiently small $t>0$, preserving all margins. In each direction increase $t$ until an entry hits its adjacent integer, without crossing any integer on the cycle. The original matrix is a strict convex combination of these two endpoints, and each endpoint has fewer nonintegral entries. Recurse on each endpoint. The depth is bounded by the number of entries, so this is a finite convex decomposition into integral nonnegative matrices. Deleting the dummy row yields integral assignments; a real row with sum one has exactly one entry one. This proves the assertion without appealing to an external matching integrality theorem.
An exact extended formulation.
For each capacity-feasible subset $S$ introduce $\lambda_S\ge0$ and variables $z^S_{ij}\ge0$ for $i\in S$, with \[ \sum_S\lambda_S=1,\quad \sum_{i\in S}z^S_{ij}=\lambda_S,\quad \sum_j z^S_{ij}\le U_i\lambda_S,\quad y_i=\sum_{S\ni i}\lambda_S,\quad x_{ij}=\sum_{S\ni i}z^S_{ij}. \] If $\lambda_S=0$, nonnegativity and the client equalities force $z^S=0$. Otherwise $z^S/\lambda_S\in P_S$ and has the integral decomposition just proved. Hence every projected $(x,y)$ is a convex combination of feasible integral facility-location solutions with exactly the opened sets $S$. Conversely, an integral solution is represented by putting its subset's $\lambda$ equal to one; convex combinations remain feasible. This proves equality with the integral convex hull, including facilities opened but unused. Minimizing the usual linear cost over this formulation has gap one, because a convex combination's cost is the same combination of integral costs and cannot be smaller than their minimum.

The precise missing step

This construction uses $O(2^m mn)$ variables and inequalities. It is polynomial for fixed $m$, and also for $m\le C\log_2 n$ with fixed $C$, but not for unrestricted $m$. Binary coefficient sizes remain polynomial: $U_i\le n$ and all other coefficients are zero or one. The source asks for a polynomial bound in the unrestricted instance size; our subset enumeration does not supply it.

An attempted compression retaining only each subset's total capacity loses the connection objective even in the smallest metric instance. On the real line place one client at $\varepsilon$, facility $a$ at zero and facility $b$ at $R>2\varepsilon$, with unit capacities and zero opening costs. The sets $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ have the same total capacity, but their optimal connection costs are $\varepsilon$ and $R-\varepsilon$ respectively. Their ratio is unbounded as $R/\varepsilon$ grows. Thus this proposed capacity-only state cannot substitute for the subset while preserving its cost information. This does not rule out more sophisticated extended variables.
