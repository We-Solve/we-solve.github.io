The literal ambient-domain omission persists.
On a single edge $uv$ with $I=\{u\}$ and $b_{uv}=1/3$, the edge-tree has two leaves but only one integral vertex, so it is excluded from the specified family. Any included singleton subtree has hull equal to its whole coordinate line, since both $\operatorname{conv}(\mathbb Z)$ and $\mathbb R$ equal $\mathbb R$. Thus the lifted subtree intersection imposes no constraint, while the global hull satisfies $x_u+x_v\ge1/3$. This also happens at denominator two, contradicting the source's stated known case if all hulls are interpreted in unrestricted ambient space. An implicit continuous relaxation or different subtree convention is therefore missing. This diagnostic is not treated as a resolution of the intended conjecture. Attempt to glue convex decompositions

Membership of $\bar x_T$ in a subtree hull supplies a finite distribution on feasible local points whose coordinate mean is $\bar x_T$. A tempting proof would glue these local distributions into a globally feasible random point with mean $\bar x$. Means on common coordinates are not enough to justify that step.
A precise gluing lemma under a stronger hypothesis.
Let finite distributions $P_A,P_B$ on coordinates indexed by $A,B$ agree as entire marginal distributions on $S=A\cap B$, with common marginal $\mu$. For consistent assignments $a,b$ having common restriction $s$ with $\mu(s)>0$, define \[ P(a,b)=\frac{P_A(a)P_B(b)}{\mu(s)}; \] put zero mass on all other pairs. Summing over compatible $b$ gives $P_A(a)\mu(s)/\mu(s)=P_A(a)$; if $\mu(s)=0$, every assignment restricting to $s$ has zero marginal mass, so nothing is lost. The same argument gives marginal $P_B$, and summing either marginal proves normalization. Every positive-mass global assignment has allowed local restrictions. Hence a gluing is possible with those full marginal hypotheses. The same construction can be iterated along a tree of variable blocks when, at each attachment, the intersection with all earlier blocks is the specified separator and its full marginal agrees. This follows by induction using the two-block argument, which preserves the already constructed marginal exactly.
Equal first moments do not imply compatible local laws.
A single coordinate with law supported equally on $\{-1,1\}$ has the same mean zero as the point mass at zero, but these distributions cannot be the two marginals of one shared coordinate. Thus even two local convex-combination witnesses cannot simply be combined from their means. Moreover cycle consistency can fail despite agreement of all singleton marginals. On four binary variables consider the relations \[ x_1=x_2,\quad x_2=x_3,\quad x_3=x_4,\quad x_4=1-x_1. \] Each individual relation admits a distribution on its two allowed pairs with each coordinate fair. More strongly, every forest obtained by deleting at least one cycle edge admits two complementary assignments on each component: choose a root bit and propagate equality or complementation along the unique paths. Fairly averaging complementary assignments gives mean $(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)$ on every such forest. Yet no global assignment exists, since the first three equalities imply $x_4=x_1$, contradicting the last relation. The incidence graph is an even cycle, so bipartiteness alone cannot make this generic gluing inference valid. These relations are not the source's one-sided covering inequalities; this is explicitly a counterexample to a general proof principle, not to the intended mixed-integer covering conjecture.

Adversarial audit and gap

Global feasible points always restrict to locally feasible points, and their finite convex combinations do too, proving the easy global-to-local inclusion. The reverse needs a structural argument producing compatible distributions, or another way to use the covering inequalities; the gluing lemma does not supply that compatibility from means. The audit separated full distributions from first moments, acyclic attachment from cycle consistency, and the generic binary counterexample from the exact source class.
