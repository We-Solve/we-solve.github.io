Why the source definition retains global connectedness

The preserved record is flagged as an unrecoverable problem extraction. The primary page already gives a spectral definition and separately lists research directions; we do not invent a new unresolved definition problem. We test what goes wrong if one drops its explicit connectedness requirement and checks only nonempty-face links. Let $r\ge4$ and take the disjoint union of two complete two-dimensional complexes on $r$ vertices each: each contains every vertex, edge and triangle on its vertex set, with no higher faces.
Proposition: Every vertex link has normalized adjacency spectrum $1,-1/(r-2),\ldots,-1/(r-2)$, whereas the underlying graph has eigenvalue 1 with multiplicity two and is disconnected.
Proof: The link of a vertex consists of every edge between its other $r-1$ component vertices, so its graph is $K_{r-1}$. The adjacency matrix of $K_t$ is $J-I$. After dividing by degree $t-1$, it acts as 1 on the all-ones vector and as $-1/(t-1)$ on the orthogonal subspace where $J$ vanishes. Substituting $t=r-1$ gives the link spectrum. The underlying graph is the disjoint union of two copies of $K_r$, whose normalized adjacency matrix is block diagonal. Each component supplies an independent eigenvector of eigenvalue 1, and there are no edges between components. ∎
Thus all vertex-link nontrivial eigenvalues can have arbitrarily small absolute value while the global graph is disconnected. In dimension two these are precisely the nonempty faces whose links have a graph of positive dimension; edge links are zero-dimensional. The example fails the source's first connectedness condition, so it is not a counterexample to the definition or to a properly hypothesized local-to-global theorem.
