Erdős Problem 100: separated distance levels and diameter

1. Statement and scope

Let $P$ consist of $n$ planar points with every mutual distance at least one. Suppose also that any two distinct distance values differ by at least one. Must its diameter be at least $cn$ for an absolute $c>0$? This remains unresolved. We give the known $n/\log n$ lower bound with its exact external input, a scale-invariant reformulation, and a calculation showing why a standard grid does not furnish a sublinear counterexample after the required rescaling.

2. Two lower bounds

Let $1\le\rho_1<\cdots<\rho_s=D$ be the distance values, where $D=\operatorname{diam}(P)$. Since $\rho_{j+1}-\rho_j\ge1$, induction gives $\rho_j\ge j$, and hence \[ D\ge s.\tag{1} \] The external Guth--Katz distinct-distance theorem states that every $n$-point planar set, for $n\ge2$, has at least $c_0 n/\log n$ distinct distances, for an absolute $c_0>0$. Its application here is immediate and gives \[ D\ge c_0 n/\log n.\tag{2} \] This theorem, not its deep proof, is an explicit input; it does not use the additional separation of our distance levels.

There is also a fully elementary bound. The open radius-$1/2$ disks about the points are disjoint. Fix $p\in P$; all these disks lie in the radius-$(D+1/2)$ disk about $p$. Comparing areas gives \[ n\pi/4\le\pi(D+1/2)^2, \qquad D\ge(\sqrt n-1)/2.\tag{3} \] This uses only the minimum point separation and is weaker than $2$ for large $n$.

3. An equivalent spacing inequality for arbitrary point sets

Now let $P$ be any finite set of $n\ge2$ distinct planar points, without a normalization. With its distinct distance values ordered as above, define \[ \sigma(P)=\min\left(\{\rho_1\}\cup \{\rho_{j+1}-\rho_j:1\le j<s\}\right)>0.\tag{4} \] For $s=1$ this minimum is just $\rho_1$. A dilation by a factor $\lambda>0$ satisfies both separation requirements in the problem exactly when $\lambda\sigma(P)\ge1$. Its least possible admissible diameter is therefore \[ \frac{\operatorname{diam}(P)}{\sigma(P)}.\tag{5} \] It follows that the original conjecture is equivalent to the existence of an absolute constant $C$ such that every finite planar point set satisfies \[ \sigma(P)\le\frac{C\operatorname{diam}(P)}{n}.\tag{6} \] Indeed, applying the conjecture to the dilation with factor $1/\sigma(P)$ proves $6$ with $C=1/c$. Conversely $6$ applied to an admissible set, for which $\sigma(P)\ge1$, gives diameter at least $n/C$. This reformulation shows exactly how the spacing condition might improve the general distinct-distance theorem.

4. Collinear sets and the square grid

For collinear admissible points, order them along the line. Consecutive gaps are at least one, so the diameter is at least $n-1$, achieved by $n$ equally spaced points. Thus the desired order is sharp and holds in this case.

For the $N\times N$ integer grid with $N\ge2$, put $m=N-1$. Its diameter is $\sqrt2\,m$, and both $m$ and $\sqrt{m^2+1}$ occur as distances, from the vectors $(m,0)$ and $(m,1)$. Even if other distance values intervene, the minimum adjacent gap obeys \[ \sigma(P)\le\sqrt{m^2+1}-m =\frac1{\sqrt{m^2+1}+m}. \] Consequently its least admissible rescaled diameter is at least \[ \sqrt2\,m\left(\sqrt{m^2+1}+m\right) \ge2\sqrt2\,m^2. \] Since $n=N^2$, this is of linear order in $n$. The grid's relatively small number of distance values therefore does not, by uniform rescaling, disprove the conjecture. This calculation does not exclude other configurations or other constructions.

5. Assessment and attribution

The spacing reformulation, packing estimate, collinear case and grid calculation are proved in full. The general estimate $2$ uses the stated Guth--Katz theorem. An argument exploiting the extra spacing to remove the remaining logarithm is missing.
