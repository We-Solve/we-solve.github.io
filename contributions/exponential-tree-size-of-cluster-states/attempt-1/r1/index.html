A separator upper bound and the meaning of exponential

We test the literal interpretation $2^{\Omega(n)}$ when $n$ denotes the total number of qubits of a rectangular grid cluster state. This is narrower than the bundled code-state, Shor-state and cluster-state question. The 2013 primary tree-size paper discusses a superpolynomial conjecture for the two-dimensional cluster state and local-operator invariance. We use the standard separator expansion below, making no novelty claim. A superpolynomial lower bound is compatible with our upper bound. For a finite graph $G=(V,E)$, write its graph state as \[ |G\rangle=2^{-|V|/2}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^V}(-1)^{\sum_{uv\in E}x_ux_v}|x\rangle. \] Let $S\subseteq V$ have size $s$, and let $G_1,\ldots,G_j$ be the nonempty components of $G-S$. For $z\in\{0,1\}^S$, define $b_v(z)=\sum_{u\in S:uv\in E}z_u$ modulo two for $v\notin S$; let $Z_v$ act by $Z_v|x_v\rangle=(-1)^{x_v}|x_v\rangle$.
Lemma: There is a state-tree representation satisfying \[ \operatorname{TS}(|G\rangle)\le 2^s\left(s+\sum_i\operatorname{TS}(|G_i\rangle)\right). \]
Proof: Partitioning the amplitude sum according to $x|_S=z$ gives the exact identity \[ |G\rangle=2^{-s/2}\sum_z(-1)^{\sum_{uv\in E(G[S])}z_uz_v}|z\rangle\otimes \bigotimes_i\left(\prod_{v\in V(G_i)}Z_v^{b_v(z)}\right)|G_i\rangle. \] Indeed every edge lies inside $S$, inside one component, or joins $S$ to a component; their phase contributions are exactly the three factors displayed. Normalization agrees because $2^{-s/2}\prod_i2^{-|V(G_i)|/2}=2^{-|V|/2}$. A local $Z_v$ only changes the one-qubit leaves labeled $v$, so it does not increase their count. Tensor products add leaf counts, and the $2^s$ summands may be represented by separate copies of the component trees. Scalar factors can be absorbed into a one-qubit leaf, or distributed through its subtree, without adding leaves. The computational-basis vector on $S$ uses $s$ leaves, proving the bound. ∎
Proposition: For a rectangular nearest-neighbor grid in fixed dimension $d\in\{2,3\}$ with $n\ge1$ vertices, \[ \operatorname{TS}(|G\rangle)\le n\,2^{C_d n^{1-1/d}},\qquad C_d=\frac1{1-2^{-(1-1/d)}}. \]
Proof: Set $\alpha=1-1/d$. A middle slice perpendicular to a longest side has size $s=n/\ell\le n^\alpha$, since $\ell\ge n^{1/d}$. Its removal leaves at most two rectangular grids, each of size $n_i\le n/2$, with $\sum_i n_i=n-s$. Use induction on $n$, the case $n=1$ being a one-leaf state. The lemma and the inductive bounds give \[ \operatorname{TS}(|G\rangle) \le2^s\left(s+\sum_i n_i2^{C_d n_i^\alpha}\right) \le n\,2^{n^\alpha+C_d(n/2)^\alpha} =n\,2^{C_d n^\alpha}. \] Empty components contribute nothing, so grids with a side of length one cause no exception. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

The separator phase identity was checked on every computational-basis string for a $2$ by $3$ grid and a $2$ by $2$ by $2$ grid. Recursive leaf counts were also computed for small rectangles. Since $\log_2n+C_dn^{1-1/d}=o(n)$, the displayed upper bound excludes $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for this precise qubit-count interpretation. It does not exclude $2^{\Omega(n^c)}$ with a smaller positive $c$, or any superpolynomial lower bound compatible with the estimate. If the source's size parameter denotes a side length or uses exponential in a broader sense, it is not refuted by that phrase alone. The code ensemble and specific Shor states remain unspecified, and no lower bound for the intended cluster-state conjecture is proved.
