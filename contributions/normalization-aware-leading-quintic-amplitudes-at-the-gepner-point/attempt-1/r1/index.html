Coordinate dependence of a leading coefficient.
Suppose in a chosen holomorphic trivialization an amplitude has leading expansion $F_g(q)=a q^r+O(q^{r+1})$, with integer $r$ and $a\ne0$. Under a local flat-coordinate change $\widetilde q=cq+O(q^2)$, $c\ne0$, inversion gives \[F_g=a c^{-r}\widetilde q^{\,r}+O(\widetilde q^{\,r+1}).\] This follows by substituting $q=c^{-1}\widetilde q+O(\widetilde q^2)$, and remains valid for negative $r$ as a Laurent expansion. If the amplitude is a section of the tensor power $2-2g$ of the period line bundle in the chosen period normalization, rescaling its frame by a nonzero constant $b$ supplies the corresponding additional frame factor; its convention must be stated rather than inferred from a numerical coefficient.
An ambiguity that affects the target directly.
An equation determining only $\bar\partial F_g$ cannot determine a holomorphic addition $h(q)$, because $\bar\partial h=0$. A constant $h$ changes a constant leading term; $h(q)=cq^r$ changes the first coefficient at any nonnegative power $r$ allowed by the boundary conditions. Thus anomaly equations alone do not fix leading holomorphic amplitudes without boundary or normalization data.
