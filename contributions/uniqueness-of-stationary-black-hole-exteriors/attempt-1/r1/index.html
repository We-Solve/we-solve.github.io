Uniqueness of Stationary Black-Hole Exteriors

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
An asymptotically flat vacuum initial data set $(\Sigma,\bar g,K)$ satisfies the Einstein constraints and has maximal globally hyperbolic development $(M,g)$ with $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$. The Kerr family describes rotating vacuum black holes by mass $m>0$ and specific angular momentum $a$; its subextremal black-hole range is $|a|<m$. Future null infinity $\mathcal I^+$ is the ideal endpoint boundary of outgoing null rays, and $J^-(\mathcal I^+)$ is its causal past. Completeness of $\mathcal I^+$ is the future-completeness condition in the report's asymptotically flat formulation. A stationary exterior admits a time-translation Killing field. A bifurcate event horizon has future and past null branches meeting in a bifurcation surface. Nondegeneracy excludes zero surface gravity. The source's uniqueness question concerns sufficiently regular asymptotically flat vacuum stationary exteriors with the stated simple-connectivity and horizon assumptions.
Source scope and qualifications.
The catalogue literature assessment calls this a dynamical settling expectation, but the clean statement and primary uniqueness contribution concern stationary uniqueness. The report explains real-analytic uniqueness and seeks weaker regularity. Dynamical Kerr stability is a different target, related to ID 30001359.

The static spherically symmetric reduction.
In the additional static spherical ansatz write $g=-e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^2+(1-2m(r)/r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$. The vacuum Einstein equations in these coordinates reduce to \[ m'(r)=0,\qquad \Phi'(r)=\frac{m(r)}{r(r-2m(r))}. \] These reduced equations are the only field-equation input used in this conditional calculation; their derivation from the Einstein tensor is not supplied here. Integrating gives $m(r)=M$ and $\Phi(r)=\tfrac12\log(1-2M/r)+C$. Asymptotic normalization of the time coordinate sets $C=0$, yielding Schwarzschild on $r>2M$.
What the reduction buys and costs.
Within those explicit reduced equations there are no additional radial vacuum metric functions. For $M>0$ the horizon is nondegenerate, while $M=0$ gives Minkowski with no black-hole horizon and negative $M$ does not give the required positive-radius horizon. The calculation therefore recovers the expected spherical member but uses staticity and spherical symmetry, neither of which follows from the source assumptions.
