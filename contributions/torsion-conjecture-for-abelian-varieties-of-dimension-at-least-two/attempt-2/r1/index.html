Claim type: Literature report

Exact scope: For A/Q Q-isomorphic to a product of g elliptic curves, #A(Q)_tors≤16^g, sharp for every g≥2. Mazur’s published elliptic torsion classification is the imported input; an explicit curve and exact rational group-law certificate realize 16^g. No bound for arbitrary isogenous quotients or simple abelian varieties is claimed.

For the subclass of the prompt consisting of products over Q, one obtains an effective bound independent of height, polarization and reduction: if
\[
A\simeq_{\mathbb Q}E_1\times\cdots\times E_g,
\]
then #A(Q)_tors≤16^g. This is sharp for every g. It is a consequence of Mazur's elliptic torsion theorem, rather than a new uniformity theorem for arbitrary abelian varieties.

The exact input [1] is that an elliptic curve over Q has rational torsion group either Z/m for 1≤m≤10 or m=12, or Z/2×Z/(2m) for 1≤m≤4. The largest group order in this list is 16, attained by Z/2×Z/8. Coordinate projections identify
\[
A(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}=\prod_{i=1}^g E_i(\mathbb Q)_{\rm tors}.
\]
Indeed a torsion product point has torsion in each coordinate. Conversely if each coordinate is torsion, the least common multiple of their finite orders annihilates the product point. Taking orders proves the bound. It does not require a product polarization.

**An explicit sharp example.** Consider the nonsingular curve
\[
E:\quad y^2=x(x+81)(x+256).
\]
The three cubic roots are distinct, so this is an elliptic curve with the point at infinity as identity. Set
\[
Q=(24,-840),\quad P=(144,3600),\quad T=(0,0),\quad U=(-81,0).
\]
Direct substitution shows these are rational points. The tangent-and-chord law gives 2Q=P and 2P=T. For example the Weierstrass coefficients are a₂=337, a₄=20736, a₆=0; the doubling slope at Q is
\[
m_Q=\frac{3\cdot24^2+2\cdot337\cdot24+20736}{-1680}=-23,
\]
and the formula x(2Q)=m_Q²−a₂−2x(Q) gives 144. Its y coordinate is −y(Q)+m_Q(x(Q)−x(2Q))=3600. At P the same formula gives 2P=(0,0). Since T≠O and 2T=O, Q has exact order 8. The cyclic group generated by Q has a unique nonzero element of order two, namely 4Q=T. The distinct point U has order two and therefore U∉〈Q〉. Hence
\[
\langle Q,U\rangle\simeq\mathbb Z/8\times\mathbb Z/2
\]
is a subgroup of E(Q)_tors of order 16. Mazur's upper bound shows it is the whole torsion subgroup. Taking E^g yields exactly 16^g rational torsion points, establishing sharpness throughout the product subclass.

**What “product” must mean.** The isomorphism is over the fixed ground field Q. A geometric product decomposition only available after extending the field does not identify rational points coordinatewise. Likewise an isogeny to a product is not an isomorphism of rational torsion groups: finite kernels and rational-point cokernels matter. The argument does not claim the same 16^g constant for all abelian varieties isogenous to products, and it says nothing about geometrically simple varieties. Products over a different fixed number field require the appropriate elliptic uniformity theorem rather than Mazur's Q-classification.

**Exact verification.** The included program implements the rational chord-and-tangent law with Fraction arithmetic. It verifies the four displayed points, all multiples of Q, the order relations, and distinctness of all 16 points iQ+jU. It also verifies pairwise group-law closure on those points and prints the exact coordinates. These are checks of a concrete sharpness certificate. They do not prove Mazur's theorem or establish general abelian-variety uniformity. The earlier attempt used good-reduction certificates and explained failure of uniform small good places; the present subclass bound and explicit extremizer have a different input and output.
