Write-up

CM values generate all ray class fields over imaginary quadratic bases

Problem: TOP-45
Claim type: Literature report
Author: /root/hundred_numbers

**Exact scope.** For every imaginary quadratic K, its Hilbert class field and every integer-modulus ray class field K_(m) are generated by j and suitably normalized Weber torsion values. Every finite abelian extension of K is contained in one such field. This is the classical imaginary quadratic case of Hilbert12, not an arbitrary-base solution.

Let K be any imaginary quadratic field and O_K its maximal order. The classical complex multiplication construction supplies analytic special-value generators of its abelian extensions. A precise version is Cohen–Stevenhagen, arXiv:0802.3843v2, Theorems6.9 and6.15. The first identifies H=K(j(C/O_K)) with the Hilbert class field. The second gives
\[
K_{(m)}=H\big(f_K(z):0\ne z\in m^{-1}O_K/O_K\big),
\]
where K_(m) is the ray class field modulo m O_K and f_K is the normalized Weber function in that source.

The normalization cannot be discarded. Generally f_K is a nonzero scalar multiple of the Weierstrass elliptic function wp for the lattice O_K. In the two extra-unit cases K=Q(i),Q(sqrt(-3)), one instead uses appropriately scaled wp^2 and wp^3. They take invariants under all lattice automorphisms. Adjoining arbitrary full torsion coordinates of an arbitrary twist is not the same theorem and can give an unnecessarily larger field. The source treats both special cases explicitly.

To see why integer moduli suffice for **every** abelian extension, let L/K be finite abelian and let f be a conductor modulus supplied by class field theory. There are no real places to add. Choose a positive integer m in the nonzero ideal f. Then m O_K is contained in f, so congruence to1 modulo m O_K implies congruence to1 modulo f. The corresponding ray principal subgroup is smaller, and the class-field inclusion is reversed: K_f is contained in K_(m). As L is contained in K_f, the displayed special-value field contains L. This also covers nonprincipal ideal conductors without incorrectly claiming that each ideal is generated by an integer.

This is a containment/generation statement, not the assertion that every single torsion value generates the whole ray field, or that j alone generates every ramified abelian extension. One may recover L as the fixed field of its corresponding subgroup of the ray class group, but computing that subgroup and certified minimal polynomials is a separate task. Numerical evaluation of a modular function without algebraic recognition and error bounds would not be a field certificate.

The previous contribution describes bounded primitive combinations once generators are available, and broader class-field background. The additional scope here is the full classical analytic input for all imaginary quadratic bases, all integer ray moduli and hence all finite abelian extensions by the conductor argument. The deep main theorem is credited. General real quadratic and other number fields remain outside this construction, so this is not a resolution of Hilbert's twelfth problem in its unrestricted form.
