Uniqueness in the Boltzmann–Nordheim Condensate Model

Definitions and mathematical statement

$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
In the isotropic Bose setting, $x=|v|^2/2$ is kinetic energy and $f_{\rm reg}(x,t)$ is the regular density in the measure $f_{\rm reg}(x,t)\sqrt{x}\,dx+n_c(t)\delta_0$. Its regular mass is $m[f]=\int_0^\infty f(x)\sqrt{x}\,dx$ in the source normalization, and mass conservation fixes $n_c(t)=m_{\rm tot}(0)-m[f_{\rm reg}(\cdot,t)]$. $C_4$ is the homogeneous Boltzmann--Nordheim collision functional; $C_3$ is obtained by inserting the condensate ansatz and extracting the condensate coupling. $\ell^1$-clustering is the summability condition on initial correlation functions used in the source's perturbative kinetic-limit argument.

For the regular part of the isotropic Boltzmann–Nordheim condensate model, consider the mass-conserving evolution equation $\partial_t f_{\mathrm{reg}}(x,t)=C_4[f_{\mathrm{reg}}(\cdot,t)](x)+(m_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)-m[f_{\mathrm{reg}}(\cdot,t)])C_3[f_{\mathrm{reg}}^{(1)}(\cdot,t)](x)$. Does this equation have a unique solution when a condensate is generated? Can the perturbative construction be extended to initial data that are not $\ell^1$-clustering, thereby deriving this equation rather than the standard Boltzmann–Nordheim equation?

A conditional uniqueness estimate for the corrected equation.
Let $X$ be a Banach space on which the regular mass $m$ is a bounded linear functional, and write $N(f)=C_4(f)+(M-m(f))C_3(f)$, using the source's $C_3[f]$ without the undefined superscript. On a set containing two trajectories suppose $C_4,C_3$ have Lipschitz constants $L_4,L_3$, $\|C_3(f)\|\leq B$, and $|M-m(f)|\leq K$. Subtracting and adding $(M-m(f))C_3(g)$ gives \[ \|N(f)-N(g)\|\leq(L_4+KL_3+B\|m\|)\|f-g\|. \] For integral solutions with equal initial data, iterating the resulting inequality $d(t)\leq L\int_0^td(s)ds$ yields $d(t)\leq D(Lt)^n/n!$ on a bounded time interval, hence $d(t)=0$ as $n\to\infty$.
What condensation changes.
The coefficient $M-m(f)$ itself is harmless under these hypotheses: it is Lipschitz even when it first becomes positive. The difficult part is finding a space in which the singular Bose collision maps satisfy the stated bounds uniformly across condensate generation. This calculation has not done so. Mass conservation alone only defines the condensate mass and cannot replace those analytic estimates.
Second bundled gap.
Uniqueness of this effective equation would not derive it from non-$\ell^1$-clustering microscopic data. That separate limit needs control of persistent initial correlations and a proof that the condensate-coupling term, rather than only $C_4$, survives. Both source targets remain unproved; no near-equilibrium theorem with a pre-existing condensate is substituted for them.
