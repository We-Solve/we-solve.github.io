Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Rank is column-space dimension. An all-zero square submatrix chooses equally many rows and columns with every selected entry zero. The hidden constant in the displayed exponent is intended to be absolute or as specified in the source.

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. Matrix completion reconstructs a low-rank matrix from a subset of its entries; matrix sensing reconstructs it from specified linear measurements. Tensor decomposition represents a multiway array as a sum of simple outer products.

Conjecture 5 on printed pp. 2308--2309 of the primary report asks for a zero submatrix with independently chosen row and column sets, not necessarily consecutive or identical. The source target has the scale $n\exp(-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon r}))$. The elementary restricted-range and rank-one calculations below are auxiliary and are not asserted to be novel.

Research attempt: Row-threshold rank argument and its density barrier

Let $M$ be $n\times n$, $n\geq1$, of real rank at most $r$, with at most $\varepsilon n^2$ nonzero entries. If $r=0$, the whole matrix is zero. Otherwise put $s=\sqrt{\varepsilon r}$ and $\theta=\sqrt{\varepsilon/r}$. For $\varepsilon=0$ the conclusion is again immediate; suppose it is positive. Call a row good if it contains at most $\theta n$ nonzeros. The number of bad rows is at most $\varepsilon n^2/(\theta n)=sn$, so at least $n(1-s)$ rows are good. Choose a basis of the span of the good rows from among these rows. There are at most $r$ basis rows, and the union of their supports has size at most $r\theta n=sn$. Every good row, being a linear combination of the basis rows, vanishes outside this union. Thus there is a zero rectangle with both dimensions at least $n(1-s)$, and it contains a zero square of at least that side length when $s<1$.

For $0\leq s\leq1/2$, integration gives \[ -\log(1-s)=\int_0^s\frac{dt}{1-t}\leq2s, \] so this proves a square of side at least $ne^{-2\sqrt{\varepsilon r}}$ in the restricted regime $\varepsilon r\leq1/4$. Cardinalities are integers: a lower real bound for each cardinality gives at least its ceiling.

Exact rank-one calculation.
If $M=uv^{\mathsf T}\ne0$, let $a,b$ be the sizes of the supports of $u,v$. Its number of nonzeros is $ab$. A zero Cartesian square $I\times J$ must have either $I$ disjoint from $\operatorname{supp}u$ or $J$ disjoint from $\operatorname{supp}v$, because otherwise a nonzero product occurs. The largest possible side is therefore \[ \max(n-a,n-b)=n-\min(a,b)\geq n-\sqrt{ab}\geq n(1-\sqrt\varepsilon). \] Each of the two alternatives is attained by taking all available zero rows or all available zero columns and a column or row set of equal size. For balanced integral support sizes this bound is sharp.
Attempted iteration and exact obstruction.
The threshold proof uses only the union of the supports of a row basis. Once $\varepsilon r\geq1$, it permits that union to fill every column, and the estimate becomes vacuous. For $M=I_n$, $n\geq3$, one has $r=n$, $\varepsilon=1/n<1/2$ and $s=1$: every basis of the row space has full union of supports. Yet $I\times J$ is zero precisely when $I\cap J=\varnothing$, so the largest zero square has side $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Thus loss of information occurs in the basis-support union, not in the conjecture. To iterate this argument one would need a restriction that reduces rank or density in a controlled ratio while retaining many rows and columns. Merely selecting the good rows proves no such improvement.

Verification and adversarial audit

The proof treats the zero matrix, zero density, and rank-one support degeneracy explicitly. The identity-matrix example was checked from its diagonal support. It is a counterexample to the sufficiency of this threshold estimate, not to the original exponential bound. The chosen row and column sets are independent, as in the source.
