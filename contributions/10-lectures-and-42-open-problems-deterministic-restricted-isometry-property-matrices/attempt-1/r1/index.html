Deterministic RIP: near-square constructions and the missing aspect-ratio quantifier

Definitions and mathematical statement

For $A\in\mathbb C^{M\times N}$ and an integer $s\ge1$, define the restricted isometry constant \[ \delta_s(A)=\sup_{\substack{x\ne0\\|\operatorname{supp}(x)|\le s}} \left|\frac{\|Ax\|_2^2}{\|x\|_2^2}-1\right|. \] Here $\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{j:x_j\ne0\}$; the same definition applies to real matrices and vectors. Thus $(s,1/3)$-RIP means $(2/3)\|x\|_2^2\le\|Ax\|_2^2\le(4/3)\|x\|_2^2$ simultaneously for every vector supported on at most $s$ coordinates.

Construct explicit deterministic rectangular matrices, with $M<N$, that achieve the source's target scale \[ s\ \gtrsim\ \frac{M^{3/5}}{(\log N)^C} \] for an absolute constant $C$, in nonvacuous growing parameter ranges. The hidden positive constant is independent of $M,N$. The requested construction should produce the matrix in polynomial time in its output dimensions, with a specified exact or adequately controlled finite-precision representation. Its guarantee must hold for its actual output, without an unverified random choice.

The interesting ranges have the displayed target substantially larger than $\sqrt M$; otherwise this does not break the usual coherence barrier. An identity matrix with $M=N$ therefore does not answer the question. Likewise, a construction good for one specified sparse vector is insufficient: the same matrix must preserve all sparse vectors of the required order. State explicitly which pairs $(M,N)$ the construction supports and any padding needed for other dimensions.

Short English statement

Construct explicit deterministic restricted-isometry matrices with sparsity of order M to the three-fifths power, up to logarithmic factors.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Construct deterministic matrices $A\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}$ (or $A\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}$ ) satisfying the $(s,\frac13)$ -RIP for $s\approx\frac{M^{0.6}}{\mathrm{polylog}(N)}$

Attempt and results

The preserved definition requires $M<N$ and sparsity substantially above $\sqrt M$, but does not fix a lower bound on $N/M-1$. This matters: near-square matrices give a literal easy family. The following construction is stated with its precise dimensions; it is not claimed as resolution of the intended compressed-sensing problem at fixed aspect ratio.
An exact deleted-Fourier construction.
Take $N=M+1$, $M\ge1$, and $A_{rj}=M^{-1/2}\exp(2\pi i rj/N)$ for $1\le r\le M$ and $0\le j<N$. The geometric-series identity gives \[ A^*A=\frac NM I_N-\frac1M J_N. \] On a support $J$ of cardinality $t$, the Gram error is $M^{-1}(I_t-J_t)$. For $t\ge2$ its eigenvalues are $(1-t)/M$ on the constant vector and $1/M$ on its orthogonal complement; for $t=1$ it is zero. Consequently $\delta_s(A)=(s-1)/M$ for every integer $1\le s\le N$. In particular $s\le1+M/3$ gives the required $1/3$ distortion, including $s$ of order $M^{3/5}$. Entries have an exact succinct algebraic description using a root of unity and a square root, and the output has $MN$ entries. This is the usual simplex Gram matrix, an elementary known construction. There is no claim of a floating-point certificate without error control.
How much codimension this method permits.
More generally delete $r=N-M$ rows from the unitary $N$-point Fourier matrix and scale the remaining rows by $\sqrt{N/M}$. Each column has norm one. For distinct columns the full Fourier inner product is zero, so the retained inner product is minus the deleted sum, of absolute value at most $r/M$. For any $t$-sparse $x$, therefore, \[ |\|Ax\|^2-\|x\|^2| \le\frac rM\sum_{i\ne j}|x_i||x_j| \le\frac rM(t-1)\|x\|^2. \] The last step is Cauchy--Schwarz: $(\sum |x_i|)^2\le t\sum|x_i|^2$. Thus $r\le M^{2/5}$ allows $s$ of order $M^{3/5}$ by this certificate, but only with $N/M\longrightarrow1$.
Why coherence alone stops at fixed aspect ratio.
Let $N$ unit columns lie in $\mathbb C^M$, and let $\mu$ be their maximum off-diagonal inner-product magnitude. If $G=A^*A$ has nonzero eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_q$, $q\le M$, then $\sum\lambda_i=N$ and Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\operatorname{tr}(G^2)\ge N^2/M$. On the other hand $\operatorname{tr}(G^2)\le N+N(N-1)\mu^2$. Hence \[ \mu^2\ge\frac{N-M}{M(N-1)}. \] If $N\ge(1+c)M$ for fixed $c>0$, this is at least $c/((1+c)M)$. A proof relying only on $(s-1)\mu\le1/3$ can then certify only $s\le1+\frac13\sqrt{(1+c)M/c}$. This limits this sufficient test; it is not an upper bound on the actual RIP order of every matrix.

Context, audit, and remaining scope

The audit explicitly distinguishes $N/M\to1$ from $N\ge(1+c)M$, checks $s=1$ and $s=N$, and does not identify a limitation of coherence with an impossibility theorem for RIP. No numerical computation was used. The literal permissive formulation has the displayed near-square family; the source does not specify enough aspect-ratio quantifiers to call that a complete answer to its intended target. A substantive construction with fixed compression ratio and exponent $3/5$ has not been obtained here.
