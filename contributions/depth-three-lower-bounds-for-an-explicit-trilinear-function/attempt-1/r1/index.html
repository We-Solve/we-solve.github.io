Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can one prove a lower bound larger than $\exp(\sqrt n)$ on the size of standard depth-three circuits computing the explicit trilinear function $F_{\mathrm{tet}}(x,y,z)=\sum_{i_1,i_2,i_3:\sum_{j=1}^3|i_j-n/2|\leq n/2}x_{i_1}y_{i_2}z_{i_3}$?

A necessary correction to the preserved editorial definition

The original report, printed pages 3085--3088, concerns standard depth-three Boolean circuits: unbounded fan-in AND and OR gates with literals at the inputs. Its trilinear and AN-complexity models over $\mathbb F_2$ are intermediate restricted models. The preserved catalogue definition above calls the standard circuit arithmetic; that editorial interpretation is inconsistent with the primary report. We retain the original definition for provenance but do not base a solution claim on this mismatch. The source explicitly gives indices $i_1,i_2,i_3\in[n]$. The author's primary webpage likewise distinguishes canonical from standard depth-three Boolean circuits. Its displayed convolution-form trilinear target is not silently identified here with the tetrahedral formula without proving a reduction.

Why the arithmetic reading cannot have the requested bound

Over any field, regard the displayed expression as a formal polynomial. There are at most $n^3$ eligible triples. Compute each monomial by a fan-in-three multiplication and add all outputs. This uses at most $n^3$ multiplication gates and one addition gate, with at most $3n^3+n^3$ wires. If exactly three arithmetic layers are required, insert identity linear forms at the bottom, giving a $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$ circuit with polynomially many gates and wires. The circuit computes the expression by the definition of polynomial addition and multiplication. For sufficiently large $n$ its size is less than $\exp(\sqrt n)$: taking logarithms, $3\log n+O(1)<\sqrt n$, which follows for example by writing $n=e^t$ and using $e^{t/2}\ge t^2/8$ for large $t$ from the exponential series. Thus an exponential lower bound in the ordinary arithmetic gate model would be false. This is a diagnosis of the editorial mismatch, not a negative answer to the actual Boolean question.

An explicit parity restriction and the depth-two barrier

Take even $n\ge2$. Set $y_{n/2}=z_{n/2}=1$ and all other $y,z$ coordinates to zero. For each $i\in[n]$ we have $|i-n/2|\le n/2$, so every remaining monomial is $x_i$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ the restricted function is exactly $x_1\oplus\cdots\oplus x_n$. Assigning constants to a Boolean circuit does not increase depth or size, since each gate still evaluates its original Boolean operation on those assigned inputs.

Every satisfiable conjunction of literals implying odd parity must fix every variable. If a variable were not fixed, two extensions differing only on that variable would satisfy the conjunction while having opposite parities. Hence each such term covers at most one odd-parity input. There are exactly $2^{n-1}$ odd-parity inputs, because flipping the first bit bijects even and odd inputs. Any DNF for parity therefore needs at least $2^{n-1}$ satisfiable terms, and the full minterm DNF attains that number. A CNF clause which is falsifiable and valid for parity must likewise contain every variable: otherwise a falsifying assignment can be extended in both parities. Its falsification set therefore covers at most one even input. This proves the matching $2^{n-1}$ clause lower bound for CNF.

Consequently the parity restriction gives an exponential lower bound for depth two. The requested circuits have depth three, and the restriction preserves those three layers. One cannot apply the depth-two counting argument to a depth-three circuit without an additional depth-reduction theorem.

A concrete reason that extra layer matters

Let $b=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and partition the $n$ variables into $\ell=\lceil n/b\rceil\le b$ nonempty blocks of size at most $b$. For each desired vector of block parities of odd total parity, require each block to have its specified parity. Each such requirement has a CNF with at most $2^{b-1}$ clauses, by listing the assignments of the wrong parity and excluding each with its full-width clause. Their conjunction is still a CNF with at most $\ell2^{b-1}$ clauses. OR these CNFs over the $2^{\ell-1}$ odd block-parity vectors. The result has Boolean layers OR--AND--OR, with literal inputs.

An assignment satisfies this circuit exactly when its actual block-parity vector has odd parity, which is exactly parity of all $n$ variables. The number of bottom clauses, counted even without sharing, is at most $2^{\ell-1}\ell2^{b-1}$, and each has at most $b$ wires. Including all other gates and wires gives $O(b\ell2^{b+\ell})=\exp(O(\sqrt n))$ size. This is an upper bound for the restricted parity function, not for $F_{\rm tet}$. It explains exactly why the depth-two lower-bound mechanism cannot by itself exceed the requested depth-three scale. The report's restricted AN-complexity or canonical-circuit lower bounds also do not transfer to all Boolean circuits simply by deleting the word canonical: a lower bound for a subclass need not hold in a larger class. No normalization theorem supplying that transfer is proved.
