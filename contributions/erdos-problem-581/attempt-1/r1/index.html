Erdős Problem 581

Statement and result.
Let $f(m)$ be the minimum, over triangle-free graphs with exactly $m$ edges, of the largest number of edges in a bipartite subgraph. The known answer is \[ f(m)=\frac m2+\Theta(m^{4/5}). \tag{1} \] We prove the lower bound in detail and derive the upper bound for every integer $m$ from an explicitly stated algebraic graph theorem. That construction theorem is the only deep external input. Equation $1$ is an order estimate for the excess, not an exact formula for each integer.
A cut from vectors.
The largest bipartite subgraph has as many edges as a maximum cut: any bipartite subgraph has a bipartition, and all the graph's edges across that partition can be included. Discard isolated vertices. For each vertex $u$ of degree $d_u>0$, define a unit vector \[ v_u=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(e_u-\frac1{\sqrt{d_u}}\sum_{w\in N(u)}e_w\right) \] in a Euclidean space with orthonormal coordinates indexed by the vertices. If $uv$ is an edge, triangle-freeness gives $N(u)\cap N(v)=\varnothing$, and hence \[ \langle v_u,v_v\rangle=-\frac12\left(d_u^{-1/2}+d_v^{-1/2}\right)=:\rho_{uv}. \] Cut vertices according to the sign of their inner product with a standard Gaussian vector. Rotational symmetry in the plane of two unit vectors says that the probability their signs differ is their angle divided by $\pi$. Since $\rho_{uv}\leq0$ and $\arcsin t\geq t$ for $t\in[0,1]$, this probability is at least $1/2-\rho_{uv}/\pi$. Summing over edges proves \[ \operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\geq\frac m2+ \frac1{2\pi}\sum_u\sqrt{d_u}. \tag{2} \] Degenerate angles are covered by continuity. An outcome attaining the expectation exists.
Graphs with no subgraph of high minimum degree.
Set $D=\lceil m^{2/5}\rceil$, with $m\geq1$. If every nonempty subgraph has a vertex of degree less than $D$, iteratively delete such vertices and orient edges toward vertices deleted later. The outdegrees $d_u^+$ satisfy $d_u^+<D$, $d_u^+\leq d_u$, and $\sum d_u^+=m$. Thus \[ \sum_u\sqrt{d_u}\geq\sum_u\sqrt{d_u^+} \geq\frac m{\sqrt D}\geq\frac1{\sqrt2}m^{4/5}. \] Equation $2$ supplies a gain of at least $m^{4/5}/(2\pi\sqrt2)$.
Graphs with a dense core.
Otherwise there is an induced subgraph $H$ of order $s$ and minimum degree at least $D$. Put $r=\lceil2s/D\rceil\leq3s/D$, and independently sample $r$ vertices of $H$, allowing repetitions. Each vertex fails to have a sampled neighbour with probability at most \[ (1-D/s)^r\leq e^{-2}<1/4. \] Let $W$ be the vertices with a sampled neighbour. By a union bound, each edge of $H$ has both ends in $W$ with probability greater than $1/2$. Some sample therefore has \[ e(H[W])\geq e(H)/2\geq sD/4. \tag{3} \] Colour each vertex of $W$ by the index of its first sampled neighbour. This is a proper colouring with at most $r$ colours, because two adjacent vertices with the same sampled neighbour would make a triangle.

Randomly partition the $r$ colour labels into parts of sizes $\lfloor r/2\rfloor,\lceil r/2\rceil$. Two distinct labels are separated with probability \[ \frac{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor\lceil r/2\rceil}{r(r-1)} \geq\frac12+\frac1{2r}. \] Hence $3$ yields a cut of $H[W]$ exceeding half its edges by at least \[ \frac{sD}{8r}\geq\frac{D^2}{24}\geq\frac{m^{4/5}}{24}. \] Assign all vertices outside $W$ independently and fairly to the two cut classes. Every other edge crosses with probability $1/2$, so some extension retains this gain above $m/2$. Together with the previous case this proves, for example, \[ f(m)\geq m/2+m^{4/5}/100. \tag{4} \]

The external algebraic family and its spectral consequence.
We use Alon's explicit construction in the following form. For each integer $a\geq2$ not divisible by three, a triangle-free regular graph exists with \[ n_a=2^{3a},\qquad d_a=2^{a-1}(2^{a-1}-1),\qquad \lambda_{\min}\geq-9\cdot2^a-3\cdot2^{a/2}-1/4. \tag{5} \] Here $\lambda_{\min}$ is the smallest adjacency eigenvalue. The finite-field construction establishing $5$ is accepted as an external theorem; it is not proved by the cut calculations below.

For any graph with adjacency matrix $A$ and cut sign vector $z\in\{-1,1\}^n$, \[ \text{cut size}=\frac m2-\frac14 z^TAz \leq\frac m2-\frac14\lambda_{\min}n. \tag{6} \] The inequality follows by expansion in an orthonormal eigenbasis and $\|z\|^2=n$. Applying $6$ to $5$, with $m_a=n_ad_a/2$, gives \[ \operatorname{MaxCut}(G_a)\leq m_a/2+C m_a^{4/5}, \tag{7} \] for an absolute $C$: indeed $2^{5a-4}\leq m_a<2^{5a-3}$, whereas $-\lambda_{\min}n_a=O(2^{4a})$.

Filling every edge count.
This last step matters because $7$ alone only covers a subsequence. The smallest allowed index is $a=2$, with $m_2=64$. Consecutive allowed indices differ by one or two. Directly from \[ m_a=2^{4a-2}(2^{a-1}-1) \] the ratio of consecutive allowed edge counts is greater than $32$ and less than $4096$. Greedily subtract the largest $m_a$ not exceeding the current remainder. The resulting representation of any integer $m$ uses at most $4095$ copies at each scale, and leaves a remainder less than $64$.

Take the disjoint union of those graph copies and one isolated edge for each remaining unit. This is triangle-free with exactly $m$ edges. Maximum cut is additive across components. Moreover, the geometric separation of the scales gives \[ \sum_a c_a m_a^{4/5} \leq4095\sum_{j\geq0}(m/32^j)^{4/5}=O(m^{4/5}), \] where $c_a$ are the greedy multiplicities; missing scales only decrease the sum. The fewer than $64$ isolated edges contribute a bounded additional excess. This proves $f(m)\leq m/2+O(m^{4/5})$ for all $m$, and completes $1$ together with $4$.
