A dissipative resonator cell.
Let $u(t)$ be a macroscopic strain coordinate, $q(t)$ an internal resonator, and \[ E=\tfrac12K u^2+g u q+\tfrac12kq^2+\tfrac12m\dot q^2, \quad m,k,K>0,\quad Kk>g^2. \] Take $m\ddot q+\gamma\dot q+kq=-gu$, with $\gamma>0$, and stress $\sigma=Ku+gq$. Differentiation gives the exact power balance $\dot E=\sigma\dot u-\gamma\dot q^2$. The condition $Kk>g^2$ makes the stored quadratic form positive by completing the square.
Elimination retains frequency and loss.
For harmonic inputs proportional to $e^{-i\omega t}$, eliminating $q$ gives \[ \sigma(\omega)=\left(K-\frac{g^2}{k-m\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}\right)u(\omega). \] The imaginary part is negative for $\omega>0$, consistent with the chosen Fourier convention and positive dissipation. Replacing the denominator by $k$ produces the static modulus $K-g^2/k$, but loses the resonance and frequency-dependent loss. In the time domain a purely instantaneous static law therefore cannot reproduce all harmonic responses of even this one-cell model.
