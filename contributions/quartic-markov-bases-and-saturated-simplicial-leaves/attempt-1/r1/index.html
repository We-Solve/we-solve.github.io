Quartic Markov bases and saturated simplicial leaves

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For an integer matrix $A$, the fiber at $b$ is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves $u$ with $Au=0$ such that steps $x\mapsto x\pm u$ staying nonnegative connect every nonempty fiber. Binary hierarchical models have two levels per variable. For a move $u$, write $u=u^+-u^-$ with disjoint nonnegative parts; its degree is the total $\sum_i u_i^+$, equal to $\sum_i u_i^-$ when the total-count statistic is fixed. The task is to characterize models admitting a Markov basis with every move of degree at most four.

Research attempt

Literature and scope

Problem 28 asks for all binary hierarchical models of Markov degree at most four. I tested the tempting expectation that fixing only binary levels controls the degree, using the no-highest-interaction family. The calculation below is a familiar parity construction, not a new general classification.
Proposition: For $r\ge2$ binary variables with all $(r-1)$-variable margins prescribed, the minimal required Markov degree is $2^{r-1}$. Thus this restricted family has a degree-at-most-four basis exactly when $r\le3$.
Proof: Index cells by $x\in\{0,1\}^r$ and put $u_x=(-1)^{x_1+\cdots+x_r}$. If an integer move $v$ preserves the margin omitting coordinate $j$, its entries satisfy $v_x+v_{x\oplus e_j}=0$. Successive bit flips therefore give $v_x=v_0u_x$. Conversely $u$ satisfies every such equation, so the integer kernel is exactly $\mathbb Z u$.

For a nonempty fiber choose a table $z$. Its elements are $z+tu$ for integers $t$ satisfying \[ -\min_{u_x=1}z_x\ \le t\le\ \min_{u_x=-1}z_x. \] All integers in this interval occur. The moves $\pm u$ connect it, and the positive part of $u$ has $2^{r-1}$ entries equal to one. To prove necessity, let $u^+,u^-$ be its positive and negative parts. The fiber through $u^+$ consists exactly of $u^+$ and $u^-$: the interval is $[-1,0]$. Any Markov basis must connect these two tables, forcing a step $\pm u$. No smaller-degree moves can do so. ∎

Failed route and original-scope comparison

Already $r=4$ forces degree eight, refuting an attempted universal quartic bound based solely on binary state spaces. The proof uses the special kernel equations of this interaction family. Removing or changing facets can enlarge the kernel and invalidate the two-point-fiber argument; no inference about all binary complexes is justified.

Verification and remaining gaps

The degree sequence for $r=2,\ldots,8$ is $2,4,8,16,32,64,128$. This is an exact formula evaluation, not an exhaustive classification. The required characterization of all binary hierarchical models remains unresolved by this attempt.
