Definitions and notation.
A hyper-Kähler metric has three parallel complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations. In four dimensions a gravitational instanton is a complete noncompact Ricci-flat geometry in the source’s self-dual setting. ALE and ALF describe different asymptotic geometries; ALF has an asymptotic circle direction over a three-dimensional base. For a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$, $L^2$ norms use the metric volume and pointwise metric on forms. Reduced $L^2$ cohomology is $\ker d/\overline{\operatorname{im}d}$ for the specified closed $L^2$ complex. On a complete manifold its harmonic representatives satisfy $d\alpha=d^*\alpha=0$ and $\int_M|\alpha|^2<\infty$. In even middle degree, the Hodge star splits harmonic forms into self-dual and anti-self-dual sectors. Their dimension difference is an $L^2$ signature when finite and suitably defined. An index theorem must specify the Fredholm operator and the asymptotic or boundary corrections needed on a noncompact space. Toric hyper-Kähler quotients have combinatorial data associated with the reducing torus.

A sign criterion without an index theorem.
Let $M$ be an oriented complete Riemannian manifold of dimension $4m$, and suppose the real space $\mathcal H$ of square-integrable harmonic $2m$-forms is finite dimensional. The Hodge star satisfies $*^2=1$ in this degree, is an isometry and preserves harmonic forms, so $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_+\oplus\mathcal H_-$. The integral pairing is \[Q(\alpha,\beta)=\int_M\alpha\wedge\beta=\langle\alpha,*\beta\rangle_{L^2}. \] It converges absolutely by Cauchy--Schwarz. On the two eigenspaces it is respectively the positive and negative L2 inner product; cross terms vanish because distinct eigenspaces of a self-adjoint involution are orthogonal. Hence $\operatorname{sign}Q=\dim\mathcal H_+-\dim\mathcal H_-$. If a geometric argument places all middle harmonic forms in one star eigenspace, the sign is forced, but that placement is an extra theorem.
Boundary control cannot be skipped.
On a compact truncation $M_R$, replacing a closed form by $\alpha+d\eta$ changes its pairing with a closed $\beta$ by $\int_{\partial M_R}\eta\wedge\beta$, by Stokes. Square integrability of $\alpha,\beta$ alone does not specify an admissible primitive $\eta$ or force that boundary term to vanish. Therefore importing a compact topological signature formula requires additional decay/range control.
