Extremal Top-$m$ Representability of Output Projection Matrices

Literal scope and a strict-separation route

The catalogue explicitly notes that its literal largest-$m$ wording admits $m=N$, which imposes no exclusion of other tokens. With that reading every matrix works for the full set, so the wording cannot express the intended nontrivial extremal question. We retain the qualification rather than declare the research problem solved. For a useful auxiliary route, we explicitly require $1\le m<N$ and strict separation of the selected tokens from the rest; this is an additional model choice, not an amendment to the source.
Proposition: For every $N>m\ge1$ and $d\ge2m$, there is an $N\times d$ matrix whose logits strictly rank every prescribed $m$-element subset above its complement.
Proof: Choose distinct real numbers $t_1,\ldots,t_N$, and let row $i$ be $(t_i,t_i^2,\ldots,t_i^{2m})$, followed by zero coordinates if necessary. For a desired subset $S$, expand \[ p_S(t)=-\prod_{i\in S}(t-t_i)^2=c_0+c_1t+\cdots+c_{2m}t^{2m}. \] Use embedding $x=(c_1,\ldots,c_{2m},0,\ldots,0)$. The $i$th logit is $p_S(t_i)-c_0$. It equals $-c_0$ for $i\in S$ and is strictly smaller for every $i\notin S$. Softmax preserves strict logit inequalities because the exponential is strictly increasing and all probabilities share the same positive denominator. ∎
This familiar polynomial-separation construction assigns equal selected logits; it does not prescribe arbitrary probabilities within the selected set. Negating an embedding reverses selected-versus-complement ranking, so the property is symmetric between $m$ and $N-m$. This is another reason an unqualified largest-$m$ parameter needs care. The one-dimensional route has a sharp elementary limitation: with scalar rows, positive $x$ can give at most one strictly separated top-$m$ set and negative $x$ at most one. Zero $x$ gives no strict separation. For $N\ge3$ and $1\le m<N$, there are at least three subsets, so no $d=1$ matrix has the all-subsets property. Allowing all ties would change this conclusion.
