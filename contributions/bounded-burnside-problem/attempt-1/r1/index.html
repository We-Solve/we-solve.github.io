A direct determination of the free two-generator group of exponent three

Problem: prb_b34e029e3add4c77869460a4aea60adc. Claim type: Partial result.

The free Burnside group $B(2,3)$ is the unitriangular Heisenberg group $\mathrm{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3)$, of order 27. We give both a universal upper bound and a matching explicit quotient. This is a classical small-exponent result, not a solution of the official all-pairs classification or of its highlighted $B(2,5)$ case.

Let $G$ be any group of exponent dividing three. For any $x,y\in G$ put
$$a=x,\qquad b=y^{-1}xy,\qquad c=yxy^{-1}.$$
Since $y^3=1$, expanding the exponent identities gives
$$1=(xy)^3=acb,\qquad 1=(xy^{-1})^3=abc.$$
Canceling $a$ shows $bc=cb$, and $a=(bc)^{-1}$ therefore commutes with both $b$ and $c$. In particular $x$ commutes with $y^{-1}xy$.

Use the commutator convention $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. The preceding observation shows $[x,y]$ commutes with $x$. Interchanging $x$ and $y$ shows $[y,x]=[x,y]^{-1}$ commutes with $y$, so $[x,y]$ also commutes with $y$. Therefore the commutator is central in the subgroup generated by $x,y$. Writing $z=[x,y]$, the relation $xy=yxz$ lets every word be collected as
$$x^a y^b z^c,\qquad a,b,c\in\{0,1,2\}.$$
Inverses and exponents reduce modulo three because every element has cube one. Hence every two-generator exponent-three group, in particular $B(2,3)$, has at most 27 elements.

For the lower bound, take the set $H=\mathbb F_3^3$ with multiplication
$$
(a,b,c)(d,e,f)=(a+d,b+e,c+f+ae).
$$
It is realized by the matrices
$$\begin{pmatrix}1&a&c\\0&1&b\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},$$
so it is a group of order 27. Alternatively associativity follows by expanding both triple products. The power formula is
$$ (a,b,c)^j=(ja,jb,jc+\tbinom j2 ab).$$
For $j=3$ every coordinate vanishes, so $H$ has exponent three. The elements $x=(1,0,0)$ and $y=(0,1,0)$ have commutator $z=(0,0,1)$ and generate all of $H$. The universal property gives a surjection $B(2,3)\twoheadrightarrow H$. Together with the bound of 27 this proves an isomorphism.

The same definitions immediately settle useful boundary rows: $B(1,n)\simeq C_n$ for every $n\ge1$, $B(m,1)$ is trivial, and $B(m,2)\simeq C_2^m$. For the last assertion, $(ab)^2=1$ and $a^2=b^2=1$ imply $ab=ba$, giving the upper bound $2^m$; the elementary abelian group realizes it. The argument above for exponent three only asserts the rank-two case, rather than assuming that every exponent-three group is of nilpotency class two.

`verify.py` checks all 19,683 associativity triples in $H$, all 27 exponent identities, the exact commutator, all 27 distinct collected forms, and the closure of the two displayed generators. This verifies the finite quotient used for the lower bound. The symbolic collection argument supplies the universal upper bound. `verification.json` records the actual output.

The checked current formulation source, Donoso-Echenique and Silva, arXiv:2608.20472v2, §1, uses the same free Burnside definition and explicitly identifies $B(2,5)$ as an unsettled small case. That status is context; no theorem about exponent five is established here. Finiteness of all finite quotients subject to a uniform order bound would not by itself prove finiteness of the entire free group in the official definition. No restricted-Burnside theorem is substituted for the present problem.
