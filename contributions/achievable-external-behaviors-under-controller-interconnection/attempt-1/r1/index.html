Research attempt: A trace-level controller criterion and an autonomous bisimulation test

What exact control can accomplish at the level of sets
Let $W,C$ be arbitrary trajectory sets, let the plant relation be $P\subseteq W\times C$, and let a controller allow a subset $D\subseteq C$. Define $P_c=\{w:(w,c)\in P\}$, so the resulting external behavior is $\bigcup_{c\in D}P_c$. For desired external set $K\subseteq W$, put \[ D_K=\{c\in C:P_c\subseteq K\}. \] Then $K$ is exactly achievable in this relation model if and only if \[ K=\bigcup_{c\in D_K}P_c. \tag{1} \] If a controller realizes $K$, every one of its fibers is contained in $K$, so $D\subseteq D_K$ and $K\subseteq\bigcup_{c\in D_K}P_c\subseteq K$. Conversely the controller $D_K$ realizes $K$ when $1$ holds. Empty fibers cause no problem. The statement assumes any subset is an admissible controller; causal realizability or finite-dimensional realization can impose further restrictions.
Trace equality does not supply bisimulation
Consider labeled transition systems with one initial state each. System $P$ has transitions $p\xrightarrow{a}p_b$ and $p\xrightarrow{a}p_c$, followed only by $p_b\xrightarrow{b}p_0$ and $p_c\xrightarrow{c}p_0$. System $Q$ has $q\xrightarrow{a}q_{bc}$ followed by both $q_{bc}\xrightarrow{b}q_0$ and $q_{bc}\xrightarrow{c}q_0$. Both have precisely the finite traces $\epsilon,a,ab,ac$. They are not bisimilar: matching the transition to $p_b$ forces it to be related to $q_{bc}$, whose $c$ transition has no match from $p_b$. Thus $1$, even when perfectly solved and realized, cannot by itself characterize the source's required branching equivalence.
A finite test for autonomous linear systems
For the restricted systems $\dot x_i=A_ix_i$, $y_i=C_ix_i$ with no inputs or auxiliary disturbances, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,A_2)$ and set \[ R_0=\ker[\,C_1\ -C_2\,],\qquad R_{k+1}=R_k\cap D^{-1}(R_k). \] The descending subspaces stabilize after at most $n_1+n_2$ strict dimension drops. At stabilization $R_*=R_*\cap D^{-1}R_*$, so $DR_*\subseteq R_*$. The flow $e^{tD}$ preserves $R_*$: each power of $D$ preserves it, and the exponential series converges inside this finite-dimensional closed subspace. Hence every pair of states in $R_*$ has equal outputs for all times. Conversely any linear bisimulation relation is contained in $R_0$ and is $D$-invariant, because differentiating its paired trajectories at time zero keeps their derivative in the linear subspace. Induction places it in every $R_k$, and thus in $R_*$. Therefore $R_*$ is the maximal linear bisimulation relation for this autonomous setting. The systems are bisimilar over their whole state spaces precisely when both coordinate projections of $R_*$ are surjective; each condition is a matrix rank test. Kernel, intersection and preimage calculations use finite Gaussian elimination.

This test can verify an explicitly supplied autonomous closed-loop candidate but does not find all admissible controllers or cover the source's disturbance quantifiers. The missing synthesis step remains substantial, rather than being supplied by trace equality.
