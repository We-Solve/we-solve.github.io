Erdős Problem #149 (Round 2): corrected formulation and a broad solved class

1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE

Here I pursue path (C): the literal Round-1 formulation \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \] is not the standard exact Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture when $\Delta$ is odd. The minimal corrected target is \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is even}, \] and \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is odd}. \] My Round-2 goal is then to prove this corrected statement for a nontrivial infinite class of graphs, thereby excluding that entire class from any potential counterexample.

The class I will handle is: all $2$-degenerate graphs.

2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED

I use the following Round-1 points.
  1. The line-graph reformulation \[ \chi'_s(G)=\chi(L(G)^2). \]
  2. The Round-1 greedy upper bound \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1. \]
  3. The Round-1 $C_5$-blow-up example showing that the conjectured constant is tight in the even-$\Delta$ regime.
  4. The Round-1 ambiguity note that the odd-$\Delta$ case requires a piecewise correction.

3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND 2)

The new ingredient is to combine two known theorems that were not used in Round 1.
  1. Andersen's theorem. Every graph with maximum degree at most $3$ has strong chromatic index at most $10$.
  2. Yu's theorem for $k$-degenerate graphs. Every $k$-degenerate graph of maximum degree $\Delta$ satisfies \[ \chi'_s(G)\le (4k-2)\Delta-k(2k-1)+1. \] For $k=2$, this becomes \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \]

These two statements together imply that the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil bound holds for every $2$-degenerate graph.

4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND 2)

Round 1 left a huge gap between the general greedy bound and the conjectured piecewise bound. I will not close that general gap, but I will prove a broad solved class.

The exact claims to prove are:

  1. If $G$ is $2$-degenerate and $\Delta\ge 4$, then Yu's theorem gives a linear bound $6\Delta-5$, and this linear bound is already at most the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil target.
  2. If $G$ is $2$-degenerate and $\Delta\le 3$, then the corrected target also holds: for $\Delta\le 2$ this follows from the Round-1 greedy bound, and for $\Delta=3$ it follows from Andersen's theorem.

This will prove the conjectured bound for all $2$-degenerate graphs and hence show that any counterexample must have degeneracy at least $3$ (and in particular maximum degree at least $4$).

5) WORK (ROUND 2)

External theorem 1 (Andersen).
If $G$ is a graph with maximum degree at most $3$, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 10. \]
External theorem 2 (Yu).
If $G$ is a $k$-degenerate graph with maximum degree $\Delta$, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le (4k-2)\Delta-k(2k-1)+1. \] In particular, if $G$ is $2$-degenerate, then \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \]
Claim.
Every $2$-degenerate graph satisfies the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil bound \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac54\,\Delta^2 \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is even}, \] and \[ \chi'_s(G)\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} \qquad \mbox{if $\Delta$ is odd}. \]
Proof.
Let $G$ be $2$-degenerate, and let $\Delta=\Delta(G)$.

Case 1: $\Delta\le 2$. By the Round-1 greedy bound, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 2\Delta^2-2\Delta+1. \] For $\Delta=1$, this is $1$, which equals the corrected target. For $\Delta=2$, this is $5$, which again equals the corrected target \[ \frac54\cdot 2^2=5. \]

Case 2: $\Delta=3$. By Andersen's theorem, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 10. \] The corrected odd-$\Delta$ target equals \[ \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4} = \frac{45-6+1}{4} =10. \] So the target holds exactly.

Case 3: $\Delta\ge 4$ and even. By Yu's theorem, \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \] So it is enough to check \[ 6\Delta-5\le \frac54\,\Delta^2. \] This is equivalent to \[ 5\Delta^2-24\Delta+20\ge 0. \] The quadratic function on the left has derivative $10\Delta-24>0$ for every $\Delta\ge 3$, so it is increasing on $[4,\infty)$. At $\Delta=4$ it equals \[ 5\cdot 16-24\cdot 4+20=4>0. \] Hence it is positive for every even $\Delta\ge 4$, proving the desired inequality.

Case 4: $\Delta\ge 5$ and odd. Again Yu gives \[ \chi'_s(G)\le 6\Delta-5. \] Now it is enough to check \[ 6\Delta-5\le \frac{5\Delta^2-2\Delta+1}{4}. \] This is equivalent to \[ 5\Delta^2-26\Delta+21\ge 0. \] Its derivative is $10\Delta-26>0$ for every $\Delta\ge 3$, so it is increasing on $[5,\infty)$. At $\Delta=5$ it equals \[ 5\cdot 25-26\cdot 5+21=16>0. \] Hence it is positive for every odd $\Delta\ge 5$.

All cases are proved.

Corollary.
Any counterexample to the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture must have degeneracy at least $3$ and maximum degree at least $4$.
Proof.
If the graph had degeneracy at most $2$, the claim above would dispose of it. If it had maximum degree at most $3$, Cases 1 and 2 would dispose of it. Therefore every counterexample lies outside both ranges.

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

I check the delicate points.
  1. Odd-$\Delta$ correction. The corrected target is the standard piecewise Erdős--Nešetřil form, not simply $\lceil 5\Delta^2/4\rceil$. This matters already at $\Delta=3$, where the exact target is $10$.
  2. Small degrees. Yu's linear estimate is not enough for $\Delta=3$, since it gives $13$. That is why Andersen's theorem is required in Case 2.
  3. Use of Round-1 results. The Round-1 greedy bound is only used for $\Delta\le 2$, where it is exact enough. There is no hidden dependence on unproved new claims.
  4. Hypotheses of external theorems. Yu requires only $k$-degeneracy and maximum degree; Andersen requires only $\Delta\le 3$. These hypotheses are exactly what is used here.

7) FINAL

UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).

Round 2 does not solve the corrected Erdős--Nešetřil conjecture in full, but it gives a real structural advance over Round 1:

  1. it replaces the vague odd-$\Delta$ formulation by the standard exact piecewise conjecture;
  2. it proves that the corrected conjecture holds for every $2$-degenerate graph;
  3. consequently, it rules out all $2$-degenerate graphs from the search for a counterexample.
