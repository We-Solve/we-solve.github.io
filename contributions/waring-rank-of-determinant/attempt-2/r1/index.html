A self-contained apolar half-length lower bound for determinant Waring rank

**Problem:** TOP-3032

**Claim type:** Partial result

**Exact scope:** For every d≥2, the complex determinant has Waring rank at least max{binomial(d,floor(d/2))², (1/2)binomial(2d,d)}; quadratic generation of its apolar ideal and the half-length bound are proved explicitly, with exact derivative-space checks through d=5.

Bound and comparison

For the generic $d\times d$ determinant $D_d$ over $\mathbb C$, with $d\ge2$, we prove

$$R_{\mathrm W}(D_d)\ge
\max\left\{\binom d{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}^2,\ \frac12\binom{2d}{d}\right\}.$$

The second inequality is the known Ranestad–Schreyer–Shafiei apolar-length bound. The proof below specializes its mechanism to ordinary Waring decompositions and establishes all required determinant algebra directly. This is not a claimed new bound or a claim of optimality. Stronger rank-specific bounds are known; the table below compares only the two inequalities actually proved here.

The earlier revision proves the first, derivative-space bound and a polarization upper bound, but does not prove the quadratic apolar ideal or the half-length obstruction. Those are the additional results here.

Apolar algebra and its length

Let $T=\mathbb C[y_{ij}]$ act on $\mathbb C[x_{ij}]$ by $y_{ij}=\partial/\partial x_{ij}$. Write $D_d^\perp$ for the homogeneous ideal of operators annihilating $D_d$. Its degree-$k$ quotient has dimension equal to the span of all order-$k$ derivatives of $D_d$.

Such a derivative is zero if it repeats a row or a column. Otherwise it is, up to sign, a complementary $(d-k)$-minor. Every such minor occurs by differentiating any matching of the deleted rows to the deleted columns. Distinct minors of fixed size have disjoint monomial supports, because a monomial specifies its row and column sets. They are consequently linearly independent. Therefore

$$h_k:=\dim(T/D_d^\perp)_k=\binom dk^2\quad(0\le k\le d),$$

and $h_k=0$ for $k>d$. The apolar algebra has length

$$L=\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk^2=\binom{2d}{d},$$

where the last identity counts choices of $d$ objects from two disjoint $d$-element sets by how many are chosen from the first.

Quadratic generation by straightening

Let $J$ be generated by the following quadrics:

- $y_{ij}^2$;
- $y_{ij}y_{i\ell}$ and $y_{ji}y_{\ell i}$ for distinct column or row indices;
- $y_{ij}y_{k\ell}+y_{i\ell}y_{kj}$ for $i\ne k$, $j\ne\ell$.

Each generator annihilates $D_d$. The first two kinds follow from determinant multilinearity. In the last kind, the two differentiated complementary minors have opposite determinant signs, so they cancel. Thus $J\subseteq D_d^\perp$.

Modulo $J$, every monomial repeating a row or a column vanishes. A remaining degree-$k$ monomial is a matching of a $k$-row set $I$ with a $k$-column set $C$. The permanent relations in the last bullet interchange the columns assigned to two selected rows and change the sign. By successive transpositions, every matching is congruent up to sign to the order-preserving matching of $I$ and $C$. Hence $(T/J)_k$ is spanned by at most one monomial for each pair $(I,C)$, so its dimension is at most $\binom dk^2$. Above degree $d$, every monomial repeats a row and vanishes.

The surjection $T/J\twoheadrightarrow T/D_d^\perp$ gives the reverse dimension inequality in every degree. The computed $h_k$ forces equality. Hence $J=D_d^\perp$: the apolar ideal is generated in degree two. No Gröbner confluence or generic term-order assertion is being assumed; spanning plus the independent derivative-space dimension proves the equality.

A quadric avoiding a proposed Waring support

Suppose $D_d=\sum_{\nu=1}^r\ell_\nu^d$. After merging proportional summands and removing cancellations, let $Z$ be its distinct projective coefficient points, of cardinality $r'\le r$. Let $I_Z$ be their homogeneous vanishing ideal. If a degree-$k$ operator $\Theta$ vanishes at the coefficient point of $\ell$, then

$$\Theta(\partial)\ell^d=\frac{d!}{(d-k)!}\Theta(\ell)\ell^{d-k}=0$$

for $k\le d$, and differentiation is zero by degree for $k>d$. Summing gives $I_Z\subseteq D_d^\perp$.

The quadrics of $D_d^\perp$ have no common projective zero, since they include every coordinate square $y_{ij}^2$. At any point of $Z$, evaluation on this vector space of quadrics is therefore a nonzero linear functional. Over the infinite field $\mathbb C$, finitely many proper hyperplanes cannot cover the whole vector space. Choose a quadric $q\in D_d^\perp$ nonvanishing at every point of $Z$.

Put $R=T/I_Z$. Multiplication by $q$ is injective on $R$: if $qg$ vanishes on every point and $q$ vanishes on none, then $g$ vanishes on every point, so $g\in I_Z$. Thus the exact degree-two sequence gives

$$\dim(R/qR)_k=h_R(k)-h_R(k-2).$$

For sufficiently large $k$, the Hilbert function of the reduced set of $r'$ projective points is $r'$. This follows, for example, by choosing a linear form nonzero on all points and homogenizing finite interpolation polynomials that separate them. Summing the difference formula telescopes to

$$\mathrm{length}(R/qR)=2r'.$$

Since $(I_Z,q)\subseteq D_d^\perp$, there is a surjection $R/qR\twoheadrightarrow T/D_d^\perp$. Consequently

$$\binom{2d}{d}=L\le2r'\le2r,$$

which is the half-length lower bound. This argument concerns actual Waring supports. It does not silently replace Waring rank by border rank or assume an approximating family of powers has a fixed support.

Computation and limitation

The derivative-space inequality $R_{\mathrm W}(F)\ge\dim V_k(F)$ follows because derivatives of a sum of $r$ powers span at most $r$ powers of the lower degree. Combining it with the new-to-this-revision apolar obstruction gives:

| d | Central derivative bound | Half apolar length | Combined proved bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 3 | 9 | 10 | 10 |
| 4 | 36 | 35 | 36 |
| 5 | 100 | 126 | 126 |
| 6 | 400 | 462 | 462 |

The standard-library verifier expands the determinant and enumerates **every differential monomial** through degree $d$, including repetitions, for $2\le d\le5$. Sparse rational Gaussian elimination computes the actual derivative ranks, rather than assuming the minor count. It separately checks all listed quadratic annihilators in those dimensions. The resulting profiles are $(1,4,1)$, $(1,9,9,1)$, $(1,16,36,16,1)$ and $(1,25,100,100,25,1)$. Their lengths are $6,20,70,252$. These finite calculations check signs and dimensions; the proof above supplies every $d$.

Exact ranks for arbitrary $d$ are not determined. In particular the displayed bound ten at $d=3$ is not advertised as the best available lower bound. The cited Shafiei paper itself explains stronger ordinary-rank bounds in small dimensions. This report strengthens the earlier elementary attempt with a fully proved apolar mechanism and an exact checkable certificate.
