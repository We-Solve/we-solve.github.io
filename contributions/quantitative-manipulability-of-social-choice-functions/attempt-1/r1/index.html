An exact exponentially rare-manipulation family and its distance caveat

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For a social choice function $f$ on profiles of voters' complete preferences, what fraction of profiles admit a profitable manipulation by some voter? Can this fraction be tiny, perhaps exponentially small, even for nontrivial functions?

Source scope and attempted construction

The primary report specifies independent uniformly random complete preference orders, and calls a rule nontrivial if it is not a dictatorship and has at least three possible outcomes. Crucially its footnote already observes that rules extremely close to a dictatorship can have very few manipulable profiles. We make that known obstruction explicit, even under neutrality, and count both manipulable profiles and successful random misreports exactly. This is a rederivation of the source's caveat, not a new quantitative theorem for rules bounded away from triviality.

Exact construction and proof

Fix three alternatives, $n\ge2$ voters, and write a complete order as a three-entry list. Define $f$ as follows. If all voters report the same order $R=(a,b,c)$, choose its second alternative $b$; at every other profile choose the first voter's top alternative. Relabeling alternatives carries both cases and the chosen list position to their relabeled versions, so $f$ is neutral. At a unanimous profile it disagrees with every voter's top, so it is no voter's dictatorship. Its range is all three alternatives, because unanimity orders can place any specified alternative second.

Every unanimous profile is manipulable: voter 1 can replace $(a,b,c)$ by $(a,c,b)$, producing the preferred outcome $a$. At a nonunanimous profile the chosen outcome is already voter 1's true top, so voter 1 cannot profit. Another voter $i>1$ can change the outcome only by making the profile unanimous. This requires all other voters to have a common order $R=(a,b,c)$ and voter $i$ to have a different true order $S$. The change from $a$ to $b$ is profitable exactly when $S$ ranks $b$ above $a$. Exactly three of the six possible orders do so; none equals $R$. Thus for each $R$ and $i>1$ there are three such profiles. These profiles are distinct: $R$ is the first voter's order and, when $n>2$, the sole exceptional index is determined; when $n=2$, the only possible index is 2. Together with the six unanimous profiles, their number is \[ 6+18(n-1)=6(3n-2),\qquad \Pr[\text{some profitable misreport exists}]=\frac{3n-2}{6^{n-1}}. \] No other profiles can be manipulable by the preceding classification. In particular the answer to the small-fraction existence subquestion is yes under the report's basic nontriviality definition, even for neutral rules. There is no claim that this family is extremal among all nontrivial rules.

Exact random-misreport powers.
The report's $M_i$ samples both the profile and a fresh independent uniform report for voter $i$. This differs from the existence probability above. Voter 1 can profit only at a unanimous profile. There exactly one of the six possible reports, $(a,c,b)$, gives the only better outcome $a$. Hence $M_1=6/6^{n+1}=6^{-n}$. For a voter $i>1$, each of the six unanimous profiles has five profitable reports, and each of the eighteen classified nonunanimous profiles for that voter has one profitable report, namely $R$. Consequently \[ M_i=\frac{6\cdot5+18}{6^{n+1}}=\frac8{6^n}\quad(i>1), \qquad \sum_iM_i=\frac{8n-7}{6^n}. \] These calculations also demonstrate why sampling a random false report and asking whether one exists must not be interchanged without a factor.
Why this cannot defeat a fixed-distance theorem.
The rule differs from voter 1's dictatorship at exactly the six unanimous profiles, and nowhere else. Its distance from that dictatorship is $6/6^n=6^{1-n}$. Its distance from the set of all dictatorships is therefore at most this quantity, which tends to zero exponentially. This is not a family with a fixed positive distance parameter $\varepsilon$. In particular the source's lower bound involving $\varepsilon^2$ is not contradicted by the exponentially small manipulation probability.

Adversarial audit, computation, and remaining scope

An exhaustive exact Python check enumerated all $36,216,1296,7776$ profiles for $n=2,3,4,5$, respectively, and all six reports of each voter. It found $24,42,60,78$ manipulable profiles and successful profile/report counts $6$ for voter 1 and $48$ for every other voter, exactly as proved above. The program and raw counts accompany this manuscript; they are finite checks, not a substitute for the general counting proof. The later Mossel--Racz abstract states inverse-polynomial profile bounds under distance from nonmanipulable rules, without neutrality. Its full proof was not inspected here and is not presented as this attempt's result. A general sharp fraction for arbitrary specified rules or for rules a fixed distance from triviality is not obtained. The construction instantiates an already acknowledged source caveat, so no original-target progress percentage is assigned despite its complete answer to that limited existence subquestion.
