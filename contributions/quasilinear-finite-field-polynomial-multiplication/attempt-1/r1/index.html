Quasilinear Finite-Field Polynomial Multiplication

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

$\mathbb F_p$ is the prime field, and multiplying two degree-at-most-$d$ inputs returns their exact product. $M_p(d)$ counts bit operations in the stated multitape Turing model. Uniform big-O means its implied constant is independent of both $p$ and $d$.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For a prime $p$, let $M_p(d)$ denote the multitape-Turing-machine cost of multiplying polynomials of degree at most $d$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$, and put $n=d\log p$. Prove that $M_p(d)=O(n\log n)$ uniformly in $p$ and $d$, including when $p=2$. [S2]

Explicit packing and its small-characteristic cost

Let $p$ be prime, $d\ge1$, and represent input coefficients of degree at most $d$ by integers in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. Set \[ b=\left\lceil\log_2\big((d+1)(p-1)^2+1\big)\right\rceil, \quad B=2^b,\quad N=(d+1)b. \]
Proposition: One multiplication of two integers of at most $N$ bits, followed by digit extraction and reduction modulo $p$, recovers the polynomial product. If integer multiplication costs $M(N)$ bit operations, the direct binary-remainder implementation costs \[ O\big(M(N)+db\log_2 p\big). \] For $p=2$ and $M(N)=O(N\log N)$, this gives $O(d(\log d)^2)$ for $d\ge2$, rather than the desired $O(d\log d)$.
Proof: Encode $a(x),c(x)$ as $a(B),c(B)$. Both are below $B^{d+1}$. The integer convolution coefficient $h_k=\sum_{i+j=k}a_ic_j$ satisfies $0\le h_k\le(d+1)(p-1)^2<B$. Therefore $a(B)c(B)=\sum_{k=0}^{2d}h_kB^k$ has exactly those coefficients as its base-$B$ digits, with no carries between digits. The digits occupy consecutive blocks of $b$ bits and can be read in $O(db)$ time.

To reduce each digit modulo $p$, scan its bits maintaining a remainder $0\le r<p$. Each next bit $e$ replaces $r$ by $(2r+e)\bmod p$, using one comparison and at most one subtraction on $O(\log p)$ bits. This proves the displayed upper bound. If $p=2$, reduction is simply the low bit of each block. Here $b=\lceil\log_2(d+2)\rceil$ and $N=\Theta(d\log d)$, yielding the final bound on substitution into $M$. ∎

The exact failed step

The tempting implication from optimal integer multiplication to optimal finite-field multiplication loses a logarithm because the packed input is $\Theta(d\log d)$ bits for fixed characteristic, not $\Theta(d)$. This is a failure of this reduction's cost analysis, not a lower bound against other algorithms. The source's sharper unconditional algorithms are not reproduced or improved here. The constant-degree boundary of the catalogue notation is preserved and is not used as a spurious asymptotic counterexample.

Verification and remaining gaps

Finite exact computations compare packed and direct convolution over several small prime fields, including the all-one binary input that reaches the central coefficient $d+1$. The missing step is a carryless or other uniform reduction avoiding the packing overhead without an unproved number-theoretic assumption.
