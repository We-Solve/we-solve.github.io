Erdős Problem #642: independent continuation

Date: 23 September 2026. Source versions read in full: 642.tex in gpt_pro_5.4.

1) OBJECTIVE AND AUDIT

Let $f(n)$ maximize edges subject to every cycle having fewer chords than vertices. Equivalently, a cycle on vertex set $S$ must satisfy $e(G[S])<2|S|$. The original question is $f(n)=O(n)$. The supplied join construction gives $f(n)\ge3n-7$ for $n\ge6$. Its classification of fixed cores valid for every independent-set size does not classify all admissible graphs.

2) GLUING AND AN EXACT ASYMPTOTIC REDUCTION

Identify one vertex of two admissible graphs, with no other new edges. Every simple cycle lies wholly in one of the two graphs: a cycle traversing both would have to visit the shared vertex twice. Thus the resulting graph is admissible, and \[ f(a+b-1)\ge f(a)+f(b). \] Set $h(m)=f(m+1)$. It is nonnegative and superadditive. For any fixed $q$, writing $m=tq+r$ gives $h(m)\ge t h(q)$, so \[ \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{h(m)}m\ge\frac{h(q)}q. \] Taking the supremum over $q$ and comparing with the trivial upper bound by that supremum proves the extended-real limit \[ \boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n-1} =\sup_{v\ge2}\frac{f(v)}{v-1}\in[3,\infty].} \tag{1} \] This also proves existence of the extended-real limit of $f(n)/n$. The lower endpoint follows from $3n-7$.

3) CONSEQUENCES FOR A POSSIBLE PROOF

The original linear-bound problem is exactly whether the supremum in $1$ is finite. It is enough to bound $e(B)/(|B|-1)$ uniformly over admissible blocks: every cycle belongs to a block, edges partition over blocks, and for a connected graph the block quantities $|B|-1$ sum to $n-1$ (bridges count as two-vertex blocks). Conversely any single block with ratio $c$ can be repeated at one shared vertex, producing arbitrarily large admissible graphs with limiting density $c$. In particular, an asymptotic constant $3$ would require the finite inequality $e(G)\le3(|G|-1)$ for every admissible graph. Testing only the three-vertex-core joins cannot establish that inequality.

4) LIMITATION

The limit in $1$ is allowed to be infinite. Its existence does not improve the known near-linear upper bounds or prove the desired uniform block bound.
