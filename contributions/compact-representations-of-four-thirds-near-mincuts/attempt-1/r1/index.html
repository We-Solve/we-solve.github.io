Four-thirds near-mincuts: a cycle encoding and failure of one circular order

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

In an undirected weighted graph, a cut is the unordered bipartition $\{S,V\setminus S\}$ with $\varnothing\ne S\ne V$. Its weight is the sum of nonnegative weights of crossing edges. Let $\lambda>0$ be the minimum cut weight. A $4/3$-near-mincut has weight at most $4\lambda/3$. A compact representation should describe the family structurally and allow its cuts to be recovered, rather than store every cut as a separate vertex subset.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

Can all cuts of weight at most $\tfrac43$ times the minimum-cut value in an $n$-vertex graph be represented compactly, exploiting the fact that there are at most $n^2$ such cuts?

A representation route with a precise test case

The report seeks structure beyond a list of all vertex subsets and does not prescribe a final representation format. We test the simplest such format: one circular order in which each represented cut is an interval versus its complement. It exactly encodes a substantial quadratic family, but fails at the inclusive four-thirds threshold on a four-vertex graph. This does not rule out more flexible polygon representations or any other compact format.
All near-mincuts of a unit cycle.
Let $G$ be a cycle on $n\ge3$ vertices, with every edge of weight one. Along the cyclic order the indicator of a nonempty proper set changes at least twice and an even number of times, because it returns to its starting value. Thus every cut has even weight at least two, and a single vertex attains two. The minimum cut value is $\lambda=2$. A cut of weight at most $4\lambda/3=8/3$ must therefore have weight exactly two. Its indicator changes twice, so its two sides are the two consecutive vertex intervals obtained by deleting the crossing edges. Conversely every unordered pair of distinct cycle edges gives such a cut: deleting them leaves exactly two nonempty path components, including the possibility that one component is a singleton. The crossing-edge pair uniquely identifies the unordered cut. There are consequently exactly $\binom n2$ near-mincuts.

They are represented using just the cyclic list of $n$ vertices: choose two gaps in the list and recover the two intervals. This is $O(n\log n)$ bits for labeled vertices, compared with listing $\Theta(n^2)$ separate subsets. It is an exact structural compression for this family, with a complete recovery rule.

The inclusive endpoint defeats that same format on $K_4$.
In the unit complete graph on four vertices, a singleton cut has weight three and a two-versus-two cut has weight four, by counting $s(4-s)$ crossing edges. Hence $\lambda=3$, and all seven unordered nontrivial cuts have weight at most $4\lambda/3=4$: four singleton cuts and three distinct two-versus-two cuts. In any circular order of four labels, only two of the three two-versus-two bipartitions are intervals. The third separates the two opposite positions from the other two; each side is disconnected in the circular order and has four changes rather than two. Therefore no single circular order on the original vertices represents all these near-mincuts as intervals. The obstruction is independent of which circular order is chosen.

This calculation also checks the endpoint convention: with the strict threshold $<4\lambda/3$, only the four singleton cuts remain in this example. Statements about a strict threshold cannot be copied to the inclusive question without checking equality cases. The example is consistent with the catalogue's coarse $n^2$ count, since seven is at most sixteen. It is not consistent with a claim of at most $\binom n2$ at the inclusive endpoint; the primary report's typographically displayed binomial count must therefore be read with its threshold conventions rather than silently strengthened into an inclusive theorem.

Why counting alone does not produce the requested structure.
Even if at most $n^2$ distinct cuts are given, listing their $n$-bit characteristic vectors takes $O(n^3)$ bits. That is a polynomial list, but does not provide the type of common geometric/combinatorial representation sought by the source. The cycle example compresses the list by explaining its compatibility. The $K_4$ example shows that its simplest compatibility rule does not extend to all graphs. A richer rule with proved size and recovery properties remains necessary; the source does not fix one for us to declare sufficient without such a proof.
