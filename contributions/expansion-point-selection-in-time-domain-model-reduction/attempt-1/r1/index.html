Research attempt: Exact scale selection for a one-term exponential approximation

One decaying exponential
For $\alpha>0$ set $\phi_\alpha(t)=\sqrt{2\alpha}e^{-\alpha t}$. Direct integration gives $\lVert\phi_\alpha\rVert_2=1$. For any real $h\in L^2$, expansion of the square shows that $c\phi_\alpha$ uniquely minimizes $\lVert h-c\phi_\alpha\rVert_2^2$ at $c=\langle h,\phi_\alpha\rangle$, with error $\lVert h\rVert_2^2-c^2$.

For $h(t)=e^{-\beta t}$, $\beta>0$, this gives \[ c=\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad E(\alpha)=\frac1{2\beta}-\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha+\beta)^2} =\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2}{2\beta(\alpha+\beta)^2}. \] Thus the unique zero-error scale is $\alpha=\beta$, and the relative squared error is $((\alpha-\beta)/(\alpha+\beta))^2$. For any fixed chosen scale $\alpha$, this relative error approaches one as $\beta/\alpha$ tends to zero or infinity. Hence no system-independent positive scale gives a uniform relative squared error strictly below one even on these single-pole stable systems.

An exact finite calculation for a two-scale positive mixture
Take $h(t)=e^{-\beta t}+w e^{-\gamma t}$, where $0<\beta<\gamma$ and $w>0$. Define \[ F(\alpha)=\frac1{\alpha+\beta}+\frac{w}{\alpha+\gamma},\quad \lVert h\rVert_2^2=\frac1{2\beta}+\frac{2w}{\beta+\gamma}+\frac{w^2}{2\gamma}. \] The projected energy is $G(\alpha)=2\alpha F(\alpha)^2$, so minimizing error is maximizing $G$. It tends to zero at both positive-domain ends, is positive within, and has derivative \[ G'(\alpha)=2F(\alpha)\left( \frac{\beta-\alpha}{(\alpha+\beta)^2} +w\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{(\alpha+\gamma)^2}\right). \] This derivative is positive for $\alpha\leq\beta$ and negative for $\alpha\geq\gamma$, with the strict endpoint signs because $w>0$ and $\beta<\gamma$. Therefore every global maximizing scale lies in $(\beta,\gamma)$ and is a root of the explicit cubic \[ (\beta-\alpha)(\alpha+\gamma)^2 +w(\gamma-\alpha)(\alpha+\beta)^2=0. \] Its leading coefficient is $-(1+w)$, so it is not the zero polynomial. Evaluating $G$ at its finitely many real roots in the interval and choosing the greatest value gives an exact characterization of the optimum; for rational inputs this can be implemented with real algebraic root isolation and comparison. Alternatively, the formulas alone specify a finite list of candidates without making an implementation-complexity claim. Uniqueness of this optimizer is not assumed or proved.
The gap to the source's reduction problem
This objective optimizes one orthogonal exponential approximation to the impulse response. Moment matching in a rational Krylov reduced state-space realization has additional algebraic constraints, and its finite-order approximation error need not equal $E(\alpha)$. Nor does reducing the impulse-response $L^2$ error alone prove a chosen norm bound for every input response. For example, scaling the input scales any nonzero linear response error. The two-scale stationary equation also depends on the actual mixture, so an iteration that estimates its parameters from a reduced model needs a separate convergence argument. The report's observed rapid iteration is not a proof of that argument.
