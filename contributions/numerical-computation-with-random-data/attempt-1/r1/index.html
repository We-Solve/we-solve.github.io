Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Definitions and notation.
Let $H$ be an $n\times n$ random matrix from ensemble $\mathcal E$, and let $\mathcal A$ be a numerical algorithm with tolerance $\varepsilon$. Its stopping time is $T=T_{\varepsilon,n,\mathcal A,\mathcal E}(H)$; the centered and standardized time is $(T-\mathbb ET)/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}T}$ when these quantities exist. For Toda flow $\dot X=[X,X_--X_-^*]$, one-row deflation time is the first $t$ with $\sum_{j=2}^n|X_{1j}(t)|^2<\varepsilon^2$. Full deflation permits a split into two blocks with off-diagonal block norm at most $\varepsilon$.

A stopping-time transfer with an exposed moment gap.
Suppose a deterministic residual has the exact form $R(t)=Ce^{-\Delta t}$, with $C>\varepsilon>0$, $\Delta>0$. Then $T_\varepsilon=\Delta^{-1}\log(C/\varepsilon)$. This elementary model illustrates why both an edge gap and an eigenvector-dependent coefficient enter deflation. A convergence theorem for $\Delta$ alone cannot determine $T$ unless the logarithmic coefficient and its dependence are controlled.
Standardization needs more than convergence in law.
Let $Y_n$ equal $n$ with probability $n^{-2}$, and zero otherwise. Then $Y_n\to0$ in probability and $\mathbb EY_n=1/n$, but $\operatorname{Var}Y_n=1-n^{-2}\to1$. The standardized variables $(Y_n-\mathbb EY_n)/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y_n}$ also converge in probability to zero despite having variance one. Rare events carry the entire limiting second moment. Therefore a distributional approximation of a rescaled stopping time does not justify replacing deterministic normalization by its true mean and variance. For the residual model, small gaps create precisely such a possible tail mechanism: $T^2=\Delta^{-2}\log^2(C/\varepsilon)$. A usable second-moment transfer would require uniform integrability of this expression, not simply a limiting gap distribution.
