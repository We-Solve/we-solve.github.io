Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A strong involution $t$ has order two and both its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces have dimensions between $n/3$ and $2n/3$. Bray's method samples independent uniform $g\in G$, sets $y=tt^g$ with $t^g=g^{-1}tg$, and extracts centralizer elements from the odd/even order cases. The centralizer is $C_G(t)=\{h:ht=th\}$.

The group $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ consists of integer matrices with integer inverses, and $\mathrm{SL}_n$ imposes determinant one. The subgroup generated by matrices consists of finite products of them and their inverses. A centralizer consists of elements commuting with the specified element.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For a finite classical group of dimension $n$ over a field of odd characteristic, does Bray's algorithm for constructing the centralizer of a strong involution succeed with high probability after examining only $O(\log n)$ random elements? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The original report treats the even-case construction of a subgroup containing the second derived subgroup $C_G(t)^{\prime\prime}$ as sufficient for its modified aim. This is not silently strengthened to generating every element of the full centralizer.

Research attempt: Sampling amplification and its missing uniformity

A quantified conditional sampling lemma.
Let $n\ge2$. Suppose each independent uniform input $g$ has probability at least $p_n>0$ of producing a useful even-case output for each specified sign $\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}$. The two signs within a trial need not be independent. Fix a positive integer $c$ and $0<\delta<1$. After $N$ trials let $X_\varepsilon$ count useful outputs for sign $\varepsilon$. Independence across trials gives, with $a=1-e^{-1}>1/2$, \[ \mathbb E e^{-X_\varepsilon}\le (1-a p_n)^N\le e^{-aNp_n}, \qquad \Pr(X_\varepsilon<c)\le e^{c-aNp_n}. \] The first formula follows by multiplying $\mathbb E e^{-I}=1-a\Pr(I=1)$ for the Bernoulli indicators; the second is Markov's inequality applied to $e^{-X_\varepsilon}$, using the weaker threshold $e^{-c}$. Hence \[ N\ge \frac{2}{p_n}\left(c+\log(2/\delta)\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Pr(X_+<c\text{ or }X_-<c)\le\delta. \] The union bound, not independence of the two counts, supplies the last step. If $p_n\ge a_0/\log n$ for a fixed $a_0>0$, the sufficient sample count is \[ O\left(\log n\,[c+\log(1/\delta)]\right). \] For fixed $c$ and fixed error tolerance this is $O(\log n)$. If a further group-generation assertion fails with conditional probability at most $\eta$ after obtaining the required outputs, the total failure is at most $\delta+\eta$; that assertion has not been supplied by the counting lemma.
The attempted stronger inference fails.
A probability lower bound of order $1/\log n$ alone does not imply success tending to one from $C\log n$ trials with fixed $C$. Indeed the independent Bernoulli model with exact success probability $a_0/\log n$ (for sufficiently large $n$) has \[ \Pr(X=0)=\left(1-\frac{a_0}{\log n}\right)^{\lfloor C\log n\rfloor} \longrightarrow e^{-a_0C}>0. \] For completeness, $\log(1-z)/z\to-1$ follows by integrating $1/(1-z)$ and bounding it between $1$ and $1/(1-z)$; this proves the displayed limit. Taking $\delta=n^{-b}$ in the sufficient bound yields $O((\log n)^2)$ for fixed $b>0$. This is an obstruction to deriving an asymptotically vanishing error from that lower bound alone, not a lower bound for Bray's actual distribution, which might be better.
Why qualitative strength is also insufficient.
In $\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb F_q)$ with odd $q$, the diagonal involutions having two $-1$ and two $+1$ entries are strong. Any collection consisting only of these diagonal elements generates an abelian group, whereas, for example, the elementary matrices $I+E_{12}$ and $I+E_{23}$ do not commute: their products differ by $E_{13}$. Thus strong eigenspace dimensions alone cannot imply generation of a nonabelian target. Uniform conjugacy-class marginals alone are also insufficient if independence is dropped: choose one uniform conjugate $z$ of an involution and repeat $z$ in every position; every marginal is correct while the generated group has order at most two. Bray's independent inputs avoid this artificial dependence, but the needed distribution and generation hypotheses still require proof.
