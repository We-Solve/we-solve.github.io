Finite constant-group invariants in every characteristic

Problem: prb_7546db8c2b5b41428f2a85077fb3ca32. Claim type: Partial result.

For every field $k$, every finite abstract group $\Gamma$ and every finite-dimensional linear representation over $k$, the invariant ring $k[V]^\Gamma$ is finitely generated. This includes the modular case $\mathrm{char}(k)\mid |\Gamma|$. It is the classical finite-group theorem; the proof below uses no averaging operator.

Here $\Gamma$ is regarded as the finite **constant** algebraic group over $k$. Its coordinate algebra is a product of $|\Gamma|$ copies of $k$, so the coaction condition in the official prompt is exactly the collection of identities $g\cdot f=f$ for all $g\in\Gamma$. This specifies a subclass of the prompt's algebraic groups over arbitrary fields.

Let $B=k[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$, $C=B^\Gamma$, and $N=|\Gamma|$. For each coordinate form the monic orbit polynomial
$$P_i(T)=\prod_{g\in\Gamma}(T-g\cdot x_i)\in B[T].$$
Every coefficient belongs to $C$, because applying any group element permutes the factors. Let $A\subseteq C$ be the $k$-algebra generated by all coefficients of all $P_i$. This is a finitely generated commutative $k$-algebra, hence Noetherian.

The identity element occurs in the product, so $P_i(x_i)=0$. Thus every $x_i$ satisfies a monic equation of degree $N$ over $A$. Successively replacing powers $x_i^N$ by smaller powers shows directly that $B$ is spanned as an $A$-module by
$$\{x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_d^{e_d}:0\le e_i<N\}.$$
There are at most $N^d$ such monomials. The invariant ring $C$, being an $A$-submodule of the finite $A$-module $B$, is itself a finite $A$-module. Choose module generators $c_1,\ldots,c_s$. Together with the chosen algebra generators of $A$, these generate $C$ as a $k$-algebra: every element of $C$ is an $A$-linear combination of the $c_j$. This proves finite generation.

No step divided by $N$. Therefore the proof still applies when the characteristic divides the group order. It also covers a nonfaithful action, the trivial group, and dimension zero; in the last case $B=C=k$.

As a concrete modular illustration, in characteristic $p$ the cyclic group of order $p$ can act on $k[x,y]$ by $x\mapsto x+y$, $y\mapsto y$. Its orbit polynomial for $x$ is
$$\prod_{j\in\mathbb F_p}(T-x-jy)=(T-x)^p-y^{p-1}(T-x).$$
This is monic of degree $p$ and vanishes at $T=x$, even though averaging by $1/p$ is unavailable. The formula follows by homogenizing $\prod_{j\in\mathbb F_p}(U-j)=U^p-U$.

`verify.py` performs coefficient computations in exact finite fields for nine actions: the two-coordinate swap and the three-coordinate symmetric group in characteristics 2, 3 and 5, and the above unipotent cyclic actions for $p=2,3,5$. It checks 21 monic root identities and 510 coefficient-invariance identities. These are checks of the algebra used by the proof, not a claim that finite-degree searches establish finite generation. The recorded output is `verification.json`.

The official universal statement is already known to be false. Totaro's Theorem 0.1 supplies counterexamples over every field using positive-dimensional additive algebraic groups. Over a finite field, their finite set of rational points does not determine algebraic-group invariance: invariance is an identity of regular functions. Applying the theorem above merely to that finite point group would change the invariant ring. Thus the affirmative finite constant-group result does not contradict Totaro's negative result.

Reference checked for that context: Burt Totaro, *Hilbert's fourteenth problem over finite fields, and a conjecture on the cone of curves*, introduction, Theorem 0.1 and §1. The finite-group proof above is self-contained apart from the Hilbert basis theorem and the elementary Noetherian module property. No earlier contribution was listed in the checked official API.
