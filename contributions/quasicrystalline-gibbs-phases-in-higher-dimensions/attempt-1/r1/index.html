Quasicrystalline Gibbs Phases in Higher Dimensions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A short-range lattice interaction has local or suitably decaying couplings as specified by its Gibbs specification. A translation-invariant Gibbs measure is invariant under all lattice shifts. Its extremal decomposition expresses it as a mixture of pure Gibbs phases; the pure phases in that decomposition need not individually share the mixture's translation invariance. Nonperiodic here excludes invariance under any finite-index translation subgroup.

Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
In every dimension at least $3$, does there exist a translation-invariant Gibbs measure for a short-range interaction whose extremal decomposition contains nonperiodic Gibbs measures?

Why averaging translated interfaces can lose them.
On $\mathbb Z^d$, define a deterministic flat-interface configuration $\sigma^{(k)}_x=-1$ for $x_d<k$ and $+1$ for $x_d\geq k$. Let $\mu_R=(2R+1)^{-1}\sum_{k=-R}^R\delta_{\sigma^{(k)}}$. For any finite observation box contained in $|x_d|\leq L$, only $2L+1$ interface positions can cross the box. Their total weight is at most $(2L+1)/(2R+1)\to0$. The remaining positions make the box uniformly plus or uniformly minus with limiting weights one half. Hence, on every cylinder observable, \[ \mu_R\longrightarrow\tfrac12\delta_{+}+\tfrac12\delta_{-}. \] The limit is translation invariant and has only periodic components, although every finite mixture was built from nonperiodic interface configurations.
What the example rules out.
Translation averaging over a noncompact orbit does not automatically produce an invariant measure whose extremal decomposition retains nonperiodic elements. Mass in the orbit parameter can escape to infinity and leave only bulk phases. The proof extends to interface measures with uniform convergence to plus/minus local laws far from the interface, provided that convergence is explicitly assumed.
Source scope and gap.
The deterministic delta measures are not claimed to be positive-temperature Gibbs states of an admissible finite-energy interaction. Thus this is a failure of a proposed averaging route, not a negative answer to the existence problem. One needs a tight invariant distribution of genuinely nonperiodic Gibbs phases, or a different construction, for every dimension $d\geq3$. Producing one isolated interface phase alone would not establish the requested invariant-mixture statement.
