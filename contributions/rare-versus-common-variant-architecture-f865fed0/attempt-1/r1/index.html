Rare-versus-common variant architecture

Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.

Target and scope

Catalogue definitions and assumptions.
Fix a population, standardized phenotype $Y$ and set of observed biallelic variants. For variant $j$, let $G_j\in\{0,1,2\}$ be its minor-allele dosage, $f_j\in(0,1/2]$ be minor-allele frequency and $\beta_j$ its additive effect in original dosage units. Under Hardy--Weinberg equilibrium for diploid dosages and linkage equilibrium between loci, its additive contribution is $2f_j(1-f_j)\beta_j^2$. Partition frequencies into predetermined bins $B_k$ and functional classes $A_l$. Under linkage disequilibrium, define joint variance components using declared genotype covariance matrices; arbitrary attribution of correlated effects is avoided. Sequencing ascertainment and ancestry structure are modeled.
Original target.
Estimate the binwise heritability measure and its cross-population sensitivity: \[ H(B_k,A_l)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\sum_{j:f_j\in B_k,\,j\in A_l}\beta_jG_j)}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}, \qquad |\widehat H-H|\leq\varepsilon_{kl} \] with confidence coverage specified for each jointly fitted component and explicit covariance terms when bins are correlated. The question is how measured allele-frequency classes contribute to trait variance.
Catalogue alternative formulations.
(i) Under Hardy--Weinberg and linkage equilibria with additive effects, estimate frequency/effect distributions from sufficiently large independent samples. (ii) With LD and rare variants, use joint random-effect components or gene burdens and test sensitivity to effect-size priors. Burden heritability and single-variant contributions are different estimands, and variant signs can cancel in a burden.

Attempted answer

Proposition: Classwise additive variance obeys $\operatorname{Var}(\sum_k A_k)=\sum_k\operatorname{Var}(A_k)+2\sum_{k<l}\operatorname{Cov}(A_k,A_l)$. Under linkage equilibrium and Hardy--Weinberg assumptions, $\operatorname{Var}(A_k)=\sum_{j\in k}2f_j(1-f_j)\beta_j^2$.
Proof: Expand covariance bilinearly and use independent binomial-dosage variances. A genotype covariance null vector can change coefficient allocation without changing the total predictor, so singular designs can leave class attribution nonunique. ∎

What remains unresolved

Joint covariance, rare-variant ascertainment and cross-population effect transfer remain unresolved.

Shared mathematical bounds

Lemma (Independent bounded sums): Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely, and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If $V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$, \[ \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V). \] In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound $\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon) \leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$ is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
Proof: For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product $(X-a)(b-X)$ gives $\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$. Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$. Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so $K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$. Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields $K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$. Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$. For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$. Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$. Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting $t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths. ∎
Finite-family extension.
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required; independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells, reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be counted as independent units.
