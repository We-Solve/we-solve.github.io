Source scope and attempted route

The primary report, printed pages 515--517, already distinguishes convergence for fixed positive entropic regularization from the open question about the limiting dynamics as the partition cells shrink. The 2023 primary article's abstract and Sections 1.2, 4 (Remark 8), 5, and 6 confirm that this distinction is material: the limiting system can have stationary nonoptimal map couplings. We do not claim that arbitrary stationary solutions are algorithmically selected. Our attempted route is to glue local first-order conditions into a global dual certificate and then pass to the fine-partition limit. The finite gluing step works under positivity; the second step does not follow. Here are complete auxiliary proofs and an explicit obstruction, not a new theorem about the source's limit dynamics.

A finite positive-limit certificate

Let $r_i,s_j>0$ be row and column masses with equal total, let all $c_{ij}$ be finite, and fix $\varepsilon>0$. On nonnegative matrices with these margins minimize \[ F(P)=\sum_{ij}c_{ij}P_{ij} +\varepsilon\sum_{ij}P_{ij}\left(\log\frac{P_{ij}}{r_i s_j}-1\right), \qquad 0\log0=0. \] The omitted normalization terms in relative entropy are constant on this feasible set. Suppose $P$ is strictly positive and, for every edge $ii'$ of a connected graph on the rows, it minimizes $F$ among plans changing only those two rows. Then $P$ is the unique global minimizer.

To prove this, write \[ g_{ij}=c_{ij}+\varepsilon\log(P_{ij}/(r_i s_j)). \] For every pair of columns $j,j'$ the matrix supported on the four corners $(i,j),(i',j'),(i,j'),(i',j)$ with respective entries $1,1,-1,-1$ preserves all margins. Strict positivity makes both signs of a sufficiently small step feasible. Differentiating at zero therefore gives \[ g_{ij}-g_{ij'}=g_{i'j}-g_{i'j'}. \] Fix a reference column and propagate this equality along row-graph paths. It gives $g_{ij}=u_i+v_j$ for suitable real numbers $u_i,v_j$. For $p>0,q\ge0$, strict convexity of $t\log t-t$, proved by its second derivative $1/t>0$ on $t>0$ and continuity at zero, gives its tangent inequality, strict when $q\ne p$. Consequently every other feasible $Q$ satisfies \[ F(Q)\ge F(P)+\sum_{ij}(u_i+v_j)(Q_{ij}-P_{ij})=F(P), \] with strict inequality unless $Q=P$. The last equality uses both margins. A single row or column is covered as well: the feasible plan is then determined by the margins.

There is a corresponding conditional convergence statement. Suppose exact two-row updates visit every edge of this finite connected graph infinitely often, and the entire sequence of plans converges to a strictly positive $P_*$. Then $P_*$ satisfies the four-corner stationarity equations and hence is globally optimal. Indeed, if one such directional derivative at $P_*$ were nonzero, choose its negative sign and a fixed small step producing a strict decrease $\delta>0$. By continuity this same step remains feasible and improves the objective by at least $\delta/2$ at all sufficiently late visits to that row edge. Exact minimization improves at least as much. But convergence of plans and continuity of $F$ imply that successive objective values converge to the same limit, a contradiction. We have assumed full-sequence convergence and positivity, not proved them from a continuum limit hypothesis.

For clarity, the finite global minimizer itself is positive. The feasible set is compact and nonempty (a positive product plan, normalized by total mass if needed, is feasible), so a minimizer exists. If it has a zero entry, mix it with that positive product plan with weight $t$. At formerly positive entries the objective change is $O(t)$; the finite cost contribution is $O(t)$; at all formerly zero entries the entropy contribution is $\varepsilon A t\log t+O(t)$ with $A>0$. For small positive $t$ this is negative. This contradicts minimality. This fact alone says nothing about uniform positivity as dimensions or $\varepsilon$ vary.

Failure of the unregularized extension

Take three unit row and column margins and cost matrix \[ C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&3\\3&1&0\\0&3&1\end{pmatrix}. \] The identity plan has cost $3$. If just two rows may change, the third row fixes one column completely and the remaining two rows have their $2$ by $2$ identity margins. Every feasible subplan is \[ \begin{pmatrix}1-t&t\\t&1-t\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le t\le1, \] in the corresponding two columns. Its cost on these rows is $2(1-t)+3t=2+t$, so the identity is the unique optimum for every possible two-row update. Nevertheless the plan with entries $P_{12}=P_{23}=P_{31}=1$ has cost zero, which is globally minimal because all costs are nonnegative. Dividing masses and costs by three gives the probability-measure version. Thus even a complete row-interaction graph does not make unregularized two-row fixed points globally optimal. The missing feasible reverse directions at zero entries invalidate the positive-matrix derivative proof. This finite example is not claimed to be a counterexample to an unstated geometric assumption in the source.

The precise remaining limit gap

Even a correct finite-scale convergence theorem cannot justify interchanging the spatial and long-time limits. As a scalar illustration, $a_N(t)=\exp(-t/N)$ satisfies \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}a_N(t)=0, \qquad \lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}a_N(t)=1. \] Both identities follow directly from the exponential formula. This is a diagnostic example, not an asserted transport trajectory. A sufficient uniform estimate of the form $\sup_N\Delta_N(t)\le h(t)\to0$, together with appropriate objective and trajectory convergence, would close that exchange; no such estimate is derived from the source's cost/data hypotheses here. The finite proof also loses any force when limiting masses vanish, regularization vanishes, or local cells collapse without a uniform certificate controlling all feasible transport directions.
