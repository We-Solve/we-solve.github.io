LOCC Dense-Codeability of the Four-Qubit W State

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A qubit has Hilbert space $\mathbb C^2$, and the normalized four-qubit state is $|W_4\rangle=\tfrac12(|1000\rangle+|0100\rangle+|0010\rangle+|0001\rangle)$. In the report's two-receiver protocol, two sending qubits are locally encoded and transmitted to their assigned receivers. The two receivers then decode by LOCC. A state is LOCC dense-codeable when the achievable classical communication rate exceeds the rate available from the transmitted systems without the shared entangled state; with two sending qubits the benchmark is two bits per use.
Source scope and qualifications.
This is the report's two-receiver LOCC question, not global decoding, a single-receiver protocol, or a teleportation question. Receiver partition and the report's encoding class remain part of the target.

Testing the phase-encoding route.
Label the two sender qubits first and put $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. For $u,v\in\{0,1\}$ set $|w_{uv}\rangle=(Z^u\otimes Z^v\otimes I\otimes I)|W_4\rangle$. Directly using the four disjoint computational basis terms gives \[ \langle w_{uv}|w_{u'v'}\rangle =\tfrac14\left(2+(-1)^{u-u'}+(-1)^{v-v'}\right). \] The overlap is zero exactly when both bits change. Hence an orthogonal subset of these four codewords contains at most two words: each word has just one orthogonal partner. No decoder, including a global one, can distinguish more than two of them perfectly in one use. To see the necessity, perfect discrimination by effects $E_m$ implies $E_m|w_m\rangle=|w_m\rangle$ and $E_m|w_j\rangle=0$ for $j\ne m$, forcing orthogonality.
Why this fails to settle dense-codeability.
Orthogonality alone does not supply the receiver-restricted decoder. A proposed local $X$-basis measurement would require checking that its outcome distributions distinguish the two words; no such decoder is asserted here. The proved statement is solely the global obstruction to a larger perfect phase code. Computational-basis measurements are even worse: they erase all four encoding phases and give exactly the same output distribution for every $u,v$.
Remaining gap.
The report permits more general local unitaries, receiver adaptation, and asymptotic decoding with small error. Restricting to diagonal Pauli encoders and demanding zero error can lose the desired advantage. This failed four-word construction gives neither a universal two-bit upper bound nor a rate strictly above two bits for the prescribed two-receiver partition.
