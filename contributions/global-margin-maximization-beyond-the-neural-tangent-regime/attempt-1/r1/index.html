Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For binary labeled data $(x_i,y_i)$ with $y_i\in\{-1,1\}$, the margin of a score function $f_\theta$ on sample $i$ is $y_i f_\theta(x_i)$. A parameter-homogeneous network satisfies $f_{c\theta}(x)=c^d f_\theta(x)$ for $c>0$ and a fixed degree $d>0$. A maximum-margin objective maximizes the smallest training margin subject to the source parameter normalization, or equivalently minimizes its normalized parameter cost under unit-margin constraints when that equivalence applies. Gradient descent updates $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\nabla L(\theta_t)$. The neural-tangent regime approximates the score by its first-order parameter expansion at initialization.

Research attempt: A solvable homogeneous margin flow and a failing invariant branch

A complete global-margin calculation in a restricted model.
Take one sample $x=y=1$, scalar output $f_{u,v}(x)=uvx$, exponential loss $L=e^{-uv}$ and Euclidean parameter normalization. Its normalized margin is \[ \gamma(u,v)=\frac{uv}{u^2+v^2}\leq\frac12, \] for nonzero parameters, because $(u-v)^2\geq0$. Equality holds exactly at $u=v\ne0$. The unit-margin minimum-cost problem is $\min\frac12(u^2+v^2)$ subject to $uv\geq1$; its value is one, attained at $u=v=1$ and at $u=v=-1$, since $u^2+v^2\geq2uv\geq2$. Gradient flow satisfies \[ \dot u=v e^{-uv},\qquad\dot v=u e^{-uv},\qquad \frac{d}{dt}(u^2-v^2)=0. \] Assume $u(0),v(0)>0$. They stay positive and increase. Put $w=uv>0$ and $c=u^2-v^2$, which is constant. Then \[ \dot w=(u^2+v^2)e^{-w}=\sqrt{c^2+4w^2}\,e^{-w}. \] The right side is positive and bounded for $w\geq w(0)$, so the scalar solution exists for all positive time: it cannot escape to infinity in finite time while its derivative is bounded. The original positive $u,v$ are recovered from $u^2=(\sqrt{c^2+4w^2}+c)/2$ and the analogous formula for $v^2$. If $w$ had a finite limit, its continuous right side would have a positive limit, contradicting boundedness of $w$. Therefore $w\to\infty$ and \[ \gamma(u,v)=\frac{w}{\sqrt{c^2+4w^2}}\longrightarrow\frac12. \] This proves global normalized-margin convergence for this model and this positive initialization region, with no inference from a KKT condition.
Homogeneity and separability alone do not ensure success.
On the invariant branch $v=-u$, $u(0)>0$, the same flow becomes $\dot u=-u e^{u^2}$. It stays positive and decreases to zero: a positive limit would leave its derivative bounded away from zero negatively. The solution exists for all positive time, since it stays in a compact interval and the smooth vector field is bounded there. Its score $uv=-u^2$ approaches zero from below, and its normalized margin equals $-1/2$ at every finite time. The data are nevertheless separable by $(u,v)=(1,1)$. At $(0,0)$ the gradient is zero and the flow is stationary as well. These explicit initializations disprove a universal global-convergence statement based solely on homogeneity and separability. They do not show failure from a random initialization with a density, since the bad branch has measure zero.
The tangent comparison still has an unresolved step.
At zero, the first-order parameter linearization of $uv$ is identically zero, whereas the nonlinear model can represent a positive separator. But the actual gradient at zero is also zero. Thus the mere existence of a stronger nonlinear representation does not imply that gradient descent discovers it or escapes its tangent model. A beyond-tangent learning theorem needs a specified initialization, trajectory and performance comparison, none of which follows from this representational observation. The successful branch above already has the same optimal normalized margin in its balanced case, so it is not presented as an example of a provable representation-learning advantage.
