A non-polynomial complex potential with entirely real spectrum

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
A Schrödinger realization is specified by its potential, domain and boundary conditions. On $\mathbb R_+$ a Robin-type boundary condition can be written $y(0)\cos\theta+y'(0)\sin\theta=0$. A Riesz basis is the image of an orthonormal basis under a bounded invertible operator. An eigensystem may involve generalized eigenvectors when the operator is not diagonalizable. Augmented eigenfunctions in the source are spectral functionals used to represent evolution problems.

Find a non-self-adjoint Schrödinger operator on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ or $L^2(\mathbb R_+)$ with a nonpolynomial potential, infinitely many real eigenvalues and at most finitely many non-real eigenvalues. The source compares with $H=-d^2/dx^2+x^m+a_1x^{m-1}+\cdots+a_m$ on $\mathbb R_+$ and the boundary condition $y(0)\cos\theta+y'(0)\sin\theta=0$, $a_j,\theta\in\mathbb C$.
  2. Let $A_0=-d^2/dx^2+x^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ and $A=A_0+V$ for complex $V\in L^\infty(\mathbb R)$. Is its eigensystem a Riesz basis? The source records an affirmative result for $V\in L^p(\mathbb R)$, some $1\le p<\infty$.

A Darboux construction with a deliberately incomplete spectral claim.
Fix real $c\ne0$ and write $z=x+ic$. Set $u(z)=ze^{z^2/2}$; direct differentiation gives $(-d^2/dx^2+z^2)u=-3u$. Define $A=d/dx-u'/u$, $B=-d/dx-u'/u$. Multiplication of differential expressions yields \[BA=H_0+3,\quad AB=H_1+3,\quad H_0=-d^2/dx^2+z^2,\quad H_1=-d^2/dx^2+z^2-2+2/z^2. \] The potential of $H_1$ is smooth on the real axis, nonpolynomial and genuinely complex. The algebra gives $H_1A=AH_0$.
Infinitely many real eigenvalues.
Let $\psi_0=e^{-z^2/2}$, and $\psi_n=(-d/dx+z)^n\psi_0$. The commutator $[H_0,-d/dx+z]=2(-d/dx+z)$ and $H_0\psi_0=\psi_0$ prove $H_0\psi_n=(2n+1)\psi_n$. Each is a nonzero polynomial times the Gaussian, square integrable on the shifted horizontal line. Thus $A\psi_n$ is also square integrable and satisfies $H_1A\psi_n=(2n+1)A\psi_n$. It cannot vanish, since $Af=0$ forces $f$ proportional to the growing function $u$. These functions lie in the natural maximal differential domain, since their derivatives and polynomial-potential products remain square integrable. Also $1/u$ is square integrable and solves $H_1(1/u)=-3/u$.
