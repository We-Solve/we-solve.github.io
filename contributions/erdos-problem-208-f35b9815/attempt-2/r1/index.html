Erdős Problem #208

1. FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Let $1=s_1<s_2<s_3<\cdots$ be the increasing sequence of squarefree positive integers. The question is whether \[ s_{n+1}-s_n = o\left(s_n^{1/5}\right)\qquad (n\to\infty). \] Equivalently: for every $\varepsilon>0$ does there exist $N(\varepsilon)$ such that $s_{n+1}-s_n \le \varepsilon\, s_n^{1/5}$ for all $n\ge N(\varepsilon)$?

2. PHASE 1 --- FAST REALITY CHECK

  • Computed small-scale gap data (squarefree sieve).
    1. Up to $10^6$, the maximum observed gap between consecutive squarefree numbers is $8$ (e.g. from $217069$ to $217077$).
    2. Up to $10^7$, the maximum observed gap is $10$ (e.g. from $8870023$ to $8870033$).
    These are far smaller than $x^{1/5}$ at these scales (for $x=10^7$, $x^{1/5}\approx 25.1$).

3. KEY DEFINITIONS / LEMMAS

  1. Squarefree indicator. Let $\mu$ be the Möbius function. Then \[ \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(n)=\mu(n)^2. \] Also, the identity \[ \mu(n)^2 = \sum_{d^2\mid n} \mu(d) \] holds for all $n\ge 1$.

  2. Counting squarefree numbers. Let \[ Q(x):=\#\{n\le x: n\text{ squarefree}\}=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)^2. \] Then \[ Q(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^2}x + O(\sqrt{x}). \]

  3. Immediate (weak) consequence for gaps. There exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that every interval $[x, x+C\sqrt{x}]$ with $x$ sufficiently large contains a squarefree integer. Equivalently, $s_{n+1}-s_n \ll \sqrt{s_n}$.

  4. A simple infinite-family lower bound (gaps exist). There exist infinitely many gaps of length at least $c\,\dfrac{\log x}{\log\log x}$ between consecutive squarefree numbers, for some absolute $c>0$. (A concrete proof is given below using CRT with prime squares.)

4. WORK (Main proof or counterexample)

Lemma 3.2 (squarefree counting with $O(\sqrt{x})$ error).
Claim. $Q(x)=\dfrac{6}{\pi^2}x + O(\sqrt{x})$.

Proof. Using $\mu(n)^2 = \sum_{d^2\mid n}\mu(d)$, \[ Q(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)^2 =\sum_{n\le x}\ \sum_{d^2\mid n}\mu(d) =\sum_{d\le \sqrt{x}}\mu(d)\,\#\{n\le x: d^2\mid n\} =\sum_{d\le \sqrt{x}}\mu(d)\,\lfloor\frac{x}{d^2}\rfloor. \] Write $\lfloor x/d^2\rfloor = x/d^2 + O(1)$, giving \[ Q(x)= x\sum_{d\le \sqrt{x}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2} + O(\sqrt{x}). \] As $x\to\infty$, the partial sums converge to \[ \sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2} = \frac{1}{\zeta(2)}=\frac{6}{\pi^2}, \] and truncation at $\sqrt{x}$ introduces an error $\ll \sum_{d>\sqrt{x}}1/d^2\ll 1/\sqrt{x}$, which after multiplying by $x$ is $O(\sqrt{x})$. Hence $Q(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^2}x+O(\sqrt{x})$. $\square$

Corollary 3.3 (gap bound $\ll\sqrt{x}$).
Claim. There is a constant $C>0$ such that for all sufficiently large $x$, \[ Q(x+C\sqrt{x})-Q(x) \ge 1. \] Consequently, $s_{n+1}-s_n \ll \sqrt{s_n}$.

Proof. By Lemma 3.2, \[ Q(x+C\sqrt{x})-Q(x) =\frac{6}{\pi^2}C\sqrt{x} + O(\sqrt{x}). \] Choosing $C$ larger than the implied constant in the $O(\sqrt{x})$ term makes the right-hand side positive for all large $x$, hence at least $1$ for all large $x$. Therefore every such interval contains a squarefree integer. Taking $x=s_n$ gives $s_{n+1}-s_n\le C\sqrt{s_n}$ for all large $n$. $\square$

Lemma 3.4 (explicit construction of infinitely many large squarefree gaps).
Claim. There exists $c>0$ such that for infinitely many $X$ there is an interval of length at least $c\,\dfrac{\log X}{\log\log X}$ containing no squarefree numbers.

Proof. Let $y$ be large and let $\mathcal{P}(y)$ denote the primes $p\le y$. For each prime $p\le y$ choose a distinct integer $i(p)$ in $\{1,2,\dots,\pi(y)\}$ (for example enumerate the primes in increasing order and set $i(p)$ to be its index). Impose the congruences \[ n\equiv -i(p) \pmod{p^2}\qquad (p\le y). \] The moduli $p^2$ are pairwise coprime, so by CRT there exists $n$ satisfying all of them. Then for each $p\le y$ we have $p^2\mid (n+i(p))$, hence each of the integers \[ n+1,\ n+2,\ \dots,\ n+\pi(y) \] contains at least one square prime factor and therefore is not squarefree. Thus there is a squarefree gap of length at least $\pi(y)$. Now set $X:=n$. The CRT solution satisfies $n\equiv -i(p)\pmod{p^2}$ for all $p\le y$, hence $n$ is bounded by the product \[ n \le \prod_{p\le y} p^2 = \exp\left(2\sum_{p\le y}\log p\right). \] A standard Chebyshev bound gives $\sum_{p\le y}\log p \asymp y$ and also $\pi(y)\asymp \dfrac{y}{\log y}$. Therefore $\log X\asymp y$ and \[ \pi(y) \asymp \frac{y}{\log y} \asymp \frac{\log X}{\log\log X}. \] This provides infinitely many $X$ with a squarefree gap of size $\gg \log X/\log\log X$. Taking $c>0$ to be a suitable absolute constant completes the proof. $\square$

5. SANITY CHECK

  • Lemma 3.2 is consistent with the known density of squarefree numbers: $6/\pi^2\approx 0.6079$.
  • The computed maximal gaps up to $10^7$ (at most $10$) are consistent with the easy upper bound $\ll\sqrt{x}$.
  • The CRT construction in Lemma 3.4 is structurally analogous to the classical constructions of long prime gaps (Erdős--Rankin type), but with prime squares.

6. FINAL

UNRESOLVED.
  • (i) Best partial results proved here. We proved:
    1. The classical counting asymptotic $Q(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^2}x+O(\sqrt{x})$.
    2. A consequent unconditional gap upper bound $s_{n+1}-s_n\ll\sqrt{s_n}$.
    3. A constructive lower bound showing gaps of size $\gg \log x/\log\log x$ occur infinitely often.
    4. Small-scale computation: max gap $\le 10$ for squarefrees up to $10^7$.

  • (ii) First gap/obstruction to a full solution. The question asks for a much stronger upper bound: $s_{n+1}-s_n=o(s_n^{1/5})$. The elementary $O(\sqrt{x})$-type methods above are far from the $x^{1/5}$ scale. Reaching exponents near $1/5$ requires deep estimates for squarefree numbers in very short intervals (lattice-point counts near curves / exponential sum technology).

  • (iii) What would be needed next. To resolve the problem one would need either:
    1. a proof that every interval $[X,X+X^{1/5}/g(X)]$ (with $g(X)\to\infty$) contains a squarefree number, or
    2. equivalently, a global upper bound on the maximal squarefree gap up to $X$ of order $o(X^{1/5})$.
    This lies beyond classical inclusion--exclusion and requires the strongest known short-interval methods. (As noted in the problem statement, much sharper results are known in the literature, including improvements beyond the $1/5$ exponent.)

7. OPTIONAL: SHARPENING / DISCUSSION

  • The lower bound construction in Lemma 3.4 can be optimized (with more sophisticated residue-class choices) to increase the constant in front of $\log x/\log\log x$; the conjectured/known-best constants in the literature are substantially larger.
  • Conditional results (e.g. from the ABC conjecture) can force gaps to be extremely small (polylogarithmic), which would imply $o(x^{1/5})$ immediately.
