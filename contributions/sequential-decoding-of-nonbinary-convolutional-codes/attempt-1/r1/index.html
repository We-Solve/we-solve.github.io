A terminated trellis baseline isolates the harder search question

The source distinguishes sequential tree-search decoders from Viterbi decoding and emphasizes dependence on the column-distance profile. We analyze a small fixed-memory baseline without calling it a general sequential-decoding theorem. Over $\mathbb F_3$, take $T\ge1$ information symbols $u_1,\ldots,u_T$, put $u_0=u_{T+1}=0$, and transmit the $T+1$ pairs \[ c_t=(u_t,u_t+u_{t-1}),\qquad 1\le t\le T+1. \] Assume independent ternary symmetric substitution noise: a symbol is unchanged with probability $1-p$ and becomes each other symbol with probability $p/2$, where $0<p<2/3$.
Proposition: This terminated code has minimum Hamming distance 3. Maximum-likelihood decoding can be performed by a three-state trellis in $O(T)$ arithmetic operations, and it corrects every single-symbol error uniquely.
Proof: For the difference of two input sequences, let $r$ and $s$ be the first and last nonzero positions. The systematic coordinates contain at least one nonzero symbol. The parity coordinate at $r$ is nonzero because its preceding difference is zero, and that at $s+1$ is nonzero because its following difference is zero. These are distinct coordinates, so the output weight is at least 3. A single nonzero input symbol attains weight 3, proving equality. Two different codewords cannot both be within distance one of a received word, by the triangle inequality, proving unique correction. A received length-$2(T+1)$ word has likelihood $(1-p)^{L-h}(p/2)^h$ at Hamming distance $h$ from a codeword, where $L=2(T+1)$. Since $1-p>p/2$, maximizing likelihood is minimizing Hamming distance. Let $D_t(s)$ be the smallest accumulated distance among paths ending in state $u_t=s$. Initialize $D_0(0)=0$ and the other states to infinity, and update \[ D_t(s)=\min_{r\in\mathbb F_3}\{D_{t-1}(r)+d_H(y_t,(s,s+r))\}. \] Induction on $t$ proves this minimum-path interpretation: every path has a unique previous state, and the displayed transition is its exact new distance cost. At the final stage require $s=0$. Back pointers recover a minimizing path. Each stage considers nine transitions, proving the runtime bound. ∎
