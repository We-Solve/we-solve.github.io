Irreducible Components of Landau Varieties

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A Landau variety is a locus of kinematic parameters where singularities of an integral, such as a Feynman integral, can arise from degeneracy of its integration geometry. Elimination removes auxiliary integration variables from algebraic criticality equations. An irreducible component is one which cannot be written as the union of two smaller algebraic closed subsets. Feynman-diagram combinatorics includes the graph data defining the Lee--Pomeransky polynomial of the integral.

A one-loop discriminant test.
For positive unequal masses $m_1,m_2$, consider the two-propagator Feynman-parameter polynomial \[ F(x)=xm_1^2+(1-x)m_2^2-sx(1-x) =sx^2+(m_1^2-m_2^2-s)x+m_2^2. \] An interior stationary zero requires $F=F'=0$. For $s\ne0$, elimination of $x$ gives the quadratic discriminant \[ (m_1^2-m_2^2-s)^2-4sm_2^2 =[s-(m_1+m_2)^2][s-(m_1-m_2)^2]. \] Thus for fixed distinct positive masses the reduced algebraic candidate locus splits into two distinct linear factors in $s$.
Real-contour qualification.
The critical point is $x=(s+m_2^2-m_1^2)/(2s)$. At the plus threshold it equals $m_2/(m_1+m_2)\in(0,1)$. If $m_1>m_2$, the minus-threshold point is $-m_2/(m_1-m_2)<0$; reversing the masses puts it above one. Hence an algebraic component need not meet this real integration interval as an interior pinch. This does not remove it from the algebraic Landau variety or rule out other sheets. Equal masses require a separate $s=0$ analysis because the displayed division is then invalid.
