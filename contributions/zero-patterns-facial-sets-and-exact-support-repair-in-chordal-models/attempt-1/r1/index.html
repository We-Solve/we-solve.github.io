Zero patterns and likelihood faces: a separation certificate and a model-dependence obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. A zero pattern lists cells with zero observed counts. In the source convention an MLE must assign strictly positive probabilities, so a boundary fit is not an existing MLE. For a model matrix $A$, the feasible-margin cone is the nonnegative span of its columns; its facets are supporting faces of codimension one.

Attempt and results

Fix a finite multinomial log-linear family $p_j(\theta)=\exp(\theta\cdot a_j)/\sum_l\exp(\theta\cdot a_l)$, where $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ are specified real statistic columns. There are counts $n_j\ge0$ with total $N>0$. A finite parameter gives strictly positive probabilities, as required by the source. Parameter directions constant on all columns do not change the distribution.
A zero-pattern certificate of nonexistence.
Suppose a vector $h$ and number $c$ satisfy $h\cdot a_j\le c$ for every cell, with a strict inequality for at least one cell, and every observed cell ($n_j>0$) satisfies equality. The log likelihood, up to a count-only constant, is $\ell(\theta)=\theta\cdot\sum n_ja_j-N\log\sum e^{\theta\cdot a_j}$. Along $\theta+th$ its derivative is \[ N\left(c-\sum_jp_j(\theta+th)h\cdot a_j\right)>0 \] for every finite $t$, because each cell has positive fitted probability and at least one inequality is strict. Consequently no finite parameter maximizes the likelihood. This proves directly that observed support contained in a proper exposed face obstructs an ordinary positive MLE. It is a certificate one can check for any stated model matrix; it is not a combinatorial enumeration of all faces of all graphical models.
Attempted simplification to a condition on zero count alone.
Consider the $2$ by $2$ count table $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Under the independence family $p_{ij}=r_i c_j$, its likelihood is $r_0r_1c_0c_1$. For positive margins summing to one, $r_0r_1\le1/4$ and $c_0c_1\le1/4$, with equality uniquely at equal margins. Thus the positive uniform table is the unique maximizing distribution despite two zero observed cells. In the saturated positive model the likelihood is $p_{00}p_{11}$. Because the other two probabilities are strictly positive, $p_{00}+p_{11}<1$, so $p_{00}p_{11}<1/4$. Taking the two unobserved probabilities to zero equally approaches $1/4$. The supremum is not attained in the strictly positive model. The same zero pattern therefore has opposite MLE-existence answers in two different models.
The remaining combinatorial task.
An attempt to classify obstruction patterns using only the number or locations of observed zeros, without the statistic matrix, cannot work by that example. For a fixed graph, the useful next step would enumerate which supports fit in proper faces of its specific marginal cone. The separation derivative alone merely provides an abstract certificate and does not furnish that requested combinatorial facet description. Structural zeros change the admissible columns and require a separate model, rather than treating forbidden cells as additional sampling zeros.
