\maketitle \begin{abstract} The four-total-insertion/deletion connectivity assertion recorded as UnsolvedMath ID 3100089 is false. A five-cycle with one pendant vertex, colored black on two adjacent cycle vertices, has exactly two successful pressing words. Their longest common subsequence has length two, although both words have length six; their total insertion--deletion distance is therefore eight. We give the entire branching proof directly from the pressing rule. This is a full disproof of the recorded four-total-edit assertion, while the separate formulation allowing four deletions from each word is consistent with this example. Supporting exhaustive checks reproduce the proof. Historical novelty and independent human or formal proof verification are not established. \end{abstract}

Original target and conventions

The target is the unchanged repository definition of UnsolvedMath ID 3100089 (AMR-030-0089), whose source-catalogue status remains open [catalogue]. A bicolored graph is a finite simple graph with each vertex black or white. A legal press at a currently black vertex $v$ complements the edges between distinct vertices of its closed neighborhood $N[v]$ and flips every color there. All other edges and colors remain unchanged. A successful pressing word consists of legal presses and ends with an edgeless all-white graph. In particular, an all-white graph with a surviving edge is not a successful terminal state. A pressed vertex becomes isolated and white, and stays that way, so no vertex can appear twice in a legal word.

Each insertion or deletion of one vertex symbol costs one edit. Thus a replacement costs two; there is no unit-cost substitution operation. Intermediate words in computing edit distance need not be successful. Words distinguish the individual vertex symbols, including vertices exchanged by a symmetry of the graph. Let $H_4(G)$ have the successful words as vertices, with distinct words joined when their total insertion--deletion distance is at most four. The exact repository target, with the four-edit wording read literally, is whether, for every such graph and coloring, every pair of successful words can be joined by a finite path in $H_4(G)$ [cooper, Conjecture 1]. The pairwise assertion is vacuous for an empty successful-word family; it also holds for a singleton. The empty word succeeds precisely for an initially edgeless all-white graph. These conventions are preserved.

Counterexample and complete proof

Let $G$ have vertices $V=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, edges \[ E=\left\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\},\{4,5\},\{5,1\},\{2,6\}\right\}, \qquad B=\{4,5\}, \] where $B$ is the set of initially black vertices. Thus $1,2,3,4,5$ form a five-cycle and $6$ is a white pendant vertex at $2$. This is a connected finite simple graph and has black vertices, so the counterexample does not rely on vacuous connectivity or on lack of successful pressing words.

Theorem: The complete successful-word family of $G$ is \[ \sigma=4\,3\,2\,6\,5\,1, \qquad \tau=5\,1\,2\,6\,4\,3. \] Their total insertion--deletion distance is eight. Consequently $H_4(G)$ consists of two isolated vertices, and the original universal connectivity assertion is false.
Proof: We first determine every successful word directly from the graph rule. Write $ij$ for the unordered edge $\{i,j\}$, and write a pressing prefix without separators since every symbol is a single digit. The color-preserving automorphism \[ \rho=(1\ 3)(4\ 5),\qquad \rho(2)=2,\quad\rho(6)=6 \] exchanges the two initially black vertices. Every legal nonempty word starts with $4$ or $5$. It therefore suffices to analyze the branch starting with $4$, and then apply $\rho$.

Here is also an explicit derivation of the branching rows. Pressing $4$ uses $N[4]=\{3,4,5\}$: the edges $34,45$ disappear, $35$ appears, and only $3$ is black. Pressing that forced $3$ uses $\{2,3,5\}$: $23,35$ disappear, $25$ appears, and the black set becomes $\{2,5\}$. The remaining edges are $12,15,25,26$.

If $5$ is chosen next, its closed neighborhood is $\{1,2,5\}$. All three edges of that triangle disappear, leaving only $26$ and the black vertex $1$. Pressing the isolated $1$ leaves an all-white graph with edge $26$, so this branch cannot succeed.

If $2$ is chosen after $43$, its closed neighborhood is $\{1,2,5,6\}$. All edges incident with $2$ disappear; $15$ disappears and $16,56$ appear. The black set becomes $\{1,6\}$. Pressing $1$ at this point removes $16$ and makes $6$ white, leaving the all-white edge $56$; this branch also fails. Choosing $6$ instead uses $\{1,5,6\}$, removes $16,56$, creates $15$, and leaves $5$ as the only black vertex. The next press $5$ removes $15$ and makes $1$ black; pressing $1$ then gives the edgeless all-white graph.

All decisions have been exhausted: the only successful word starting with $4$ is $\sigma$. Applying $\rho$ gives the only successful word starting with $5$, namely $\tau$. The initial graph is not terminal, so the empty word adds no success. A stalled all-white graph admits no further legal press, and a successful terminal state also admits no further press. This proves completeness of the two-word family, without any matrix theorem or computational assumption.

For the distance, divide the symbols into the ordered pairs \[ P=(4,3),\qquad Q=(2,6),\qquad R=(5,1). \] The words have block orders $\sigma=PQR$ and $\tau=RQP$, with the same internal order within each pair. The relative order of any two symbols from different blocks is reversed between the two words. Thus a common subsequence can contain symbols from only one block, and has length at most two. Each pair itself is common, so ${\operatorname{LCS}}(\sigma,\tau)=2$.

Under insertions and deletions, the original symbols that survive form a common subsequence. At most two of the six can survive, so at least four deletions and four insertions are necessary. Conversely, keep the common pair $26$, delete the other four symbols from $\sigma$, and insert $51$ before it and $43$ after it. This gives $\tau$ with eight edits. Hence ${\operatorname{ed}}(\sigma,\tau)=8$ exactly. Since these are the only successful words, they have no edge in $H_4(G)$ and there is no intermediate successful vertex through which to connect them. This disproves the universal statement. ∎

Corollary: Any total insertion--deletion threshold that connects successful words for every bicolored graph must be at least eight. For the displayed graph, eight is exactly the smallest threshold giving connectivity.
Proof: The same two-vertex word graph has no edge at any threshold below eight, and has its single connecting edge at threshold eight. ∎

Formulation and literature check

The distinction between four total edits and four deletions from each word is essential. Cooper--Davis [cooper, Conjecture 1] and the repository definition use the former wording. Their 2015 revision also expresses doubts about that statement for general graphs. The counterexample above answers that precise four-total-edit question negatively. It does not replace the original target with a different bound.

Bixby--Flint--Miklós [bixby, Conjecture 6] use a longest-common- subsequence deficit of four for the general conjecture. For two successful words of common length $k$, a deficit $d$ means total insertion--deletion distance $2d$. Their general formulation therefore permits eight total edits. Their Theorem 7 proves the stronger deficit-two result for paths. Our graph is not a path, and the two words here have deficit four, so the example is consistent with both of those statements. These literature results supply context and are not dependencies of our proof.

An indexed Joint Mathematics Meetings abstract by Bowen Li, István Miklós, and Carter Rockhold Teplica [jmm], for January 2024, announces connectivity when neighboring words can be made identical by deleting at most four terms from each. That is likewise the eight-total-edit formulation. The indexed abstract was inspected; direct access to the PDF failed and no underlying proof was accessed. We make no claim to have verified that general announcement, and our counterexample does not contradict it.

The Bixby--Flint--Miklós arXiv formulation already states the larger bound explicitly. This manuscript gives a counterexample to the literal four-total-edit formulation and clarifies the difference between the recorded formulations. We do not claim to have refuted Bixby--Flint--Miklós Conjecture 6 or resolved the historical Pressing Game Conjecture under its eight-total-edit meaning.

The pre-submission review on 11 October 2026 expanded the source check. Besides the Cooper--Davis and Bixby--Flint--Miklós papers, we inspected the accessible text of the uniquely pressable graph and autonomous poset papers [unique,vposets]. The illustrated two-poset example in [vposets, Figure 2] has four vertices, unlike our graph; its illustrated four-vertex generators and the text's two-word example are also distinct. We inspected the bicolored graph illustration in the Cooper--Whitlatch poster [poster]; it is a different six-vertex graph. Miklós's dissertation [miklosthesis, Chapter 9, pp. 167--178], in its conjecture statement and discussion, uses the LCS-deficit-four formulation and the stronger path result. No matching counterexample was located in these inspected passages or figures.

We also consulted Cooper's problems page [problems] and the indexed JMM abstract. Searches included counterexamples, two successful words, six-vertex graphs, cycles with pendant vertices, equivalent edit/LCS wordings, and the named conjecture and authors. Queries of the target repository's public issues and pull requests for the ID and pressing terminology returned no matching records at the time of checking. These are search results, not a proof of absence of prior work.

Whitlatch's 2019 dissertation [whitlatchthesis] is particularly relevant because its abstract names the complexity of the pressing space. Its landing page and abstract were accessible, but the linked full-text PDF returned HTTP 403 and was not inspected. The JMM full proof was also not accessed. Historical novelty therefore remains unestablished. Neither absence of a search result nor the size of the example justifies calling it new. Prior publication of this exact graph, a relabeling, or an equivalent construction is not ruled out.

Discovery, reproducibility, and verification

This new version preserves the earlier star manuscript and the earlier double-star manuscript [priorstar,priordouble] without alteration. Those restricted positive results do not address this cyclic graph. They motivated continued research, but no lemma from them is required for the counterexample proof.

Discovery used a standard-library Python enumeration of successful pressing words, with augmented-adjacency updates and common two-deletion signatures for four-edit adjacency. The input was the NetworkX graph-atlas data [atlas], downloaded from its public repository without importing the NetworkX package. The search stopped at atlas index 104, the graph above, with zero-based black-color mask 24. It had checked 4,596 graph/color cases and enumerated 97,571 successful words; none of those cases was skipped for the word-count cap. This is a discovery log, not an exhaustive check of the entire atlas or of all graphs. No vertex-minimality claim is needed or made here.

The separate repository script \ttfamily scripts/verify_pressing_3100089_counterexample.py  does not import the search script or require atlas data. It enumerates the whole legal tree by edge sets and color sets: 19 prefixes, exactly the two displayed successes, and four stalled maximal words \[
              4321,\quad4351,\quad5123,\quad5143.
\] It checks every row of the proof table and the reflection $\rho$. A separate dense principal-determinant implementation checks all 64 vertex subsets and all 1,957 distinct-symbol words of lengths zero through six, including all 720 full permutations. Its legal flags agree exactly with the direct pressing tree, and its full successful family agrees with the theorem. Direct insertion/deletion dynamic programming and enumeration of common subsequences both confirm distance eight and longest-common-subsequence length two. All these checks passed with Python 3.13.2.

Scope, remaining limits, and contributions

Full-solution scope declaration.
Theorem Theorem disproves the entire original universal four-total-edit assertion by one explicit admissible colored graph. Every legal pressing branch on that graph is exhausted, and the entire successful-word family has disconnected $H_4$. A counterexample is sufficient to disprove the universal quantifier; it need not classify all other graphs, colorings, empty families, isolated vertices, or the empty graph. No significant external theorem or computation is a proof dependency. There is no remaining mathematical gap in the disproof claimed here. Independent human/formal verification and historical novelty are unestablished. The distinct universal eight-total-edit formulation has not been proved or disproved in this manuscript.

This manuscript therefore records ATTEMPT_STATUS: solved as an LLM full-disproof claim for the exact recorded target. The catalogue definition and its source status remain unchanged. Neither that metadata nor the model's audit is an external acceptance or peer-review verdict.
