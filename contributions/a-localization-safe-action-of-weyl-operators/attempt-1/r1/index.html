Fraction-field evaluation of Weyl words and a quotient-ring obstruction

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A differential operator on a commutative ring is built from multiplication and derivations, subject to their composition relations. On a polynomial ring, the Weyl operators satisfy $\partial_i x_j-x_j\partial_i=\delta_{ij}$. A derivation extends to fractions by $\partial(a/b)=(b\partial a-a\partial b)/b^2$ when the fraction field exists.

A precise polynomial-domain action.
Let $R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ for a characteristic-zero field $k$, and $K$ its fraction field. Multiplication by $x_i$ and ordinary polynomial partial differentiation $D_i$ act on $R$. Extend $D_i$ by \[ D_i(f/g)=\frac{gD_if-fD_ig}{g^2},\qquad g\ne0. \] This is independent of representation: if $fg'=f'g$, differentiating that identity and multiplying out shows $(gD_if-fD_ig)(g')^2=(g'D_if'-f'D_ig')g^2$. The sum and product rules follow by putting fractions over common denominators and expanding numerators. Any extension satisfying the product rule must have $D_i(g^{-1})=-g^{-2}D_ig$, by differentiating $gg^{-1}=1$, so it is unique. The commutator of two derivations is a derivation: expanding its action on $ab$ cancels the two mixed terms. Hence $[D_i,D_j]$ is a derivation that vanishes on $k$ and every $x_l$, and consequently on $R$ and on inverses of all nonzero polynomials. Thus it vanishes on $K$. The product rule also gives $[D_i,x_j](h)=\delta_{ij}h$. These checks establish a representation of the Weyl algebra defined by these generator relations, so two equal operator expressions give equal rational functions.
Finite polynomial numerator recursion.
For one ordered word $w=(i_1,\ldots,i_t)$ of derivatives, define $N_0=f$ and \[ N_{s+1}=gD_{i_{s+1}}N_s-(s+1)N_sD_{i_{s+1}}g. \] Induction using the quotient rule proves $D_{i_s}\cdots D_{i_1}(f/g)=N_s/g^{s+1}$. For an operator given as a finite coefficient-left sum $P=\sum_w a_w D_{i_{|w|}}\cdots D_{i_1}$ of order at most $m$, we obtain \[ P(f/g)=g^{-m-1}\sum_w a_w N_{|w|}g^{m-|w|}. \] Thus only polynomial arithmetic and one final fraction are required; this is not a bound on coefficient growth or runtime. Shared prefixes can reuse $N_s$. Derivative commutation, already proved, shows that two orders with the same multiplicities give the same $N_s$, since their denominators coincide in a domain. For $g=x$, $f=1$, and $P=D_x^m$, induction gives $P(1/x)=(-1)^m m!/x^{m+1}$. In characteristic zero its reduced denominator cannot be replaced uniformly by $x^m$, because $x$ does not divide the nonzero constant $m!$. This verifies the worst-case denominator exponent.
The attempted quotient shortcut fails.
A $k$-linear operator $P:R\to R$ induces a well-defined map on $R/I$ exactly when $P(I)\subseteq I$. Necessity follows by applying the proposed quotient map to the zero class of each $h\in I$. Conversely, representatives differing by an element of $I$ then have images differing by an element of $I$. For $R=k[x]$, $I=(x^2)$, differentiation fails: $D(x^2)=2x\notin I$. In particular the representatives $0$ and $x^2$ would give distinct output classes. Moreover this quotient has a nonzero nilpotent and no fraction field. Consequently dispatching the polynomial formula on arbitrary quotient-ring representatives is incorrect even before performance is considered.
