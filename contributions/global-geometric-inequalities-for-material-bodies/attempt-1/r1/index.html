Global Geometric Inequalities for Material Bodies

Definitions and conventions.
A material body is a bounded matter region in gravitational initial data or a spacetime, with mass, angular momentum, charge and geometric size defined by a stated convention. A purely global inequality compares global quantities, unlike a bound confined to a single horizon section. Shape restrictions such as convexity or roundedness control how a radius or size describes the whole body. A sharp inequality includes the best constant and, where appropriate, an extremal or equality description.

A size-sensitive angular momentum estimate.
In a Euclidean diagnostic model let matter density $\rho\ge0$ and momentum density $j$ be integrable, with $|j|\le\rho$, support in a ball of radius $R$, mass $m=\int\rho$, and angular momentum $J=\int x\times j$. Then \[ |J|\le\int|x|\,|j|\le Rm. \] This uses a circumscribed radius, not a volume radius. To see why those cannot be exchanged without shape control, place two identical balls of fixed small radius at $\pm Re_1$, each with total density mass $m/2$, and assign momenta $j=\rho e_2$ in the positive ball and $j=-\rho e_2$ in the negative one. By symmetry their internal first moments vanish, so total momentum is zero and $J=mR e_3$. Total occupied volume and mass stay fixed as $R\to\infty$.
