Nonconvex Gradient Gibbs States and Free Energy

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
A height field is $\phi:\mathbb Z^d\to\mathbb R$ and its gradient variables are $\eta_i(x)=\phi(x+e_i)-\phi(x)$. A gradient Gibbs state is a probability measure on these variables satisfying the local Gibbs conditional distributions. Boundary tilt $u\in\mathbb R^d$ controls the mean gradient. The free energy, or surface tension, is the appropriately normalized limit of $-\beta^{-1}\log Z_\Lambda(u)$. Nonconvex interactions have potentials whose Hessian need not be positive, so standard uniformly convex uniqueness and fluctuation arguments need not apply.
Source scope and qualifications.
The report discusses perturbations of a Gaussian potential and a low-temperature RG breakthrough. The question asks what can be proved more broadly; it does not assert universal uniqueness or strict convexity for every nonconvex interaction.

An independent-bond nonconvex subcase.
In one spatial dimension take an open chain with independent gradient variables $\eta_j$ and potential $V(s)=(s^2-1)^2$. In a stress ensemble of parameter $h\in\mathbb R$, \[ z(h)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\beta V(s)+hs}ds,\qquad p(h)=\beta^{-1}\log z(h). \] All derivatives in $h$ can be taken under the integral on compact $h$-intervals, because the quartic negative tail dominates any polynomial and any bounded linear tilt. Direct differentiation gives \[ p'(h)=\beta^{-1}\mathbb E_h\eta,\qquad p''(h)=\beta^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_h(\eta)>0. \] Strictness holds because the density is positive on every interval and cannot be concentrated at a single value. Thus a nonconvex microscopic potential can yield strictly convex pressure in this exactly decoupled gradient ensemble.
A unique local specification in this setting.
The product measure with the displayed one-bond density has the Gibbs property. Conversely, if every finite set of bond variables has this product conditional law given its complement, integrating that conditional law gives the same finite-dimensional product marginals; hence the infinite gradient measure is that product measure. This elementary uniqueness uses the open one-dimensional bond representation and the chosen stress ensemble.
Why higher dimensions differ.
In dimensions at least two, gradients satisfy plaquette constraints, so they cannot be assigned independently. Fixed-tilt boundary ensembles also require a constrained limit not proved by the finite-stress calculation. The source asks broadly about free energy and Gibbs states, including those interacting constraints. Controlling them without uniform convexity is the first missing step; no universal uniqueness claim for nonconvex gradient models is made.
