Mathematical Classification of Atomic Chemical Groups

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For nuclear charge $Z>0$ and $N\ge1$ electrons, the source uses the nonrelativistic atomic Hamiltonian in atomic units, \[ H_{N,Z}=\sum_{i=1}^N\left(-\tfrac12\Delta_{x_i}-\frac{Z}{|x_i|}\right)+ \sum_{1\le i<j\le N}\frac1{|x_i-x_j|}, \] on antisymmetric wavefunctions in $L^2((\mathbb R^3\times\{\uparrow,\downarrow\})^N)$. The nucleus is fixed in the Born--Oppenheimer approximation. Total orbital angular momentum, total spin, and parity commute with this Hamiltonian. Their labels on symmetry eigenspaces are $L,S,p$, with eigenvalues $L(L+1)$ and $S(S+1)$ for the squared angular-momentum operators.
Source scope and qualifications.
The source proposes equality of total L,S,p ground-state quantum numbers as a candidate equivalence relation but notes that it need not separate every chemical group. Choosing it is part of the investigation, not a completed derivation of the periodic table. Classification, assigning atoms, and obtaining a simple structural description are all retained.

Hydrogenic symmetry labels from a square completion.
For one electron let $r=|x|$ and work initially with smooth compactly supported scalar wavefunctions away from zero. Integration by parts using $\operatorname{div}(x/r)=2/r$ gives \[ \langle\psi,(-\tfrac12\Delta-Z/r)\psi\rangle +\tfrac{Z^2}{2}\|\psi\|^2 =\tfrac12\int|\nabla\psi+Z(x/r)\psi|^2dx\geq0. \] Approximation extends the identity to the natural form domain. Equality is attained by $\psi(x)=Ce^{-Zr}$, which is square integrable for $Z>0$. Conversely the first-order equality equation forces $e^{Zr}\psi$ to have zero gradient and hence to be constant on connected $\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}$. Thus the spatial ground eigenspace is one-dimensional, radial and even.
The proposed equivalence labels in this subcase.
Including the two spin components gives total labels $L=0,S=1/2,p=+1$ and ground multiplicity two for every one-electron ion, regardless of $Z$. These labels determine the symmetry representation but discard the radial scale: the normalized density has characteristic radius proportional to $1/Z$, as follows by the substitution $y=Zx$. Thus even exact labels do not encode all quantitative electronic structure.
Why the full classification is missing.
The calculation reconstructs the hydrogenic baseline mentioned in the source. It does not assign many-electron neutral atoms to chemical groups or justify that equality of $(L,S,p)$ matches those groups. Electron repulsion and antisymmetrization for $N>1$ prevent the scalar square completion from identifying the ground multiplet. The first gap is a rigorous many-electron ground-state classification with enough information to test the proposed equivalence relation, followed by a simple configuration description.
