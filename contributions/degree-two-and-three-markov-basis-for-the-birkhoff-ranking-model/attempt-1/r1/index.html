A finite degree-p Markov basis for complete-ranking surveys

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

For an integer matrix $A$ and statistic vector $b$, the nonnegative integer fiber is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb N^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves in $\ker A$ connecting every such fiber by moves that remain nonnegative. Its binomial generators form the toric ideal $I_A=\langle x^u-x^v:Au=Av\rangle$. In the ranking model, coordinates count permutations and $A$ has the vectorized permutation matrices as columns. The fixed summary counts how often each candidate appears at each rank.

Exact target and construction

Fix any integer $p\ge1$. A survey is a vector of nonnegative integer permutation multiplicities; zero voters are allowed. No running-time or degree-three restriction occurs in original Problem 1. We construct a finite basis of degree at most $p$. Its enumeration is large and is not claimed efficient. This is a complete rederivation of an elementary known type of solution, not a claim of mathematical novelty. For every $q\in\{1,\ldots,p\}$, enumerate all pairs of multisets of $q$ permutations whose permutation-matrix sums agree, and include their nonzero multiplicity differences as moves (one sign suffices). This is explicit finite enumeration: there are only $p!$ available permutations and finitely many multisets of each bounded cardinality.
Integer regular bipartite decomposition.
Every $r$-regular bipartite multigraph with $p$ vertices on either side, where $r\ge1$, has a perfect matching. Here is the needed matching proof. Take a matching of largest size. If some left vertex is unmatched, form the vertices reachable from it by paths alternating between unmatched edges from left to right and matched edges from right to left. A reached unmatched right vertex would give an augmenting path, contradicting maximal size. Thus all reached right vertices are matched, and their matched partners are reached left vertices; there is one more reached left vertex than reached right vertices. Every edge from a reached left vertex reaches a right vertex, so, writing the two reached sets $S,T$, regularity implies $r|S|\le r|T|$, a contradiction. A perfect matching exists. Removing it leaves an $(r-1)$-regular multigraph. Induction decomposes the multigraph into $r$ perfect matchings. The case $r=0$ is the empty decomposition. Parallel edges are counted with multiplicity throughout, so this proof applies to sums of permutation matrices.
Connectivity theorem and proof.
Let $f,g$ be surveys with the same first-order summary. Their voter counts are equal, since a row sum of the summary is the voter count. Cancel common permutation occurrences. If nothing remains, the surveys agree. Otherwise choose one permutation $\pi$ occurring in the remaining target survey $g$. Every one of its $p$ edges occurs in the summary of the remaining current survey $f$. For each edge choose one permutation occurrence of $f$ containing that edge; take the union of the chosen occurrences, counting any one occurrence only once. This selects $q\le p$ occurrences, with $q\ge1$. Their sum $B$ is entrywise at least $P_\pi$. The matrix $B-P_\pi$ is nonnegative integral with every row and column sum $q-1$. By the preceding decomposition it is a sum of $q-1$ permutation matrices. Replacing the selected $q$ occurrences by $\pi$ and these $q-1$ permutations is one of our moves, or the zero change. It remains nonnegative because only actually present occurrences were removed. Cancel the newly obtained occurrence of $\pi$ against its target occurrence. Repetition decreases the remaining target voter count by at least one and terminates. Every fiber is therefore connected. The empty fiber requires nothing; the zero-voter fiber is a singleton. For $p=1,2$, distinct permutation matrices have independent multiplicities determined by the summary, so all fibers are singletons and the empty nonzero move set is correct.
Generation of the toric ideal.
Use one polynomial variable $X_\pi$ for each ranking and write $X^f$ for its survey monomial. For a permitted move $u=u^+-u^-$ with disjoint nonnegative parts, both $Au^+=Au^-$ and its binomial $X^{u^+}-X^{u^-}\in I_A$ hold by construction. If a nonnegative path step takes $f$ to $g=f-u$, feasibility gives $f-u^+\ge0$ and \[ X^f-X^g=X^{f-u^+}(X^{u^+}-X^{u^-}). \] The opposite move changes only the sign. Telescoping this identity along our connecting path expresses every generating binomial $X^f-X^g$ with $Af=Ag$ in the ideal generated by the displayed finite move set. The reverse containment follows because each move binomial lies in $I_A$. Thus the construction also answers the source's toric-ideal formulation.
Why quadratic moves alone cannot be assumed.
For $p=3$, the three even permutations and the three odd permutations each sum to the all-one $3$ by $3$ matrix. These are the only surveys with that summary: any two chosen disjoint permutations have union a single six-cycle, whose complement is the uniquely remaining permutation; the two one-factorizations are precisely the parity classes. More explicitly, after picking the image of candidate one, its two remaining image choices for candidate two determine the permutation, and pairwise disjointness forces either the three cyclic shifts or their three reversals. These two surveys have disjoint support, so their difference has degree three and no nontrivial degree-two step within this two-point fiber exists. This check prevents an unjustified reduction of our bounded degree-p construction to pairwise swaps.
