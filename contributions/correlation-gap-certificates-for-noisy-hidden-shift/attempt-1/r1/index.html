Research attempt: Hamming separation, an arbitrarily small noise obstruction, and sampling cost

Let $G$ be a finite additive group of size $N$, let $f:G\to\mathcal A$, and put $f_s(x)=f(x+s)$. Define the period subgroup $H=\{h:f(x+h)=f(x)\ \forall x\}$. It is a subgroup because zero is a period, sums of periods are periods, and translating the identity by $-h$ proves that $-h$ is a period. Also $f_s=f_t$ exactly when $s-t\in H$. If more than one distinct translate exists, define \[ \Delta=\min_{f_s\ne f_t}d_H(f_s,f_t), \] where $d_H$ counts differing coordinates. Suppose the observed function $g$ differs from some true translate $f_s$ on at most $e$ coordinates. If $2e<\Delta$, its nearest translate is uniquely $f_s$ modulo $H$. Indeed every different translate satisfies $d_H(g,f_t)\geq d_H(f_s,f_t)-d_H(g,f_s)\geq\Delta-e>e$.

The factor two cannot be discarded for adversarial noise. On coordinates where two functions at distance $\Delta$ disagree, construct $g$ by taking half the values from each. When $\Delta$ is even, $g$ has distance $\Delta/2$ from both; for odd $\Delta$ the two distances are the neighboring integers. More drastically, take $f=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}$ on any group of size $N\geq2$. Its distinct translates have distance two, and the all-zero observation is one corrupted coordinate away from every translate. Thus noise fraction $1/N$, tending to zero, can leave all $N$ shifts compatible with the same noisy data. A universal positive tolerated fraction cannot follow without a separation or function-class promise.

To quantify a route that does work under a gap, let $C_t=N^{-1}|\{x:g(x)=f(x+t)\}|$. Suppose a unique translate class maximizes $C_t$, and every other class is at least $\gamma>0$ below it. Draw $m$ independent uniform positions $X_j$, and estimate every score by \[ \widehat C_t={1\over m}\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf1\{g(X_j)=f(X_j+t)\}. \] Each estimator has variance at most $1/(4m)$. Hence $\Pr\{|\widehat C_t-C_t|\geq\gamma/2\}\leq1/(m\gamma^2)$ by applying Markov's inequality to the squared deviation. A union bound over at most $N$ shifts makes all deviations strictly less than $\gamma/2$ with probability at least $1-N/(m\gamma^2)$. On that event no lower-score class can win. Therefore $m\geq N/(\eta\gamma^2)$ suffices for error probability at most $\eta$, with at most $m(N+1)$ straightforward function evaluations. Shared sampled positions create dependence between candidate scores, but neither the per-score variance bound nor the union bound needs independence across candidates.

Under the earlier adversarial-noise promise, the score gap is at least $(\Delta-2e)/N$, by the distance inequalities already proved. Substitution gives an explicit, generally inefficient recovery bound whenever that quantity is positive. If all translates are identical, every shift is compatible and the identification task must instead specify a convention for its output.

Verification and adversarial audit

The point-mass example falsifies a noise tolerance depending only on the fraction corrupted. The sampling argument uses independent sampled positions with replacement, not independent oracle noise. It recovers the translate class, and does not falsely distinguish shifts in the stabilizer.
