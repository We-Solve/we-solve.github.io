Fingerprinting-rate attempt: one-pirate capacity and a finite-t information gap

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A fingerprinting code assigns distinct marked codewords to users. A coalition combines its copies into a pirate output; a tracing algorithm must identify a coalition member under the allowed attack model. Rate compares the logarithm of the user count with codeword length.

A code $C\subseteq\Sigma^N$ is a set of permitted length-$N$ words. Its Hamming minimum distance is the minimum number of unequal coordinates of two distinct words. For a linear $[N,K,D]_q$ code, rate is $K/N$ and relative distance is $D/N$; decoding recovers a codeword from corrupted observations.

Verbatim supplied catalogue question

For digital fingerprinting codes secure against collusion by $t$ pirates, what is the exact asymptotic rate, and does the known probabilistic construction attain that rate for every $t$?

Primary model and attempted information calculation

The original report specifies binary length-$L$ fingerprints for $N$ users, random code generation, and an accusation of one user. A valid coalition strategy sees its members' codewords and obeys the marking condition: a coordinate on which they agree cannot be altered. Security means the accused user belongs to the coalition with probability at least $1-\varepsilon$. We measure rate in bits, $R=(\log_2N)/L$. The report already asserts a matching leading constant as $t\to\infty$, while asking optimality of its construction for each fixed $t$. Reproducing a large-$t$ leading term is therefore insufficient.
Complete boundary case: one pirate has capacity one.
When $t=1$, marking forces the forged word to be that pirate's own word. For any fixed codebook, including one with repeated words, write $q_i(w)$ for the probability the decoder accuses user $i$ on input $w$. For a uniform pirate index, the success probability is $N^{-1}\sum_i q_i(c_i)$. Grouping users with the same codeword $w$ gives $\sum_{i:c_i=w}q_i(w)\le1$. There are at most $2^L$ groups, so success is at most $2^L/N$. This also holds after averaging a randomized codebook. Security for every singleton implies security for a uniform singleton, hence $N(1-\varepsilon)\le2^L$ and $R\le1+L^{-1}\log_2(1/(1-\varepsilon))$. Conversely assign all $2^L$ distinct binary words to distinct users and look up the received word. This has rate one and zero error. Thus the one-pirate asymptotic capacity is exactly one as $L\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\to0$. This elementary boundary case is not claimed new.
Interleaving attack and a controlled single-coordinate expansion.
Try independent pirate bits $X_1,\ldots,X_t$ with common bias $p\in(0,1)$. Let a pirate strategy choose a uniform member and output that bit $Y$. It obeys marking. Its conditional probabilities are \[ \Pr(Y=1\mid X_1=1)=p+(1-p)/t, \quad \Pr(Y=1\mid X_1=0)=p-p/t, \quad \Pr(Y=1)=p. \] With binary entropy $h(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)$, conditional entropy therefore gives the exact single-user mutual information \[ I(X_1;Y)=h(p)-p h(p+(1-p)/t)-(1-p)h(p-p/t). \] Fix $0<\delta\le1/2$ and $p\in[\delta,1-\delta]$, $t\ge2$. Taylor's formula applies on $[\delta/2,1-\delta/2]$, where $h^{(3)}$ is bounded. The weighted first-order shifts cancel, and their weighted squared sum is $p(1-p)/t^2$. Since $h''(p)=-1/(\ln2\,p(1-p))$, \[ I(X_1;Y)=\frac1{2\ln2\,t^2}+O_\delta(t^{-3}). \] More explicitly, if $H_\delta$ is the maximum of $|h^{(3)}|$ on that compact interval, the remainder in absolute value is at most $H_\delta/(6t^3)$, because each shift has magnitude at most $1/t$ and the two weights sum to one. This justifies the expansion and its lack of uniformity near biases zero and one. It does not prove a coding theorem or a converse for the source model.
Why one user's mutual information is not the fixed-t answer.
For $t=2$ and $p=1/2$, the preceding expression is exactly $1-h(1/4)$. For the pair of pirate bits jointly, equal bits occur with probability $1/2$ and make $Y$ deterministic; unequal bits make $Y$ fair. Thus $H(Y\mid X_1,X_2)=1/2$ and $\tfrac12 I((X_1,X_2);Y)=1/4$. These quantities differ: $h(1/4)=2-\tfrac34\log_2 3>3/4$, because $\log_2 3<5/3$ is equivalent to $27<32$. Hence $1-h(1/4)<1/4$. The two natural information expressions already disagree at fixed $t$; choosing one by analogy would not prove the source's operational capacity. One must justify the code ensemble, the allowable adversarial strategies, the tracing criterion, and the relevant achievability/converse reductions. No such equivalence is established by this calculation.

Adversarial audit and unresolved scope

The audit retained the per-coalition security quantifier, allowed randomized codebooks and decoders in the boundary converse, and separated fixed-$t$ optimality from the already reported large-$t$ constant. The inspected Anthapadmanabhan--Barg--Dumer primary abstract discusses bounds and distinct multiuser-channel converses; its full proof was not used here. No simulation was performed. The exact rates and optimality of the report's specific probabilistic construction for every $t$ remain unproved by this attempt.
