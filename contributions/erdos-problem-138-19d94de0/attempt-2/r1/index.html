Problem 138: van der Waerden numbers (two colors)

1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Definition (Two--color van der Waerden number): For an integer $k\ge 1$, define $W(k)$ to be the least integer $N$ such that for every coloring $c:[N]\to\{\text{red},\text{blue}\}$, there exist integers $a\ge 1$ and $d\ge 1$ with $a+(k-1)d\le N$ such that \[ c(a)=c(a+d)=\cdots=c\left(a+(k-1)d\right). \] Equivalently, every $2$--coloring of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $k$.

The requested "improvement of bounds" is open--ended; the specific sample target statement in the prompt is:

Show that $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$.

Equivalently: for every constant $C>0$, there exists $k_0$ such that $W(k)>C^k$ for all $k\ge k_0$.

Trivial/edge cases. $W(1)=1$ and $W(2)=3$ (since a monochromatic $2$--term progression is just two numbers of the same color). The first nontrivial case is $k=3$.

2) QUICK LITERATURE / CONTEXT CHECK

  • Small exact values are known: $W(3)=9$, $W(4)=35$, $W(5)=178$, $W(6)=1132$; for $k=7$ only bounds are known (e.g. $W(7)>3703$).(See the table in [WikiVDW] and the computational proof for $W(2,6)=1132$ in [KourilPaul2008].)

  • Lower bounds: there are explicit and probabilistic constructions giving exponential growth in $k$. A classical application of the Lovász Local Lemma gives $W(k)\ge c\,2^k/k$ for some absolute constant $c>0$ (a proof is included below). Stronger exponential lower bounds $W(k)\gg 2^k$ are known (e.g. work of Kozik--Shabanov, improving constants in the exponential base).(See [KS2016].) Berlekamp proved that for a prime $p$, $W(p+1)\ge p2^p$ (still only exponential in $k$).(See the discussion in [ErdosProblems138] and references therein.)

  • Upper bounds: the best general upper bounds are enormous (tower--type); Gowers obtained a very large explicit tower bound in his quantitative proof of Szemerédi's theorem.(See [Gowers2001] and summaries such as [ErdosProblems138,WikiVDW].)

  • The specific target $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ is much stronger than any currently known lower bound and remains open.

3) ATTACK PLAN

To prove $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ one needs super--exponential lower bounds on $W(k)$. Known methods (random coloring + Local Lemma, algebraic constructions such as Berlekamp's for $k=p+1$, and computer--assisted "certificates") only give $\exp(\Theta(k))$ growth.

A plausible route would be to construct, for each large $k$, a $2$--coloring of $[N]$ with no monochromatic $k$--AP for some $N$ of size, say, $\exp(k\log k)$ (or larger). Concretely:

  1. Probabilistic approach: strengthen Local Lemma--type arguments using sharper dependency structure or advanced hypergraph container/nibble constructions.

  2. Algebraic/recursive constructions: build long colorings by concatenation/product constructions that provably destroy long monochromatic APs.

  3. Disproof attempt: show instead that $W(k)\le C^k$ for some fixed $C$ (which would refute $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$). No such bound is known; existing upper bounds are far larger.

In the "work" below I carry out (i) sanity checks on tiny cases, and (ii) the standard Local Lemma lower bound to locate precisely where current methods stall.

4) WORK

4.1 Tiny-case reality check: $W(3)=9$

A concrete $2$--coloring of $[8]$ with no monochromatic $3$--term arithmetic progression is \[ \text{red }\{1,2,5,6\},\qquad\text{blue }\{3,4,7,8\}, \] (i.e. the pattern RRBBRRBB). One checks directly that every $3$--AP in $[8]$ meets both colors.

For $N=9$, an exhaustive enumeration over all $2^9=512$ colorings confirms that every coloring of $[9]$ contains a monochromatic $3$--AP (this is the standard fact $W(3)=9$).

4.2 A fully proved exponential lower bound via the Lovász Local Lemma

The following is classical; I include a complete proof.

Proposition (Local Lemma bound): Fix $k\ge 3$ and let \[ N \le \frac{(k-1)\,2^{k-1}}{e\,k^2}. \] Then there exists a $2$--coloring of $[N]$ with no monochromatic $k$--term arithmetic progression. Consequently, \[ W(k) > \frac{(k-1)\,2^{k-1}}{e\,k^2}, \] and in particular $W(k)\ge c\,2^k/k$ for some absolute constant $c>0$.
Proof: Color each $x\in[N]$ independently red/blue with probability $1/2$. For each $k$--term arithmetic progression $P=\{a,a+d,\dots,a+(k-1)d\}\subseteq[N]$, let $E_P$ be the "bad" event that $P$ is monochromatic. Then \[ \mathbb{P}(E_P)=2\cdot (1/2)^k = 2^{1-k}=:p. \]

We use the symmetric Lovász Local Lemma. Two events $E_P,E_Q$ are independent unless the progressions $P$ and $Q$ share at least one element. So we need an upper bound on how many $k$--APs in $[N]$ can intersect a fixed $k$--AP.

Claim. Each integer $x\in[N]$ lies in at most \[ T := k\,\lfloor\frac{N-1}{k-1}\rfloor \] many $k$--term arithmetic progressions in $[N]$.

Proof of claim. Any $k$--AP in $[N]$ has common difference $d$ satisfying $1\le d\le \lfloor (N-1)/(k-1)\rfloor$ (since $a+(k-1)d\le N$). Fix such a $d$. If $x$ is the $j$th term of the progression ($j\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}$), then the start is forced to be $a=x-jd$. Thus for each $d$ there are at most $k$ progressions containing $x$ (one for each possible $j$), and there are at most $\lfloor (N-1)/(k-1)\rfloor$ possible values of $d$. Multiplying gives the claim. $\triangleleft$

Now fix a progression $P$. It has $k$ elements, and for each element $x\in P$ there are at most $T$ progressions containing $x$. Therefore the number of progressions $Q$ with $Q\cap P\ne\emptyset$ is at most $kT$ (this overcounts but is an upper bound). Hence each event $E_P$ depends on at most \[ D := kT-1 \] other events. In particular, \[ D+1 \le kT = k^2\lfloor\frac{N-1}{k-1}\rfloor \le \frac{k^2 N}{k-1}. \]

The symmetric Lovász Local Lemma states that if \[ e\,p\,(D+1) \le 1, \] then $\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_P \overline{E_P})>0$, i.e. a coloring exists with no bad events. A sufficient condition is \[ e\cdot 2^{1-k}\cdot \frac{k^2 N}{k-1} \le 1, \] which is exactly $N\le \frac{(k-1)2^{k-1}}{e k^2}$. Thus such a coloring exists, implying $W(k)>N$. ∎

Remark: Proposition Proposition (Local Lemma bound) only yields $\log W(k)=\Omega(k)$, hence $W(k)^{1/k}$ stays bounded (indeed the lower bound gives $W(k)^{1/k}\gtrsim 2$). This illustrates the size of the gap to the desired $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$, which would require $\log W(k)/k\to\infty$.

5) VERIFICATION / EDGE CASES

  • The dependency bound in Proposition Proposition (Local Lemma bound) is intentionally crude but valid: counting progressions through each point and summing over the $k$ points of $P$ yields an upper bound on intersecting progressions.

  • For $k=1,2$ the definition of $W(k)$ is trivial; Proposition Proposition (Local Lemma bound) assumes $k\ge 3$ so that the "$k$--AP" events are genuinely constrained.

  • The tiny-case verification $W(3)=9$ was checked both by an explicit $[8]$ coloring and by exhaustive enumeration on $[9]$.

6) FINAL

UNRESOLVED

  1. Farthest point reached. I proved the standard Lovász Local Lemma lower bound $W(k)\ge c\,2^k/k$ (Proposition Proposition (Local Lemma bound)) and sanity-checked $W(3)=9$.

  2. Best partial lemma. Proposition Proposition (Local Lemma bound): existence of $2$--colorings of length $\asymp 2^k/k$ with no monochromatic $k$--AP.

  3. Smallest missing step. To prove $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ one needs a qualitatively new lower bound giving $\log W(k)\gg k\log k$ (or stronger), far beyond Local Lemma/exponential constructions.

  4. Completion estimate. The current best general lower bounds are exponential in $k$; obtaining super--exponential growth appears to require new structural/algebraic constructions or breakthroughs in quantitative Ramsey/additive combinatorics.
