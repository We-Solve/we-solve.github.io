Research attempt: A quantitative local certificate and the missing global explanation

Let $F$ be continuously differentiable on the closed ball $B(z_0,r)$ in a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space, and let $A$ be an invertible linear map. Suppose \[ \beta=\|AF(z_0)\|,\quad \sup_{z\in B(z_0,r)}\|I-ADF(z)\|\le\kappa<1, \quad \beta\le(1-\kappa)r. \] For $T(z)=z-AF(z)$, integrating its derivative along a straight segment gives $\|T(z)-T(w)\|\le\kappa\|z-w\|$. Also $\|T(z)-z_0\|\le\kappa r+\beta\le r$. Starting from $z_0$, the iterates stay in the ball; consecutive differences shrink geometrically, so the sequence is Cauchy. Its limit $z_*$ belongs to the closed ball and satisfies $T(z_*)=z_*$ by continuity, hence $F(z_*)=0$ because $A$ is invertible. Two fixed points would have distance at most $\kappa$ times their distance, so the zero in the ball is unique. This proves the certificate in full.

As a concrete complex calculation take $F(z)=z^2-z+1$, $z_0=1/2+7i/8$, $r=1/64$, and $A=(2z_0-1)^{-1}=-4i/7$. Then $F(z_0)=-1/64$, $\beta=1/112$, and \[ |1-AF'(z)|=2|A|\,|z-z_0|\le1/56=\kappa. \] The self-map inequality is $1/112\le55/3584$, which is true. The disk lies strictly in the upper half-plane since $7/8-1/64>0$; it therefore certifies a positive-imaginary root. This is an exact rational certificate for a shape-type polynomial, not a check of all equations of any claimed manifold.

The failed shortcut would infer certification from a small numerical residual alone. For $F_\varepsilon(z)=\varepsilon(z-1)$ and $z_0=0$, the residual tends to zero with $\varepsilon$, while the zero remains at distance one. The inverse derivative has norm $1/\varepsilon$, and $\beta=1$ correctly exposes the problem. A residual tolerance that ignores inverse-Jacobian scale is therefore not a proof.

Applied to a selected square gluing system, the certificate still requires proof that the selected equations imply the remaining edge and peripheral conditions, that the ball stays in the appropriate shape domain, and that the geometric interpretation follows. Explaining practice additionally requires a reason why the numerical search finds such well-conditioned balls on a specified input class. None of these global or statistical facts follows from the local contraction theorem.
