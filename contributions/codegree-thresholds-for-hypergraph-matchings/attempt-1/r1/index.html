Codegree Thresholds for Hypergraph Matchings

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A $k$-uniform hypergraph has edges that are $k$-element vertex subsets. A matching is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges. Its minimum $(k-1)$-codegree is the minimum number of edges containing a given $(k-1)$-subset.

A graph matching is a set of edges with pairwise disjoint endpoints, and it is perfect when every vertex is incident to one chosen edge. A weighted matching objective sums the chosen edge weights, unless an ordered objective is specified.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
For $n\geq k\geq2$, let $t_k(n)$ be the least $t$ such that every $n$-vertex $k$-uniform hypergraph $H$ with $\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq t$ has a matching of size $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$. If $k\nmid n$, is $t_k(n)=\lfloor n/k\rfloor$? [S2]

Short English statement

Determine the exact minimum-codegree threshold guaranteeing a largest possible matching when vertex count is not divisible by edge size.

The sharp space barrier and the complete graph case

Let $n\ge k\ge2$ with $k\nmid n$, and put $q=\lfloor n/k\rfloor$. The source conjectures minimum codegree $q$ suffices for a matching of size $q$. Later primary work by Han states this conclusion for each fixed $k\ge3$ and sufficiently large $n$; that external theorem is not reproved here and is not our new progress. We test the lower threshold and give a full proof of the elementary $k=2$ case.
Proposition: There is a $k$-uniform hypergraph with minimum codegree $q-1$ and largest matching size $q-1$. For $k=2$, every graph on $2q+1$ vertices with minimum degree at least $q$ has a matching of size $q$.
Proof: For the first assertion choose a set $A$ of $q-1$ vertices and take as edges all $k$-sets meeting $A$. A $(k-1)$-set disjoint from $A$ has exactly $q-1$ extensions to an edge. Such sets exist because the complement is large enough. A $(k-1)$-set meeting $A$ has every one of its $n-k+1$ possible extensions, and $n-k+1\ge q-1$. Thus the minimum codegree is $q-1$. Every matching uses a distinct vertex of $A$ per edge, so has size at most $q-1$. This size is attained by pairing each vertex of $A$ with its own $k-1$ distinct vertices of the complement; there are enough because $n-(q-1)\ge(k-1)(q-1)$. If $A$ is empty, the matching and edge set are both empty and the same conclusion holds. For the graph assertion take a maximum matching of size $j$. If $j<q$, at least two vertices $u,v$ are unmatched. They have no unmatched neighbors, since an edge between unmatched vertices would enlarge the matching. For any matched edge $ab$, there are at most two edges from $\{u,v\}$ to $\{a,b\}$: three or four would include either $ua,vb$ or $ub,va$, permitting replacement of $ab$ by two disjoint edges. Hence $\deg u+\deg v\le2j<2q$, contradicting the minimum-degree assumption. Therefore $j=q$. ∎
