Research attempt: Balanced rescaling, finite atomic cancellation, and a gradient obstruction

What positive homogeneity proves exactly.
Let $f(x)=\sum_j a_j\sigma(v_j^Tx)$, $\sigma(t)=\max(t,0)$. For any $c>0$, replacing $(a,V)$ by $(a/c,cV)$ preserves $f$. Consequently \[ \inf_{c>0}\frac12(c^{-2}\|a\|_2^2+c^2\|V\|_F^2) =\|a\|_2\|V\|_F. \] For positive norms, subtracting the right side leaves half the square $(\|a\|_2/c-c\|V\|_F)^2$, with equality at $c^2=\|a\|_2/\|V\|_F$. If either norm is zero, the infimum is zero by a limit and the function is zero, although the infimum need not be attained by a finite $c$. Allowing separate positive rescalings for each neuron gives instead \[ \inf_{c_j>0}\frac12\sum_j(a_j^2/c_j^2+c_j^2\|v_j\|_2^2) =\sum_j |a_j|\|v_j\|_2 \leq\|a\|_2\|V\|_F, \] where the last inequality follows by expanding the nonnegative square norm in the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.
Cancellation within a specified finite dictionary.
Drop zero rows and write $v_j=r_j u_j$, with $r_j>0$ and $\|u_j\|=1$. For each distinct direction $u$, combine coefficients $c_u=\sum_{j:u_j=u}a_jr_j$. This preserves the function and gives the cancellation-sensitive quantity \[ B=\sum_u|c_u|\leq\sum_j|a_j|r_j. \] Since ReLU is one-Lipschitz and vanishes at zero, $|f(x)|\leq B\|x\|$ and $|f(x)-f(y)|\leq B\|x-y\|$. For a fixed dictionary of $K\geq1$ unit directions and sample points $\|x_i\|\leq R$, $1\leq i\leq N$, the exact empirical signed-sum supremum for the coefficient ball is \[ \mathbb E_\epsilon\sup_{\|c\|_1\leq B} \frac1N\sum_i\epsilon_i\sum_{j=1}^Kc_j\sigma(u_j^Tx_i) =B\,\mathbb E_\epsilon\max_j\left|\frac1N\sum_i \epsilon_i\sigma(u_j^Tx_i)\right| \leq BR\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2K)}N}. \] Here $\epsilon_i$ are independent uniform signs and $N\geq1$. For completeness, $\mathbb E e^{t\epsilon z}=\cosh(tz)\leq e^{t^2z^2/2}$: the series inequality follows from $(2r)!\geq2^r r!$ term by term. Each signed average consequently has moment generating function at most $e^{t^2R^2/(2N)}$. Bound the exponential of the maximum over its $2K$ signed versions by their sum, take logarithms and Jensen's inequality, and minimize $\log(2K)/t+tR^2/(2N)$ over $t>0$; $R=0$ is immediate. This is a proved empirical process bound for a fixed finite dictionary, not an unproved population test-error bound for adaptively learned directions.
Why cancellation alone cannot replace parameter information in GD.
Use a single input $x=1$, target $1$, and two active neurons with $a_1=K,a_2=-K,v_1=v_2=1$, where $K>0$. The represented function is identically zero, so its combined coefficient norm $B$ is zero. For loss $\frac12(f(1)-1)^2$, one gradient step of size $\eta>0$ gives \[ a_1^+=K+\eta,\quad a_2^+=-K+\eta, \qquad v_1^+=1+\eta K,\quad v_2^+=1-\eta K. \] If $\eta K<1$, both neurons remain active and direct multiplication yields $f^+(1)=2\eta(1+K^2)$. Equivalently its infinitesimal change at this parameterization is $2(1+K^2)$, unbounded while the initial represented function and cancellation norm are fixed at zero. Thus a gradient-step or parameter-sensitivity estimate based only on that function norm cannot hold uniformly for ordinary Euclidean parameter gradient descent. A function-complexity quantity and the metric of parameter optimization encode different information.

Verification and adversarial audit

Zero rows and zero norms are treated before normalization. Rescalings are positive, as ReLU is not homogeneous for negative scaling. The dictionary bound fixes its directions before the Rademacher signs and does not exchange an adaptively chosen infinite dictionary with a finite $K$. The GD example states $\eta K<1$ so all derivatives and output calculations stay in a single smooth activation region.

Audit corrections.
Distinguished the global product rescaling from per-neuron variation and from cancellation. Did not promote the empirical signed-sum bound to a general risk theorem; the explicit GD counterexample limits the proposed substitution.

Scope and remaining gaps

The exact rescaling identities and finite-dictionary bound support restricted uses of a cancellation quantity. They do not prove that one quantity can replace the source parameter norm simultaneously in arbitrary GD and population-risk theorems. The displayed gradient obstruction identifies an additional metric or parameterization assumption that such a theorem would require.
