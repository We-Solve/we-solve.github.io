Separatrix Splitting under Quasiperiodic Forcing

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.

Fix $J,g>0$ and even zero-average trigonometric polynomials $f$ on $(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$ and $m$ on $\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$. For actions $A\in\mathbb R^2,I\in\mathbb R$ and angles $\alpha\in(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2,\varphi\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$, set \[H=\omega(\eta)\cdot A+\frac{I^2}{2J}+Jg^2(\cos\varphi-1)+\varepsilon f(\alpha)(\cos\varphi-1)+\varepsilon^{\prime}m(2\alpha_2)(\cos\varphi-1),\] where $\omega(\eta)=(\eta^{1/2}\overline\omega_1,\eta^{-1/2}\overline\omega_2)$. Let $\eta_n\downarrow0$ satisfy $|\omega(\eta_n)\cdot\nu|>\eta_n^a|\nu|^{-\tau}$ for every $0\ne\nu\in\mathbb Z^2$, with fixed $a,\tau>0$. The invariant torus has $A=0,I=0,\varphi=0$. On the section $H=0,\varphi=\pi$, write its unstable and stable manifolds as graphs $A^u(\alpha),A^s(\alpha)$ when these exist and put $\Delta(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta)=\det[\partial_\alpha(A^u-A^s)]_{\alpha=0}$. The evenness gives the indicated symmetric homoclinic point.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
With $\varepsilon=\eta^c$ for a fixed sufficiently large $c>0$, determine the asymptotic splitting $\Delta(\eta^c,\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta)$ as $\eta=\eta_n\to0$ when $\varepsilon^{\prime}=O(1)$, or at least obtain a positive lower bound for its absolute value in the nondegenerate cases. This includes understanding the transition in the splitting asymptotics when $\varepsilon^{\prime}=\eta^T$ crosses a critical exponent $T$, despite convergence of the perturbation series throughout this larger parameter region. [S2]

Short English statement

Find the exponentially small separatrix splitting when one quasiperiodic forcing component is small and the fast component remains of bounded size, including the change in asymptotics inside the perturbation series convergence region.

A first-order calculation and its limitation.
The question concerns a bounded fast forcing, so expansion at zero in both couplings is only a diagnostic. For the unperturbed positive separatrix choose $\varphi_0(t)=4\arctan e^{gt}$; then $\cos\varphi_0-1=-2\operatorname{sech}^2(gt)$. Write $f(\alpha)=\sum_k f_k\cos(k\cdot\alpha)$, taking one representative of each opposite Fourier pair, and similarly $m(2\alpha_2)=\sum_l m_l\cos(2l\alpha_2)$. The integral of the perturbing potential along this orbit is \[ L(\alpha)=-2\varepsilon\sum_k f_k W(k\cdot\omega)\cos(k\cdot\alpha)-2\varepsilon'\sum_l m_l W(2l\omega_2)\cos(2l\alpha_2),\quad W(v)=\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{sech}^2(gt)\cos(vt)\,dt. \] All sums are finite, and exponential decay permits differentiation. Thus its Hessian at zero is the real symmetric matrix $M=2\varepsilon\sum_k f_kW(k\cdot\omega)kk^T+8\varepsilon'\sum_l l^2m_lW(2l\omega_2)e_2e_2^T$, where $e_2=(0,1)^T$. If the first term is $\varepsilon C$ and the second is $\varepsilon'De_2e_2^T$, elementary expansion gives \[\det M=\varepsilon^2\det C+\varepsilon\varepsilon'D C_{11}.\] This identifies precisely the two competing terms in this linear-response determinant; it does not identify the determinant of the true splitting for order-one $\varepsilon'$.
Countercheck and failed extension.
If every Fourier vector of $f$ is parallel to $e_2$, then the full Hamiltonian is independent of $\alpha_1$. Hamilton's equation gives $\dot A_1=0$; both invariant-manifold graphs have $A_1=0$, and their splitting determinant vanishes identically whenever those graphs exist. Thus the nondegeneracy proviso cannot be dropped. Even when $C_{11}\ne0$, cancellation between the two displayed determinant terms is possible. Bounding a higher-order error merely by $O(\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon'^2)$ is useless against an exponentially small determinant. A uniform error smaller than the actual candidate leading term along the stated Diophantine sequence, with bounded fast forcing, is the first missing step. The source's critical-exponent transition and a positive lower bound remain unresolved; this is a first-order diagnostic, not a new splitting theorem.
