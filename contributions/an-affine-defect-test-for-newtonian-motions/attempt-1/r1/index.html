An affine-defect test for Newtonian motions

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and notation.
In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. A central configuration has $\nabla_{q_i}U=-\lambda m_iq_i$ in center-of-mass coordinates for a common scalar $\lambda$. After fixing $I=1$, it is a critical point of $U$ on the collision-free inertia ellipsoid. Congruent configurations and scales are factored out for a finiteness count. A relative equilibrium is a rigidly rotating solution with time-independent shape. Two configurations are affinely related if a common affine map sends every labeled position of one to the corresponding position of the other. After removing translations, this concerns common linear maps. An orbit remaining in one affine class is not necessarily rigidly rotating; the source’s symmetric examples motivate looking for additional motion types.

An affine-dependence obstruction.
Let fixed reference points $a_i$ span an affine d-plane and suppose $q_i(t)=A(t)a_i+b(t)$, with $A$ invertible. Every affine relation $\sum_ic_i=0$, $\sum_ic_ia_i=0$ then gives $\sum_ic_iq_i(t)=0$, and differentiating twice yields the necessary force relation \[\sum_i c_i\sum_{j\ne i}m_j\frac{q_j-q_i}{|q_j-q_i|^3}=0. \] This is a concrete test for a proposed fixed-affine-class motion: the accelerations must preserve every affine dependency, not merely the positions at one time.
A dimension boundary.
For $n=d+1$ affinely independent labeled points there are no nontrivial affine dependencies. Any two such configurations are related by a unique invertible affine map, obtained by mapping the d difference-basis vectors. Thus any collision-free solution that stays affinely independent on a time interval automatically lies in one affine class there. The constraint becomes substantive only when there are additional points or global rank requirements. For example, three planar unit masses starting at $(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)$ with zero velocities remain noncollinear for sufficiently short time by continuity. The first body's acceleration is $(1/4,1)$; relative to the center of mass $(2/3,1/3)$, its two proportionality ratios are unequal, so this initial acceleration is not a common scalar homothety. Zero initial velocities and nonzero accelerations also exclude a relative equilibrium. This is only a local example, and its time-reversal symmetry is not hidden.
