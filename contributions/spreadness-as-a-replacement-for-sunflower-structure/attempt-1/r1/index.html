Spreadness as a Replacement for Sunflower Structure

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

A sunflower is a family of sets whose pairwise intersections all equal the same core. Spreadness bounds the fraction of the family containing each prescribed subset, with the bound depending on its size. Robust variants allow the required petals to appear with a stated probability.

A second-moment route for a precisely defined spread model

The workshop discusses several sunflower variants and applications. Rather than silently repair the direction of containment in an extracted robustness definition, we specify our own auxiliary random-containment experiment. Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty family of $k$-element subsets of a finite universe, $k\ge1$. Suppose a uniform $B\in\mathcal F$ satisfies \[ \Pr(S\subseteq B)\le r^{-|S|}\quad\hbox{for every subset }S, \] where $r>0$. Let $R$ include each universe element independently with probability $0<p\le1$, and let $Z$ count members of $\mathcal F$ contained in $R$.
Proposition: The containment probability obeys \[ \Pr(Z>0)\ge\left(1+\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^{-k} \ge 1-\frac{k(1-p)}{pr}. \]
Proof: The mean is $|\mathcal F|p^k$, and independence gives \[ \frac{\mathbb EZ^2}{(\mathbb EZ)^2} =\mathbb E_{A,B\in\mathcal F}p^{-|A\cap B|}. \] Fix $A$. Expand the product over its elements: \[ \mathbb E_B p^{-|A\cap B|} =\sum_{S\subseteq A}(p^{-1}-1)^{|S|}\Pr(S\subseteq B) \le\sum_{S\subseteq A}\left(\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^{|S|} =\left(1+\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^k. \] All terms are nonnegative. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $Z\mathbf1_{Z>0}$ gives $(\mathbb EZ)^2\le\mathbb EZ^2\Pr(Z>0)$, proving the first bound. For $u\ge0$, the derivative of $(1+u)^{-k}-(1-ku)$ is $k[1-(1+u)^{-k-1}]\ge0$, proving the second. ∎
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, the elementary sufficient condition $pr\ge k/\varepsilon$ therefore gives failure probability at most $\varepsilon$. This is a weak spread-to-containment bound, not an improvement of modern sunflower thresholds. For example, all two-element subsets of a six-element universe satisfy the spread condition with $r=3$. At $p=1/2$, the first bound is $9/16$, while the exact containment probability is $57/64$. The attempted route toward sharper applications stops at the product bound: it uses only the separate upper bounds for each intersection event, discarding dependencies that stronger sunflower arguments exploit. Moreover, obtaining a suitably spread subfamily or core from an arbitrary family has not been proved here. The displayed estimate alone does not supply that extraction step.

Verification and remaining gaps
