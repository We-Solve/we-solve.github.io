Research attempt: Exact expressiveness versus approximation and trainability

An exact representation boundary.
For $d\ge2$, the function $f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^d x_j$ on $[0,1]^d$ is computed by a binary tree of $d-1$ product operations, with balanced depth $\lceil\log_2d\rceil$ up to padding. It is therefore a natural elementary product-pooling computation. A finite network built only from affine maps, ReLU, maximum and average pooling is piecewise affine with finitely many pieces: affine maps preserve this property, a ReLU or maximum refines finitely many affine regions by finitely many linear inequalities, and compositions remain finite. On the diagonal $x_1=\cdots=x_d=t$, its restriction is piecewise affine on finitely many intervals. It cannot equal $t^d$ on $(0,1)$, whose second derivative is $d(d-1)t^{d-2}>0$. Thus exact representation separates these operations on this continuum domain.
That exact separation is not an approximation lower bound.
Interpolate $t^2$ linearly on the uniform grid $0,h,\ldots,mh=2$, $h=2/m$. On a grid interval $[a,b]$, the interpolation error is exactly $(t-a)(b-t)$, between zero and $h^2/4=1/m^2$. The interpolant has the ReLU representation \[ q_m(t)=h t+2h\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\max(0,t-jh),\qquad 0\le t\le2. \] The slope starts at $h$ and increases by $2h$ at each knot, agreeing with the secant slopes of the square.

For $x,y\in[0,1]$, use $xy=((x+y)^2-x^2-y^2)/2$ and define \[ M_m(x,y)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]} \left(\frac{q_m(x+y)-q_m(x)-q_m(y)}2\right). \] Clipping is itself $\max(0,z)-\max(0,z-1)$ and cannot increase the distance to a target in $[0,1]$. Therefore $|M_m(x,y)-xy|\le3/(2m^2)$. Compose these modules on the same binary tree. If child approximations have errors $e_1,e_2$ and all exact and approximate values lie in $[0,1]$, then \[ |ab-\widetilde a\widetilde b| \le|a-\widetilde a|+|b-\widetilde b|, \] so the root error is at most $(d-1)3/(2m^2)$ by induction over the tree. Choosing $m\ge\sqrt{3(d-1)/(2\epsilon)}$ yields uniform error at most $\epsilon$, with $O(dm)$ ReLU units and depth $O(\log d)$. The multiplication module can share coefficients between nodes. This is a constructive general ReLU approximation; it is not asserted to obey every exact channel, filter and pooling restriction of the source's comparison architecture. It does prove that the bare piecewise-affine argument alone cannot establish a superpolynomial approximate-representation gap.

Expressibility alone does not make gradient training fast.
Even this elementary arithmetic computation can have flat training dynamics. Put one shared scalar parameter $w$ at all $d$ leaves, so the output at the all-ones input is $w^d$, and train on target one with \[ L(w)=\tfrac12(w^d-1)^2,\qquad L'(w)=d w^{d-1}(w^d-1). \] The global loss is zero at $w=1$, but gradient descent started at $w=0$ never moves for $d\ge2$. More quantitatively, with a fixed step size $\eta>0$ and start $w_0=1/4$, before the first iterate reaching $1/2$ every increment is positive and at most $\eta d\,2^{-(d-1)}$. Thus the number of updates needed to reach $1/2$, if it is ever reached, is at least \[ \frac{1/4}{\eta d\,2^{-(d-1)}}=\frac{2^{d-3}}{\eta d}. \] The bound counts all pre-crossing updates, including the final one, so it remains valid if that step overshoots. For a step size bounded independently of $d$, this is exponentially many iterations in this parameterization. It is not a lower bound for all optimizers, initializations or reparameterizations; it identifies a concrete failure of the inference from a short product circuit to efficient gradient training.
