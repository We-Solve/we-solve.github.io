OpenAI's claim

Manuscript 1 (family 039, 2026-10-05): In every dimension at least three, the ordinary multipoint Seshadri constant is claimed to reach $(L^n/r)^{1/n}$ at very general points for every sufficiently large $r$. Manuscript 2 (family 039, 2026-09-23): For any polarized smooth complex projective surface, the equal-weight multipoint Seshadri constant is claimed to reach $\sqrt{L^2/r}$ for all integers beyond a polarization-dependent threshold.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
Let $X$ be smooth integral projective over $\mathbb C$, with $n=\dim X\ge3$, and $L$ ample. OpenAI claims an integer $r_0(X,L)$ such that for every $r\ge r_0$ and very general ordered distinct $p_1,\ldots,p_r$, \[ \varepsilon(X,L;\mathbf p)=\left(\frac{L^n}{r}\right)^{1/n}. \] The multipoint Seshadri constant is the supremum of $t\ge0$ for which $\pi^*L-t\sum_iE_i$ is nef on the blow-up at these points. For each $r$, the exceptional tuples lie in a union, indexed by a countable set, of proper algebraic closed subsets with nonempty complement. The threshold depends on $(X,L)$ and is not specified uniformly.
Mathematical claim 2.
For any smooth integral projective complex surface $S$ and ample line bundle $L$, OpenAI claims $r_0(S,L)$ such that every $r\ge r_0$ and very general tuple of distinct points give, on the blow-up $\pi$ with exceptional curves $E_i$, \[ \pi^*L-\sqrt{L^2/r}\sum_{i=1}^rE_i\text{ nef},\qquad \varepsilon(S,L;\mathbf p)=\sqrt{L^2/r}. \] The constant is the supremum of $t$ making $\pi^*L-t\sum E_i$ nef. Very general excludes countably many proper closed subsets of the distinct-point configuration space; the statement is for sufficiently many points, not every prescribed small $r$.

Outline of the argument

Manuscript 1 (family 039, 2026-10-05): Graded exponent supports are reshaped into a volume-preserving simplex compatible with section multiplication. Full density forces every sufficiently deep interior lattice point to occur, allowing torus interpolation to separate the needed jets; very-general tuples inherit all the finite tests.

Manuscript 2 (family 039, 2026-09-23): Finite jet tests are transferred to an algebraic torus through nodal-curve degeneration. A varying exponent region is tuned to a primitive lattice direction and a subgroup with exactly $r$ points, giving injectivity for all required orders rather than only a fixed multiplicity.

Formal verification

For family 039, the scope page documents the following selections. The selected plane-curve inequality allows unequal multiplicities and repeated components at every allowed point count. A separate statement gives eventual maximality on each polarized complex surface, with a surface-dependent threshold; the higher-dimensional and positive-characteristic companions are not selected here. Manuscript 1 is not among the papers selected by that page, so the family link does not formalize its main conclusion. The documented statement covers the main claim of manuscript 2.
