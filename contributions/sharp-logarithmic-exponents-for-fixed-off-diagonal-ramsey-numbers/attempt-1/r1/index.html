Sharp logarithmic exponents for fixed off-diagonal Ramsey numbers

OpenAI's claim

Ramsey logarithms for clique size at least six: For every fixed $s\ge6$, the manuscript claims $r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}$; the separate parameter $s=5$ is treated by another manuscript.

The logarithmic exponent of r$5,t$: The companion manuscript claims $r(5,t)=t^4/(\log t)^{3+o(1)}$; the lower estimate allows any arbitrarily small extra logarithmic power, while the matching upper estimate has power three.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Mathematical claim 1.
For every fixed integer $s\ge6$, the claimed bounds on the Ramsey number $r(s,t)$ are \[\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2+\epsilon}}\le r(s,t)\le C_s\frac{t^{s-1}}{(\log t)^{s-2}}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ t\ge t_0(s,\epsilon)).\] Here $r(s,t)$ is the least order forcing a clique of size $s$ or an independent set of size $t$. In particular $((s-1)\log t-\log r(s,t))/\log\log t\to s-2$. This paper covers $s\ge6$.
Mathematical claim 2.
The complementary fixed-clique-size result claims an absolute $C$ with \[\frac{t^4}{(\log t)^{3+\epsilon}}\le r(5,t)\le C\frac{t^4}{(\log t)^3}\quad(\epsilon>0,\ t\ge t_0(\epsilon)).\] Therefore $(4\log t-\log r(5,t))/\log\log t\to3$. Together with the separately cited $s\ge6$ manuscript, this covers every fixed $s\ge5$.

Outline of the argument

Ramsey logarithms for clique size at least six: Random incident point--hyperplane flags define an ordered graph in projective dimension $s-1$. Linear independence forbids the target clique, while independent sequences satisfy an asymmetric consistency rule. Sparse-incidence descriptions, validation samples, and dimensional projection give short encodings of such sequences, contradicting the entropy needed for a long independent set.

The logarithmic exponent of r$5,t$: Random flags in projective four-space produce a graph without five-cliques. Two marking scans and a small set of representative flags bound the information in a purported long consistent sequence. Geometric incidence analysis controls exceptional subspace rectangles, causing its upper entropy bound to conflict with the entropy forced by occurrence in the original random stream.
