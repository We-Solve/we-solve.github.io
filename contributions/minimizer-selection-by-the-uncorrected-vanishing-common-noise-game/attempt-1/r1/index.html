A stochastic action certificate for the uncorrected selection problem

Problem C73-25. Status: unresolved. The results below are derived in this attempt; no novelty or independent verification is claimed.

Target and restriction

The target concerns the exact finite-state common-noise master equation with the original potential costs unchanged. The corrected-cost minimizing-selection theorem does not answer it [CD, Section 4.3]. The primary paper explicitly introduces a correction to restore potential structure [CeD, Sections 2 and 4]. We derive that structural defect directly and prove a conditional minimizing-selection theorem with a specified boundary layer and an explicit missing feedback estimate.

Fix $d\ge3$, $T>0$, $m_0\in\operatorname{int}\Delta_d$, and smooth potentials $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$. Write $f_i=\partial_i\mathcal F$, $g_i=\partial_i\mathcal G$, $H_i(p)=\frac12\sum_{j\ne i}(p_i-p_j)_+^2$, and \[ b_i(m,p)=\sum_{j\ne i}[m_j(p_j-p_i)_+-m_i(p_i-p_j)_+],\quad A_{jk}(m)=m_j\delta_{jk}-m_jm_k. \] For a rate matrix $a$, $b_i(m,a)=\sum_{j\ne i}(m_ja_{ji}-m_i a_{ij})$ instead denotes its linear population drift. The deterministic action is \[ J(m,a)=\int_0^T\left[\frac12\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i a_{ij}^2+\mathcal F(m)\right]{\mathrm d} t+\mathcal G(m(T)). \] Its admissible paths are absolutely continuous, start from $m_0$, obey $\dot m=b(m,a)$, and have finite weighted quadratic rate energy.

For clarity, the exact uncorrected regularized equation used below is \begin{align*} 0={}&\partial_tU_i+\varepsilon A:D^2U_i +(b(m,U)+r^\varepsilon(m))\cdot DU_i-H_i(U)+f_i(m)\\ &+2\varepsilon\sum_jm_j(\partial_iU_i-\partial_jU_i) +\sum_{j\ne i}\phi_\varepsilon(m_j)(U_j-U_i), \qquad U_i(T,m)=g_i(m), \end{align*} where $r_i^\varepsilon=\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)-m_i\sum_j\phi_\varepsilon(m_j)$. An admissible solution is continuous on the closed time--simplex cylinder, $C^1$ in time and $C^2$ in the interior, with bounded values and first derivatives for each fixed $\varepsilon$, and locally bounded second derivatives. Admissibility also requires the selected population SDE, with drift $b(m,U^\varepsilon)+r^\varepsilon$ and covariance $2\varepsilon A(m){\mathrm d} t$, to have a unique interior-valued solution. These are assumptions on the regularization, not existence claims proved here.

The precise diffusion mismatch

Proposition: Work in an interior region where the boundary repulsion vanishes. Suppose a smooth scalar function $V$ solves the stochastic planner equation \[ 0=V_t+\varepsilon\sum_{j,k}A_{jk}V_{jk}-\sum_jm_jH_j(DV)+\mathcal F(m). \] Let $U_i=\partial_iV$ for a fixed smooth extension. The component differences of the residual in the uncorrected game master equation are \[ R_i-R_d=\varepsilon(V_{ii}-V_{dd}),\quad i<d. \] This residual one-form need not be a gradient. For the polynomial $V(m)=m_1^2m_2$ in $d=3$, the diffusion-mismatch one-form on the chart $(m_1,m_2)$ is $2\varepsilon m_2{\mathrm d} m_1$, whose exterior derivative is $-2\varepsilon{\mathrm d} m_1\wedge{\mathrm d} m_2$.
Proof: Direct differentiation gives \[ \partial_i\left(\varepsilon A:D^2V\right) =\varepsilon A:D^2U_i+ \varepsilon\left(V_{ii}-2\sum_jm_jV_{ij}\right). \] Also $D_p\sum_jm_jH_j(p)=-b(m,p)$, as follows by differentiating the positive-part squares. Thus differentiating the scalar planner equation produces all the deterministic terms of the game equation, and the extra diffusion term just displayed. The game instead contains \[ 2\varepsilon\sum_jm_j(\partial_iU_i-\partial_jU_i) =2\varepsilon\left(V_{ii}-\sum_jm_jV_{ij}\right). \] Their difference is $\varepsilon V_{ii}$. The scalar equation is prescribed on the simplex, so take the tangent derivative $\partial_i-\partial_d$ of that equation; this yields exactly the asserted residual difference. All component representatives here use the fixed extension: an arbitrary population-dependent common shift of $U$ is not being declared a symmetry of the noisy equation. For the polynomial, $V_{11}=2m_2$, $V_{22}=V_{33}=0$; differentiation of the indicated one-form proves its nonzero curl. This polynomial illustrates the operator defect alone; it is not asserted to solve the scalar planner equation for arbitrary prescribed data. ∎

A factor $\varepsilon$ does not make this harmless without uniform Hessian control. Conversely, a nonzero finite-$\varepsilon$ curl does not prove failure of zero-noise minimizing selection. The proposition diagnoses a blocked argument, not a counterexample to the target.

Verification by a smooth deterministic value

Suppose, as an additional hypothesis, that a scalar $V\in C^1_tC^2_m([0,T]\times\Delta_d)$ satisfies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-48-4a-42} V_t-\sum_i m_iH_i(DV)+\mathcal F(m)=0,\qquad V(T,m)=\mathcal G(m), \end{equation} with bounded indicated derivatives. Tangent derivatives, or any fixed smooth extension, give the same formulas below. Set $p=DV$ and \[ \mathcal B(m,a,p)=\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i\left[ \tfrac12a_{ij}^2+a_{ij}(p_j-p_i)+\tfrac12(p_i-p_j)_+^2\right]. \]
Lemma: For deterministic admissible paths, \[ J(m,a)-V(0,m_0)=\int_0^T\mathcal B(m,a,DV){\mathrm d} t\ge0. \] The feedback $a_{ij}=(p_i-p_j)_+$ attains equality, so $V(0,m_0)$ is the global minimum. For a continuous semimartingale population satisfying \[ {\mathrm d} m=b(m,a){\mathrm d} t+r{\mathrm d} t+{\mathrm d} M,\qquad {\mathrm d}\langle M_i,M_j\rangle_t=2\varepsilon A_{ij}(m_t){\mathrm d} t, \] with bounded rates and $m(0)=m_0$, its expected original action obeys the exact identity \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-69-74-6f} {\mathbb{E}} J(m,a)-V(0,m_0) ={\mathbb{E}}\int_0^T\left[\mathcal B(m,a,DV)+DV\cdot r+\varepsilon A:D^2V\right]{\mathrm d} t. \end{equation} For $a_{ij}=(U_i-U_j)_+$ one further has \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-73-71-75-61-72-65} 0\le\mathcal B(m,a,p)\le\frac12\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i\big[(U_i-U_j)-(p_i-p_j)\big]^2. \end{equation}
Proof: The chain rule and (HJB) give the deterministic identity. For $q=p_i-p_j$, $\frac12a^2-aq+\frac12q_+^2=\frac12(a-q_+)^2+a(-q)_+\ge0$, with equality at $a=q_+$. The feedback is bounded and Lipschitz in $m$, since $DV$ is Lipschitz and positive part is Lipschitz. Its forward ODE therefore has a unique simplex-valued solution, yielding an admissible equality case.

For the stochastic identity, apply Itô's formula to $V(t,m_t)$, whose martingale part has zero expectation by boundedness of $DV$ and the quadratic variation. Substitute (HJB) and add the running costs to obtain (ito). Finally put $z=U_i-U_j$, $a=z_+$. If $q,z\ge0$ the scalar gap is $(z-q)^2/2$. If $q,z\le0$ it is zero. If $q\ge0\ge z$, it is $q^2/2\le(z-q)^2/2$. If $z\ge0\ge q$, it is $z^2/2-zq\le(z-q)^2/2$. Multiplying by $m_i$ and summing proves (square). ∎

An explicit boundary-layer removal estimate

Let $\phi_\varepsilon$ be any admissible repulsion family for the exact game satisfying additionally \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6c-61-79-65-72} 0\le\phi_\varepsilon\le\kappa,\qquad \phi_\varepsilon(r)=0\text{ for }r\ge\delta_\varepsilon,\qquad \delta_\varepsilon\downarrow0, \end{equation} where $\kappa$ is fixed. No rate relation between $\delta_\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon$ is imposed beyond the source's admissibility; that admissibility includes existence and interior uniqueness for each $\varepsilon$. The estimate below does not assert admissibility of every cutoff with (layer).
Lemma: Suppose $0\le a_{ij}^\varepsilon\le M_*$ uniformly. The repelled population has \[ r_i^\varepsilon(m)=\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)-m_i\sum_j\phi_\varepsilon(m_j), \] and the common-noise martingale covariance of Lemma Lemma. Let $\mu=\min_i m_{0i}$, $C=(d-1)(M_*+\kappa)$ and $\delta_*=(\mu/2)e^{-CT}$. Then \[ {\mathbb{P}}\left(\min_{t\le T,i}m_i^\varepsilon(t)<\delta_*\right) \le\frac{32d\varepsilon T e^{2CT}}{\mu^2}. \] For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ with $\delta_\varepsilon<\delta_*$, ${\mathbb{E}}\int_0^T\|r^\varepsilon(m_t^\varepsilon)\|_1{\mathrm d} t=O(\varepsilon)$.
Proof: The total drift of coordinate $i$ is at least $-Cm_i$, because all incoming flows are nonnegative and its outgoing rate is at most $(d-1)(M_*+\kappa)$. Thus \[ m_i(t)\ge e^{-Ct}\left[m_{0i}+\int_0^te^{Cs}{\mathrm d} M_i(s)\right]. \] The bracket of $M_i$ is $2\varepsilon m_i(1-m_i){\mathrm d} t\le2\varepsilon{\mathrm d} t$. Doob's inequality gives ${\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\le T}|\int_0^te^{Cs}{\mathrm d} M_i|^2\le8\varepsilon T e^{2CT}$. If these martingale suprema are at most $\mu/2$ for all $i$, every coordinate stays above $\delta_*$. Markov's inequality and a union bound give the claimed probability. On this good event all repulsions vanish for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. On its complement, $\|r\|_1\le2\sum_i\phi_\varepsilon(m_i)\le2d\kappa$. Multiplication by $T$ and the probability bound proves the last assertion. ∎

A conditional selection theorem for unchanged costs

Theorem: Assume (HJB), (layer), and admissibility of the exact uncorrected master solutions $U^\varepsilon$. Suppose their rates $a^\varepsilon_{ij}=(U_i^\varepsilon-U_j^\varepsilon)_+$ are uniformly bounded by $M_*$, and along their population processes \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-6d-69-73-73-69-6e-67} {\mathbb{E}}\int_0^T\sum_{i,j\ne i}m_i^\varepsilon \left[(U_i^\varepsilon-U_j^\varepsilon)(t,m_t^\varepsilon) -(V_i-V_j)(t,m_t^\varepsilon)\right]^2{\mathrm d} t\longrightarrow0. \end{equation} Then the population laws are tight in $C([0,T],\Delta_d)$, and every weak subsequential limit is supported on globally minimizing law paths of the original deterministic action $J$.
Proof: Lemma Lemma removes repulsion in expected $L^1$ time norm. The bounded Hessian in (HJB) gives $|\varepsilon A:D^2V|\le C_V\varepsilon$. Combining (ito), (square), and (missing) therefore gives ${\mathbb{E}} J(m^\varepsilon,a^\varepsilon)\to V(0,m_0)$.

Both original and repulsion drifts are uniformly bounded. The martingale has expected squared supremum $O(\varepsilon)$ in every coordinate. Thus $m^\varepsilon$ is the sum of a uniformly Lipschitz path starting at $m_0$ and a martingale tending to zero uniformly in probability. Arzelà--Ascoli and this approximation give tightness in the continuous-path space.

Here are details for passing the action to the limit without asserting that stochastic paths themselves are deterministic admissible paths. Adjoin the random occupation measures $\Lambda^\varepsilon({\mathrm d} t,{\mathrm d} a)={\mathrm d} t\,\delta_{a^\varepsilon(t)}({\mathrm d} a)$ on the compact set $[0,T]\times[0,M_*]^{d(d-1)}$. They have fixed first marginal ${\mathrm d} t$ and hence form a compact set. Take a joint convergent subsequence with $m^\varepsilon$, and realize it with almost sure uniform convergence of paths and weak convergence of occupation measures. Such a realization is available because both spaces are Polish. Extract a further subsequence so that the vanishing martingale and integrated repulsion also vanish almost surely. Write the limit measure as ${\mathrm d} t\,\pi_t({\mathrm d} a)$ and define $\bar a(t)=\int a\pi_t({\mathrm d} a)$. Passing to the integrated forward equations, using uniform path convergence and the continuity and linearity of $b(m,a)$ in $a$, gives $\dot m=b(m,\bar a)$ and $m(0)=m_0$.

The relaxed kinetic integral converges jointly because its integrand $\frac12\sum m_i(t)a_{ij}^2$ is continuous and bounded on the compact time-control domain after uniform path convergence. The potential terms converge as well. Jensen's inequality gives \[ J(m,\bar a)\le\lim J(m^\varepsilon,a^\varepsilon) \] on this realization. All actions are uniformly bounded, so expectations pass to the limit, giving ${\mathbb{E}} J(m,\bar a)\le V(0,m_0)$. The pair $(m,\bar a)$ is admissible, and Lemma Lemma gives $J(m,\bar a)\ge V(0,m_0)$ for every realization. Equality therefore holds almost surely. Its law paths belong to the original minimizing set, which proves the support statement. ∎

The theorem keeps $f,g$ unchanged and supplies both tightness and a boundary-layer estimate. Its unproved hypotheses are substantial: a globally smooth deterministic value excludes shocks, and (missing) is exactly the rate identification that the uncorrected master equation does not obtain automatically from a potential representation. A bounded-value estimate alone supplies neither. Extending the certificate through singularities or proving (missing) for the full admissible class remains the first gap. No unconditional selection theorem or counterexample for the catalogue target is claimed.
