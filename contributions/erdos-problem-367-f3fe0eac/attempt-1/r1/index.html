Erdős Problem #367: full parts of consecutive integers

1. The two proposed bounds

Define \[ B_r(n)=\prod_{\substack{p^a\Vert n\\a\ge r}}p^a. \] For each fixed $k$, the main question is whether $\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}B_2(n+i)\le n^{2+o(1)}$. The stronger proposal is an $O_k(n^2)$ upper bound. We reconstruct van Doorn's counterexample to the stronger bound, using the explicit algebraic-integer computation given in Tao's discussion. It gives $\gg n^2\log n$ infinitely often for $k=3$. The main $n^{2+o(1)}$ question remains unresolved.

2. Consecutive powerful numbers from a Pell recurrence

Let $\alpha=3+\sqrt8$, so $\alpha^{-1}=3-\sqrt8$. Write \[ \alpha^j=x_j+y_j\sqrt8,\qquad j\ge1. \] Multiplication by $\alpha$ proves inductively that $x_j,y_j$ are positive integers; conjugation gives $x_j^2-8y_j^2=1$. Set $n_j=8y_j^2$. Then \[ n_j+1=x_j^2,\quad B_2(n_j)=n_j,\quad B_2(n_j+1)=n_j+1, \] and direct expansion gives \[ 4(n_j+2)=\alpha^{2j}+6+\alpha^{-2j}. \tag{1} \]

3. Force a growing full divisor of the third integer

In the ring $\mathbb Z[\sqrt8]$, \[ \alpha^{\pm3}=99\pm35\sqrt8 =-1+5(20\pm7\sqrt8). \] If $u=-1+5^t v$ with $t\ge1$, the binomial expansion of $u^5$ shows $u^5\equiv-1\pmod{5^{t+1}}$: the linear term has factor $5^{t+1}$, and every term of degree at least two has at least this divisibility as well. Induction therefore gives \[ \alpha^{\pm3\cdot5^{t-1}}\equiv-1\pmod{5^t}. \] Choose the positive integer \[ j_t=\frac{3\cdot5^{t-1}-1}{2}. \] Substituting $2j_t=3\cdot5^{t-1}-1$ in $1$ yields \[ 4(n_{j_t}+2) \equiv-\alpha^{-1}+6-\alpha=0\pmod{5^t}. \] The congruence is in the quadratic-integer ring, and its left side is an ordinary integer. Divisibility of its integer coefficient, and invertibility of $4$ modulo $5^t$, give $5^t\mid n_{j_t}+2$ in $\mathbb Z$. For $t\ge2$, the prime $5$ therefore occurs in the full part and $B_2(n_{j_t}+2)\ge5^t$.

Also $n_j<\alpha^{2j}$, so \[ \log n_{j_t}<3\cdot5^{t-1}\log\alpha,\qquad 5^t>\frac5{3\log\alpha}\log n_{j_t}. \] As $t\to\infty$ the $n_{j_t}$ are distinct and tend to infinity. Consequently \[ \prod_{i=0}^2B_2(n_{j_t}+i) \ge n_{j_t}(n_{j_t}+1)5^t \gg n_{j_t}^2\log n_{j_t}. \tag{2} \] The first case $t=2$ has $j_t=7$ and $n_{j_t}=13051463048$. The infinite sequence, rather than one large numerical ratio, is what disproves an upper bound with an unspecified constant.

4. Consequences and the higher-fullness wording

For $k=1,2$ one has trivially $\prod_{i<k}B_2(n+i)\le n(n+1)\le2n^2$. For every fixed $k\ge3$, the extra factors are at least $1$, so $2$ disproves the stronger $O_k(n^2)$ proposal. Thus that proposal holds precisely for $k\le2$.

The question also discusses $B_r$ for $r\ge3$. Its displayed wording with every $\varepsilon>0$ cannot literally give an unbounded ratio to $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for fixed $k$: always \[ \prod_{i<k}B_r(n+i)\le\prod_{i<k}(n+i)=O_k(n^k), \] so the ratio is bounded when $\varepsilon\ge k-1$. The nontrivial interpretation, also identified in the source discussion, asks for some positive $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(r,k)$. No proof of that interpretation for general $r,k$ is claimed here.

5. Assessment and references

The logarithm in $2$ is $n^{o(1)}$, so it is fully compatible with the main proposed upper bound. That bound is unresolved in this attempt, even though the stronger variant has been completely classified.
