Finite Markov templates with several varying level sets

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For an integer matrix $A$, the fiber at $b$ is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves $u$ with $Au=0$ such that steps $x\mapsto x\pm u$ staying nonnegative connect every nonempty fiber. A hierarchical categorical model is specified by a collection of allowed interactions. Its level sets give the possible values of each variable, and varying their sizes changes the model matrix and its Markov bases. Finiteness can concern move degrees, symmetry classes, or a finite collection of templates rather than a bound on the total number of moves.

Research attempt

Literature and scope

Question 27 varies several level counts in hierarchical models. I attempted a uniform move construction for complete independence, with a fixed number $r$ of variables and arbitrary finite level sets. This is a standard special case, rederived here, not a finiteness theorem for arbitrary interaction structures. The attempted extension fails at preservation of higher-order margins.
Proposition: For tables with fixed one-variable margins, the moves exchanging one coordinate between two observations form a degree-two Markov basis. Up to independent relabeling of levels, at most $r(2^{r-1}-1)$ nonzero move templates suffice. For total count $N\ge1$, any two tables in a fiber can be connected in at most $r(N-1)$ such steps.
Proof: Represent each table by $N$ labeled rows, each an $r$-tuple. Choose any labeling of the target rows. In coordinate $j$, the current and target lists have the same multiset, so a permutation of the $N$ positions makes them equal. Every permutation is a product of at most $N-1$ transpositions: on a cycle of length $m$, use $m-1$ transpositions. Exchange the $j$th entries of the corresponding two rows for each transposition. This keeps all other coordinates fixed and preserves every one-variable margin. The two rows exist before the exchange, so the induced table remains nonnegative. A step subtracts two observations and adds two, hence has degree two unless it cancels to zero. Apply this separately for each coordinate. Previously matched coordinates are unchanged, and finally the labeled arrays agree. The empty table needs no moves.

To count templates, specify the exchanged coordinate $j$ and the subset of the other $r-1$ coordinates in which the two rows differ. If this subset is empty the table change is zero. Otherwise each coordinate uses at most two levels, which independent relabeling sends to fixed labels. There are at most $2^{r-1}-1$ subsets for each $j$. Some descriptions coincide, so this is an upper bound, not an exact orbit count. ∎

Failed extension and original-scope comparison

For two binary variables, exchanging the first coordinates of $(0,0),(1,1)$ produces $(1,0),(0,1)$. It preserves single-variable margins but changes the joint two-variable margin. Thus applying the same sorting algorithm to an arbitrary hierarchical model is invalid precisely when a prescribed interaction contains both the swapped coordinate and another differing coordinate. No replacement construction for those interactions is obtained here. Finite templates also do not mean a bounded number of instantiated moves.

Verification and remaining gaps

Explicit graph search checked all 495 binary three-way tables of total at most four, grouped into 225 fibers; the indicated exchanges connected every fiber. The proof covers all level sizes for complete independence. The unrestricted finiteness classifications in Question 27 remain outside this attempt.
