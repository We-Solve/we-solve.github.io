A high-color coupling and the exact gap to the conjecture

The actual primary conjecture is $O(1)$ spectral independence for all proper $q$-colorings when $q\ge\Delta+2$. We prove only a familiar rapid-mixing bound in the more restrictive regime $q>2\Delta$. A mixing estimate alone is not asserted to prove the source spectral-independence statement. Let $G$ be a finite simple graph with $n\ge1$ vertices, maximum degree $\Delta$, and integer $q\ge2$ with $q>2\Delta$. At each step choose a vertex uniformly and recolor it uniformly from the colors absent at its neighbors, allowing its old color. Let $\pi$ be uniform on all proper colorings; this set is nonempty by sequential greedy coloring, since $q\ge\Delta+1$.
Proposition: Put $\kappa=(q-2\Delta)/(q-\Delta)>0$. From every proper starting coloring $\sigma$, \[ \|P^t(\sigma,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}\le n e^{-\kappa t/n}. \]
Proof: Two colorings differing only at vertex $v$ have the same neighbor colors there, hence the same available set and identical forward/reverse transition probabilities. Other distinct states have zero transition in either direction. This proves detailed balance with the uniform law and therefore stationarity. Couple any two chains by choosing the same vertex $v$. Let $A,B$ be their available color sets. Couple their uniform choices maximally: match each common color with probability $\min\{1/|A|,1/|B|\}$, then couple the remaining disjoint residual masses. Their disagreement probability is \[ 1-\frac{|A\cap B|}{\max\{|A|,|B|\}}. \] Let $d_v$ be the number of neighbors on which the colorings disagree. Each color in either directional difference of available sets must be supplied by a disagreeing neighbor in the other coloring. Thus both directional differences have size at most $d_v$, and $|A|,|B|\ge q-\Delta$. The disagreement probability is at most $d_v/(q-\Delta)$. If $D$ denotes their current Hamming distance, updating $v$ removes its old disagreement and adds one with at most this probability. Averaging over $v$ gives \[ \mathbb E[D_{t+1}\mid\sigma_t,\tau_t] \le D_t-D_t/n+\frac{\sum_vd_v}{n(q-\Delta)} \le(1-\kappa/n)D_t, \] since each disagreeing vertex is counted in at most $\Delta$ neighbor lists. Iteration gives $\mathbb ED_t\le n(1-\kappa/n)^t\le ne^{-\kappa t/n}$. Start the second chain at stationarity. For any event, the difference of the two probabilities is at most the probability of unequal coupled states, which is at most $\mathbb ED_t$. Taking the supremum over events proves the result. ∎
This direct all-pairs coupling needs no unproved path-coupling lemma. At the target boundary $q=\Delta+2$, its coefficient $\kappa$ is nonpositive once $\Delta\ge2$ (zero at two), so this estimate supplies no contraction there. Better influence control is precisely the missing step. Frozen colorings of $K_q$ at $q=\Delta+1$ do not refute the source conjecture, because they lie outside its range.

Verification and remaining gaps
