Initial Data for Physical Late-Time Schwarzschild Wave Tails

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

The wave operator is $\Box_g\phi=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi$. A hyperboloidal spacelike hypersurface approaches future null infinity rather than spatial infinity. Late-time tails are the leading decay terms of the radiation field of $\phi$ on $\mathcal I^+$. The background is the Schwarzschild exterior with fixed mass.

For solutions of the scalar wave equation $\Box_g\phi=0$ on a Schwarzschild background arising from physically realistic isolated-system data, how should one choose or characterize the data on an asymptotically hyperboloidal hypersurface $\Sigma$ so as to obtain physically meaningful predictions for the late-time asymptotics along future null infinity $\mathcal{I}^+$?

A radiation-data diagnostic in the flat limit.
For a radial wave in three-dimensional Minkowski space, set $\psi(t,r)=r\phi(t,r)$. Direct substitution into $-\phi_{tt}+r^{-2}(r^2\phi_r)_r=0$ gives $-\psi_{tt}+\psi_{rr}=0$, so on an exterior region \[ \psi(t,r)=F(t-r)+G(t+r). \] If $G(v)\to0$, the radiation field at future null infinity is $F(u)$, where $u=t-r$. For any $p>0$, a smooth outgoing profile agreeing with $(1+u)^{-p}$ at large positive $u$ has finite late-time radiative energy $\int|F'(u)|^2du$, because the integrand decays as $u^{-2p-2}$. Smooth compactly supported profiles instead have no late tail in this flat model.
Implication for an attempted data criterion.
Finite radiation energy alone permits a continuum of decay powers and also compact support. Thus that scalar norm does not determine a unique late-time asymptotic. A global regular center in the flat radial problem imposes a relation between ingoing and outgoing pieces; the exterior calculation does not impose it artificially on black-hole data.
Source gap.
On Schwarzschild, curvature scattering changes the relation between input and radiation and can generate tails from compact data. This calculation therefore does not predict a Schwarzschild exponent. The first gap is a physical scattering/isolated-system characterization of hyperboloidal data, including their asymptotic charges or low-frequency content, followed by a controlled propagation to null infinity. Replacing that task by a convenient compact-support condition would alter the source question.
