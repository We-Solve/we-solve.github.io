Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Sparse regression models $y=X\beta_*+\varepsilon$ with few nonzero coordinates in $\beta_*$. An information threshold is the least sample scale admitting statistically successful recovery without runtime restrictions; an algorithmic threshold concerns the specified efficient algorithm class.

A learning problem specifies a data distribution, a hypothesis class and a loss. Statistical consistency means that the estimator approaches its population target as the sample size increases; statistical and computational requirements are distinct.

For a discrete random variable $X$, entropy is $H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)$, and mutual information is $I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)$. Coding rates, capacities, and exponents depend on the stated channel or source model and error criterion.

Research attempt: A finite-search recovery bound and the small-amplitude barrier

An explicit exhaustive estimator.
Let $1\le k<p$, let the unknown $\beta_*\in\{0,1\}^p$ have exactly $k$ ones, take $X\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ with independent standard Gaussian entries, and take independent $W\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n)$ with $\sigma>0$. Minimize $\|Y-X\beta\|^2$ over all $\binom pk$ candidates, with any deterministic tie rule.

For a fixed incorrect candidate put $\Delta=\beta-\beta_*$ and $z=X\Delta$. It can beat the truth only if \[ \|W-z\|^2\le\|W\|^2, \quad\text{equivalently}\quad W^Tz\ge\|z\|^2/2. \] Conditional on $X$, the random variable $W^Tz$ is centered Gaussian with variance $\sigma^2\|z\|^2$. Its moment-generating function, obtained by completing the square in the Gaussian density, is $\mathbb Ee^{tW^Tz}=e^{t^2\sigma^2\|z\|^2/2}$. Markov's inequality with $t=1/(2\sigma^2)$ therefore bounds the error by $e^{-\|z\|^2/(8\sigma^2)}$.

If the candidate swaps $\ell$ true coordinates for $\ell$ false ones, then $\|\Delta\|^2=2\ell$, and the $n$ coordinates of $z$ are independent $N(0,2\ell)$. Integrating each Gaussian square gives \[ \mathbb E_X e^{-\|z\|^2/(8\sigma^2)} =\left(1+\frac{\ell}{2\sigma^2}\right)^{-n/2}. \] There are $\binom k\ell\binom{p-k}\ell$ such candidates. A union bound proves the finite-sample estimate \[ \Pr(\widehat\beta\ne\beta_*)\le \sum_{\ell=1}^{\min(k,p-k)} \binom k\ell\binom{p-k}\ell \left(1+\frac{\ell}{2\sigma^2}\right)^{-n/2}. \] In particular, with $r=\min(k,p-k)$ and $0<\delta<1$, a sufficient condition for error at most $\delta$ is \[ n\ge2\max_{1\le\ell\le r} \frac{\log\left(r\binom k\ell\binom{p-k}\ell/\delta\right)} {\log(1+\ell/(2\sigma^2))}. \] This is an actual statistically successful estimator under that bound, but it enumerates $\binom pk$ possibilities. No polynomial-time implementation or identification of this bound with the source's sharp $n_*$ is established.

Sparsity without amplitude normalization is insufficient.
Even with Gaussian design, distinguish the two one-sparse signals $a e_1$ and $a e_2$, with $a>0$ and equal prior probabilities. Conditional on $X$, their observations are Gaussians with the same covariance and mean difference $d=a(X_1-X_2)$. Orthogonal rotation along $d$ reduces their optimal distinguishing test to one dimension: the densities cross at the midpoint, so their total variation distance is \[ 2\Phi(\|d\|/(2\sigma))-1 \le \frac{\|d\|}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}, \] using the bound $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ on the standard Gaussian density. Since the design distribution is identical under the two hypotheses, the joint total variation is the expectation of the conditional distance; Cauchy--Schwarz and $\mathbb E\|d\|^2=2na^2$ give the upper bound $a\sqrt n/(\sigma\sqrt\pi)$. Any support estimator therefore has average error at least \[ \frac12\left(1-\frac{a\sqrt n}{\sigma\sqrt\pi}\right), \] when the right side is positive. The testing formula follows because, under equal priors, choosing the more likely density maximizes success and yields error $(1-\mathrm{TV})/2$.

Thus if $a\sqrt n/\sigma\to0$, information itself is insufficient, regardless of computation. This does not challenge the source's normalized signal model; it shows why the omitted normalization cannot be ignored when interpreting its thresholds.

Verification and adversarial audit

Both Gaussian integrals were derived by completing the square, the swap count covers every incorrect binary support exactly once, and the union bound does not assume independent candidate errors. The small-amplitude lower bound conditions on the observed design and then averages, so it does not confuse a fixed-design obstruction with the random-design model. No overlap-gap theorem is treated as a universal computational lower bound.
