Research attempt

Literature and scope

Question 42 asks about the Behrens--Fisher likelihood equations. The primary paper by Buot, Hoşten and Richards reports the generic complex count $2p+1$ when both sample sizes exceed dimension $p$. That established result is not claimed as new progress. I instead tested the inference that three real univariate roots give three local maxima, using unequal sample sizes.
Proposition: For two univariate normal samples of sizes $2,3$, means $-2,2$, and sample variances $1,1$ computed with their respective sample-size divisors, the common-mean likelihood has three real interior stationary solutions. Exactly two are local maxima; the third is a saddle in the full parameter space.
Proof: For fixed mean $\mu$, the unique variance maximizers are $q_1=1+(\mu+2)^2$ and $q_2=1+(\mu-2)^2$, both positive. Indeed, each variance $v$ has log likelihood $-n(\log v+q/v)/2$, whose derivative is $n(q-v)/(2v^2)$. The profile log likelihood is therefore, up to a constant, \[ L(\mu)=-\log q_1-\tfrac32\log q_2, \qquad L'(\mu)=-\frac{P(\mu)}{q_1q_2},\quad P(\mu)=5\mu^3+2\mu^2-15\mu-10. \] The values $P(-2)=-12$, $P(-1)=2$, $P(0)=-10$ and $P(2)=8$ force roots in $(-2,-1)$, $(-1,0)$ and $(0,2)$. A cubic cannot have more than three distinct roots, so these are all its roots and each is simple. Its positive leading coefficient implies alternating signs, giving profile maxima at the first and third roots and a minimum at the middle root. Every full stationary solution must use the unique variance critical points, so there are exactly three.

Near an outer root, the profile has a strict local maximum and each conditional variance maximum is strict. The full likelihood is bounded above by its profile, so these give strict full local maxima. At the middle root, moving along the profiled variance curve increases likelihood, whereas varying either variance at fixed mean decreases it. It is therefore a saddle. The sample summaries are realizable: use observations $(-3,-1)$ and $(2-\sqrt{3/2},2,2+\sqrt{3/2})$. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

Numerical roots were approximately $-1.509654,-0.722921,1.832575$; the exact sign argument, not these approximations, proves their number and classification. Positive sample variances are essential here: if one group is constant at $m$, holding $\mu=m$ and sending its variance to zero makes its likelihood unbounded while the other group's likelihood can remain positive. This example neither replaces the established generic complex count nor solves all multivariate or nongeneric cases. The attempted identification of real roots with modes is disproved.
