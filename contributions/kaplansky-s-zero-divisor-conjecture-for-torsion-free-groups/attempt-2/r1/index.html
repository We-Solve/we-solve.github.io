Kaplansky's Zero-Divisor Conjecture for Torsion-Free Groups

OpenAI's claim

The linked manuscripts present the following claims by OpenAI: A finitely presented torsion-free group is claimed to have nonzero elements $a,c\in\mathbb F_2[G]$ with $ac=0$, despite admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying complex; Another construction claims a torsion-free, finitely presented $G$ for which $\mathbb F_2[G]$ contains $a,b$ satisfying $ab=1$ and $ba\ne1$. The announced examples provide an affirmative answer to the existence question and refute the universal zero-divisor conjecture.

Mathematical statement of the claimed result

Manuscript 1.
For some torsion-free, finitely presented group $G$ admitting a finite two-dimensional classifying space, the source claims \[ \exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2[G]\setminus\{0\},\qquad\alpha\beta=0. \] The coefficients are in the field with two elements. This is a zero-divisor example, without a claim here about integral units.
Manuscript 2.
The construction supplies a torsion-free, finitely presented group $G$, with a finite two-dimensional $K(G,1)$, and elements of its characteristic-two group algebra satisfying \[ a,b,c\in\mathbb F_2[G],\qquad ab=1,\quad ac=0,\quad c\ne0,\quad ba\ne1. \] In particular, $a\ne0$ and $e=ba$ obeys $e^2=e$, $e\notin\{0,1\}$. Thus this same example supplies failures of direct finiteness, absence of zero divisors, and triviality of scalar idempotents. These assertions concern $\mathbb F_2[G]$; the separate integral-unit assertion is not a consequence.

Outline of the argument

Finite labeled graphs encode supports whose product coefficients cancel modulo two. A random-label counting argument excludes planar path arrangements; cone surgeries turn that exclusion into asphericity and root separation, so the coefficients remain nonzero in a torsion-free group.

An unequal-side parity pattern supplies the one-sided inverse relation in labeled graphs. Weighted counts handle repeated edge traversals, and a separator reduces arbitrarily large planar arrangements to bounded path collections; cone attachment and root separation then preserve the inverse defect while proving torsion-freeness.
