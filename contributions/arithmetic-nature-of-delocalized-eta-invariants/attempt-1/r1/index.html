Arithmetic Nature of Delocalized Eta Invariants

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.
Definitions and conventions.
For an odd-dimensional Dirac operator $D$, an eta invariant measures spectral asymmetry through a regularized trace integral. On a universal cover with deck group $\Gamma$, a delocalized trace sums the integral kernel over a specified nontrivial conjugacy class of $\Gamma$. Replacing the usual trace by this trace gives a delocalized eta invariant when the summation and regularization converge. An algebraic number is a root of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients.

A finite spectral arithmetic check.
An elementary equivariant analogue explains why finite-order group elements alone do not settle the analytic eta problem. Let $D$ be an invertible self-adjoint matrix, let $U$ be unitary with $U^m=I$, and suppose $UD=DU$. Each positive or negative spectral subspace of $D$ is $U$-invariant. On either subspace, the minimal polynomial of $U$ divides $x^m-1$, whose roots are distinct. Consequently \[ \operatorname{Tr}(U\operatorname{sgn}D)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}n_j e^{2\pi i j/m},\qquad n_j\in\mathbb Z. \] This is algebraic: each root of unity satisfies a rational polynomial and finitely many algebraic numbers form a finite-dimensional field extension, so their sum remains algebraic. Zero eigenvalues, if present, may be assigned sign zero and omitted without changing the conclusion.
The limiting obstruction.
The actual delocalized eta invariant in [S2] uses an infinite-dimensional heat trace, conjugacy-class summation and regularization. Even a convergent sequence of algebraic approximants does not preserve algebraicity. For example, rational decimal truncations converge to every real number, whereas algebraic numbers form a countable set (there are countably many rational polynomials, each with finitely many roots). Thus a finite-order trace calculation plus convergence is insufficient.
