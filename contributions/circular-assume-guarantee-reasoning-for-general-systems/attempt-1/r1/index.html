Research attempt: Circular simulation for a fully observed linear subclass

A complete simulation criterion with identity output
Consider $\Sigma_S:\dot z=A_Sz+G_Sd_S$, $y=z$ and $\Sigma_T:\dot z=A_Tz+G_Td_T$, $y=z$, with the same state dimension and unrestricted auxiliary disturbance signals. Simulation is required from every source state, preserving the output trajectory. Since the output is the entire state, the matched target state must always be the identical vector. Such simulation holds exactly when \[ \operatorname{im}(A_S-A_T)+\operatorname{im}G_S\subseteq\operatorname{im}G_T. \tag{1} \] For necessity, apply any linear annihilator of $\operatorname{im}G_T$ to the equality of trajectories. This removes the target disturbance even when it is only locally integrable. For a constant source disturbance the source trajectory is smooth, so the resulting derivative identity holds at every time by continuity. Evaluate at time zero for every initial $z$, first with $d_S=0$, and then subtract the zero-disturbance case. This gives $1$, since the intersection of kernels of all such annihilators is $\operatorname{im}G_T$; this last assertion follows by extending a basis of that image to a basis of the whole state space. For sufficiency choose a linear right inverse $L$ of $G_T$ on its image, obtainable by selecting a basis of independent columns. Given a source trajectory, set \[ d_T=L((A_S-A_T)z+G_Sd_S). \] The target derivative is then the source derivative. The identical trajectory satisfies the target equation and output constraint. For continuous or locally integrable signals these expressions give admissible signals on each common existence interval. This proves the criterion without selecting incompatible state relations.
Applying the criterion to the circular rule
For $i=1,2$, let both $P_i$ and $Q_i$ have state/output $x_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}$ and dynamics \[ \dot x_i=A_{X_i}x_i+B_{X_i}u_i+G_{X_i}d_i, \qquad X_i\in\{P_i,Q_i\}. \] Use feedback $u_1=-x_2$, $u_2=x_1$, and observe the full pair $(x_1,x_2)$. The composite matrices are \[ A_{X_1X_2}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{X_1}&-B_{X_1}\\ B_{X_2}&A_{X_2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad G_{X_1X_2}=\operatorname{diag}(G_{X_1},G_{X_2}). \] The first premise $P_1\parallel Q_2\preccurlyeq Q_1\parallel Q_2$, by $1$, implies \[ \operatorname{im}[\,A_{P_1}-A_{Q_1}\quad -(B_{P_1}-B_{Q_1})\,] +\operatorname{im}G_{P_1}\subseteq\operatorname{im}G_{Q_1}. \tag{2} \] Indeed the drift difference has only its first block row nonzero, and the target disturbance image is the direct product of the two component images. Conversely $2$ implies that first premise, since its unchanged second disturbance is already permitted. The second premise similarly gives \[ \operatorname{im}[\,B_{P_2}-B_{Q_2}\quad A_{P_2}-A_{Q_2}\,] +\operatorname{im}G_{P_2}\subseteq\operatorname{im}G_{Q_2}. \tag{3} \] For the fully implemented pair $P_1\parallel P_2$, its drift difference from $Q_1\parallel Q_2$ has precisely these two block rows. Equations $2$ and $3$ place each row value in its target disturbance image, for every shared state pair, and also place each source disturbance there. Their direct product is therefore $1$ for the desired conclusion. This proves the circular rule for this subclass, allowing nonzero $G$ and hence genuine nondeterminism.
Where the proof stops
With general outputs $y_i=C_ix_i$, output equality need not force identical internal states. Simulation witnesses are then relations, possibly matching the same source state to different target states. The two premises may supply different hidden-state matches or different disturbance witnesses on the shared feedback ports. The block-row inclusions $2$--$3$ were obtained only because the relation was forced to be the identity, so importing them into the general case is invalid. Establishing a compatible joint relation, rather than merely having two separate existential matching relations, is the unfinished part of this approach. No counterexample to the report's general rule is asserted.

Verification and adversarial audit

Criterion $1$ includes drift differences that can be absorbed by target disturbances; demanding equality of drift matrices would have been unnecessarily strong. The block matrices use the source's signed feedback convention, and both component disturbance spaces remain independent. Zero disturbance columns recover the deterministic special case. Full output observability is a declared restriction, not a consequence of the source premises.
Audit corrections.
Allowed drift matching through the target disturbance image and exposed the missing compatibility of hidden-state witnesses, instead of treating existential simulations as interchangeable.
