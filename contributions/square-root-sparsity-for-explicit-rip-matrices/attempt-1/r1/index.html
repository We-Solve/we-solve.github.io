Square-Root Sparsity for Explicit RIP Matrices

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

A real $n\times p$ matrix $A$ has the restricted isometry property of order $s$ with distortion $\delta\in(0,1)$ if $(1-\delta)\|x\|_2^2\leq\|Ax\|_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\|x\|_2^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb R^p$ with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates. Here $n$ counts measurements and $p$ is the ambient dimension. The asymptotic question fixes a nontrivial distortion bound independently of $n,p$. An explicit construction produces the matrix deterministically with controlled construction time.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does there exist an explicit construction of real-valued restricted-isometry matrices with sparsity $s\geq c\sqrt n$ in the polylogarithmic regime $n=(\log p)^{O(1)}$? [S2]
Scope qualification.
The source asks about the compressed regime $n<p$, with a fixed distortion below one. Sparsity must obey the feasibility restrictions of this regime; the supplied text does not state a range of exponents or a numerical distortion. Construction cost and representation precision must be stated in any answer.

The direct random-coherence route loses a logarithm

The primary report already discusses deterministic constructions close to the square-root scale and asks for a stronger polylogarithmic-regime result. We test a direct sign-matrix and exhaustive-search route. Let $n\ge1$, $p\ge2$ be integers and let $A$ have independent entries $\pm1/\sqrt n$ with equal probabilities. Its columns have norm one.
Proposition: With probability at least $1/2$, all distinct column inner products have magnitude at most \[ \mu=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2p(p-1))}{n}}. \] Such a matrix has RIP distortion at most $(s-1)\mu$ at order $s$ whenever this expression is below one.
Proof: For a fixed pair of columns their inner product is $n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n X_j$, where the $X_j$ are independent uniform signs. The series comparison $(2k)!\ge2^k k!$ gives $\cosh t\le\exp(t^2/2)$. Thus Markov's inequality, independence and $t=\mu$ yield \[ \Pr\left\{n^{-1}\sum X_j>\mu\right\} \le\exp(-nt\mu)(\cosh t)^n\le\exp(-n\mu^2/2). \] The lower tail has the same bound. Summing over $p(p-1)/2$ pairs gives a failure probability at most $p(p-1)\exp(-n\mu^2/2)=1/2$. For a vector supported on at most $s$ columns, expansion of its squared output norm bounds the error by $\mu\sum_{i\ne j}|x_ix_j|\le\mu(s-1)\|x\|_2^2$, again by Cauchy--Schwarz. ∎
For fixed desired $0<\delta<1$, this proves only \[ s-1\le\delta\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\log(2p(p-1))}}. \] The proved guarantee is smaller than a fixed multiple of $\sqrt n$ by a factor growing as $\sqrt{\log p}$. This is a limitation of this elementary argument; it is not an upper bound on the achievable RIP order of random matrices.

A finite search is not the requested explicit construction

The probabilistic proof implies at least one acceptable sign matrix, so one may enumerate all $2^{np}$ sign patterns and compute every correlation. The verification can avoid irrational entries by using the integer unnormalized column dot products. This gives a finite deterministic search with an exponential worst-case bound, not a polynomial construction. The search space and the logarithmic loss remain even when $n=(\log p)^{O(1)}$. Thus substituting exhaustive search for randomness does not finish the target.

Verification and remaining gaps

Exact binomial probabilities for sums of 16 and 64 independent signs were compared with the two-tail bound, and the coherence inequality was checked on explicit sign matrices and sparse rational vectors. These checks are illustrative; the probability proof is the argument for arbitrary sizes. The source's more sophisticated explicit constructions are not superseded by this weaker baseline. No derandomization at the required sparsity or proof escaping the coherence loss is obtained.
