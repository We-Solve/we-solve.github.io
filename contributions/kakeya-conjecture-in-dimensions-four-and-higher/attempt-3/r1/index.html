The Kakeya conjecture in dimensions four and higher
A detailed exposition of the all-dimensional deduction from local smoothing

Abstract

The Math-95 question asks whether every compact Kakeya set in $\mathbb R^n$ has full Hausdorff dimension for each integer $n\ge4$. OpenAI's October 25, 2026 manuscript Local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation reports an all-dimensional local-smoothing estimate at the critical wave exponent, and as consequences a scale-uniform Kakeya maximal inequality and the full Hausdorff dimension of Kakeya sets in every $n\ge2$. This source-based exposition extracts and expands the Kakeya proof from the longer Math-273 report. The main analytic input is explicitly stated and traced to the original wave argument; we prove the passage from that input to local spherical extension, to weighted tube overlap, to the maximal bound, and finally to the exact Hausdorff assertion using arbitrary unequal-scale covers. In particular, the deduction establishes Hausdorff, not just Minkowski, dimension. We distinguish the stronger logarithmic bounds in the manuscript from the weaker $\delta^{-\varepsilon}$ statement documented in the Lean formalization, and from an earlier four-dimensional-only contribution already recorded for Math-95. This is an exposition of OpenAI's reported solution, not a claim of independent discovery or a complete independent audit of the foundational wave estimate.
Attribution and verification boundary. All underlying discoveries and the original analytic proof belong to OpenAI's cited public manuscript. The proofs of the Kakeya deductions are independently re-expressed here but are based on its arguments. The fundamental local-smoothing theorem and its auxiliary analytic lemmas are used from that source; this document does not re-prove them from first principles. OpenAI describes corresponding Lean results, but the Lean library was not rebuilt or independently checked in preparing this exposition. Its inclusion on We-Solve should therefore be understood as a documented external result, not a new authorial claim of proof verification.

Exact We-Solve target and scope of the claimed result

The selected catalogue assertion is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-61-74-61-6c-6f-67-75-65} \boxed{\text{For every integer }n\ge4\text{ and every compact Kakeya set } E\subset\mathbb R^n,\quad \dim_{\mathrm H} E=n.} \end{equation} Here $E$ is Kakeya if each $\omega\in S^{n-1}$ admits some $a_\omega\in\mathbb R^n$ such that \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-65-67-6d-65-6e-74} \{a_\omega+t\omega:0\le t\le1\}\subset E. \end{equation} The direction-to-center map need not be measurable or continuous. Hausdorff dimension is defined by $\dim_{\mathrm H} E=\inf\{s:\mathcal H^s(E)=0\}$, where $\mathcal H^s$ is the usual Hausdorff measure constructed from covers by arbitrarily small sets. No positive-volume conclusion is part of the target [catalogue].

The pinned OpenAI source we analyse states a stronger conclusion:

Theorem (OpenAI's reported Kakeya theorem): For every integer $n\ge2$, if $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ contains a unit segment in every direction, then $\dim_{\mathrm H} E=n$. No compactness or measurability assumption on $E$ is needed for this statement.
This clearly implies (eq:catalogue). It is a result for all dimensions, not an extrapolation from the previously reported four-dimensional case. As a mathematical statement, it is weaker than a claim that Kakeya sets have positive Lebesgue measure, and logically distinct from endpoint Fourier restriction claims.

Analytic input: critical local smoothing and the proof dependency

Fix $n\ge2$, put \[ d=n-1,\qquad p=p_{\mathrm c}=\frac{2n}{n-1}=\frac{2n}{d}, \qquad A=\frac{2}{p}=\frac dn, \qquad U(t)=e^{-\mathrm it|D|}. \] Our Fourier transform convention is $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-\mathrm ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,\mathrm d x$. Let $P_N=\chi(D/N)$ for a smooth multiplier $\chi$ supported in an annulus separated from the origin.
Theorem (Critical annular wave estimate, source theorem): For $N\ge2$ and Schwartz data $f$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-6e-6e-75-6c-61-72} \|U(t)P_N f\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n\times[1,2])} \le C_{n,\chi}[\log(2+N)]^{A}\|f\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}. \end{equation}
This is the source's critical frequency-annulus estimate. It is slightly more quantitative than the usual local-smoothing conclusion with $N^\varepsilon$ loss. Its constant is uniform in $N$, and its exponent obeys the critical scaling identity \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-63-61-6c-69-6e-67} \frac np=\frac d2, \qquad dp=2n. \end{equation} Both identities will be used at the points where the powers of the frequency or the tube width cancel [introduction,transfer].

Where the annular estimate comes from

A complete proof of Theorem Theorem (Critical annular wave estimate, source theorem) is in the cited manuscript, and its extended exposition is the separate Math-273 report. For clarity, the route relevant to the Kakeya conclusion is:
  1. In null coordinates for the wave equation, a symmetric first-order system has negative symbol $\kappa\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}|v|^2&-v^{\mathsf T}\\-v&I_d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ (up to the sign convention); its kernel singles out the polarization $(1,v)$. Imaginary translation in a lightlike direction multiplies a Fourier mode by a directional Gaussian, giving angular localization before amplitudes are squared.
  2. Two auxiliary flows of the analytically continued full wave produce a positive-semidefinite conserved tensor. Its physical-time density records interference between the modes, which would be lost under an incoherent sum of their separate energies.
  3. A rank-one background and a quaternionic Hessian Dirichlet problem on moving spatial fibers turn that density into a nonnegative corrected energy $\mathcal F(t,\tau)$, with $\det H_{\mathrm{fib}}(\lambda|\Phi|^2+u)=\lambda\mu$ and $\mu$ the time component of the current. The determinant comparison $(\beta+\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}$ explains the power $n/(n-1)$ of $\mu$, hence $p=2n/(n-1)$ for the wave.
  4. An Aubin--Mabuchi-type correction cancels an indefinite full Hessian determinant from $\Delta_{t,\tau}\mathcal F$. Boundary Gaussian decay and an interior comparison yield a scalar inequality $\Delta\mathcal F\le Cs^{-2}e^{-cs/h}\mathcal F$, where $s=|\tau|$ and $h=N^{-1}$.
  5. Comparison across two radii in the auxiliary two-plane, with physical-time transfer between them, loses two logarithms at the level of the $p$-th power. This gives the $(\log N)^{2/p}$ factor. Periodization is removed with off-cone kernel decay.
The existence, cofactor, boundary and scalar comparison arguments are not treated as elementary established facts by the following Kakeya calculation; they are precisely the source-dependent content of Theorem Theorem (Critical annular wave estimate, source theorem). Source sections are individually linked in the bibliography [current,calculus,energy,barrier,propagation].

From a wave annulus to local spherical extension

Define the Fourier extension operator for surface measure $\sigma$ on the unit sphere by \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-78-74-65-6e-73-69-6f-6e} Eg(x)=\int_{S^{n-1}}e^{\mathrm ix\cdot\omega}g(\omega) \,\mathrm d\sigma(\omega). \end{equation} The first reduction is a localized extension estimate, uniform in the spatial center. We give its focusing-shell proof, because that uniformity later permits tube centers to be arbitrary.
Proposition (Local extension at the critical exponent): Assuming Theorem Theorem (Critical annular wave estimate, source theorem), for every $R\ge2$, every $z\in\mathbb R^n$, and every $g\in L^p(S^{n-1})$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-63-61-6c-65-78-74} \|Eg\|_{L^p(B(z,R))} \le C_n[\log(2+R)]^{A}\|g\|_{L^p(S^{n-1})}. \end{equation}
Proof: It is enough first to take bounded smooth $g$ and $z=0$. Choose nonnegative $\psi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$ with $\psi\not\equiv0$, and a radial cutoff $\chi$ supported near the unit frequency sphere and equal to one on a smaller annulus. For $N\ge2$ define \[ M_N f=\int_1^2\psi(t)e^{\mathrm iNt} U(t)\chi(|D|/N)f\,\mathrm d t. \] Hölder in $t$ and (eq:annular) imply \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-4d-4e} \|M_N f\|_p\le C[\log(2+N)]^A\|f\|_p. \end{equation} Fourier transformation in time shows that $M_N$ has radial symbol $\chi(\rho/N)\widehat\psi(\rho-N)$, with the convention $\widehat\psi(s)=\int e^{-\mathrm its}\psi(t)\,\mathrm d t$.

The Fourier inversion formula in polar coordinates and stationary phase on the sphere give, uniformly when $1/2\le|w|\le3$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6b-65-72-6e-65-6c} \mathcal K_N(w)=c_nN^{d/2}|w|^{-d/2} e^{\mathrm iN|w|}\psi(|w|) +O(N^{d/2-1}),\qquad c_n\ne0. \end{equation} The two stationary directions are $\pm w/|w|$. In the radial integral the incoming leading phase is suppressed because $\psi$ is supported at positive times; Taylor expansion of $\chi(\rho/N)\rho^{d/2}$ near $\rho=N$ controls the stated error. This is the source's focusing-shell computation, not an assertion that the kernel has such a form uniformly outside the indicated shell [kakeya-source].

Put $f_N(r\omega)=e^{-\mathrm iNr}\psi(r)g(\omega)$. Its $L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ norm is a fixed multiple of $\|g\|_{L^p(S^{n-1})}$. For $|X|\le R$ and $N\ge4R$, Taylor expansion gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-69-73-74-61-6e-63-65} N|r\omega-X/N|=Nr-X\cdot\omega+O(R^2/N), \qquad r\in\operatorname{supp}\psi. \end{equation} Apply (eq:kernel) to $M_N f_N(X/N)$. Its leading oscillation $e^{\mathrm iNr}$ cancels $e^{-\mathrm iNr}$ from $f_N$, leaving \[ N^{-d/2}M_N f_N(X/N) =c' E g(-X)+\mathcal E_N(X),\qquad c'=c_n\int_0^\infty r^{d/2}\psi(r)^2\,\mathrm d r\ne0, \] where the estimates for the kernel and its amplitude give \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-72-72-6f-72} |\mathcal E_N(X)|\le C(1+R^2)N^{-1}\|g\|_{L^1(S^{n-1})}. \end{equation} By the substitution $x=X/N$, the $L^p$ norm of the left side over $B(0,R)$ is \[ N^{n/p-d/2}\|M_N f_N\|_{L^p(B(0,R/N))}. \] The exponent vanishes by (eq:scaling). Choose, for example, $N=(4+R)^{3+n/p}$, rounded upward. Then the $L^p(B(0,R))$ norm of (eq:error) is at most $C\|g\|_p$, while $\log(2+N)\lesssim_n\log(2+R)$. Applying (eq:MN) proves (eq:localext) at $z=0$.

For general $z$, observe $Eg(X+z)=E(e^{\mathrm iz\cdot\omega}g)(X)$ and $\|e^{\mathrm iz\cdot\omega}g\|_p=\|g\|_p$. Approximation extends the estimate from smooth to arbitrary $L^p$ data. The same constant therefore works for every ball center $z$. ∎

Remark (No endpoint-restriction assumption): The Kakeya proof below uses only the local $p$-to-$p$ estimate (eq:localext). It does not require an endpoint global Fourier restriction theorem, or the epsilon-removal step used in other consequences of the local-smoothing manuscript.

From extension to weighted overlaps of directional tubes

For $0<\delta\le1$, $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and $\omega\in S^{n-1}$, define the open unit $\delta$-tube \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-75-62-65} T_{\delta}(x,\omega)=\{y\in\mathbb R^n: \operatorname{dist}(y,x+[-1/2,1/2]\omega)<\delta\}. \end{equation} Its volume is comparable to $\delta^d$, uniformly in $x$ and $\omega$, for $\delta\le1$. Recentring the segment from $[0,1]$ to $[-1/2,1/2]$ has no effect on the geometric problem.
Lemma (Weighted tube overlap): Let $0<\delta\le1/2$. Choose a finite $\delta$-separated family $\omega_j\in S^{n-1}$, arbitrary centers $x_j\in\mathbb R^n$, and numbers $a_j\ge0$. Put $q=p/2=n/(n-1)$ and $T_j=T_{2\delta}(x_j,\omega_j)$. Then \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6f-76-65-72-6c-61-70} \left\|\sum_j a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}\right\|_{L^q(\mathbb R^n)} \le C_n[\log(2/\delta)]^{2A} \left(\delta^d\sum_j a_j^q\right)^{1/q}. \end{equation} The constant is independent of the number of tubes, their centers, and their relative arrangement.
Proof: Take a small fixed $c>0$ and disjoint spherical caps $C_j=\{\omega:|\omega-\omega_j|<c\delta\}$. Choose functions \[ h_j(\omega)=e^{-\mathrm i\delta^{-2}x_j\cdot\omega} \mathbf 1_{C_j}(\omega). \] On $Y\in T_j$, one can write $Y-x_j=t\omega_j+v$ with $|t|\le1/2$ and $|v|<2\delta$, allowing an inessential adjustment near the round tube ends. For $\omega\in C_j$, subtract the phase at $\omega_j$: \[ \delta^{-2}(Y-x_j)\cdot(\omega-\omega_j) =\delta^{-2}\left(-\frac t2|\omega-\omega_j|^2 +v\cdot(\omega-\omega_j)\right). \] Its absolute value is at most $C(c^2+c)$. Choosing $c$ sufficiently small puts every phase in an arc of length less than (say) $\pi/4$. There is therefore no destructive cancellation across the cap: \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-61-70-6c-6f-77-65-72} |Eh_j(\delta^{-2}Y)|\ge c_n\sigma(C_j) \asymp_n\delta^d,\qquad Y\in T_j. \end{equation} This is the only use of the tube's spatial geometry in the lower bound.

First collect the indices $j$ whose centers lie in one unit lattice cube $Q$; denote this collection $J_Q$. All their tubes are contained in a fixed enlargement $5Q$. Give each $j\in J_Q$ an independent random sign $\epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}$ and set \[ g_\epsilon=\sum_{j\in J_Q}\epsilon_j a_j^{1/2}h_j. \] Disjointness of the caps and $p=2q$ show \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-4c-70} \|g_\epsilon\|_{L^p(S^{n-1})}^p =\sum_{j\in J_Q}a_j^q\sigma(C_j) \lesssim\delta^d\sum_{j\in J_Q}a_j^q. \end{equation} At each fixed $Y$, independence of signs gives \[ \mathbb E|Eg_\epsilon(\delta^{-2}Y)|^2 =\sum_{j\in J_Q}a_j|Eh_j(\delta^{-2}Y)|^2. \] Convexity of $u\mapsto u^q$ for $q\ge1$ and (eq:caplower) imply \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-72-61-6e-64-6f-6d-6c-6f-77-65-72} \mathbb E|Eg_\epsilon(\delta^{-2}Y)|^p \ge c\,\delta^{dp}\left(\sum_{j\in J_Q} a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}(Y)\right)^q. \end{equation} Apply Proposition Proposition (Local extension at the critical exponent) on a ball containing $\delta^{-2}(5Q)$. Its radius is $O_n(\delta^{-2})$; its center can be arbitrary because (eq:localext) is uniform. Changing variables back from $\delta^{-2}Y$ and combining (eq:gLp)--(eq:randomlower) gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-63-61-6c-74-75-62-65-73} \delta^{dp-2n}\int_{5Q} \left(\sum_{j\in J_Q}a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}\right)^q\,\mathrm d Y \le C[\log(2/\delta)]^{Ap}\delta^d\sum_{j\in J_Q}a_j^q. \end{equation} The factor on the left is $1$ by $dp=2n$.

Finally sum over unit cubes $Q$. At a given point only $O_n(1)$ of the enlarged cubes $5Q$ can contribute, so the $q$-th power of the whole tube sum is at most a dimensional constant times the sum of its cube-group $q$-th powers. Thus \[ \int\left(\sum_j a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}\right)^q \le C[\log(2/\delta)]^{Ap}\delta^d\sum_j a_j^q. \] Taking a $q$-th root and noting $Ap/q=2A$ proves (eq:overlap). ∎

The Kakeya maximal estimate by duality

Define \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6d-61-78-69-6d-61-6c} K_\delta f(\omega)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^n} \frac1{|T_\delta(x,\omega)|} \int_{T_\delta(x,\omega)}|f(y)|\,\mathrm d y. \end{equation} The volume of the tube is independent of the center and direction. For $f\in L^n$, the numerator depends continuously on $(x,\omega)$ by Holder duality and continuity of tube indicators under translations and rotations in $L^{n/(n-1)}$. Thus the supremum may be taken over a countable dense set of centers and defines a measurable function of $\omega$; there is no measurability assumption on a selection of witnessing Kakeya segments.
Theorem (Logarithmic Kakeya maximal estimate, source deduction): For every $n\ge2$ and $0<\delta\le1$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6b-6d-61-78} \boxed{\|K_\delta f\|_{L^n(S^{n-1})} \le C_n[\log(2/\delta)]^{2A}\|f\|_{L^n(\mathbb R^n)}, \qquad 2A=\frac{2(n-1)}n.} \end{equation} Consequently, for each $\varepsilon>0$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-65-70-73-6d-61-78} \|K_\delta f\|_{L^n(S^{n-1})} \le C_{n,\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\|f\|_n. \end{equation}
Proof: For $\delta\le1/2$, choose a maximal $\delta$-separated net $\{\omega_j\}$ in $S^{n-1}$. The $O(\delta)$-caps around these points cover the sphere with bounded multiplicity and measure $\asymp\delta^d$ each. A tube in direction $\omega$ within $\delta$ of $\omega_j$ is contained in a tube of radius $2\delta$ in direction $\omega_j$ with the same center. The ratio of the two tube volumes is bounded. Hence \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-69-73-63-72-65-74-65} \|K_\delta f\|_{L^n(S^{n-1})} \lesssim\left(\delta^d\sum_j K_{2\delta}f(\omega_j)^n\right)^{1/n}. \end{equation} To bound this weighted $\ell^n$ norm, dualize against $a_j\ge0$ with \[\left(\delta^d\sum_j a_j^q\right)^{1/q}=1, \qquad q=\frac n{n-1}.\] For an arbitrary choice of tube centers $x_j$, set $T_j=T_{2\delta}(x_j,\omega_j)$. Then, since $|T_j|\asymp\delta^d$, Holder's inequality and Lemma Lemma (Weighted tube overlap) give \begin{align*} \delta^d\sum_j a_j\frac1{|T_j|}\int_{T_j}|f| &\lesssim\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f(y)|\sum_j a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}(y)\,\mathrm d y\\ &\le\|f\|_n\left\|\sum_j a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}\right\|_q\\ &\lesssim[\log(2/\delta)]^{2A}\|f\|_n. \end{align*} The bound is uniform in the centers. Taking suprema over centers, then over the dual coefficients $(a_j)$, yields the bound for (eq:discrete). For $1/2\le\delta\le1$, Holder on a single tube gives $K_\delta f(\omega)\le C_n\|f\|_n$, because tube volumes are bounded below. This completes (eq:kmax). Finally $[\log(2/\delta)]^{2A}\le C_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}$ proves (eq:epsmax). ∎

Why a neighborhood-volume estimate is not enough

Let $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded Kakeya set and let $E_\delta=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,E)<\delta\}$. Every chosen unit segment lies in $E$, hence its entire open radius-$\delta$ tube lies in $E_\delta$. Therefore $K_\delta\mathbf 1_{E_\delta}(\omega)\ge1$ for every $\omega\in S^{n-1}$. Theorem Theorem (Logarithmic Kakeya maximal estimate, source deduction) gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-76-6f-6c-75-6d-65} |E_\delta|\ge c_n[\log(2/\delta)]^{-2An} =c_n[\log(2/\delta)]^{-2(n-1)}. \end{equation} Thus the upper and lower Minkowski dimensions of a bounded Kakeya set are $n$. But this conclusion alone does not prove the Hausdorff dimension assertion in Math-95: covers defining Hausdorff measure can use vastly different radii. The key remaining step is to allocate different pieces of each witnessing segment to possibly different scales and combine the maximal bounds over all those scales.

The essential multiscale Hausdorff-dimension argument

The next proposition supplies the conclusion required by Math-95. In fact, the logarithmic bound yields a stronger quantitative gauge statement. We spell out the arbitrary-cover argument to avoid silently replacing Hausdorff dimension by Minkowski dimension.
Theorem (Full Hausdorff dimension from logarithmic maximal bounds): Assume (eq:kmax), with any fixed $A\ge0$ in place of $d/n$. For any set $E\subset\mathbb R^n$ containing a unit segment in every direction, one has $\dim_{\mathrm H} E=n$. More precisely, for each $\gamma>n-1$ define, for sufficiently small $r>0$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-61-75-67-65} h_\gamma(r)=r^n[\log(1/r)]^{2An} [\log\log(1/r)]^{\gamma}. \end{equation} Then $E$ has infinite Hausdorff measure for this gauge.
Proof: It suffices to prove the assertion for a compact Kakeya set (as required by Math-95); the same proof applies to arbitrary $E$ by taking covers of its witnessing segments and does not use compactness at any analytic step. Fix a large integer $m$ and consider any countable open-ball cover \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-76-65-72} E\subset\bigcup_j B(x_j,r_j),\qquad 0<r_j\le e^{-2^m}. \end{equation} If its $h_\gamma$ cost is infinite there is nothing to show. Otherwise partition the indices into \[ J_k=\{j:2^k\le\log(1/r_j)<2^{k+1}\},\quad k\ge m. \] Put \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-6b} \delta_k=e^{-2^{k+1}},\quad U_k=\bigcup_{j\in J_k}B(x_j,r_j),\quad G_k=\bigcup_{j\in J_k}B(x_j,2r_j),\quad v_k=\sum_{j\in J_k}r_j^n. \end{equation} For $j\in J_k$, we have $\delta_k\le r_j\le e^{-2^k}$ and $|G_k|\le C_nv_k$. The factor two in $G_k$ has a geometric purpose: a radius-$\delta_k$ transverse disk centered at a point inside $B(x_j,r_j)$ remains in $B(x_j,2r_j)$, since $\delta_k\le r_j$.

Fix a direction $\omega$, and choose for this argument only a unit segment $\ell_\omega\subset E$ parallel to $\omega$. Write $\lambda_k(\omega)$ for the one-dimensional length of $\ell_\omega\cap U_k$. Since the balls cover the entire segment, countable subadditivity implies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-65-6e-67-74-68} 1\le\sum_{k\ge m}\lambda_k(\omega). \end{equation} For a point on $\ell_\omega\cap U_k$, its transverse $\delta_k$-disk lies in $G_k$. Integrating along the segment in tube coordinates gives \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-69-6e-64-69-63-61-74-6f-72} K_{\delta_k}\mathbf 1_{G_k}(\omega) \ge c_n\lambda_k(\omega). \end{equation} The right side is used only pointwise in this deduction: a measurable global choice of $\ell_\omega$ is not needed. Combining (eq:length)--(eq:indicator), for every $\omega\in S^{n-1}$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-6f-69-6e-74-77-69-73-65} 1\le C_n\sum_{k\ge m}K_{\delta_k}\mathbf 1_{G_k}(\omega). \end{equation} Take the $L^n(S^{n-1})$ norm. Minkowski's inequality and the assumed maximal estimate yield \begin{align} |S^{n-1}|^{1/n} &\le C_n\sum_{k\ge m}\left\|K_{\delta_k} \mathbf 1_{G_k}\right\|_n\notag\\ &\le C_n\sum_{k\ge m} [\log(2/\delta_k)]^{2A}|G_k|^{1/n}\notag\\ &\le C_n\sum_{k\ge m}2^{2Ak}v_k^{1/n}. \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-73-74} \end{align} At any $r_j$ in the $k$th group, $\log(1/r_j)\asymp2^k$ and $\log\log(1/r_j)\asymp k$, with constants independent of $k$. Consequently the cost of the cover in (eq:gauge) satisfies \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-63-6f-73-74-63-6f-6d-70-61-72-65} \sum_jh_\gamma(r_j) \asymp_{n,A,\gamma}\sum_{k\ge m} 2^{2Ank}k^{\gamma}v_k. \end{equation} Write $b_k=2^{2Ank}k^\gamma v_k$. Holder's inequality with exponents $n$ and $n/(n-1)$ gives \begin{align} \sum_{k\ge m}2^{2Ak}v_k^{1/n} &=\sum_{k\ge m}b_k^{1/n}k^{-\gamma/n}\notag\\ &\le\left(\sum_{k\ge m}b_k\right)^{1/n} \left(\sum_{k\ge m}k^{-\gamma/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}. \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-68-6f-6c-64-65-72} \end{align} Since $\gamma>n-1$, the tail of the series on the right tends to zero as $m\to\infty$. The positive constant on the left of (eq:cost) therefore forces \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-6c-6f-77-65-72-63-6f-73-74} \sum_jh_\gamma(r_j) \ge c_{n,A,\gamma} \left(\sum_{k\ge m}k^{-\gamma/(n-1)}\right)^{-(n-1)}, \end{equation} for every cover (eq:cover). The lower bound diverges as $m\to\infty$. The definition of Hausdorff gauge measure thus gives $\mathcal H^{h_\gamma}(E)=\infty$.

Finally, for each fixed $s<n$, $h_\gamma(r)/r^s\to0$ as $r\downarrow0$. Therefore the infinite $h_\gamma$-measure forces $\mathcal H^s(E)=\infty$, and hence $\dim_{\mathrm H} E\ge s$ for every $s<n$. The general upper bound $\dim_{\mathrm H} E\le n$ completes the proof. ∎

Proof: For each such $n$, apply successively Proposition Proposition (Local extension at the critical exponent), Lemma Lemma (Weighted tube overlap), Theorem Theorem (Logarithmic Kakeya maximal estimate, source deduction), and Theorem Theorem (Full Hausdorff dimension from logarithmic maximal bounds). Sharpness context and exactly what is (not) claimed

The logarithmic maximal inequality (eq:kmax) has exponent $2A=2(n-1)/n$. The source explains an obstruction to lowering this exponent too far. Consider \[ f_\delta(x)=|x|^{-1}\mathbf 1_{\{2\delta<|x|<1/2\}}(x). \] For $0<\delta\ll1$, \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-73-68-61-72-70-4c-70} \|f_\delta\|_n^n\asymp\int_{2\delta}^{1/2}\frac{\,\mathrm d r}{r} \asymp\log(1/\delta). \end{equation} Every unit $\delta$-tube centered at the origin has average at least $c\log(1/\delta)$: integrate along the central line at distances from $4\delta$ to $1/4$, and over a fixed fraction of the transverse disk. Consequently \[ \frac{\|K_\delta f_\delta\|_n}{\|f_\delta\|_n} \gtrsim[\log(1/\delta)]^{1-1/n}. \] Thus any universal logarithmic Kakeya maximal upper bound must have power at least $1-1/n=A$. The available upper power is $2A$. An improvement in this logarithmic power is a separate quantitative issue: it is not a gap in the proof of full Hausdorff dimension for Math-95.

The following scope distinctions matter:

  1. The proof yields full Hausdorff dimension, not positive Lebesgue measure. Even the volume estimate (eq:volume) tends to zero with $\delta$.

Its published formalization summary separately describes a Kakeya maximal bound with arbitrary $\delta^{-\varepsilon}$ loss and the Hausdorff-dimension conclusion. This report does not claim that the sharper logarithmic numerical power was itself one of the formal comparator targets.
  3. The four-dimensional-only earlier manuscript is superseded for the scope of the catalogue question, but remains a distinct argument and a prior contribution. Nothing in this note retracts its authorship or its reported four-dimensional result.
  4. The deduction does not resolve a demand for zero Lebesgue measure, a lower bound on $\mathcal H^n(E)$, an endpoint Fourier restriction bound, or a variable-coefficient/curved-space Kakeya conjecture; those are mathematically different assertions.
