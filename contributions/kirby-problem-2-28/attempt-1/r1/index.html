Write-up

Purely pseudo-Anosov embeddings for every free right-angled Artin group

**Problem:** TOP-2776

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For every finite edgeless graph Γ, its RAAG embeds in a closed surface mapping class group so that every nonidentity element is pseudo-Anosov; this verifies the simultaneous loxodromic requirement for all free RAAGs.

One embedding for all the elements

Let $\Gamma$ be edgeless with $r\ge2$ vertices. Then $A_\Gamma=F_r$. No induced subgraph is a nontrivial join: a join with two nonempty factors has an edge between them. Hence every nonidentity element is loxodromic according to the exact algebraic definition in the prompt.

There exists an embedding $F_r\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(S)$, for a closed oriented surface of genus at least two, whose entire nonidentity image consists of pseudo-Anosov mapping classes. This gives the required single embedding simultaneously for the infinitely many loxodromic elements. It is stronger than asking only for the selected free generators to be pseudo-Anosov.

The Schottky input

Farb and Mosher define a Schottky subgroup to be a free convex cocompact subgroup of a surface mapping class group. Their Theorem1.4 constructs such groups from sufficiently high powers of pairwise independent pseudo-Anosovs. Proposition3.1 implies that every infinite-order element of a convex cocompact subgroup is pseudo-Anosov. In a free group all nonidentity elements have infinite order. These statements therefore supply a purely pseudo-Anosov embedded $F_2$.

The independence requirement is not replaced by the weaker assertion that the elements are different: independent pseudo-Anosovs have disjoint pairs of fixed points in projective measured foliation space. The existence/high-powers result is the substantive geometric input imported from the source. Two unrelated pseudo-Anosov generator images, without its theorem, would not control arbitrary mixed words.

Obtain every finite rank without losing the property

Here is an elementary deduction giving every $r\ge2$ from that same $F_2=\langle x,y\rangle$. Set $d=r-1$ and let

$$H_d=\ker(F_2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z),\qquad x\mapsto1,\ y\mapsto0.$$

For $d=1$ interpret the target as the trivial group. A Schreier transversal $1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}$ gives the free basis

$$x^d,\quad x^j yx^{-j}\quad(0\le j<d).$$

To verify its rank and freeness geometrically, take the $d$-sheeted cover of the rose with two oriented loops: the $x$ edges make a $d$-cycle and there is one $y$ loop at each vertex. A spanning tree consists of $d-1$ consecutive $x$ edges, leaving $d+1=r$ edges. Collapsing the tree gives exactly the displayed free basis and no relators. Thus $H_d\cong F_r$.

Restrict the embedding of $F_2$ to $H_d$. Restriction remains injective, and every nonidentity image remains pseudo-Anosov because it was already nonidentity in the original Schottky group. This proves the desired assertion in every finite rank on the same closed surface. For the optional one-vertex graph, an infinite cyclic subgroup generated by one pseudo-Anosov handles rank one as well.

For graphs with edges, some elements lie in join subgroups and are exempt from the condition, while other mixed words must still be controlled. The free-group argument does not provide those embeddings. In particular, the faithful Dehn-twist embeddings of arbitrary RAAGs have reducible vertex images and cannot simply be declared to meet this additional loxodromic requirement.
