Locally, on a one-dimensional configuration space, $(a^w(x,hD)u)(x)=(2\pi h)^{-1}\int e^{i(x-y)\xi/h}a((x+y)/2,\xi)u(y)\,dy\,d\xi$. Here $D=-i\partial_x$. For a complex symbol the operator need not be self-adjoint and its eigenvalues need not be real.

A one-degree-of-freedom circle model.
On periodic functions on $[0,2\pi]$, consider $P_h=hD+iv(x)$, where $D=-i\partial_x$ and $v$ is real analytic and periodic. The eigenvalue equation is \[u'(x)=h^{-1}(v(x)+iz)u(x),\quad u(x)=\exp\left(h^{-1}\int_0^xv(s)ds+izx/h\right). \] Here the displayed $u$ denotes the solution with initial value one. Periodicity requires $\exp(2\pi\bar v/h+2\pi iz/h)=1$, with $\bar v=(2\pi)^{-1}\int v$. Thus all eigenvalues are exactly $z_k=hk+i\bar v$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. The first-order scalar ODE also shows that there are no other eigenvalues. They lie on a horizontal line although the symbol $\xi+iv(x)$ can take values in a two-dimensional strip.
Similarity and sensitivity.
Define $\phi(x)=\int_0^x(v(s)-\bar v)ds$, a periodic function. Multiplication $S_hf=e^{\phi/h}f$ gives $S_h^{-1}P_hS_h=hD+i\bar v$. For each fixed $h$ this is a bounded invertible similarity, but $\|S_h\|\|S_h^{-1}\|=e^{(\max\phi-\min\phi)/h}$. Thus exact curve localization can coexist with exponential sensitivity; a bounded-condition-number comparison cannot be assumed in the semiclassical limit.
