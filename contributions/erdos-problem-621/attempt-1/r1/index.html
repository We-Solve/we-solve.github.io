Erdős Problem 621: triangle-independent edges and triangle covers

Statement and stronger target

Let $\alpha_1(G)$ be the largest size of an edge set containing at most one edge from each triangle of an $n$-vertex graph $G$, and let $\tau_1(G)$ be the fewest edges meeting every triangle. We prove \[ \alpha_1(G)+\tau_1(G)\le n^2/4. \] The proof reconstructs Norin--Sun's stronger cut inequality. Fix a triangle-independent edge set $S$, put $C=E(G)\setminus S$, and construct a vertex bipartition with at most $n^2/4-|S|$ internal edges. Deleting those edges makes the graph bipartite, and therefore triangle-free.

1. Four-vertex sums

Let $s_{uv}$ and $c_{uv}$ be the indicator functions of $S$ and $C$ on ordered pairs, and put $q_{uv}=1-s_{uv}-c_{uv}$, including $q_{uu}=1$. Two $S$-edges with a common endpoint force their other endpoints to be nonadjacent in $G$: \[ s_{uv}s_{uw}=s_{uv}s_{uw}q_{vw}. \tag{1} \] All sums below are over ordered vertices, with repetitions allowed. Define \begin{align*} P&=\sum_{u,v,w,x}s_{uv}s_{vw}s_{wx}(1-s_{xu}),& Q&=\sum_{u,v,w,x}s_{uv}s_{vw}s_{wx}s_{xu},\\ K&=\sum_{u,v,w,x}s_{uv}s_{uw}s_{ux},& D&=\sum_{u,v,w,x}(1-s_{uv})s_{uw}s_{ux}q_{vw}q_{vx},\\ R&=\sum_{u,v,w,x}s_{uv}s_{uw}q_{wx}c_{vx}. \end{align*} Two inequalities will be used: \[ P+Q\le K,\qquad P\le D. \tag{2} \] For the first, with $d_u=\sum_ws_{uw}$, \[ 0\le\sum_{u,v}s_{uv}(d_u-d_v)^2=2K-2(P+Q). \] For the second put $a_{uv}=\sum_ws_{uw}q_{vw}$. Symmetry gives \[ D=\sum_{u,v}(1-s_{uv})a_{uv}^2 \ge\sum_{u,v}(1-s_{uv})a_{uv}a_{vu}. \] In the last sum retain only those terms with $s_{wx}=1$ after expanding the two factors. They total $P$: the path $u,w,x,v$ forces $q_{vw}=q_{ux}=1$ by $1$. This proves $2$.

2. The required identity, with its expansion

In any sum carrying $s_{uv}$ set $A_w=s_{uw}+s_{vw}$. By $1$, $A_w$ is either zero or one. Put \[ U=\sum s_{uv}A_w,\quad X=\sum s_{uv}c_{wx}A_w(1-A_x),\quad Y=\tfrac12\sum s_{uv}(1-A_w)(1-A_x), \] where each sum includes all four indices. Expanding $Y$ gives \[ Y=n^2|S|-U+P+Q+K. \tag{3} \] We also have \[ U-X=4P+2Q+2K+2D+2R. \tag{4} \] Here are details to make this algebra checkable. Substitute $1-c_{wx}(1-A_x)=q_{wx}+s_{wx}+c_{wx}A_x$ and use the symmetry between $u,v$. The part containing $s_{wx}$ is $2(P+Q)$. In the remaining part the product $s_{uw}c_{wx}s_{ux}$ vanishes by $1$, leaving \[ 2\sum s_{uv}s_{uw}(q_{wx}+c_{wx}s_{vx}). \] Split this by $s_{vx}+q_{vx}+c_{vx}=1$. The three contributions are respectively $2P$, $2(K+D)$, and $2R$. For the middle contribution, split $\sum s_{uv}s_{uw}q_{vx}q_{wx}$ according as $s_{ux}=1$ or zero; these are $K$ and $D$, after relabelling indices. This proves $4$. Equations $3$--$4$ imply \[ X+Y=n^2|S|-3P-Q-K-2D-2R. \tag{5} \]

3. Recursive random cut

If $S$ is empty, independently placing every vertex in either side gives expected internal-edge count $|C|/2\le n^2/4$. Otherwise choose a uniformly random ordered edge $uv$ of $S$. Place $A'=N_S(u)$ on one side and $B'=N_S(v)$ on the other. These are disjoint independent sets in $G$ by $1$. On $Z=V(G)\setminus(A'\cup B')$, recursively apply the same procedure to the induced graph, independently reversing its two sides with probability one half. This extra reversal makes each remaining vertex's side unbiased. Inductively, if $I$ is the final number of internal edges, then conditionally on the first edge, \[ \mathbb E_{uv}I+|S| \le\tfrac12e(A'\cup B',Z)+\tfrac14|Z|^2 +s(A',B')+s(A'\cup B',Z). \tag{6} \] Indeed, the recursion contributes at most $|Z|^2/4-s(Z)$, boundary edges contribute one half each, and there are no internal edges inside $A'$ or $B'$. Decomposing $|S|$ into its three possible locations gives $6$. Twice the right side of $6$ equals $\sum_{w,x}f(u,v,w,x)$, where \[ f=s_{uv}\left[(3s_{wx}+c_{wx})A_w(1-A_x) +\tfrac12(1-A_w)(1-A_x) +2s_{wx}s_{uw}s_{vx}\right]. \] There are two further immediate path-count identities: \[ \sum s_{uv}s_{wx}A_w(1-A_x)=2P, \qquad \sum s_{uv}s_{wx}s_{uw}s_{vx}=Q. \] For the first, its two terms describe an $S$-path whose endpoints are not joined by an $S$-edge; the other excluded adjacency is automatically zero by $1$. The second counts an ordered $S$-four-cycle. Thus $5$ gives \begin{align*} F:=\sum f &=n^2|S|+3P+Q-K-2D-2R\\ &\le n^2|S|+(P+Q-K)+2(P-D) \le n^2|S|. \end{align*} Averaging $6$ over the $2|S|$ ordered choices of $uv$ yields \[ \mathbb EI+|S|\le F/(4|S|)\le n^2/4. \] Some resulting cut achieves this bound. Take $|S|=\alpha_1(G)$ and delete its internal edges. Every triangle has an internal edge in any bipartition, so \[ \boxed{\alpha_1(G)+\tau_1(G)\le n^2/4.} \]

Checks and sharpness

Balanced complete bipartite graphs attain equality: every edge can belong to $S$, there are no triangles to cover, and there are $n^2/4$ edges for even $n$. The ordered-pair convention accounts for the factor $4|S|$ in the averaging step. Repeated vertices were retained throughout the algebra; discarding them would spoil its exact form.

Status: solved. This is a full proof reconstruction of the known Norin--Sun inequality; no computer algebra or unproved four-vertex inequality is required as an external input.
