Reflection-Positive Two-Dimensional Quantum Field Theories with Boundary

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.

A von Neumann algebra is a weakly closed unital $*$-algebra of bounded operators. $O(2)$ describes rotations and reflections in two dimensions; a generalized action can involve higher or categorical data. Reflection positivity encodes positivity after a Euclidean reflection. The proposed flat ultraviolet setting has a vanishing energy--momentum tensor and product structures near the chosen boundary, together with reflection positivity and remote detectability. A bimodule carries commuting left and right algebra actions. The spaces $L^p(A)$ used for boundary angles need not be invertible bimodules.

Further primary-source questions.
Formulate the required noninvertible generalized $O(2)$ action. Determine what excludes concave boundary angles when the source's expression $L^{2\pi/\theta}(A)$ only makes sense for positive convex angles $\theta$ in its construction.
Catalogue wording retained for traceability.
Identify the generalized $O(2)$-action on algebras whose fixed points are von Neumann algebras. Classify reflection-positive, remote-detectable, flat-space Lorentz-invariant two-dimensional quantum field theories with boundary: are von Neumann algebras the complete list, or do other examples exist?
Scope and source qualification.
The fixed-point claim and field-theory classification are both retained. The classification concerns the proposed flat ultraviolet and boundary axioms above; it is not a classification of arbitrary Lorentz-invariant two-dimensional quantum field theories.

A finite-algebra reflection test.
For $A=M_n(\mathbb C)$, use the Hilbert space of matrices with $\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(x^*y)$. Left and right multiplication commute. The antilinear map $Jx=x^*$ satisfies $J^2=I$ and \[ J L_a J(x)=xa^*=R_{a^*}(x),\qquad \langle x,x\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(x^*x)\geq0. \] Thus a reflection exchanges the two boundary actions and gives a positive pairing in this finite model. This elementary structure is consistent with the von Neumann-algebra examples but is not the full field-theory reflection-positivity axiom.
Why invertible actions are too restrictive.
Even for $A=\mathbb C$, the bimodule $H=\mathbb C^n$, $n>1$, cannot have a finite-dimensional tensor inverse: $\dim(H\otimes K)=n\dim K\ne1$ for every nonzero finite-dimensional $K$. Thus replacing the source noninvertible generalized action by an ordinary group action through invertible bimodules discards basic candidates. Similarly the expression $p=2\pi/\theta$ requires an actual positive angle in the stipulated construction; formally assigning negative $p$ would fail on a nonzero matrix projection with zero eigenvalues and cannot define its usual Schatten norm.
