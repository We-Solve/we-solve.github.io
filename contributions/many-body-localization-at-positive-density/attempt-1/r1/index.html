Many-Body Localization at Positive Density

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.

Definitions and conventions.
A random interacting quantum many-body system at positive density has particle number $N_\Lambda$ in a finite region $\Lambda$ with $N_\Lambda/|\Lambda|\to\rho>0$ in the thermodynamic limit, or the analogous finite-energy-density spin-system description. Localization can mean spectral localization, suppressed transport, or suitable dynamical correlation bounds; these notions need to be distinguished. Stability under interactions asks whether an appropriate localized regime survives adding nonzero couplings between particles.

An exactly localized interacting benchmark.
On a finite spin chain let $n_i=(I-Z_i)/2$ and \[ H=\sum_i h_i n_i+\sum_iJ_i n_in_{i+1}, \] with arbitrary real coefficients. Every term is diagonal in the occupation basis, so $[H,n_i]=0$ and $n_i(t)=n_i$ for all time. All product occupation states are eigenvectors, including configurations with any prescribed positive limiting density. The interactions $J_i$ can be nonzero, but there is identically no particle transport because each local occupation is separately conserved.
Why perturbative stability is not automatic.
Add a hopping or spin-flip term of strength $\epsilon$. In a two-configuration subspace with diagonal detuning $\Delta$, the matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\epsilon\\\epsilon&\Delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has mixing angle satisfying $\tan(2\theta)=2\epsilon/\Delta$. At $\Delta=0$, arbitrarily small $\epsilon$ produces equal-weight eigenvectors. Therefore a global eigenvector perturbation bound cannot depend only on the operator strength; it must handle small denominators. At positive density the number of competing configurations grows rapidly with volume, so a fixed-particle estimate supplies no uniform control.
Original-target gap.
The diagonal benchmark is an integrable frozen model, not proof of a robust many-body localized phase. It does not determine which spectral, transport or dynamical definition is stable in an infinite system. The first missing step is a volume-uniform treatment of resonant regions under genuinely transport-generating interactions. No universal disorder threshold or stability for all interactions is inferred from the exact commuting example.
