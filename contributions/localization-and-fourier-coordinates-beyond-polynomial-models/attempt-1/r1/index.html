Research attempt: Denominator domains and positivity in Fourier coordinates

First consider the rational equation $y/x=0$ on its specified domain $x\ne0$ in $\mathbb C^2$. Its solutions are $(x,0)$ with $x\ne0$. Clearing denominators gives $y=0$, which adds $(0,0)$ unless the domain condition is kept. More generally, on $x\ne0$ the equation $xy=0$ is equivalent to $y=0$, while in the polynomial ring the first zero set includes the entire line $x=0$. The saturation calculation here is exact: \[ (xy):x^\infty=(y). \] Indeed $x y\in(xy)$ proves one inclusion. Conversely if $x^k f\in(xy)$, substitution $y=0$ gives $x^k f(x,0)=0$ in the integral domain $\mathbb Q[x]$, so $f(x,0)=0$. Expansion in powers of $y$ then shows $y\mid f$. Even saturation describes the closure, and the condition $x\ne0$ is still needed to describe the original rational domain.

For a Fourier example use the four-point group $G=\{-1,1\}^2$ with coordinatewise multiplication. For a probability mass function define $a=E S_1$, $b=E S_2$, $c=E S_1S_2$. The inverse formula is \[ p(s_1,s_2)=\tfrac14(1+a s_1+b s_2+c s_1s_2). \] To verify it, sum the right-hand side against each of $1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2$; every nonconstant sign character sums to zero and each character squared sums to four. Those four moment equations determine four masses because the character matrix times its transpose is $4I$. Thus the precise probability region in these coordinates is the four inequalities \[ 1+a s_1+b s_2+c s_1s_2\ge0\quad(s_1,s_2\in\{-1,1\}). \] The separate necessary bounds $|a|,|b|,|c|\le1$ are insufficient: $(a,b,c)=(9/10,9/10,-9/10)$ gives $p(-1,-1)=-17/40$.

There is nevertheless a genuine algebraic simplification. For independent group-valued $S,T$, each character satisfies $\chi(ST)=\chi(S)\chi(T)$, hence $E\chi(ST)=E\chi(S)E\chi(T)$. Convolution therefore becomes coordinatewise multiplication of the three Fourier moments. The failed extension route is to use that simple multiplication law and discard the coupled positivity inequalities; the displayed triple falsifies it.

Verification and adversarial audit

The Fourier masses sum to one for every triple; the counterexample fails only positivity. Its negative mass is $(1-9/10-9/10-9/10)/4=-17/40$. The rational example distinguishes localization, closure, and actual points in the domain.
