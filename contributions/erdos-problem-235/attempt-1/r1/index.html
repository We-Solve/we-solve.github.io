Erdős Problem 235: gaps between reduced residues of a primorial

1) Statement and scope

Let $N=\prod_{j\leq k}p_j$, and list its $\phi(N)$ reduced residues in $[1,N)$ in increasing order. The question asks for a continuous limiting distribution of successive gaps divided by $N/\phi(N)$. Hooley proved that the limit is $1-e^{-c}$ at $c\geq0$. Here we derive exact finite formulas, tightness, and disappearance of bounded unscaled gaps. The exponential limit is unresolved in this attempt.

2) Cyclic normalization

Write $a_{\phi+1}=a_1+N$ and $d_i=a_{i+1}-a_i$ for $1\leq i\leq\phi=\phi(N)$. Then \[ \sum_i d_i=N,\qquad \frac1\phi\sum_i\frac{d_i}{N/\phi}=1. \] For each $c>0$, Markov's inequality gives \[ \frac1\phi\#\{i:d_i>cN/\phi\}\leq\frac1c. \] Thus the normalized cyclic gap laws are tight. The empirical distribution in the problem omits just the wraparound gap and uses the same denominator $\phi$, so it differs from the cyclic one by at most $1/\phi$, which tends to zero.

3) An exact formula for the zero count

Choose a reduced residue $a$ uniformly modulo $N$. For $0\leq H<N$ put \[ X_H(a)=\sum_{h=1}^H1_{\gcd(a+h,N)=1}. \] The next cyclic gap exceeds $H$ if and only if $X_H(a)=0$. For a subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,H\}$, let $\nu_p(S)$ denote the number of distinct residues represented by $\{0\}\cup S$ modulo $p$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives \[ \mathbb P\left(\gcd(a+h,N)=1\text{ for every }h\in S\right) =\frac{\prod_{p\mid N}(p-\nu_p(S))}{\phi(N)}. \] Consequently, for each integer $r\geq0$, \[ \mathbb E\binom{X_H}{r} =\frac1{\phi(N)} \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[1,H]\\|S|=r}} \prod_{p\mid N}(p-\nu_p(S)), \] and inclusion--exclusion gives the exact identity \[ \mathbb P(X_H=0) =\sum_{r=0}^H(-1)^r\mathbb E\binom{X_H}{r}. \] No independence of the different shifts has been assumed.

4) Fixed, unscaled gaps disappear

Fix $h\geq1$. If $p\mid h$, its factor in the conditional probability $\mathbb P(\gcd(a+h,N)=1)$ is one; otherwise its factor is $(p-2)/(p-1)$. In particular the probability is zero for odd $h$, once $2\mid N$. For any fixed even $h$, all primes greater than $h$ contribute $(p-2)/(p-1)$, and \[ \prod_{h<p\leq p_k}\left(1-\frac1{p-1}\right)\longrightarrow0 \] by divergence of the sum of prime reciprocals. The union bound over $1\leq h\leq H$ therefore proves $\mathbb P(d_i\leq H)\to0$ for every fixed $H$. This is consistent with the mean scale $N/\phi(N)\to\infty$.

5) The precise missing estimate

For fixed $c>0$, take $H=\lfloor cN/\phi(N)\rfloor$. It would suffice to prove, for every fixed $r$, \[ \mathbb E\binom{X_H}{r}\longrightarrow\frac{c^r}{r!}. \] Indeed the odd and even Bonferroni partial sums then sandwich $\mathbb P(X_H=0)$ between the corresponding partial sums for $e^{-c}$. Letting the truncation order tend to infinity gives the desired limit. The case $c=0$ is immediate because all gaps are positive. The displayed moment asymptotic is not proved here; neither the exact mean nor tightness can replace it.

Sources: https://www.erdosproblems.com/235; C. Hooley, On the difference between consecutive numbers prime to $n$. II, Publ. Math. Debrecen 12 $1965$, 39--49. The historical resolution is credited to Hooley; no new resolution or formal verification is claimed.

6) Completion estimate

The CRT formulas and limiting reductions are proved, but their essential averaged asymptotic at the growing gap scale is missing. COMPLETION: 30%
