Polynomial-Time Algorithms for High-Dimensional Private Queries

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.

A learning problem specifies a data distribution, a hypothesis class and a loss. Statistical consistency means that the estimator approaches its population target as the sample size increases; statistical and computational requirements are distinct.

An $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differentially private randomized mechanism satisfies $\Pr[M(D)\in E]\leq e^\varepsilon\Pr[M(D^{\prime})\in E]+\delta$ for every event $E$ and databases differing in one record. A statistical query asks for the population expectation of a bounded function.

An explicit-query mechanism and its sample-cost bottleneck

The report's hard regime involves very many queries with high accuracy and high-dimensional data, and it already presents more advanced methods. We test the elementary independent-noise route. Let $n\ge1$ and $Q\ge1$ be integers. Let $D=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, with replacement of one row as adjacency, and let $q_1,\ldots,q_Q$ be a fixed explicit list of functions into $[0,1]$. Define empirical answers $v_j=n^{-1}\sum_iq_j(x_i)$. For $\varepsilon>0$, release $v_j+Z_j$ with independent Laplace noises of scale $b=Q/(n\varepsilon)$, whose density is $(2b)^{-1}e^{-|z|/b}$.
Proposition: This mechanism is pure $\varepsilon$-differentially private. With probability at least $1-\beta$, all its empirical-answer errors are at most $\alpha$, provided \[ n\ge\frac{Q\log(Q/\beta)}{\alpha\varepsilon},\qquad 0<\beta<1,\ \alpha>0. \]
Proof: Each coordinate changes by at most $1/n$ between neighboring databases, so $\|v(D)-v(D')\|_1\le Q/n$. At any possible output vector $y$, the ratio of its two joint densities is \[ \exp\left(\frac{\|y-v(D')\|_1-\|y-v(D)\|_1}{b}\right) \le\exp\left(\frac{\|v(D)-v(D')\|_1}{b}\right)\le e^\varepsilon, \] by the triangle inequality. Integrating over every measurable event proves privacy. Direct integration of the Laplace density gives $\Pr(|Z_j|>\alpha)=e^{-\alpha/b}$. A union bound gives total failure probability at most $Qe^{-\alpha n\varepsilon/Q}$, which is at most $\beta$ under the displayed condition. ∎
If one query evaluation costs at most $t_q$, computing the empirical answers takes $O(nQt_q)$ operations, plus $Q$ noise draws. This statement uses an ideal arithmetic model with exact Laplace sampling; finite-bit sampling and implementation are not established here. It is polynomial in an explicitly listed $Q$, but the sufficient sample bound is linear in $Q$, and merely writing $Q$ explicit answers also costs at least $Q$ output operations. Therefore this construction does not supply a polynomial-in-dimension procedure with the report's accuracy for a nearly exponential implicit query family.

Verification and remaining gaps

Sensitivity was exhaustively checked for a small binary data universe and a fixed list of Boolean queries; the noise scale and union bound were evaluated at stated numerical parameters. The guarantee concerns empirical answers. Converting it into a population guarantee requires a specified sampling model, and adaptively selected queries require a separate analysis. The attempted route stalls at its sample cost, explicit-output size and ideal sampling model, rather than proving the sought high-dimensional efficiency result.
