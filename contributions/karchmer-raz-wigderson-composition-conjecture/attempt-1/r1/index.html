Karchmer–Raz–Wigderson Composition Conjecture

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

For $f:\{0,1\}^a\to\{0,1\}$ and $g:\{0,1\}^b\to\{0,1\}$, block composition applies $g$ to each of $a$ disjoint blocks and then applies $f$ to the resulting bits. The standard depth measure uses fan-in-two AND/OR gates and NOT gates; it minimizes the longest gate path of a Boolean circuit computing the function.

A Boolean function maps $\{0,1\}^n$ to $\{0,1\}$. A Boolean circuit is an acyclic network of logical gates; a formula is a circuit with no sharing of intermediate gate outputs. Size and depth measure its number of gates and longest gate path.

Composition works exactly for conjunctions up to rounding

The source retains the qualitative phrase about lower-order terms; we do not assign it a new universal asymptotic meaning. Work with fan-in-two AND/OR and unary NOT gates, counting every gate on a longest input-output path. A single input variable has depth zero. Let $\operatorname{AND}_a$ be conjunction of $a\ge1$ inputs.
Proposition: For arbitrary Boolean functions, substitution of circuits gives $D(f\circ g)\le D(f)+D(g)$. For conjunctions, \[ D(\operatorname{AND}_a\circ\operatorname{AND}_b)=\lceil\log_2(ab)\rceil, \] which differs from $D(\operatorname{AND}_a)+D(\operatorname{AND}_b)$ by at most one.
Proof: Replace each input occurrence of a circuit for $f$ by a copy of a circuit for $g$ on its corresponding disjoint block. Every resulting path passes through at most $D(g)$ gates followed by at most $D(f)$, proving the upper bound. Any output at depth $h$ can depend on at most $2^h$ distinct input variables. This follows by induction: a binary gate takes the union of at most two sets each of size $2^{h-1}$; a unary NOT gate cannot increase the number. Conjunction of $r$ inputs depends on all $r$, because changing one input while the others are 1 changes the output. Its depth is thus at least $\lceil\log_2 r\rceil$. A balanced binary AND tree achieves that depth. Block composition of the two conjunctions is conjunction of all $ab$ variables. Finally, for real $x,y$, $\lceil x\rceil+\lceil y\rceil-1\le\lceil x+y\rceil\le\lceil x\rceil+\lceil y\rceil$, proving the rounding assertion. ∎
The exact loss can occur: $a=3,b=5$ gives depths 2 and 3 separately, but the composed conjunction has depth 4. This does not refute a conjecture allowing lower-order terms. The attempted general lower-bound route stalls because dependence on all $ab$ variables gives only $\log_2(ab)$, not a lower bound involving the actual potentially larger complexities $D(f)$ and $D(g)$. The substitution upper bound has no corresponding reverse-substitution proof here.

Verification and remaining gaps
