Adding a dynamics link can destroy controllability

Consider $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t$ with \[ A_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}. \] Only one previously zero dynamics entry is added; both matrices have eigenvalues $1/4,1/2$ and are stable.
Proposition: The first system is controllable in two steps, while the second is not controllable in any number of steps. Their two-step Gramian determinants are $1/16$ and zero, respectively.
Proof: The columns of $[B,A_0B]$ are $(1,1)^T,(1/4,1/2)^T$, so their determinant is $1/4$. Arbitrary target states from zero can be reached with two controls because this matrix (up to column order) is invertible. For $A_1$, one has $A_1B=B/2$, hence $A_1^tB=2^{-t}B$ for every $t\ge0$; the reachable space is only the line spanned by $B$. The two-step Gramian equals $CC^T$ with $C=[B,AB]$, so its determinant is $(\det C)^2$, as asserted. ∎

Where a monotonicity proof actually works

Keep $A$ fixed and add independent actuator columns, replacing $B$ by $[B,D]$. For any integer horizon $T\ge1$, \[ W_T([B,D])-W_T(B)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}A^tDD^T(A^T)^t\succeq0, \quad W_T(B)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}A^tBB^T(A^T)^t. \] Indeed each summand has quadratic form $\|D^T(A^T)^tv\|^2\ge0$. If $W_T(B)$ is positive definite, writing the new Gramian as $W_T(B)^{1/2}(I+K)W_T(B)^{1/2}$ with $K\succeq0$ proves that its determinant is at least the old determinant: every eigenvalue of $I+K$ is at least one. If the old Gramian is singular, its determinant is zero and the new one is nonnegative. This conclusion applies to genuinely additional unconstrained inputs; it does not justify changing $A$ or relaxing one constraint while changing disturbances.
