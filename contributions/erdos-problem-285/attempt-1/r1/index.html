FORMAL RESTATEMENT

Erdős #285; reconstruction using Martin's exact-length construction, 2026-09-24. For positive integers $k$ for which a representation exists, let \[ f(k)=\min\{\max E: E\subset\mathbb N,\ |E|=k,\ \sum_{n\in E}1/n=1\}. \] Prove that $f(k)\sim ek/(e-1)$ as $k\to\infty$.

QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK

Greg Martin proved the assertion in Denser Egyptian Fractions. The exact number of summands matters: an existence theorem with an unspecified number of denominators would not establish this statement. Below, Martin's uniform exact-length construction is the declared external input; the lower bound and all deductions are supplied. There is a harmless finite exception: $k=2$ admits no representation with distinct positive denominators.

OBJECTIVE AND ATTACK PLAN

First settle which lengths are feasible. Then compare any $k$ denominators with the $k$ largest integers below their maximum. Finally specialize Martin's construction to the rational target $1$ and verify its smoothness hypothesis.

WORK

Feasible lengths. The singleton $\{1\}$ represents $1$. If $1/u+1/v=1$ with $u<v$, then $(u-1)(v-1)=1$, forcing $u=v=2$, a contradiction. For three terms use $\{2,3,6\}$. From any representation with largest denominator $m\geq2$, replace $1/m$ by \[ \frac1{m+1}+\frac1{m(m+1)}. \tag{1} \] Both new denominators exceed $m$ and are distinct. Thus $1$ increases the number of distinct terms by exactly one, proving existence for every $k\geq3$. We may set $f(2)=+\infty$ without affecting the limit.

A sharp elementary lower bound. Suppose $E=\{n_1<\cdots<n_k=M\}$ represents $1$. Since $n_i\leq M-k+i$, \[ 1\geq\sum_{j=M-k+1}^{M}\frac1j \geq\int_{M-k+1}^{M+1}\frac{dt}{t} =\log\frac{M+1}{M-k+1}. \] Exponentiating and rearranging gives \[ f(k)\geq\frac{e}{e-1}k-1. \tag{2} \] The direction of the comparison is essential: among $k$ distinct denominators at most $M$, the last $k$ have the smallest reciprocal sum.

Declared external construction. Write $P^*(b)$ for the largest prime power dividing $b$, with $P^*(1)=1$. Martin's Proposition 5 states: for each closed interval $I\subset(0,\infty)$, all sufficiently large integers $t$, and every rational $r=a/b\in I$ in lowest terms satisfying \[ P^*(b)<t(\log t)^{-22}, \] there is a set $E$ of exactly $t$ distinct positive integers such that \[ \sum_{n\in E}\frac1n=r,\qquad \max E\leq\frac{t}{1-e^{-r}}+O_I\!\left(\frac{t\log\log t}{\log t}\right). \tag{3} \] The construction proving $3$, including its prime-power denominator clearing and control of the exact number of terms, is imported from Martin; it is not re-proved here.

Take the fixed interval $I=[1/2,3/2]$, $r=1$, $b=1$, and $t=k$. Its hypothesis becomes $1<k/(\log k)^{22}$, which holds for every sufficiently large $k$. Thus \[ f(k)\leq\frac{k}{1-e^{-1}} +O\!\left(\frac{k\log\log k}{\log k}\right). \tag{4} \] Since $\log\log k/\log k\to0$, $2$ and $4$ imply \[ \frac{f(k)}k\longrightarrow\frac1{1-e^{-1}}=\frac e{e-1}. \]

VERIFICATION AND SCOPE

The exact-length input applies for every sufficiently large integer $k$, not merely a subsequence. Formula $1$ handles all other feasible lengths but does not supply the optimal asymptotic bound. The proof is complete relative to the explicitly stated Martin construction. No new mathematical discovery is claimed.
