Attempt and results

The source requests key establishment without a selected platform or threat model. We construct an algebraically correct interactive agreement procedure, then show why agreement and the formal action law are insufficient for secrecy. This is not a secure key-exchange construction.
A leave-one-out action procedure.
Let an abelian group $A$ act on $X$, fix $x\in X$, and let $n\ge2$ parties hold $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A$. For each index $i$, start a token at $x$ and pass it, in a fixed public order, through every party except $i$. When party $j$ receives that token, it replaces $u$ by $a_j\cdot u$. Induction on the visited parties shows that the resulting token is \[ u_i=\left(\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\right)\cdot x. \] Party $i$ then applies its own action, obtaining the common point $K=(\sum_j a_j)\cdot x$. This proves agreement for honest participants using only associativity of the action and commutativity of $A$. For this straightforward schedule, constructing the $n$ tokens uses $n(n-1)$ action evaluations, and their final private updates use $n$ more, for $n^2$ total. This count includes operations often hidden in initialization; no claim of optimal rounds, messages, or evaluations is made.
A restricted one-round expression barrier.
Consider a party $i$ that knows $a_i$ and public points $x_j=a_j\cdot x$. Allow point expressions built only by starting from $x$ or one public $x_j$ and repeatedly applying actions of group elements computable from $a_i$ and public constants. No operation combines two points, extracts a transporter, or inspects their encodings. Structural induction on the expression gives \[ u=b(a_i)\cdot x\quad\hbox{or}\quad u=(b(a_i)+a_j)\cdot x \] for some single $j$: applying another local group element only changes $b$. For $n\ge3$ this cannot uniformly equal $(\sum_l a_l)\cdot x$ on a regular translation action, because there is a foreign index $l\ne i,j$ absent from the expression. Changing only $a_l$ changes the target but leaves the expression unchanged. Taking $A=\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ makes this a finite explicit counterexample to a uniform identity in this grammar. This is a syntactic restriction on a straight-line interface, not an impossibility theorem for all one-round protocols, representation-dependent algorithms, pairings, or other extra structure.
The proposed secrecy inference fails in a concrete platform.
Let $A=X=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$ act by addition on canonical residues and let $x=0$. Every public point $a_j\cdot x$ reveals $a_j$ outright, and an observer computes $K=\sum_j a_j$ from the public data. The same action identities and agreement proof hold, but no secrecy follows. More generally, even a hardness assumption for recovering one transporter does not by itself bound an adversary seeing all intermediate leave-one-out tokens; a reduction would have to simulate that entire public transcript and recover the right challenge relation from an adversary's success. No such reduction is supplied. The procedure also gives no authentication against replacement of tokens.
