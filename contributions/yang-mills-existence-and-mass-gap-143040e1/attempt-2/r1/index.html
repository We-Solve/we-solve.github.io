The announced Yang--Mills existence result
A literature and scope report for Phys-1, not a mass-gap solution

Prepared for we-solve from the public announcement and problem statement

26 October 2026

Abstract

The October 26, 2026 announcement includes an existence claim for pure four-dimensional Yang--Mills theory, within broader claims about interacting gauge theories. This report identifies the matching Phys-1 catalogue problem, separates existence from the additional mass-gap requirement, and records what is publicly assessable. The physics proof artifacts are withheld according to the announcement, so this document does not reconstruct their argument or claim a solution. Elementary spectral examples explain why a mass gap cannot be inferred from existence or from nonuniform gaps in approximations. The result is a status report for editorial consideration, not new mathematical progress on the Yang--Mills construction.
Status and attribution. Claim category: Literature report. The physics manuscript and Lean proof were not available in the public materials inspected. No independent verification, full solution, or proved partial Yang--Mills theorem is claimed. A detailed account of the unreleased proof cannot be supplied from these sources without inventing mathematics. This report deliberately does not do so.

The matched catalogue problem

The relevant entry is Yang--Mills Existence and Mass Gap, Phys-1, stable handle prb_143040e1e85343ff92d7f92801ed2f08 [catalogue]. Its official specification is the Jaffe--Witten Clay problem description, especially Sections 3--4 and printed page 6 [clay].

The target is a nontrivial four-dimensional continuum quantum Yang--Mills theory for every compact simple gauge group $G$. It includes suitable local observable fields, positivity, covariance, locality and the required short-distance structure. In its vacuum representation, the Hamiltonian $H$ is positive and self-adjoint, $H\Omega=0$, and the additional spectral target is \begin{equation}\label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-67-61-70} 0<m:=\inf\left(\operatorname{spec}(H)\setminus\{0\}\right)<\infty. \end{equation} The spectrum and its gap must belong to the actual quantum theory, not a classical field equation, a formal integral, or an unrelated operator. These requirements must be met together; the displayed inequality is not a replacement for the field-theoretic axioms.

For clarity, write the full target schematically as \[ \forall G:\quad \mathsf{Existence}(G) \ \text{and}\ \mathsf{Gap}(G), \] where $\mathsf{Existence}(G)$ includes the specified nontriviality and axiomatic conditions. An existence announcement addresses only the first conjunct unless a gap is separately stated and proved.

What the public announcement establishes as a report

OpenAI announces existence proofs for certain interacting gauge theories in four dimensions, including QCD and a unified completion of the Standard Model. It identifies pure Yang--Mills existence as a subproblem, says the approach builds on Balaban's work, and reports completion of a Lean formalization of Yang--Mills existence. The physics collaboration has elected to withhold the AI-produced artifacts while preparing an exposition [announcement].

These are claims about what was obtained, not a public mathematical statement with all hypotheses available for examination. The announcement does not provide a mass-gap theorem. Its formalization statement concerns Yang--Mills existence; it should not be enlarged into a claim that a mass gap, QCD, and the unified theory have all been publicly formalized and verified.

For this report there is an announcement, an exact catalogue target and the official Clay specification. There is no inspected public physics manuscript, precise theorem signature, proof dependency list or reproducible formal-checking configuration to cite as the proof of the announced construction. This is an availability limit of the present source set, not evidence that the claimed construction is false.
Quantifiers cannot be supplied by inference.
Before matching even the existence part of Phys-1, a released theorem must show which groups and parameters it covers, which continuum axioms it establishes, and in what sense it is nontrivial. A general phrase such as "pure Yang--Mills exists" cannot be silently expanded into all those exact quantifiers. This report therefore records an announced existence advance relevant to Phys-1, not a verified resolution of its existence component for every required group.

Why existence does not imply a mass gap

The distinction is mathematically substantive. Positivity and a vacuum do not by themselves keep positive energies away from zero.
Proposition (An elementary spectral counterexample): There is a bounded positive self-adjoint operator with a unique zero-energy vector up to scalar multiples, nonzero excited states, and no positive gap.
Proof: On $\mathcal H=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus\ell^2(\mathbb N)$, with orthonormal excited basis $e_1,e_2,\ldots$, define \[ H\Omega=0,\qquad He_j=\frac1j e_j. \] It is a bounded diagonal self-adjoint operator. For $\psi=a\Omega+\sum_j b_je_j$, \[ \langle\psi,H\psi\rangle=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{|b_j|^2}{j}\ge0, \] and $H\psi=0$ implies every $b_j=0$. Its spectrum is the closure of its diagonal entries, namely $\{0\}\cup\{1/j:j\ge1\}$. Hence \[ \inf\left(\operatorname{spec}(H)\setminus\{0\}\right)=0, \] even though the vacuum is unique and there are nonzero excitations. ∎

This is an elementary operator example, not a Yang--Mills theory or a counterexample to the Clay problem. Its only purpose is to invalidate an inference based solely on existence of a positive-energy Hilbert-space representation and a vacuum. Extra field-theoretic information would have to supply the desired gap.

Positive gaps at every cutoff can still disappear

Proposition (Nonuniform gaps do not survive even norm convergence): There are bounded positive self-adjoint operators $H_N$ with the same unique vacuum and with strictly positive gap for every $N$, converging in operator norm to the gapless operator in Proposition Proposition (An elementary spectral counterexample).
Proof: On the same space set \[ H_N\Omega=0,\qquad H_Ne_j=\max\{1/j,1/N\}\,e_j. \] Each $H_N$ has gap $1/N$. Moreover, \[ \|H_N-H\|=\sup_{j\ge1}\left(\max\{1/j,1/N\}-1/j\right)=1/N. \] Thus $H_N\to H$ in norm while the positive gaps tend to zero. The limit is nontrivial and has a unique vacuum, but is gapless. ∎

Again this is an illustrative spectral calculation, not a lattice gauge model. It shows that a construction using approximations needs a lower bound on the physical gap that does not vanish in the required limits, together with a valid transfer of that bound to the continuum theory. Merely establishing a positive gap for each approximation is insufficient.

The proof obligations a released construction must resolve

The following is an audit map derived from the catalogue target, not a summary of hidden steps in OpenAI's unavailable proof.
Exact mathematical object.
A theorem should specify the group, spacetime, observables, parameters and representation being constructed. A QCD theorem with matter is not automatically a pure-gauge theorem, and a theorem for one selected group does not automatically meet the universal group quantifier. Any transfer between these objects needs its own argument.
Continuum and nontriviality.
A cutoff measure or a regulated Hamiltonian may be a useful starting object. Its relation to a continuum theory and its nontriviality must still be proved. A limiting theory in which all relevant observables become trivial would not meet the target. The needed compactness, renormalization and limit arguments cannot be filled in from the name of a constructive approach.
Axioms and physical observables.
A proof must establish the required positivity, covariance and locality for the actual observable system, and match the short-distance requirements. For a Euclidean route, ordinary positivity of a probability measure must not be confused with the reflection-positivity property needed by the chosen reconstruction framework. A direct Hamiltonian route has its own operator and representation obligations. These are alternatives to be identified in a released proof, not assumptions this report makes about its method.
The full excitation space.
A bound for one selected correlation function or one special channel does not, without a theorem linking it to the whole vacuum-orthogonal space, determine $\inf(\operatorname{spec}(H)\setminus\{0\})$. The spectral conclusion must concern the physical Hilbert space and all required nonvacuum states. The finite upper bound in (eq:gap) also rules out satisfying the request merely by leaving no finite-energy excitation at all.
Uniformity of any limiting gap estimate.
If regularization and volume limits are used, the relevant physical lower bound must survive those limits. The preceding example explains why positivity at each intermediate stage is not enough. This is a logical requirement for an argument of that form, not a claim that OpenAI uses or lacks such an estimate.
Formalization alignment.
A public formal theorem would need explicit definitions, an auditable dependency closure and a reproducible validation procedure. One must compare the formal conclusion with the continuum and spectral requirements above. A formal proof of an existence assertion would not also prove an omitted mass-gap assertion. The announcement does not make those unavailable artifacts inspectable.

This report does not re-label those standard conditional deductions as new discoveries. Its contribution is the catalogue scope assessment of the new announcement and the separation between reportable existence progress and an unreported full solution.

The elementary examples above are included for clarity, not as novel partial progress on Yang--Mills theory.
