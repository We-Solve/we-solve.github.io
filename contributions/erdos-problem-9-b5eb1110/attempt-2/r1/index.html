OUTPUT 1) FORMAL RESTATEMENT Define \[A:=\{n\in 2\mathbb Z+1: \text{$n$ is not of the form } p+2^k+2^l\text{ with }k,l\ge 0,\ p\text{ prime}\}.\] Question: Is the upper density \[\overline d(A):=\limsup_{N\to\infty} \frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,N\}|}{N} \] positive?

2) QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK Browsing is not available. I only record what the problem statement itself claims. I have not verified those results here.

3) ATTACK PLAN Proof track: 1. Use sieve methods to show representable numbers have density < 1 among odd integers. 2. Control the union over all pairs $(k,l)$ of translated primes.

Disproof track: 1. Try to show representable numbers are dense by covering odd integers with $p+2^k+2^l$.

Chosen path: establish elementary counting bounds and a baseline construction of representable odds.

4) WORK Lemma 1 (Infinitely many representable odd integers). There are infinitely many odd integers of the form $p+2^k+2^l$.

Proof. Fix any $k,l\ge 0$. As $p$ ranges over odd primes, $p+2^k+2^l$ ranges over infinitely many odd integers. $\square$

Lemma 2 (Crude upper bound on the count of representable integers up to $N$). Let $R(N)$ be the number of integers $\le N$ that are representable as $p+2^k+2^l$. Then \[R(N)\le \pi(N)\cdot (1+\lfloor \log_2 N\rfloor)^2,\] where $\pi(N)$ is the prime-counting function.

Proof. For each pair $(k,l)$ with $2^k+2^l\le N$, the count of primes $p$ with $p+2^k+2^l\le N$ is at most $\pi(N)$. There are at most $(1+\lfloor \log_2 N\rfloor)^2$ such pairs. Summing over all pairs gives the bound. $\square$

Lemma 3 (A trivial inclusion for $p=2$). Every integer of the form $2+2^k+2^l$ is representable.

Proof. Take $p=2$ in the defining form. $\square$

5) VERIFICATION - Lemma 2 is a union bound; it does not attempt to estimate overlaps. - Lemma 3 is purely definitional.

6) FINAL UNRESOLVED

(i) Strongest fully proved partial result: A crude counting upper bound: $R(N)\le \pi(N)(1+\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor)^2$.

(ii) First gap: No nontrivial lower bound on $|A\cap[1,N]|$ is proved; in particular, no positive-density bound.

(iii) Top 3 next moves: 1. Use sieve methods to estimate the density of $p+2^k+2^l$ uniformly in $(k,l)$. 2. Study distribution of $2^k+2^l$ modulo small primes to create local obstructions. 3. Attempt a lower bound on $|A\cap[1,N]|$ via a large-sieve inequality.

(iv) Minimal counterexample structure (if the conjecture were false): A scenario where the union of shifted primes $\bigcup_{k,l}(\mathcal P+2^k+2^l)$ has density 1 within the odd integers, requiring highly uniform prime distribution across the shifts by all sums of two powers of 2.
