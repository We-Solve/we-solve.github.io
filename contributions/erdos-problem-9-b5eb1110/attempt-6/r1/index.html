5.2 A Fermat divisibility lemma

Lemma (Fermat divisor of $2^a+2^b$): Let $a>b\ge 1$ and write $a-b=2^r t$ with $t$ odd, i.e. $r=v_2(a-b)$. Then \[F_r=2^{2^r}+1\ \mid\ 2^{a-b}+1\ \mid\ 2^a+2^b. \] Consequently, if $B_r\mid F_r$, then $B_r\mid (2^a+2^b)$.
Proof: Since $2^{2^r}\equiv -1\pmod{F_r}$, raising to the odd power $t$ gives \[(2^{2^r})^t\equiv (-1)^t\equiv -1\pmod{F_r}. \] But $(2^{2^r})^t=2^{2^r t}=2^{a-b}$, so $2^{a-b}\equiv -1\pmod{F_r}$ and hence $F_r\mid (2^{a-b}+1)$. Multiplying by $2^b$ yields $F_r\mid 2^b(2^{a-b}+1)=2^a+2^b$. If $B_r\mid F_r$, then also $B_r\mid (2^a+2^b)$. ∎

5.3 A mod-$16$ obstruction lemma (Crocker Lemma II, proved)

We now prove the exact Lemma II input needed for the $p+2^a+2^b$ part of $N_0\in A$.

Lemma (Prime factors of Fermat numbers are $1\bmod 2^{i+2}$): Let $i\ge 2$ and let $p$ be an odd prime divisor of the Fermat number $F_i=2^{2^i}+1$. Then \[p\equiv 1\pmod{2^{i+2}}. \] Consequently, every divisor $B_i\mid F_i$ satisfies $B_i\equiv 1\pmod{16}$.
Proof: Since $p\mid 2^{2^i}+1$, we have $2^{2^i}\equiv -1\pmod p$, hence $2^{2^{i+1}}\equiv 1\pmod p$. Thus the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $p$ is exactly $2^{i+1}$, so $2^{i+1}\mid (p-1)$. Write \[p-1=2^{i+1}s\qquad (s\in\mathbb{N}). \] Because $i\ge 2$, we have $8\mid 2^{i+1}$ and hence $p\equiv 1\pmod 8$. By the standard quadratic-reciprocity criterion for $2$, $p\equiv 1\pmod 8$ implies that $2$ is a quadratic residue modulo $p$, i.e. \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-74-77-6f-2d-71-72} 2^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\pmod p. \end{equation} On the other hand, \[ 2^{(p-1)/2}=2^{2^is}=(2^{2^i})^s\equiv (-1)^s\pmod p. \] Combining with (eq:two-qr) gives $(-1)^s\equiv 1\pmod p$, hence $s$ is even. Therefore $2^{i+2}\mid (p-1)$, i.e. $p\equiv 1\pmod{2^{i+2}}$.

Finally, if $B_i\mid F_i$ and $i\ge 2$, then every prime divisor of $B_i$ is $\equiv 1\pmod{2^{i+2}}$, hence in particular $\equiv 1\pmod{16}$; so $B_i\equiv 1\pmod{16}$. ∎

Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved): Fix an integer $n\ge 6$. Let integers $B_0,\dots,B_{n-1}$ satisfy $B_i>1$ and $B_i\mid (2^{2^i}+1)$ for each $i$. Let $w\equiv 1\pmod{16}$ and define \[N:=w\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i. \] Assume $N\le 2^{2^n}-1$. Then $N$ cannot be written as \[N=p+2^a+2^b\qquad\text{with $p$ prime and $a,b\ge 1$ distinct.} \]
Proof: Assume for contradiction that $N=p+2^a+2^b$ with $a>b\ge 1$ and $p$ prime. Then \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-69-66-66} N-2^a-2^b=p>0. \end{equation}

Step 1: find a nontrivial divisor of $N-2^a-2^b$. Write $a-b=2^r t$ with $t$ odd; thus $r=v_2(a-b)$. Since $a<2^n$ (because $2^a<N\le 2^{2^n}-1$), we have $a-b<2^n$, hence $2^r\le a-b<2^n$, so $r\le n-1$. By Lemma lem:fermat-div, $B_r\mid (2^a+2^b)$. But also $B_r\mid N$ since $B_r$ is a factor of the product defining $N$. Therefore \[B_r\mid (N-(2^a+2^b))=p. \] Since $p$ is prime and $B_r>1$, this forces \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-72-69-6d-65-65-71-75-61-6c-73} p=B_r. \end{equation}

Step 2: show $N-2^a-2^b\neq B_r$ by a mod-$16$ contradiction. Suppose (as forced by (eq:primeequals)) that \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-61-73-73-75-6d-65-42-72} N-2^a-2^b=B_r. \end{equation} Then \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-4e-2d-61-73-2d-73-75-6d} N=2^a+2^b+B_r. \end{equation}

We claim $N\equiv -1\pmod{16}$. Indeed, by Lemma lem:fermat-prime-cong every divisor $B_i\mid (2^{2^i}+1)$ with $i\ge 2$ satisfies $B_i\equiv 1\pmod{16}$. Also $B_0\mid 3$ and $B_1\mid 5$, hence $B_0\equiv 3\pmod{16}$ and $B_1\equiv 5\pmod{16}$. Therefore \[\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i\equiv B_0B_1\equiv 15\equiv -1\pmod{16}, \] and multiplying by $w\equiv 1\pmod{16}$ gives $N\equiv -1\pmod{16}$.

Next we lower-bound $2^a+2^b$. Since $r\le n-1$, the product $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i$ contains the factor $B_r$ and also contains $B_0,B_1$; thus \[\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i\ge B_0B_1B_r\ge 15B_r. \] Hence $N=w\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i\ge 15B_r$ and so \[2^a+2^b=N-B_r\ge 14B_r\ge 42. \] Since $a>b\ge 1$, this implies $a\ge 4$, hence $2^a\equiv 0\pmod{16}$. Also $2^b\equiv 2,4,8,$ or $0\pmod{16}$. Finally, as noted above, $B_r\equiv 1,3,$ or $5\pmod{16}$. Thus \[2^a+2^b+B_r\not\equiv -1\pmod{16}, \] contradicting $N\equiv -1\pmod{16}$ and the identity (eq:N-as-sum). This contradiction shows (eq:assumeBr) is impossible.

Therefore $N-2^a-2^b$ is divisible by $B_r>1$ but not equal to $B_r$, so it is composite, contradicting (eq:diff). ∎

5.4 Excluding $p+2^d$ by the overlapping congruence system

Lemma (No representation $N_0=p+2^d$ for $d\ge 1$): For the integer $N_0$ defined by (eq:CRTpi)--(eq:CRT16), there do not exist a prime $p$ and integer $d\ge 1$ such that $N_0=p+2^d$.
Proof: Fix $d\ge 1$. Because the congruence system $d\equiv a_i\pmod{m_i}$ is overlapping (verified in §sec:certificate), there exists some index $i\in\{1,\dots,28\}$ such that \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-64-2d-69-6e-2d-63-6c-61-73-73} d\equiv a_i\pmod{m_i}. \end{equation} Since $\operatorname{ord}_{p_i}(2)=m_i$, the congruence (eq:d-in-class) implies \[2^d\equiv 2^{a_i}\pmod{p_i}. \] By (eq:CRTpi), $N_0\equiv 2^{a_i}\pmod{p_i}$ as well, hence \begin{equation} \label{wsref-0-65-71-3a-70-69-2d-64-69-76-69-64-65-73} N_0-2^d\equiv 0\pmod{p_i}. \end{equation} So if $N_0-2^d$ were prime, then it would have to equal $p_i$. But reducing the putative identity $N_0-2^d=p_i$ modulo $8191$ and using (eq:CRT8191) gives \[c\equiv p_i+2^d\pmod{8191}. \] Since $\operatorname{ord}_{8191}(2)=13$, the residue $2^d\pmod{8191}$ depends only on $d\pmod{13}$ and ranges over $\{2^e:0\le e\le 12\}$. By the choice of $c$ (verified in §sec:certificate), $c$ is not congruent to any $p_i+2^e\pmod{8191}$ for $1\le i\le 28$ and $0\le e\le 12$. Contradiction. Therefore $N_0-2^d$ is composite for every $d\ge 1$, proving the lemma. ∎

5.5 Concluding $N_0\in A$

Theorem (Certified explicit element of $A$): The integer $N_0$ defined by (eq:CRTpi)--(eq:CRT16) belongs to $A$. Equivalently, there do not exist a prime $p$ and integers $k,\ell\ge 0$ with \[N_0=p+2^k+2^\ell. \]
Proof: We verify the two hypotheses of Round 2 Lemma 4.

(i) No representation $N_0=p+2^a$ with $a\ge 1$. This is Lemma lem:no-p-plus-power. In particular, this excludes $N_0=p+2^a+2^a$ for any $a\ge 1$ because $2^a+2^a=2^{a+1}$.

(ii) No representation $N_0=p+2^a+2^b$ with $a,b\ge 1$ distinct. Write $N_0=w\,M_1$ with $w:=N_0/M_1$; this is an integer by (eq:CRTM1). Since $M_1\equiv -1\pmod{16}$ and $N_0\equiv -1\pmod{16}$, we have $w\equiv 1\pmod{16}$. Also $N_0<2^{2048}-1=2^{2^{11}}-1$, so with $n=11$ we have $N_0\le 2^{2^n}-1$. Finally $M_1=\prod_{i=0}^{10}B_i$ with each $B_i\mid (2^{2^i}+1)$. Hence Lemma Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved) applies (with this $n$ and these $B_i$), showing $N_0$ is not of the form $p+2^a+2^b$ with distinct $a,b\ge 1$.

Applying Round 2 Lemma 4 gives $N_0\in A$. ∎

6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION

We stress-test the new Round 3 argument.

  • Quantifiers in Lemma Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved). The key is that for any distinct $a,b\ge 1$ in a hypothetical representation, one has $a<2^n$ because $2^a<N\le 2^{2^n}-1$, hence $r=v_2(a-b)\le n-1$ so that $B_r$ is among the factors of $N$. No hidden assumption about $a,b$ beyond positivity and distinctness is used.

  • Case $a=b$ is not forgotten. Lemma Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved) only treats distinct $a,b\ge 1$, but Lemma lem:no-p-plus-power excludes $p+2^d$ for all $d\ge 1$, which covers the diagonal case $a=b$ via $2^a+2^a=2^{a+1}$.

  • Residue-class coverage for all $d\ge 1$. The overlapping system is checked on $\{1,\dots,720\}$; since each congruence condition is periodic modulo $m_i$ and $\mathrm{lcm}(m_i)=720$, this implies coverage for every $d\ge 1$.

  • Potential loophole $N_0-2^d=p_i$. This is explicitly excluded by the extra modulus $8191$ and the choice of $c$ avoiding all residues $p_i+2^e\pmod{8191}$ for $0\le e\le 12$. The dependence on $d\bmod 13$ is justified by $\operatorname{ord}_{8191}(2)=13$.

  • Use of modulus-$16$ in Lemma Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved). The proof only needs $w\equiv 1\pmod{16}$ and that all $B_i$ for $i\ge 2$ are $\equiv 1\pmod{16}$; Lemma lem:fermat-prime-cong supplies this. Even if some $B_i$ are composite, they are products of such primes and remain $\equiv 1\pmod{16}$.

  • Interaction with Round 2 Lemma 4. All hypotheses of the reduction lemma are satisfied: (i) is Lemma lem:no-p-plus-power; (ii) is Lemma Lemma (Crocker-type lemma, proved) applied to $N_0=wM_1$ with $n=11$. Thus the final implication $N_0\in A$ is sound.
