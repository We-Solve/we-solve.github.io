Multicolored Sunflower Conjecture over Ternary Vector Spaces

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.

$\mathbb Z_3^n$ is the ternary vector space with coordinatewise addition modulo three. Every supplied triple sums to zero; the proposed conclusion mixes one coordinate from each of three selected triples.

Statement recorded in the supplied source.
Does there exist $c>0$ such that every $S\subseteq(\mathbb Z_3^n)^3$ with $|S|\geq3^{n(1-c)}$ and $x+y+z=0$ for every $(x,y,z)\in S$ contains three elements $(x_i,y_i,z_i)$ satisfying $x_1+y_2+z_3=0$? [S2]

Resolve the interpretation before applying the polynomial method

The printed Conjecture 7 does not say whether its three selected elements must be distinct; the earlier uniquely solvable puzzle condition instead says that the three indices are not all equal. These readings differ. If arbitrary repetitions including three copies of one element are allowed, any nonempty $S$ works because its elements already satisfy $x+y+z=0$. If three pairwise distinct elements are required, the family \[ S=\{(x,-x,0):x\in\mathbb F_3^n\} \] is a counterexample: $x_1+y_2+z_3=x_1-x_2=0$ forces the first two selected elements to coincide. Its size is $3^n$, exceeding every proposed $3^{(1-c)n}$ with $c>0$, $n\ge1$. The standard tri-colored sum-free interpretation requires an off-diagonal choice of indices, meaning not all three equal. For this reading a later polynomial-method theorem applies. We give a complete, deliberately nonoptimal rederivation of that known bound, not a new discovery or a silent repair of the original quantifiers.
Proposition: Let $n\ge1$ and let $(a_i,b_i,c_i)\in(\mathbb F_3^n)^3$, $1\le i\le m$, satisfy \[ a_i+b_j+c_k=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i=j=k. \] Then $m\le\theta^n$ where $\theta=(7/4)2^{2/3}<14/5<3$. In particular, the not-all-equal interpretation holds with $c=1-\log_3(14/5)>0$.
Proof: A slice of a three-index array is an expression $f(i)g(j,k)$, or one of the other two analogous forms. First we prove that an $m$ by $m$ by $m$ array equal to 1 when $i=j=k$ and zero otherwise requires at least $m$ slices over any field. Suppose it were expressed using $r$ slices separated in $i$, $s$ in $j$, and $t$ in $k$, with $r+s+t<m$. Let $U$ be the subspace of vectors $u\in\mathbb F^m$ annihilating the $r$ one-variable functions in the first type. Its dimension $d$ is at least $m-r$. Select $d$ coordinate functionals whose restrictions form a basis of $U^*$; prescribing all these coordinates to be 1 produces a vector $u\in U$ with at least $d$ nonzero coordinates. Contract the array against $u$ in its first index. The diagonal description gives a matrix with diagonal $u$, of rank at least $d$. The first $r$ slices vanish, while each of the remaining $s+t$ slices becomes a rank-at-most-one matrix. Thus its rank is at most $s+t<m-r\le d$, a contradiction. The $m$ single-coordinate slices show that the required number is exactly $m$. Over $\mathbb F_3$, form the polynomial array \[ T(i,j,k)=\prod_{\ell=1}^n\left(1-(a_{i\ell}+b_{j\ell}+c_{k\ell})^2\right). \] A factor is 1 exactly when the corresponding sum is zero and is 0 otherwise. The hypothesis therefore makes $T$ the diagonal array just analyzed. Its expansion has total degree at most $2n$, and each individual coordinate variable has exponent at most 2. In every monomial one of the three variable groups has degree at most $\lfloor2n/3\rfloor$. Assign each monomial to one such group, with a fixed tie rule, and collect monomials having the same factor in that group's variables. Each collection is one slice. Consequently \[ m\le3D_n,\qquad D_n=\left|\{\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^n:\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell\le2n/3\}\right|. \] For $0<t<1$, each counted exponent has $t^{\sum\alpha_\ell}\ge t^{2n/3}$. Summing and then including all exponents gives \[ D_n\le t^{-2n/3}(1+t+t^2)^n. \] Taking $t=1/2$ yields $m\le3\theta^n$. To remove the factor 3, take the $r$-fold Cartesian product of the indexed family. A mixed sum vanishes precisely when the three index tuples agree in every component, so the same bound in dimension $nr$ gives $m^r\le3\theta^{nr}$. Taking $r$th roots and letting $r$ tend to infinity gives $m\le\theta^n$. Finally, $\theta^3=343/16<(14/5)^3<27$. A family of size at least $3^{(1-c)n}=(14/5)^n$ cannot be tri-colored sum-free, proving the last assertion. ∎

Verification and remaining gaps

The pairwise-distinct counterexample was enumerated for $n=1,2$. Exponent counts $D_n$ were checked by finite enumeration for $1\le n\le8$, and the strict cubic comparison was checked with exact rational arithmetic. The diagonal-slice argument was audited over a finite field: choosing pivot coordinates equal to 1 avoids an invalid appeal to a generic vector over an infinite field. The resulting exponential bound is a known consequence of the 2016 polynomial/slice-rank methods, and later work gives sharper constants. We claim no new progress beyond that literature. The original ambiguous phrase must still be interpreted explicitly; the three readings above are not interchangeable, and the catalogue status is not edited.
