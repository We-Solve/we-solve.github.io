Explicit torsion points: information retained and lost by fields of definition

Definitions and mathematical statement

Shared notation.
Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.

An $\ell$-torsion point on an abelian variety is a point $P$ with $\ell P=0$. Its field of definition contains coordinates for $P$, whereas a defining extension can sometimes be represented without writing every coordinate. Explicit point arithmetic also requires a curve or abelian-variety model and a representation for its elements.

A proposed compression and a recoverable invariant

Question 16 asks how to deal explicitly with points appearing in Edixhoven's method and whether one can work just with their fields of definition, especially where arithmetic is harder. We test that replacement directly. The calculations below show which orbit data a supplied Frobenius action determines, and why bare fields do not determine point arithmetic. They do not construct Edixhoven's required geometric representation.
Orbit counts from a supplied Frobenius matrix.
Suppose the relevant point group is supplied as $V=\mathbb F_\ell^n$ with an invertible Frobenius matrix $F$. Then \[ A_d=|\ker(F^d-I)|=\ell^{\dim\ker(F^d-I)} \] counts points fixed by the $d$th iterate, by the elementary coordinate count in a vector space. If $E_d$ denotes the number of vectors of exact period $d$, every vector fixed by $F^d$ has a unique minimal period dividing $d$. Indeed division of $d$ by a minimal period leaves a smaller fixing remainder, so the remainder is zero. Thus $A_d=\sum_{e\mid d}E_e$, and recursively \[ E_d=A_d-\sum_{\substack{e\mid d\\e<d}}E_e. \] There are $E_d/d$ orbits of length $d$, since those disjoint orbits each contain exactly $d$ vectors. This computes field-degree counts when the supplied matrix really represents geometric Frobenius: an algebraic point has coordinates in $\mathbb F_{q^d}$ precisely when all coordinates are fixed by the $q^d$-power map, since that field is exactly the root set of $X^{q^d}-X$.
A characteristic polynomial loses even this information.
Over $\mathbb F_\ell$ compare $I_2$ with $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Both characteristic polynomials are $(T-1)^2$. Writing $J=I+N$ with $N^2=0$ gives $J^d=I+dN$, by induction or the binomial formula. The identity fixes all $\ell^2$ vectors. The matrix $J$ fixes exactly $\ell$ vectors unless $\ell\mid d$, and fixes all vectors when $\ell\mid d$. Hence $J$ has $\ell$ orbits of length one and $(\ell^2-\ell)/\ell=\ell-1$ orbits of length $\ell$, whereas the identity has only singleton orbits. This is an abstract linear-algebra counterexample to recovering orbit data from a characteristic polynomial without further hypotheses; no claim is made that these two matrices realize a particular geometric family in the source.
Even exact fields cannot specify point addition.
For an actual elliptic-curve example over $\mathbb Q$, take $E:y^2=x^3+1$ and $P=(0,1)$. It is nonsingular: an affine singularity would have $2y=3x^2=0$ and violate the equation; at the unique point at infinity the partial derivative with respect to $Z$ of $Y^2Z-X^3-Z^3$ is $Y^2\ne0$. The tangent slope at $P$ is $3x^2/(2y)=0$. The chord-and-tangent group law therefore gives $2P=(0,-1)=-P$, since its formulas are $x(2P)=\lambda^2-2x(P)$ and $y(2P)=\lambda(x(P)-x(2P))-y(P)$. Equivalently the line $y=1$ meets the cubic at $P$ three times, and reflection gives the same doubling. Thus $P$ has order three. Both $P$ and $-P$ have the identical field of definition $\mathbb Q$. The ordered input pairs $(P,P)$ and $(P,-P)$ consequently give the same pair of bare fields, but their sums are $-P$ and the point at infinity respectively. Those are distinct. Any procedure depending only on that pair of fields must give the same output for both pairs, so cannot recover both correct sums.

This refutes only replacing points by unlabelled fields alone. A field equipped with a chosen coordinate tuple, embedding or other point label is richer data and escapes the counterexample. The source may contemplate just such additional structure; the present argument does not rule that out or rule out an invariant-specific computation that never needs arbitrary addition.
