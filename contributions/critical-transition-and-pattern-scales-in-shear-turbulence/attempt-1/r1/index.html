Definitions and conventions.
The Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}$ is the dimensionless ratio of inertial and viscous effects in the specified shear geometry. $\mathrm{Re}_c$ denotes the source's threshold where sustained turbulence becomes possible. Turbulent--laminar patterns consist of spatially varying regions of turbulent and laminar flow, with a characteristic wavelength much larger than the basic shear-layer scale.

A subcritical amplitude test.
Consider the real amplitude equation $\dot a=\mu a+ba^3-ca^5$ with $b,c>0$. Nonzero equilibria have $r=a^2>0$ satisfying $cr^2-br-\mu=0$. They first appear at $\mu=-b^2/(4c)$ in a saddle-node, although the zero state stays linearly stable until $\mu=0$. This elementary distinction prevents identifying the onset of sustained finite-amplitude behavior with a linear-instability threshold.
A large length can arise, but is not selected.
Add diffusion: $a_t=D a_{xx}+\mu a+ba^3-ca^5$, $D>0$. Linearizing a stationary profile near a stable homogeneous equilibrium $a_*$ gives $D v''+f'(a_*)v=0$, where $f(a)=\mu a+ba^3-ca^5$. Exponentially decaying perturbations have length \[ \ell=\sqrt{D/(-f'(a_*))}\quad\text{when }f'(a_*)<0. \] On the upper nonzero branch, $f'(a_*)=2r(b-2cr)$, which approaches zero from below at the saddle-node. Thus this toy interface width diverges there. It is a decay length, not automatically the wavelength of a stable pattern; the scalar equation alone can coarsen instead of selecting periodic bands.
Source gap.
These formulas exhibit two plausible ingredients, subcriticality and a slow relaxation length, without deriving them from Navier--Stokes or identifying $\mu$ with the source Reynolds parameter. The missing step is a controlled reduction giving coefficients, mode interactions and wavelength selection in the actual shear geometry. Neither the recurrent proximity to $R_{e,c}$ nor the observed pattern scale is thereby explained quantitatively.
