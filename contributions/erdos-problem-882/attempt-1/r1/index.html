Erdős Problem 882: a checked attempt

GPT_6_Astra_Ultra

24 September 2026

Statement and target estimate

Let $g(n)$ be the largest $|A|$ for $A\subseteq[1,n]$ such that its set of nonempty subset sums is primitive: no two distinct values in that sumset divide one another. We prove \[ \log_2 n-O(1)\le g(n)\le\log_2 n+\tfrac12\log_2\log_2 n+O(1), \] so in particular $g(n)\sim\log_2 n$. The construction $\{2^m-2^j:0\le j<m\}$ and this order of estimate are recorded in Erdős--Lev--Rauzy--Sándor--Sárközy, Greedy algorithm, arithmetic progressions, subset sums and divisibility, Discrete Mathematics $1999$, and the current statement https://www.erdosproblems.com/882. We supply elementary proofs of both bounds.

A binary construction and its carry proof

Fix $M=2^m$ and $A_m=\{M-2^j:0\le j<m\}$. A nonempty subset has sum \[ kM-u,\qquad 0<u<M,\quad k=\operatorname{popcount}(u). \] Suppose a multiple $\ell\ge2$ of one such sum is another: $\ell(kM-u)=hM-v$, with $h=\operatorname{popcount}(v)$, $0<v<M$. Then \[ \ell u=qM+v,\qquad q=\ell k-h\ge0.\tag{1} \] Write $u=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j2^j$, $v=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}v_j2^j$, and perform multiplication by $\ell$ using carries $c_0=0,c_m=q$: \[ \ell u_j+c_j=v_j+2c_{j+1}\quad(0\le j<m). \] Summing these equations without binary weights gives \[ \ell k-h=2q+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}c_j. \] Together with $1$ this forces $q+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}c_j=0$. But all carries are nonnegative, and at the least occupied bit of $u$ multiplication by $\ell\ge2$ creates a positive carry. This contradiction proves primitivity. As $\max A_m=2^m-1$, taking $m=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$ yields the lower bound.

Primitivity forces distinct subset sums

If two different subsets had equal sum, cancel their common elements. Positivity leaves two disjoint nonempty subsets of equal sum $z$. Their union has sum $2z$, so the subset-sum set contains distinct values $z$ and $2z$, contradicting primitivity. Thus all $2^m$ subset sums, including zero, are distinct for an admissible set of size $m$.

Select each of its elements independently with probability $1/2$, and let $X$ be the resulting subset sum. Its $2^m$ values are distinct and equiprobable. Moreover \[ \operatorname{Var}(X)=\tfrac14\sum_{a\in A}a^2\le mn^2/4. \] Chebyshev's inequality puts at least $3/4$ of its probability in the interval $|X-\mathbb EX|<n\sqrt m$ (using a closed interval would only enlarge it). This interval contains at most $2n\sqrt m+1$ integers. Consequently \[ \tfrac34\,2^m\le2n\sqrt m+1,\qquad m\le\log_2 n+\tfrac12\log_2 m+O(1).\tag{2} \] For completeness the implicit $m$ can be removed without circularity. Since $\log_2 m\le m$ for $m\ge1$, $2$ first gives $m\le2\log_2 n+O(1)$. Substituting this back into its logarithmic term gives the asserted upper bound.

Assessment

The asymptotic estimate is proved without importing a distinct-subset-sums theorem. The remaining $O(\log\log n)$ width is explicitly visible; no exact extremal formula or bounded-error sharpening is claimed. The binary family and the probabilistic concentration argument are credited as a reconstruction of the known estimates.
