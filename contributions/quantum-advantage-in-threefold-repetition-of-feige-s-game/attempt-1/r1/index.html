Research attempt: Testing product and one-sided quantum improvements

In one round, independent uniform bits $x,y$ are sent to Alice and Bob. Answers are $0,1,\perp$, and they win exactly for $(a,b)=(\perp,x)$ or $(a,b)=(y,\perp)$. Three repetitions require all coordinates to win. For the following known strategy from the cited paper, write inputs as $(x_1,x_2,x_3),(y_1,y_2,y_3)$: \[ a_1=\perp,\quad a_2=x_1\vee x_3,\quad a_3=\begin{cases}\perp&x_1=0,\\1&x_1=1,\end{cases} \] \[ b_1=y_2\wedge y_3,\quad b_2=\perp,\quad b_3=\begin{cases}y_2&y_2\wedge y_3=0,\\\perp&y_2\wedge y_3=1.\end{cases} \] The first two rounds require $x_1=y_2\wedge y_3$ and $y_2=x_1\vee x_3$. These conditions also guarantee the third round, by separating $x_1=0$ and $x_1=1$. Among the eight triples $(y_2,y_3,x_3)$, five satisfy $y_2=(y_2\wedge y_3)\vee x_3$; the independent required value of $x_1$ contributes a factor $1/2$. The value is therefore $5/16$.

The direct product of the source's optimal two-round strategy and optimal one-round quantum strategy has value $(1/2)(9/16)=9/32$, below $5/16$ by $1/32$. Three independent optimal one-round strategies give $(9/16)^3=729/4096<5/16$. Shared-randomness mixtures of these strategies and the displayed classical strategy have scores equal to the corresponding convex combinations, and hence cannot exceed $5/16$. Thus neither independent tensoring nor convex mixing of these candidates closes the gap.

Why changing only the quantum resources on one commuting side cannot help.
Suppose Alice's finite-dimensional POVM effects for all her input triples commute pairwise. They can be simultaneously diagonalized: diagonalize one Hermitian effect, observe that all the others preserve each eigenspace by commutation, and repeat on those eigenspaces for the finite collection. In a common orthonormal basis indexed by $\ell$, write their diagonal entries as $a_{\mathbf a}^{\mathbf x}(\ell)\geq0$, summing to one for each $\mathbf x,\ell$.

For any joint state $\rho$, let $\sigma_\ell=\langle\ell|\rho|\ell\rangle$ be the positive, unnormalized conditional operator on Bob's space and $\mu_\ell=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_\ell$. Then $\mu_\ell\geq0$ and $\sum\mu_\ell=1$. For Bob's arbitrary POVM effects $B_{\mathbf b}^{\mathbf y}$, the correlation is \[ p(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\mid\mathbf x,\mathbf y) =\sum_{\ell:\mu_\ell>0}\mu_\ell\, a_{\mathbf a}^{\mathbf x}(\ell) \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_\ell B_{\mathbf b}^{\mathbf y})}{\mu_\ell}. \] This is a classical shared-randomness strategy with hidden variable $\ell$ and local response probabilities. Zero-weight terms vanish because a positive operator of trace zero is zero. Therefore any such commuting-Alice construction is bounded by the classical value, whatever state or Bob measurements are chosen. In particular embedding the displayed deterministic Alice table into a commuting quantum measurement and optimizing only Bob cannot yield the desired advantage. A successful quantum route must use noncommuting measurements on both parties, or otherwise escape this explicitly proved restricted class.

The exact finite search in Verification also shows that, with the displayed Alice table fixed, optimizing each of Bob's eight answer triples independently still gives total score $20/64$. This is an actual exhausted restricted search, not a global search over entangled strategies.

Verification and adversarial audit

The script evaluates all 64 input pairs and obtains 20 wins. It enumerates all 27 Bob answers for each of his eight inputs, obtaining best scores $(2,2,2,4,2,2,2,4)$ out of the eight Alice inputs. It also checks all 432 single-question replacements on either party's displayed table, none exceeding 20 wins. These searches do not prove the global classical upper bound or the quantum upper bound. The common-eigenbasis argument proves a separate structural obstruction to one-sided commuting strategies.
