\section{The Crossing Number of the Hypercube}\label{3320}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3320 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


The hypercube $Q_d$ has vertex set $\{0,1\}^d$, with two vertices adjacent when they differ in exactly one coordinate. The crossing number $\operatorname{cr}(G)$ is the minimum number of interior edge crossings over plane drawings of $G$; drawings may be taken in general position, with no triple crossings and no edge passing through a nonincident vertex.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does the asymptotic identity\[ \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{cr}(Q_d)}{4^d}=\frac5{32} \] hold? \sref{3320}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is five thirty-seconds the leading constant for the crossing number of the high-dimensional hypercube, normalized by four to the dimension?
