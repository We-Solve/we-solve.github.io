{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400020 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb N$, let $\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d$ and $s(n)=\sigma(n)-n$. Distinct positive integers $m,n$ form a betrothed pair when $s(m)=n+1$ and $s(n)=m+1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a betrothed pair $m,n$ with $m\equiv n\pmod2$? \sref{9400020}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a betrothed pair consist of two integers of the same parity?
