\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A domain $D\subset\mathbb R^n$ is linearly accessible if for every $y\notin D$ there is a unit vector $v$ for which the ray $\{y+sv:s\ge0\}$ avoids $D$. Suppose that $D$ is a linearly accessible Green domain, meaning it admits a positive Green function $g_D(x,x_0)$ for the Laplacian, zero on the boundary in the potential-theoretic sense, with pole at $x_0\in D$. Set $g_D(x_0,x_0)=+\infty$ and
\[D_t=\{x\in D:g_D(x,x_0)>t\}.\]

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every dimension $n\ge3$, every such $D$, every pole $x_0\in D$ and every $t>0$, is $D_t$ linearly accessible? \sref{2307048}{2}
The source records that the corresponding preservation property holds in two dimensions. \sref{2307048}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do all positive Green-function superlevel domains inherit linear accessibility in dimensions at least three?
