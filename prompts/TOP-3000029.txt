\section*{Destroying rigidity}\label{3000029}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000029 · AMR-029-0029 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph with $n\ge2$. For a placement
$p:V\to\mathbb R^2$, its rigidity matrix $R_2(G,p)$ has one row per
edge $uv$: the two columns of $u$ contain $p(u)-p(v)$, those of $v$
contain $p(v)-p(u)$, and all other entries vanish. A placement is generic
if its coordinates are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$.
The generic matrix rank depends only on the graph.

Assume $G$ is generically rigid in the plane, meaning
$\operatorname{rank}R_2(G,p)=2n-3$ for generic $p$. Define
\[
 b_2(G)=\min\{|F|:F\subseteq E,\quad
        \operatorname{rank}R_2(G-F,p)<2n-3\}.
\]
Can one find $b_2(G)$ and an attaining set $F$ in deterministic
polynomial time?

Every deletion retains the full original vertex set. Thus isolating
a vertex can destroy rigidity, and no subsequent removal of isolated
vertices is implicit. The objective is cardinality; arbitrary edge
deletion costs would define a weighted extension. Testing whether a
specified deletion succeeds is different from finding the smallest
successful deletion among all choices. The property at issue is generic
infinitesimal rigidity, not global uniqueness of every realization,
nor rigidity at a nongeneric placement with special coordinates.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one efficiently find the fewest edges whose removal destroys
a graph's generic rigidity in the plane?
