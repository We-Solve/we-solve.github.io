\section*{Uniform iterated-sumset growth at the first doubling threshold}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $A$ be a finite nonempty subset of $\mathbb Z$. For a positive
integer $n$, define the iterated sumset
\[
                 nA=\{a_1+\cdots+a_n:a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A\},
\]
where repeated summands are allowed. Thus $2A=A+A$, not the dilate
$\{2a:a\in A\}$. All cardinalities count distinct integers.
For a real parameter $k>1$, define the optimal uniform multiplier
\[
 f(n,k)=\sup\left\{\frac{|nA|}{|A|}:
       A\subseteq\mathbb Z\text{ finite and nonempty},
                                      |2A|<k|A|\right\}.
\]
The supremum, rather than an assumed maximum, is appropriate because
sets of arbitrarily large cardinality are allowed. It is the smallest
constant in $|nA|\le f(n,k)|A|$ valid simultaneously for all sets
meeting the strict doubling hypothesis.

The problem asks to determine this function and, especially, its
growth as $n\to\infty$ with $k$ fixed. Estimates should be uniform
in $A$, including its cardinality, diameter, and additive structure.
For an individual fixed set, the behaviour of $|nA|$ can be much
more constrained than the supremum over all eligible sets, which
may vary with $n$. The strict sign in $|2A|<k|A|$ is part of the
source question and matters at threshold values of $k$. For $k>1$
singletons are eligible; the range avoids an empty family at
$k\le1$. The case $n=1$ gives the normalization $f(1,k)=1$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the optimal uniform bound on repeated sumset growth when the original set has doubling ratio strictly below a fixed threshold?
