\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\geq1$ let $z_1=1$ and let $z_2,\ldots,z_n$ be arbitrary complex numbers, with no modulus restriction. Put $S_k=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j^k$ and define
\[c_*=\sup\left\{c\in\mathbb R:\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|S_k|>c
\text{ for every }n\geq1\text{ and every such }(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\right\}.\]
Equivalently, $c_*$ is the infimum of these maxima over all sizes and tuples; the source records that $c=1/3$ is valid.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $c_*$, the sharp universal constant in this power-sum bound, including whether the strict inequality remains valid at the sharp endpoint. \sref{2307004}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the sharp uniform lower bound for a power sum of a complex tuple containing 1.
