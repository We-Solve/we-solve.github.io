\section*{Kirby Problem 3.6}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M=\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$ be a connected closed hyperbolic three-manifold,
with curvature $-1$, and let $\Gamma\geq\Gamma_1\geq\Gamma_2\geq\cdots$
be finite-index subgroups. They define a tower of connected finite covers
$M_n=\mathbb H^3/\Gamma_n$. The tower is cofinal if
$\bigcap_n\Gamma_n=\{1\}$. Let
$T_n=\operatorname{Tor}H_1(M_n;\mathbb Z)$, the finite subgroup of homology
classes of finite order, and put $|T_n|=1$ when it is trivial.

The displayed question in the catalogue is whether
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\log|T_n|}{\operatorname{vol}(M_n)}=\frac1{6\pi},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{vol}(M_n)=[\Gamma:\Gamma_n]\operatorname{vol}(M),
\]
where $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm. The hypothesis on the tower is
substantive. The source distinguishes the arithmetic congruence-tower
conjecture from the nonarithmetic formulation using regular covers and
$b_1(M_n)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_1(M_n;\mathbb Q)=0$ for every $n$.
Regular means $\Gamma_n$ is normal in $\Gamma$; congruence means that the
subgroups arise from finite congruence quotients of the arithmetic lattice.
For arbitrary cofinal towers the same equality is posed as a broader question,
with source evidence warning that positive Betti numbers can change the behavior.
These additional hypotheses must not be silently dropped from the restricted conjectures.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine when torsion in first homology grows at the universal exponential rate predicted by hyperbolic volume along a cofinal tower of finite covers.
