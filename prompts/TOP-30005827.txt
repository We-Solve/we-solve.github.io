\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


An Erdos-Renyi graph $G(n,p)$ has independent edges, each present with probability $p$. A $k$-clique is a $k$-vertex set with every possible joining edge. Average-case complexity is measured over this graph distribution, separately from worst-case clique hardness.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For Erdős–Rényi graphs with edge probability near the threshold for containing a $k$-clique, does deciding $k$-Clique require time $n^{\Omega(k)}$? More modestly, is there an $n^{o(k)}$-time algorithm for deciding whether such a graph contains a clique of size $n^\varepsilon$? \sref{30005827}{2}
