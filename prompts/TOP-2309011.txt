\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, and let $H^{\infty}(D)$ denote the algebra of bounded analytic functions on a planar domain $D$, with supremum norm. Let $(z_j)\subset D$ and suppose there is one finite $C>0$ such that for every $j$ there exists $F_j\in H^{\infty}(D)$ with \[F_j(z_j)=1,\quad F_j(z_k)=0\ (k\ne j),\quad\|F_j\|_\infty\le C.\] The norm bound applies to these functions $F_j$, not to an unspecified additional function.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does this imply that for every bounded complex sequence $(a_j)\in\ell^{\infty}$ there exists $F\in H^{\infty}(D)$ with $F(z_j)=a_j$ for every $j$? \sref{2309011}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do uniformly bounded single-node interpolants imply interpolation of arbitrary bounded data on every planar domain?
