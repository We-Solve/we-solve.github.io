\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.80}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A left order on a group $G$ is a strict total order $<$ on its elements such that $x<y$ implies $ax<ay$ for all $a,x,y\in G$. A group is left-orderable if it admits at least one such order; the order is not required to be invariant under multiplication on the right. Finite generation means that some finite subset $S\subseteq G$ generates $G$ using products and inverses.

For $g\in G$, its conjugacy class is $g^G=\{h^{-1}gh:h\in G\}$. The identity always forms a conjugacy class by itself. Having exactly one nontrivial conjugacy class means
\[
 G\ne\{1\},\qquad
 \forall a,b\in G\setminus\{1\}\ \exists h\in G:
 h^{-1}ah=b.
\]
Thus $G$ has exactly two conjugacy classes, one equal to $\{1\}$ and one equal to its complement.

Does there exist a group satisfying all three requirements: finite generation, left-orderability, and this two-class condition? Left-orderability already excludes elements of nontrivial finite order, so any example must be infinite and torsion-free. The conjugating element may depend on the ordered pair $(a,b)$, and no bound on its word length is part of the question. The target concerns conjugacy inside the proposed group itself.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a finitely generated left-orderable group with exactly two conjugacy classes exist?
