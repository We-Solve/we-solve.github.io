\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


On the round sphere $\mathbb S^2$ or the hyperbolic plane $\mathbb H^2$, billiard trajectories follow geodesics and reflect at a smooth strictly convex boundary with equal angles. The $\delta$-Gutkin property means that the family arriving at the boundary at the fixed angle $\delta\in(0,\pi/2)$ is invariant under this billiard map: the next chord has the same contact angle. The source motivates the question using noncircular plane curves.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\gamma$ be a smooth closed strictly convex curve and $\delta\in(0,\pi/2)$. Say that $\gamma$ has the $\delta$-Gutkin property if the curve of incoming oriented lines meeting $\gamma$ at the constant angle $\delta$ is invariant under the Birkhoff billiard. What are the Gutkin billiards on the sphere and on the hyperbolic plane? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify the Gutkin billiard curves on the sphere and hyperbolic plane, including the possibility of noncircular curves.
