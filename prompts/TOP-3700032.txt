\section*{Maximizing quadratic pressure}\label{3700032}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3700032 · AMR-036-0032 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $p_c(z)=z^2+c$ for $c\in\mathbb C$, and
let $\mathcal H$ be the hyperbolic parameter set:
the orbit of its critical point $0$ tends to
an attracting periodic orbit or escapes to infinity.
The Julia set $J_c$ is the complement of the
normality set of the iterates on the sphere.
For $c\in\mathcal H$, the map is expanding on
$J_c$ and has no critical point there.

For a base point $w\in J_c$, define
\[
 P_c=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log
       \sum_{\substack{z\in J_c\\p_c^m(z)=w}}
                            |(p_c^m)'(z)|^{-1}.
\]
All $2^m$ preimages are counted; on this expanding
set they are distinct. The resulting pressure,
for the potential $-\log|p_c'|$, is independent
of the chosen Julia-set base point. Put
$P_*=\sup_{c\in\mathcal H}P_c$.

Determine which parameters maximize or nearly maximize
this pressure. Does a hyperbolic parameter attain
$P_*$? If not, describe maximizing sequences
$c_j\in\mathcal H$ with $P_{c_j}\to P_*$
and the possible locations of their limiting
parameters in the complex parameter plane.
Quantitative identification of regions where
$P_c>P_*-\varepsilon$, for arbitrary
$\varepsilon>0$, is part of the localization
question.
Both bounded and escaping critical orbits are
included. Numerical evidence for a large value
at one parameter is insufficient to establish
global maximality over all hyperbolic components.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Where in quadratic parameter space is the hyperbolic pressure largest, or arbitrarily close to largest?
