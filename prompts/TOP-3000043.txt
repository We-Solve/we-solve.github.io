\section*{Incomplete splitting-off in digraphs}\label{3000043}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000043 · AMR-029-0043 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $D=(V\cup\{s\},A)$ be a finite loopless directed multigraph,
with a distinguished vertex $s\notin V$ and integer $k\ge1$.
Assume that for every ordered pair of distinct vertices $x,y\in V$
there are $k$ pairwise arc-disjoint directed $x$--$y$ paths in $D$.
These paths may pass through $s$; no corresponding connectivity
requirement is imposed with $s$ as an endpoint.

A split at $s$ selects an incoming arc $u\to s$ and an outgoing
arc $s\to v$, removes them, and adds an arc $u\to v$.
If $u=v$, the resulting loop may be discarded, since it contributes
to no connectivity between distinct vertices. A collection of split
pairs is disjoint when no original arc occurs in two pairs.

Determine the maximum number of pairs that can be split simultaneously
while preserving $k$-arc-connectivity between every ordered pair in
$V$. Seek a min--max or other effective characterization and a
construction attaining the maximum.

The trivial upper bound is $\min\{d_D^-(s),d_D^+(s)\}$, but connectivity
can obstruct attaining it when the two degrees differ. The problem
does not assume that they are equal. A pair feasible on its own may
conflict with another feasible pair; the final graph after all chosen
splits must satisfy every connectivity requirement simultaneously.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many incoming-outgoing arc pairs can be split off at one
vertex while preserving the required connectivity among all other vertices?
