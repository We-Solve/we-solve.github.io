\section*{Kirby Problem 3.18}\label{2816}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2816 · KP-3.18 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(M,g)$ be a closed connected hyperbolic three-manifold with sectional
curvature $-1$. A smooth codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ partitions
$M$ into connected immersed surfaces called leaves, with local charts in
which leaves are the level sets of one coordinate. In particular it has no
singularities and its tangent planes form an integrable rank-two subbundle
of $TM$.

A leaf is minimal with respect to $g$ if its mean-curvature vector vanishes
identically. Locally, after choosing a unit normal, this says that the sum
of its two principal curvatures is zero. It means that area has zero first
variation under every compactly supported normal deformation.

Does there exist at least one closed hyperbolic three-manifold with a
codimension-one foliation all of whose leaves are minimal in its hyperbolic
metric? The leaves may be noncompact or dense in $M$; they are not required
to be fibers of a surface bundle. The question is existential over $M$ and
$\mathcal F$, not an assertion for every hyperbolic manifold. Making a
foliation minimal by choosing a different, nonhyperbolic Riemannian metric
does not answer it, since the curvature $-1$ metric is part of the requirement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does any closed hyperbolic three-manifold admit a nonsingular foliation whose every leaf has zero mean curvature in the hyperbolic metric?
