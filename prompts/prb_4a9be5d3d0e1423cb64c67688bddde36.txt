\section*{Perfect rational triangles}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle let $a,b,c>0$ be its side lengths,
$\Delta>0$ its area, and $m_a,m_b,m_c$ its medians, each joining a vertex
to the midpoint of the opposite side. Its altitudes are
$h_a=2\Delta/a$, $h_b=2\Delta/b$, and $h_c=2\Delta/c$.
The question asks for a triangle for which all ten of these quantities
are rational.

Equivalently, find positive rational $a,b,c$ satisfying the strict triangle
inequalities, together with positive rational $\Delta,m_a,m_b,m_c$, such that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 16\Delta^2&=(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)(a-b+c)(a+b-c),\\
 4m_a^2&=2b^2+2c^2-a^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
and the two cyclic versions of the median equation also hold. Rationality
of the altitudes then follows from the displayed formulas. Conversely,
rational sides and altitudes force rational area.

Uniform rational scaling preserves every rationality requirement and can
clear all denominators simultaneously. Thus an equivalent existence
question asks for integral sides, medians, altitudes, and area after a
suitable scaling. Strict nondegeneracy is indispensable; collinear triples
with zero area are excluded. Examples with rational medians but irrational
area do not settle the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a genuine triangle exist whose sides, medians, altitudes, and area
are all rational numbers?
