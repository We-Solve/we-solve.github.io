\section{First Hardy–Littlewood zeta-function conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\zeta(s)$ denote the meromorphic continuation to $\mathbb C$ of $\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}$, initially defined for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. A real ordinate $t$ is an odd-order zero on the critical line if $\zeta(\tfrac12+it)=0$ and the multiplicity of this zero as a zero of the holomorphic function $\zeta$ is an odd positive integer.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is it true that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $T_0(\varepsilon)>0$ such that for every real $T\ge T_0(\varepsilon)$ the interval
\[\bigl(T,T+T^{1/4+\varepsilon}\bigr]\]
contains an ordinate of an odd-order zero on the critical line? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every positive allowance in the exponent, must every sufficiently high interval of the specified length contain a critical-line zero of odd multiplicity?
