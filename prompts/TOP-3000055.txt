\section*{Min-sum two edge-disjoint paths}\label{3000055}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000055 · AMR-029-0055 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite undirected graph and let $(s_1,t_1)$ and
$(s_2,t_2)$ be specified vertex pairs. A path is simple and has length
equal to its number of edges. Seek paths $P_1,P_2$ satisfying
\[
 P_i:s_i\leadsto t_i\quad(i=1,2),\qquad
 E(P_1)\cap E(P_2)=\varnothing,
\]
whose total length $|E(P_1)|+|E(P_2)|$ is minimum.

The source asks for a combinatorial polynomial-time algorithm that
finds an optimum pair or reports that no feasible pair exists.
Its discussion already records randomized algebraic polynomial-time
methods; the desired conclusion is a deterministic combinatorial
method for the exact optimum. One can focus on four distinct terminals;
coinciding-terminal cases have the same explicit path convention.

Paths may share intermediate vertices: only their edge sets must be
disjoint. Their prescribed pairings cannot be exchanged. The objective
is the minimum sum, so neither path need be individually shortest
in the original graph; accepting a longer first path may permit a
much shorter second path. Finding two feasible disjoint paths, or
deciding whether two individually shortest paths can be disjoint,
does not solve the optimization problem. All edges in this formulation
have unit length; no ambiguity about arbitrary real input weights
is needed for the original unweighted target.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a deterministic combinatorial polynomial-time method for
two edge-disjoint paths with prescribed pairings and minimum total length.
