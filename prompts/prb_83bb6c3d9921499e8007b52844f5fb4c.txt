\section*{Kirby Problem 1.29}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A knot $K\subset S^3$ is hyperbolic if its complement admits a complete
finite-volume metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$. Write
$\operatorname{vol}(K)$ for that metric's volume. Define
$\det(K)=|\Delta_K(-1)|$, where $\Delta_K$ is its one-variable Alexander
polynomial normalized by $\Delta_K(1)=1$ and symmetry.
Let $\widetilde{\mathrm{Kh}}^{i,j}(K;\mathbb Q)$ be reduced Khovanov homology
over $\mathbb Q$, with its homological and quantum gradings. Set
\[
R(K)=\sum_{i,j}\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde{\mathrm{Kh}}^{i,j}(K;\mathbb Q).
\]
Equivalently this is the total free rank of reduced integral Khovanov homology,
so torsion contributes nothing to $R(K)$.

The questions are whether the following strict bounds hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{vol}(K)<2\pi\log\det(K)$ for every alternating hyperbolic knot;
\item $\operatorname{vol}(K)<2\pi\log R(K)$ for every hyperbolic knot.
\end{enumerate}
Here alternating means admitting a diagram with alternating overpasses and
underpasses, and $\log$ is the natural logarithm. These compare the volume of
the complete knot complement with discrete invariants of that same knot;
no choice of a particular diagram occurs in either inequality.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can determinant, and more generally reduced Khovanov rank, always bound a hyperbolic knot's volume by these logarithmic formulas?
