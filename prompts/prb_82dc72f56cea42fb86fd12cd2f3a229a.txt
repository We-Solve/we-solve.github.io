\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A connected manifold $X$ is aspherical if $\pi_k(X)=0$ for all $k\ge2$, equivalently its universal cover $\widetilde X$ is contractible in the usual manifold setting. Equip a compact Riemannian $X$ with its lifted metric on $\widetilde X$.

The source's conjecture is
\[
 X\text{ compact and aspherical}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       K\operatorname{-area}(\widetilde X)=\infty.
\]
The source does not specify whether compact manifolds have empty boundary, which dimensions are intended, or the precise noncompact $K$-area definition.

In a standard curvature formulation, $K$-area tests admissible homologically nontrivial Hermitian bundles with unitary connection, allowing their ranks to vary, against the supremum norm of curvature. Infinitude corresponds schematically to finding admissible nontrivial bundles with arbitrarily small curvature. The needed meaning of admissible, characteristic-number nontriviality, behavior outside compact sets, and possible odd-dimensional stabilization is not supplied here. No particular noncompact or stabilized definition is silently imposed, and no equivalence with Hilbert-space area is asserted.

The source states that the inequality, even in a stabilized form, implies the strong Novikov conjecture for $\pi_1(X)$. It does not define the stabilization or assembly-map version. It further describes this as stronger than ``non-asphericity for $\operatorname{Sc}\ge0$'' and cites [101]. That abbreviated scalar-curvature comparison lacks its manifold hypotheses and may contain a strictness or extraction issue; it is not promoted to an unrestricted claim about nonnegative scalar curvature.
