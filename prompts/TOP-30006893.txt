\section*{\texorpdfstring{Černý Conjecture on Shortest Reset Words of Synchronizing Automata}{Černý Conjecture on Shortest Reset Words of Synchronizing Automata}}\label{30006893}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A complete deterministic finite automaton consists of a finite state set $Q$, finite alphabet $\Sigma$, and transition function $\delta:Q\times\Sigma\to Q$, extended to words by successive transitions. A word $w\in\Sigma^*$ resets the automaton if $\delta(q,w)$ is the same state for every $q\in Q$. The automaton is synchronizing if some reset word exists. For $n=|Q|$, \v{C}ern\'y's conjecture asserts
\[
 \min\{|w|:w\text{ resets the automaton}\}\le(n-1)^2.
\]
The empty word is permitted and resets a one-state automaton. The assertion is about every synchronizing complete automaton, with no strong-connectivity or alphabet-size restriction. A polynomial algorithm finding some reset word does not prove this particular sharp quadratic length bound.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15655}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every synchronizing $n$-state deterministic automaton have a reset word of length at most $(n-1)^2$?
