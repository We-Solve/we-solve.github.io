\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $B^3=\{x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\mathbb R^3:|x|<1\}$. A real function $u$ is harmonic on $B^3$ if it is twice continuously differentiable and $\Delta u=\sum_{j=1}^3\partial^2u/\partial x_j^2=0$. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist harmonic functions $u_1,u_2,u_3:B^3\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[x\longmapsto (u_1(x),u_2(x),u_3(x))\]
is a homeomorphism from $B^3$ onto all of $\mathbb R^3$? \sref{2307026}{2}
The source distinguishes the three-dimensional question from Radó's negative result in two dimensions. \sref{2307026}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the three coordinates of a homeomorphism from the open three-dimensional ball onto Euclidean space all be harmonic?
