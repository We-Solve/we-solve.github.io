No commutativity of $G$ is assumed, and $gx,gy$ denote left multiplication. For $A\subseteq G^2$ put $\alpha=|A|/|G|^2$ and
\[C(A)=\sum_{x,y,g\in G}1_A(x,y)1_A(gx,y)1_A(x,gy).\]
The notation $C(A)\gg_\alpha |G|^3$ asks for a positive constant depending only on $\alpha$, uniformly over the groups. The source includes $g=1$; those degenerate triples number at most $|G|^2$. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Suppose $G$ is a finite group, and let $A \subset G \times G$ be a subset of density $\alpha$. Are there $\gg_\alpha |G|^3$ triples $x, y, g$ such that $(x, y), (gx, y), (x, gy)$ all lie in $A$? \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Prove or refute a uniform c(alpha)|G|\textasciicircum{}3 lower bound for nontrivial naïve corners in every sufficiently large finite group. \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Does density alone force many multiplicative corner configurations in the Cartesian square of every finite group?
