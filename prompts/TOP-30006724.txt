\section*{\texorpdfstring{Entanglement Area Law for 2D Gapped Quantum Systems}{Entanglement Area Law for 2D Gapped Quantum Systems}}\label{30006724}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $H_\Lambda$ be a finite-range bounded Hamiltonian on a finite square-lattice region, with fixed finite site dimension, unique normalized ground state $|\psi_\Lambda\rangle$, and spectral gap at least $\Delta>0$ uniformly in $\Lambda$. For $A\subseteq\Lambda$, put
\[
 \rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_{\Lambda\setminus A}|\psi_\Lambda\rangle\langle\psi_\Lambda|,
 \qquad S(\rho_A)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A\log\rho_A).
\]
The conjecture is $S(\rho_A)\le C|\partial A|$, where $|\partial A|$ counts lattice edges crossing to the complement. The constant may depend on the fixed interaction parameters and gap but not on the region or system size.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-026-05554-z}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Must entanglement in every uniformly gapped two-dimensional local ground state grow at most in proportion to a region's boundary?
