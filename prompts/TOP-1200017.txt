\section*{Some Questions — Question 17}\label{1200017}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200017 · AMR-011-0017 · Graph Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G$ be a group with a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of cardinality $d$, excluding the identity, and let $\mathcal C$ be its undirected Cayley graph. Its adjacency operator on $\ell^2(G)$ is
\[
 (Af)(g)=\sum_{s\in S}f(gs).
\]
This is a bounded self-adjoint operator of norm at most $d$. Its spectral measure at the identity is the probability measure $\mu$ on $[-d,d]$ characterized by
\[
 \int t^j\,d\mu(t)=\langle A^j\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle
                      \quad(j\geq0).
\]
These moments count closed walks based at the identity, and determine the measure uniquely on the compact interval.

For a finite $d$-regular simple graph $H$, let $\mu_H=|V(H)|^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{\lambda_i}$, where the adjacency eigenvalues are counted with multiplicity. Must there be finite $d$-regular graphs $H_n$ with $\mu_{H_n}$ converging weakly to $\mu$? Weak convergence means convergence of integrals of every continuous function on $[-d,d]$; equivalently here, all moments converge.

The approximating graphs need not be Cayley graphs, finite quotients of $G$, or connected. They may be chosen independently at each stage. One may arrange their orders to tend to infinity by taking disjoint unions without changing spectral measures. Using normalized random-walk operators divides every eigenvalue by $d$ and gives an equivalent formulation. The question asks only for spectral approximation, not convergence of all rooted neighborhood statistics.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every Cayley-graph spectral measure a weak limit of spectral measures of finite regular graphs of the same degree?
