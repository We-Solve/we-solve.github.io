\section*{Some Questions — Question 16}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G$ be a finite simple $d$-regular graph on $N$ vertices, where $d\geq2$ is fixed. Its adjacency matrix $A_G$ is real symmetric, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N$ counted with algebraic multiplicity. Define its empirical spectral distribution by
\[
 \mu_G=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{\lambda_i}.
\]
Determine which probability measures can occur in this way, and find effective natural restrictions on these distributions. A related limiting formulation asks which measures are weak limits of such $\mu_G$ as $N$ tends to infinity; weak convergence means convergence of integrals against every continuous function on $[-d,d]$.

Every exact distribution is a finitely supported measure with rational atom weights, is supported in $[-d,d]$, and satisfies
\[
 \int t\,d\mu_G(t)=0,\qquad \int t^2\,d\mu_G(t)=d.
\]
More generally, $N\int t^k\,d\mu_G(t)=\operatorname{tr}(A_G^k)$ counts closed walks of length $k$. These elementary constraints illustrate the kind of restrictions sought; they are not asserted to characterize all possible distributions.

Connectedness is not required in the source, so disjoint unions are permitted. Exact realizability and weak-limit realizability should be distinguished: limits need not be atomic, and the single eigenvalue $d$ in a connected graph can have vanishing normalized mass. A characterization must account for regular-graph structure beyond the usual conditions on arbitrary symmetric matrices.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What intrinsic restrictions characterize eigenvalue distributions of finite regular graphs and their limits?
