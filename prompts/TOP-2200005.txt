\section*{Problems Around Polynomials — Problem 2}\label{2200005}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2200005 · AMR-021-0005 · Algebra · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For integers $k,\ell\geq1$, let $\mathcal N_{k,\ell}$ be the set of real
polynomials in $\ell$ variables of total degree exactly $2k$ that are
nonnegative at every point of $\mathbb R^\ell$. For $P\in\mathcal N_{k,\ell}$,
a point $a\in\mathbb R^\ell$ is an isolated zero if $P(a)=0$ and some
Euclidean ball centered at $a$ contains no other zero of $P$. Count
distinct points, without algebraic multiplicities.

Define
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(P)=\#\{a:a\text{ is an isolated real zero of }P\},
 \qquad
 N(k,\ell)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_{k,\ell}}Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(P).
\]
Other components of the zero set are allowed; only isolated points
contribute to this count. Zeros at infinity of a homogenization are
not included. Coefficients are arbitrary real numbers, with no bound
on their size and no sum-of-squares assumption.

Determine $N(k,\ell)$, or obtain sharp asymptotics as $k\to\infty$ for
each fixed $\ell$. Sharpness requires examples as well as an upper
bound valid for every admissible polynomial. The scale is already
nontrivial: the polynomial
\[
 P(x_1,\ldots,x_\ell)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}
       \left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}(x_j-s)\right)^2
\]
has precisely the $k^\ell$ points of $\{1,\ldots,k\}^{\ell}$ as zeros.
This construction supplies a lower bound, but is not a conjectural
formula imposed on the unrestricted nonnegative class.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the largest possible number of isolated real zeros of a nonnegative polynomial of prescribed degree and number of variables.
