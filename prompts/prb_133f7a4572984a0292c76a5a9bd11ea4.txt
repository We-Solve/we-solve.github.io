\section*{Kirby Problem 3.77}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed rational homology $3$-sphere is a connected, compact, boundaryless
$3$-manifold $Y$ with $H_i(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong H_i(S^3;\mathbb Q)$ for every
$i$. Thus its rational Betti numbers are $1,0,0,1$.
A hyperbolic metric has constant sectional curvature $-1$.
For a compact Riemannian manifold $W$ with smooth boundary, the boundary
is totally geodesic if its second fundamental form vanishes; a geodesic
initially tangent to the boundary remains in it. The induced metric on
a totally geodesic hypersurface of a hyperbolic manifold is hyperbolic.

Do there exist an orientable compact connected hyperbolic $4$-manifold
$W$ and a hyperbolic rational homology $3$-sphere $Y$ such that
$\partial W$ is isometric to $Y$ with its induced metric? The boundary
must be the whole of $Y$, and the whole boundary of $W$ must have this
single component. The metric on $W$ is required to extend smoothly to
its boundary with the stated totally geodesic condition.

This is a geometric filling question, stronger than asking for a
smooth or topological manifold with boundary $Y$. Orientability is
required of the filling, not only its boundary. A nonorientable
hyperbolic filling does not meet that hypothesis. A cusp cross-section
of a noncompact finite-volume hyperbolic manifold is also a different
object: it is not a totally geodesic boundary of a compact hyperbolic
manifold. The problem imposes no arithmeticity condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a hyperbolic rational homology three-sphere be the entire totally geodesic boundary of a compact orientable hyperbolic four-manifold?
