\section*{Isolated left orders on amenable groups}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A left order on a group $G$ is a strict total order $<$ satisfying
$x<y\Rightarrow gx<gy$ for all $g,x,y\in G$.
Write $\operatorname{LO}(G)$ for the set of these orders.
An order is isolated if there is a finite set $F\subset G\setminus\{1\}$
such that it is the only left order assigning the prescribed sign to
every element of $F$. Thus isolation is with respect to the topology
of agreement on finitely many comparisons.

A group is amenable if it admits a finitely additive probability measure
on all its subsets that is invariant under left translation.
Does there exist a finitely generated amenable group $G$ for which
$\operatorname{LO}(G)$ is infinite and has an isolated point?
Finite generation means that products and inverses of some finite set
of elements exhaust $G$.

For countable virtually solvable groups, the space of left orders is
either finite or a Cantor set. Such groups cannot supply the requested example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an amenable group with finitely many generators have infinitely many left orders, one of which is determined by finitely many comparisons?
\subsection{Sources}
A. Navas, \emph{Group actions on 1-manifolds: a list of very concrete open
questions}, arXiv:1712.06462v2, Question 5,
\url{https://arxiv.org/html/1712.06462v2}.
C. Rivas and R. Tessera, \emph{On the space of left-orderings of virtually
solvable groups}, Groups, Geometry, and Dynamics 10 (2016), 65--90,
\url{https://doi.org/10.4171/GGD/343}.
\end{document}
