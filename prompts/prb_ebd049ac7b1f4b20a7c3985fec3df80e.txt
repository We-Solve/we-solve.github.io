\section*{Sharp lower bounds for entropic subspace approximation}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a random variable $X$ taking values in a finite abelian group, put
$H(X)=-\sum_x\mathbb P(X=x)\log_2\mathbb P(X=x)$, with $0\log_2 0=0$.
For distributions $X,Y$ on the same group, their entropic Ruzsa distance is
\[
 d[X,Y]=H(X'-Y')-\tfrac12 H(X)-\tfrac12 H(Y),
\]
where $X',Y'$ are independent variables having the indicated distributions.
In particular, the two copies used to define $d[X,X]$ are independent.
For a linear subspace $V\leq\mathbb F_2^n$, let $U_V$ have the uniform distribution on $V$.

To express a lower bound that must survive the choice of the best subspace, define, for $K>1$,
\[
 F(K)=\sup_{n\geq1}\ \sup_{X:\,d[X,X]\leq\log_2 K}
             \min_{V\leq\mathbb F_2^n}d[X,U_V].
\]
The workshop theorem supplies the upper estimate $F(K)\leq11\log_2 K$.
The problem is to obtain sharp lower bounds on $F(K)$, and thereby determine how small the universal coefficient of $\log_2 K$ can be in such approximation theorems.
A lower-bound example consists of a dimension and a distribution for which every subspace has the required separation; the subspace may depend on the distribution.
Uniform distributions on subspaces can have distance zero, so a positive lower bound is necessarily an extremal assertion over $X$, rather than an assertion about every admissible variable.
Translations of subspaces do not change this distance, so restricting the comparison distributions to linear subspaces does not lose affine translates.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How far can a distribution with small entropic doubling remain from every uniform subspace distribution?
