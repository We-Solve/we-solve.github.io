\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.65}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite soluble group $L$, its Fitting height $h(L)$ is the
least length $h$ of a normal series
$1=L_0\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq L_h=L$ having nilpotent
factors; set $h(1)=0$. A group is nilpotent when its lower central
series terminates at $1$. Solubility means that the derived series,
obtained by repeatedly taking commutator subgroups, terminates.

Let $\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ for a finite soluble group $G$.
Write $m=|\phi|$ for the order of the automorphism and
$s=|C_G(\phi)|$, where
$C_G(\phi)=\{g\in G:\phi(g)=g\}$.
Part (a) asks whether functions $f(m,s)$ and $a(m)$ exist such that
every such pair $(G,\phi)$ has a subgroup $H\le G$ satisfying
\[
 [G:H]\le f(m,s),\qquad h(H)\le a(m).
\]
Part (b) asks the stronger height bound $h(H)\le b(\Omega(m))$,
while still allowing the index bound to depend on $m,s$.
Here $\Omega(m)$ is the number of prime factors of $m$ counted with
multiplicity, with $\Omega(1)=0$. This is the composition length of
the cyclic group $\langle\phi\rangle$.

All bounding functions must work for every group and automorphism
with the indicated parameters. The subgroup may depend on both.
It is not required to be normal or $\phi$-invariant. Neither question
assumes the automorphism order is coprime to $|G|$. In particular,
part (b) allows the primes themselves to vary while keeping their
total multiplicity fixed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a finite soluble group admit a bounded-index subgroup of Fitting height controlled solely by the order, or the composition length, of a given automorphism?
