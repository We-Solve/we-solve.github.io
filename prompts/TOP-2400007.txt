\section{Mean value problem for polynomial critical points}\label{2400007}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2400007 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let \(P\in\mathbb C[z]\) be a polynomial of degree \(d\ge2\), and let \(z\in\mathbb C\) satisfy \(P'(z)\ne0\). A critical point is a complex number \(c\) such that \(P'(c)=0\). For such \(z\), no critical point equals \(z\), so the quantity
\[\mu(P,z)=\min_{P'(c)=0}\left|\frac{P(c)-P(z)}{(c-z)P'(z)}\right|\]
is well defined. Repeated critical points do not affect this minimum. Smale's known mean-value estimate gives \(\mu(P,z)\le4\); the usual sharper conjecture asks for \(\mu(P,z)\le1-1/d\).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does the bound \(\mu(P,z)\le1\) hold for every polynomial \(P\) of degree at least two and every point \(z\) at which \(P'(z)\ne0\)? Equivalently, must there exist a critical point \(c\) such that
\[|P(c)-P(z)|\le |P'(z)|\,|c-z|?\] \sref{2400007}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For any complex polynomial and any noncritical point, is there a critical point whose secant slope in absolute value is at most the derivative at the given point?
