\section*{Head-disjoint strongly connected orientations}\label{3000041}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000041 · AMR-029-0041 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $H=(V,\mathcal E)$ be a finite $3$-uniform hypergraph: every
hyperedge is a three-element subset of $V$. Hyperedges may be indexed
separately if repetitions are allowed. An orientation chooses one
head $h(E)\in E$ for each hyperedge. The other vertices of $E$ can
reach that head in one directed step.

Call an orientation strongly connected when every nonempty proper
$X\subset V$ has a hyperedge with
\[
                         h(E)\in X,\qquad E\setminus X\ne\varnothing.
\]
This is equivalently mutual reachability through finite directed
hyperedge steps. Three orientations $h_1,h_2,h_3$ are head-disjoint
when their three heads on every fixed $E$ are distinct, and hence
together use all vertices of that triple.

Characterize the $3$-uniform hypergraphs admitting three head-disjoint
strongly connected orientations. Seek conditions expressed in the
unoriented hypergraph that are both necessary and sufficient, together
with a construction when feasible.

Each orientation uses every hyperedge, so the requested disjointness
does not mean an edge partition into three subhypergraphs. A necessary
condition for every nontrivial $X$ is
$\sum_{E\text{ crossing }X}|E\cap X|\ge3$: across the three orientations
each crossing edge can contribute exactly $|E\cap X|$ heads inside $X$.
Whether proposed cut conditions suffice is part of the characterization
problem, not assumed in the definition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
When can every triple of a hypergraph be given three different
heads so that each of the three resulting orientations is strongly connected?
