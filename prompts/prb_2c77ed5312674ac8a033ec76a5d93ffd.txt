\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


The image of a map $f$ is $f(\mathbb F^n)$. The requested noncontainment says that some point in this image is absent from the comparison map\textquotesingle s image. $n^{\omega(1)}$ denotes growth faster than every fixed power of $n$.

Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a field. First, for functions $s=s(n)$, $m=m(n)$, and $r=r(n)$ satisfying $n^{\omega(1)}\leq s<m=n^r$, with $r$ relatively small (for example, $r=\log\log n$), construct an explicit polynomial map $f:\mathbb{F}^n\to\mathbb{F}^m$ of degree at most $2^n$ whose image is not contained in the image of any polynomial map $\Gamma:\mathbb{F}^s\to\mathbb{F}^m$ of degree at most $r$. Second, for integers $s<m=n^{r+1}$ and $2\leq r\leq O(1)$, given a map $\Gamma:\mathbb{F}^s\to\mathbb{F}^m$ of degree at most $2r-1$, design a polynomial-time algorithm that outputs an explicit map $f:\mathbb{F}^n\to\mathbb{F}^m$ of degree $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ whose image is not contained in the image of $\Gamma$. \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The field, computational meaning of explicit, and encoding of the input map remain those of the report. Both distinct parameter regimes are retained.
