\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.6}\label{2304006}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2304006 · AMR-022-4006 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer $\nu\geq0$, use the Hermite polynomial normalization
\[
 H_\nu(z)=(-1)^\nu e^{z^2}
                \frac{d^\nu}{dz^\nu}e^{-z^2};
 \qquad H_0=1,\quad H_1=2z.
\]
These are real polynomials of degree $\nu$, regarded as functions of
the complex variable $z$. For arbitrary integers $2\leq n<m$ and
arbitrary complex numbers $a,b$, set
$F_{n,m,a,b}(z)=1+H_1(z)+aH_n(z)+bH_m(z)$.
The choices $a=0$ or $b=0$ are permitted. The fixed linear term
ensures that this polynomial is never constant.

Is there a finite constant $C\geq0$ such that, for every choice of
$n,m,a,b$, some zero $\zeta$ of $F_{n,m,a,b}$ satisfies
$|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|\leq C$? Equivalently, is
\[
 \sup_{\substack{2\leq n<m\\a,b\in\mathbb C}}
     \min_{F_{n,m,a,b}(\zeta)=0}|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<\infty?
\]
The strip is centered on the real axis, and its width must be
independent of both polynomial degrees and both coefficients.
Neither a bound on the real part of the selected zero nor a
positive proportion of zeros in the strip is requested.
The normalization of the Hermite family is part of the problem:
rescaling the variable separately at each degree would change the
uniform-strip assertion. A negative answer requires parameters for
which the distance of every zero from the real axis tends to infinity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a combination of the constant and linear Hermite polynomials with two higher Hermite polynomials always have a zero in one fixed horizontal strip?
