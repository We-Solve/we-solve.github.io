\section*{Expansive directions of two-dimensional SFTs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X\subset A^{\mathbb Z^2}$ be an infinite nonempty subshift over a finite
alphabet, defined by finitely many forbidden finite patterns. Its shift
action is $(\alpha^v x)(u)=x(u+v)$ for $u,v\in\mathbb Z^2$. Fix any metric
$d_X$ compatible with the product topology. A line $L\subset\mathbb R^2$
through the origin is expansive if there exist $R,\delta>0$ such that
\[
 d_X(\alpha^v x,\alpha^v y)<\delta\quad
 \text{for every }v\in\mathbb Z^2\text{ with }\operatorname{dist}(v,L)<R
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=y.
\]
Define $E_1(\alpha)=\{w\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}:\mathbb Rw
\text{ is expansive}\}$. This definition is independent of the chosen
compatible metric.

Impose the additional hypothesis that, for at least one nonzero
$v_0\in\mathbb Z^2$, the single homeomorphism $\alpha^{v_0}:X\to X$ is
topologically conjugate to a two-sided shift of finite type. Characterize
precisely the sets $E\subset\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}$ that can arise as
$E_1(\alpha)$ under this hypothesis. A classification must give both
necessary restrictions and realizations of every permitted set.

Directions are unoriented lines, so $w$ and $-w$ determine the same test;
the set records all real directions, including irrational slopes.
This is the constrained part of Boyle's Problem 11.1. The unrestricted
realization question, without the finite-type element hypothesis, is
recorded as solved on the author's update page.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which expansive directions can a two-dimensional finite-type shift have when one lattice direction itself acts as a finite-type shift?
