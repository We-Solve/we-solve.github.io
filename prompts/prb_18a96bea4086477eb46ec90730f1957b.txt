\section{Open Problems on Billiards and Geometric Optics}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A flow has time parameter $t\in\mathbb R$; a cascade is an invertible discrete-time system with $t\in\mathbb Z$. They satisfy $g^{t+s}=g^t\circ g^s$ and $g^0=\mathrm{id}$. The trapped set is $T_g=\{x:\{g^t x:t\ge0\}\text{ is bounded and }\{g^t x:t\le0\}\text{ is unbounded}\}$. Hausdorff dimension is the metric dimension in the Euclidean plane. The source asks for $C^m$ regularity in $x$, or in $(x,t)$ for flows.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $g^t:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be a Lebesgue-measure-preserving flow or cascade. A point is trapped if its positive semiorbit is bounded and its negative semiorbit is unbounded; let $T_g$ be the trapped set. What is the maximum Hausdorff dimension of $T_g$? More specifically, for $C^m$ systems determine the maximal dimension $d(m)$, between $1$ and $2$. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the largest Hausdorff dimension of points that stay bounded forward in time but escape backward in area-preserving planar flows or discrete systems, and determine its dependence on differentiability.
