\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 7.55}\label{2307055}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2307055 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\ge2$, $B^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|<1\}$ and $S^{n-1}=\partial B^n$. Let $D\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a Jordan domain and let $f:B^n\to D$ be a quasiconformal homeomorphism with a homeomorphic boundary extension $\bar f:\overline{B^n}\to\overline D$. Here quasiconformal means $f\in W^{1,n}_{\rm loc}$ and $\|Df\|^n\le K|\det Df|$ almost everywhere for some finite $K$. Assume $\mathcal H^{n-1}(\partial D)<\infty$, where $\mathcal H^{n-1}$ is Hausdorff surface measure. Boundary absolute continuity is the following preservation of surface-measure-zero Borel sets; the inverse is tested in the opposite direction.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must both of the following hold?
\[\mathcal H^{n-1}(E)=0,\ E\subset S^{n-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal H^{n-1}(\bar f(E))=0,
\]
\[\mathcal H^{n-1}(F)=0,\ F\subset\partial D\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal H^{n-1}(\bar f^{-1}(F))=0?
\]
Retain also the inverse question in dimension $3$ under the additional assumption that $f$ extends quasiconformally to $\mathbb R^3$. The source records affirmative planar conformal results, planar quasiconformal counterexamples, and Gehring's affirmative forward result in this three-dimensional extension case. \sref{2307055}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether a quasiconformal boundary map, and its inverse, preserve surface-null sets when the target boundary has finite surface measure, including the inverse question for extendable three-dimensional maps.
