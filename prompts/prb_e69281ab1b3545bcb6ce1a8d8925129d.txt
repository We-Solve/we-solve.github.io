\section*{Random linear differential equations}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer $n\ge1$, let $A_0,\ldots,A_n$ be independent real normal
random variables of mean zero and variance one. With probability one
$A_n\ne0$, so
\[
 P_n(z)=A_nz^n+A_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+A_0
\]
has degree $n$. Define
\[
 p_n=\mathbb P\{\operatorname{Re}z<0
                 \text{ for every root }z\text{ of }P_n\}.
\]
Roots are tested with their usual complex multiplicities, although only
their locations matter for the event. Strict negativity excludes roots
on the imaginary axis.

Equivalently, $p_n$ is the probability that the zero solution of the
constant-coefficient equation
$A_nx^{(n)}+\cdots+A_1x'+A_0x=0$ is globally asymptotically stable
when the state consists of $x,x',\ldots,x^{(n-1)}$. The question has two
parts: determine an asymptotic expansion of $p_n$ as $n\to\infty$, and
decide whether $p_{n+1}<p_n$ for every $n\ge1$.

The distribution is exactly the independent identically distributed
Gaussian coefficient model above. Conditioning a leading coefficient to
equal one, choosing random roots directly, or taking independent entries
of a random matrix gives different probabilities. An asymptotic result
should specify its scale and error term, distinguishing a logarithmic
decay estimate from a full leading equivalent or higher-order expansion.
Strict monotonicity is a separate assertion for all degrees and would not
follow merely from eventual asymptotic decay.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the asymptotic probability that a Gaussian-coefficient polynomial is Hurwitz stable, and decide whether it strictly decreases with degree.
