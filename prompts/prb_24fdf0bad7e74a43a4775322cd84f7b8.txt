\section*{A collision budget for Eulerian second neighborhoods}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $D=(V,E)$ be a finite simple oriented graph, with no loops or
opposite arcs. It is Eulerian in the degree sense when
$d^+(v)=d^-(v)$ for every vertex $v$; isolated vertices and separate
components are allowed. Here $d^+(v)$ and $d^-(v)$ are the numbers
of outgoing and incoming neighbours.
Let $N_2^+(v)$ be the set of vertices at shortest directed distance
two from $v$, excluding both $v$ itself and its immediate outgoing
neighbours. Only distinct vertices are counted.

The proposed aggregate second-neighbourhood inequality is
\[
                       \sum_{v\in V}|N_2^+(v)|
                              \ \ge\ \sum_{v\in V}d^+(v)=|E|.
\]
Thus the number of ordered pairs $(v,w)$ at directed distance exactly
two should be at least the number of arcs. A pair admitting several
two-edge paths contributes only once to the left-hand side, and a
pair joined directly by an arc never contributes there.

The problem asks for this inequality for every Eulerian oriented
graph, not just a vertex whose individual second-neighbourhood is
large. Balanced indegree and outdegree are required vertex by vertex;
equality of the total indegree and total outdegree, which holds for
every digraph, is insufficient. Connectedness can equivalently be
imposed component by component since both sides add across components.
The unweighted simple-graph convention prevents multiplicity from
changing the degree balance while leaving neighbourhood cardinalities
unchanged.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every Eulerian oriented graph have at least as many ordered distance-two pairs as arcs?
