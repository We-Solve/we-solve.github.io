\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
In the hard-sphere model, configurations whose particle centers are closer than the sphere diameter are forbidden; allowed configurations otherwise have no pair energy. The thermodynamic limit sends volume and particle number to infinity at fixed density or fixes chemical potential. A fluid--solid phase transition concerns distinct macroscopic equilibrium phases and a thermodynamic singularity or coexistence criterion; crystalline order and symmetry breaking require specified observables and Gibbs-state notions.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can statistical mechanics prove the existence of a thermodynamic fluid-solid phase transition, for example in the hard-sphere model?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The dimension, ensemble and phase criterion are not fixed by the extracted question. Numerical crystallization or a finite-system packing optimum does not by itself prove the requested thermodynamic transition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove a genuine equilibrium fluid-to-solid phase transition, with hard spheres as a central example.
