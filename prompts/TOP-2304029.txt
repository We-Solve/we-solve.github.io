\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a polynomial $R\in\mathbb C[z]$, write $Z(R)=\{z\in\mathbb C:R(z)=0\}$, ignoring multiplicities. Let $P,Q$ be monic complex polynomials with
\[Z(P)=Z(Q),\qquad Z(P')=Z(Q').
\]
For a constant monic polynomial the polynomial is $1$ and its zero derivative has zero set $\mathbb C$; the nonconstant case is the substantive one.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must there exist positive integers $a,b$ such that $P(z)^a\equiv Q(z)^b$ as polynomials? The assumptions require equality of zero sets, not equality of multiplicities; the source's special cases of at most five distinct common zeros or all common zeros collinear do not replace the general question. \sref{2304029}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If two monic polynomials have exactly the same zeros and their derivatives also have exactly the same zeros, must positive powers of the polynomials coincide?
