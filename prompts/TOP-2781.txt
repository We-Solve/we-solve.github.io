\section*{Kirby Problem 2.33}\label{2781}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2781 · KP-2.33 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a compact connected surface $S$ without boundary. Let
$\operatorname{Homeo}(S)$ be the group of all self-homeomorphisms of $S$,
with composition as its operation. A group $G$ acts on $S$ by homeomorphisms
through a homomorphism $\rho:G\to\operatorname{Homeo}(S)$. Its action is
faithful precisely when $\ker\rho=\{1\}$.

A group is finitely generated if some finite subset generates it under
multiplication and inversion. It is torsion-free if $g^n=1$ for a positive
integer $n$ implies $g=1$. The question asks whether, for each fixed $S$,
there exists a finitely generated torsion-free group $G$ such that
\[
 \ker\rho\ne\{1\}\quad
 \text{for every homomorphism }\rho:G\to\operatorname{Homeo}(S).
\]
Thus $G$ must admit no faithful topological action on that surface. The group
may depend on $S$; the formulation does not require a single group working
for all surfaces. No differentiability, measure-preservation, or orientation-
preservation assumption is imposed on the actions. In particular, obstructing
only smooth actions would not answer the question, and nonfaithful actions
are allowed to be nontrivial.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each closed surface, find a finitely generated torsion-free group that cannot act faithfully on it by homeomorphisms, or show this is impossible.
