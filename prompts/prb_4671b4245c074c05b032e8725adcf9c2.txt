\section{Kirby Problem 4.123}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $M$ be an oriented smooth four-manifold and $\mathcal F$ a nonsingular smooth foliation by two-dimensional leaves. Coorientability is an orientation of the rank-two normal bundle $TM/T\mathcal F$; together with the orientation of $M$ this orients its leaves. The source calls $\mathcal F$ taut if there is a smooth two-form $\omega$ whose restriction to each leaf is a positive area form and
\[
 d\omega(v_1,v_2,z)=0
 \quad(v_1,v_2\in T\mathcal F,\ z\in TM).
\]
This does not require $\omega$ to be a symplectic form. A closed leaf $L$ is a compact connected oriented surface without boundary. Its class $[L]$ lies in integral singular homology $H_2(M;\mathbb Z)$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such $M,\mathcal F$ and every closed leaf $L$, is $L$ smoothly genus-minimizing in its homology class? Explicitly, for every closed connected oriented smoothly embedded surface $S\subset M$ with $[S]=[L]$, must $\operatorname{genus}(S)\geq\operatorname{genus}(L)$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every compact leaf of a coorientable smooth taut two-dimensional foliation in an oriented four-manifold have the least genus among smooth surfaces representing its homology class?
