\section*{Edge-covering number of 2-polymatroids}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S$ be finite. A $2$-polymatroid rank function is a map
$f:2^S\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ with $f(\varnothing)=0$, monotone under
inclusion, submodular, and satisfying $f(X)\le2|X|$. Submodularity means
$f(X)+f(Y)\ge f(X\cap Y)+f(X\cup Y)$.
Assume a matroid $M$ on $S\times\{1,2\}$, with rank function $r$,
represents $f$:
\[
                    r(X\times\{1,2\})=f(X).
\]
A circuit is an inclusion-minimal dependent set of $M$. Require that
every circuit contain at most one member of each pair
$\{(e,1),(e,2)\}$.

A matching of $f$ is $J\subseteq S$ with $f(J)=2|J|$.
Let $\mathcal M$ be the family of these matchings and define
\[
 \beta(f)=\min\{t:S=J_1\cup\cdots\cup J_t, J_i\in\mathcal M\},
\]
\[
 \beta^*(f)=\min\left\{\sum_{J\in\mathcal M}z_J:
 z_J\ge0,\ \sum_{J\ni e}z_J\ge1\ (e\in S)\right\}.
\]
In the nonvacuous case where a cover exists, must
$\beta(f)\le\beta^*(f)+1$ hold?

Coverability is equivalent to $f(\{e\})=2$ for every $e$; otherwise
both covering problems are infeasible. Matchings in a cover may overlap,
and fractional coefficients are arbitrary nonnegative reals. The
circuit restriction is a hypothesis on the given representation and
must not be discarded in favor of an arbitrary $2$-polymatroid. The
claimed additive loss is one, rather than a multiplicative approximation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For the specified matroid-representable 2-polymatroids, is the integral matching-cover number at most the fractional number plus one?
