\section{Sphere Packing in High Dimensions}\label{1227}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1227 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


An equal-sphere packing in $\mathbb R^n$ is a union $P=\bigcup_{c\in C}B(c,1)$ of closed Euclidean unit balls whose interiors are pairwise disjoint; equivalently $\lVert c-c'\rVert\geq2$ for distinct centers. Let $B(x,R)$ be the radius-$R$ ball centered at $x$ and define
\[
\overline\delta(P)=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^n}
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(P\cap B(x,R))}{\operatorname{vol}_n(B(x,R))},\qquad
\Delta_n=\sup_{P}\overline\delta(P).
\]
The supremum is over all packings, including nonlattice and nonperiodic ones.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each integer $n\geq1$ with $n\notin\{1,2,3,8,24\}$, determine $\Delta_n$ and an equal-sphere packing attaining it. Determine also the asymptotic behavior of $\Delta_n$ as $n\to\infty$. \sref{1227}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the densest sphere packings in the remaining dimensions and determine how their optimal densities behave as the dimension grows.
