\section{Olson's Conjecture}\label{3376}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3376 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be any finite group, written multiplicatively, with identity $1_G$ and $|G|=n\ge1$. A sequence may repeat elements. A subsequence retains the order of its indices. Also define $z(G)$ as the least $m$ such that every length-$m$ sequence in $G$ has an ordered subsequence of length $n$ with product $1_G$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every sequence $a_1,\ldots,a_{2n-1}$ in $G$, must there exist
\[
 1\le j_1<j_2<\cdots<j_n\le2n-1,
 \qquad a_{j_1}a_{j_2}\cdots a_{j_n}=1_G?
\]
Equivalently, is $z(G)\le2|G|-1$ for every finite group? The source also asks for improved general bounds on $z(G)$ beyond $z(G)\le|G|(|G|-1)+1$. Multiplication follows the original order even for nonabelian groups. \sref{3376}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a product-one subsequence of group-order length without rearranging its terms, and improve bounds on the minimum sequence length guaranteeing one.
