\section{Equilibrium point configurations on the line}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $(a_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$ be a strictly increasing, locally finite sequence of points on the real line, with $a_n\to\pm\infty$ as $n\to\pm\infty$. Each point represents an identical particle, and the repulsive force at distance $r>0$ has magnitude $r^{-2}$. The force from each side is required to be finite:
\[\sum_{i<n}(a_n-a_i)^{-2}<\infty,\qquad \sum_{i>n}(a_i-a_n)^{-2}<\infty.\]
Mechanical equilibrium at $a_n$ means equality of these two sums, or equivalently $\sum_{i\ne n}|a_i-a_n|^{-2}\operatorname{sgn}(a_i-a_n)=0$, where $\operatorname{sgn}$ is the sign function.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If every particle is in equilibrium, must there exist $\alpha>0$ and $\beta\in\mathbb R$ such that $a_n=\alpha n+\beta$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z$? The question includes aperiodic configurations and does not assume a global bound on consecutive gaps. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must an infinite equilibrium configuration of identical particles with inverse-square repulsion be equally spaced?
