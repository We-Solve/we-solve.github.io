\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 1.9}\label{7000009}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000009 · AMR-069-0009 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $g$ be a $C^\infty$ Riemannian metric on a neighborhood of
a point $p$ in a smooth two-manifold. In local coordinates
$(u^1,u^2)$ write $g=\sum_{i,j=1}^2g_{ij}\,du^i\,du^j$, with
$(g_{ij})$ positive definite. A local smooth isometric embedding
is a $C^\infty$ embedding $f:U\to\mathbb R^3$ on some open
neighborhood $U$ of $p$ satisfying
\[
        \left\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial u^i},
                       \frac{\partial f}{\partial u^j}\right\rangle
                         =g_{ij}\qquad(1\le i,j\le2).
\]
Does such a neighborhood and embedding exist for every smooth
positive-definite metric and every point $p$?

The neighborhood may be shrunk as much as necessary; no global
embedding is requested. There is no assumption that the Gauss
curvature is nonzero, has a fixed sign, or vanishes to finite
order. In particular, difficult behavior at flat points is part
of the statement.

A theorem for analytic metrics or metrics with additional
curvature hypotheses establishes only a special case. An
immersion of rank two is locally an embedding after further
restriction, so the local issue is the smooth solvability of
the metric equations, not global self-intersection.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every smooth metric on a surface, including near degenerate
zero-curvature points, be realized locally by a smooth surface
in ordinary three-dimensional space?
