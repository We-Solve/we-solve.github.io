\section{Stable Density on Subspaces}\label{143}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 143 · Combinatorics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix $0<\epsilon<1$. If $A\subset\mathbb F_2^n$ and $V\leq\mathbb F_2^n$ is a linear subspace, put $\alpha_A(V)=|A\cap V|/|V|$. Call $V$ $\epsilon$-stable for $A$ if
\[
 |\alpha_A(V)-\alpha_A(V')|\leq\epsilon
 \quad\text{for every linear }V'\leq V\text{ with }\dim V'=\dim V-1.
\]
The zero subspace is stable vacuously. Write $\operatorname{codim}(V)=n-\dim V$ and define the optimal uniform bound
\[
 D(\epsilon)=\sup_{n\geq1}\ \sup_{A\subset\mathbb F_2^n}\
 \min\{\operatorname{codim}(V):V\text{ is }\epsilon\text{-stable for }A\}.
\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Obtain sharp bounds for $D(\epsilon)$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$: how small a codimension, uniformly in $n$ and $A$, suffices to find a subspace whose density changes by at most $\epsilon$ on every codimension-one subspace? The source records an exponential-in-$1/\epsilon$ upper bound and power-of-$1/\epsilon$ lower examples; the question is to determine the correct quantitative dependence. \sref{143}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine how small a codimension guarantees a linear subspace on which the density of any given binary-vector-space set is stable under passing to every hyperplane.
