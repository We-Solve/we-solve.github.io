\section{A discrete iteration related to Pierce expansions}\label{3348}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3348 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For integers $a>b>0$, set $b_1=b$ and, whenever $b_i>0$, let $b_{i+1}$ be the least nonnegative remainder of $a$ on division by $b_i$. The numerator $a$ remains fixed throughout. Since $0\leq b_{i+1}<b_i$, the sequence reaches zero. Define $P(a,b)=n$ when $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ are positive and $b_{n+1}=0$. Here $\log$ is the natural logarithm.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there an absolute constant $C>0$ such that $P(a,b)\leq C(\log a)^2$ for every pair of integers $a>b>0$? \sref{3348}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does repeatedly replacing the current denominator by the remainder of one fixed numerator always reach zero within a constant times the square of the numerator logarithm?
