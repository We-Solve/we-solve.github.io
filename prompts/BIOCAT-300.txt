\section*{Number of independent origins of life on Earth}
\label{prob:300}
\label{rank:329}
\par\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Domain.} Origins of life; lost-lineage inference. \quad \textbf{Retained status/source context.} Open historical inference question that is generally non-identifiable from surviving common ancestry alone. A shared ancestor of extant life does not count all extinct or assimilated origins.

\paragraph{Definitions and assumptions.} Let $K\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$ be the number of independent abiogenesis events on Earth within a declared early-history interval, where independence excludes ordinary descent or import of already living systems. Origin $i$ generates descendants with survival probability $s_i$ and may merge, exchange material or leave no trace. Data $D$ include extant phylogenies, geological signatures and chemically discriminating observations with specified detection models. LUCA is the ancestor of sampled survivors, not the definition of $K$. A finite event--survival model and prior on $K$ are required for posterior calculation.

\paragraph{Problem statement.} Characterize the identified set
\[
\mathcal K_D=\{K:\exists\theta\in\Theta_K\text{ with }P_{K,\theta}(D)
\text{ compatible with observation uncertainty}\},
\]
and report when it has no informative observation-derived upper bound within the declared event--survival model. If estimating $P(K\mid D)$, expose sensitivity to priors, extinction and assimilation assumptions. Determine what evidence can distinguish $K=1$ from $K\geq2$ with a declared error criterion.

\paragraph{Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.} Permit $K=0$ when importation is allowed; extant terrestrial life alone does not establish a local origin within the chosen interval. If the model allows extra origins with no surviving descendants, no traces and no effects on recorded lineages, arbitrarily many such origins can be appended without changing the observable law. This is a known-by-construction non-identification result. An informative upper bound therefore needs explicit trace, survival or event-rate restrictions, not merely a prior favoring small counts.

\paragraph{Alternative formulations.} (i) An extant shadow-biosphere search asks whether a chemically distinct lineage with an independent origin survives today, requiring explicit sampling and contamination models. (ii) A geological formulation asks whether lost origins left distinguishable relics; absence of such relics constrains $K$ only through preservation and detection assumptions. A simplified Poisson-thinning model observes survivors at mean $\lambda s$, identifying their product rather than total origin intensity $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Mathematical-status qualification.} The restricted calculation, identity, obstruction or construction identified in this entry is not itself a new open conjecture. The residual target is the explicitly delimited extension, identification, implementation or independent biological validation; an already known special case does not satisfy that residual target.

\paragraph{Obstructions and partial progress.} Universal relatedness among known cellular life supports shared surviving ancestry, while LUCA reconstruction provides information about that ancestry. Neither yields an informative observation-derived upper bound on erased origins in this survival model. Resource and time constraints may impose an extrinsic finite cap, which common surviving ancestry does not estimate. With survival or trace probability allowed to approach zero, arbitrarily many past events may fit the same data. The supplied origin essay frames the historical question and Wikipedia is background only. Recognizing a non-identifiability result is a legitimate resolution of one formulation; obtaining new discriminating evidence remains the narrowed research target.

\paragraph{Probability laws and data.}

\mathbb{P}\{\lvert\widetilde g_j-g_j^*\rvert\le r_j\text{ for every }j\}\ge1-\alpha.

\[
\max_j\frac{|\widehat g_j-\widetilde g_j|+r_j}{\varepsilon_j}\le1.
