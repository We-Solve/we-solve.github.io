\section{Kirby Problem 5.9}\label{3016}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3016 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix $n\geq p\geq1$. A smooth map germ $(\mathbb R^n,0)\to(\mathbb R^p,0)$ is singular when its derivative at the distinguished point has rank less than $p$. Germs are right-left equivalent if local source and target diffeomorphisms conjugate one to the other. Let $S$ be any specified set of such singular-germ equivalence classes. A smooth map $f:M\to\mathbb R^p$ is an $S$-map if its germ at every singular point represents a member of $S$; regular points impose no extra restriction.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every choice of $n,p,S$ and smooth $n$-manifolds $M_1,M_2$ that are homeomorphic, does the existence of an $S$-map $M_1\to\mathbb R^p$ imply the existence of an $S$-map $M_2\to\mathbb R^p$? Equivalently, is the existence of such maps independent of the smooth structure on a fixed topological manifold? \sref{3016}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does changing the smooth structure of a manifold preserve the existence of a map into Euclidean space having only a prescribed set of singularities?
