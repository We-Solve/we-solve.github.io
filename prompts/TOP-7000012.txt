\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 2.3}\label{7000012}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7000012 · AMR-069-0012 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M,M'\subset\mathbb R^3$ be smooth connected closed oriented
embedded surfaces. Write $N:M\to S^2$ and $N':M'\to S^2$ for
their oriented unit normal maps, and $K,K'$ for their Gauss
curvatures, the products of the principal curvatures.

Suppose a smooth diffeomorphism $f:M\to M'$ satisfies the two
pointwise identities
\[
                         N'\circ f=N,\qquad K'\circ f=K.
\]
Must the two surfaces be congruent? Precisely, must there exist
an orthogonal matrix $Q\in O(3)$ and a vector $a\in\mathbb R^3$
such that $M'=QM+a$?

The correspondence preserves the actual normal vector in the
fixed ambient coordinates, not merely the set of directions
visited by the Gauss map. It also preserves curvature at
corresponding points; matching total curvature is insufficient.
No intrinsic isometry, area-preservation assumption, or convexity
is imposed in addition to the two displayed equations.

The conclusion concerns congruence of the surfaces as subsets;
it does not require the given diffeomorphism itself to be the
restriction of that rigid motion. Flat points and regions, where
$K=0$ and the Gauss map may fail to be locally invertible, remain
within the statement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do matching unit normals and matching Gauss curvature under a
diffeomorphism force two closed surfaces in space to have the
same shape up to a rigid motion?
