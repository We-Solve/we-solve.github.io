\section{Sticky Cantor sets}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $K\subseteq[0,1]$ be the middle-thirds Cantor set. An embedded Cantor set in $\mathbb R^n$ means a subset $C$ homeomorphic to $K$; it need not lie on a line. Say $C$ can be moved off itself at scale $\varepsilon>0$ if there is a homeomorphism $h:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
\[
 \|h(x)-x\|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^n,
 \qquad h(C)\cap C=\varnothing.
\]
Call $C$ sticky in this small-homeomorphism sense if this fails at some scale $\varepsilon>0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each $n\ge1$, is every embedded Cantor set movable off itself at every scale? Equivalently, determine exactly which dimensions admit a $C$ and an $\varepsilon>0$ such that every ambient homeomorphism moving each point by less than $\varepsilon$ has $h(C)\cap C\ne\varnothing$. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine in which Euclidean dimensions an embedded Cantor set cannot be separated from itself by a sufficiently small ambient homeomorphism.
