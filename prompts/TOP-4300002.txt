\section*{Mixing of all orders}\label{4300002}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4300002 · AMR-042-0002 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a compact connected metrizable abelian group with normalized
Haar measure $\mu$, and let $\alpha:\mathbb Z^d\to\operatorname{Aut}(X)$
be an action by continuous group automorphisms, where $d\ge1$.
The action is mixing if
\[
 \mu(A\cap\alpha^{-h}B)\longrightarrow\mu(A)\mu(B)
             \quad\text{as }\|h\|\to\infty
\]
for all measurable $A,B\subset X$. It is mixing of all orders if, for
every $k\ge2$ and measurable $A_1,\ldots,A_k$,
\[
 \mu\!\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^k\alpha^{-h_i}A_i\right)
       \longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^k\mu(A_i)
 \quad\text{as }\min_{i\ne j}\|h_i-h_j\|\to\infty.
\]
The theorem that mixing implies mixing of all orders in this connected
algebraic setting is established. Ward's question is to find a proof
using simpler dynamical ideas that avoids the deep Diophantine estimates
used in the known argument.

Accordingly the desired output is a new proof method, not a counterexample
to the theorem or another statement of its conclusion. Any proposed proof
must retain arbitrary order $k$ and arbitrary mutually separating
translation vectors; a proof for three sets or for one prescribed family
of directions is only partial. Connectedness is essential to the stated
setting, since zero-dimensional algebraic actions can have a finite mixing
order. The abelian-group formulation fixes the standard algebraic-dynamics
class to which the quoted theorem applies and makes the Haar measure and
all translation limits explicit.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a dynamical proof that connected mixing algebraic actions are mixing of all orders, avoiding the known Diophantine machinery.
