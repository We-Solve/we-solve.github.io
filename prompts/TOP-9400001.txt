\section{Büchi's problem}\label{9400001}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400001 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A B\"uchi sequence of length $L\ge3$ is an integer tuple $(x_0,\ldots,x_{L-1})$ satisfying
\[
x_{j+2}^2-2x_{j+1}^2+x_j^2=2\quad(0\le j\le L-3).
\]
It is trivial if there exists $a\in\mathbb Z$ with $x_j^2=(a+j)^2$ for every $0\le j<L$. Signs of the $x_j$ themselves are unrestricted; consecutive squares may decrease before increasing.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist an integer $M\ge3$ such that every B\"uchi sequence of every length $L\ge M$ is trivial? Equivalently, is there a uniform upper bound on the length of nontrivial integer B\"uchi sequences? \sref{9400001}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every sufficiently long integer-square sequence with second differences equal to two come from consecutive integers squared?
