\section*{Rational Goldberg extremals}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $N\geq2$. A rational function of degree
$N$ means $f=P/Q$ with relatively prime complex
polynomials and $\max(\deg P,\deg Q)=N$.
Require $Q$ to have no zero in $\mathbb D$.
Only zeros and $1$-points inside this open disk enter
the following problems; poles outside it are allowed.

First consider functions with exactly one simple zero
$z_0$ and two $1$-points $z_1,z_2$, counting
multiplicity. Minimize their hyperbolic enclosing radius
$\inf_{c\in\mathbb D}\max_jd_{\mathbb D}(c,z_j)$,
where
$d_{\mathbb D}(z,w)=2\operatorname{artanh}
|(z-w)/(1-\overline w z)|$.

Next impose $z_0=0$ and determine both the infimum
of $\max(|z_1|,|z_2|)$ and the supremum of
$|f'(0)|$. Determine the same two quantities in the
real subclass $f(\overline z)=\overline{f(z)}$.
Finally, within that real subclass, require two
distinct simple $1$-points precisely at $\pm ia$,
$0<a<1$, and minimize $a$.

Solve these extremal problems for each fixed $N$,
including attainment and the corresponding extremal
rational functions. Except in the final variant,
a double $1$-point is permitted. The sole zero is
simple in every variant, and there are no additional
zeros or $1$-points in the disk.
Degree is the global rational-map degree, not the
number of preimages counted within the disk. A
limit in which numerator and denominator acquire a
common factor can lower degree and therefore need
not attain an extremum in the prescribed class.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Solve the Goldberg and related disk extremal problems among rational functions of each fixed degree.
