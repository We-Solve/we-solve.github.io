\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.86}\label{2302086}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2302086 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an entire function $f$, write $f^{\circ0}(z)=z$ and $f^{\circ(n+1)}=f\circ f^{\circ n}$. Its Fatou set is the set on which the iterates form a normal family: every sequence has a subsequence converging locally uniformly in the spherical metric to a meromorphic function or to $\infty$. A Fatou component is a connected component of this set. Put $f_\lambda(z)=\lambda(e^z-1)$, $|\lambda|=1$. A Siegel domain $S$ containing $0$ is an invariant Fatou component on which $f_\lambda$ is conformally conjugate to an irrational rotation of a disk. The unique finite asymptotic value of $f_\lambda$ is $-\lambda$.
The source update attributes affirmative answers to both parts to Rempe (the first using a method of Ghys).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
(a) Is there a parameter $\lambda$ for which a Siegel domain $S$ containing $0$ is bounded? (b) Whenever such a domain $S$ is unbounded, must $-\lambda$ lie on $\partial S$? \sref{2302086}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can this exponential family have a bounded Siegel domain, and must its unique asymptotic value lie on the boundary of every unbounded Siegel domain?
