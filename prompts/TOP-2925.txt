\section*{Kirby Problem 4.49}\label{2925}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2925 · KP-4.49 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An open topological $4$-manifold $M$ has empty boundary and no compact
component; manifolds are Hausdorff and second countable. It admits a
compactification by a manifold boundary if there is a compact topological
$4$-manifold $W$ with boundary and a homeomorphism
$M\cong\operatorname{int}(W)$. The added set must be an actual
$3$-manifold boundary, rather than arbitrary points or a singular
compactifying space.

Give an effective necessary and sufficient criterion for this property.
Such a criterion should identify exactly the interiors of compact
$4$-manifolds using accessible data about $M$, in particular its behavior
near infinity. It should supply both an obstruction when compactification
fails and a sufficient condition ensuring a manifold-boundary
compactification.

A neighborhood of infinity is a subset containing the complement of
some compact subset of $M$. An end records a compatible choice of
components of such complements as the compact subset increases.
A compact manifold boundary gives collared neighborhoods of its ends,
modeled on $Y\times[0,\infty)$ for closed $3$-manifolds $Y$.
Recognizing when such collars exist is therefore part of the problem.

The question is in the topological category; neither a smooth
compactification nor a chosen smooth structure is required. The source
does not specify a finite encoding of arbitrary open manifolds, so
``effective'' asks for a usable criterion rather than an algorithm
on an unstated class of finite inputs. Merely restating the existence
of a compact filling is not such a criterion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find an effective criterion for an open four-manifold to be homeomorphic to the interior of a compact manifold with boundary.
