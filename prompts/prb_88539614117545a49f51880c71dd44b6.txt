\section*{Kirby Problem 4.118}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An index-$k$ handle in dimension four is $D^k\times D^{4-k}$,
attached to the boundary of the manifold already constructed along
$\partial D^k\times D^{4-k}$, for $0\le k\le4$.
A smooth handle decomposition constructs a compact smooth manifold
by finitely many such attachments and rounds the resulting corners.
For a closed manifold the construction ends with empty boundary.

Let $X$ be a closed connected simply connected smooth
$4$-manifold. Does $X$ admit a handle decomposition with no
index-one handles? More strongly, does it admit a handle
decomposition with neither index-one nor index-three handles?
Such a decomposition may be taken to have one index-zero handle
and one index-four handle, with the remaining handles of index two
in the stronger version.

Simple connectivity is an algebraic property of the whole manifold:
its fundamental group is trivial. Absence of one-handles asks for
a particular geometric presentation, and is commonly called geometric
simple connectivity. The question is whether every $X$ with the
former property admits a presentation with the latter.

The desired handle decomposition need not be obtained from a given
one by a prescribed bounded sequence of moves. One is free to
choose any smooth decomposition of $X$. No stabilization by
$S^2\times S^2$, connected sum with another manifold, or change
of smooth structure is allowed. Closedness is essential to the
stated domain; contractible manifolds with boundary are not included.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed simply connected smooth four-manifold have a handle decomposition without one-handles, and even without both one- and three-handles?
