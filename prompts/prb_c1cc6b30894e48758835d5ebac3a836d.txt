\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.24}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and let
$P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kz^k$ be a nonconstant polynomial of degree
$n\geq1$. Call $P$ self-inversive if $a_0a_n\ne0$ and there is a
complex number $\eta$ with $|\eta|=1$ such that
\[
 P(z)=\eta z^n\overline{P(1/\overline z)}.
\]
The right-hand side is understood as the polynomial obtained after
expanding the conjugated expression. Equivalently, its zero multiset
is invariant under reflection $\zeta\mapsto1/\overline\zeta$ in the
unit circle, with multiplicities preserved.

Put $A=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}|a_k|$. Must there be a point $w_0\in
\mathbb C$ such that
\[
 \{w:|w-w_0|<A\}\subseteq P(\mathbb D)?
\]
The center is free and may depend on $P$. The assertion is about
the ordinary image set: each point of the disk needs at least one
preimage in $\mathbb D$, but no common univalent inverse branch is
required. Since $P$ is nonconstant, its image is an open domain.
The radius is tied to the largest coefficient and scales correctly
when $P$ is multiplied by a nonzero complex number.
The question ranges over every degree and all self-inversive
polynomials, including those with repeated zeros or zeros lying
on the unit circle.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the image of the unit disk under every self-inversive polynomial contain a disk as large as its largest coefficient?
