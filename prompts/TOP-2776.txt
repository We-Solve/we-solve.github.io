\section*{Kirby Problem 2.28}\label{2776}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2776 · KP-2.28 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite simplicial graph $\Gamma$, the right-angled Artin group is
\[
 A_\Gamma=\langle v\in V(\Gamma)\mid uv=vu\text{ whenever }\{u,v\}\in E(\Gamma)\rangle.
\]
A nontrivial join is a graph $J=J_1*J_2$ with both vertex sets nonempty,
containing every edge between $J_1$ and $J_2$; its Artin group is the direct
product $A_{J_1}\times A_{J_2}$. A join subgroup means the subgroup generated
by the vertices of an induced subgraph of this form. Assume $\Gamma$ itself
is not a nontrivial join.

Call a nonidentity element loxodromic in the RAAG sense used here if it is
not conjugate into any join subgroup. Equivalently its cyclically reduced
support is not contained in an induced nontrivial join. This is the algebraic
criterion for the usual rank-one elements, avoiding a choice of conjugate word.

Is there an oriented finite-type surface $S$ and an injective homomorphism
$\iota:A_\Gamma\to\operatorname{Mod}(S)$ that sends every such element to a
pseudo-Anosov mapping class? A pseudo-Anosov preserves transverse measured
singular foliations with reciprocal stretching factors $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$,
$\lambda>1$. The surface and embedding may depend on $\Gamma$, but one
embedding must satisfy the condition for all loxodromic elements simultaneously.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Embed each right-angled Artin group that does not split as a direct product into a mapping class group so that every rank-one element becomes pseudo-Anosov.
