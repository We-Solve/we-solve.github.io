{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30000235 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


$\mathbb F_2=\{0,1\}$ with addition and multiplication modulo two; $\langle s,a_i\rangle$ is the parity of coordinatewise products. Bernoulli-$\varepsilon$ noise equals one with probability $\varepsilon$, independently for each sample.

Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.

A learning problem specifies a data distribution, a hypothesis class and a loss. Statistical consistency means that the estimator approaches its population target as the sample size increases; statistical and computational requirements are distinct.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, let $a_i$ be independent uniform vectors in $\mathbb F_2^n$ and let $b_i=\langle s,a_i\rangle+e_i$, where $s\in\mathbb F_2^n$ is hidden and the $e_i$ are independent Bernoulli-$\varepsilon$ errors. Can $s$ be recovered in polynomial time from polynomially many samples $(a_i,b_i)$? \sref{30000235}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The required success probability and encoding of the fixed noise rate are not specified in the supplied question.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Recover a hidden parity from uniformly random examples corrupted by a fixed nonzero noise rate.
