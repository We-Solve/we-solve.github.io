\section*{Traveling Salesman Problem in Solid Grid Graphs}\label{5500054}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500054 · AMR-054-0054 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite set $V\subset\mathbb Z^2$, let $G$ contain exactly the
unit horizontal and vertical edges whose endpoints both lie in
$V$. Assume $G$ is connected. It is solid when the complementary
lattice vertex set $\mathbb Z^2\setminus V$ is connected by unit
horizontal and vertical steps, so it has no missing-vertex holes.

A tour is a closed walk in $G$ visiting every vertex at least once.
Its length is the number of traversed edges, counted with
multiplicity. Determine the computational complexity of finding
a shortest tour in a solid grid graph. The associated decision
problem supplies an integer $B$ and asks whether a tour of length
at most $B$ exists; the vertices of $G$ are given explicitly.

Equivalently, one may solve the travelling-salesman problem on
$V$ with the shortest-path metric $d_G$. This is the intrinsic
graph distance. It need not equal the unrestricted Manhattan
distance $\|u-v\|_1$, because an allowed path must remain in $G$.

A Hamiltonian cycle is the special case of a tour of length
$|V|$. Deciding that special case alone does not determine the
minimum tour length when repeated edges or vertices are necessary.
The source asks whether the full optimization problem has a
polynomial algorithm or a corresponding hardness classification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine how hard it is to find the shortest closed walk visiting
every vertex of a hole-free grid graph, allowing repeated visits.
