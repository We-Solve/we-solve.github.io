\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A smooth knot $K\subset S^3=\partial B^4$ is ribbon if it bounds a smoothly embedded disk in $B^4$ whose radial Morse function has no local maxima. A symmetric-union diagram is constructed from a knot diagram and its reflected mirror: join them along the reflection axis to obtain the symmetric connected-sum diagram, and allow twists at crossings on that axis. Off the axis the diagram retains reflection symmetry. A knot is a symmetric union if it has a diagram obtained by this construction.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every smooth ribbon knot in $S^3$ admit a symmetric-union diagram? \sref{2710}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every ribbon knot have a symmetric-union presentation?
