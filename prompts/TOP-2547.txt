{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2547 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a group $G$, let $s(G)$ be the largest nonnegative integer $k$ for which
\[
 \forall x_1,\ldots,x_k\in G\setminus\{1\}\ \exists y\in G:\quad
 \langle x_i,y\rangle=G\quad(1\leq i\leq k),
\]
with $s(G)=\infty$ if this holds for every $k$. Here $\langle\cdot\rangle$ denotes the subgroup generated by the listed elements; repeated $x_i$ are permitted.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a group $G$ with spread $s(G)=1$? Explicitly, does some group satisfy
\[
 \forall x\ne1\ \exists y:\langle x,y\rangle=G,
\]
while having nonidentity elements $u,v$ such that
\[
 \forall y\in G:\quad \langle u,y\rangle\ne G\ \text{or}\ \langle v,y\rangle\ne G?
\] \sref{2547}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a group where every nonidentity element has a generating partner, but some pair of nonidentity elements has no common generating partner?
