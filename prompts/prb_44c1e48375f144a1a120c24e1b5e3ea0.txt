\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.64}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $p$ be a prime. A finite group $G$ is $p$-soluble if it has a
normal series whose factors are either $p$-groups or $p'$-groups,
where a $p'$-group has order prime to $p$. Its $p$-length
$\ell_p(G)$ is the smallest number of nontrivial $p$-group factors
in any such series; intervening $p'$-factors are not counted.
All terms of a normal series are normal in its ambient group.

Choose a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P\le G$, so $|P|$ is the largest power
of $p$ dividing $|G|$, and let $A\trianglelefteq P$ have exponent
$p^e$. The exponent is the least positive integer annihilating every
element by powering. The normal closure of $A$ in $G$ is
\[
 N=\langle A^g:g\in G\rangle,
 \qquad A^g=g^{-1}Ag.
\]
It is a normal $p$-soluble subgroup of $G$, although it need not be
a $p$-group.

Is there a function $f$ depending only on $p$ and $e$ such that
$\ell_p(N)\le f(p,e)$ for every choice of $G,P,A$ above? More
strongly, can one choose a function $b(e)$ independent of the prime
with $\ell_p(N)\le b(e)$ in all cases?
The bounds must be independent of $|G|$, $|A|$, the index of $P$,
and the number of conjugates needed to generate $N$. Normality of $A$
is assumed inside $P$ only, not inside $G$. The case $A=1$, with
exponent $p^0=1$, has $\ell_p(N)=0$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the p-length of the normal closure of a normal Sylow-subgroup part bounded by its exponent and the prime, or even by the exponent parameter alone?
