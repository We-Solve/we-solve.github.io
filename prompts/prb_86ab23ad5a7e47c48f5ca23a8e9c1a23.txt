\section*{Geometry of Curves and Surfaces — Problem 6.3}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a smooth compact connected embedded
orientable surface with boundary. Choose a continuous unit
normal and define its mean curvature by
$H=(k_1+k_2)/2$, where $k_1,k_2$ are the corresponding principal
curvatures. Assume $H$ is constant throughout the interior.

Suppose $\partial M$ is one smooth simple closed convex curve
$\Gamma$ in an affine plane $\Pi$, and the entire surface lies
in one of the closed half-spaces bounded by $\Pi$. Is $M$
necessarily homeomorphic to a closed disk?

The assertion is topological: it rules out handles on a
one-boundary-component surface. It does not claim that the
surface is planar, spherical, or uniquely determined by
$\Gamma$. The curve being convex means that it bounds a
convex planar region; convexity of the whole surface is not
assumed. The half-space may be either side of the boundary
plane, but it is fixed for the entire surface.

Embeddedness excludes self-intersections, and no stability or
area-minimizing hypothesis is imposed. Adding such a variational
assumption gives a special case rather than the full question.
Reversing the chosen normal changes the sign of $H$ but does
not change the constant-mean-curvature condition or the problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must an embedded constant-mean-curvature surface with one convex
planar boundary curve have no handles if it stays on one side
of the boundary plane?
