\section{Semi-magic square of distinct positive cubes}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $1\le i,j\le3$, let $a_{ij}$ be positive integers, with all nine $a_{ij}$ pairwise distinct. A $3\times3$ array of cubes is semi-magic when all row sums and all column sums equal the same integer $M$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist such $a_{ij}$ and $M\in\mathbb N$ with
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}^{3}=M\quad(i=1,2,3),\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{ij}^{3}=M\quad(j=1,2,3)?
\]
Construct an example or prove there is none. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a three-by-three array of nine distinct positive cubes with every row and every column having the same sum, or prove no such array exists.
\subsection{Sources}
