\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For compact $K\subset\mathbb C$, let $R(K)$ be the uniform closure on $K$ of rational functions with poles outside $K$. A point $x_0\in K$ is a peak point if some $h\in R(K)$ satisfies $h(x_0)=1$ and $|h(x)|<1$ for every $x\in K\setminus\{x_0\}$; a non-peak point is one for which no such $h$ exists. A curve terminating at $x_0$ means a continuous $\gamma:[0,1]\to K$ with $\gamma(1)=x_0$ and $\gamma(t)\ne x_0$ for $0\le t<1$.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every compact $K\subset\mathbb C$ and every non-peak point $x_0$ for $R(K)$, does such a curve in $K$ terminating at $x_0$ exist? \sref{2307047}{2}
The source gives a sufficient Hausdorff-content condition for a straight segment, but also mentions examples where no such segment exists; the question asks for an arbitrary continuous curve. \sref{2307047}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every non-peak point for rational approximation be accessible through a continuous curve contained in the compact set?
