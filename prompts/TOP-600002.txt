\section*{Baker's Dozen — Periodic billiard trajectories}\label{600002}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 600002 · AMR-005-0002 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a compact strictly convex region with smooth boundary
$\gamma$. An inner billiard trajectory consists of straight segments in $K$,
with the tangential component of velocity preserved and the normal component
reversed at each reflection. Its least period is the smallest positive number
of reflections returning to the same boundary point and outgoing direction.
A one-parameter family means a nonconstant smooth family of periodic phase
points, rather than repeated traversal of one orbit.

For two distinct integers $p,q\ge2$, ask whether a nonelliptic $\gamma$ can carry
both a one-parameter family of trajectories of least period $p$ and one of
least period $q$. Determine the possible pairs and the restrictions they impose
on the table. This is a simultaneous periodic-family question; it does not
assume that the whole billiard phase space is foliated by invariant curves.

Ask also the outer billiard version. Fix an orientation and, for $x$ outside
$K$, choose the supporting tangent of the prescribed side, with contact point
$t$; set $T(x)=2t-x$. Replace the inner billiard map above by $T$, and require
nonconstant smooth families with two distinct least periods. An ellipse means
an affine image of a Euclidean circle.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a smooth convex billiard table other than an ellipse support continuous
families of periodic motions of two different least periods? Ask the same
question for reflection in exterior tangent points.
