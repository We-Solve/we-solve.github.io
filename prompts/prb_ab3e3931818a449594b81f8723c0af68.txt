\section*{Exponential upper and lower bounds for the additive basis order of multiplicative subgroups}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a prime $p$, let $\mathbb F_p^*$ denote its multiplicative group.
A multiplicative subgroup $H\leq\mathbb F_p^*$ is nonempty and closed under multiplication and inverses.
For an integer $k\geq1$, its additive $k$-fold sumset is
\[
 kH=\{h_1+\cdots+h_k:h_i\in H\}\subseteq\mathbb F_p.
\]
Repetitions are allowed, but zero is not available as a summand unless it is represented by several elements of $H$.
Thus $kH$ means exactly $k$ summands, rather than at most $k$.

Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$.
Determine the smallest integer $K(\delta)$ for which
\[
 \forall p\text{ prime}\ \forall H\leq\mathbb F_p^*:
       |H|>p^\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ K(\delta)H=\mathbb F_p,
\]
allowing the value $\infty$ if there is no such integer.
In particular, seek sharp dependence on $\delta$ as $\delta\downarrow0$ and matching examples requiring many summands.
The parameter must be uniform in both the prime and the choice of subgroup.
Once $kH$ equals the entire field, every larger number of summands also gives the entire field, since $H$ is nonempty.
Consequently the least uniform order is a coherent threshold despite the exact-summand convention.
The source gives an existence bound in terms of $\delta$ and asks for improvement; the target here is the optimal additive-basis order rather than a claim that the historical estimate is best known.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many additive summands from a multiplicative subgroup of size greater than a fixed power of the field size are uniformly sufficient to cover the prime field?
