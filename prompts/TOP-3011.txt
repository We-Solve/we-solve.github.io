\section*{Kirby Problem 5.4}\label{3011}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3011 · KP-5.4 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a Hausdorff second-countable topological manifold.
Its homeomorphism group $\operatorname{Homeo}(M)$ consists of
continuous bijections $M\to M$ with continuous inverses, under
composition. Give it the compact-open topology: subbasic
neighborhood conditions have the form $h(K)\subset U$ with
$K\subset M$ compact and $U\subset M$ open. This is the
usual transformation-group topology in the question.

A metrizable space $A$ is an absolute neighborhood retract
(ANR, in the metrizable category) if whenever $A$ is embedded
as a closed subset of any metrizable space $Z$, there is an
open neighborhood $U$ of $A$ in $Z$ and a continuous
retraction $r:U\to A$, meaning $r(a)=a$ for all $a\in A$.
The condition must hold for every such closed embedding, not
only for a preferred ambient space.

Is $\operatorname{Homeo}(M)$ an ANR for every manifold $M$?
The group is regarded as a topological space for this question;
the retraction need not be a homomorphism and no group structure
on $Z$ is assumed.

The desired neighborhood-retraction property is stronger than
merely constructing contractions of small neighborhoods of the
identity. Local contractibility alone should not be substituted
for the ANR condition. All homeomorphisms are included; no
orientation-preserving restriction, compact-support restriction, or
boundary-fixing subgroup is silently imposed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the full homeomorphism group of every manifold, with its compact-open topology, an absolute neighborhood retract?
