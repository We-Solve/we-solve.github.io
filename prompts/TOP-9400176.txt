\section{Is 509,203 the lowest Riesel number}\label{9400176}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400176 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A Riesel number is an odd positive integer $k$ for which $k2^n-1$ is composite for every integer $n\ge1$. Composite means greater than one and not prime. The integer $509203$ is a proved Riesel number.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $509203$ the least Riesel number? Equivalently, for every odd integer $k$ with $1<k<509203$, does some $n\ge1$ make $k2^n-1$ prime? (The remaining smaller odd value $k=1$ also fails the Riesel condition, since $2^2-1=3$.) \sref{9400176}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is five hundred nine thousand two hundred three the smallest odd multiplier whose entire powers-of-two sequence minus one is composite?
