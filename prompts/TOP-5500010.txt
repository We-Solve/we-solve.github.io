\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A simple polygon has a non-self-intersecting polygonal boundary with $n$ vertices. A triangulation partitions its interior into triangles by noncrossing diagonals, using its vertices. Linear time means $O(n)$ running time. Simplicity of the algorithm is the qualitative criterion used in the source.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Find a deterministic linear-time triangulation algorithm for arbitrary simple polygons that is significantly simpler than Chazelle\textquotesingle s. The related question recorded with it asks for a simple linear-time algorithm computing a shortest path within a simple polygon without first using a more complicated triangulation algorithm. \sref{5500010}{2}
