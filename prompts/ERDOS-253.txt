## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
Suppose $a_{i+1}/a_i\to1$, and every infinite arithmetic
progression contains infinitely many sums of distinct $a_i$.
Must all sufficiently large integers be such sums? No.
We reconstruct the irrational-rotation mechanism of Cassels's
counterexample, with weaker quantitative bounds sufficient for
the full negative answer. Our sequence itself meets every
progression infinitely often, while its subset sums have upper
density at most $1/4$.
>>>
