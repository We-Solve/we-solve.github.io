\section{Instance-Independent Drivers in Quantum Annealing}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


Quantum annealing interpolates from a driver Hamiltonian to an instance-dependent problem Hamiltonian. A transverse driver mixes the problem basis, and instance-independent means that this mixing operator is chosen without knowledge of the particular instance. Quantum speedup must use the time or complexity criterion of the cited no-speedup theorem.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Extend the no-quantum-speedup theorem for the transverse-field driver to every instance-independent transverse Hamiltonian.

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The statement concerns the source instance ensemble and theorem assumptions; it is not an assertion against every quantum algorithm on every optimization problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether the no-speedup result for the standard transverse-field driver holds for every instance-independent transverse Hamiltonian.
