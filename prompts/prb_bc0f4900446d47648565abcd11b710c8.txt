\section*{A sharp lower-bound certificate for the first open transitive tournament case}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $T_k$ be the transitive tournament on $1,\ldots,k$, with an arc
$i\to j$ exactly when $i<j$. A subdivision of $T_k$ in a digraph
$D$ consists of distinct branch vertices $v_1,\ldots,v_k$ and, for
every pair $i<j$, a directed path from $v_i$ to $v_j$.
The internal vertices of all these paths are mutually disjoint and
none is a branch vertex. Paths may have length one. Extra arcs of
$D$ are allowed, since the subdivision is a subgraph and need not
be induced.

Mader's conjecture asks whether, for every positive integer $k$,
there exists a positive integer $r=r(k)$ such that every finite
nonempty simple loopless digraph with outdegree exactly $r$ at each
vertex contains a subdivision of $T_k$. There is no restriction on
indegrees, and opposite arcs are allowed. Equivalently, one may
replace exact outdegree by minimum outdegree at least $r$: from
such a digraph one can retain any $r$ outgoing arcs at each vertex
and then apply the exact-degree statement.

The requested threshold depends only on $k$, not on the number of
vertices or any further structure of the host. Internal vertex
disjointness is essential; pairwise edge-disjoint paths would instead
define an immersion. Simplicity is also necessary for the intended
conjecture, since repeating arcs can create arbitrarily large
outdegree on too few vertices to host the required branch vertices.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does sufficiently large minimum outdegree force a subdivision of every fixed transitive tournament?
