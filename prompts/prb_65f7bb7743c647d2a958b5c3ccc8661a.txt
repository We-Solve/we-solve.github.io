\section*{Better estimates for Littlewood constants}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a polynomial $p$, put $p^\#(z)=|p'(z)|/(1+|p(z)|^2)$,
and let $dA$ denote Euclidean area. Define
\[
 \Phi(N)=\sup_{\deg p=N}\int_{|z|<1}p^\#\,dA,
 \qquad \alpha=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\Phi(N)}{\log N}.
\]
For a connected compact plane set $E$ of positive
logarithmic capacity, assume its Green function $G_E$
with pole at infinity tends continuously to zero
at the boundary of the unbounded complementary component.
Extend it by zero over the remaining components.
Set
\[
 \ell_E(\varepsilon)=\mathcal H^1\{G_E=\varepsilon\},
 \quad \beta_E=\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
             \frac{\log\ell_E(\varepsilon)}{-\log\varepsilon},
 \quad \beta=\sup_E\beta_E .
\]
Here $\mathcal H^1$ is ordinary curve length.

A parameter $c$ is hyperbolic if the critical orbit
of $p_c(z)=z^2+c$ tends to an attracting cycle,
possibly infinity. On its Julia set choose $w$
and define the pressure
\[
 P_c=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
       \log\sum_{p_c^m(z)=w}|(p_c^m)'(z)|^{-1},
 \qquad P_*=\sup_{c\ \mathrm{hyperbolic}}P_c.
\]
For this expanding dynamics the pressure is independent
of the Julia-set base point.

Obtain sharp, rigorously justified bounds for
$\alpha,\beta,P_*$, with the goal of determining
their values. Computer-assisted estimates are admissible
if numerical and truncation errors are controlled.
Bounds for a single polynomial, compact set, or
parameter provide examples, while universal upper
bounds must cover the full indicated class. The
definitions fix the normalizations rather than treating
historical numerical estimates as exact constants.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Improve rigorous bounds for the polynomial spherical-derivative exponent, the Green-level length exponent, and maximal quadratic pressure.
