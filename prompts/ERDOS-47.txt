## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
Fix $\delta>0$. If $N$ is sufficiently large in terms of $\delta$ and
$A\subset[1,N]$ satisfies
\[
 R(A):=\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a>\delta\log N,
\]
must there be a subset $S\subseteq A$ with $R(S)=1$?
The current problem page, checked on 23 September 2026, records the
affirmative answer due to Bloom, with a subsequently improved
quantitative bound by Liu and Sawhney.

We use Bloom's explicitly stated quantitative theorem to deduce the
original assertion and its stability under deleting small denominators.
We then prove elementary arithmetic obstructions explaining why an
exact unit sum does not follow from a merely large fixed reciprocal
mass. The analytic theorem is an external dependency, not a proof
newly established in this note.
>>>
