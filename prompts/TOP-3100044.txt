\section*{For any poset P, define ex(n,P) to be the size of the largest subset of the Boolean lattice B\_(n) which includes no (inj}\label{3100044}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100044 · AMR-030-0044 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $P$ be a fixed nonempty finite partially ordered set. A family
$\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}$ contains $P$ if there is an injective
map $\varphi:P\to\mathcal F$ such that
\[
                  x<_P y\ \Longrightarrow\
                         \varphi(x)\subsetneq\varphi(y).
\]
Images of incomparable elements may be comparable; this is weak
poset containment. Say that $\mathcal F$ is $P$-free when no such
map exists, and write
\[
 \operatorname{La}(n,P)
   =\max\{|\mathcal F|:\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}
                                      \text{ is }P\text{-free}\}.
\]
Does the limit
\[
                 \lim_{n\to\infty}
                    \frac{\sqrt n\,\operatorname{La}(n,P)}{2^n}
\]
exist for every fixed $P$? Equivalently, by the asymptotic formula
for the central binomial coefficient, does
$\operatorname{La}(n,P)/\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ converge?

The poset is fixed throughout the limit; its size or relations
cannot vary with $n$. The question asks for actual convergence,
not merely upper and lower bounds of the same order of magnitude.
It includes degenerate cases with limiting constant zero, such
as a one-element forbidden poset. An induced-poset embedding
would also reflect noncomparability and defines a different
extremal problem. No such order-reflection condition belongs to
this entry.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every fixed finite poset, does the maximum size of a weak-poset-free Boolean-lattice family have a limiting normalized density?
