\section*{Boundaries of Groups and Kleinian Groups — Problem 8}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Consider a proper geodesic polyhedral complex $X$ with a complete CAT($-1$) metric. Proper means that closed bounded sets are compact; CAT($-1$) means that triangle distances are bounded above by the corresponding distances in the hyperbolic plane of curvature $-1$.

Assume each $m$-dimensional cell is a convex hyperbolic polytope combinatorially equivalent to an $m$-cube, and faces are glued by isometries respecting their cubical face structures. These are hyperbolic cubical complexes: the cells are not ordinary Euclidean unit cubes, whose flat two-dimensional interiors would be incompatible with a strictly negative curvature upper bound.

The ideal boundary $\partial_\infty X$ is the set of geodesic rays modulo bounded-distance equivalence, with the cone topology obtained by representing rays at a common basepoint and taking uniform convergence on bounded time intervals.

Find necessary topological conditions for a compact metrizable space $Z$ to occur as
\[
 Z\cong\partial_\infty X
\]
for such an $X$, and determine how restrictive cubical cell structure is compared with general CAT($-1$) complexes. The source records obstructions involving high-dimensional essential spheres as motivation; the question seeks further restrictions and their sharpness.

The requested conditions should be invariant under homeomorphism of the boundary. An obstruction that depends only on a particular drawing, cell subdivision, or selected visual metric does not by itself obstruct the topological boundary type.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What topological boundary types are ruled out by negative curvature together with hyperbolic cubical cell structure?
