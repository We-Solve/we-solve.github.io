\section{Giuga's Conjecture on Prime Characterization}\label{1870}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1870 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an integer $n\geq2$, put $S(n)=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}a^{n-1}$. Divisibility $n\mid S(n)+1$ means $S(n)\equiv-1\pmod n$. A prime integer is an integer greater than one with no positive divisors other than one and itself.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the equivalence
\[\forall n\geq2,\qquad n\mid\left(1+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}a^{n-1}\right)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\text{ is prime}\]
true? In particular, does the displayed divisibility condition rule out every composite integer? \sref{1870}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the power-sum congruence characterize primes, with no composite integer satisfying it?
