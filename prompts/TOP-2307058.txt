\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


In $\mathbb R^2\cong\mathbb C$ let $\Delta(A,B;\mathbb R^2)$ denote all plane curves joining $A$ and $B$. Its $2$-modulus is the infimum of $\int\rho^2\,dA$ over nonnegative Borel densities with line integral at least $1$ on every rectifiable joining curve; the infimum is $+\infty$ if none exists. Let $E\subset[0,1]\subset\mathbb R$ be compact and assume positive conformal capacity in the explicit sense
\[M_2(\Delta(E,\{z:|z|=2\};\mathbb R^2))>0.
\]
Put $F=\mathbb R\setminus E$, considered as a subset of the plane.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must $M_2(\Delta(E,F;\mathbb R^2))=+\infty$? \sref{2307058}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the modulus between a compact positive-capacity subset of the unit interval and the rest of the real line be infinite?
