\section*{Kirby Problem 4.9}\label{2885}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2885 · KP-4.9 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth $2$-knot is a smooth embedding $K:S^2\hookrightarrow S^4$.
Its normal bundle is trivial, so a tubular neighborhood can be
identified with $S^2\times D^2$. Write
$E_K=S^4\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)$, whose boundary is
$S^2\times S^1$ after this identification.
Let $R_\theta\in SO(3)$ be rotation through angle $\theta$ around
a fixed axis, for $0\le\theta\le2\pi$. Define
\[
 \tau(x,e^{i\theta})=(R_\theta x,e^{i\theta}).
\]
The loop of rotations represents the nontrivial element of
$\pi_1(SO(3))\cong\mathbb Z/2$.

The Gluck twist $X_K$ is obtained by removing $\nu K$ and attaching
$S^2\times D^2$ again, composing its original boundary identification
with $\tau$. Gluing the smooth boundary collars gives a closed smooth
$4$-manifold. Is $X_K$ diffeomorphic to the standard $S^4$ for every
smooth $2$-knot $K$?

Standardness here concerns the ambient manifold after surgery. It does
not assert that $K$ itself is an unknotted sphere, nor that a resulting
diffeomorphism respects the removed neighborhood or its core sphere.
The twist is the specified rotating boundary map, not an arbitrary
regluing of $S^2\times S^1$. The domain of the question includes all
smoothly knotted spheres, without a ribbon, spinning, or symmetry
assumption. A homeomorphism to $S^4$ is weaker than the requested
smooth identification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does Gluck surgery on every smoothly embedded two-sphere in the standard four-sphere produce the standard smooth four-sphere?
