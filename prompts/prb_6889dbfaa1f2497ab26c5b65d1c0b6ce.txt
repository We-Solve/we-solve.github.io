\section*{Kirby Problem 1.3}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a smooth knot $K\subset S^3$, the unknotting number $u(K)$ is the least number
of crossing changes needed to obtain the unknot, minimized over all diagrams of
$K$. A crossing change reverses which of the two strands passes over the other;
ambient isotopies between changes are allowed. A knot is nontrivial if it is not
ambiently isotopic to the round circle. Write $K_1\#\cdots\#K_n$ for connected sum.

A Conway mutation cuts a knot along a sphere meeting it transversely in four
points, rotates one of the resulting two-string tangles through a half-turn
preserving its endpoint set, and reglues. When the result is a knot, call it $K'$.
The questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $n\geq1$ and every collection of nontrivial knots $K_i$, is
$u(K_1\#\cdots\#K_n)\geq n$?
\item For every pair of mutant knots, is $u(K')=u(K)$?
\end{enumerate}
Part (a) is a lower bound by the number of summands. It makes no assertion that
unknotting number is additive, and therefore retains the distinct target of this
catalogue entry.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a sum of $n$ nontrivial knots need at least $n$ crossing changes, and does mutation preserve unknotting number?
