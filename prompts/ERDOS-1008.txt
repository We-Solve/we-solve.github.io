## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Every finite simple graph with \(m\) edges has a subgraph without a four-cycle and with at least \(\tfrac12m^{2/3}\) edges. We prove this by the elementary alteration argument given by Zach Hunter in the problem's discussion. The exponent is optimal for arbitrary host graphs.

\paragraph{Counting the forbidden configurations.}
Let \(C(G)\) be the number of unoriented copies of \(C_4\), including those with diagonals present in the host. A matching of two edges can be the pair of opposite edges of at most two four-cycles: on its four endpoints there are only two ways to join the opposite edges into a cycle. Each four-cycle has exactly two opposite-edge matchings. Double counting therefore gives
\[
 2C(G)\leq 2\binom m2,\qquad C(G)\leq\binom m2.
\tag{1}
\]
The argument counts subgraphs, not induced subgraphs, so chords cause no difficulty.

\paragraph{Sampling and deletion.}
For \(m\geq1\), retain each edge independently with probability \(p=m^{-1/3}\), and call the resulting graph \(H\). Linearity of expectation gives
\[
 \mathbb E e(H)=pm,\qquad
 \mathbb E C(H)=p^4C(G).
\]
For each four-cycle of \(H\), choose one of its edges and delete the union of the chosen edges. The resulting graph \(H'\) has no four-cycle: any such cycle would already have been a cycle of \(H\), from which a chosen edge was deleted. Since the union of the chosen edges has size at most \(C(H)\),
\[
 e(H')\geq e(H)-C(H).
\]
At least one outcome satisfies
\[
 e(H')\geq pm-p^4\binom m2
 =\frac12m^{2/3}+\frac12m^{-1/3}
 \geq\frac12m^{2/3}.
\]
When \(m=0\), the empty graph proves the assertion. This proves the requested uniform bound, without an asymptotic qualification on \(m\).

\paragraph{Why the exponent cannot be increased.}
Take the complete bipartite host \(K_{n,n^2}\), which has \(m=n^3\) edges. In a four-cycle-free subgraph, let \(d_1,\ldots,d_{n^2}\) be the degrees on the larger side. Any two vertices on the smaller side have at most one common neighbour, so
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^{n^2}\binom{d_i}{2}\leq\binom n2.
\]
For every integer \(d\geq0\), \(d\leq1+\binom d2\). Consequently
\[
 e(H)=\sum_i d_i\leq n^2+\binom n2<\frac32n^2
 =\frac32m^{2/3}.
\]
Thus no bound \(c\,m^\beta\), with fixed \(c>0\) and \(\beta>2/3\), can hold for all host graphs. This is Folkman's obstruction to the earlier \(m^{3/4}\) proposal.

\paragraph{Attribution and assessment.}
The general result was published by Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov, \emph{Large subgraphs without complete bipartite graphs}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6711}. The proof reconstructed here is Hunter's elementary argument at \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1008}; the current statement is \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/1008}, checked 24 September 2026.
