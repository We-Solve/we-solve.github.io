\section{Are there infinitely many Kummer primes}\label{9400150}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400150 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $p_1=2<p_2=3<\cdots$ enumerate the primes, and put $P_n=\prod_{j=1}^n p_j$ for $n\ge1$. A Kummer number is $K_n=P_n-1$, and a Kummer prime is a prime value of this sequence.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many $n\ge1$ for which $K_n$ is prime? \sref{9400150}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are infinitely many primorials minus one prime?
