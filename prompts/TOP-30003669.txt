\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A Ward identity is a relation between correlation functions or functional-integral derivatives obtained from a symmetry of the model and its measure. Renormalization-group methods compare effective descriptions at successive scales and often rely on a controlled perturbative regime. The question asks whether the symmetry identities themselves can supply information outside the regimes accessible by those conventional estimates.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can symmetry-generated identities help study models for which standard renormalization-group techniques do not apply?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
No particular model, identity or estimate is fixed by this research programme. A valid result must state the nonperturbative model class and information obtained, rather than asserting that every symmetry identity solves a difficult model.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can exact identities from symmetries give control over models beyond the reach of standard renormalization methods?
