\section*{Kirby Problem 4.4}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $g\ge0$, let $\Sigma_g$ be the closed connected orientable surface
of genus $g$, with its standard smooth structure; $\Sigma_0=S^2$ and
$\Sigma_1=T^2$. Let $Y$ be a closed connected smooth $3$-manifold.
The products $S^1\times Y$ and $\Sigma_g\times\Sigma_h$ carry their
product smooth structures. An exotic smoothing of one of these products
means a smooth $4$-manifold homeomorphic to that product but not
diffeomorphic to it with the product smoothing.

Do exotic smoothings exist on any products in these two families?
In each family, does every product admit an exotic smoothing? The
parameters $g,h$ range over all nonnegative integers, and $Y$ ranges
over closed $3$-manifolds. The source does not impose a special geometry
or a restriction on the fundamental group of $Y$.

The homeomorphism type must be that of the actual product. A manifold
which is only homotopy equivalent to the product is insufficient unless
a homeomorphism is also established. Conversely, an exotic smoothing
need not itself exhibit any smooth product decomposition or retain
the product projections as smooth maps.

The two levels of quantification are separate: one verified exotic
example answers an existence question but not the every-product
question. The comparison allows arbitrary diffeomorphisms, including
ones that do not preserve factors; failure of a factor-preserving
smooth identification alone does not prove exoticness.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which products of a circle with a closed three-manifold, or of two closed orientable surfaces, admit exotic smooth structures?
