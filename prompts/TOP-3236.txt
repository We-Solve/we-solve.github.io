\section{Double-critical graph conjecture}\label{3236}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3236 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite connected simple graph $G$ is double-critical if $\chi(G-\{u,v\})=\chi(G)-2$ for every edge $uv$. Here vertex deletion removes all incident edges, and $\chi$ is the proper vertex chromatic number. Let $K_n$ denote the complete graph on $n$ vertices.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $n\ge2$, is every $n$-chromatic double-critical graph isomorphic to $K_n$? \sref{3236}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are complete graphs the only connected graphs whose chromatic number drops by two after deleting any adjacent pair?
