\section{Chern's Conjecture (Affine Geometry)}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


An affine structure on a smooth $n$-manifold $M$ is an atlas of coordinate charts to open subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ whose transitions are restrictions of maps $x\mapsto Ax+b$ with $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)$ and $b\in\mathbb R^n$. Equivalently, $TM$ has a flat torsion-free connection. For a closed manifold, let $\chi(M)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j\dim H^j(M;\mathbb Q)$; closed means compact without boundary.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every closed smooth manifold $M$ admitting an affine structure, must
\[
 \chi(M)=0?
\]
Neither completeness of the affine connection nor orientability of $M$ is assumed. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every compact manifold without boundary that has affine coordinate changes have zero Euler characteristic?
