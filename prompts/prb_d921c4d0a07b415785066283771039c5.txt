\section*{Generic static-output stabilizability}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix positive integers $m,p$ and put $n=mp$.
Consider real matrices
$A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$,
$B\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$, and
$C\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ defining
\[
                  \dot x=Ax+Bu,\qquad y=Cx.
\]
Here $x\in\mathbb R^n$ is the state,
$u\in\mathbb R^p$ the input, and
$y\in\mathbb R^m$ the measured output.
A static output feedback is a constant real
matrix $K\in\mathbb R^{p\times m}$ with
$u=Ky$, producing the closed-loop matrix
$A+BKC$.

The system is stabilizable by this feedback if
some such $K$ makes every eigenvalue of
$A+BKC$ have strictly negative real part.
Equivalently, all roots of
$\det(zI-A-BKC)$ lie in the open left
half-plane. There is no bound on the size
of $K$, but dynamic or time-dependent feedback
is not permitted.

Determine for which pairs $(m,p)$ this
stabilizability property holds generically for
triples $(A,B,C)$ of the indicated sizes.
Here generically means on an open dense
subset of their real Euclidean parameter
space. A single unstabilizable exceptional
triple does not refute generic stabilizability;
an open set of such triples does.
The target is existence of some stable
closed-loop spectrum, not assignment of
arbitrary prescribed poles. The equality
$n=mp$ is fixed throughout and is the
critical dimension condition in this problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
At the critical state dimension, which input and output dimensions make a generic real system stabilizable by static output feedback?
