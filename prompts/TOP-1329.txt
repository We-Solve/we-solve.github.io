\section{Lovász Conjecture}\label{1329}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1329 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A graph $G=(V,E)$ is finite, simple and undirected. It is connected if every two vertices are joined by a path. It is vertex-transitive if for every $u,v\in V$ there exists a bijection $\phi:V\to V$ preserving adjacency with $\phi(u)=v$. A Hamiltonian path is an ordering $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of all $n=|V|$ vertices such that $v_iv_{i+1}\in E$ for $1\le i<n$. A single vertex is itself a Hamiltonian path.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every nonempty finite connected vertex-transitive graph admit a Hamiltonian path? Explicitly, can one order all its vertices as $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ with every consecutive pair adjacent? The path need not close up to a cycle. \sref{1329}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every vertex of a finite connected graph having the same symmetry type at every vertex be visited exactly once along one path?
