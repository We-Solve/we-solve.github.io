\section*{Defect feedback sets and a fractional obstruction for four-order majority digraphs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Choose four linear orders $<_1,<_2,<_3,<_4$ on a finite nonempty
vertex set $V$, with $n=|V|$. Form a digraph $D$ by putting
$u\to v$ for distinct $u,v$ exactly when $u<_i v$ in at least
three of the four orders. Such a graph is a $3/4$-digraph.
It is simple and has no opposite arcs. A two-to-two split produces
no edge in either direction. For a vertex $v$, let $d^+(v)$ be its
outdegree, and let the girth be the length of a shortest directed
cycle when one exists.

The conjecture asks whether every $3/4$-digraph satisfies the
Caccetta--H\"aggkvist bound: for every integer $r\ge1$, if
$d^+(v)\ge r$ for all $v$, then there is a directed cycle of length
at most $\lceil n/r\rceil$. The four orders are arbitrary and may
coincide; the statement is uniform over their choices.
These majority digraphs have no directed triangles, since a linear
order cannot support all three arcs of a cyclic triangle.
Accordingly, a particularly significant consequence would be a vertex
of outdegree strictly smaller than $n/3$ in every such graph.

The source also asks about the larger majority classes: given
integers $1\le a\le b$ with $a/b>2/3$, use $b$ orders and require
agreement in at least $a$ of them. Does the same short-cycle bound
hold for these classes as well?
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the Caccetta–Häggkvist short-cycle bound hold for digraphs defined by agreement of at least three of four linear orders, and for larger majority classes?
