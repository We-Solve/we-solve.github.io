\section*{Kirby Problem 3.11}\label{2809}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2809 · KP-3.11 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset S^3$ be a knot whose complement $M=S^3\setminus K$ admits
a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric of curvature $-1$. It has one cusp.
Expand a horospherical cusp neighborhood until its interior is maximal embedded.
Its boundary carries the limiting flat torus metric of this maximal cusp;
this fixes the scale for all slope lengths below.

A slope is an unoriented isotopy class of essential simple closed curves on
the peripheral torus. The meridian $\mu$ is the slope bounding a disk in the
removed tubular neighborhood of $K$. In the maximal-cusp metric its geodesic
representative has a well-defined Euclidean length $\ell_M(\mu)$, equivalently
the length of its primitive vector in the cusp lattice.

Is the universal inequality
\[
 \ell_M(\mu)\leq4
\]
valid for every hyperbolic knot in $S^3$? The upper bound is measured on the
maximal cusp, not on an arbitrarily rescaled horotorus. The ambient sphere
and the one-component knot condition are part of the assertion: analogous
claims for multicomponent links or knots in other manifolds are different
questions. A proof or a single knot with meridian length greater than four
would decide the proposed bound.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the meridian of every hyperbolic knot in the three-sphere at most four units long on its maximal cusp torus?
