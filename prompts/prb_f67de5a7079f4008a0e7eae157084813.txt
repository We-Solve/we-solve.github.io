\section*{Local edge-connectivity augmentation of a hypergraph with fixed rank}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $q\ge2$. Let $H_0=(V,\mathcal E_0)$ be a finite
hypergraph whose edges have size at most $q$, and let
$r(x,y)=r(y,x)$ be prescribed nonnegative integer demands for distinct
vertices. For a hypergraph $H$, define
\[
 \lambda_H(x,y)=\min_{x\in X,\ y\notin X}
       |\{E\in\mathcal E(H):E\cap X\ne\varnothing,
                              E\setminus X\ne\varnothing\}|.
\]
Indexed parallel hyperedges are counted separately. This is the least
number of hyperedges whose deletion separates $x$ and $y$.

Can one find, in polynomial time for each fixed $q$, an undirected
loopless multigraph $G$ on $V$ minimizing $|E(G)|$ subject to
\[
                          \lambda_{H_0+G}(x,y)\ge r(x,y)
                                        \quad(x\ne y)?
\]
The added objects are ordinary two-vertex edges, while the original
hyperedges are retained. The rank bound $q$ is a fixed constant,
not an input parameter on which the exponent of the polynomial must
be uniform.

Demands and any multiplicities may be encoded in binary; the output
may give edge multiplicities in binary as well. This avoids requiring
an exponentially long list of identical added edges. The objective
counts added edge copies, not the number of endpoint pairs used.
The optimization must meet all pairwise demands in one augmentation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can minimum edge augmentation for prescribed pairwise connectivity
be solved efficiently when the original hyperedges have a fixed maximum size?
