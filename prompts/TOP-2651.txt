{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2651 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $a^g=g^{-1}ag$. A group $G$ is invariably generated by $a,b$ if $\langle a^g,b^h\rangle=G$ for every $g,h\in G$. An embedding is an injective group homomorphism.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each pair of distinct primes $p,q$ and every finite group $F$, does there exist a finite group $G$ containing an embedded copy of $F$ and elements $a,b\in G$ of orders $p,q$, respectively, that invariably generate $G$? \sref{2651}{1}\sref{2651}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every finite group be embedded into a finite group generated by any independent conjugates of two elements having prescribed distinct prime orders?
