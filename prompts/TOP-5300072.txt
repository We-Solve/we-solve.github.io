\section*{Local connectivity of quadratic Julia sets}\label{5300072}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5300072 · AMR-052-0072 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $c\in\mathbb C$, define $P_c(z)=z^2+c$, its filled Julia set
$K_c=\{z:\sup_{n\ge0}|P_c^n(z)|<\infty\}$, and its Julia set
$J_c=\partial K_c$. Restrict to parameters for which $J_c$ is
connected, equivalently $c$ belongs to the Mandelbrot set
$M=\{c:(P_c^n(0))_{n\ge0}\text{ is bounded}\}$.
A topological space is locally connected if every point has a
neighborhood basis consisting of connected open sets in the
relative topology.

The problem is to characterize the locus
\[
 \mathcal L=\{c\in M:J_c\text{ is locally connected}\}.
\]
Seek necessary and sufficient dynamical, combinatorial, or arithmetic
conditions on $c$ or its critical orbit, rather than simply restating
the topological definition. Such conditions must cover all connected
quadratic Julia sets, including infinitely renormalizable cases and
irrationally indifferent periodic points.

The conclusion is a property of the dynamical-plane Julia set
$J_c$, not local connectivity of the Mandelbrot set at the parameter
$c$. Connectedness alone is insufficient. If local connectivity
holds, the external Böttcher coordinate extends continuously to
the circle and gives a useful boundary model; this is one
motivation for the classification. Known sufficient criteria and
known non-locally-connected examples delimit parts of the locus,
but the source's question asks for a complete distinction. A
criterion only for finitely renormalizable maps would leave the
general problem unresolved.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize exactly which connected quadratic Julia sets are locally connected.
