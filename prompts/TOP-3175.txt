\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A finite connected Eulerian graph has an Euler tour $W$, a closed trail using every edge exactly once. Consecutiveness in $W$ is cyclic, including the last and first edges. A cycle decomposition is an edge partition into connected $2$-regular subgraphs.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every Eulerian graph $G$ of minimum degree at least $4$ and every prescribed Euler tour $W$, does there exist a cycle decomposition such that no cycle contains two edges consecutive in $W$? \sref{3175}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the edges of an Eulerian graph be split into cycles that avoid every transition in a prescribed Euler tour?
