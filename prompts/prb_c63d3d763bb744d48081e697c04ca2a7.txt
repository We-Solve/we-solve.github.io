\section{What is the largest graph of positive curvature?}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Consider a simple connected planar graph embedded in the sphere, with minimum vertex degree three. For a vertex $v$, let $\deg(v)$ be its degree and let $|f|$ be the boundary length of a face $f$. Its combinatorial curvature is\[ \kappa(v)=1-\frac{\deg(v)}2+\sum_{f\ni v}\frac1{|f|}, \] where face incidences are counted around $v$. The prism graph on $2r$ vertices consists of two $r$-cycles joined by corresponding edges, for $r\geq3$. The antiprism graph has two $r$-cycles, with each vertex of one cycle joined to the two consecutive corresponding vertices of the other.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
What is the greatest possible number of vertices of such a graph satisfying $\kappa(v)>0$ at every vertex, excluding the prism and antiprism families? The later primary source gives the exact maximum $208$. \sref{statement}{2} \sref{statement}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many vertices can a connected planar graph of minimum degree three have if every vertex has positive combinatorial curvature and the graph is neither a prism nor an antiprism?
