\section{MacEachen Conjecture}\label{3345}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3345 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $p_1=2,p_2=3,\ldots$ be the primes in increasing order and $P_j=\prod_{i=1}^j p_i$ their primorials. Let $\mathcal S=\{\prod_{j\in J}P_j:J\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ finite}\}$, with the empty product equal to $1$; each primorial may occur at most once. This is OEIS A129912. Two integers are adjacent when their absolute difference is $1$. The author-confirmed distance restriction is included as in the OEIS comment.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every odd prime $p$, does there exist $s\in\mathcal S$ such that either $|p-s|=1$, or $|p-s|$ is a prime $q<p$? \sref{3345}{2} \sref{3345}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every odd prime within distance one, or within a smaller prime distance, of a product of distinct primorials?
