\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A concept class $\mathcal C\subseteq\{0,1\}^X$ has VC dimension $d$ when $d$ is the largest size of a set on which all binary labelings occur as restrictions of concepts. A labeled sample is realizable if some $c\in\mathcal C$ agrees with every label. A compression scheme selects a bounded-size labeled subsample and auxiliary information; a reconstruction map must produce a hypothesis consistent with the \emph{entire} original sample. The target is at most $Cd$ selected examples for an absolute $C$, for every finite-VC class. The side-information alphabet must be fixed with the scheme and independent of sample length; the catalogue does not specify a stronger bit-budget convention. No efficiency or proper-learning requirement is imposed by this combinatorial existence statement.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13012v1}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every realizable sample from a finite-VC concept class be compressed to only linearly many labeled examples, plus fixed bounded side information, while retaining enough information to reconstruct a consistent hypothesis?
