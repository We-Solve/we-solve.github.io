\section{Setwise convergence versus total-variation convergence of shifted processes}\label{9900005}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9900005 · Probability}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $(E,\mathcal E)$ be a measurable state space and let $X=(X_k)_{k\ge0}$ and $X'=(X'_k)_{k\ge0}$ be processes with values in the path space $(E^{\mathbb N_0},\mathcal E^{\otimes\mathbb N_0})$, where $\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. The shift is $\theta_n(x)_k=x_{n+k}$. Setwise convergence of laws means convergence of probabilities for every measurable path set. Total variation distance of two probability laws is $\sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|$. A coupling means a joint construction preserving the two complete marginal process laws.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $\mathbb P(\theta_nX\in A)\to\mathbb P(X'\in A)$ for every measurable path set $A$, must
\[\sup_A|\mathbb P(\theta_nX\in A)-\mathbb P(X'\in A)|\longrightarrow0?\] \sref{9900005}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does setwise convergence of time-shifted process laws to their limit imply total-variation convergence?
