\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.26}\label{2535}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2535 · KOU-21.26 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G\ne1$ be a finite group, and list the distinct prime divisors of
its order as $p_1,\ldots,p_k$. For each $i$, fix a Sylow $p_i$-subgroup
$H_i$, meaning a subgroup whose order is the largest power of $p_i$
dividing $|G|$. For $g\in G$, use the conjugation convention
$H_i^g=g^{-1}H_i g$ and define the finite family
\[
 \mathcal I_i=\{H_i\cap H_i^g:g\in G\}.
\]
A member $J\in\mathcal I_i$ is inclusion-minimal if no other member of
$\mathcal I_i$ is a proper subgroup of $J$.

The problem asks whether there always exists one element $x\in G$
such that $H_i\cap H_i^x$ is inclusion-minimal in $\mathcal I_i$ for
every $i$ simultaneously. Equivalently,
\[
 \forall i\ \forall g\in G,\qquad
 H_i\cap H_i^g\subseteq H_i\cap H_i^x
 \ \Longrightarrow\ H_i\cap H_i^g=H_i\cap H_i^x.
\]
Each family individually has a minimal member because it is finite.
The assertion requires a single conjugator realizing minimal members
for all prime divisors, even though the choices giving minimality for
individual primes may initially differ.

Inclusion-minimal does not mean smallest cardinality, nor a least member
contained in every other member. Several incomparable minimal intersections
are possible. The Sylow subgroups are part of the given input and are
fixed before $x$ is chosen. No solubility or simplicity assumption is
imposed on $G$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one conjugating element simultaneously make every chosen Sylow subgroup’s intersection with its conjugate inclusion-minimal?
