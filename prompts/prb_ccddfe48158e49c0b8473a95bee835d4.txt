\section*{Binary matroid representation of cyclic families}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix integers $n\ge1$ and $1\le k\le n$, and a $k$-element subset
$B\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. For $i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, put
$B+i=\{b+i:b\in B\}$, with addition modulo $n$.
A binary matroid on this labelled ground set is specified by a matrix
over $\mathbb F_2$: a subset is independent precisely when its columns
are linearly independent. A basis is a maximal independent set.

The conjecture asks whether, for every choice of $n,k,B$, there exists
a binary matroid of rank $k$ for which all the translates $B+i$ are bases.
Equivalently, can one choose column vectors $a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb F_2^k$
so that
\[
              \det[a_j]_{j\in B+i}=1
                       \quad\text{for every }i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z?
\]
Column order is immaterial to nonsingularity; over $\mathbb F_2$ every
nonzero determinant equals one.

The representing matrix may depend on $B$. The requirement does not say
that these translates are the only bases, or that cyclic translation
must preserve every independent set of the resulting matroid. Different
indices may give the same translate, imposing the same constraint twice.
The field is fixed to two elements: a construction over a sufficiently
large field does not establish the asserted binary representation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every cyclic family of translates of one fixed subset be
simultaneously realized as bases of a binary matroid?
