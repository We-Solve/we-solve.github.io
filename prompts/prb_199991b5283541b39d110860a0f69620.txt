\section{Density of Symmetric Primes}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A symmetric pair is a pair of distinct odd primes $p,q$ with $\gcd(p-1,q-1)=|p-q|$. A prime is symmetric if it belongs to at least one such pair. Its partner is not required to lie below the counting threshold. Equivalently, if $S(q,p)$ counts integer points $(m,n)$ with $0<m<p/2$, $0<n<q/2$ and $n<(q/p)m$, then the pair is symmetric when $S(q,p)=S(p,q)$. Define
\[
 P(x)=\bigl|\{p<x:p\text{ is an odd prime and }\exists q\ne p\text{ an odd prime with }\gcd(p-1,q-1)=|p-q|\}\bigr|.
\]
All logarithms are natural, and $\sigma=2-(1+\log\log2)/\log2$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is
\[
 P(x)=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\sigma+o(1)}}\qquad(x\to\infty)?
\]
Equivalently, does $\bigl(\log x-\log P(x)\bigr)/\log\log x$ tend to $\sigma$? This is a counting question about primes belonging to pairs, rather than the number of pairs whose two members are below $x$. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the number of primes below x that have a symmetric-prime partner have the proposed logarithmic exponent?
