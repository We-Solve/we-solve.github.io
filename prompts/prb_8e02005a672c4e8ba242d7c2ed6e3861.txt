\section*{Recognition of Seymour graphs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite loopless undirected graph, with parallel
edges permitted. A circuit is the edge set of a simple cycle;
two parallel edges can form a circuit of length two.
For $X\subseteq V$, the cut $\delta(X)$ consists of the edges
with exactly one endpoint in $X$.

Call $F\subseteq E$ cycle-sparse when
\[
                   |F\cap C|\le|C\setminus F|
                         \quad\text{for every circuit }C.
\]
The graph is a Seymour graph when every such $F$ admits a family
$\delta(X_1),\ldots,\delta(X_{|F|})$ of pairwise edge-disjoint cuts
satisfying
\[
                       |\delta(X_i)\cap F|=1
                                      \quad(1\le i\le|F|).
\]
The family is empty when $F=\varnothing$. Edge-disjointness and the
number of cuts ensure that their unique selected edges exhaust $F$.

Is there a polynomial-time algorithm deciding whether an input graph
is a Seymour graph? The input is only $G$: the universal quantifier
over cycle-sparse sets is part of the property being recognized.
Testing or constructing a packing for one supplied $F$ is therefore
not the whole task. The cuts need not be bonds, their vertex sides
may overlap, and it is the cut edge sets that must be disjoint.
A structural characterization can help, but its existence alone does
not establish an effective polynomial-time recognition algorithm.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can Seymour graphs, defined by their cycle-sparse-set cut-packing property, be recognized in polynomial time?
