\section{Decidability and Finiteness of Rational Joint Spectral Radii}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


The joint spectral radius is $\rho(\mathcal A)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\max_{i_1,\ldots,i_k}\lVert A_{i_k}\cdots A_{i_1}\rVert^{1/k}$. Finite-product attainment means equality with $\rho(A_{i_k}\cdots A_{i_1})^{1/k}$ for some product, where the latter is ordinary spectral radius.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For rational matrices $A_1,\ldots,A_m$, is there an algorithm that halts in finite time and decides whether their joint spectral radius is strictly less than $1$? If the matrices have rational entries, is the joint spectral radius always attained by the spectral radius of a finite product, i.e., does equality hold for some finite product length $k$? \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The strict inequality $\rho<1$ and rational-entry finiteness question are distinct targets; neither is replaced by a non-strict test or real-entry variant.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Decide strict stability of rational matrix families and determine whether their growth rate is always realized by a finite product.
