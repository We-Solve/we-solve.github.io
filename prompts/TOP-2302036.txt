\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.36}\label{2302036}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2302036 · AMR-022-2036 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $f$ be a transcendental entire function of finite order
$\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log\log M(r,f)/\log r$, with $0<\rho<1$.
Fix $\alpha$ satisfying $\rho<\alpha\le1$ and set
\[
E_\alpha(f)=\{r\ge1:\log m_0(r,f)>\cos(\pi\alpha)\log M(r,f)\},
\]
where $m_0$ and $M$ are the minimum and maximum of $|f|$ on $|z|=r$, respectively, and $\log0=-\infty$.
For a measurable $E\subset[1,\infty)$ its upper ordinary density and lower logarithmic density are
\[
\overline d(E)=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{|E\cap[1,R]|}{R},
\qquad
\underline d_{\log}(E)=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac1{\log R}\int_{E\cap[1,R]}\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
Besicovitch's bound gives $\overline d(E_\alpha)\ge1-\rho/\alpha$; Barry's logarithmic version gives the same lower bound for $\underline d_{\log}$. Determine the best universal lower bound for the ordinary upper density as a function of $(\rho,\alpha)$. In particular, can its stated bound be strictly improved? Sharpness for logarithmic density alone does not settle this ordinary-density question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Improve, or prove optimal, the ordinary-density bound for circles on which the minimum modulus is large in the cosine theorem.
