\section{The Erdos-Turan conjecture on additive bases}\label{3363}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3363 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Here use $\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. For $B\subseteq\mathbb N_0$ define the ordered representation count
\[
 r_B(k)=\#\{(a,b)\in B^2:a+b=k\}\qquad(k\in\mathbb N_0).
\]
An additive basis of order two, in the source's everywhere-positive convention, satisfies $r_B(k)>0$ for every $k\in\mathbb N_0$. Repeated summands are allowed, and $(a,b),(b,a)$ are counted separately when $a\ne b$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every such additive basis have an unbounded representation function? Equivalently, must
\[
 \forall M\in\mathbb N\quad\exists k\in\mathbb N_0:\quad r_B(k)>M
\]
hold whenever $r_B(k)>0$ for all $k$? \sref{3363}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the number of ordered representations grow without bound for any set that represents every nonnegative integer as a sum of two of its elements?
