\section*{Herman-ring retracts for Hénon maps}\label{5300081}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5300081 · AMR-052-0081 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $F:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^2$ be a polynomial automorphism,
with constant nonzero Jacobian determinant $\delta$. Define
\[
 K^+(F)=\{z:\sup_{n\ge0}\|F^n(z)\|<\infty\},
\]
and let $U$ be a component of its interior which is periodic under
$F$. After replacing $F$ by an iterate, assume $F(U)=U$.
Suppose that for some sequence $n_j\to\infty$ the iterates
$F^{n_j}|_U$ converge locally uniformly to a holomorphic map
$g:U\to\mathbb C^2$ of complex rank one.

Can this limiting map be a holomorphic retraction onto an embedded
rotation annulus $V\subset U$? Retraction means $g(U)=V$ and
$g|_V=\operatorname{id}_V$, equivalently $g^2=g$. The proposed
possibilities are that $V$ is biholomorphic either to
\[
 \{z:r<|z|<1\}\quad(0<r<1),
 \qquad\text{or to }\{z:0<|z|<1\},
\]
and that the restriction of $F$ to $V$ is conjugate to an irrational
rotation in the annulus coordinate.

The first possibility is the Herman-ring alternative, and the
second is a punctured Siegel disk. Ordinary disk retracts give
known rank-one rotation behavior, but do not settle these annular
alternatives. The issue is realization by a polynomial automorphism
on its actual stable component, not merely an abstract annular
holomorphic map. The rank is complex differential rank; convergence
and the retraction identity are required on all of $U$, not just
along one selected orbit.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a polynomial automorphism have a rank-one stable-component limit retracting onto a Herman ring or a punctured Siegel disk?
