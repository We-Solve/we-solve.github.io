\section*{O17 — Constructing irreducible polynomials over finite fields}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $p$ be a prime and $d\ge1$ an integer. A monic polynomial $F\in\mathbb F_p[X]$ of degree $d$ is irreducible if it cannot be written as a product of two positive-degree polynomials over $\mathbb F_p$. Such a polynomial presents the extension field with $p^d$ elements as $\mathbb F_p[X]/(F)$.

Can one construct an irreducible polynomial of degree exactly $d$ by a deterministic classical algorithm using a number of bit operations bounded by a fixed polynomial in $d$ and $\log p$? The output is the full list of its $d+1$ coefficients, represented by binary residues. Accordingly, the degree parameter is measured in unary, or equivalently the complexity bound is explicitly polynomial in the numerical degree $d$; polynomial time in $\log d$ would not even permit writing the required general dense output.

No polynomial to be factored is part of the input, and the algorithm may choose any irreducible polynomial of the requested degree. The same uniform construction must work as both the characteristic and degree vary. Neither randomness nor an oracle for selecting suitable elements of the field is allowed without accounting for its deterministic implementation.

The question concerns an entirely deterministic procedure. A randomized algorithm whose outputs usually agree with one canonical polynomial is still randomized and does not, on that property alone, supply the requested construction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an irreducible polynomial of any prescribed degree over any prime field be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in the degree and logarithm of the prime?
