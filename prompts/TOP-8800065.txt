\section{NP-hardness of minimum distance for cyclic codes}\label{8800065}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 8800065 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A linear code $C\subseteq\mathbb F_q^n$ is cyclic if it is invariant under the coordinate rotation $(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\mapsto(c_n,c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1})$. It can be specified by a generator matrix. The Hamming weight $\operatorname{wt}(c)$ is the number of its nonzero coordinates, and its minimum distance is $d(C)=\min_{0\ne c\in C}\operatorname{wt}(c)$. The decision form, given a nonzero cyclic code and integer $w$, asks whether $d(C)\le w$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the minimum-distance problem NP-hard when its input codes are restricted to cyclic codes? \sref{8800065}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does restricting linear codes to cyclic codes preserve NP-hardness of computing their minimum distance?
