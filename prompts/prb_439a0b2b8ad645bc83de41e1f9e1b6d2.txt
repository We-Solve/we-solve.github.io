\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 1.15}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Choose a finite symmetric generating set $S$ for a group $G$. Give its Cayley graph unit-length edges and the induced path metric. The group is word-hyperbolic if there is a number $\delta\geq0$ such that each side of every geodesic triangle lies within distance $\delta$ of the other two sides. Changing the finite generating set does not change whether this property holds.

A group is residually finite if each nonidentity element survives in some finite quotient:
\[
 \forall g\in G\setminus\{1\}\ \exists Q\text{ finite}\
 \exists\phi:G\to Q\text{ a homomorphism},\quad\phi(g)\ne1.
\]
The target may be replaced by the image of $\phi$, making the homomorphism surjective. Equivalently, the intersection of all finite-index normal subgroups of $G$ is trivial.

Is every word-hyperbolic group residually finite? The finite quotient and its cardinality may depend on the element being separated; the question requests neither a uniform bound nor an efficient procedure for finding such a quotient. It includes groups with torsion as well as torsion-free groups. Hyperbolicity alone is the hypothesis: linearity, a cubical action, or membership in a particular manifold class is not assumed. A negative answer requires a hyperbolic group containing a nonidentity element killed by every homomorphism to every finite group, rather than merely a group with few small quotients.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every nonidentity element of every hyperbolic group be detected in a finite quotient?
