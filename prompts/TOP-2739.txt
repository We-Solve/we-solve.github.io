\section*{Kirby Problem 1.80}\label{2739}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2739 · KP-1.80 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A crossing change in a knot diagram interchanges its overpassing and
underpassing strands. The unknotting number is
\[
u(K)=\min_{D\text{ a diagram of }K}
\min\{k:D\text{ is unknotted by }k\text{ crossing changes}\}.
\]
The minimum ranges over all diagrams, with unrestricted Reidemeister moves or
ambient isotopies allowed between changes. It is a finite nonnegative integer,
and equals zero exactly for the unknot.

Is $u$ computable? Precisely, does a single algorithm accept any finite
combinatorial knot diagram, halt, and output this minimum integer? The source
also singles out the weaker decision question: is there an algorithm that halts
on every knot diagram and decides whether its knot has $u(K)=1$?

Searching diagrams and crossing changes can produce explicit upper bounds and
certificates that $u(K)\leq k$. Such a search alone does not certify that no
shorter sequence exists. The requested computation must establish the minimum
and terminate on negative instances of the decision question as well. No bound
on its running time is imposed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can unknotting number always be computed, or at least can one always decide whether it equals one?
