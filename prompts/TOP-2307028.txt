\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $D\subset\mathbb C$ be a domain and $f:D\to\mathbb C$ continuous. All circle integrals below are counterclockwise contour integrals with the complex differential $d\zeta$. The radii used at $z$ are small enough that the closed disc lies in $D$.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does either of the following conditions imply that $f$ is holomorphic on $D$?
(i) For every $z\in D$ there is $r(z)>0$ such that
\[\int_{|\zeta-z|=r}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0\qquad(0<r\le r(z)).\]
(ii) For every $z\in D$,
\[\lim_{r\downarrow0}r^{-2}\int_{|\zeta-z|=r}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0.\]
The second, weaker condition is a separate question. \sref{2307028}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does vanishing of all sufficiently small circular contour integrals force analyticity, and is their scaled limit being zero already sufficient?
