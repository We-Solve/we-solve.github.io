\section*{Kirby Problem 4.53}\label{2929}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2929 · KP-4.53 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite triangulation is encoded by finitely many vertices and
simplices, together with their face incidences. Its geometric realization
$|K|$ is obtained by gluing standard Euclidean simplices along their
specified faces. The inputs here are promised to be triangulations of
closed connected $4$-manifolds; the task does not include deciding
whether an arbitrary finite complex is a manifold.

Does there exist a single algorithm which, on every such input $K$,
halts after finitely many steps and answers yes exactly when $|K|$
is homeomorphic to the standard topological sphere $S^4$? A
homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse.
No homeomorphism needs to be supplied with the input, and no bound
on the number of simplices is fixed in advance.

This is recognition in the topological category. The question asks
for homeomorphism to $S^4$, rather than diffeomorphism to a prescribed
smooth sphere. The input is not promised to be simply connected, to
have the homology of a sphere, or to lie in any special construction
family.

A procedure which eventually verifies all positive instances but may
run forever on negative ones is insufficient. Likewise, a collection
of computable obstructions which fails to settle some inputs does
not decide recognition. The running time may be arbitrarily large,
but termination and correctness are required for every valid finite
triangulation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one terminating algorithm recognize the topological four-sphere among all closed triangulated four-manifolds?
