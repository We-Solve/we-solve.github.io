\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.61}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $n\ge1$, the free group $F_n$ on a specified basis
$x_1,\ldots,x_n$, and an automorphism $\varphi$ of $F_n$.
The associated free-by-cyclic group is
\[
 G=F_n\rtimes_\varphi\mathbb Z
   =\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,t\mid
       tx_it^{-1}=\varphi(x_i)\ (1\le i\le n)\rangle.
\]
The words $\varphi(x_i)$ and this generating alphabet are fixed as
part of the ambient group, rather than supplied afresh as variable
inputs. Elements of $G$ are specified by finite words in these
generators and their inverses.

Is there, for every such fixed $G$, an algorithm that takes any finite
list of words $s_1,\ldots,s_k$ and outputs a finite presentation for
$H=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_k\rangle\le G$? A finite presentation consists
of finitely many formal generators and finitely many relator words;
the quotient of their free group by the normal closure of those
relators must be isomorphic to $H$.

The algorithm must halt on every input list, including lists with
redundant generators or relations among their entries. It may use a
presentation on new generators: the question asks for a finite
presentation of the subgroup, not a prescribed choice of generators.
The number and lengths of the input words are unrestricted.
One algorithm must work for every finite subset inside the fixed
ambient group. The assertion does not impose a complexity bound or
require that a single algorithm work uniformly from arbitrary input
automorphisms $\varphi$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every fixed finitely generated free-by-cyclic group, can one algorithm compute a finite presentation for each subgroup given by finitely many words?
