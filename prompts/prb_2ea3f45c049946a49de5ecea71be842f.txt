\section{Conjecture 3.26}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $P\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]$ be irreducible in $\mathbb Q(X)[Y]$ and of positive $Y$-degree. Set $d=\deg P$ and $H(P)=\max|a_{ij}|$ for its integer coefficients. An admissible specialization is a positive integer $n$ for which $P(n,Y)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb Q[Y]$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a polynomial bound $B(d,H)$, uniform over these $P$, such that an admissible $n\le B(d,H(P))$ always exists? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one always find a positive irreducible specialization within a bound polynomial in the polynomial degree and coefficient height?
