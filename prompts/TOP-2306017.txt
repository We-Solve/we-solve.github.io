{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306017 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$, and $\mathbb D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}$. Let $S$ be the class of analytic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$, $f^{\prime}(0)=1$, with expansion $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_nz^n$ and $a_1=1$. The image area is the extended quantity \[A(f)=\operatorname{area}(f(\mathbb D))=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_n|^2\in[\pi,\infty].\] For a prescribed feasible second coefficient $b\in\mathbb C$, let $S_b=\{f\in S:a_2=b\}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $\inf_{f\in S_b}A(f)$ for every feasible $b$, and the minimizing functions when the infimum is attained. The elementary bound is $A(f)\ge\pi(1+2|b|^2)$; the source notes that it is not sharp for $|b|>1/2$. \sref{2306017}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the smallest image area of a normalized univalent disk function when its second Taylor coefficient is prescribed.
