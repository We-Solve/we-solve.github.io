\section{Dense rational distance sets in the plane}\label{3077}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3077 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Distances are Euclidean: $\|x-y\|_2=\sqrt{(x_1-y_1)^2+(x_2-y_2)^2}$ for $x,y\in\mathbb R^2$. A set $S$ is dense in the plane if every open disk of positive radius meets $S$, equivalently $\overline S=\mathbb R^2$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there $S\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ such that
\[\overline S=\mathbb R^2\quad\text{and}\quad\|x-y\|_2\in\mathbb Q\quad\text{for every }x,y\in S?\] \sref{3077}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a set dense throughout the plane have rational Euclidean distances between every pair of its points?
