\section{Kirby Problem 1.28}\label{2687}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2687 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $I=[0,1]$ and $L\subset S^1\times I\times I$ be an annular link. Its wrapping number $w(L)$ is the minimum transverse geometric intersection number with a meridional disk, over isotopies. The Kauffman bracket skein module of the solid torus has basis $1,z,z^2,\ldots$, where $z$ is an essential core circle. Write $\langle L\rangle=\sum_k f_k(A)z^k$ and define its annular degree as $\max\{k:f_k\ne0\}$. Annular Khovanov homology $\mathrm{AKh}^{i,j,k}(L)$ is the associated-graded homology for the annular filtration: in each resolution an essential circle labeled $v_+$ or $v_-$ contributes $+1$ or $-1$ to $k$, while contractible circles contribute zero. Use the conventional integral theory.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
(a) Is $w(L)=\max\{k:f_k(A)\ne0\}$? (b) Is $w(L)=\max\{k:\mathrm{AKh}^{i,j,k}(L)\ne0\text{ for some }i,j\}$? \sref{2687}{1}\sref{2687}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do the annular Kauffman bracket and annular Khovanov grading each detect the minimum geometric winding of a link?
