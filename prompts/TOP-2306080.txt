{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306080 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. A holomorphic function $h$ on $\mathbb D$ is normal if the family $\{h\circ\psi:\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb D)\}$ is normal in the spherical metric on compact subsets. Equivalently, $\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}(1-|z|^2)|h'(z)|/(1+|h(z)|^2)<\infty$. For holomorphic injective $f$ on $\mathbb D$, its primitive $F(z)=\int_0^z f(t)\,dt$ is independent of the integration path.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must $F$ be a normal function for every holomorphic injective $f$ on $\mathbb D$? No normalization of $f$ at $0$ is assumed. \sref{2306080}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the first primitive of every univalent function in the disc a normal function?
