\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.54}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a set $S\subseteq\mathbb C$, a function is bounded on $S$ if there is a finite $C$ with $|f(z)|\le C$ for all $z\in S$, and bounded away from zero there if some $c>0$ satisfies $|f(z)|\ge c$ for all $z\in S$. A transcendental entire function is entire and is not a polynomial.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $E\subseteq\mathbb C$ be closed. Suppose that one transcendental entire function is bounded on $E$ and another is bounded away from zero on $\mathbb C\setminus E$.
Must there be a single transcendental entire function $h$ and constants $C<\infty$, $c>0$ satisfying
\[|h(z)|\le C\ (z\in E),\qquad |h(z)|\ge c\ (z\in\mathbb C\setminus E)?\] \sref{statement}{2}
Update 2.54 records no progress on the simultaneous-existence question. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the two separate entire-function existence properties of a closed set and its complement always be realised by one function?
