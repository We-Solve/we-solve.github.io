\section*{Little shift equivalence conjecture}\label{4600001}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600001 · AMR-045-0001 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Write $\mathbb Z_+=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. Two square matrices $A$ and $B$ over
$\mathbb Z_+$, possibly of different sizes, are elementary strong shift
equivalent if rectangular matrices $R,S$ over $\mathbb Z_+$ satisfy
$A=RS$ and $B=SR$. They are strong shift equivalent over $\mathbb Z_+$
if there is a finite chain of square nonnegative integer matrices
$A=A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_k=B$ with this property for every consecutive pair.
Intermediate sizes and the length $k$ have no prescribed bound.

Let $A$ be an $r\times r$ nonnegative integer matrix whose only nonzero
complex eigenvalue is $n$, of algebraic multiplicity one. Equivalently,
\[
 \det(tI-A)=t^{r-1}(t-n),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
The conjecture asserts that $A$ is strong shift equivalent over $\mathbb Z_+$
to the one-entry matrix $[n]$.

This formulation counts eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity and allows
nilpotent Jordan blocks at zero. In dynamical terms, construct the directed
multigraph with $A_{ij}$ edges from vertex $i$ to vertex $j$, and shift its
bi-infinite compatible edge paths. The requested equivalence says that this
edge shift is topologically conjugate to the full shift on $n$ symbols.
Agreement of characteristic polynomials alone is the hypothesis; exhibiting
the required nonnegative factorizations, or proving that they always exist,
is the additional content of the conjecture.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a nonnegative integer matrix with just one simple nonzero eigenvalue always present the corresponding full shift?
