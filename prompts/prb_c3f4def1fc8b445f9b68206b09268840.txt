\section*{List colouring of two matroids}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M_1=(S,\mathcal I_1)$ and $M_2=(S,\mathcal I_2)$ be two finite
matroids on the same ground set. Each independent-set family is
hereditary and obeys exchange: if independent sets $I,J$ satisfy
$|I|<|J|$, some element of $J\setminus I$ can be added to $I$
while preserving independence. A subset is common independent if
it belongs to both families.

Suppose $S$ has a partition into $k$ common independent sets, where
$k\ge1$ and empty parts are permitted. Give each $s\in S$ an arbitrary
list $L(s)$ of $k$ distinct colours. Must there be a map $f$ with
\[
 f(s)\in L(s)\quad(s\in S),\qquad
 f^{-1}(a)\in\mathcal I_1\cap\mathcal I_2
                                  \quad\text{for every colour }a?
\]
The available colours need not come from a common palette of size $k$.
Only the size of each individual list is prescribed.

The same colour classes must be independent in both matroids.
Solving two list-colouring problems separately would not ensure this
compatibility. No field representation or special matroid class is
assumed. A list-colouring theorem for a single matroid therefore does
not directly give the two-matroid assertion. The source's ordinary
colourability hypothesis is exactly the stated partition into common
independent sets, rather than separate partitions for $M_1$ and $M_2$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Whenever two matroids admit a common $k$-colouring, do they admit
a common colouring from every assignment of lists of size $k$?
