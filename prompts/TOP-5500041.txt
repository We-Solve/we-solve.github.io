\section*{Sorting $X+Y$ (Pairwise Sums)}\label{5500041}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500041 · AMR-054-0041 · Computer Science · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The input consists of two lists of real numbers
$X=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ and $Y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$. Form the $n^2$ labelled
pairwise sums $s_{ij}=x_i+y_j$. The required output orders all pairs
$(i,j)$ so that their sums are nondecreasing; equal sums may be
ordered arbitrarily. Keeping labels avoids ambiguity about whether
repeated values disappear from the output.

Determine the smallest worst-case running time of a uniform algorithm
for this task in the real-arithmetic comparison model. In particular,
can the obvious $O(n^2\log n)$ sorting time be reduced to $O(n^2)$,
which matches the size of the explicit output?

The special additive structure permits far fewer possible orderings
than arbitrary unrelated lists of $n^2$ real numbers. However, a
bound on the number of comparisons alone is insufficient: the time
needed to choose each comparison and manage the partial order must
also be charged. The source records quadratic-comparison methods,
so a generic comparison-count lower bound does not settle the
algorithmic question.

The inputs are unrestricted real numbers. Methods relying on small
integer ranges must account for the range parameter separately.
Reporting only distinct sums is a related output convention, rather
than the labelled sorting task fixed here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can all pairwise sums of two real-number lists be explicitly sorted
in quadratic total time, including the work needed to choose comparisons?
