\section*{A divisor-profile lower bound and a polynomial-height near-Sidon theorem for squares}\label{20001119}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20001119 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0244 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathcal Q=\{m^2:m\in\mathbb Z\}$ be the set of integer
squares, including zero. For a positive integer $N$, consider a set
$A\subseteq\mathcal Q$ of exactly $N$ distinct square values.
Its sumset is
\[
                         A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}.
\]
Summands may coincide, and equal sums arising from different pairs
are counted only once. There is no bound on the sizes of the squares
in $A$ or on the integers whose squares they are.
Define the extremal function
\[
                         S(N)=\min_{\substack{A\subseteq\mathcal Q\\|A|=N}}
                                      |A+A|.
\]
The question is to obtain the strongest possible lower bound for
$S(N)$ as a function of $N$, and ultimately determine its asymptotic
growth by matching it with constructions.

The constraint is that every element individually is a square; the
set need not consist of the first $N$ squares or any consecutive
range of squares. In particular, estimating the sums of one such
special family does not give a universal lower bound.
One elementary normalization is $2N-1\le S(N)\le N(N+1)/2$:
the first is the integer sumset bound and the second counts unordered
pairs with repetition. The research target is the improvement forced
by restricting to squares, including growth beyond any fixed
multiple when applicable, rather than the number of pair
representations of a typical sum. No probabilistic choice of $A$
is part of the assertion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the sharp lower bound on the number of distinct pairwise sums of N distinct integer squares?
