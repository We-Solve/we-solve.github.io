\section*{Symmetric one-points in the unit disk}\label{3700046}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3700046 · AMR-036-0046 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathcal S$ consist of pairs $(f,b)$
with $f$ holomorphic on $\mathbb D$ and
$0<|b|<1$, satisfying
\[
 f^{-1}(0)=\{0\},\quad f'(0)\ne0,\qquad
 f^{-1}(1)=\{b,-b\},\quad f'(b)f'(-b)\ne0.
\]
These are exact preimage sets within the
unit disk. The zero and both $1$-points
are simple, and no additional zeros or
$1$-points are allowed. Define
$b_0=\inf_{(f,b)\in\mathcal S}|b|$.

Define $c$ as the infimum of $a\in(0,1)$
for which a holomorphic disk function has
one simple zero at $0$ and exactly one
$1$-point at the real point $a$, of
multiplicity two. This is the source's
double-point covering constant, approximately
$0.0505468$.

Determine $b_0$, whether it is attained,
and the extremal functions or limiting
extremal sequences. In particular, prove
or disprove the strict inequality $b_0>c$.
The two prescribed $1$-points in this
problem are opposite about the origin;
they cannot merge into one nonzero point
while retaining that symmetry.
Rotation of the variable allows one to
take $b$ positive real without changing
the infimum. No reality condition on the
whole function is imposed, so this class
differs from the real-function version
with imaginary $1$-points. The comparison
asks whether the opposite-point geometry
forces a strictly larger radius.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the least radius for two opposite simple one-points, and decide whether it exceeds the double-one-point covering constant.
