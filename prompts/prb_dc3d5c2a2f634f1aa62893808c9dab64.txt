\section*{Order of mixing}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Choose a prime $p$ for which
\[
 f(u,v)=1+uv+u^2v+u^3v+u^4+v^2+u^4v^2
\]
is irreducible in $\mathbb F_p[u^{\pm1},v^{\pm1}]$. Let $X$ be the closed
subgroup of $\mathbb F_p^{\mathbb Z^2}$ consisting of all configurations
such that, for every $(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2$,
\[
 x_{a,b}+x_{a+1,b+1}+x_{a+2,b+1}+x_{a+3,b+1}
 +x_{a+4,b}+x_{a,b+2}+x_{a+4,b+2}=0.
\]
All sums are in $\mathbb F_p$. Equip $X$ with its normalized Haar
probability measure $\mu$, and let $(T^h x)_g=x_{g+h}$ denote the shift
action of $\mathbb Z^2$.

The action is mixing of order $k$ if, for every measurable
$A_1,\ldots,A_k\subset X$,
\[
 \mu\!\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^kT^{-h_i}A_i\right)
       \longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^k\mu(A_i)
 \quad\text{as }\min_{i\ne j}\|h_i-h_j\|\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Let $M$ be the greatest integer $k$ for which this holds. The source gives
$3\le M<7$. Determine the exact value of $M$ for the displayed action,
and clarify any dependence on the admissible prime.

The limit allows arbitrary independently varying translation vectors,
not just multiples of a single fixed configuration of directions. Thus
checking one mixing shape alone does not establish the definition. To
identify $M$, one needs both mixing at that order and failure at the next,
with the failure witnessed by suitable measurable sets and translations.
The finite-field alphabet and Haar law are fixed once the prime has been chosen.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the exact order of mixing of the explicit algebraic two-dimensional shift, known to lie between three and six.
