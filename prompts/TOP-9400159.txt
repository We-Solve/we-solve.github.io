\section{Are there infinitely many prime triplets}\label{9400159}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400159 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


The two prime-triplet patterns are $(p,p+2,p+6)$ and $(p,p+4,p+6)$, with every entry prime. Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be the sets of prime starting values $p$ for these respective patterns.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many prime triplets; equivalently, is $T_1\cup T_2$ infinite? \sref{9400159}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many three-prime configurations of diameter six in either of the two triplet patterns?
