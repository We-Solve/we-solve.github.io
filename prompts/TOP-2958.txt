\section*{Kirby Problem 4.82}\label{2958}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2958 · KP-4.82 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a closed simply connected smooth $4$-manifold and
$\phi:X\to X$ a self-diffeomorphism. For embedded closed smooth
surfaces $S_0,S_1\subset X$, smooth isotopy means an ambient
isotopy: a smooth family $F_t\in\operatorname{Diff}(X)$ with
$F_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $F_1(S_0)=S_1$.
The surfaces are treated as embedded images, without a specified
parameterization.

Suppose that for every smoothly embedded closed surface
$\Sigma\subset X$, the two images $\Sigma$ and $\phi(\Sigma)$
are smoothly isotopic. The witnessing isotopy may depend on
$\Sigma$; no one family working simultaneously for every surface
is assumed.

Part (a) asks whether $\phi$ must be smoothly isotopic to the
identity on $X$. Part (b) asks the weaker conclusion that $\phi$
is smoothly isotopic to a diffeomorphism $\psi$ supported inside
some smoothly embedded $4$-ball in $X$. Support is the closure
of $\{x:\psi(x)\ne x\}$; ball-supported here means this closed
set lies in the interior of such a ball, so the map is the
identity outside it and near its boundary.

The hypothesis is stronger than preservation of homology classes
of surfaces, but weaker than preserving every parameterized surface
embedding up to isotopy. These distinctions are retained explicitly.
The supporting ball in (b), if it exists, may depend on $\phi$,
and (b) does not assert that the remaining diffeomorphism inside
that ball is itself isotopically trivial.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If a diffeomorphism preserves the smooth isotopy class of every embedded surface, must it be isotopic to the identity, or at least to a map supported in a ball?
