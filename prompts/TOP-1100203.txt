\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 2.3}\label{1100203}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100203 · AMR-010-0203 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group $G$, let $\mathbb ZG$ be its integral group ring, and regard $\mathbb Z$ as the trivial module. The integral cohomological dimension $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G$ is the minimum length of a projective resolution
\[
 0\longrightarrow P_n\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_0
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0
\]
over $\mathbb ZG$, or infinity if there is no finite-length resolution. The modules are not required to be finitely generated. A classifying space $K(G,1)$ is a connected CW complex with fundamental group $G$ and contractible universal cover. The geometric dimension $\operatorname{gd}G$ is the least dimension of such a space.

Is there a group satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G=2\quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \operatorname{gd}G=3?
\]
Equivalently, must every group of integral cohomological dimension two admit a two-dimensional classifying space? The question places no finite-generation, finite-presentation or finite-cell assumption on $G$ or on the desired classifying space. Integral coefficients are essential: replacing them by rational coefficients changes the problem and allows torsion phenomena excluded here.

A proposed example must show both that a length-two projective resolution exists and that no two-dimensional $K(G,1)$ exists. Exhibiting a particular three-dimensional classifying space alone does not establish minimality. This is the exceptional dimension in the comparison of algebraic cohomological dimension with the smallest dimension of an aspherical CW model.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a group have algebraic cohomological dimension two while every classifying space has dimension at least three?
