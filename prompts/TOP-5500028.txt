\section*{Flip Graph Connectivity in 3D}\label{5500028}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500028 · AMR-054-0028 · Computer Science · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a finite set with no four coplanar points.
A tetrahedralization of $S$ is a face-to-face subdivision of
$\operatorname{conv}(S)$ into nondegenerate tetrahedra whose vertex set
is exactly $S$. In particular, every input point is used and no
Steiner vertices are added.

A $2\leftrightarrow3$ bistellar flip replaces two tetrahedra sharing
a triangular face by three sharing an edge, or reverses this operation.
The five involved vertices must form a convex triangular bipyramid,
and both local subdivisions must have the same support and boundary.
Only replacements that remain geometric tetrahedralizations are legal.

Form a graph $\mathcal F(S)$ with one node per tetrahedralization and
an edge for each legal flip. The question is whether $\mathcal F(S)$
is connected for every such $S$. Equivalently, can any two
tetrahedralizations of the same point set be related by a finite
sequence of these moves?

The point coordinates stay fixed throughout. Vertex-inserting
$1\to4$ and vertex-deleting $4\to1$ moves are excluded. Connectivity
of abstract triangulations, or connectivity restricted to a special
class such as regular triangulations, does not settle the general
geometric statement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can any two tetrahedralizations of the same points in three-dimensional
space be connected by legal two-to-three and three-to-two flips, without
moving, inserting, or deleting vertices?
