\section{Kirby Problem 1.79}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A knot diagram is a finite generic planar projection with over/under information at its crossings. Write $c(D)$ for its number of crossings. The three Reidemeister moves preserve knot type. An additional allowed diagram move must also preserve knot type. The source permits a collection of moves depending on $c(D)$, but requires that crossing-reducing instances be findable in time polynomial in $c(D)$; an arbitrary entire Reidemeister sequence declared to be one move does not meet this requirement.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can one augment Reidemeister moves by such effectively findable moves so that every unknot diagram $D$ with $c(D)>0$ admits a simplification step $D\to D'$ with $c(D')<c(D)$, and repetition reaches the zero-crossing diagram? Require strict decrease at the simplification steps and a polynomial-time procedure for finding those steps, rather than merely a process with nonincreasing crossing number. \sref{statement}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Find efficiently discoverable extra diagram moves that simplify every unknot by strictly decreasing the crossing count at each simplification step.
