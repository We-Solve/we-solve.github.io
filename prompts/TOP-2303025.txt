\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $P\in\mathbb R[x_1,x_2,x_3]$, harmonicity means $\Delta P=0$, with $\Delta=\partial_{x_1}^2+\partial_{x_2}^2+\partial_{x_3}^2$. Divisibility by $Q=x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4$ means that $P=QR$ for some polynomial $R\in\mathbb R[x_1,x_2,x_3]$. Allowing complex coefficients gives the equivalent existence question by taking a nonzero real or imaginary part.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a polynomial $R\not\equiv0$ such that
\[\Delta\bigl((x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4)R\bigr)=0?\]
Equivalently, can $Q$ divide a nonzero harmonic polynomial? \sref{2303025}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the sum of the fourth powers of three coordinates divide a nonzero harmonic polynomial?
