## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
Does a planar $n$-point configuration with $n-1$ distinct distances of multiplicities $1,2,\ldots,n-1$ have to consist of equally spaced points on a line or circle? No. Multiplicity must count unordered pairs for this intended question: the current page says ``ordered pairs'', but every positive-distance ordered multiplicity is even, so that literal convention cannot give a multiplicity one. The construction below answers the intended unordered formulation; its ordered multiplicities are twice the displayed values.

Clemen, Dumitrescu and Liu give the arc-and-center family in \emph{On multiplicities of interpoint distances}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04283}. The discussion at \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/958} also records a four-point example and formal verification. We prove the geometric assertions directly.
>>>
