\section{The Jacobian Conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $n\in\mathbb N$ and let $F=(F_1,\ldots,F_n):\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^n$ be a polynomial map, meaning $F_i\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ for every $i$. Its Jacobian matrix and determinant are
\[
 J_F(x)=\left(\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}(x)\right)_{1\le i,j\le n},
 \qquad \det J_F(x).
\]
A polynomial automorphism means a polynomial map with a polynomial two-sided inverse: some $G:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^n$ with $G\circ F=F\circ G=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb C^n}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
The historical Jacobian conjecture asserts that, for every $n\in\mathbb N$ and every such polynomial map $F$, if there is a number $c\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ such that
\[
 \det J_F(x)=c\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb C^n,
\]
then $F$ is a polynomial automorphism. Is this implication valid in every dimension? The inverse is required to be polynomial, rather than merely a local holomorphic inverse. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every polynomial map of complex affine space whose Jacobian determinant is a nonzero constant have a polynomial inverse? The assertion quantifies over all positive dimensions.
