{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400166 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A Wolstenholme prime is a prime $p\ge5$ such that $\binom{2p-1}{p-1}\equiv1\pmod{p^4}$, where $\binom ab=a!/(b!(a-b)!)$. This is stronger than Wolstenholme's congruence modulo $p^3$ valid for every prime $p\ge5$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many Wolstenholme primes? \sref{9400166}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do infinitely many primes satisfy the binomial congruence with a fourth power modulus?
