\section{Unique Ground-State Representability under Linear Constraints}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For observables $B_i$ and density matrix $\Gamma$, the expectation vector is $b_i=\operatorname{Tr}(B_i\Gamma)$. Constrained search fixes $b$ while minimizing energy. Multipliers $\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)$ enter a Hamiltonian of the form $H_0+\sum_i\beta_iB_i$, with the sign convention fixed by the source. The report initially assumes a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, self-adjoint operators $H_0,B_i$, commuting $B_i$, and linear independence of $\{I,B_1,\ldots,B_m\}$. It proves uniqueness of the multiplier vector for regular expectation values and treats critical or degenerate exceptions.

\paragraph{Question recovered from the primary report.}Extend this constrained-search and multiplier theory to an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, potentially retaining a finite family of observables $B_i$, with appropriate domains and regularity assumptions.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For a constrained-search problem with observables $B_1,\ldots,B_m$, characterize when a prescribed expectation vector has a unique representing parameter $\beta\in\mathbb{R}^m$ and a unique ground-state representation, including critical values and degenerate ground spaces.

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The catalogue wording below summarizes finite-dimensional uniqueness and degenerate cases. The primary report already develops those cases under its stated independent-observable assumptions; its explicit open question is the infinite-dimensional extension. Linear dependence of the original observable family is excluded in the report's finite-dimensional setup.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Extend the finite-dimensional constrained-search and multiplier theory to an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.
