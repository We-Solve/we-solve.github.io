## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
A consecutive-sum-free set $A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_t\}\subseteq[1,N]$ has no sum $a_i+\cdots+a_j\in A$ with $i<j$. The length-at-least-two qualification is essential. The proposed bound $|A|\le N/2+O(1)$ is false. We reconstruct Freud's $19/36$ construction with a bounded number of boundary deletions, rather than pursue the stronger subsequent $13/24$ result.

Sources: R. Freud, \emph{Adding numbers---on a problem of P. Erd\H{o}s}, James Cook Mathematical Notes (1993), pp. 6199--6202, in \url{https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/cook/iss_no60.PDF}; and the explicit Freud construction formalized by Pietro Monticone and Aristotle at \url{https://gist.github.com/pitmonticone/5c1b173e3140f869d7425d8f1003ac70}. The elementary verification below is independent of running that formalization.
