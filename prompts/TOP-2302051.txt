\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 2.51}\label{2302051}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2302051 · AMR-022-2051 · Analysis · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a nonconstant entire function $f$, let
$M(r,f)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|$ and
$\mathcal M(f)=\{z:|f(z)|=M(|z|,f)\}$.
Suppose this entire maximum-modulus locus is a single curve $\Gamma$ joining the origin to infinity: all its points are maximum-modulus points, it is connected, and there are no maximum-modulus points outside it.
Determine how prescribed geometric information about $\Gamma$ constrains the minimum possible growth of $M(r,f)$. The main example in the source is an infinitely winding spiral. For a concrete radial parametrization, let $\vartheta:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ be continuous and prescribe
\[
\Gamma=\{0\}\cup\{re^{i\vartheta(r)}:r>0\},
\qquad |\vartheta(r)|\to\infty\quad(r\to\infty).
\]
Among entire functions having exactly this maximum locus, find sharp lower growth bounds in terms of the spiral's winding, and determine when such a locus is realizable. More general prescribed curves are also included in the question. A useful answer must distinguish geometries forcing large growth from the straight-ray case, which permits very slowly growing examples. The hypothesis is about the complete maximum locus, not one selected path inside a larger locus.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How fast must an entire function grow when its unique maximum-modulus curve has prescribed geometry, particularly an endlessly winding spiral?
