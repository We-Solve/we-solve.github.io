\section*{Kirby Problem 4.56}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth $4$-dimensional cobordism from a closed $3$-manifold $M_0$
to $M_1$ is a compact smooth $4$-manifold $W$ with
$\partial W=M_0\sqcup M_1$, with the usual boundary identifications.
It is an $h$-cobordism if both inclusions $M_j\hookrightarrow W$
are homotopy equivalences.

It is an $s$-cobordism if these homotopy equivalences are simple.
For finite CW complexes, a simple homotopy equivalence is a map
homotopic to a composition of elementary expansions and collapses,
and cellular isomorphisms. An elementary collapse removes a cell
together with a free face attached to no other cell; an expansion
is the inverse operation. Smooth compact manifolds have compatible
finite CW structures, so this definition applies to the inclusions.
Equivalently their Whitehead torsions vanish.

Is every smooth $s$-cobordism $(W;M_0,M_1)$ diffeomorphic to
$M_0\times[0,1]$? The product carries its standard product smooth
structure. The assertion asks for a diffeomorphism of the entire
cobordism, not merely for $M_0$ and $M_1$ to be diffeomorphic.

No restriction on the fundamental group is imposed, and the source
does not require the boundary manifolds to be orientable. The
smooth category is essential. A topological product decomposition
would not establish the proposed smooth product structure. Similarly,
a homotopy equivalence $W\simeq M_0$ alone is already part of the
hypothesis and falls short of the conclusion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every smooth four-dimensional s-cobordism between closed three-manifolds a smooth product?
