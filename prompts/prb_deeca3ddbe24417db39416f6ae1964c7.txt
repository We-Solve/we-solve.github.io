\section*{\texorpdfstring{Effective Roth Theorem}{Effective Roth Theorem}}\label{statement}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Encode a real algebraic irrational $\alpha$ by its integer minimal polynomial and a rational interval containing exactly the chosen real root. Given also rational ${\varepsilon}>0$, the requested single total algorithm must output an integer $Q\ge1$ such that
\[
 \gcd(p,q)=1,\quad q\ge1,\quad
 |\alpha-p/q|<q^{-2-{\varepsilon}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\le Q.
\]
The algorithm must halt on every valid input; $p$ may be any integer. The bound can be enormous and the running time unrestricted. Merely proving that finitely many such approximations exist, or giving a bound depending on an unspecified noncomputable constant, does not supply this effective version.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.02243v1}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an algorithm compute a guaranteed denominator cutoff for every exceptionally accurate rational approximation to a specified algebraic irrational?
\subsection{Sources}
\begin{itemize}
\item[S1]Effective Approximation and Diophantine Applications; arXiv1601.02243v1,10January2016.
\url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.02243v1}.

\end{itemize}
