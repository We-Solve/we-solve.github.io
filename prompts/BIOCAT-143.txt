\section*{Higher-order epistasis in evolution}
\label{prob:143}
\label{rank:88}
\par\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Domain.} Higher-order epistasis. \quad \textbf{Retained status/source context.} Open empirical structure and mechanism programme. Higher-order interaction effects are documented; there is no warranted universal decay or sparsity law across organisms and fitness definitions.
\paragraph{Definitions and assumptions.} On a declared biallelic genotype panel $g\in\{-1,1\}^L$, let $f(g)$ be log relative fitness in a fixed assay. Its Walsh expansion is $f(g)=\sum_{A\subseteq[L]}\beta_A\prod_{i\in A}g_i$ under the uniform genotype measure. Define order energy $E_k=\sum_{|A|=k}\beta_A^2$ and total nonconstant energy $E_{\rm tot}=\sum_{k\geq1}E_k$. Assume $E_{\rm tot}>0$. Coefficients depend on allele coding, background and fitness scale; replicated measurements estimate noise separately.
\paragraph{Problem statement.} Determine which biological mechanisms predict interaction spectra and evolutionary consequences:
\[
\frac{\sum_{k>K}E_k}{E_{\rm tot}}\leq\varepsilon
\quad\text{or identify a reproducible violation},
\qquad
\Delta_if(g)=f(g^{(i)})-f(g).
\]
Here $g^{(i)}$ flips locus $i$. Test whether a truncated model correctly predicts the signs of $\Delta_i f$ and accessible paths on held-out backgrounds. Small mean-square truncation error need not preserve rare path bottlenecks.
\paragraph{Additional binding assumptions and resolution criterion.} The Walsh expansion is with respect to a declared measure on the full genotype cube, even when measurements cover only part of it. A small spectral tail gives an $L^2$ approximation under that measure, not automatically preservation of every selection-gradient sign or accessible path. Such claims need a pointwise error bound and a positive fitness-difference margin, or an explicit probability of path misclassification under the chosen evolutionary law.

\paragraph{Alternative formulations.} (i) Analyse the complete combinatorial panel and estimate an orthogonal spectrum without extrapolation. (ii) Use sparse assays to infer only interactions supported by a declared low-order model. A mechanistic alternative distinguishes specific biochemical interactions from global epistasis $f(g)=h(\sum_i a_i g_i)$; the nonlinear map $h$ can generate high-order coefficients without direct multi-locus physical contacts.
\paragraph{Obstructions and partial progress.} Recent protein-landscape modelling addresses higher-order interactions, and small empirical landscapes show their evolutionary relevance. Assay saturation or nonlinear fitness scaling can create apparent interaction orders. Missing genotypes leave coefficient estimates underdetermined unless assumptions are imposed. The unresolved biology concerns reproducible structure and physical origin, distinct from the overall prediction target in \href{https://we-solve.github.io/problems/genome-scale-fitness-landscape-cd180341/}{Problem~142}.

\paragraph{Probability laws and data.}
Every expectation or probability refers to the specified stochastic law, including initial uncertainty, process noise, observation noise and sampling as appropriate. A deterministic equation is not a probability model without a declared random input or initial law. ``Observed'' targets in population-level formulas mean population observables $g_j^*$, not noisy estimates asserted to be exact. Let $\widetilde g_j$ be independent validation estimates and suppose
\[
\mathbb{P}\{\lvert\widetilde g_j-g_j^*\rvert\le r_j\text{ for every }j\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Then an uncertainty-aware sufficient certification rule for positive target tolerances $\varepsilon_j$ is
\[
\max_j\frac{|\widehat g_j-\widetilde g_j|+r_j}{\varepsilon_j}\le1.
\]
On that simultaneous confidence event it implies every population error is at most its tolerance, by the triangle inequality. Vector/norm versions use corresponding valid simultaneous bounds. Training uncertainty must also be included when the claim averages over repeated training data. Evaluation on finitely many conditions does not establish a continuous-domain supremum without covering and regularity bounds.

\paragraph{Dynamics and well-posedness.}

Moving-domain concentration equations include dilution/transport terms. A stationary law is used only for a time-homogeneous augmented model under existence assumptions; a stationary approximation to a driven system must be justified.
