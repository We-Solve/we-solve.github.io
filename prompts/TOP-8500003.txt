{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 8500003 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb Z$, a $D(n)$-$m$-tuple is a set $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb N$ of distinct positive integers such that $a_i a_j+n$ is the square of an integer for every $i<j$. Let $Q_4(n)$ denote the number of distinct unordered sets which are $D(n)$-quadruples; this number may be infinite.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the set $U=\{n\in\mathbb Z:n\not\equiv2\pmod4,\ Q_4(n)\le2\}$ finite? \sref{8500003}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there only finitely many parameters outside the automatic modulo-four obstruction with at most two positive integer quadruples?
