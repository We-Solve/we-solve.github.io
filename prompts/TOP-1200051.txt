\section*{Some Questions — Question 51}\label{1200051}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200051 · AMR-011-0051 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $F_r$ be the free group on variables $x_1,\ldots,x_r$, where $r\geq1$ is finite. A word $w\in F_r\setminus\{1\}$ is a law in a finite group $P$ if $w(g_1,\ldots,g_r)=1$ for every tuple $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\in P^r$. Its length is the number of letters in its freely reduced form, counting a variable or its inverse as one letter.

For each integer $n\geq1$, define
\[
 L_2(n)=\min\{|w|:w\ne1\text{ is a law in every group }P
                                      \text{ with }|P|=2^n\}.
\]
The number of variables may vary in this minimization, but one word must work for all the groups of that order and every assignment in each group. Determine the asymptotic growth of $L_2(n)$ as $n\to\infty$, ideally with matching upper and lower bounds.

The power word $x^{2^n}$ shows that the minimum is finite. A law in all groups of order exactly $2^n$ is also a law in every smaller $2$-group: embed a group of order $2^m$ in its direct product with an elementary abelian group of order $2^{n-m}$. Thus replacing exactly by at most does not change the extremal function. This problem differs from finding the shortest law in one iterated wreath product, and word length is measured in the variables rather than in a chosen generating set of the tested group.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the shortest word that is an identity in every group of order two to the n?
