\section{Brouwer's Conjecture on Graph Laplacians}\label{1388}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 1388 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple undirected graph with $n=|V|\ge1$ and $m=|E|$. Its adjacency matrix is $A$, its diagonal degree matrix is $D$, and its combinatorial Laplacian is $L=D-A$. Order the real eigenvalues as $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n=0$, counting multiplicity.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such graph $G$ and every integer $k$ with $1\le k\le n$, is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\le m+\binom{k+1}{2}?
\]
\sref{1388}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the sum of any k largest graph Laplacian eigenvalues at most the number of edges plus $k(k+1)/2$?
