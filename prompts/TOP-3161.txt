\section{Cycle double cover conjecture}\label{3161}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3161 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be a finite undirected graph, possibly with parallel edges. A bridge is an edge whose removal increases the number of connected components. A circuit is a connected $2$-regular subgraph; in a multigraph, two parallel edges form a circuit, and a loop alone may be treated as a circuit. A circuit list is a multiset, so repetitions are permitted. For an orientation of $G$, orienting the traversal of a circuit distinguishes forward and backward use of each edge relative to the fixed orientation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every bridgeless graph admit a circuit list in which every edge occurs exactly twice? The source also records two directed strengthenings: for every orientation of every bridgeless graph, (i) does a list of circuits with chosen traversal directions use every edge once forward and once backward; and (ii) does such a list use every edge twice forward and twice backward? \sref{3161}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every bridgeless graph be covered exactly twice by cycles, with repetitions allowed? The same source asks for oriented double and fourfold covers with equal forward and backward counts.
