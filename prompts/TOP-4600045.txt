\section*{Sparse jointly periodic points}\label{4600045}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600045 · AMR-045-0045 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer $N\ge2$, write $X_N=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^{\mathbb Z}$
and let $\sigma_N$ be its left shift. A one-dimensional
cellular automaton $F:X_N\to X_N$ is a continuous shift-commuting
map, equivalently one given by a finite local rule. For $k\ge1$
count
\[
 P_k(F)=\#\{x\in X_N:\sigma_N^k x=x
                    \text{ and }F^m x=x\text{ for some }m\ge1\},
 \qquad \nu(F)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}P_k(F)^{1/k}.
\]
Construct a surjective $F$ for some finite $N\ge2$ such that
$\nu(F)<N$, or disprove the asserted existence.

This is an existential question about both the alphabet and the
rule. A successful construction must prove global surjectivity
and a strict asymptotic gap: equivalently, there must be
$c<N$ and $k_0$ with $P_k(F)\le c^k$ for every $k\ge k_0$.
Finite computations showing small counts at selected periods
do not establish the required limsup inequality.

The count includes spatial periods dividing $k$ and all temporal
cycle lengths. Since $\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma_N^k)$ has
$N^k$ points, the threshold $N$ represents full exponential
growth. Invertible automata have every point of each such finite
set on a temporal cycle; thus any proposed example must use
surjectivity without invertibility. The question concerns this
growth deficit, without also requiring failure of density of
jointly periodic configurations.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Construct an onto cellular automaton whose jointly periodic configurations have exponential growth strictly below the full alphabet rate.
