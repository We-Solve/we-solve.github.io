\section{Nerve graphs of Euclidean sphere packings}\label{10000063}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10000063 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a positive integer $d$, a sphere packing in $\mathbb R^d$ is a family of closed Euclidean balls $B(c_v,r_v)$ with radii $r_v>0$ and pairwise disjoint interiors. Its tangency graph has one vertex $v$ per ball and an edge $\{v,w\}$ exactly when $\|c_v-c_w\|_2=r_v+r_w$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $d$, characterize the graphs that are tangency graphs of such sphere packings in $\mathbb R^d$. \sref{10000063}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which graphs can be realized by touching Euclidean balls with disjoint interiors in each dimension?
