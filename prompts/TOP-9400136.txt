\section{Selfridge's conjecture}\label{9400136}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400136 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A Sierpi\'nski number is an odd positive integer $k$ for which $k2^n+1$ is composite for every integer $n\ge1$. Here composite means an integer greater than one which is not prime. The integer $78557$ is a known such number.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $78557$ the least Sierpi\'nski number? Equivalently, does every odd integer $k$ with $1\le k<78557$ have some $n\ge1$ for which $k2^n+1$ is prime? \sref{9400136}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is seventy-eight thousand five hundred fifty-seven the smallest odd multiplier whose entire exponential sequence plus one is composite?
