\section{Generalised Empty Hexagon Conjecture}\label{3091}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3091 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^2$, an empty hexagon consists of six points $S\subseteq P$ in strictly convex position such that
\[\operatorname{conv}(S)\cap P=S.\]
Thus no additional point lies either inside or on the boundary of the hexagon.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $\ell\geq3$, does there exist an integer $f(\ell)$ such that every finite $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ with $|P|\geq f(\ell)$ contains $\ell$ collinear points or an empty hexagon? \sref{3091}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If collinear subsets have a fixed bounded size, must every sufficiently large planar point set contain a convex hexagon with no other point in its closed hull?
