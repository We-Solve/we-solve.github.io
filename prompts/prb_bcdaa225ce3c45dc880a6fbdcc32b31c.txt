\section*{Independent arborescences in acyclic digraphs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $D=(V,A)$ be a finite acyclic digraph. Choose roots
$r_1,\ldots,r_k$, which need not be distinct, and convex sets $U_i\subseteq V$
with $r_i\in U_i$. A set is convex here if every directed path whose
endpoints lie in that set has all its vertices in the set.
An $r_i$-arborescence on $U_i$ is an outward-directed tree spanning
exactly $U_i$.

For a vertex $v$, put $I(v)=\{i:v\in U_i\}$. Paths $P_i$ from $r_i$
to $v$, indexed by $I(v)$, are openly disjoint when, for $i\ne j$,
\[
 A(P_i)\cap A(P_j)=\varnothing,\qquad
 V(P_i)\cap V(P_j)=\{v\}\cup(\{r_i\}\cap\{r_j\}).
\]
A length-zero path is allowed when $r_i=v$.

Is the following condition sufficient for independent arborescences
$T_i$ on the prescribed $U_i$: for each $v$ there exists such an
openly disjoint family of $r_i$--$v$ paths? Independence requires that
the unique $r_i$--$v$ paths in the chosen $T_i$ satisfy the displayed
conditions for every $v$. Necessity is immediate, so sufficiency gives
the requested equivalence.

The local path families may depend on $v$; the conclusion must realize
all of them compatibly through one fixed family of trees. Convexity
ensures that paths with endpoints $r_i,v\in U_i$ remain in $U_i$.
No bound is imposed on the number of sets containing a vertex.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For convex vertex sets in an acyclic digraph, do local disjoint-path
conditions guarantee a compatible family of independent rooted trees?
