{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2606 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite group $G$, $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is the group of all bijective homomorphisms from $G$ to itself. Its order and $|G|$ are positive integers. No commutativity, simplicity, or restriction on the order of $G$ is imposed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is
\[
 \left\{\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}{|G|}:G\text{ is a finite group}\right\}
 =\mathbb Q_{>0}?
\]
Equivalently, for every positive rational $r$, does some finite group satisfy $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|/|G|=r$ exactly? Density of this set in the nonnegative real numbers would be a weaker assertion. \sref{2606}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every positive rational occur exactly as a finite group’s automorphism-group order divided by its own order?
