\section{Finite entailment of Positive Horn logic}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix a finite relational vocabulary with equality. A positive Horn sentence is built from atomic relation formulas and equalities using only conjunction and existential and universal quantification. Structures have nonempty domains; no function symbols or negations inside these sentences are assumed. Write $\phi\models\psi$ for entailment in all structures and $\phi\models_{\mathrm{fin}}\psi$ for entailment in all finite structures.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every pair of positive Horn sentences $\phi,\psi$, if some structure satisfies $\phi\wedge\neg\psi$, must some finite structure satisfy it? Equivalently, does $\phi\models_{\mathrm{fin}}\psi$ imply $\phi\models\psi$? The accompanying decision question asks whether an algorithm, given $\phi,\psi$, decides $\phi\models_{\mathrm{fin}}\psi$. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every counterexample to entailment between positive Horn sentences have a finite counterexample, and is finite entailment decidable?
