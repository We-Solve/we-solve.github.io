\section{Kirby Problem 1.18}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


All knots and links below are smooth in the oriented $3$-sphere, up to ambient isotopy, unless another ambient manifold is specified. The meridional rank $\mu(L)$ is the least size of a generating set of $\pi_1(S^3\setminus L)$ consisting of meridians (loops linking one component once, transported to a basepoint). The bridge number $b(L)$ is the minimum number of local maxima in a bridge presentation. Two groups have the same finite quotients if the same finite groups occur, up to isomorphism, as quotients. A hyperbolic knot has a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric on its complement.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
(a) Is $\mu(L)=b(L)$ for every link? (b) For knots whose groups have the same finite quotients, must their meridional ranks agree; can meridional rank be explicitly recovered from these quotients? (c) Does a meridian-preserving epimorphism of link groups force $b(L_1)\geq b(L_2)$? (d) For an arbitrary epimorphism of knot groups, must both $\mu(K_1)\geq\mu(K_2)$ and $b(K_1)\geq b(K_2)$ hold? (e) For a hyperbolic knot $K$, is every subgroup generated by at most $b(K)-1$ meridians free? \sref{statement}{1}\sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Relate meridional generators and bridges, determine what finite quotients and epimorphisms detect, and test freeness of small meridional subgroups of hyperbolic knot groups.
