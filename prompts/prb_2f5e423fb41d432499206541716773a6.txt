\section{Local-to-global covering rigidity for Cayley graphs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finitely generated group $\Gamma$ and finite symmetric generating set $S$ not containing the identity, its Cayley graph has vertices $\Gamma$ and edges $\{g,gs\}$ for $s\in S$. For a graph $H$ with unit edges, $B_H(v,r)$ is the induced rooted graph on vertices of distance at most $r$ from $v$. A graph covering $q:G\to H$ is a surjective graph map inducing a bijection from the neighbors of each vertex $v$ onto the neighbors of $q(v)$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every Cayley graph $G$, is there an integer $r=r(G)\ge1$ such that every connected graph $H$ whose rooted radius-$r$ balls are isomorphic to $B_G(1,r)$ admits a covering map $G\to H$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does sufficiently large local agreement with a Cayley graph force a global graph covering from it?
