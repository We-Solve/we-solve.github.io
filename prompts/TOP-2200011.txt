\section*{Problems Around Polynomials — Problem 5}\label{2200011}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2200011 · AMR-021-0011 · Algebra · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a degree $d\geq1$ and a sign pattern
$\sigma=(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_d)\in\{+,-\}^{d+1}$, read from the
leading coefficient down to the constant term. Let $c(\sigma)$ be
the number of changes between consecutive signs and let
$s(\sigma)=d-c(\sigma)$ be the number of preservations.
An ordered pair $(u,v)$ of nonnegative integers is admissible when
\[
 u\leq c(\sigma),\quad u\equiv c(\sigma)\pmod2,\qquad
 v\leq s(\sigma),\quad v\equiv s(\sigma)\pmod2.
\]

Call this pair realizable if there is a real polynomial
$P(x)=a_0x^d+a_1x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d$, with every coefficient nonzero
and $\operatorname{sign}(a_j)=\sigma_j$, having exactly $u$ positive
and $v$ negative real roots, counted with algebraic multiplicities.
Its remaining $d-u-v$ roots must be nonreal and occur in conjugate
pairs; zero itself cannot be a root because $a_d\ne0$.

Determine, for every sign pattern in every degree, precisely which
admissible pairs are realizable. The displayed conditions are the
necessary restrictions coming from applying Descartes' rule to
$P(x)$ and $P(-x)$; the problem is to characterize any additional
obstructions. A solution should supply realizations for the allowed
pairs as well as proofs of impossibility for excluded pairs.
The classification includes pairs with either coordinate zero and
pairs with both coordinates positive. No claim that either of
these subclasses is automatically realizable is built into the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which positive and negative root counts can occur for a specified pattern of coefficient signs?
