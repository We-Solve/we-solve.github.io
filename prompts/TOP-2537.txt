\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.28}\label{2537}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2537 · KOU-21.28 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)$ be a finite nontrivial simple
permutation group acting transitively on a finite set $\Omega$.
The action is faithful because $G$ is a subgroup of the full permutation
group. An element $g\in G$ is a derangement if it has no fixed point,
that is, $g\alpha\ne\alpha$ for all $\alpha\in\Omega$.
Define the proportion of derangements in this action by
\[
 \delta(G,\Omega)=\frac{|\{g\in G:g\text{ fixes no point of }\Omega\}|}{|G|}.
\]
The denominator counts all elements, including the identity, rather than
conjugacy classes. A uniformly random group element is therefore a
derangement with probability $\delta(G,\Omega)$.

The proposed inequality is
\[
 \delta(G,\Omega)\ge\frac{89}{325}
\]
for every such simple group and every faithful transitive action.
The source writes $\delta(G)$, with the action understood; displaying
$\Omega$ makes this dependence explicit. Simple means nontrivial with
no proper nontrivial normal subgroup, and thus includes cyclic prime-order
cases as well as nonabelian cases.

The lower bound is one absolute rational number, independent of group
order and permutation degree. The claim does not require the action to
be primitive, nor impose any structural hypothesis on a point stabilizer.
One cannot check only a group's natural representation: all eligible
transitive representations are part of the question. A counterexample
would be an explicit action with strictly smaller derangement proportion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the fraction of derangements at least $89/325$ in every faithful transitive action of a finite simple group?
