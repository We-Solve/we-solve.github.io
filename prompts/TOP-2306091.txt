\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.91}\label{2306091}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2306091 · Analysis}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C$ be a domain, choose $p\in\Omega$, and let $\omega_\Omega^p$ denote harmonic measure on its finite boundary (the distribution of the first finite boundary hit by planar Brownian motion started at $p$). A set $E\subset\partial\Omega$ has full harmonic measure if $\omega_\Omega^p(\partial\Omega\setminus E)=0$. For an arc $C_r$ on the circle $|w-z|=r$, its angular measure is $\theta(C_r)=\operatorname{length}(C_r)/r$, in radians. The integral below requires the selected angular measures to be measurable in $r$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every $\Omega$ have a full-harmonic-measure set $E$ such that for each $z\in E$ one can choose circular arcs $C_r\subset\Omega\cap\{w:|w-z|=r\}$ for all sufficiently small $r>0$, with \[\lim_{R\to0^+}\frac1{\pi R^2}\int_0^R\theta(C_r)r\,dr=\frac12?\] \sref{2306091}{2}
The source records McMillan's result for simply connected domains. Its separate weak sequence statement is not substituted for this averaged target. \sref{2306091}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does almost every boundary point for harmonic measure admit small interior circular arcs with the specified averaged half-disc angular density?
