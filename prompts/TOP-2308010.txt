\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. For $p>0$, the Bergman space is $A^p(\mathbb D)=\{f\text{ analytic}:\|f\|_{A^p}=(\int_{\mathbb D}|f|^p\,dA)^{1/p}<\infty\}$, where $dA=dx\,dy$ is ordinary area measure. For $f\in A^2$ write $\mathcal Pf=\{pf:p\in\mathbb C[z]\}$. Call $f$ cyclic if the closure of $\mathcal Pf$ in the $A^2$ norm is $A^2$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is every $f$ for which both $f$ and its analytic reciprocal $1/f$ belong to $A^2$ cyclic? More generally, if $f\in A^2$ and there are $a,c>0$ with $|f(z)|\ge c(1-|z|)^a$ for every $z\in\mathbb D$, must $f$ be cyclic? \sref{2308010}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do an integrable analytic reciprocal or the stated polynomial lower bound guarantee Bergman cyclicity?
