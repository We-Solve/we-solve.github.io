\section{Closed Curves with a Nondegenerate Frenet Frame}\label{6500006}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 6500006 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Here a regular closed curve means a smooth $\gamma:S^1\to\mathbb R^n$ with $\gamma'(t)\wedge\gamma''(t)\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma^{(n)}(t)\ne0$ everywhere. Gram--Schmidt applied to these ordered derivatives gives its Frenet frame $E(t)\in\mathrm O(n)$. The Frenet-frame length is the length of $E$ in the standard bi-invariant Riemannian metric on $\mathrm O(n)$. Embed a convex plane oval in $\mathbb R^n$ and traverse it $m$ times. Let $\mu(n)$ be the least positive such $m$ for which this multiple traversal admits arbitrarily small smooth perturbations that are regular in the above nondegeneracy sense. The source records $\mu(2)=1$ and $\mu(3)=2$. Let $\ell_2$ be the length, in the same metric, of the circle subgroup $\mathrm{SO}(2)\subset\mathrm O(n)$ rotating a fixed plane.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $\mu(n)$ for $n>3$. Does every regular closed curve in $\mathbb R^n$ have Frenet-frame length strictly greater than $\mu(n)\ell_2$? \sref{6500006}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many traversals of a plane oval permit a nearby nondegenerate curve in higher dimension, and do they determine a strict lower bound for frame length?
