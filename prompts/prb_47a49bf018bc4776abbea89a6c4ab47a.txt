\section{Partitionning a tournament into k-strongly connected subtournaments.}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A tournament is an orientation of a finite complete graph. For $r\geq1$, a tournament is strongly $r$-connected if it has more than $r$ vertices and remains strongly connected after deletion of fewer than $r$ vertices. This convention excludes a one-vertex part. For $W\subseteq V(T)$, $T[W]$ is the induced subtournament.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given arbitrary positive integers $k_1,\ldots,k_p$, does there exist an integer $g(k_1,\ldots,k_p)$ such that every strongly $g(k_1,\ldots,k_p)$-connected tournament $T$ has a partition $V(T)=V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_p$ with every $T[V_i]$ a nontrivial strongly $k_i$-connected tournament? \sref{statement}{2} \sref{statement}{3}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can enough initial connectivity guarantee a partition into induced tournaments with any prescribed positive connectivity requirements?
