\section*{Conjecture 2.5 — Lang-Waldschmidt}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a real $\varepsilon>0$. For any integer $m\ge1$, choose nonzero integers $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ and nonzero integer exponents $b_1,\ldots,b_m$. Negative bases and negative exponents are allowed; integer powers therefore define an unambiguous nonzero rational number
\[
A=\prod_{i=1}^m a_i^{b_i}.
\]
Write $B=\max_i|b_i|$, and suppose $A\ne1$.

The Lang--Waldschmidt conjecture in this multiplicative formulation asks whether there is a constant $C(\varepsilon)>0$, depending only on $\varepsilon$, such that every such choice satisfies
\[
|A-1|\ge
\frac{C(\varepsilon)^m B}
 {\left(\prod_{i=1}^m|b_i|\prod_{i=1}^m|a_i|\right)^{1+\varepsilon}}.
\]
The constant must be chosen before $m$, the bases, and the exponents. Its allowed dependence on the number of factors is precisely through the displayed power $C(\varepsilon)^m$.

No coprimality or multiplicative-independence assumption is imposed on the bases. The sole exclusion $A=1$ is necessary, since otherwise the left side would vanish while the denominator and numerator remain positive. Exponents are required nonzero to keep the formula defined; factors with zero exponent can simply be omitted before applying the statement.

The problem is a uniform quantitative lower bound for a nontrivial rational multiplicative expression near one. Establishing a positive bound that depends arbitrarily on the individual bases or exponents would not give the asserted dependence.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every nontrivial product of integer powers bounded away from one by the stated uniform Lang–Waldschmidt estimate?
