\section*{Kirby Problem 4.48}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Delta$ be a compact contractible topological $4$-manifold with
boundary. Contractibility means that its identity map is homotopic
to a constant map. Define
\[
 \operatorname{Homeo}_{\partial}(\Delta)
 =\{h:\Delta\to\Delta\text{ homeomorphism}:h|_{\partial\Delta}=\mathrm{id}\}.
\]
Use the compact-open topology, whose subbasic sets specify that the
image of a compact subset lies in a chosen open subset. Since $\Delta$
is compact and metrizable, this is the usual topology of uniform
convergence on maps; restricted to the homeomorphism group it gives
the standard group topology.

Is this space contractible for every such $\Delta$? Explicitly, does
there exist a continuous map
\[
 F:\operatorname{Homeo}_{\partial}(\Delta)\times[0,1]
           \longrightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}_{\partial}(\Delta)
\]
with $F(h,0)=h$ and $F(h,1)=\mathrm{id}$ for every $h$?
Every intermediate map must remain a homeomorphism fixing the boundary
pointwise. A contraction to any other fixed group element is equivalent
by composition with its inverse.

Contractibility of the underlying manifold is a hypothesis; contractibility
of its homeomorphism space is the proposed conclusion. Constructing
an isotopy separately for each boundary-fixing homeomorphism would show
path connectedness only. The stronger request requires those isotopies
to vary continuously with the entire homeomorphism parameter.
No smoothability of $\Delta$ is assumed, and replacing homeomorphisms
by diffeomorphisms would change the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the compact-open space of boundary-fixing homeomorphisms of every compact contractible four-manifold contractible?
