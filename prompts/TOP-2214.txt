An $r$-uniform hypergraph is a family of distinct $r$-element subsets of a vertex set. A proper vertex colouring has no monochromatic edge. A transversal is a vertex set meeting every edge; its minimum size is the covering number. \sref{2214}{1} \sref{2214}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $r\geq 3$. For an $r$-uniform hypergraph $G$ let $\tau(G)$ denote the covering number (or transversal number), the minimum size of a set of vertices which includes at least one from each edge in $G$.
Determine the best possible $t$ such that, if $G$ is an $r$-uniform hypergraph $G$ where every subgraph $G'$ on at most $3r-3$ vertices has $\tau(G')\leq 1$, we have $\tau(G)\leq t$. \sref{2214}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Determine the sharp t(r). \sref{2214}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For a uniform hypergraph, how large can its covering number be when every sufficiently small subhypergraph has all edges through a single common vertex?
