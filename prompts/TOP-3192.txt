\section{Total Colouring Conjecture}\label{3192}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3192 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite simple graph $G$, a proper total coloring assigns colors to $V(G)\cup E(G)$ so that adjacent vertices, edges sharing a vertex, and incident vertex--edge pairs have different colors. Let $\chi''(G)$ be the minimum number of colors and $\Delta(G)$ the maximum degree.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every graph $G$ satisfy \[\chi''(G)\in\{\Delta(G)+1,\Delta(G)+2\},\] equivalently $\chi''(G)\le\Delta(G)+2$? \sref{3192}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are at most two more colors than the maximum degree enough to color vertices and edges simultaneously?
