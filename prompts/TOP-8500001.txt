\section{Absolute bounds for rational Diophantine tuples}\label{8500001}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 8500001 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $q\in\mathbb Q$, a rational $D(q)$-$m$-tuple is a set $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ of distinct numbers such that $a_i a_j+q$ is a square in $\mathbb Q$ for every $i<j$. Elements may have either sign; zero squares are allowed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a constant $M\in\mathbb N$ such that every rational $D(1)$-tuple has cardinality at most $M$? \sref{8500001}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there one absolute bound on the size of all rational Diophantine tuples?
