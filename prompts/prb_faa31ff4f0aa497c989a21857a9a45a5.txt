\section*{Faces of intricate polytopes}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a full-dimensional convex polytope $P\subset\mathbb R^d$, let $f_i(P)$
be its number of $i$-dimensional faces. Vertices correspond to $i=0$ and
facets to $i=d-1$. Count nonempty proper faces by
$F(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}f_i(P)$; including the empty face and $P$ itself adds
only two. For each feasible $n$, consider
\[
 M_d(n)=\max\{F(P):f_0(P)=f_{d-1}(P)=n\}.
\]
Determine the growth of this function for each fixed dimension. In
particular, is $M_4(n)/n$ unbounded along feasible $n$? This asks for
superlinear total face complexity despite having only $n$ vertices and
$n$ facets.

A closely related four-dimensional formulation asks whether the fatness
$\phi(P)=(f_1(P)+f_2(P))/(f_0(P)+f_3(P))$ is uniformly bounded over all
four-polytopes, without requiring $f_0=f_3$. These formulations should keep
their different input constraints explicit.
In higher dimensions the source asks whether constructions from joins of
polygons have the optimal order of growth. The join of $P$ and $Q$ is their
convex hull after placing them in disjoint affine subspaces so that its
dimension is $\dim P+\dim Q+1$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a convex polytope have far more intermediate-dimensional faces than
vertices and facets? Determine the sharp growth, especially in dimension four.
