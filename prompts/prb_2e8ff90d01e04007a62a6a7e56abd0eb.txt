\section*{Chern-lattice obstructions to positively curved principal two-torus bundles}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $T^2=S^1\times S^1$ be the two-dimensional torus, with its usual Lie-group structure.
A smooth principal $T^2$-bundle $\pi:E\to B$ consists of a smooth free right $T^2$-action on $E$ whose orbit space is $B$, with local equivariant trivializations $\pi^{-1}(U)\cong U\times T^2$.
Free means that the only torus element fixing any point is the identity.
Consider bundles for which the total space, and hence the base, is a closed connected smooth manifold.

Does there exist such a bundle whose total space admits a Riemannian metric $g$ with
\[
 \sec_g(\sigma)>0
 \qquad\text{for every two-plane }\sigma\subseteq T_xE
 \text{ and every }x\in E?
\]
The question is existential over the dimension, the base, the bundle, and the metric.
No metric is prescribed on the base, and the positively curved metric on $E$ is not required to make the torus action isometric.
In particular, the target is not restricted to connection metrics or Riemannian submersions.
Equivalently, can a closed manifold carrying a free smooth two-torus action also carry some metric of positive sectional curvature?
A merely locally free action, with finite nontrivial stabilizers, would give a different question involving an orbifold quotient and does not supply the required principal bundle.
Curvature is required in every tangent two-plane, not only in planes transverse to the torus orbits.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the total space of a principal two-torus bundle carry a metric of strictly positive sectional curvature?
