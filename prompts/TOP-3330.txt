\section{Subgroup formed by elements of order dividing n}\label{3330}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3330 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be a finite group with identity $1$, and let $n\geq1$ divide its order $|G|$. Define $S_n(G)=\{x\in G:x^n=1\}$, the set of elements whose orders divide $n$. A subgroup is a subset containing $1$ and closed under multiplication and inversion; a characteristic subgroup is preserved by every automorphism of $G$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $|S_n(G)|=n$, must $S_n(G)$ be a subgroup of $G$? If it is a subgroup, it is automatically characteristic, because its defining equation is invariant under every automorphism. \sref{3330}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does having exactly n elements whose orders divide n force those elements to form a subgroup, whenever n divides the finite group order?
