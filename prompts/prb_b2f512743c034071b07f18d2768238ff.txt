\section*{Uniform pinching via the best-pinching functional}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a closed connected smooth manifold of dimension $n\geq2$.
For a Riemannian metric $g$ of strictly positive sectional curvature, define
\[
 K_{\min}(g)=\min_{x,\,\sigma\subseteq T_xM}\sec_g(\sigma),
 \qquad
 K_{\max}(g)=\max_{x,\,\sigma\subseteq T_xM}\sec_g(\sigma),
\]
where $\sigma$ ranges over tangent two-planes.
Compactness ensures $0<K_{\min}(g)\leq K_{\max}(g)<\infty$.
Call the metric globally $\delta$-pinched if
$K_{\min}(g)/K_{\max}(g)\geq\delta$.
This ratio is unchanged by multiplying the metric by a positive constant.

Does there exist, for each $n$, a number $\delta(n)>0$ such that every closed $n$-manifold admitting some positive-sectional-curvature metric also admits a globally $\delta(n)$-pinched metric?
Equivalently, after rescaling the new metric, must every such manifold admit $h$ with
\[
 \delta(n)\leq\sec_h(\sigma)\leq1
 \quad\text{for every tangent two-plane }\sigma?
\]
The replacement metric may depend on the manifold and need not resemble an initially supplied metric.
The bound is uniform over manifold types, rather than a separate positive pinching constant for each compact metric, which exists automatically.
Global pinching compares planes at different points as well as at the same point; merely pointwise pinching is a different requirement.
No symmetry, connectivity beyond connectedness, or lower volume hypothesis is added.
The question asks whether allowing a new metric removes arbitrarily poor optimal pinching within any fixed dimension.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every positively curved closed manifold be given a new metric with a positive global pinching ratio depending only on its dimension?
