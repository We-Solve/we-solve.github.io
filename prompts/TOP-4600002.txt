\section*{Classify shifts of finite type}\label{4600002}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 4600002 · AMR-045-0002 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a square matrix $A$ with nonnegative integer entries, form the finite
directed multigraph having $A_{ij}$ directed edges from $i$ to $j$. Let
$X_A$ consist of all bi-infinite edge sequences in which the terminal
vertex of each edge is the initial vertex of the next. The shift
$\sigma_A((e_j)_j)=(e_{j+1})_j$ acts on this compact product space.
Such edge shifts represent all two-sided shifts of finite type up to
topological conjugacy.

The classification problem asks for usable complete invariants of these
systems. Its decision version asks for an algorithm which, on every finite
input pair $A,B$, halts and correctly decides whether a homeomorphism
$H:X_A\to X_B$ exists with $H\sigma_A=\sigma_BH$. No irreducibility,
mixing, or equal-size assumption is imposed on the input matrices.

Equivalently, one must decide strong shift equivalence over $\mathbb Z_+$:
whether $A$ can be connected to $B$ by finitely many replacements
$RS\leftrightarrow SR$ with nonnegative integer rectangular matrices $R,S$.
The sizes of intermediate matrices are unrestricted. A procedure which only
enumerates successful chains does not meet the decision requirement, since
it must also terminate when no conjugacy exists. Counts of periodic points
and entropy provide necessary invariants but do not constitute the requested
complete classification. Conjugacy here preserves one application of the
shift, without passing to higher powers.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a complete, effective classification of finite-type shifts, including an algorithm that always decides whether two presentations are conjugate.
