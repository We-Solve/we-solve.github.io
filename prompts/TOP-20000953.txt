\section*{Free-cut decomposition for ordered matchings versus triangles}\label{20000953}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20000953 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0078 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An ordered graph is a finite simple graph together with a linear order
on its vertices. An ordered copy of $H$ in $G$ is an injective map
$f:V(H)\to V(G)$ that preserves the vertex order and takes every edge
of $H$ to an edge of $G$. Nonedges need not be preserved.
For ordered graphs $H_1,H_2$, let $r_<(H_1,H_2)$ be the least $N$
such that every red--blue coloring of the complete ordered graph on
$1<2<\cdots<N$ contains a red ordered copy of $H_1$ or a blue ordered
copy of $H_2$.

Let $M$ range over all ordered matchings on $n$ vertices, with $n$
even: its edges are disjoint pairs that cover its vertices, but the
endpoints can interleave arbitrarily in the prescribed order.
The question is whether there are absolute constants $\varepsilon>0$
and $C<\infty$ such that
\[
                       r_<(K_3,M)\le Cn^{2-\varepsilon}
\]
for every such $n$ and every ordering of every matching $M$.
The order on the triangle has only one isomorphism type.
Both constants must be independent of the particular matching and its
ordering. A bound with an exponent depending on $M$ would not establish
the assertion. The target is a uniform polynomial saving over a
quadratic bound, even for highly crossing or nested arrangements of
the matching edges.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a uniform subquadratic bound for ordered Ramsey numbers of a triangle versus every ordered matching?
