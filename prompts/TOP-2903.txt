\section*{Kirby Problem 4.27}\label{2903}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2903 · KP-4.27 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $B^3$ be the standard closed smooth $3$-ball. Consider smooth
embeddings into the standard $S^4$ with images $B_0,B_1$ and a
common boundary $S=\partial B_0=\partial B_1$, a smoothly embedded
$2$-sphere. The interiors of the two balls are not required to be
disjoint.

A topological ambient isotopy relative to $S$ is a continuous family
of homeomorphisms $H_t:S^4\to S^4$, $0\le t\le1$, with $H_0$
the identity, $H_t|_S$ the identity for every $t$, and $H_1(B_0)=B_1$.
A smooth ambient isotopy has the analogous properties with $(x,t)
\mapsto H_t(x)$ smooth and each $H_t$ a diffeomorphism.

Do there exist $B_0,B_1$ which admit such a topological isotopy but
admit no such smooth isotopy? This is the requested exotic pair of
embedded $3$-balls relative to their common boundary. The distinction
concerns their placements in $S^4$; each individual ball already has
the standard smooth ball structure.

The boundary sphere is fixed pointwise throughout both sorts of
isotopy. Merely finding two nonisotopic balls would not answer the
question unless they can also be shown topologically isotopic under
this boundary restriction. No fixed parameterizations of the interiors
are required, and no prescribed geometry of the boundary sphere is
assumed. The entire smooth-isotopy class, not just a specific attempted
isotopy, must be excluded.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two smooth three-balls with the same boundary in the four-sphere be topologically but not smoothly ambient isotopic while fixing that boundary?
