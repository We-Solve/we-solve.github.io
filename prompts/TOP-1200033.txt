\section*{Some Questions — Question 33}\label{1200033}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1200033 · AMR-011-0033 · Graph Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an integer $k>1$, and let $C$ range over transient simple $k$-regular Cayley graphs rooted at the identity $o$. Simple random walk starts at $o$ and at each discrete time chooses a neighbor uniformly. Define the first positive return time and eventual return probability by
\[
 \tau_o^+=\inf\{t\geq1:X_t=o\},\qquad
 q(C)=\mathbb P_o(\tau_o^+<\infty).
\]
An empty event gives infinite return time. Transience means $q(C)<1$, equivalently that the expected total number of root visits is finite.

Does there exist a number $c(k)<1$ such that $q(C)\leq c(k)$ for every transient $k$-regular Cayley graph? The constant may depend on the degree but not on the group, generating set, or any further geometric parameter. If there are no transient graphs of a particular degree, its assertion is vacuous.

By the Markov property, the Green value including time zero is $1/(1-q(C))$. Thus the question is equivalently a uniform finite bound on this expected visit count in each fixed degree. The probability here is that of ever returning, not a fixed-time return probability or the spectral radius of the random-walk operator. Transient graphs can be amenable, so a bound restricted to nonamenable groups is insufficient. A counterexample would be a sequence of transient graphs of one degree whose eventual return probabilities approach one.
\subsection{Short English statement}
In each fixed degree, is the eventual return probability uniformly bounded away from one for all transient Cayley graphs?
