\section*{Unique balance and a codegree-variance obstruction for the tetrahedron conjecture}\label{20001021}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 20001021 · AIM-COMBINATORICS-0146 · Combinatorics · AIM Workshop Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A simple $3$-uniform hypergraph $H$ on $n$ vertices has an edge set
$E(H)\subseteq\binom{V(H)}3$. The tetrahedron $K_4^{(3)}$ consists
of all four triples on four vertices. A copy in $H$ means four distinct
vertices whose four triples all belong to $E(H)$.
Let $\operatorname{ex}(n,K_4^{(3)})$ be the maximum number of edges
in a tetrahedron-free $3$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices. Its
Tur\'an density is
\[
 \pi(K_4^{(3)})=\lim_{n\to\infty}
       \frac{\operatorname{ex}(n,K_4^{(3)})}{\binom n3}.
\]
The limit exists by the usual averaging monotonicity of normalized
extremal densities. The conjecture asks whether this limit is $5/9$.

In upper-bound form, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently
large $n$, every $3$-uniform hypergraph with more than
$(5/9+\varepsilon)\binom n3$ edges should contain a tetrahedron.
The candidate density has a concrete interpretation: partition the
vertices into three nearly equal parts $A,B,C$; include triples meeting
all three parts, and triples of types $AAB$, $BBC$, and $CCA$.
This construction is tetrahedron-free and has density tending to $5/9$.
The problem is whether any construction can have a larger limiting
density. The forbidden copy is an ordinary subhypergraph, with no
induced-copy convention or restrictions on vertex degrees.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is five ninths the maximum limiting edge density of a tetrahedron-free triple system?
