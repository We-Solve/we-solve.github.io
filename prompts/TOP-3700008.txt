\section*{Number of degenerate Herman rings}\label{3700008}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3700008 · AMR-036-0008 · Dynamical Systems · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a rational map $f$ of degree $d\geq2$, let $J(f)$
be its Julia set. Call a Jordan curve $C$ a degenerate
Herman ring of $f$ if all the following conditions hold.
The curve is contained in $J(f)$ and invariant under $f$.
The restriction $f|_C$ is topologically conjugate to an
irrational rotation: there are a homeomorphism
$h:\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to C$ and $\theta\notin\mathbb Q$
such that $f(h(t))=h(t+\theta)$.
Finally, $C$ is neither a generalized circle nor a boundary
component of a rotation domain.

A generalized circle means a round circle or an extended
straight line. A rotation domain is a periodic Fatou component
whose return map is analytically conjugate to an irrational
rotation on a disk or annulus. No smoothness or analyticity
is assumed for the Jordan curve $C$.

Let $\mathcal R(f)$ be the set of distinct curves satisfying
these conditions, counted as subsets of the sphere.
Is $\mathcal R(f)$ finite for every rational map $f$?
More strongly, does there exist a finite integer $B(d)$ such
that
\[
                    \#\mathcal R(f)\leq B(d)
             \quad\text{whenever }\deg f=d?
\]
The first question permits a bound depending on the individual
map; the second asks for a bound depending only on degree.
The count concerns invariant degenerate rings themselves,
not the ordinary annular Fatou components called Herman rings.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there finitely many degenerate Herman rings, and can their number be bounded using only the rational map's degree?
