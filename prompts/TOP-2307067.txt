\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A domain is a connected open subset. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^2$ be strictly pseudoconvex: near each boundary point it has a $C^2$ defining function $\rho$ with nonzero gradient and positive Levi form $\sum_{j,k}\rho_{z_j\bar z_k}v_j\bar v_k$ on every nonzero complex tangent vector $v$. Let $f\in\mathcal O(\Omega)$ and set $V=\{z\in\Omega:f(z)=0\}$. Finite area means $\mathcal H^2(V)<\infty$ inside $\Omega$, using ordinary real two-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Equality of zero sets below means equality of sets, not an imposed equality of vanishing multiplicities.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does finite area of $V$ imply that there exists a bounded holomorphic $g$ on $\Omega$ with $\{g=0\}=V$? The domain is arbitrary within the strictly pseudoconvex class in $\mathbb C^2$; no ball or additional cohomology hypothesis is imposed. The source records an affirmative answer for the ball as a special case. \sref{2307067}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finite-area holomorphic zero set in a strictly pseudoconvex domain in two complex dimensions have a bounded holomorphic defining function?
