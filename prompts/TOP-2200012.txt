\section{Problems Around Polynomials — Conjecture 7}\label{2200012}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2200012 · Algebra}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Fix $d\geq1$ and a sign pattern $\sigma=(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_d)\in\{+,-\}^{d+1}$, ordered from the leading coefficient to the constant coefficient. Let $c(\sigma)$ count sign changes between consecutive entries and $u(\sigma)$ count preservations, so $c(\sigma)+u(\sigma)=d$. A pair $(r_+,r_-)$ of nonnegative integers is admissible if
\[r_+\leq c(\sigma),\quad r_+\equiv c(\sigma)\pmod2,\qquad r_-\leq u(\sigma),\quad r_-\equiv u(\sigma)\pmod2.\]
A realization is a real polynomial $p(x)=a_0x^d+\cdots+a_d$ with every coefficient nonzero, $\operatorname{sgn}(a_j)=\sigma_j$, and exactly $r_+$ positive and $r_-$ negative roots, counted with multiplicity.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every sign pattern $\sigma$, is each admissible pair $(r_+,r_-)$ with $r_+>0$ and $r_->0$ realizable? Equivalently, can nonrealizability occur only when at least one of the two root counts is zero? \sref{2200012}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all Descartes-admissible positive and negative root counts attainable whenever both counts are positive?
