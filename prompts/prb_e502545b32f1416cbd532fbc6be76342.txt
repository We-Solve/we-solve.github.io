\section*{Kirby Problem 4.1}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S^4=\{x\in\mathbb R^5:\|x\|=1\}$ carry its usual smooth structure.
A smooth structure on a topological $4$-manifold is a maximal atlas
of charts to open subsets of $\mathbb R^4$ whose transition maps are
smooth. Two smooth structures are equivalent here if the resulting
smooth manifolds are diffeomorphic: there must be a bijection that is
smooth with smooth inverse. The diffeomorphism need not be the identity
on the underlying point set.

Is every smooth closed manifold $X$ homeomorphic to $S^4$ diffeomorphic
to the standard $S^4$? Equivalently, does the topological $4$-sphere
have exactly one diffeomorphism class of smooth structures?
A smooth manifold homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the standard
sphere would be an exotic smooth $4$-sphere and would give a negative
answer.

A closely related formulation uses a smooth homotopy $4$-sphere: a
closed smooth manifold homotopy equivalent to $S^4$. The topological
four-dimensional Poincar\'e theorem identifies its underlying topological
manifold with $S^4$, so the smooth question asks whether every such
homotopy sphere is standard as a smooth manifold.
The required comparison is a diffeomorphism, not merely a homeomorphism,
a homotopy equivalence, or an agreement of homology groups. No particular
atlas, handle decomposition, embedding, or candidate construction is
part of the input; the assertion quantifies over all smoothings.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every smooth manifold homeomorphic to the four-sphere diffeomorphic to the standard four-sphere?
