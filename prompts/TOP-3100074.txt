\section*{An asymmetric covering code of radius R is a set of binary n-words so that every binary n-word can be reached from one o}\label{3100074}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3100074 · AMR-030-0074 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Order binary words coordinatewise, so $x\le y$ means that every
one in $x$ is also a one in $y$. The upward asymmetric ball of
radius $R$ centered at $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ is
\[
 B_R^+(x)=\{y\in\{0,1\}^n:x\le y,\ d_H(x,y)\le R\}.
\]
It consists of words obtained by changing at most $R$ zeroes
of $x$ to ones; changing ones to zeroes is forbidden.
An upward covering code is a set $C$ with
$\bigcup_{x\in C}B_R^+(x)=\{0,1\}^n$. Let $K^+(n,R)$ be the
minimum possible $|C|$.

For each fixed integer $R\ge1$, determine its sharp asymptotic
growth as $n\to\infty$. The proposed leading term is
\[
                K^+(n,R)\sim 2^R R!\,\frac{2^n}{n^R}.
\]
Is this correct, and if not, what leading constant or upper and
lower limiting densities replace it?

Unlike symmetric Hamming balls, these balls have sizes depending
on their centers: if $x$ has $w$ ones, their size is
$\sum_{j=0}^{\min(R,n-w)}\binom{n-w}{j}$. Thus uniform sphere
sizes cannot simply be assumed. Complementing every bit gives
the equivalent downward convention. The source's exponential
factor $2^R$ in its general order formula is corrected to $2^n$;
the separate factor $2^R$ in the conjectured coefficient remains.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the sharp asymptotic minimum size of fixed-radius asymmetric binary covering codes with zero-to-one errors.
