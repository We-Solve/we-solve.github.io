\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite simple graph $G$ and $1\le k\le|V(G)|$, define
\[
 \operatorname{OPT}(G,k)=\max_{\substack{S\subseteq V(G)\\|S|=k}}|E(G[S])|.
\]
The assertion seeks an absolute ${\varepsilon}>0$ such that distinguishing
\[
 \operatorname{OPT}(G,k)\ge t
 \quad\text{from}\quad
 \operatorname{OPT}(G,k)<\frac{t}{1+{\varepsilon}}
\]
is NP-hard under deterministic polynomial-time gap reductions, for positive integer threshold $t$. A gap reduction maps yes-instances of an NP-hard language to the first promise and no-instances to the second. Inputs inside the gap need not be classified. The selected subgraph has exactly $k$ vertices; an at-least-$k$ density problem is not interchangeable. Hardness conditional on an average-case conjecture or a stronger time hypothesis does not establish the stated NP-hardness.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25464v1}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Is approximating the maximum number of edges induced by exactly $k$ vertices within some fixed nontrivial constant factor already NP-hard?
\subsection{Sources}

\item[S1]Bundit Laekhanukit, Pasin Manurangsi and Ohad Trabelsi, A Note on Approximability of Densest At-Least-k-Subgraph, arXiv:2605.25464v1 (25 May 2026).
