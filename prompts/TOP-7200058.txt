\section{Tripod packing}\label{7200058}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 7200058 · Geometry}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $p=(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3$, define the tripod
\[T(p)=\{(x+t,y,z):t\geq0\}\cup\{(x,y+t,z):t\geq0\}\cup\{(x,y,z+t):t\geq0\}.\]
Thus its three arms are infinite closed rays in the positive coordinate directions. For an integer $n\geq1$, the allowed apices are the lattice points $\{1,\ldots,n\}^3$. Define $F(n)$ to be the maximum cardinality of a subset $A$ of this lattice cube such that $T(p)\cap T(q)=\varnothing$ for all distinct $p,q\in A$. The rays need not stay in the cube.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $F(n)$, or its asymptotic growth as $n\to\infty$: how many pairwise disjoint coordinate tripods can have their apices in the $n\times n\times n$ lattice cube? \sref{7200058}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
How many disjoint tripods with three infinite coordinate rays can have their apices among the lattice points of a cube?
