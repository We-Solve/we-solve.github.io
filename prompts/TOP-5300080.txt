\section{Boundary fixed points in rank-zero Hénon components}\label{5300080}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5300080 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $f:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^2$ be a polynomial diffeomorphism, meaning a polynomial automorphism with polynomial inverse, with nonzero constant complex Jacobian determinant $\delta$. Set
\[K^+(f)=\{z\in\mathbb C^2:\{f^n(z):n\geq0\}\text{ is bounded}\}.\]
Assume $\operatorname{int}K^+(f)\ne\varnothing$, let $U$ be one of its connected components, and let $g:U\to\mathbb C^2$ be a limit of a subsequence of iterates, uniformly on compact subsets. Rank is complex differential rank. The component is wandering if its forward images are pairwise distinct. Volumes mean four-dimensional Euclidean volume in $\mathbb C^2$. For a periodic component replace $f$ by an iterate so that $f(U)=U$.Assume $U$ is invariant after the indicated replacement by an iterate and $g$ has rank zero, so $g$ is constant with value $x_0$. The eigenvalues at $x_0$ are those of the complex derivative $Df(x_0)$ for this invariant-component iterate.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $x_0\in\partial U$, prove that one eigenvalue at the fixed point $x_0$ equals $1$. \sref{5300080}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove that a boundary limit fixed point of an invariant rank-zero stable component has an eigenvalue equal to one.
