\section{Jordan boundary of a cubic parameter component}\label{5300019}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 5300019 · Dynamical Systems}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, let $f_\lambda(z)=\lambda z^2+z^3$. The point zero is a superattracting fixed point. Its immediate basin is the connected component containing zero of the set of points whose forward iterates tend to zero. Let $U$ be the parameter component containing $\lambda=0$ for which both finite critical points (zeros of $f'_\lambda$) lie in this immediate basin. Take its boundary $\partial U$ in the complex parameter plane. A cusp on $\partial U$ is a parameter for which $f_\lambda$ has a parabolic periodic cycle, whose return-map multiplier is a root of unity. A Jordan curve is the image of a circle under a homeomorphism onto its image.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $\partial U$ a Jordan curve? Prove this conjecture. \sref{5300019}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove that the boundary of the designated cubic parameter component is a Jordan curve.
