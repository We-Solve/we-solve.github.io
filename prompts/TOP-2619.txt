\section{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.110}\label{2619}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2619 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite nonabelian simple group $S$, identify $\operatorname{Inn}(S)$ with its inner automorphism group inside $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$. For $1\ne x\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ put $G_x=\langle x,\operatorname{Inn}(S)\rangle$ and
\[
 \alpha_S(x)=\min\{m:\exists g_1,\ldots,g_m\in G_x,
 \langle g_1^{-1}xg_1,\ldots,g_m^{-1}xg_m\rangle=G_x\}.
\]
Conjugation is within $G_x$, not within the full automorphism group. Exceptional finite groups of Lie type are the standard simple groups from the exceptional root systems $G_2,F_4,E_6,E_7,E_8$ and their twisted analogues, with parameters for which the group is simple.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
(a) If $S$ is an exceptional finite simple group of Lie type, must $\alpha_S(x)\leq5$ for every nonidentity $x\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$?

(b) For each such $S$, determine the exact maximum
\[
 \max_{x\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)\setminus\{1\}}\alpha_S(x).
\]
Both inner and outer automorphisms are included. \sref{2619}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each exceptional finite simple group of Lie type, bound by five the conjugates needed to generate with an automorphism, and determine the exact worst-case number.
