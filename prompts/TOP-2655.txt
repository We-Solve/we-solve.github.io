\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.146}\label{2655}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2655 · KOU-21.146 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A classifying space for a group $H$, denoted $K(H,1)$, is a connected CW-complex whose fundamental group is $H$ and whose higher homotopy groups vanish. Equivalently its universal cover is contractible. Its $n$-skeleton is the union of all cells of dimension at most $n$.

A group is of type $F_n$ if it has some classifying space with a finite $n$-skeleton. Thus $F_1$ is equivalent to finite generation and $F_2$ to finite presentability. Type $F_\infty$ means type $F_m$ for every positive integer $m$, equivalently the existence of a classifying space with finitely many cells in each dimension. This does not require the space to have finite dimension.

Fix any integer $n\ge3$. The first question asks whether every group $H$ of type $F_{n-1}$ embeds into a group $G$ of type $F_n$. The stronger question asks whether every such $H$ embeds into some group $G$ of type $F_\infty$.

In both formulations an embedding is an injective homomorphism $H\hookrightarrow G$, and the overgroup may depend on $H$ and on $n$. No finite-index or normality condition on the image is imposed. The request is for each $n\ge3$ and every group meeting the stated hypothesis, not just for groups already satisfying a higher finiteness property. The two targets distinguish a single improvement of skeleton finiteness from simultaneous finiteness in all dimensions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every group of type $F_{n-1}$, for $n\ge3$, be embedded into one of type $F_n$, or even one of type $F_\infty$?
