\section*{Which lattices occur as intervals in subgroup lattices of finite groups?}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite lattice is a nonempty finite partially ordered set $L$ in
which every pair $x,y$ has a greatest lower bound $x\wedge y$ and
a least upper bound $x\vee y$. It has a least and a greatest element.
An isomorphism of lattices is a bijection preserving the order in
both directions, equivalently preserving these two operations.

For a finite group $G$ and a subgroup $H\le G$, the subgroup interval
is the ordered set
\[
                         [H,G]=\{K:H\le K\le G\},
\]
ordered by inclusion. It is a lattice: the meet of $K_1,K_2$ is
$K_1\cap K_2$, and their join is the subgroup
$\langle K_1\cup K_2\rangle$ generated by their union. The bottom
and top are $H$ and $G$. No normality assumption on $H$ or on the
intermediate subgroups is made.

The conjecture recorded here is the negative representation assertion
\[
 \exists\text{ finite lattice }L\ \forall\text{ finite groups }G
 \ \forall H\le G:\qquad L\not\cong[H,G].
\]
Thus the task is to exhibit a finite lattice and prove that no such
interval represents it, or to refute the conjecture by proving
that every finite lattice is representable. Restricting a search
to groups of bounded order cannot establish nonrepresentability.

An interval $[H,K]$ inside the subgroup lattice of a larger finite
group gives the same object by taking $K$ itself as ambient group,
so allowing two proper interval endpoints does not enlarge the
representation class. This is an interval problem, not the stronger
requirement that $L$ be an entire subgroup lattice.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a finite lattice that cannot be realized as the interval of all subgroups between H and G in any finite group?
