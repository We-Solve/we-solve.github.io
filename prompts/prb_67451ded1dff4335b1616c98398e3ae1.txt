\section{Hyperbolic geometry — Question 10.6}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct. For a cooriented codimension-one foliation choose a nowhere-zero $1$-form $\alpha$ with $T\mathcal F=\ker\alpha$. Integrability gives $d\alpha=\alpha\wedge\omega$. The Godbillon--Vey class is $\operatorname{gv}(\mathcal F)=[\omega\wedge d\omega]\in H^3(M;\mathbb R)$, independent of these choices, and its value on an oriented closed $M$ is the integral of this $3$-form.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a universal finite-valued bound depending only on hyperbolic volume for the absolute Godbillon--Vey number of taut foliations? More precisely, seek a function $b$ such that $|\operatorname{gv}(\mathcal F)[M]|\le b(\operatorname{vol}(M))$ for every hyperbolic $3$-manifold $M$ and every sufficiently regular taut foliation $\mathcal F$ for which this number is defined. No linear form of $b$ is imposed. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can hyperbolic volume alone uniformly bound the absolute Godbillon--Vey invariant of taut foliations?
