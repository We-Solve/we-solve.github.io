\section{Geometric Spin–Statistics under Weakened Assumptions}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} The spin--statistics relation assigns Bose symmetry to integer spin $s\in\mathbb N_0$ and Fermi antisymmetry to half-integer spin $s\in\tfrac12+\mathbb N_0$. In three spatial dimensions the configuration space of $n$ indistinguishable particles is $((\mathbb R^3)^n\setminus\Delta)/S_n$, where $\Delta$ is the collision locus and $S_n$ permutes particles. Wavefunctions can be sections of bundles over this space; for two particles the relative direction space is $\mathbb{RP}^2$. The geometric formulation studies the exchange action and the rotation-group action on these bundles.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The nonrelativistic bundle framework is the context. Topology alone permits a choice of exchange sign and does not automatically prove the physical relation; the report explicitly requires an additional physically motivated input. The two source questions remain separate.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Find a geometric proof of the spin–statistics relation. Determine whether the relation remains valid under weaker assumptions and identify the minimal sufficient hypotheses.

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Geometric and projective-module approaches give spin--statistics results in important nonrelativistic configuration spaces, but the requested theorem under substantially weaker assumptions is not generally settled. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a geometric derivation of spin and statistics and identify which weaker physical assumptions still force their usual relation.
