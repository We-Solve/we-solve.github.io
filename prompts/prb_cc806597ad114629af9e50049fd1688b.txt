\section{Question 4.17 — Mazur}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $V$ be a smooth algebraic variety over $\mathbb Q$. Give $V(\mathbb R)$ its usual real topology and set $Z=\overline{V(\mathbb Q)}^{\,V(\mathbb R)}$. The condition that $V(\mathbb Q)$ is Zariski dense means it is not contained in a proper Zariski-closed subset of $V$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $V(\mathbb Q)$ is Zariski dense in $V$, must $Z$ be a union of connected components of $V(\mathbb R)$? \sref{statement}{2}
The cited survey records a negative answer to this general question: Colliot-Thélène, Skorobogatov and Swinnerton-Dyer constructed a smooth surface with Zariski-dense rational points whose real closure is not a union of components. The survey subsequently distinguishes the separate abelian-variety case. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
If rational points are algebraically dense, must their real closure consist of whole real connected components?
