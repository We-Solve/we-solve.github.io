A graph has a vertex set $V$ and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A subgraph need not be induced unless that word is stated. A proper vertex colouring gives adjacent vertices different colours; $\chi(G)$ is the least number of colours. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For which functions $g(n)$ with $n>g(n)\geq (\log n)^2$ is there a graph on $n$ vertices in which every induced subgraph on $g(n)$ vertices contains a clique of size $\geq \log n$ and an independent set of size $\geq \log n$?
In particular, is there such a graph for $g(n)=(\log n)^3$? \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Decide existence at g(n)=(log n)\textasciicircum{}3 and, more generally, locate the transition between the published lower obstruction and upper construction. \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How small can the tested subsets be while every one of them is guaranteed to contain both a logarithmically large clique and independent set?
