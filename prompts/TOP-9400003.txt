{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400003 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb N$ define $s(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ 1\le d<n}d$, the sum of positive proper divisors. Starting at $a_0\in\mathbb N$, iterate $a_{j+1}=s(a_j)$ until a zero is reached, at which point the sequence terminates. A cycle means $a_r=a_t>0$ for some $0\le r<t$, which forces repetition thereafter.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $a_0\in\mathbb N$, does this aliquot sequence either terminate at zero or eventually enter a cycle? Equivalently, is there no infinite nonrepeating aliquot sequence? \sref{9400003}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must repeated summation of proper divisors always terminate or eventually repeat?
