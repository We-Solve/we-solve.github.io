\section*{Kirby Problem 4.101}\label{2977}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2977 · KP-4.101 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb{CP}^2$ have its standard complex structure and
Fubini--Study symplectic form $\omega_{FS}$, normalized so a
projective line has area one. A smooth symplectic surface is a
closed smoothly embedded surface $S$ on which $\omega_{FS}$
restricts to a positive area form, giving its orientation.
Write $h\in H_2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)$ for the class of
a projective line. The degree of $S$ is the integer $d$ with
$[S]=dh$; its symplectic area implies $d>0$.

A smooth complex plane curve of degree $d$ is the zero set of
a homogeneous complex polynomial of degree $d$ in three variables,
with nonsingular zero locus in projective space. Such a curve is
a symplectic surface in the same homology class.
A symplectic isotopy is a smooth family of embeddings of a fixed
surface whose images remain symplectic at every time.

Is every smooth symplectic surface in $\mathbb{CP}^2$
symplectically isotopic to a smooth complex plane curve?
Equivalently, for every $d\ge1$, is there exactly one symplectic
isotopy class of embedded symplectic surfaces of degree $d$?
The equivalence uses connectedness of the family of nonsingular
complex curves of a fixed degree.

The ambient form remains $\omega_{FS}$ during the isotopy.
A deformation passing through singular surfaces, an arbitrary smooth
isotopy whose intermediate surfaces are not symplectic, or a change
of ambient symplectic form would not meet the requested condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every embedded symplectic surface in the complex projective plane symplectically isotopic to a nonsingular algebraic curve of the same degree?
