## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{amsmath,amssymb,hyperref}
\begin{document}
\section*{Complete negatively curved surfaces embedded in a ball}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Does there exist a connected smooth surface $\Sigma$ without boundary and
a smooth embedding
\[
 F:\Sigma\longrightarrow\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\lVert x\rVert<1\}
\]
whose induced Riemannian metric $g=F^*g_{\mathrm{Euclidean}}$ is complete
and has Gaussian curvature $K_g(p)<0$ at every $p\in\Sigma$?

Completeness is for the distance measured along the surface.
An embedding is an injective immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its
image. No uniform upper bound $K_g\leq-c<0$ is required. The surface need
not be minimal or proper in the ball, and its topology is not prescribed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a smooth embedded surface fit inside a ball while remaining intrinsically complete and having strictly negative Gaussian curvature everywhere?
\subsection{Sources}
Hadamard's problem (1898), as attributed and formulated in M. Ghomi,
\emph{Open problems in geometry of curves and surfaces}, Problem 7.2,
\url{https://ghomi.math.gatech.edu/Papers/op.pdf}.
The formulation above distinguishes embeddings from the known immersed
examples and retains pointwise, rather than uniformly bounded, negative curvature.
\end{document}
>>>
