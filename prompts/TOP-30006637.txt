\section{Finite-Depth Channel Classification of Mixed-State Topological Order}\label{30006637}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30006637 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A quantum channel is a completely positive trace-preserving map on states. A finite-depth local channel is a composition of a bounded number of layers of bounded-range operations, uniformly in the system size. Mixed-state topological order concerns long-range structures not removable by the allowed operations. Anyons and their braiding provide potential obstructions.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Classify mixed-state topological orders under finite-depth quantum channels: given two states, determine whether a finite-depth channel maps one to the other, and characterize the obstructions supplied by anyons and nontrivial braiding.

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The permitted ancillary systems and direction of channel conversion must be fixed. One-way conversion is not automatically an equivalence relation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Decide which mixed topological states can be connected by finite-depth local channels and characterize their anyonic obstructions.
