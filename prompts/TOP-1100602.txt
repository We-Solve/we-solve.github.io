\section*{Questions in Geometric Group Theory — Q 6.2}\label{1100602}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 1100602 · AMR-010-0602 · Group Theory · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a torsion-free group $H$, its integral cohomological dimension $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}H$ is the projective dimension of the trivial $\mathbb ZH$-module $\mathbb Z$. Equivalently, it is the least $d$ such that $H^i(H;M)=0$ for every module $M$ and all $i>d$. A virtually torsion-free group $G$ has virtual cohomological dimension
\[
 \operatorname{vcd}(G)=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}H
\]
for a torsion-free subgroup $H$ of finite index; the value is independent of that choice when finite.

Does there exist such a group $G$ with $d=\operatorname{vcd}(G)<\infty$ for which there is no $d$-dimensional acyclic $G$-complex with finite stabilizers? Here a $G$-complex is a CW complex with cellular group action, using the usual convention that a cell stabilizer fixes that cell pointwise. Acyclic means nonempty with $\widetilde H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=0$ in every degree. Every point stabilizer must be finite.

The complex need not be contractible, locally finite, or cocompact under the action. In particular, the question is weaker than demanding a classifying space for proper actions, where all finite-subgroup fixed-point sets must be contractible. A counterexample to that stronger requirement does not settle this question. Asking for dimension at most $d$ gives the same existence problem, since equivariant harmless cells can increase the dimension if necessary. Integral acyclicity, rather than rational acyclicity, is the convention throughout.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can virtual cohomological dimension be too small for any acyclic complex on which the group acts with finite stabilizers?
