\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 4.30}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $n\geq2$ and let
$p(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^{n}(z-\zeta_\nu)$ be a monic polynomial whose
zeros satisfy $|\zeta_\nu|\leq1$. Repeated zeros are allowed.
Write $K(p)$ for the convex hull of these zeros in the real
plane $\mathbb C$, and write $C(p)=\{w:p'(w)=0\}$ for the
set of critical points.

Consider the assertion
\[
 \text{for every }\zeta\in K(p),\qquad
     \min_{w\in C(p)}|\zeta-w|\leq1.
\]
The point $\zeta$ may lie anywhere in the convex hull, including
its interior and boundary; it need not itself be a zero of $p$.
The minimum is over the nonempty critical set and multiplicities
do not affect this distance.

Does the assertion hold for each of the following two classes,
considered separately?
First, all such polynomials with $p(0)=0$.
Second, all such polynomials of the form
$p(z)=z^n+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0$ with every $a_j$ real and
$a_j\leq0$ for $0\leq j<n$.
The closed-unit-disk condition on every zero remains in force
in both classes. A proof for the zeros of $p$ alone would leave
the convex-hull assertion unanswered. The task is to prove the
displayed covering property in both classes or provide a
counterexample in one of them, with the class identified.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is every point of the convex hull within distance one of a critical point in each of two special polynomial classes?
