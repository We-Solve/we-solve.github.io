\section{Kirby Problem 4.108}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A four-dimensional Liouville domain $(W,\lambda)$ is a compact smooth four-manifold with boundary, $d\lambda$ symplectic, and outward transverse Liouville vector field $V$ defined by $\iota_Vd\lambda=\lambda$. Its boundary contact structure is $\ker(\lambda|_{\partial W})$. A Weinstein domain also has a Morse function for which $V$ is gradient-like. A coorientation-preserving contactomorphism identifies the boundary contact hyperplane fields.

Given two Liouville domains $(W_+,\lambda_+)$ and $(W_-,\lambda_-)$ and a contactomorphism $\Phi:\partial W_-\to\partial W_+$, the convex-hypersurface gluing construction produces an $\mathbb R$-invariant cooriented contact structure $\xi$ on $\mathbb R\times X$, where
\[
X=W_+\cup_\Phi(-W_-).
\]
The minus sign means reversal of the symplectic orientation on the negative region. In the standard invariant model the contact form is $f\,dt+\beta$ on $\mathbb R\times X$; its dividing set is $\Gamma=f^{-1}(0)$, and the positive/negative regions have Liouville forms $\beta_\pm=\pm\beta/f$. This specifies the usual gluing germ; the two original domains give its compact positive and negative pieces. The hypersurface $\{0\}\times X$ is convex: a vector field preserving the contact structure is transverse to it. \sref{statement}{3}

A five-dimensional contact structure is overtwisted if it contains an embedded Borman--Eliashberg--Murphy overtwisted four-disk with the standard overtwisted contact germ, as in the source's definition. It is tight if it is not overtwisted.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Retain all three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] When both $W_+$ and $W_-$ are Weinstein, find a necessary and sufficient topological criterion on $(\Phi,W_+,W_-)$ for the resulting $\xi$ to be overtwisted; this is the proposed four-dimensional analogue of Giroux's surface criterion.
\item[(b)] Does every four-manifold $X$ admit such a decomposition with tight associated contact structure? A decomposition by the specified compact Liouville domains necessarily gives a closed orientable $X$; thus the substantive universal version is for closed oriented four-manifolds. If the word ``every'' is read to include noncompact or nonorientable manifolds, those cannot admit this kind of decomposition.
\item[(c)] Is there a Liouville domain $W$ and a boundary contactomorphism $\Phi:\partial W\to\partial W$ extending to a diffeomorphism $\Psi:W\to W$, for which the associated contact structure on $\mathbb R\times(W\cup_\Phi(-W))$ is overtwisted?
\end{enumerate}
Part (c) only asks that $\Phi$ extend smoothly, and does not assume a symplectic extension or that $W$ is Weinstein. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Seek a topological overtwistedness criterion for glued Weinstein pieces, tight Liouville decompositions of four-manifolds, and an overtwisted double produced by a boundary contactomorphism that extends smoothly.
