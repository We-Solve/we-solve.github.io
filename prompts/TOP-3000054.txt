\section*{Maximum weight k-element subsets of perfect matchings}\label{3000054}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 3000054 · AMR-029-0054 · Combinatorics · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(U\sqcup W,E)$ be a finite bipartite graph with $|U|=|W|=n$,
rational edge weights $w:E\to\mathbb Q$, and an integer $0\le k\le n$.
A matching is an edge set with no shared endpoints. It is extendable
when it is contained in some perfect matching of $G$.

Find a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that computes
\[
       \max\left\{\sum_{e\in F}w(e):|F|=k,\quad
                     F\subseteq M\text{ for a perfect matching }M\right\}
\]
and returns an optimal $F$ together with an extension $M$, or reports
that no feasible solution exists. Polynomial time is measured in the
graph size and binary encoding lengths of the weights and $k$.

The objective charges only the $k$ selected edges of $F$, not all
edges in the perfect extension. Hence maximizing the weight of an
entire perfect matching and then discarding edges does not immediately
solve the stated problem. Similarly, an unconstrained optimum among
$k$-edge matchings may fail to extend at all. The extension can depend
on the chosen $F$ and is not specified in advance. The condition is
existential for this particular selected matching; the graph need not
have the stronger property that every $k$-edge matching is extendable.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one efficiently find the heaviest $k$ edges that form a
matching extendable to a perfect matching of a bipartite graph?
