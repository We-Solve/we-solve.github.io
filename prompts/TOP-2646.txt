{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 2646 · Group Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a finite $p$-group $G$ distinguish the set $P=\{g^p:g\in G\}$ from the subgroup $G^p$ generated by that set. A finite $p$-group $H$ is powerful if $[H,H]\leq H^p$ for odd $p$, or $[H,H]\leq H^4$ for $p=2$. The exponent is the least positive integer killing every element.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $P$ is a subgroup, must it be powerful? In particular, (a) for odd $p$ and $G$ of exponent $p^2$, must $P$ be abelian; and (b) for a $2$-group $G$ of exponent $8$ whose squares form a subgroup, must that subgroup be abelian? \sref{2646}{1}\sref{2646}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether a power image that is already a subgroup must be powerful, including the proposed abelianness conclusions for exponent p squared and exponent eight.
