\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$, and $\mathbb D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}$. Let $S$ be the class of analytic injective functions $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ normalized by $f(0)=0$, $f^{\prime}(0)=1$, with expansion $f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_nz^n$ and $a_1=1$. For $k,n\ge1$ define the Hankel determinant \[A_n^{(k)}=\det(a_{n+i+j})_{0\le i,j<k}.\] An exponent $\beta$ is admissible for order $k$ if for every $f\in S$ one has $|A_n^{(k)}|^{1/k}=O_f(n^{\beta})$ as $n\to\infty$. A function in $S$ is starlike if its image is starlike about $0$: $tw\in f(\mathbb D)$ for every $w\in f(\mathbb D)$ and $0\le t\le1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there admissible exponents strictly below $-1/2$ for all sufficiently large $k$? For the starlike subclass the source gives the sharp exponent $-1+2/k$. The update records $A_n^{(k)}=O(n^{3/2-k/2-k\beta_0})$ for $k\ge2$ and a constant $\beta_0>1/4000$, yielding an affirmative large-order conclusion. \sref{2306014}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do Hankel determinants of univalent-function coefficients admit exponents below minus one half for all sufficiently large determinant orders? The source update answers the large-order question affirmatively.
