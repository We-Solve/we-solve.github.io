\section*{Comparing sums of square roots}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Given nonnegative integers $a_1,\ldots,a_m,b_1,\ldots,b_n$ in binary,
consider the real algebraic number
\[
 D=\sum_{i=1}^m\sqrt{a_i}-\sum_{j=1}^n\sqrt{b_j},
\]
where square roots are the nonnegative ones. Let $L$ be the total binary
input length, including list delimiters. The computational question is
whether a deterministic Turing machine can output the exact sign of $D$
in time polynomial in $L$. Equality is an allowed outcome. Comparing a
single sum with an integer is included by using one square radicand on
the other side.

A related separation problem asks for a polynomial $p$, independent of
the input, such that every nonzero $D$ satisfies $|D|\ge2^{-p(L)}$.
Such a bound would make sufficiently accurate rational interval
approximations decisive, with equality handled by the same separation
gap. It would therefore suffice for polynomial-time comparison.

The catalogue calls these requests equivalent, but no converse is assumed
here: an exact symbolic decision algorithm need not obtain its answer
from a uniform numerical separation bound. The computational model is
bit arithmetic, not a real-arithmetic model in which arbitrary radicals
and their comparisons are single unit-cost operations.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two sums of integer square roots be compared exactly using only
polynomially many bit operations? A polynomial separation bound would be
one sufficient route, rather than an assumed equivalent statement.
