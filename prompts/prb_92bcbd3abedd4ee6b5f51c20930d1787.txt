\section*{Arithmetic removal in cyclic groups: corrected quantifiers, matching distances, and exact CRT-compatible lifts}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $m\geq1$, let $G_m=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$, written additively.
For three subsets $A,B,C\subseteq G_m$, define the ordered solution count
\[
 T(A,B,C)=|\{(a,b,c)\in A\times B\times C:a+b+c=0\}|.
\]
The three sets are distinguished roles: deleting a residue from one does not automatically delete it from another.
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, let $\delta_{\rm cyc}(\varepsilon)$ be the supremum of those $\delta\in[0,1]$ for which the following assertion holds for every $m$ and every such triple.
If $T(A,B,C)\leq\delta m^2$, there exist $A'\subseteq A$, $B'\subseteq B$, $C'\subseteq C$ such that
\[
 |A\setminus A'|,\ |B\setminus B'|,\ |C\setminus C'|
 \leq\varepsilon m,
 \qquad T(A',B',C')=0.
\]
Cardinality inequalities have their literal integer meaning, so no rounding convention is needed.
The problem is to determine, or give substantially sharper quantitative estimates for, $\delta_{\rm cyc}(\varepsilon)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, uniformly over all cyclic group orders.
Upper bounds require examples far from solution-free despite few solutions; lower bounds must give a deletion guarantee for every triple.
The source's ``at least'' solution hypothesis is reversed here to ``at most'', as required by a removal lemma.
Its final reference to regularity is interpreted in the context of the explicitly stated arithmetic removal problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Quantify how few additive triples force three subsets of a cyclic group to become solution-free after small deletions.
