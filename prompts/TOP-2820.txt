\section*{Kirby Problem 3.22}\label{2820}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2820 · KP-3.22 · Topology · Kirby's Problems in Low-Dimensional Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a connected complete finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold and
$G=\pi_1(M)$. A faithful action on a one-manifold $Y$ is an injective
homomorphism $G\to\operatorname{Homeo}(Y)$. For an integer $r\geq1$,
a $C^r$ action has image in $\operatorname{Diff}^r(Y)$: each map and its
inverse has $r$ continuous derivatives. A $C^{k,\alpha}$ action additionally
requires the $k$th derivatives to be Hölder continuous with exponent
$0<\alpha\leq1$. Smooth means $C^\infty$.

Determine the greatest regularity attainable by faithful actions of $G$ on
$S^1$, and on the compact interval $[0,1]$. For the latter, differentiability
is required up to the endpoints, with one-sided derivatives there. Also
ask the virtual version: replace $G$ by some finite-index subgroup $H\leq G$,
which may be chosen to obtain the highest possible regularity.

The question concerns existence of an action, rather than regularity of one
fixed construction. A related refinement asks whether natural topological
actions can be conjugated to smoother ones. Compactness of the interval and
endpoint regularity matter: a smooth action on the open line need not extend
smoothly to a compact interval. If no action attains a proposed supremal
regularity, its nonattainment should be distinguished from existence at that level.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How smooth can a faithful circle or compact-interval action of a hyperbolic three-manifold group be, possibly after passing to a finite-index subgroup?
