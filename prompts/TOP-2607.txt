\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.98}\label{2607}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 2607 · KOU-21.98 · Group Theory · Kourovka Notebook - New Problems, Issue 21}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the commutator convention $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$. A multilinear commutator word, also called an outer commutator word, is formed recursively from distinct variables by bracketing words that involve disjoint sets of variables. For example $[[x_1,x_2],[x_3,x_4]]$ is such a word; no variable is repeated. Regard $w=w(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ as a fixed word in a free group.

For an arbitrary group $G$, its set of $w$-values and its verbal subgroup are
\[
 G_w=\{w(g_1,\ldots,g_r):g_1,\ldots,g_r\in G\},
 \qquad w(G)=\langle G_w\rangle.
\]
Suppose there are finitely many cyclic subgroups $C_1,\ldots,C_t\le G$, with $t\ge1$, such that $G_w\subseteq C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_t$. A cyclic subgroup means one generated by a single element, possibly of finite order.

Must $w(G)$ be finite-by-cyclic? Precisely, must there be a finite normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq w(G)$ such that $w(G)/N$ is cyclic, allowing a finite or trivial cyclic quotient? The covering assumption concerns individual values of the word. Arbitrary products of values need not lie in one of the covering cyclic subgroups. The problem asks for the stated structural conclusion for every multilinear commutator word and every group satisfying that covering assumption, without assuming finite generation of $G$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If finitely many cyclic subgroups cover every value of a multilinear commutator word, must its verbal subgroup have a finite normal subgroup with cyclic quotient?
