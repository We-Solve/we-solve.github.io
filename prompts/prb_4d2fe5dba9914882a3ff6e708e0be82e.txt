\section{The Kusner conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an integer $d\geq1$, let $\ell_1^d$ mean $\mathbb R^d$ with norm $\|x\|_1=\sum_{j=1}^d|x_j|$. An equilateral set $S\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ has a number $a>0$ such that $\|x-y\|_1=a$ for every two distinct $x,y\in S$. Let $e(\ell_1^d)$ be the largest possible cardinality of such a set. The $2d$ points $\{\pm e_j:1\leq j\leq d\}$, where $e_j$ are the standard coordinate vectors, have all pairwise distances two, so $e(\ell_1^d)\geq2d$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every equilateral set in $\ell_1^d$ have at most $2d$ points, for every $d\geq1$? Equivalently, is $e(\ell_1^d)=2d$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can there ever be more than twice the dimension many mutually equidistant points in a real space with the sum-of-coordinate-distances metric?
