\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A vacuum spacetime is a Lorentzian manifold $(M,g)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$. Its Cauchy data are a Riemannian metric and second fundamental form satisfying the vacuum constraint equations. The maximal globally hyperbolic development is the largest Cauchy evolution. Relative to future null infinity $\mathcal I^+$, the black-hole region is $M\setminus J^-(\mathcal I^+)$; a naked singularity is visible from the exterior.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Weak Cosmic Censorship for the Einstein vacuum equations: for generic complete, regular, asymptotically flat Cauchy data, are all singularities in the corresponding maximal development hidden inside a black hole (so that naked singularities do not occur)?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The source gives a weak-cosmic-censorship programme. A choice of topology for genericity and a precise conformal-completion and singularity criterion is needed for a theorem; no such extra choice is imposed here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For generic regular isolated-system data, decide whether gravitational singularities are always hidden from distant observers.
