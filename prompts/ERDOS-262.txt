## Problem statement (quoted material)
<<<
An increasing sequence of positive integers $(a_n)$ is called an irrationality sequence here if $\sum_{n\ge1}1/(t_na_n)$ is irrational for every sequence of positive integers $(t_n)$. How slowly can $a_n$ grow?

Han\v cl's resolution gives the necessary threshold
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2\log_2 a_n}{n}\ge1.
 \tag{1}
\]
We prove a constructive obstruction implying this threshold and the stronger condition recorded on the problem page. For the matching example we explicitly use Erd\H{o}s's known theorem that $a_n=2^{2^n}$ is an irrationality sequence. Thus the answer concerns the double-exponential growth scale, not a classification of every sequence at the boundary. No novelty is claimed.
>>>
