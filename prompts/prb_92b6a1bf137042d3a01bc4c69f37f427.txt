\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.4}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group $L$, its derived subgroup $L'$ is generated by all
commutators $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a,b\in L$. Repeatedly taking derived
subgroups gives the derived series, starting with $L$ itself.
A finite group is soluble when this series eventually becomes trivial.
The soluble radical $R(G)$ of a finite group $G$ is its largest soluble
normal subgroup. The hypothesis $R(G)=1$ rules out every nontrivial
soluble subgroup that is normal in $G$.

Given a finite group with $R(G)=1$ and five soluble subgroups
$H_1,\ldots,H_5\le G$, is there a choice $x_1,\ldots,x_5\in G$ for which
\[
 \bigcap_{i=1}^{5}x_i^{-1}H_i x_i=\{1\}?
\]
All five subgroups and all finite ambient groups satisfying the radical
condition are quantified over. Repeated subgroups and the trivial subgroup
are allowed, and no maximality condition is imposed on any $H_i$.

The conjugators may be chosen independently, but they must give one
simultaneous trivial intersection. It is not required that every pair
of conjugate subgroups have trivial intersection. Conjugating the entire
configuration by the same element does not change whether the total
intersection is trivial, so one could equivalently fix $x_1=1$ and
choose the other four. Normality is imposed only on the radical condition;
the given soluble subgroups need not be normal and generally are not.
The proposed number five is an absolute bound, independent of $|G|$.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can five arbitrary soluble subgroups of a finite group with trivial soluble radical be independently conjugated to have trivial total intersection?
