\section*{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.68}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A holomorphic function $f$ on the unit disc $\mathbb D$ is a Bloch function if
$\beta(f)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}(1-|z|^2)|f'(z)|<\infty$.
Consider the additional normalization $f(0)=1$ and positivity $\operatorname{Re}f(z)>0$ in $\mathbb D$, and write $f(z)=1+\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n$.
For $N\ge1$ put $S_f(N)=\sum_{n=1}^N|a_n|^2$.
Determine the sharp possible rates of growth of $S_f(N)$ as $N\to\infty$ for this class. A quantitative universal formulation fixes $B>0$ and asks for optimal asymptotic bounds on
\[
G_B(N)=\sup\{S_f(N):f(0)=1, \operatorname{Re}f>0,\ \beta(f)\le B\}.
\]
One should also distinguish bounds on this supremum from growth rates attained by a single fixed function along all $N$ or a subsequence. Normalizing the Bloch seminorm prevents an implicit constant from hiding arbitrary dependence on $f$.
For comparison, positivity alone gives a uniform bound on each individual coefficient, but summing those separate bounds need not reflect the added Bloch condition. Parseval's identity rewrites the corresponding weighted coefficient sum as
\[
1+\sum_{n\ge1}|a_n|^2r^{2n}
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta.
\]
The issue is the best joint consequence of positivity and Bloch growth, with matching examples rather than only a nonsharp estimate.
\subsection{Short English statement}
How quickly can the squared Taylor coefficients accumulate for a normalized Bloch function with positive real part?
