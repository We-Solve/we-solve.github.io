\section*{Minimum Euclidean Matching in 2D}\label{5500006}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 5500006 · AMR-054-0006 · Geometry · AMR Open Problem Lists}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S=\{p_1,\ldots,p_{2n}\}$ be distinct points in the Euclidean plane.
A perfect matching is a partition of $S$ into $n$ unordered pairs. Its
cost is
\[
                  c(M)=\sum_{\{p_i,p_j\}\in M}\|p_i-p_j\|_2.
\]
The optimization task is to output a matching of minimum cost. Every
pair of points is allowed; the main problem is not restricted to a
prescribed bipartition. Ties may be resolved arbitrarily.

Determine the optimal asymptotic running time for this exact geometric
optimization problem as $n$ grows. For the standard geometric formulation,
work in a real-arithmetic model with exact arithmetic, square roots,
and comparisons, and state whether an algorithm's bound is deterministic,
expected, or amortized. A bit-complexity result on rational coordinates
must instead account for coordinate lengths and exact comparisons of
sums of distances.

The source also highlights the bipartite variant, with $n$ red and $n$
blue points and only red--blue pairs permitted. Bounds for that variant
must be identified separately. Approximation schemes which return a
matching within a factor $1+\varepsilon$ of the optimum do not determine
the complexity of the exact task.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine how quickly the minimum-total-length perfect pairing of
planar points can be computed exactly, and distinguish the bipartite
version and approximation algorithms.
