{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 3239 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Local complementation at a vertex $v$ of a finite simple graph toggles adjacency for each distinct pair of neighbors of $v$, leaving all other adjacencies unchanged. A vertex-minor is obtained through a sequence of local complementations and vertex deletions. A class $\mathcal F$ is vertex-minor closed if it contains every vertex-minor of each member, and proper if it is not the class of all finite simple graphs. The class is $\chi$-bounded if some function $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ satisfies $\chi(G)\le f(\omega(G))$ for all nonempty $G\in\mathcal F$, where $\omega$ is the largest clique size.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is every proper vertex-minor-closed class of finite simple graphs $\chi$-bounded? \sref{3239}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does forbidding at least one vertex-minor force a chromatic bound depending only on the largest clique?
