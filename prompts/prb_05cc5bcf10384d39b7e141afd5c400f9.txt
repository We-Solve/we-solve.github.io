\section{Kirby Problem 3.54}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a closed oriented $3$-manifold $M$, $\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(M;\mathbb Z)$ denotes hat Heegaard Floer homology using the canonical isomorphism class of coherent orientation systems, as specified in the source. For a knot $K\subset S^3$, $\widehat{\operatorname{HFK}}(S^3,K;\mathbb Z)$ is the integral hat knot Floer homology, the homology of the associated Alexander-graded knot Floer complex. All gradings are included. An abelian group has torsion if it has $x\ne0$ with $nx=0$ for some integer $n\ge2$. A rational homology $3$-sphere satisfies $H_*(M;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(S^3;\mathbb Q)$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine separately whether there exists:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a closed $3$-manifold $M$ with torsion in $\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(M;\mathbb Z)$;
\item a rational homology $3$-sphere $M$ with torsion in $\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(M;\mathbb Z)$;
\item a knot $K\subset S^3$ with torsion in $\widehat{\operatorname{HFK}}(S^3,K;\mathbb Z)$.
\end{enumerate}
The orientation-system convention is essential: torsion from a noncanonical choice or from a plus/infinity Floer variant does not answer these questions. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can torsion occur in canonical integral hat Floer homology of a closed three-manifold, of a rational homology sphere, or of a knot in the three-sphere?
