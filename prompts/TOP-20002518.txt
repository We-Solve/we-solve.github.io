\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}Kerr space-time models a rotating vacuum black hole; Schwarzschild is its nonrotating counterpart. Lensing maps initial null-ray directions to source locations. Critical curves are loci where the lens-map derivative is singular; caustics are their images. The strong-deflection limit concerns rays passing near the trapped photon region and requires a specified expansion parameter. Higher-order images correspond to rays making additional turns.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Retain source questions (1)--(13): obtain analytic descriptions of relativistic caustics beyond their equatorial intersections; describe lensing off the equatorial plane; understand higher-order caustics analytically and numerically; decide observational accessibility of strong-field lensing; find caustic shapes in the strong-deflection limit; describe off-equatorial strong-deflection lensing and additional images; identify effective coordinates for Kerr lensing; formulate projects and non-disk lensing events for MAXIM; determine whether accretion-disk gas trajectories need fully general-relativistic or magnetohydrodynamic/Kerr codes; compare time delays in Kerr and shifted Schwarzschild and determine the order of agreement; and compare caustics and critical curves at first order in the strong-field limit with shifted Schwarzschild.
